# Receiver Alignment

## Definition
The framework of evaluating a wide receiver primarily by where he lines up
(X/outside-on-the-line, Z/flanker off the line, or slot) and how that role
changes, rather than treating "wide receiver" as one undifferentiated
position. Proponents argue two receivers can be similarly talented/ranked
overall while being nearly incomparable in what they're actually asked to do
— and that a receiver's alignment mix is often the single best predictor of
how a role (and opportunity) will change year to year.

## Expert Takes
- 2026-07-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-28 - Matt Harmon]]): this has been the explicit ethos of Reception Perception since its 2021 launch (with roots going back to 2014-2018) — "every conversation with a receiver has to start with what is their role." Draws an analogy to defensive line: lumping all wide receivers together is like lumping together a defensive tackle and a defensive end just because both are "on the D-line." Cites [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and Nico Collins as an example: both would rank close together 1-10 overall, but align so differently that direct comparison is close to meaningless without accounting for role.
