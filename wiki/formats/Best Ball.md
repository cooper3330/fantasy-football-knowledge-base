# Best Ball

No in-season lineup management — the highest-scoring eligible players at
each position each week count automatically. This is the wiki owner's
highest-priority format.

## What matters here
- Weekly ceiling/variance over floor/safety
- Bye week and injury-window stacking across the whole roster (no waivers to
  patch holes)
- Upside picks in the middle/late rounds over "safe" low-ceiling starters

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]): the early-market call for 2024 is to buy the *unloved* half of a debate the market has just resolved. - 2023-12-18 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode: on drafting in the January window, before free agency and coaching moves resolve — his default is to keep betting on established route-running profiles until better information arrives, naming [[Keenan Allen]] and [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]. Notes the asymmetry between the two: St. Brown has the same play-caller, Allen's offense may change again.
- 2023-12-18 — **Matt Waldman**, same episode: [[Christian McCaffrey]] is the unqualified 1.01 for 2024 despite turning 28 — his framing of what an early pick buys is "leverage over the field."
- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]): [[Mike Evans]] is going in the fourth round of early best ball drafts off his new Tampa Bay deal, expected to rise further now the signing is official — his ceiling hinges on who's throwing him the ball ([[Baker Mayfield]] keeps him a fringe top-10 option; a rookie caps him closer to 1,000 yards). Michael Pittman Jr. is going in the late third round if he stays in Indianapolis, a price Waldman argues may be better value than Evans's given [[Anthony Richardson]]'s breakout upside.
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]) [Best Ball]: The hosts argue 2024 rewards what they call a 'fuck quarterback strategy' -- deep, safe veteran QB value lets drafters wait well past the traditional early rounds. Citing best-ball ADP, they note Justin Herbert still goes top-12, but Tua Tagovailoa (13), Jared Goff (14, off a QB7 finish), Trevor Lawrence (15), Kirk Cousins (16), Matthew Stafford (17), Caleb Williams (18), Aaron Rodgers (19) and Justin Fields (20) are all available afterward, meaning even reaching for a rookie like Williams as value still leaves a viable low-cost backup available later. Waldman's caveat: if you know you're bad at picking the right late-round QB, don't force the strategy -- take a safe early quarterback like Patrick Mahomes instead and build certainty around that pick rather than chasing upside.
- 2024-07-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]) [Best Ball]: contract-holdout noise is a buying window, not a fade signal. He wants the [[CeeDee Lamb]] negotiation to get as acrimonious as possible in public — the loud part is theater while the work happens behind the scenes — so he can pounce on discounted shares, and he says the same about [[Brandon Aiyuk]], whose Underdog ADP has already slid from 12 to 16 and who becomes an outright value if it falls further. Bob Harris (not tracked) shares the approach, hoping people 'start dialing back as they get fearful.' Elsewhere Waldman argues the market's regression-to-the-mean instinct about one-year quarterbacks is superstition, making [[Jordan Love]] a good deal at QB10, and flags [[Brandin Cooks]] at WR59 in round 11 as a WR4 at a WR5 price.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]) [Best Ball]: guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) argues ADP varies enormously by site and that best ball markets — where the most drafting has already happened and three-receiver starting lineups are required — price rookie receivers highest, using [[Malik Nabers]] as the case study (Underdog 27, Sleeper 38, FFPC 47, ESPN 64, Yahoo 67-71). He frames the ESPN/Yahoo home-league pools as casual and the Sleeper/Underdog pools as reactive, comparing them to dog owners versus dog shows. Waldman's contribution is the last-round dart hunt — [[Calvin Austin III]] as the type of name he wants at the very end of a best ball draft — and both note [[Drake Maye]] is a best ball pick rather than a home-league one. McFarland also says he'll stack Tennessee ([[Calvin Ridley]], [[DeAndre Hopkins]], [[Will Levis]]) in best ball if the draft falls that way, betting on the offense rather than a player.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]) [Best Ball]: two late-round best-ball positions — [[DeMarcus Robinson]] as a flyer he loves all day long on Stafford's willingness to target him and [[Puka Nacua]]'s X-receiver endorsement, and [[Nick Chubb]] as a round-10 dart he will burn a pick on if the first ten rounds go well, a bet he explicitly separates from redraft, where he would wait for the price to fall. Waldman also distinguishes best-ball-style platforms like Underdog, which he jokes are 'dog show competitions' where prices are hyper-tweaked, from home leagues where rookies such as [[Malik Nabers]] remain underpriced.
- 2024-08-05 — According to Bob Harris (co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]: because he is drafting thousands of teams rather than one, portfolio-level share-building beats picking a single winner in a muddy backfield — he holds both [[J.K. Dobbins]] at RB47 and [[Gus Edwards]] at RB37 out of the same Chargers committee, narrowing to either/or only as the end of August approaches. He made the same case for a third quarterback, taking [[Justin Fields]] at QB31 over [[Russell Wilson]] at QB29 on the strength of 18.3 points per game. [[Matt Waldman]] framed the choice as a roster-construction question: if you built early stability, swing late on upside; if you already reached for upside, take the perceived-safer play.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]) [Best Ball]: pure vertical speed is worth materially more in best ball than in weekly lineups. On [[Xavier Worthy]], he splits the verdict — the football evaluation is a yes, and Worthy will 'absolutely' deliver huge weeks in best ball formats, but the redraft verdict is no because defenses answer Kansas City's deep game with two-high safety shells, the same coverage that ended [[Tyreek Hill]]'s deep production there. The best-ball case rests exactly on the weeks a spike-play profile produces; the weekly case requires underneath volume that may not come.
