# Dynasty

Rosters carry over year to year; players are held as long-term assets, not
just season-long pieces. Second-priority format for the wiki owner.

## What matters here
- Age curves, contract/draft-capital signals, and long-term role over
  current-year points
- Rookie/prospect evaluation (this is exactly Matt Waldman's specialty — see
  [[Matt Waldman]])
- Trade value and rebuild/contend timeline framing

- 2023-12-14 — **Adam Harstad** (guest — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-14]]: productive seasons by players 30+ have collapsed league-wide since ~2017 — the 30+ share of 1,000-yard receiving seasons fell from a stable ~33% (1993–2010) to **7.7%** (2017–2022), with parallel declines at RB, LB, and OL. Cause unknown. Direct implication: historical age curves overvalue older players, and dynasty managers should discount the tail end of careers more aggressively than past data suggests. Full detail: [[Aging Curves and Career Longevity]].
- 2023-12-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] (same episode): expects a possible partial correction in the next data window, since the 2023–24 move back toward 12/13 personnel restores some of the middle-of-field demands that spread offenses had stripped out.
- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]: the 2024 rookie-receiver call, and the reasoning generalizes. [[Malik Nabers]] over [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] "by a healthy margin" — not on ceiling (he gives Harrison the higher one, and expects both to be top-10 NFL receivers within five years) but on **pro-readiness**: Nabers is "NFL-ready on all three levels of the field," projects for 1,100+ yards and 10+ touchdowns as a rookie, and can be aligned anywhere from day one. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees, adding Odell Beckham Jr. and [[Justin Jefferson]] as Nabers's skill-set comps. Rookie-draft framework: [[Prospect Pro-Readiness vs Ceiling]].
- 2023-12-21 — **Angelo**, same episode: the NIL wrinkle that bears directly on rookie-draft supply — one blue-chip prospect returning to school on NIL money could "start a chain reaction," leaving classes with "prospects that are a year older" and, over three to ten years, possibly an NBA-style one-and-done rule. Both this and Harstad's contrary read below are checkable predictions. See [[NIL and Player Development]].
- 2023-12-21 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]: on whether NIL money starts thinning rookie classes — expects it to keep "two or three pro-ready players per team off the market for another year or two" at most, not to compete with the NFL for mid-tier prospects, because college football splits less total revenue roughly a hundred ways against the NFL's 32. He is also skeptical the NFL responds by developing players, since the supply of willing players is unlimited. Detail and the contrary case: [[NIL and Player Development]].
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] (same episode): the dynasty-manager discipline point, using [[James Cook]] as his own example — managers who dropped his rookie-year fit call because Cook's first season was quiet were judging a multi-year evaluation on one year of box score. "They may not turn out to be misses after just one year or two years."
- 2023-12-21 — **Waldman**, same episode: warns against pricing a scheme-dependent player as if the scheme travels. [[Deebo Samuel]] "might not even be remotely the player he is right now in fantasy football" on a team that saw more man coverage — a team-change risk that belongs in dynasty valuation, not just in weekly projections. Same logic applied contrarily to [[Lamar Jackson]].
- 2024-02-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-27]]): a general dynasty rookie-ranking principle, illustrated retroactively with [[Quentin Johnston]] vs. [[Zay Flowers]] from the 2023 class — "you should never have ranked Quentin Johnston ahead of Zay Flowers just because he went to [Justin Herbert]," since Johnston wasn't as good a prospect as Flowers even before landing spot is weighed, a call Harmon says holds up on-record, not in hindsight. Applies the same logic forward to 2024: with [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], [[Malik Nabers]] and [[Rome Odunze]] graded as a tight, talent-close top tier, he expects landing spot — not the order analysts rank them in — to decide whose career ends up looking better.
- 2024-03-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-08]]), solo cast on the 2024 QB class: [[Caleb Williams]] is this year's only true dynasty priority pick at quarterback — he doesn't see a clear-cut "one-two" or "one-two-three" answer behind him the way many expected pre-season (Richardson/Stroud/Young-style). His standard-format recommendation: invest early rookie-draft capital in receivers (maybe a running back or two) rather than a quarterback outside Williams, build a talent surplus, and trade it for an established starting QB — or wait for one of the class's six or so first-round-caliber names to fall to the second round as their draft stock slides on draft day. In superflex formats, names three to four worth taking in the second half of round one or early round two. For rebuilding/startup rosters, floats stockpiling two of his top-three QB options in the first two rounds (or one now plus a strong option next year) as a viable alternate strategy — but still says he'd lean receiver-heavy even then.
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo cast on the 2024 RB class: a historical hit-rate framework directly informing rookie-draft strategy. Reviewing pre-draft grades against outcomes for the 2016-2023 RB classes, he finds the true number of multi-year successful starters per class is consistently just one to three (e.g. two of 23 graded 2016 prospects — Ezekiel Elliott and Derrick Henry — became sustained starters; the loaded 2018 class produced only Saquon Barkley and Nick Chubb as "true survivors"; 2017's six hits, headlined by [[Christian McCaffrey]] and [[Alvin Kamara]], is "a complete outlier"). His conclusion: "it's probably best to presume that most running back classes are bad classes," including 2024 — which he grades as having a wider (24-player) but shallower group of plausible contributors than 2023, with only two grading at true immediate-starter level versus five or six the year before. Explicit strategic recommendation: place a higher rookie-draft priority on receivers and quarterbacks, then trade for established veteran running backs once a team has built a talent stockpile elsewhere — rather than reaching for running backs early on the (statistically unlikely) bet that a given prospect beats the class's own base rate.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]) [Dynasty]: dynasty buys from this episode are [[Andrei Iosivas]] (a definite invest-in if you are building, though [[Jermaine Burton]] is the better 2024 pick), [[Michael Penix Jr.]] as a stash behind [[Kirk Cousins]] that he would not make in redraft, and [[Eric All]] long term but not this season. - 2024-09-05 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Film and Theory)]] [Dynasty]: the most common dynasty approach — be competent everywhere and have no weaknesses — is backwards, because leagues are not won by the team with the fewest weaknesses but by the team with the most strengths. He illustrated it with distributions: a team with [[Christian McCaffrey]] and the RB40 outscored a team with the RB11 and RB12 last year, and in 2006 you could have started Ladainian Tomlinson and yourself and beaten a team with the RB7 and RB8. A bad starter should be embraced rather than feared, because terrible-to-mediocre is an easy upgrade whereas mediocre-to-good is hard; [[Matt Waldman]] added that a mediocre starter is worse partly because it makes weekly decisions harder and invites overanalysis. Waldman's own construction is anchor-based — pick the positions your scoring system rewards most and stockpile them (receivers and linebackers in his IDP leagues), then trade surplus into the rest. Harstad anchors by player rather than position, wanting as many players as possible he is confident will still produce in three years, since their production is effectively free.
- 2024-09-11 — According to guest co-host Felix Sharpe of Campus to Canton (NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-11]]) [Dynasty]: devy names to know for future rookie drafts — [[Cam Ward]] as the season's biggest quarterback riser, true freshman receivers [[Jeremiah Smith]] and [[Ryan Williams]] as three-years-out board-toppers, [[Sire Gaines]] as the backup behind [[Ashton Jeanty]] likely to inherit the Boise State backfield in 2025, and Tulane's [[Darian Mensah]] as a deep-dive passer expected to transfer up. Sharpe also noted college fantasy interest is growing sharply, which he attributes partly to the college football video game's release — relevant because devy acquisition costs rise as more players watch. - 2024-09-12 — According to co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-12]]) [Dynasty]: the dynasty market has become materially more efficient. When he started there were no dynasty sites and barely any rankings, so a back could be ranked anywhere from 8th to 52nd depending on who you asked; DLF's monthly dynasty ADP and then, over the last five years, KeepTradeCut and FantasyCalc produced descriptive community consensus values that most managers now rationally use. The consequence is that pure disagreement swaps are essentially extinct, and trading now requires finding places of *agreement* where the same package is worth different amounts to two different rosters — see [[Win-Win Trade Construction]]. He still encourages active trading for anyone who can do it competently, but warns it takes far more work and consensus-building than it used to, and that you should trade around consensus price rather than your own valuation. Also filed here: his [Dynasty] read on [[Jayden Daniels]] — a heavy-rushing 2024 would be good for 2024-2026 fantasy value but a worrying signal for 2027.
