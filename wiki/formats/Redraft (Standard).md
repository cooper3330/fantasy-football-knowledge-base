# Redraft (Standard)

A traditional single-season league with a new draft each year and active
in-season lineup/waiver management. Lowest priority format for the wiki
owner, but still tracked.

## What matters here
- Current-year role, floor, and matchup-driven starts/sits
- Waiver wire and in-season roster management
- Standard scoring nuances (as opposed to best ball or dynasty-specific
  scoring/roster rules)

Worth separating the two questions before acting on a hot-take verdict.
- 2023-12-18 — **Matt Waldman**, same episode: don't bench pass catchers over a backup quarterback — bump them one tier and keep starting them. Full argument: [[Weak Quarterback Play and Receiver Value]].
- 2023-12-18 — **Matt Waldman**, same episode: one big game is not a role. Explicit on [[Treylon Burks]] and [[Tre Tucker]] — wait a week to see whether touches and targets follow before trusting the breakout.

- 2024-09-05 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Film and Theory)]] [Redraft (Standard)]: redraft only requires knowing the what — will this player produce — whereas dynasty requires the why, and that difference drives his in-season behavior. Four weeks is the magic mark where production to date becomes as predictive as preseason ADP; before that he is very slow to pivot, and ADP retains some weight all the way through week 16. On waivers he spends aggressively and early rather than optimizing bids, and reserves the last bench spots for players who will resolve within two weeks. [[Matt Waldman]] noted the redraft version of the talent-over-need principle in practice: in a long-running experts keeper league he kept no running backs and still had his season carried by [[Raheem Mostert]], available ridiculously late because managers discount older backs.
