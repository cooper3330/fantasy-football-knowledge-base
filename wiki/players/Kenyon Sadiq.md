# Kenyon Sadiq

## Expert Takes

- 2026-04-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-06]]) [Dynasty]: on Oregon TE Kenyon Sadiq's 4.3 speed, Waldman calls him a good, exciting prospect who gets excellent separation on whip routes and against safeties on deep seams, but flags mental/technical rawness -- there are reps where he isn't processing or playing fast enough and can't even beat linebackers up the seam, plus catch-point lapses that aren't habitual but recur while he's still learning. Waldman expects him used as a situational chunk-play threat as a rookie rather than a featured piece, likening his Year One outlook to [[Elijah Arroyo]]'s. Asked if Sadiq is a top-five talent as a move tight end, Waldman says absolutely, and would go as far as top three -- but that talent grade doesn't translate to a 'gangbuster' Year One starting role given the mental and technical loose ends still to tie up.
- 2026-04-16 — According to Dalton Wasserman (guest draft analyst, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: [[Kenyon Sadiq]] is projected mid-first-round, but Wasserman (33rd on his big board, right at the 1st/2nd-round line) and Chris Harris both flagged him as a 'Kyle Pitts' risk -- big combine, athletic tools, but only a single, statistically unremarkable college season behind him. He's not in the same class as recent elite college tight ends [[Brock Bowers]], [[Colston Loveland]] or [[Tyler Warren]]. Route running still grades stiff, but his seam speed is real and he's a willing, aggressive blocker; Wasserman would deploy him early as more of a flexed-out move piece than a true in-line Y, floating Baltimore as a scheme fit to replace [[Isaiah Likely]] alongside [[Mark Andrews]]. Despite the tools-over-production profile, Wasserman still expects Sadiq to go in the first 12 picks of dynasty rookie drafts as the class's consensus TE1, mainly because the running back class is thin beyond Jeremiah Love and the top three receivers (Makai Lemon, Jordyn Tyson, Carnell Tate) don't push him out of the first round.
- 2026-04-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-27]]) [Dynasty]: Sadiq's landing spot with the Jets is bad. Athletically he has real traits -- speed and big-time catch ability -- but across the board his hands, blocking and route running grade worse than incumbent [[Mason Taylor]], whom Waldman calls a legit starting-caliber tight end who battled poor quarterback play under Justin Fields; Waldman thinks Geno Smith should help Taylor more than Sadiq. The bigger issue is scheme fit: if the Jets lean on 12 personnel, Taylor is the better blocker and Jeremy Ruckert may be the best all-around combo piece, squeezing Sadiq's early role, and at Oregon he looked slow reading the field -- a problem against NFL-level speed of processing. Waldman fears the Jets 'inadvertently turned Sadiq into a gadget player' by drafting him in round one without a clear role, and predicts that if the team struggles with Sadiq making mistakes, his role gets scaled back and he gets scapegoated. Waldman calls him one of two boom-bust early-round rookie tight ends this class, with real bust risk as strong as the upside.
- 2026-08-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-07]]): Sadiq, who had hernia surgery in the spring, has suffered a training-camp setback and will reportedly miss 'a couple weeks' -- which, with camp running only to about the 29th, wipes out most of the remaining camp for a rookie tight end. Harris calls this a real downgrade, noting that negative news leaking out during camp (as opposed to staying quiet) is itself a signal worth weighting, and says outright that Sadiq cannot be drafted as a fantasy starter right now.
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and [[Dan Hanses]] (not tracked, Heed The Call, [[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-20]]): Kenyon Sadiq profiles as a move tight end rather than an in-line/blocking body, which sets up a 12-personnel package with Mason Taylor handling the traditional blocking role and creates some overlap/competition for slot-area work with Omar Cooper Jr. Sadiq has been banged up through camp.
