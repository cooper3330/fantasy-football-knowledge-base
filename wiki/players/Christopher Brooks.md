# Christopher Brooks

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January–April 2024. Team/role are as of that date
> and are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until
> superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and guest **Daniel Harms** (not a
  tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Dynasty]:
  flagged as a depth name worth a late-round dynasty look, Miami's Gus
  Edwards-style contingency if [[Raheem Mostert]]/Jeff Wilson (not a tracked
  page) age out.
  restates the case more strongly — doubts Jeff Wilson is really Miami's third
  back "at this stage," and instead expects Brooks (undrafted out of
  California) to "continue to surprise," calling him "a free square." Draws a
  size/movement comparison to [[Braelon Allen]]: watching Allen's tape at
  Wisconsin, Waldman kept thinking about what Brooks might have done with
  similar draft capital if he'd played in that offense.

- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: reiterates Brooks, not Jeff Wilson (not tracked), as Miami's real third back — "I think Christopher Brooks is going to continue to surprise, and he's a free square for me." Repeats the [[Braelon Allen]] comp: watching Allen's Wisconsin tape, Waldman kept wondering what Brooks would have done with Allen's draft capital in that same offense.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]) [Dynasty]: Brooks would be better off cut by Miami — 'fuck yes' — because he is not a great fit in a system that wants speed, despite ripping off big runs, making plays as a receiver, contributing on special teams and offering a short-yardage battering-ram dimension. Waldman's comps are Mike Anderson, or a more fully dimensional [[Gus Edwards]] with better receiving chops and more moves. He lists fits: Cleveland if [[Nick Chubb]] does not work out and they tire of overpriced veterans, or Tennessee as the third back to eventually pair with [[Tyjae Spears]] once [[Tony Pollard]] moves on. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) corroborates from multiple Dolphins beat writers who consider him a fantastic player stuck in the wrong offense and behind better-fitting backs; Harris floats Detroit, which Waldman waves off because they already have [[Sione Vaki]].
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): Brooks is his second monitor-from-afar back, having taken a concussion in the preseason game Waldman watched. The scouting image: he is an elephant among road runners, trying to disguise himself in a road runner outfit — it does not work, but put him on a team that wants an elephant and he will be the best elephant you can get. [[Brandon Angelo]] agreed and named the fits: Baltimore, Detroit if [[David Montgomery]] goes down, and Pittsburgh if [[Najee Harris]] goes down given the injury already in that backfield. A pure archetype-fit call rather than a redraft recommendation.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Going Deep)]]): in theory Green Bay is a great fit — Brooks catches the ball well and runs with power, giving the offense what [[A.J. Dillon]] was supposed to give it, with maybe a bit more versatility between the tackles. In practice Waldman is skeptical about opportunity: this may be the GM adding a player he liked while the coaching staff stays invested in [[Marshawn Lloyd]] and Emanuel Wilson. If so, Waldman sees a realistic path where Brooks is stuck on the practice squad, never comes off it, gets cut and is labelled 'just a guy' by the league. He frames it as a talent-versus-opportunity split rather than a knock on the player.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Film and Theory)]]) [Dynasty]: Brooks is his running example of a good player waiting on an opportunity — cut by Miami despite broad agreement that he had talent, and intriguing on tape when healthy. He is a theoretical scheme fit in Green Bay but is not a roster-worthy add yet: the questions are whether he gets promoted to the active roster at all and, if promoted, whether he takes snaps with Marshawn Lloyd, Emanuel Wilson and [[Josh Jacobs]] ahead of him. Waldman's stated trigger is preemptive — three or four weeks in, after Lloyd fumbles repeatedly, or Wilson fails to read creases, or Jacobs gets hurt, you add him free and may end up with an end-of-year starter. His caution is the flip side: do not fall in love, because Brooks may be on two more teams before the year is out and may never get his shot.
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): Brooks is moving up Green Bay's depth chart. He is getting more play than Emanuel Wilson, and although Wilson took more of the two-minute-drill opportunities, the fact that Brooks is imposing himself in that role at all suggests the staff sees him as their number-two back down the stretch and will let him keep competing for it. Waldman expects him to end up the direct backup to [[Josh Jacobs]], while leaving room for other backs to hold ancillary roles.
- 2024-11-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-18]]): Waldman is 'feeling' the idea that Brooks is what the Packers thought they had in [[A.J. Dillon]], with the caveat that he does not think the Packers themselves are there yet — he expects them to see Brooks as a good bet to compete for a roster spot again next year rather than as a solution. His forward-looking line: Brooks will be in the league for a long time if he stays healthy, whether in Green Bay or elsewhere.
- 2024-11-22 — According to Adam Harstad, untracked co-host ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]) [Dynasty]: Harstad added Brooks the morning of recording with the roster spot freed by [[Dak Prescott]], and said he checked himself — was he only doing it because he knows [[Matt Waldman]] likes him? — then pulled up the Football Guys depth charts and found Brooks legitimately is the highest-placed backup running back available in his leagues. He called him a bit of a meme but genuinely one of the best adds right now on depth-chart position and likely availability. Waldman agreed and gave the comp: Brooks is basically [[Gus Edwards]] with more versatility.
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]): fuck it as a standalone playoff add, but monitor him closely as the handcuff. Bob Harris (not tracked) read out his carry line — 3, 0, 0, 4, 0, 4, 2, 0, 4, 3 — as evidence there is no standalone role behind [[Josh Jacobs]]. Waldman agrees on the volume but says Green Bay media calls for more Brooks are growing, they like the kid, and he is making the most of what he gets. If Jacobs went down, Waldman does not believe [[Marshawn Lloyd]] simply steps in and tears it up; he expects a Brooks and Emanuel Wilson one-two punch and would not be surprised if Brooks emerged as the lead back. Harris added that he keeps looking good on tape and only wishes he would get an opportunity.
- 2026-05-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-28 (Going Deep)]]) [Dynasty]: of Green Bay's backfield options, Brooks has the best chance at a heavier workload if [[Josh Jacobs]] misses time -- the team let Emmanuel Wilson go over him, which Angelo took as a signal even if some of it was price-driven. Brooks is a strong pass protector and pass catcher who decelerates well for his size and 'will run through you,' but he doesn't offer the speed element the offense needs. Angelo floated a [[Marshawn Lloyd]]/Brooks committee as workable if Lloyd 'sees the light' in camp, but said the more likely outcome if Jacobs is out for real is Green Bay bringing in a veteran like [[James Connor]] rather than leaning on Brooks alone.
