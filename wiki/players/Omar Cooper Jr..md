# Omar Cooper Jr.

## Expert Takes

- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Cooper Jr. projects as a lower-ceiling, high-floor player teams will specifically scheme for -- Angelo compared him to DJ Moore, saying 'you're going to have a plan for him... he's the energy giver, the energy bunny of an offense.' Waldman likes him in the range just after the Tate/Lemon tier and noted teams picking in the late teens could vie for him given how hard his brand of tough, physical football is to find; the floor is durable even without a huge ceiling, per Waldman, describing him as a safe pick for a long career if not a superstar.
- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman pushes back on the popular [[Deebo Samuel]] comp for Cooper as too literal -- Cooper is at least 20 pounds lighter than Samuel's playing weight and isn't likely to add that much mass, so the comparison only holds stylistically. Cooper is physical after the catch and athletic at the catch point like Samuel, but unlike Samuel he has real upside as a man-coverage route runner; he's not yet a refined press technician but already has releases sharp enough to bait defenders. If he adds 5-10 pounds and keeps developing route skills, Waldman's ceiling comp is a Roddy White-style 1B receiver -- six straight 1,150-yard, 7-TD seasons alongside a true alpha.
- 2026-03-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-31]]): Cooper (Indiana, 69/937/13 in 2025, 6'0'/199 lbs, 4.42 forty, 37-inch vertical) is a slot-heavy prospect (79.6% of RP sample snaps in the slot) who Harmon has come around on significantly after initially doubting the '19th overall prospect' buzz -- he now grades him a real late-first-to-second-round talent. RP numbers back it up: 82.6% success rate vs. zone (tied with [[Makai Lemon]], sandwiched between [[Jalen Waddle]] and [[Chris Olave]] since 2021), 71% vs. man, 95% on slants, and elite after-catch data -- in space on 53% of catches, broke a tackle on 35% of those and went down on first contact on only 35%, which Harmon says he has not charted at that level since [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Malik Nabers]]. Weak spot is press coverage (20th percentile, 55%) tied to inefficient footwork/throttle-down at route breaks -- Cooper told Harmon in a pre-draft interview his own worst route is the curl (63.2% success), matching Harmon's charting exactly, and named the out route (17.1% of his routes, 80% success) as his best, also matching the data. Lance Zierlein's Deebo Samuel comp is discussed and partially endorsed -- Harmon agrees on the after-catch and route-running fit but stresses Cooper is not the bruiser/high-rush-volume version Deebo was, cautioning that Deebo comps get handed out too liberally across draft classes (cites [[Treylon Burks]] as a prior ill-fitting one).
- 2026-04-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman ranked Cooper his WR8, lower than some boards (one analyst reportedly has him WR2), because he pushed back on the popular Deebo Samuel comp -- Cooper is roughly 20 pounds lighter and a better pure route runner but won't be the same ball-in-space difference-maker. His preferred comp is former Falcon Roddy White, a physical route runner rather than a space-creation weapon. Waldman graded Cooper similarly to Jayden Reed and projected him as a strong complementary receiver capable of an 800-1,100-yard season in a good offense, with real ceiling above that if his landing spot is right, calling him a good but not elite fantasy proposition.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-07 (Going Deep with Brandon Angelo)]]) [Dynasty]: Cooper Jr. is on Waldman's 2026 do-not-draft list. He said he actually prefers Cooper Jr. as a player, but that his rookie-draft ADP of roughly 108-109 is too steep given the Jets' bottom-five offensive environment and uncertain coaching future -- owner Woody Johnson's instability could see Aaron Glenn fired and a new offensive-minded staff 'clean house' at receiver, especially with 2027 blue-chip prospects Jeremiah Smith and Cam Coleman potentially entering the fold. Waldman doesn't see a path for Cooper Jr. to hold long-term value with [[Garrett Wilson]] entrenched atop the depth chart.
- 2026-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-23]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon called Omar Cooper Jr. a talented first-round prospect who is 'almost completely undiscussed' and possibly the cheapest first-round rookie receiver draftable this year, but expects a limited role in year one behind [[Garrett Wilson]] in a crowded Jets room -- he'd rather bet on Cooper for a hypothetical 2027 redraft than for 2026, while saying he's 'willing to be wrong' given how much he likes the prospect.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]): Cooper apparently isn't going to start, which is discouraging for his rookie-year outlook after a strong Indiana college story; both hosts put him in 'prove me wrong' mode rather than writing him off, noting some rookies just need more time to put it together.
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and [[Dan Hanses]] (not tracked, Heed The Call, [[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-20]]): Omar Cooper Jr. profiles as a true slot receiver (he played slot at Indiana) and has reportedly been competing with -- and per camp buzz, possibly losing the starting slot job to -- veteran Isaiah Williams, creating an awkward fit alongside move-TE Kenyon Sadiq in 12 personnel. Harmon graded him more a 'high Round 2' talent than his actual late-Round-1 draft slot, coming from a limited Indiana scheme with a likely slow rookie learning curve and probable slow start, but praises his work ethic: in a pre-draft interview, Cooper was the only prospect in Harmon's career to ask which of his own charted routes graded worst (Harmon told him it was stopping on curls vs. man, which Cooper said he had already identified as a weakness to fix). Harmon says he would bet on Cooper 'eventually figuring it out.'
- 2026-09-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-09-01]]): a good possession-style receiver at Indiana, but he's gotten almost zero buzz for a high pick, positive or negative. Co-host James Koh (untracked) notes Cooper briefly lost the slot job to Isaiah Williams in the first preseason game (seemingly a reward for a strong camp) before reclaiming the starting slot role in game two -- though he still leaves the field in two-tight-end sets, currently manned by Jeremy Ruckert and Mason Taylor rather than an injured [[Kenyan Sadiq]]. Harmon compares Cooper's floor to [[Khalil Shakir]] -- a better real-life player than fantasy asset who wins in the short area and breaks tackles after the catch -- but jokes Cooper could end up the Jets' fifth-best pass catcher behind [[Garrett Wilson]], [[Mason Taylor]] and [[Breece Hall]], calling him unlikely to be fantasy-relevant this year.
- 2026-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-09-15]]): Cooper made a big play to set up the Jets' opening touchdown drive against Tennessee but suffered a high-ankle sprain and had to be carted off, leaving him week-to-week. Both Harris and Funston flagged his likely absence as a key reason to speculatively add Adonai Mitchell for Week 2.
- 2026-10-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-10-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Cooper dominated the Jets' receiving numbers and is worth a streaming add, per Waldman, who praised his run-after-catch ability and ball-tracking on a deep shot he had to adjust for after Geno Smith underthrew him. Waldman rejected the idea that subtracting the big play erases the performance, calling that reasoning 'a fallacy.' He slots Cooper into the same bucket as Jeremy Bernard: an extension of the run game who'll pick up a couple of intermediate plays and an occasional deep shot, meaning a genuinely volatile weekly role even as the sample grows. Bob was more cautious about trusting the Jets' offense that deep but agreed the performance opens the door to more opportunity.
- 2026-10-19 — According to Patrick Doherty (Rotoworld, untracked guest) and Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-10-19]]): Cooper caught a 50-yard touchdown off a blown coverage, tracking a deep ball 'like a center fielder' through traffic on a throw [[Geno Smith]] had to launch roughly 60 yards -- a play Doherty called genuinely impressive given Cooper's very limited rookie snaps so far (about five quarters of NFL action total). Both agreed the ceiling on his role is capped less by talent than by the numbers game -- once [[Adonai Mitchell]] and the Will Fuller situation resolve, the Jets will suddenly have three receivers competing for targets, and Geno Smith 'is probably not going to support three.' Doherty's bottom line: usable as a desperation WR4/second flex in deep leagues, with real but unproven upside.
- 2026-10-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-10-20]]): [[Omar Cooper Jr.]] broke out in Week 6 -- 7 targets, 6 catches, 103 yards, a touchdown, 3.55 yards per route run, and a 37% air-yard share -- in a game Harmon hand-charted and came away 'even more impressed' with than his first viewing. Unlike his slot-heavy pre-draft profile (79.6% slot, 4.4% backfield), he lined up off-ball on 73% of snaps here (67% behind the line of scrimmage, 55% slot) and saw real 12-personnel usage (22% of snaps). His production came almost entirely in two-or-fewer-receiver sets: a perfectly-run post route for a touchdown, and a second TD negated by offensive pass interference on veteran [[Tim Patrick]], on a designed pivot route Cooper smartly adjusted back inside against man coverage once [[Geno Smith]] scrambled. Harmon flagged two deviations from his prospect profile worth tracking: he won mostly on in-breaking routes here despite grading best on deep out-cuts pre-draft, and he got almost no after-the-catch work (only 29% of catches in space) despite that being a signature strength coming out. Both hosts praised his toughness and contested-catch ability (70% contested catch rate as a prospect), and Harmon expects [[Garrett Wilson]] to keep playing X/condensed-X while Cooper works more as the Z opposite him once [[Adonai Mitchell]] returns, with real chemistry already built with Smith on scramble-drill shot plays. Harmon called him one of the most impressive prospects and people he's ever interviewed and views him as an early dynasty riser in an otherwise weak 2026 rookie WR class.
- 2026-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Cooper was activated off IR and immediately went 6-for-103 with another touchdown called back on a holding penalty, playing over 85% of the Jets' routes and sliding into the clear number-two role behind Garrett Wilson. Guest Scott Fish (untracked) makes him his number-two waiver add (he says it was neck-and-neck with his number one), partly because a losing Jets team should be throwing more -- Harris pushes back on that specific logic, noting that 'if losing in games were a reason to like fantasy players, we'd like a bunch of different players that way' and calling the game-script argument backwards from how it's usually deployed. Harris still rates Cooper highly as a $2-bid pickup, while cautioning that no ancillary weapon in Geno Smith's offense is likely to be worth much on a weekly basis.
- 2026-10-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-10-26 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman says to add Omar Cooper Jr. off waivers but not trust him as a weekly starter against tougher defenses yet. Breaking down the tape against Miami, he notes one long gain came off a completely broken coverage and another off a defender narrowly missing an angle to undercut the pass, with the touchdown essentially 'a gimme' -- most of Cooper's production in back-to-back weeks has come off similarly fluky, big-play scenarios rather than consistently skillful reps. Waldman expects regression on the lucky plays but still likes him as a promising young receiver whose skillful production should eventually replace the lucky kind; for now he's only a confident start in four- or five-receiver formats. He also notes Garrett Wilson was visibly 'politely lobbying' on the sideline for more targets during the same game, and Waldman admits he'd personally like to see more Garrett Wilson and less Omar Cooper Jr.
- 2026-10-27 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-10-27]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Cooper's first play from scrimmage against the Jets was a 60-yard catch on a badly blown coverage -- Harris: 'they did not cover him' -- and he later scored on a designed trick-formation touchdown (lined up at tailback, Braelon Allen at tight end) out of the backfield. Harris isn't down on Cooper but warns his big-play totals the last two weeks (vs. the Patriots and Dolphins) are inflated by coverage busts rather than won matchups: 'he's a third weapon at best on a pretty bad offense... reconcile yourself to the idea that there are 50 different guys who can be a wide receiver three in any given week.' He is encouraged the Jets are specifically scheming Cooper red-zone touchdowns, which bodes well for a rookie, and notes Cooper played far ahead of a returning Adonai Mitchell (18 routes, barely targeted) -- though it was Mitchell's first game back from a hand injury, so that could change.
