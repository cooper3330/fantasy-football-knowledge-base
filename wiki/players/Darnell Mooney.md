# Darnell Mooney

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): signs
  with Atlanta for $39M total value, with the fully-guaranteed portion
  matching [[Saquon Barkley]]'s Eagles deal ($37.8M) — used at length by the
  hosts as the marquee example of how far the wide-receiver replacement-level
  price has risen relative to running backs. Koh calls it borderline
  collusion against running backs; Harmon's framing of the market: "you hit
  free agency, and you're a guy that should be on the field in 11
  personnel... you're going to get $11, $12, $13 million a year," while a
  running back has to be a Saquon Barkley-level talent to command the same
  money. On Mooney himself: "I actually kind of like the signing by the
  Falcons" — a "nice player" who fits alongside [[Drake London]].

- 2024-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-21]]): projects Mooney as a flanker rather than a true slot receiver despite Chicago playing him in the slot ~60% of his snaps in 2023 (~50% in 2022) — his best season, a 2021 1,055-yard, 140-target breakout, came when he played outside about 55% of the time. Harmon: "I don't see him as a slot guy that's going to win on slant routes," instead projecting a "vertical slot" or speed-Z role on roughly a 60-40 outside/slot split, leaning on his 4.38 speed. Calls him a good fit next to [[Drake London]] but still just a WR3-caliber piece ("a 3") in Atlanta's room, not a true X.
- 2024-07-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-04]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman noted Mooney signed for real money in Atlanta and views him as a viable second receiving option behind [[Drake London]], especially since the depth behind them ([[Casey Washington]], Ray-Ray McCloud, Scotty Miller) offers little proven production.
- 2024-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-25]]): Mooney profiles best as a flanker rather than an outside X, described as a decent man-coverage beater who can kick inside to the slot and function as a vertical threat on one side of the formation while [[Drake London]] works in-cutting routes underneath. Koh calls him 'fine' and 'a decent three' with a 1,000-yard season on his resume, but groups him with [[Rondale Moore]] as a move Atlanta made that did not add an impact player. Neither host sees him changing the fact that London is essentially the only real outside receiver on the roster.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): asked whether Mooney is the difference maker in Atlanta's offense, Waldman said right now yes. He describes Mooney as a mashup of [[Adam Thielen]] and [[Stefon Diggs]] — used over the middle the way Minnesota used Diggs, and on the outside on certain trust throws the way they used Thielen — and calls him probably the best route runner on the team, with [[Kirk Cousins]] doing a great job of targeting him. The one head-scratcher: Cousins is throwing Mooney back-shoulder balls up the boundary, which really want a receiver with length and size, and Mooney did not come through on those plays while [[Drake London]] is not used in that capacity at all.
- 2024-11-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]): Waldman offered Mooney as the change-of-scenery archetype that the market routinely misprices. Mooney was a late-round pick in a lot of 2024 drafts because the consensus was that he had already failed out in Chicago and could not be a real WR2, while the Falcons were plainly saying he was their WR2 and behaving accordingly. His broader point: the most common source of genuinely useful free agents is older players getting opportunities whom you have already seen prove it, or players getting a second chance in a new city.
- 2024-11-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-29]]): Mooney is the single most surprising name on Harmon's list, 14th in receiving yards at 711 — one more than [[Drake London]] on far fewer targets and receptions, purely on shot plays. The explanation is inverted usage: in his 1,000-yard 2021 Bears season his best routes were nines (65.6%), corners (71.4%) and outs (72%), yet Matt Nagy's staff ran him disproportionately on slants, screens, flats and curls, and his 67.5% vs man was only 44th percentile. Atlanta asks for the deep out-breakers he is actually good at, and [[Kirk Cousins]] will fire into a shallow-corner window against two-high; Harmon cites 2.7 yards per route run vs two-high looks. He argues the cascading benefit is to [[Drake London]], since the Falcons lacked any pure vertical speed last year, opening crossers underneath. Projection: 800-1,000 yards sustainable, with Harmon guessing about 900 for Mooney next year while London pushes 1,200-1,300 — he still calls London the clearly better receiver and true number one. James Koh credits the coaching staff for identifying the trait.
- 2024-12-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-10]]): asked whether Mooney is Atlanta's number one given he leads [[Drake London]] in yards 873 to 866, Harmon says false. He calls Mooney awesome this year and credits the Falcons for scheming both receivers into matchup advantages — everyone gets slot reps, everyone gets shots down the field — and Mooney's supporting numbers are strong, a 100.0 passer rating when targeted with five touchdowns against two interceptions, versus London's six touchdowns and eight interceptions when targeted. But Harmon still holds London as more important to the passing game, and says that is about how he views London rather than shade at Mooney. Koh agrees while noting Mooney has closed the gap far more than anyone anticipated entering 2024.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-19]]): the volatility people fear from a rookie quarterback was already priced in — Mooney went 140 yards against Minnesota and zero against Las Vegas in consecutive weeks with [[Kirk Cousins]] under center. Harmon says Mooney surprised him with his work on deeper out-breaking patterns and that he and Cousins connected on some of those during the good stretch of Cousins' season, which makes him a plausible fit for [[Michael Penix Jr.]]'s charted strengths on outs and corners. He liked the Week 16 Giants matchup for both Atlanta receivers, pointing to [[Rashad Bateman]] tearing up that secondary down the field.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-19 (NFL QB Lunacy)]]): Mooney is the pass catcher hurt most by the switch to [[Michael Penix Jr.]]. Mooney's value is in the timing passing game — deep comebacks, deep outs — and that is precisely where Waldman expects Penix to struggle earliest, because that timing and placement takes reps together the two haven't had. Compounding it, Mooney is playing through a foot and an Achilles issue: cleared last week but limited in practice this week, so it is clearly still bothering him. Waldman calls him a boom-bust type and expects fewer looks to Mooney and more to the bigger targets [[Drake London]] and [[Kyle Pitts]].
- 2024-12-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-24]]): Mooney is the receiver whose profile most obviously matches [[Michael Penix Jr.]]'s arm — Harmon was impressed that Penix had the juice to drive deep out-breaking routes from the far hash to Mooney, the exact throw class Penix was projected to live on. The caveat Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) both raise is chemistry: Mooney had built real timing with [[Kirk Cousins]] during the middle of the season, so the Penix transition may not be a straight upgrade for him even if it is for the offense. Koh notes Mooney publicly said he had not built chemistry with Penix yet, and that if the debut is what no chemistry looks like, that is encouraging.
- 2025-02-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-21]]): Mooney's Atlanta season was a role-fit success, not a fluke. Harmon's point is that Zac Robinson's offense finally used him on downfield out-breaking routes, which were his best routes in his 2021 Reception Perception sample from Chicago, whereas Matt Nagy had wasted him on short layup and gimmick concepts. He ran 42.1% of his routes from the slot, part of a Falcons distribution where nobody exceeded 50% but [[Drake London]] (37.2%) and Ray-Ray McCloud (46.5%) were all above 30% — the motion-heavy, alignment-flexible design Harmon says makes both this and the Tampa offense dynamic. James Koh (not tracked) called him an incredibly important, pivotal player, citing 106 targets, 64 receptions and nearly 1,000 yards after a career that had drifted into dime-a-dozen territory. Harmon calls him a nice long-term running mate for London, with the caveat that Atlanta has almost no cap room and badly needs a blocking slot receiver behind them.
- 2025-07-30 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-30]]): Mooney's shoulder injury is a red flag — he is out several weeks, which Koh reads as not the minor variety, and a shoulder matters enormously for a receiver even if he takes fewer hits than a running back. Koh calls him a good second-tier WR2 and says Atlanta has nothing behind him but randoms (Ray-Ray McCloud, [[Casey Washington]], [[D.J. Chark]]). [[Matt Harmon]] agrees he is critical, calling him not just a role player but a genuinely good player last year — the vertical guy who drew targets and was efficient on downfield looks, deeper out-breakers and comebacks. Harmon's structural worry is that Atlanta weaponized everyone from the slot last year (McCloud 46.5%, London 37%, Mooney 42.1%, [[Kyle Pitts]] 39% per Fantasy Points data), and without Mooney they cannot keep moving people around, since Chark is not part of that.
- 2025-08-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Mooney's shoulder injury has cost him all of training camp and Harris says 'we still don't really know' whether he will be ready for Week 1. In his absence [[Casey Washington]] has run with the starters at the X spot Mooney occupies, and Harris frames continued Mooney absence as the main path to Washington getting real playing time.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Mooney is back at practice after a shoulder injury suffered essentially on the first day of training camp, and the coaching staff's line that they hoped to get him back at some point this year now looks pessimistic — Harris says teams have no incentive to tell the truth and you only know when you know. It seems Mooney avoided an injury that will cost him a lot of games, but whether he plays Week 1 is unclear, and given where he was drafted you can afford to be conservative with him in lineups even if he is active.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-04]]): a disappointing and 'weird' season — the coaching staff has publicly called Mooney out at points, a hamstring injury has hampered him, and Harmon says something has been off in the room all year, noting Atlanta cut third receiver Ray-Ray McCloud and fired its wide receivers coach. James Koh (not a tracked expert) says Atlanta gets essentially nothing from a second receiver and blames injuries first. Harmon counters that he almost does not care: spreading it out has not worked, [[Michael Penix Jr.]] cannot consistently elevate two receivers, and the offense functions when the ball goes to [[Drake London]]. He classes Mooney with [[Kyle Pitts]] as a role player.
