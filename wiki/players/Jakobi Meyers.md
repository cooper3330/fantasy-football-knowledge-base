# Jakobi Meyers

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2024-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-01]]): Meyers is awesome and badly underrated — a really good player who will command volume. Harmon argues he would be the second-best receiver on the Broncos roster and maybe the best, is probably a better NFL receiver than anybody in Kansas City including [[Marquise Brown]], and that the 2023 Chiefs could have used him; he concedes [[Ladd McConkey]] might be better but is a rookie. Meyers is a core reason Harmon ranks Las Vegas as the best receiver room in the division despite having no real third receiver — though he also projects the Raiders last in pass attempts, with [[Davante Adams]], Meyers, [[Brock Bowers]] and [[Michael Mayer]] all needing targets in a run-heavy Antonio Pierce / Luke Getsy offense.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): asked to choose between [[Brock Bowers]] and Meyers, Waldman said he likes Meyers but loves Bowers, while insisting there is room for both to hold roles in the Las Vegas offense without cutting into [[Davante Adams]]. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) called Meyers perennially overlooked, pointing to his WR24 finish the prior season, and relayed that Kirk Morrison — who spent significant time at Raiders camp — said not to forget Meyers because he is a very good player, and that Adams calls him 'the businessman' because he does the job.
- 2024-10-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-03]]): asked by James Koh whether Meyers can be a true number one if [[Davante Adams]] is dealt, Harmon says he likes Meyers as a rock-solid veteran who can absorb a ton of targets, but he is 'definitely not a number one wide receiver' and 'certainly not a coverage dictator' — not a vertical threat, and a player you still have to scheme open. He has run roughly 50% of his routes from the slot in 2024, so Harmon sees the interesting counter as mixing alignments with [[Brock Bowers]] (one off the ball, one on the line as the widest receiver) rather than asking Meyers to replace Adams's vertical role outright.
- 2024-10-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-17]]): Meyers is the only Raider receiver Harmon has any confidence in after the [[Davante Adams]] trade — 'a pro's pro,' a dirty-work, steady-Eddie player he has long liked — but emphatically not a No. 1. Splits are about 60.5% outside / 38.9% slot, and Harmon notes he cannot simply slide inside because [[Brock Bowers]] is functionally the Raiders' slot receiver. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) supplied the with/without-Adams numbers: with Adams, an 82.4% catch rate on 5.7 targets and 4.7 catches for ~51 yards per game; without Adams, 9.5 targets but only 5.5 catches, a 57.9% catch rate. Volume nearly doubled while efficiency collapsed. Harmon called the O'Connell/Minshew-led Raiders 'not a real offense' and possibly the worst skill group in the NFL, and floated trading Meyers — he is a free agent after 2025 and would fit the Chiefs, though a division rival makes it unlikely.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]) [Dynasty]: Meyers at 11 million per year is the archetype Harmon wants teams to buy — a receiver coming off a rookie deal who does not command top-of-market money — and he called the Raiders' deal unbelievable value at the time, arguing Meyers' agent failed him given New England spent comparable money on [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]] and [[Allen Lazard]] got 11 million from the Jets. Both are former undrafted players, Harmon noted, but nobody with eyeballs could watch the film and call Lazard and Meyers the same vicinity of player. He named [[Darnell Mooney]]'s 13 million with Atlanta as the same kind of win, and used Meyers as the reference point for what [[Elijah Moore]] should cost.
- 2025-02-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-04]]): Meyers is the other Raider who fits the Jordan Matthews / Jeremy Kerley role in a Chip Kelly offense — first read on play action, worked into space on quick-hitting concepts underneath. Harmon pushes back on the idea that Meyers is only an underneath slot, calling him a genuine all-around receiver who was a productive outside player for Las Vegas in 2024 and who also thrives in the short area from the slot. Koh (not a tracked expert) agrees Kelly will put him in position to pick up chunk yardage. The shared caveat is roster context: Harmon says the Raiders have [[Jakobi Meyers]] and [[Brock Bowers]] and 'nothing else', no quarterback, and no lid-lifting boundary receiver to open the middle of the field, and notes Kelly's history of producing career-high seasons for whoever occupies these roles.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'feel' Meyers over [[Ricky Pearsall]] at Underdog WR45 — Waldman grants Pearsall has far more upside on ability but argues people reach for upside too early, and that Meyers is the solid player with a defined role who is good for that ADP with a little more ceiling than the price implies. Untracked co-host Bob Harris agreed and pulled his fantasy finishes: WR32 in 2022, WR21 in 2023, WR29 in 2024 while missing games — 'underrated every year, easily overlooked'. Harris expects him to be the Raiders' de facto WR2 because [[Brock Bowers]] remains their WR1 in his estimation.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): Meyers is a perennially underrated fantasy asset and should not be underrated in 2025 drafts with [[Geno Smith]] now in Las Vegas. The specific market inefficiency he flagged is that Meyers goes at a WR4 price and produces at WR2 levels, as he almost always has going back to his New England years. The upgrade at quarterback puts the offense on schedule and should help the run game too.
- 2025-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-21]]): Harmon calls Meyers badly underpaid at $11 million per year — barely more than [[Josh Palmer]], the same as [[Allen Lazard]] got two offseasons ago, and less than [[Darius Slayton]] — and says he would not be surprised if Meyers makes a stink about the contract, or if the Raiders, now two regimes removed from the McDaniels group that signed him, decline to pay and shop him. He rates Meyers as possibly better than [[Khalil Shakir]] at $13.3 million. James Koh (co-host, not tracked) says Meyers is the Raiders' only reliable receiver and the rest of the depth chart looks like a created Madden roster. Harmon's takeaway: with Meyers and [[Brock Bowers]], Las Vegas still needs a true X very badly, ideally one with vertical juice, and he likes them to sign [[Tyler Lockett]] as a speed flanker.
- 2025-04-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-29]]): Meyers is on the last year of his deal and is a previous-regime holdover under the new Chip Kelly staff, but Harmon does not see [[Jack Bech]] as pushing him out — Meyers is not small, has posted 70%-plus success rates vs man coverage in multiple New England seasons and has been more useful outside than inside for Las Vegas, because separation and blocking are the strengths of his game. The caveat Harmon flags is that Meyers gives essentially no pop after the catch, which is exactly the trait Bech adds to the room.
- 2025-06-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-04]]): Tashaun Reed of The Athletic reports Chip Kelly has been moving receivers around a lot in OTAs, with Meyers and [[Jack Bech]] lining up both outside and in the slot and Kelly saying the goal is to 'remain amorphous'. Harmon supports the Meyers part of it with charting: 1.91 yards per route run when lined up out wide last year, which he calls pretty good, and notes Meyers was mostly not a slot receiver in 2024, so he can absolutely play outside. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is more skeptical of amorphous offenses generally, saying it has really only worked once — the Patriots with Gronkowski and Tom Brady — and Harmon frames the split as their standard dynamic, Koh naturally skeptical and Harmon more prone to excitement. Koh calls Meyers a highly productive player in a suddenly interesting Raiders weapon group.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he agrees with Bob Harris's framing of Meyers as fantasy's most overlooked eighth-round pick. The evaluation is a good zone player who can win some man coverage and offers real contested-catch skill, and Waldman projects roughly 80 receptions on that target base for about 900 yards and six touchdowns — enough to land at the low end of the top 24 receivers. Harris notes Meyers has been on the field enough over the last two years to post combined WR21-ish numbers. Waldman groups him with [[Jack Bech]] as unsung rather than underrated pieces [[Geno Smith]] can work with.
- 2025-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-25]]): Meyers is proof of concept that these flanker-slot types are not slot-only players — he was in the slot on just 33 percent of his snaps in 2024 and has been a great man-coverage beater across multiple seasons of Reception Perception charting. Harmon calls him a very good player and pencils him alongside [[Jack Bech]] as a flanker-slot piece in a Raiders room that also needs a true vertical X, which is where [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]] comes in. Koh (not tracked) opens the segment expecting Meyers to be a big-time flanker-slot for Las Vegas.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]]: Meyers is going to be really productive and get a ton of targets, and is one of the better players in the league at his position — but that position is the slot and the intermediate middle of the field, which is exactly where Las Vegas already has a logjam ([[Brock Bowers]], [[Michael Mayer]] in 12 personnel, and [[Ashton Jeanty]] taking slot snaps). Nguyen also flagged the business risk: Meyers wants a long-term contract, the Raiders appear unwilling to give him a huge deal, and Nguyen suspects rookie [[Jack Bech]] was drafted partly as an eventual Meyers replacement if no agreement is reached.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): news leaked that Meyers requested a trade at some point in August over his contract, and Las Vegas responded by signing [[Amari Cooper]]. Harris has not lowered Meyers yet — as far as he knows Meyers took part in training camp, and it is not clear whether he now plans to miss any time. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-27]]): Meyers requested a trade on August 25 and wants either a raise or a move, and Harmon stresses that a trade request means nothing on its own — [[Terry McLaurin]] requested one too and is playing for Washington. Harmon calls his $11M per year 'criminal' for his skill set, arguing he should be in the [[Jerry Jeudy]] / [[Christian Kirk]] $18M range at bare minimum and is closer to a [[Courtland Sutton]] type at $23M; he is currently paid less than [[Tutu Atwell]] and [[Darius Slayton]]. Koh (not tracked) blames his representation. Harmon reports a team had interest but wanted Las Vegas to eat salary, which the Raiders refused. He frames Meyers as blocking [[Jack Bech]], and speculates the refusal to extend is because Bech is the intended power-slot successor in 2026 and beyond — if Meyers is moved, that playing time goes to Bech, not [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]].
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the box score flatters him and even that isn't good — four catches for 32 yards, and it's misleading, because he had almost nothing before garbage time and his back-to-back end-zone targets came in a meaningless fourth quarter. Harris' conclusion covers the whole room: 'hard to love any Raiders receiver.'
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06]]): Waldman is puzzled by the trade on Liam Coen's stated preferences — Meyers is not a speedster either, so Coen now has two 'slow pokes' on the field, and Waldman wonders whether this was Coen's call or the front office's. Angelo reads it as the staff accepting what [[Trevor Lawrence]] actually is and prioritizing sure hands and yards after catch over explosives. Importantly for usage, Coen said publicly that Meyers will be worked in on the boundary rather than the slot, which is why Angelo thinks the market is wrong to suppress [[Parker Washington]] on the news. Both list Meyers alongside Washington as Lawrence's best available options with [[Travis Hunter]] and [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] hurt.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-06]]): the trade from Las Vegas to Jacksonville does move the needle, and Harmon says he feels good about the analysis because Liam Coen confirmed Meyers will start outside with [[Parker Washington]] left in the slot — easier onboarding, since the F receiver in this offense handles all the motions and shifts (the thing Harmon believes made [[Travis Hunter]]'s brain spin). Harmon's case: Jacksonville has struggled with drops and with routes over the middle, and Meyers is 'really good at catching the damn football' on in-breakers. From his week-nine charting notebook: in a two-game 2025 sample Meyers ran a dig on 50% of routes at a 75% success rate and a slant on 13.8% of routes at 81.8%, versus both man and zone; he has cleared a 70% success rate vs man in multiple seasons and had his best year in 2024 as an outside receiver with only 33 slot snaps. Harmon concedes you can quibble about giving up picks for a possible rental. WR coach Edgar Bennett, with the Raiders since 2018, is the connective tissue.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-06]]): after the deadline trade from Las Vegas to Jacksonville, Harris calls Meyers 'awesome' and 'a specific kind of awesome' — open in a phone booth, a completely unexceptional athlete who runs perfect routes and wins in short areas — and says there is 'zero question' he is a better football player than [[Parker Washington]]. Harris argues no receiver should be more plug-and-play: if you can run a seven-yard curl, a post and a shallow corner, you are in the offense. Still, for Week 10 only he ranks Meyers one spot BEHIND Washington on acclimation risk. Guest Ben Gretch (Stealing Signals, not tracked) likes the fit with [[Trevor Lawrence]], who has historically fed slot types (Jamal Agnew, [[Christian Kirk]], [[Evan Engram]]) more than outside men like [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and [[Travis Hunter]], both out; he compares the possible Liam Coen usage to [[Chris Godwin]] in Tampa. Both agree Meyers is above the flex conversation once up to speed.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): Meyers finished 3-41 with a touchdown taken off the board by a false start, but the encouraging part is that when Jacksonville got to the one they called his number, and his catches included a nice sideline gain and a sticks route won against great coverage. The volume is the caveat - he ran only 23 routes to 47 for [[Parker Washington]] and 38 for [[Tim Patrick]] - but Harris says 'I do have confidence that Meyers will play more going forward' as he integrates after the midseason move.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-13]]): Jacksonville traded for Meyers as another middle-of-the-field/intermediate answer to [[Trevor Lawrence]]'s vertical limitations, but Waldman confirms Liam Coen has said Meyers will play more on the outside, which pushes the more valuable slot role to [[Parker Washington]]. [[Chris Harris]] observed the same in the tape — Meyers on the boundary, Washington in the slot — notes Meyers has not yet gotten a full complement of snaps, and ranked Washington ahead of him for Week 11.
