# George Pickens

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): the headline beneficiary of Pittsburgh hiring Arthur Smith as OC. Harmon's specific charted evidence: under Matt Canada in 2022, Pickens ran dig routes on just 7.7% of his snaps and slants on 10.7% — a "static," disconnected route tree Harmon calls emblematic of Canada's broader scheme failure (receivers running unrelated routes to nowhere on opposite sides of the field, no route combinations building off each other). Direct comp: [[Drake London]] ran dig routes on 16.6% and slants on 24.5% of his routes as a rookie under Arthur Smith the same year. Harmon's explicit projection: Pickens gets real volume over the middle of the field for the first time in his career under Smith, and profiles as exactly the kind of "big X receiver who can run over the middle of the field and make plays in space" Smith's offense is built to feature — "the boys on Twitter starving for a George Pickens dig route... you're gonna get it in Arthur Smith's offense, baby."

- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]), a coachability rather than talent read, cited as evidence for how Pittsburgh's organization handles young-player friction: singles out a specific in-season incident — Pickens publicly said postgame he wasn't going to throw a block "because I'm not looking to get hurt," then was visibly throwing blocks again the following week — as the Steelers handling it internally (accountability within the building, not a public callout) rather than letting immaturity become a lasting pattern. Waldman's explicit framing: "he's an immature young man," but the organization coached him up rather than writing him off. See [[Player Development and Coachability]].
- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]): on [[Russell Wilson]] signing with Pittsburgh, calls Pickens "arguably a better player" than [[Courtland Sutton]] and sees real upside in the pairing — "Russell Wilson turning George Pickens into a superstar" is plausible given a receiving room "by a significant margin" more talented than what Wilson had in Denver.

- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): more detail on the charted profile now that [[Russell Wilson]] has signed. Rookie-year success rate vs. zone coverage was a concerning 68% — below the ~70% floor Harmon wants from a starting X receiver — but success rate vs. press was a strong 72.4%, showing real ability to beat press at the line. Overall verdict is deliberately in the middle of the "Pickens truther" vs. "he secretly sucks" debate: an explosive but not always technically sound player, still inconsistent in separation, with some contested-catch numbers inflated by highlight bias. Currently "a really good starting X receiver" when locked in, but volatile both in production and as a person. On the Wilson fit: sees real stylistic overlap, comparing Pickens to the vertical X receivers Wilson has had success with (DK Metcalf, not a tracked page, and [[Courtland Sutton]]) — "somewhere in the middle" of that mold, not quite Metcalf but "definitely better than Courtland Sutton" in Harmon's opinion. Notes Wilson posted a 121.5 passer rating on throws 10+ air yards to receivers last year (3rd-best in the NFL, behind only Kirk Cousins and Dak Prescott), largely built on Sutton's slant-and-go-ball role — if Pickens can fill that same role in Pittsburgh, "Pickens could absolutely go crazy," especially in touchdowns, "but we'll see."

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]), an aside while discussing free-agent [[Mike Williams]]'s meeting with Pittsburgh: calls the fit redundant with Pickens — same contested-catch, X-receiver archetype and route diet — while noting Pickens has "a little bit better separation than people give him credit for."

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on *Reception Perception: The Show* ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-11]]): used as Harmon's own "cautious" player comp for prospect [[Adonai Mitchell]] — flags Pickens as a receiver he's "one foot in, one foot out" on because of inconsistency/volatility in the short-to-intermediate area, though notes that trait was also present, if underrated, in Pickens's own pre-draft profile. Separately, in the same episode's free-agent buy/sell segment, cautions against projecting Pickens for a specific target share in Pittsburgh's Arthur Smith/[[Russell Wilson]]/[[Justin Fields]] offense — notes an Arthur Smith-led passing attack has burned this kind of projection before ("with a better receiver," referencing [[Drake London]]).
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman comps Pickens to Courtland Sutton — a boundary, contested-catch receiver who profiles better paired with Russell Wilson's off-script, ball-in-the-air style than with Justin Fields — and pegs him at a '60/40' rather than true 50/50 shot to handle a true WR1 alpha role in Pittsburgh under new OC Arthur Smith, given his length and ability to win above the rim. He's optimistic on Pickens as long as he isn't drafted as a top-24 receiver, preferring him in the WR17-24 range for redraft, and notes he's finding Pickens around WR29-30 in his own best ball drafts right now, which he considers good value if you're not relying on him for weekly floor.
- 2024-06-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-06]]): Pickens improved from year one to year two across the board -- success rate vs. press 72.4% to 73.9%, vs. man 64.9% to 68.7% -- but his vs.-zone number remains weak (17th percentile, though better than his rookie 68.3%). Harmon calls him an imperfect, still mostly vertical X receiver, comping him to [[Mike Williams]] rather than an elite tier-one and explicitly rejecting the 'secretly elite' vs. 'he's DJ Chark' framing of the discourse. Contested-catch rate was an identical 78.6% in back-to-back seasons -- good, not elite, and Harmon suggests the 'legend' of Pickens' hands exceeds the actual results. His 6.57 YAC/reception is likely inflated by a few big plays (Bengals, Browns, Ravens go-route touchdowns) rather than reflecting a tackle-breaker. Harmon notes Pickens' target share was just 20.8% (31st among WRs) and projects a ceiling closer to Mike Evans' ~23% rather than the 27%+ range of true high-volume WR1s (Tyreek Hill, Keenan Allen, CeeDee Lamb). Co-host James Koh (not tracked) pushed back, arguing 42% of Pickens' routes being deep (nine/post/corner) caps his volume ceiling and should come down toward a more average 35%. Harmon also cites reported effort/engagement issues flagged by the Steelers coaching staff and Pickens' visible frustration on the bench (e.g., the Titans game) as a real, acknowledged flaw, ahead of a 2024 offense with Russell Wilson and Arthur Smith.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: New OC Arthur Smith's prior X-receivers (Drake London in Atlanta, A.J. Brown in Tennessee) ran heavy in-breaking route trees -- London ran nine routes on just 9.9% of routes, A.J. Brown was below 18% on nine routes in 2019-20 -- while Pickens is the opposite profile: 23% nine routes, 10% corner routes, and a below-average dig-route success rate through two years (61.9% up to 69% year-over-year, still below the 75%+ Harmon wants; his slant success rate of 78% is closer to passable). Harmon is hesitant to pull Pickens off his best skill (deep sideline routes) given the scheme mismatch. Co-host James Koh (untracked) counters that Arthur Smith runs 'system above talent' and doesn't tailor concepts to his personnel. Both note Russell Wilson (ahead of Justin Fields per minicamp reports) is an out-breaking, sideline-oriented passer that also doesn't naturally overlap with Smith's in-breaking scheme, but agree Pickens should still land in a better overall situation than under former OC Matt Canada given an improved run game.
- 2024-06-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-27]]): Pickens' 2023 profile (68.7% success vs. man, 73.3% vs. zone, 73.9% vs. press) was compared to 2015 DeSean Jackson, a retired burner Harmon called a 'one-of-one' talent (69.4% man, 78.3% zone, 68.6% press). Harmon gave the edge to Jackson, citing his rare ball-tracking and contested-catch ability downfield -- traits he said get overlooked in 'next DeSean Jackson' comps for smaller/faster prospects like Xavier Worthy, who lack that same downfield tracking and press-beating skill. Harmon's biggest standing criticism of Pickens remains inconsistent separation, especially his zone success rate; he said if Pickens could close that gap to something like Jackson's 78.3% vs. zone in his third season under Arthur Smith (with more intermediate/over-the-middle usage), it would be 'huge' for his target-earning profile and overall consistency. He noted Pickens already led the NFL in yards per reception this season, and that Pickens' short/intermediate route success (78% on slants, 75% on curls) is actually comparable to Jackson's, even though their overall styles and injury profiles differ greatly.
- 2024-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-23]]): Pickens is a good player who now has the whole room to himself after Pittsburgh traded [[Diontae Johnson]] for essentially nothing, and Harmon expects him to own around 28% of the passing targets. Charting says he needs to improve on base over-the-middle work — below-average post and dig success rates, curl and slant right around league average — while he is really good on nines, corners and outs, routes Harmon thinks [[Russell Wilson]] still wants to throw, so there is genuine stylistic overlap there. The tension is that Arthur Smith's offense mostly asks the quarterback to work the middle of the field (Harmon believes [[Desmond Ridder]] failed in it for exactly that reason, and that it shrank [[Drake London]]'s best dig/slant/crossing routes), so simply upgrading on Ridder could help maximize Pickens. Harmon's caveat is unchanged: at 23 and coming off a 1,100-yard second season, Pickens still has to show more consistency and 'frankly, effort' to justify that target share. Both hosts expect a run-heavy Pittsburgh offense and think the room around Pickens is bad any way you slice it.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): Waldman expects a productive fantasy player but doubts Pickens reaches WR1 level. Guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) ties him directly to [[Russell Wilson]]: a 26% target share in snaps without [[Diontae Johnson]] over the last two years and an expanded route tree in those games, with strengths and flaws both echoing [[DK Metcalf]]. McFarland's tiering runs off the passing volume — near 4,000 yards makes Pickens a WR1 shot, 3,500 a good WR2 (his projection), 3,200 in an Arthur Smith Atlanta-style run-heavy basement makes him a frustrating WR3. He is going round 4 in best ball, rounds 5-6 in home leagues. Waldman is more optimistic still, projecting about 1,200 yards, and says Pickens is his most-drafted receiver among those taken in the first four rounds.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-12]]): Harmon calls Pickens' Week 1 role 'perfect' — Arthur Smith designed the passing game around what [[Justin Fields]] likes to do, which is avoid the middle of the field, so Pickens got a heavy diet of deep outside-the-numbers targets. Harmon says Reception Perception charting already establishes the nine, the corner and the out as Pickens' best patterns, so the play-calling and the quarterback's preferences line up with the receiver's strengths. He adds a game-specific reason for avoiding the middle: Smith knew Jesse Bates was one of the few Falcons defenders capable of wrecking an offense.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): this is the best Pickens has looked on film since his college RP profile, and Harmon says he is finally ready to have a normal conversation about him. Pickens looks locked in, popping especially against press-man and on outside breaking routes; Harmon wants him never off the field and playing to 28 percent of the targets rather than his current 25 percent (53 percent of team air yards), noting he trails [[Van Jefferson]] in routes run for tendency-breaking reasons Harmon dismisses. Charting context: 2023 was 50th percentile vs man and an alarming 17th percentile vs zone (23 percent of routes were nine routes), but as a prospect he hit 86th percentile vs press, 79.7 vs zone, 82.4 percent on slants, 81.6 on curls, 88.9 on comebacks. Harmon attributes the NFL gap to effort and technique lapses and floats a [[D.J. Moore]]-style year-three RP breakout. Koh (not tracked) speculates the [[Diontae Johnson]] trade came with a you-are-the-guy message. Extension talk looms after this season.
- 2024-10-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Pickens played only 59% of snaps in Week 5 and ran 20 routes — fourth on the team behind [[Calvin Austin III]] and [[Van Jefferson]] (25) and [[Pat Freiermuth]] (24) — yet still led the Steelers in targets with seven. Harmon says the first four weeks were the best football of Pickens' career and his best game statistically (11 targets, 7 catches, 113 yards vs. the Colts), which makes the reversion to 'low-effort' tape more disappointing; he thinks Pickens believes his own hype, isn't accomplished enough to loaf, and that Freiermuth's postgame quote — leaders do the stuff without the ball, 'I block my ass off regardless' — was clearly about him. Harmon notes an offseason source told him the building isn't thrilled with how Pickens handles himself, cites Pittsburgh's pursuit of [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and interest in [[Davante Adams]], and says if he ran the Steelers he would trade Pickens before the deadline — this being year two of the same problem, with no offense or QB play capable of feeding him ten targets a game. Both hosts also frame it as fumbling a 2025 contract year.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-10]]): following up on their prior episode, co-host James Koh (not tracked) watched every Steelers offensive snap and reported Pickens played only 59% of snaps in the Week 5 prime-time game, with 100% of his missed snaps coming on one- or two-receiver sets — of roughly 31 such snaps he played two. Koh's theory is that Arthur Smith is pulling him to sell run, since everyone knows Pickens will not block; on the 14 runs Pickens was out there for, seven were back-side (Koh doesn't grade effort there) and of the seven play-side he gave real effort on two, being generous. Mike Tomlin said it is not an effort thing; Koh doesn't buy it. Harmon agreed Pickens has never been a good blocker and never given the effort — the viral college clips are inconsequential back-side blocks — but pushed back that pulling him also tips the defense, and said the deeper issue is that this is what happens when you can't trust your No. 1 receiver to be the passing-game threat. Koh added [[Van Jefferson]] and Scotty Miller are on the field because they actually block.

Harmon says Pickens should keep being fed there because that is where he wins.
