# Jaylin Lane

## Expert Takes

- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): the Virginia Tech receiver is a lot like Mecole Hardman as a prospect — both can flip the field as runners and have deep speed, and neither has been good at the catch point in traffic or against tight coverage, where their games short-circuit, they overthink how to attack the ball, and their technique is too lackluster to hold up on contested throws. Waldman's projection follows Hardman's actual career: offenses find a place for that speed as a space player on fly sweeps, end-arounds, RPOs, screens, crossers, over routes and the occasional play-action vertical shot — routes where speed and ball-carrying acumen matter more than route technique. Those are the routes where Lane wins too.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: Lane is a talented kid with real physical traits. [[Matt Waldman]] was more guarded — he does not like how Lane catches the football and has concerns there — and said the only way to draft him is with the expectation of a Mecole Hardman-type role, in which case a surprise is upside; if you are expecting what people expected of Hardman when Kansas City drafted him, that role simply is not chased in the league anymore.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): if any Jaylin in this class is 'Mecole Hardman part two' it is Lane, not [[Jaylin Noel]] — the Virginia Tech receiver is generating excitement purely off timed speed, but his film profile (what he does well, what he does not, and how much a scheme must manufacture for him to make plays) maps closely onto Hardman. Waldman goes further: he would argue Hardman may have the better hands of the two.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21]]): flatly no on being higher post-draft. Waldman's objection is technical — Lane 'clap attacks' the ball far too much at the catch point — and his comp is Mecole Hardman. He frames Lane as the kind of prospect fantasy players hope others take while they wait for a pick to slide, i.e. cannon fodder for someone else's draft strategy rather than a target of his own.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Koh (not tracked) flags the fourth-round Middle Tennessee/Virginia Tech receiver as a genuine athlete — 5-foot-10, 190-192, a 4.34 forty with a 40-inch vertical — and likes that Washington went and got speed, which is exactly what [[Luke McCaffrey]] does not bring. Harmon did not chart him, and says Lane was mostly discussed pre-draft as a returner and open-field catch-and-run gadget player, but would consider a three-game sample for the rookie roundup if he looks likely to play a real role. His speculative framing is [[Deebo Samuel]] insurance, since Harmon views Deebo as probably a one-year piece. Koh's pitch line: is Lane Cliff Kingsbury's [[Rondale Moore]] redux? Neither host commits.
- 2025-08-08 — According to James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-08]], as discussed with [[Matt Harmon]]: Lane is the fourth-round speed slot Washington drafted at 5-10, 191 with through-the-roof testing — a 4.34 forty, 40-inch vertical and 11-foot broad jump. At Virginia Tech he was mostly a special-teamer and gadget piece: just 38 receptions and 18 carries last season on jet sweeps and reverses. Koh questions the fit, since Washington's static Kingsbury offense used motion on only 307 dropbacks (18th most, per Fantasy Points) in a run-heavy season, and worries Lane becomes only a bubble-screen guy. Harmon counters that a Kingsbury offense throws a lot of screens and frames Lane as the [[Rondale Moore]] / [[Greg Dortch]] Arizona archetype — with the caveat that outside [[Terry McLaurin]] someone has to stretch the field.
- 2025-08-29 — According to James Koh (co-host, not tracked) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-29]]: Lane is the one Washington receiver who can really break out — 5'10, 190, a 4.34 forty, a 40-inch vertical and an 11-foot broad jump make him easily the best athlete in the room, even with [[Terry McLaurin]] back, [[Deebo Samuel]] moving around and veteran [[Noah Brown]] present. [[Matt Harmon]] agreed the [[Noah Brown]] penciling-in is strange given he has been injured all offseason and is not a needle mover, and said Lane is ahead of [[Luke McCaffrey]], a pure developmental player Washington probably should not have spent a third-rounder on. Harmon did not chart Lane as a prospect but will publish on him if he plays early; he profiles as a gadget and screen player, what Cliff Kingsbury wanted [[Rondale Moore]] to be in Arizona, with speed that forces safeties to play back if he gets outside snaps.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]) [Dynasty]: a deep-bench dynasty flag only. Harris admits he knows nothing about Lane as a prospect — a rookie out of Virginia Tech he had never heard of — but has been meaning to say something for a couple of weeks because it keeps feeling like Lane has 'a little something'. Against Atlanta it was two catches for 18 yards plus a long catch called back by penalty, and he plays special teams. Harris's description: a smaller guy but thick, looks really fast, like a real athlete. He is filing it away rather than recommending an add.
