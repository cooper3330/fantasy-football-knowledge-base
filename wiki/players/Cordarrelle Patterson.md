# Cordarrelle Patterson

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said a three-headed Steelers backfield including Cordarrelle Patterson could happen but likely requires an injury elsewhere; he put Patterson in his top-15 waiver-wire monitor list given the upside he's shown when he gets volume, though he flagged Patterson's age (entering his mid-30s) and bruising 230-235 lb running style as reasons his workload ceiling is limited to short stretches, not a full-season role. Bob Harris (untracked) agreed Patterson won't recapture his 2021 Atlanta RB1 form but credited Arthur Smith for creatively carving out that earlier role when other coaches, including Bill Belichick, hadn't found a way to use Patterson effectively.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): a short-term streaming stab — grab Patterson for a couple of weeks while injuries sort out in Pittsburgh, since he could handle roughly 25 to 40 carries across the first two weeks if asked, and once [[Jaylen Warren]] is back they will push him out to special teams. Waldman notes people forget he is a 230-235 pound running back and that he loved what Patterson did in Atlanta. [[Brandon Angelo]], a Bears fan, calls him the most delightfully frustrating back to own since converting from receiver, remembering Matt Nagy subbing him in on third-and-one during [[David Montgomery]]'s rookie year; he adds that offensive coordinators love him for good reason and that he produces a couple of boom weeks every year.
- 2024-10-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-03]]): Patterson is his pick for a current player who should have started at his eventual position much earlier — entering today's league as a running back, with all the gap scheme teams run and at 230-235 pounds, Waldman says he would have been 'an absolute sensation', because he took to power running like a duck to water and shouldn't have had to endure the positional struggles he did. Co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) would delay him a year instead, betting the touchback moves to the 35 next offseason and return rates spike: a bold team willing to use him on kickoffs as well as offense could put him in range of the career all-purpose and kickoff-return yardage records, given he already holds the career kickoff-return touchdown record in the most hostile era for returns.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-13 (Paper Champions and All-Timers Across Eras)]]): untracked co-host Adam Harstad argues Patterson is the most era-cursed player in the league — he would love to see him play in any era except the one he got. Kickoffs mattered in every period of NFL history except the decade of Patterson's career, when the league effectively legislated them out, and he still set a pile of return records in the environment most hostile to his skill set. Harstad's claim is that dropped into the 1960s, the 1980s or today's rules, Patterson does to kickoff returns what Jerry Rice did to receiving records — puts them so far out of reach that he becomes the asterisk on everyone else's résumé, the 'non-Patterson division' of return men. Waldman agrees without pushback. Relevant to [[Kickoff Rule Change and Return Scoring]].
