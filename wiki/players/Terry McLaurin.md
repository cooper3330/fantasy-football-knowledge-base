# Terry McLaurin

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): "a great player," a top-15 wide receiver in the NFL, raised specifically in a discussion about the league-wide shortage of true X-receiver talent. Harmon's real critique isn't McLaurin's play but Washington's roster construction around him — he's forced to play X almost exclusively (on the line of scrimmage, outside) because he's the offense's best separator and best answer against man coverage, even though it isn't the most efficient way to get him the ball, simply because Washington has no alternative X-caliber receiver on the roster (Jahan Dotson, Curtis Samuel, Dyami Brown and Byron Pringle all named as not fitting the role).

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]), relaying a conversation with The Athletic's Ben Standig (not tracked): agrees McLaurin projects as "a solid WR2" rather than a true WR1, and says he can't build a strong counter-argument. Detailed technique critique — his hand position and catch technique are "some of the worst I've seen for a very good NFL wide receiver," a backhanded compliment given how much he still produces around it, comping him to retired receivers Golden Tate and Early Doucet (neither tracked) as the rare players who make plays consistently despite poor hand position. Not physical enough to consistently win contested/timing-based routes, can absorb contact but can't dish it out at an elite level, and isn't a big after-the-catch threat because most of his catches are contested rather than in-stride. Bottom line: "a great number two who could pose as a wide receiver one" playing opposite a true alpha, but not a true WR1 himself. Separately, discusses backfield-mate [[Jahan Dotson]] as part of why McLaurin is stuck playing X almost exclusively.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: McLaurin remains one of Washington's more consistent options, and Waldman ties his 2024 yardage range directly to [[Jayden Daniels]]'s development — roughly 800-900 yards if Daniels struggles, climbing toward 1,100 if Daniels performs at the level of the journeyman-caliber QBs Washington has run out in recent years. The framing is essentially a floor/ceiling bet on rookie QB play rather than a change in McLaurin's own role.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon thinks McLaurin could finally get his long-awaited career season -- rookie QB Jayden Daniels graded as a very accurate passer per Derek Klassen's prospect profile despite long-term projection concerns, and Harmon expects heavy RPO usage with McLaurin as the first read, comparable to how Shane Steichen's Colts fed Michael Pittman Jr. Co-host James Koh notes new OC Kliff Kingsbury's offense should also concentrate targets far more than Eric Bieniemi's spread-it-to-10-guys approach did last year, which never got either McLaurin or Jahan Dotson a high target share. Both flag touchdown-upside uncertainty given the weak supporting cast (Jahan Dotson, Jamison Crowder, Luke McCaffrey, Olamide Zaccheaus).
- 2024-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-16]]): Harmon called McLaurin 'a tier-two receiver, like a superstar receiver' who was badly underused in 2023 -- his 0.21 targets per route run were, in Harmon's words, 'not high enough' under since-departed OC Eric Bieniemy's scheme, which spread designed looks to fringe roster names (Byron Pringle, Jamison Crowder, Olamide Zaccheaus) at the expense of the top receivers. With Bieniemy out and new OC Kliff Kingsbury expected to concentrate targets on the top two options ([[Jahan Dotson]] the other), both hosts expect a significant target-share bounce back for McLaurin in 2024. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): McLaurin is a strong vertical receiver who maps well stylistically to rookie QB [[Jayden Daniels]], himself 'a strong vertical passer,' but Harmon flagged real risk that Washington's offensive line -- which guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn (not tracked) called 'well below average, bottom five,' with a rookie left tackle (Brandon Coleman) and the unit ranked 26th in total game experience -- could cap that connection if it can't hold up in true drop-back protection. Harmon noted Thorn's point that a heavier 12/21-personnel, run-first approach under new OC Kliff Kingsbury could mask the OL's weaknesses and push more targets to McLaurin and [[Jahan Dotson]] as the top two options, though Kingsbury's pass-heavy track record in Arizona makes that uncertain.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): McLaurin is a really good receiver but not a wide receiver one — a high-end WR2 in the league, in [[Brandon Angelo]]'s phrasing, which Waldman endorsed 100 percent. The larger point was about Washington's target tree behind him: with [[Jahan Dotson]] rumored not to make the team or at best be the No. 2, [[Olamide Zaccheaus]] a late-round slot type Waldman liked out of school (Waldman cites coach Drew Lieberman saying Zaccheaus could have been a starter with better draft capital), [[Ben Sinnott]] unlikely to be a [[Sam LaPorta]] part two, and [[Zach Ertz]] probably starting, neither host could name a credible second read — which feeds their bearishness on [[Jayden Daniels]].
- 2024-08-27 — According to host James Koh (not a tracked expert) with agreement from guest Derek Klassen (not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): after the [[Jahan Dotson]] trade, McLaurin is the only real receiver in Washington, and if he goes down the room is 'the worst wide receiver room in the NFL by a million miles' — behind [[Luke McCaffrey]], Dyami Brown and Jamison Crowder. Koh compares it to Dallas's fragility behind [[CeeDee Lamb]] and says he would rather have an old [[Brandon Cooks]] and an unproven [[Jalen Tolbert]] than Washington's non-McLaurin group; Klassen adds that even Tolbert, whom he has criticized as a WR3, is probably better than every non-McLaurin Washington receiver. Framed as a real-football depth argument about [[Jayden Daniels]]'s supporting cast rather than a change of view on McLaurin himself.
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): Harmon loves McLaurin but raises an aging flag — he wonders whether we have already seen the best of him. He had charted most of McLaurin's 2023 sample before stepping away, and while the numbers are still good, they are not as good as his earlier seasons; McLaurin is 29 and Harmon calls him a 'sneaky older guy.' He also dislikes the deployment: used as a pure X, and in the preseason simply lining up on one side of the field under Kliff Kingsbury, so he does not expect great usage this year. The upside case is volume — with a depth chart both hosts call one of the worst in the league behind him and a team unlikely to throw much, McLaurin could still get a boatload of first-read targets, and Harmon would love to see him get one big season before real decline.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): the preseason alignment worry was real, not a preseason artifact. Citing Grant Paulson's unofficial charting, Harmon reports McLaurin lined up on the left 46 times, on the right three times, and was put in pre-snap motion zero times against Tampa Bay. Harmon's frustration with Cliff Kingsbury is not that motion and quick game are bad — they're good — it's that dating back to Arizona he uses them on 'the Mickey Mouse players' (he cites [[Olamide Zaccheaus]]) while the best player does static iso-receiver work, exactly as [[DeAndre Hopkins]] did. He adds that he thought McLaurin took a step back last year and that the rest of the room, with [[Zach Ertz]] drawing targets in 2024, is brutal. Both hosts note the two leading Washington receivers in Week 1 were running backs, and Koh (not tracked) says he was 'sounding the alarms' about the Kingsbury hire all offseason.
