# Terry McLaurin

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): "a great player," a top-15 wide receiver in the NFL, raised specifically in a discussion about the league-wide shortage of true X-receiver talent. Harmon's real critique isn't McLaurin's play but Washington's roster construction around him — he's forced to play X almost exclusively (on the line of scrimmage, outside) because he's the offense's best separator and best answer against man coverage, even though it isn't the most efficient way to get him the ball, simply because Washington has no alternative X-caliber receiver on the roster (Jahan Dotson, Curtis Samuel, Dyami Brown and Byron Pringle all named as not fitting the role).

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]), relaying a conversation with The Athletic's Ben Standig (not tracked): agrees McLaurin projects as "a solid WR2" rather than a true WR1, and says he can't build a strong counter-argument. Detailed technique critique — his hand position and catch technique are "some of the worst I've seen for a very good NFL wide receiver," a backhanded compliment given how much he still produces around it, comping him to retired receivers Golden Tate and Early Doucet (neither tracked) as the rare players who make plays consistently despite poor hand position. Not physical enough to consistently win contested/timing-based routes, can absorb contact but can't dish it out at an elite level, and isn't a big after-the-catch threat because most of his catches are contested rather than in-stride. Bottom line: "a great number two who could pose as a wide receiver one" playing opposite a true alpha, but not a true WR1 himself. Separately, discusses backfield-mate [[Jahan Dotson]] as part of why McLaurin is stuck playing X almost exclusively.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: McLaurin remains one of Washington's more consistent options, and Waldman ties his 2024 yardage range directly to [[Jayden Daniels]]'s development — roughly 800-900 yards if Daniels struggles, climbing toward 1,100 if Daniels performs at the level of the journeyman-caliber QBs Washington has run out in recent years. The framing is essentially a floor/ceiling bet on rookie QB play rather than a change in McLaurin's own role.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon thinks McLaurin could finally get his long-awaited career season -- rookie QB Jayden Daniels graded as a very accurate passer per Derek Klassen's prospect profile despite long-term projection concerns, and Harmon expects heavy RPO usage with McLaurin as the first read, comparable to how Shane Steichen's Colts fed Michael Pittman Jr. Co-host James Koh notes new OC Kliff Kingsbury's offense should also concentrate targets far more than Eric Bieniemi's spread-it-to-10-guys approach did last year, which never got either McLaurin or Jahan Dotson a high target share. Both flag touchdown-upside uncertainty given the weak supporting cast (Jahan Dotson, Jamison Crowder, Luke McCaffrey, Olamide Zaccheaus).
- 2024-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-16]]): Harmon called McLaurin 'a tier-two receiver, like a superstar receiver' who was badly underused in 2023 -- his 0.21 targets per route run were, in Harmon's words, 'not high enough' under since-departed OC Eric Bieniemy's scheme, which spread designed looks to fringe roster names (Byron Pringle, Jamison Crowder, Olamide Zaccheaus) at the expense of the top receivers. With Bieniemy out and new OC Kliff Kingsbury expected to concentrate targets on the top two options ([[Jahan Dotson]] the other), both hosts expect a significant target-share bounce back for McLaurin in 2024. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): McLaurin is a strong vertical receiver who maps well stylistically to rookie QB [[Jayden Daniels]], himself 'a strong vertical passer,' but Harmon flagged real risk that Washington's offensive line -- which guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn (not tracked) called 'well below average, bottom five,' with a rookie left tackle (Brandon Coleman) and the unit ranked 26th in total game experience -- could cap that connection if it can't hold up in true drop-back protection. Harmon noted Thorn's point that a heavier 12/21-personnel, run-first approach under new OC Kliff Kingsbury could mask the OL's weaknesses and push more targets to McLaurin and [[Jahan Dotson]] as the top two options, though Kingsbury's pass-heavy track record in Arizona makes that uncertain.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): McLaurin is a really good receiver but not a wide receiver one — a high-end WR2 in the league, in [[Brandon Angelo]]'s phrasing, which Waldman endorsed 100 percent. The larger point was about Washington's target tree behind him: with [[Jahan Dotson]] rumored not to make the team or at best be the No. 2, [[Olamide Zaccheaus]] a late-round slot type Waldman liked out of school (Waldman cites coach Drew Lieberman saying Zaccheaus could have been a starter with better draft capital), [[Ben Sinnott]] unlikely to be a [[Sam LaPorta]] part two, and [[Zach Ertz]] probably starting, neither host could name a credible second read — which feeds their bearishness on [[Jayden Daniels]].
- 2024-08-27 — According to host James Koh (not a tracked expert) with agreement from guest Derek Klassen (not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): after the [[Jahan Dotson]] trade, McLaurin is the only real receiver in Washington, and if he goes down the room is 'the worst wide receiver room in the NFL by a million miles' — behind [[Luke McCaffrey]], Dyami Brown and Jamison Crowder. Koh compares it to Dallas's fragility behind [[CeeDee Lamb]] and says he would rather have an old [[Brandon Cooks]] and an unproven [[Jalen Tolbert]] than Washington's non-McLaurin group; Klassen adds that even Tolbert, whom he has criticized as a WR3, is probably better than every non-McLaurin Washington receiver. Framed as a real-football depth argument about [[Jayden Daniels]]'s supporting cast rather than a change of view on McLaurin himself.
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): Harmon loves McLaurin but raises an aging flag — he wonders whether we have already seen the best of him. He had charted most of McLaurin's 2023 sample before stepping away, and while the numbers are still good, they are not as good as his earlier seasons; McLaurin is 29 and Harmon calls him a 'sneaky older guy.' He also dislikes the deployment: used as a pure X, and in the preseason simply lining up on one side of the field under Kliff Kingsbury, so he does not expect great usage this year. The upside case is volume — with a depth chart both hosts call one of the worst in the league behind him and a team unlikely to throw much, McLaurin could still get a boatload of first-read targets, and Harmon would love to see him get one big season before real decline.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): the preseason alignment worry was real, not a preseason artifact. Citing Grant Paulson's unofficial charting, Harmon reports McLaurin lined up on the left 46 times, on the right three times, and was put in pre-snap motion zero times against Tampa Bay. Harmon's frustration with Cliff Kingsbury is not that motion and quick game are bad — they're good — it's that dating back to Arizona he uses them on 'the Mickey Mouse players' (he cites [[Olamide Zaccheaus]]) while the best player does static iso-receiver work, exactly as [[DeAndre Hopkins]] did. He adds that he thought McLaurin took a step back last year and that the rest of the room, with [[Zach Ertz]] drawing targets in 2024, is brutal. Both hosts note the two leading Washington receivers in Week 1 were running backs, and Koh (not tracked) says he was 'sounding the alarms' about the Kingsbury hire all offseason.
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): McLaurin's Week 3 Monday night line came with a 26 percent target share and 61.8 percent of Washington's air yards, plus a team-high 23 routes when no other Commanders receiver ran more than 14. Koh's central complaint is alignment: [[Jayden Daniels]] threw 18 of his 21 completions, 81 percent, to the right side of the field, which makes sense for a right-handed mobile quarterback resetting his platform, yet Cliff Kingsbury keeps McLaurin static on the left. McLaurin told the sideline reporter he went to Kingsbury and asked to line up right, which set up the game-clinching go-route touchdown; he played 10 right-side snaps that night, 21 percent of his snaps, versus only nine combined in Weeks 1 and 2. Koh says failing to move him permanently would be criminal malpractice. Harmon agrees but adds a wrinkle, that McLaurin can stay the farthest-left receiver if he is tightened to the line and runs routes back to the right, and says the base of this offense should simply be dropping back and pushing it downfield to a great deep-game player.
- 2024-09-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-26 (Win-Now Trades and Jayden Daniels)]]): McLaurin caught the deep double-move that won the Bengals game late, which Waldman credits as a heady play by [[Jayden Daniels]] under pressure — but the season-long picture is worse than the highlight. McLaurin is Washington's best receiver and sits at only WR41; [[Zach Ertz]] is around TE9, which on a tight-end-to-receiver equivalency is roughly the 64th pass catcher, and [[Austin Ekeler]] and [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] at RB17 and RB26 are not adding meaningful receiving production. Waldman's concern is structural: this is a Cliff Kingsbury offense, and he points to [[Kyler Murray]] never supporting a receiver better than WR26 outside one [[DeAndre Hopkins]] season, so Daniels may be a fine fantasy quarterback without the offense feeding anyone else.
- 2024-10-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-03]]): when a quarterback is projected to earn 20% or more of his total yardage as a runner in a heavily schemed offense, Waldman says you draft the quarterback but take a chance on only one of his pass catchers — and it has to be the clear-cut go-to option. In Washington's case that is McLaurin alongside [[Jayden Daniels]]. He contrasts this with Baltimore, where the presumed one supportable option was [[Mark Andrews]] and may instead be [[Zay Flowers]], and with Arizona, where he is unsure whether [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] or [[Trey McBride]] is that guy or whether both settle as low-end weekly plays.
- 2024-10-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-15]]): McLaurin is finally getting the season Harmon always hoped for, in the year Harmon was most skeptical because of the surrounding situation — he admits he has been wrong on Cliff Kingsbury and is impressed by [[Jayden Daniels]]. He is 'sneaky old' at 29 but looks more explosive on film than last year, which Harmon attributes partly to entering 2023 with a late-preseason turf toe injury. His EPA per target is 0.45, best since his rookie year by a lot. Harmon still wants more base routes over the middle, arguing McLaurin is a high-level intermediate route runner, not just a vertical threat. Both hosts caveat heavily on matchups — Tampa Bay, the Giants, Cincinnati, Arizona and a regressed Baltimore secondary — and say they will revisit after the Week 8 Bears and Week 10 Steelers games. Koh (not tracked) credits McLaurin for never creating drama and for asking Kingsbury directly for a field-side go ball that won a game.
- 2024-11-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-05]]): McLaurin is having a career breakthrough, not merely a renaissance — through Week 9 he owns career bests in touchdown rate (10%), EPA per target (0.62), yards per route run (2.32) and catch rate (70%), and scored twice on just three targets in Week 9. Harmon credits the [[Jayden Daniels]] fit: Daniels is a precise placer in tight windows and a strong downfield attacker, and McLaurin has always been comfortable in contested situations because 12 different starting quarterbacks forced him to win there. Harmon's standing point is that a high contested-target rate does not imply an inability to separate. Caveat: McLaurin is 29, close to 30, so Harmon notes they are entering the age zone even if he stops short of calling it a cliff. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) emphasizes McLaurin's professionalism at a high-maintenance position.
- 2024-11-22 — According to Adam Harstad, untracked co-host ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]): McLaurin is the process-defying outlier on his rookie-model leaderboard and its worst outcome to date — a star who 'keeps rolling snake eyes' and has been a very good fantasy receiver without ever becoming the high-end WR1 the rest of that list became. Harstad is openly skeptical of the technique: he joked McLaurin must be wearing army-grade stickum because by the laws of physics his catch rate shouldn't be possible, and said he would bet against him ten times out of ten and still feel fine. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed it is not teach tape, likened it to Adrian Peterson's inefficient-but-effective jump cut, and said he likes McLaurin but the ship has sailed on the star comparisons — a what-if career rather than an indictment of the people who hyped him. Harstad used him as the example of correlated film opinions all being wrong for the same reason.
- 2024-11-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-29]]): McLaurin is fourth in the NFL in receiving yards at 823 through 12 weeks after ranking 23rd over the same span in 2023, and Harmon calls him the least surprising name on the leaderboard because his profile has long been 'fringe, not quite elite, a step down from elite.' The jump is efficiency, not volume — he is on pace for roughly 1,166 yards, about his normal, but with a 71% catch rate against a 63% career mark and air yards per target up from 10.6 to 14.4. Harmon credits [[Jayden Daniels]]: McLaurin has always had the vertical chops (nine-route success rates of 70.7% in 2021, 68.9% in 2020, 68.6% in 2022) but never a downfield thrower, having played with Alex Smith, Case Keenum, Taylor Heinicke and Carson Wentz. James Koh, who admits he had McLaurin filed as a 10-to-15-yard in-breaker and contested-catch winner, says the deep-ball effectiveness genuinely surprised him. Both remain partial skeptics of Cliff Kingsbury, though Harmon pushes back on the second-half narrative, calling Washington's problem a talent issue with McLaurin as the only receiver defenses fear.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-19]]): a hard Week 16 draw against Quinyon Mitchell and Darius Slay, the toughest matchup on the board for outside receivers, and the first meeting did not go well when McLaurin was locked to the left side. Harmon and Koh both want Washington to move him around — Harmon noted approvingly that a chunk of his production last week came from the right side. The lever is coverage: the Eagles play the most cover six in the league and only 16.3% cover two, with cover one on 23.5% of snaps and heavy cover three. McLaurin leads Washington with 13 catches for 150 yards against cover one and 483 yards at 17.3 yards per target against cover three, so Harmon frames the game as a test of whether [[Jayden Daniels]] can identify single-high pre- and post-snap out of Washington's league-leading no-huddle dropback volume and take the vertical shot to his side.
- 2025-01-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-09]]): McLaurin takes honorable mentions in both the deep-ball and ball-winner categories. Deep: third in yards per route run on deep targets with zero drops, on a connection with [[Jayden Daniels]] on go routes and corner routes outside the numbers that Harmon says exploited him downfield 'like we've never seen before'. His framing is that McLaurin was always able to cook on downfield routes and finally has a quarterback who can get him the ball. Contested: Koh's Next Gen numbers have McLaurin leading the NFL in total contested-catch yards on the most contested targets, with a 57.5% contested catch rate, second among qualified receivers, and seven of his 13 touchdowns coming in contested situations. Harmon uses him as the case study for his thesis on contested-catch data — high contested target counts historically meant [[Taylor Heinicke]] throwing an already-open McLaurin into coverage, whereas this year it was Daniels trusting him to win in tight windows.

His framing is that the question has shifted from whether McLaurin misses games — Harris tends to think he will not — to whether he can be in good enough football shape to produce at a high level in September.
- 2025-08-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he finally dropped McLaurin a full round, from a third-rounder to a fourth-rounder, calling it losing his nerve on the holdout. He framed the question he had posed in a July podcast — at what point in August can a receiver no longer be drafted where you would normally take him if he has not practiced with the team — and said this drafting weekend is that point for him. Even if McLaurin signs a new contract, Harris worries about how ready he will be to hit the ground running and what shape he is in, and admitted there is more risk than even the one-round drop bakes in. He noted the move brings him into alignment with where ADP is already treating McLaurin.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): the three-year extension worth up to $96M was never in doubt, but the holdout carries a real cost. Waldman: when you stay away from camp and are 'just kind of jogging off to the side', there is a chance your body is not 'weaponized enough' to stay healthy, so he sees a slight bump in muscle pull, ankle sprain and ligament/tendon risk. Co-host Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) noted McLaurin had slid from a WR13-14 draft price to about WR19, behind [[Davante Adams]]. Waldman said that at those values he would still step back to [[Courtland Sutton]] or [[DJ Moore]], reasoning that those offenses are on the way up while Washington's very specific second-year system may plateau. Harris framed the deal as inevitable once Washington paid [[Deebo Samuel]] more for 2025. Waldman said he would move [[Jayden Daniels]] up a spot or two.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]]): he lowered McLaurin at the end of the prior week to WR18, a late fourth-round pick, and calls him 'still high, still risky' because McLaurin was still holding in for a new contract as of recording. Harris's reasoning is less about the deal itself than the missed reps: even if McLaurin gets paid at the 11th hour, going this deep into training camp with no practice work raises the odds of a slow start to the season. He frames it as a lot of reward if everything goes right, but a lot of risk attached to the price.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): McLaurin finally has his Commanders contract after roughly four weeks of not practicing, with the opener against the Giants 12 days out — Harris calls it 'ludicrous that it took this long' and is glad to watch him play with [[Jayden Daniels]] again. He bumped McLaurin back up in his ranks but deliberately not all the way back to where he had him entering August: expected value has to include a slow possible start in the mould of [[Brandon Aiyuk]] after his 2024 summer holdout, so the floor is a little worse than it was. Net position: WR15 for Harris, 'a solid third round pick'. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-27]]): McLaurin signed a three-year, $96M extension ($32M per year if he hits the 'up to'), just under [[DK Metcalf]]'s $33M, after his camp asked for $40M-plus and Washington pushed toward $30M — with guarantees not yet reported. Harmon rejects the idea that [[Courtland Sutton]]'s $23M deal was instructive, saying Sutton simply is not as good a player even if the counting stats looked similar. His real concern is the missed camp: he cites [[CeeDee Lamb]] going from a 29% target-per-route rate and 102.9 yards per game in 2023 to 22.6% and 75.6 in Weeks 1-5 after his holdout, and [[Brandon Aiyuk]] from 24% and 83.9 yards per game to 22.3% and 62.8, noting both had far more entrenched QB and coaching continuity than Washington. Harmon does not predict a slow start but says it should not be a surprise if one comes. He also worries Washington lacks any other full-field separator or X receiver, is skeptical of Year 2 of Kliff Kingsbury and of a front office he says borders on arrogance, and thinks the offense is in trouble if defenses take McLaurin away the way Philadelphia did.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-04]]): McLaurin wins the 'Year We Go Behind a Paywall' Huggy Award for spending August squandering a fair amount of the goodwill he had finally generated in his sixth NFL season by proving he was not allergic to the end zone. Harris is careful to say he is never mad at a player for using the only leverage he has to get paid what he considers fair market value — he blames the Commanders for being dumb and putting their offense at risk to squeeze a few more nickels out of their best receiver. Regardless of fault, the hold-in took steam out of McLaurin's draft stock, and Harris groups him with [[Jauan Jennings]] this year and [[Brandon Aiyuk]] last year as players where we are left watching and wondering whether a slow start is coming, and whether we would even be able to tell something is different.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): a worrying game masked by one near-touchdown. McLaurin should have had a deep score; Harris says he does not know how McLaurin could have run any faster or further and does not think the miss was about having no training camp, and guest **Sam Gold** (not tracked) agrees it was simply a bad throw from [[Jayden Daniels]], praising a stem outside that created space against the red line five or six yards off the sideline. But it was a 10-to-4 target lead for [[Deebo Samuel]], and Harris invokes the [[Brandon Aiyuk]] post-holdout experience from last year as reason not to be shocked, warning this could last a while. Gold notes that in Cliff Kingsbury's offense McLaurin lines up on one side the vast majority of the time and can be schemed out, and that Samuel is now taking the over-the-middle work. Gold is still starting McLaurin and hoping it is a week-by-week game plan thing, but says it must be monitored.
