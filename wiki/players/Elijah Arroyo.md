# Elijah Arroyo

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]) [Dynasty]: Arroyo is Waldman's upside swing at tight end — he has the speed to stretch a defense vertically, which [[Mason Taylor]] does not offer to the same degree. Waldman framed it as a legitimate argument that Arroyo is not so far behind Taylor that you couldn't simply wait and roll the dice on the higher-variance option, while cautioning that a few guys in this class have more upside than Taylor and it remains to be seen whether they hit that upside mark at all. Taylor remains the safest and best option in Waldman's view.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18]]) [Dynasty]: Arroyo is a skilled pass catcher who makes the athletic adjustments the NFL expects of a tight end winning at the boundary, and who also wins on routes where he is thrown open — one of the four in the class Waldman would want maximized as a receiver with blocking minimized. The specific fix standing between him and top-pass-catcher status is hand uniformity: Waldman sees no ingrained reaction for where Arroyo's hands should go on throws that are high and behind, low and behind, or low and into his body depending on the break, so his hands 'short-circuit' when a route makes him turn against his grain. Waldman's prescription is daily drilling of each attack position until it becomes muscle memory; it might take a season or two, but he believes Arroyo should be a top-level talent if the reaction gets ingrained.
- 2025-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-07]]): both hosts dismissed the report that Arroyo could hold an X receiver role in Seattle. Harmon pointed out he is 6-5, 250 and that there are no 250-pound X receivers in the league; per Mock Draftable he sits at a 95th-percentile wingspan and 94th-percentile height among ball carriers, which Harmon says is great for a tight end and irrelevant as a receiver. He expects Arroyo to be the move-around receiving mismatch with [[A.J. Barner]] as the true inline presence, and allowed that Arroyo might technically end a snap as the widest man on the line when [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] motions across, but will not be split wide against NFL corners. Koh (not tracked) was blunter, saying X-receiver work against Cal or Stanford defensive backs does not translate. Both read the messaging as a symptom of Seattle's desperation on the outside, where the actual X is [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]]; Harmon wants [[Tory Horton]] there instead. The signal is that Seattle is bullish on Arroyo, not that the alignment is real.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]) [Dynasty]: pushing back on the social-media hype after [[Noah Fant]]'s release — 'not yet.' In Klint Kubiak's two-tight-end sets Arroyo inherits the Fant role rather than a featured one: play-action crossers, seams and wheel routes that produce three or four 15-to-20-point games a year, with real doubt about his red-zone usage. Waldman expects [[A.J. Barner]] to keep the underneath and inline work, and thinks Arroyo simply gives what Fant gave — Seattle cut Fant as 'an aging redundancy who costs more money,' not because Arroyo has surpassed him. Arroyo's Miami tape shows blocking that should improve, but as a route runner right now it is too much to expect him to be better than Fant was. Waldman lands on TE15-TE20 in redraft this year, while noting he had Arroyo as a top-five dynasty tight end in the class and remains bullish in rookie drafts, especially TE-premium — but he does not vault into the top three immediately.
- 2025-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]): both hosts flatly reject the idea that Arroyo plays X receiver for Seattle, despite GM John Schneider saying some coaches thought he could and Clint Kubiak reportedly being the source. Harmon's first reaction was 'no effing way' and his second was dread that people would start requesting a Reception Perception profile on him — he says he will never chart a tight end. The reasoning is the [[Kyle Pitts]] precedent: Pitts got banged up in evaluation because deploying him at X made him the biggest, slowest X receiver in the league, and at 6-5 and 254 pounds Arroyo is bigger and probably slower still, with an injury-limited pre-draft process leaving his real speed unknown. Harmon projects him as a similar player to [[Noah Fant]], a red-zone threat and deep-seam option who could be a real fantasy sleeper — but the honest label would have been big slot, which is what Seattle actually needs. James Koh (not tracked) agrees fully.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-29]]): Waldman is 'feeling' Arroyo as an emerging threat — he wins in zones doing what [[Noah Fant]] does but is a little faster and catches the ball better, and [[Sam Darnold]] showing willingness to throw him open is a good sign. He scored a touchdown getting open against an underneath corner who could not catch up, so the speed and hands look fine. The caveat is he is not an instant starter: he is a matchup play against weak teams with weak tight ends, still splitting time with [[A.J. Barner]], who owns the low red zone. Waldman frames Arroyo as all-or-nothing — one catch for 30 yards, or three catches with a long touchdown for 70-80 — whereas Barner is more likely three catches for 20 yards and two scores.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-05]]): Arroyo dealt with injuries as a rookie but flashed, and Harmon says Seattle needs him to break out as a dynamic move tight end in his second season given how thin the receiver room is behind [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]]. Co-host James Koh (untracked) separately praised rookie A.J. Barner as 'a dog' at tight end for the Seahawks.
