# Will Howard

## Expert Takes

- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman says 'f**k it' on Howard — his line is that Howard is 'a [[Josh Allen]] starter kit, but with directions from Paxton Lynch and Kyle Trask for assembly'. Big, strong, moves pretty well, but Waldman does not think he sees the field at the level required. He expects Howard to get an early look anyway because, per an unnamed friend in the league, at least half the NFL still follows the rule that a big, doughy-faced quarterback with arm strength can be mentally filled in later — qualities Waldman says never actually get filled in.
- 2025-02-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-27]]): [[Will Howard]] is a big man with a big arm who can run a little and is built somewhat like Tim Tebow; the physicality is what Waldman likes most, but Howard gets greedy with covered second reads and attempts prohibitive throws. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees on the physicality and calls him a good runner and a real red-zone rushing asset, but says the Ohio State offense should have been far better given [[TreVeyon Henderson]], [[Quinshon Judkins]], [[Jeremiah Smith]] and [[Emeka Egbuka]] behind a good line, and stalled too often. Angelo's core doubt is whether Howard can run a timing-based system — Ohio State ran few intermediate timing patterns, and [[Jeremiah Smith]] was so dominant so early that he became the escape hatch whenever Howard was in trouble. Angelo projects a third-round pick who sits as a backup rather than being thrust into a starting job, with a long-term backup or eventual bridge-starter outcome; the combine interview matters a lot.
- 2025-03-28 — According to Cory Kinnan (quarterback charter, not a tracked expert) with [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-28]]): Howard grades out as a long-term reliable backup rather than a starter — Kinnan's comp is Mason Rudolph, 'you're gonna have this guy for a half decade as a reliable backup.' The oddity in the data is his deep-ball success rate of 75%, the best of the five quarterbacks charted (ahead of [[Shedeur Sanders]] and [[Jalen Milroe]] at 60%, [[Jackson Dart]] at 51% and [[Cam Ward]] at 42%), a number Koh says 'boggles the mind' given where Howard sits in the class hierarchy. Harmon volunteers that he shares the backup-tier view and has been told he is nuts for it.
- 2025-03-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-31]]) [Dynasty]: Howard has a good foundation — drops, play fakes, pump fakes and release mechanics need only small tweaks — and his arm strength is an asset that stretches the field vertically, with a good feel for when to loft the ball and let a wide-open vertical option trot under it rather than getting too fine with placement. As a decision maker Waldman calls him a mixed bag but salvageable: he has the confidence in his velocity to fire the opposite-boundary throw into a tight cover-two window, but that confidence borders on arrogance and Waldman expects it to translate the same way in the NFL, so he may need to revise his perceived limits on what he can attempt. The largest conceptual weakness is losing track of defenders or ignoring their favorable leverage. Verdict: enough to develop into a capable backup if a team expects his game to sing.
- 2025-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: Howard has traits — speed, arm talent, size and enough ability as a thrower — that could make him a good backup and eventually a journeyman starter if he cleans up his diagnostic work, though Waldman is skeptical it happens. He frames the arc bluntly: the idea a year earlier that Howard might be one of the top quarterbacks in this class was 'laughable,' but you are not laughing when you consider him as a developmental project who could turn into a journeyman starter. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) set the segment up as a joke about Howard being a Frankenstein blend of Josh Allen and Tim Tebow.
- 2025-04-11 — According to guest Cory Kinnan of Daft on Draft (not a tracked expert), on [[Matt Harmon]]'s show ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-11]]): the John Gruden 'young [[Josh Allen]]' comp is wrong — Howard (6'4', 236, 4,000 yards, 35 TD, 10 INT at Ohio State) is closer to a slightly more athletic Mason Rudolph, a big, efficient, high-floor passer with a smaller arm than his frame suggests. Kinnan calls the profile Jekyll-and-Hyde: half the charting sample comes from the four-game playoff run, where Howard suddenly threw anticipatory seam and dig balls over the middle he had not shown in four years at Kansas State or all season at Ohio State, while the Penn State, Michigan and first Oregon games were not good. He avoided the middle of the field for most of the year, which Kinnan warns is harder in the NFL with narrower hashes. On the 76.9% nine-route success rate, Kinnan credits anticipation and receiver tracking — [[Jeremiah Smith]], Emeka Egbuka and Carnell Tate coached by Brian Hartline to throttle down and stay stacked — over arm talent, and says Howard's velocity into intermediate tight windows is average. Roughly a 4.8 athlete with some juice; Chip Kelly's diverse play-action scheme helps, but he is a work in progress at 24 and profiles as a survivable backup you can install a high-floor plan around.
- 2026-01-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-26]]) [Dynasty]: with Mike McCarthy now the Steelers' head coach and Aaron Rodgers' return uncertain, Waldman jokes Howard is 'young Frankensteined with an abnormal brain' from a quarterbacking standpoint, but says there is real physical talent and that Howard offers more as a passer than Tim Tebow did at a similar developmental stage. He is a clear hold in deep leagues and, at low cost, a plausible quick-profit stash given Pittsburgh currently lacks a settled long-term answer at quarterback. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) notes Howard's size (listed 6'4', 230) and mobility as part of the appeal.
- 2026-03-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-09]]) [Dynasty]: with reports Pittsburgh could develop Howard if Aaron Rodgers moves on (Rodgers himself has praised Howard's 'mindset and moxie'), Waldman says Howard clears his personal bar for legitimate starter potential -- a 76 on his depth-of-talent scale, just above the threshold he uses. He jokingly comped Howard's skill blend to crossing Josh Allen's and Tim Tebow's DNA to produce an Andrew Luck (calling Howard the 'Young Frankenstein' version) -- velocity and footwork are there, and he's a confident, borderline-arrogant athlete who can extend plays -- but decision-making is still 'a mixed bag,' albeit salvageable with more experience. Waldman compared the big-armed, escape-artist archetype Pittsburgh covets in Howard to past Steelers QBs like Terry Bradshaw or Ben Roethlisberger.
