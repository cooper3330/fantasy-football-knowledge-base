# Marvin Harrison Jr.

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023, when Harrison was a draft-eligible Ohio State
> receiver and had not yet been drafted. Treat as historical pre-draft scouting
> context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]: prefers [[Malik Nabers]] "by a healthy margin," while being explicit that this is not a pan — Harrison "could very well end up being the most productive rookie in this class if he finds the right fit." His two named weaknesses: "he doesn't take contact over the middle of the field, he doesn't protect himself well enough," and "he's not as good after the catch as people think he is." He is also "not the mover that Nabers is, which I think is going to limit his route tree initially." Angelo's compressed verdict: "I don't think he's as good of a player as he is a prospect."
- 2023-12-21 — **Angelo**, same episode, on deployment and ceiling: at 6'3"–6'4" with real speed he will win on go balls and posts immediately ("very Big Bird"), and he is "a very unique linear athlete because of his speed and how good of an accelerator he is," which is where the higher ceiling comes from. But until he proves he can beat NFL No. 1 corners one-on-one, "he's not going to be a true [X]" — Angelo would move him into the slot early to acclimate. His stated frustration is with the discourse rather than the player: "there's this aura around Harrison Jr. right now that I just don't understand as much."
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, the technique case behind the learning-curve call, framed as "concern relative to the buzz." **(1) Releases:** playing outside, "he opens his chest up too often to the defender as he's trying to work off the line and doesn't provide a move" — he owns footwork releases but doesn't always use them, and gets pinned to the boundary or fails to overcome contact. Waldman cites Penn State and Michigan in 2022 and Georgia at the end of 2023. **(2) Catch windows:** he waits for the latest possible arrival, often turning it into an over-the-shoulder grab rather than catching at a high point with an overhand position — where a Chris Olave will vary which window he attacks depending on coverage. Waldman offers two possible motives (maximizing YAC by turning downfield, or comfort) without claiming to know which. **(3) Attack position:** "he can catch overhand, but he likes to catch underhand — that's his default attack position," which lets the ball into his frame, invites break-ups against tight coverage, and produces drops "you wouldn't expect a prospect of his skills" to have. Waldman's exceptions to the underhand rule are Terry McLaurin among current players and Marvin Harrison Sr. historically ("everything you would say don't do — but he never dropped the ball"), with the caveat that "you afford yourself a little leeway if you do anything at 99th percentile." He calls all three items correctable.
- 2023-12-21 — **Both hosts**, same episode, the non-evaluative wrinkle: there was live speculation Harrison might return to Ohio State for a senior season on NIL money. Raised as a scenario that would "dramatically shift what some of these front offices are seeing," not as a prediction. See [[NIL and Player Development]].
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): first Reception Perception charting look, an explicitly early and non-final 3-game sample ("cannot hold me responsible... this is not the final product"). Estimated 6'4"/205 lb. Praises his release and how he gets in and out of breaks against man coverage for his size, and says his press-coverage success rate through three games is on pace to be the best of the last four draft classes if it holds. Two flagged technique items, both graded "good, not elite": can be a step slow getting out of breaks specifically against zone coverage, and occasionally lets his size create overconfidence at the catch point in contested situations rather than winning cleanly on technique. Explicitly not built like his more slender father, Marvin Harrison Sr. — "a damn pterodactyl out there." Independently corroborates Waldman/Angelo's read that he isn't yet a proven true X against elite man coverage, while agreeing the ceiling case is obvious. See [[Reception Perception Methodology]].

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]), on combine-workout skepticism generally: cites Harrison — who will skip all combine workouts and is also going without an agent — as the correct call for a prospect who's "done all that he needs to do" on film. Argues that gaming a workout (e.g., cutting weight to look faster in a drill that isn't representative of his actual game) would only cost him development time on things that matter more. See [[NFL Combine and Pro Day Skepticism]].
- 2024-02-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-27]]): a more skeptical gloss on the same decision than Waldman's the day before — Harmon's "antenna is raised" specifically by the addition of skipping his Ohio State pro day too (the "trifecta," on top of no agent and no combine), a step beyond precedents like DeVonta Smith/Jalen Waddle skipping only the combine. Says he personally has no questions about Harrison that combine drills would answer, and cites reported testing numbers (6'3"/206 lb, 380 lb bench for 22 reps at 225, a 3.94 pro shuttle, 10'9" broad jump, 23 mph top GPS speed, via Albert Breer) as evidence teams already have what they need. Still plans to give top-10 rankings to all three of Harrison, [[Malik Nabers]] and [[Rome Odunze]], declining to rank them 1-2-3 against each other and arguing landing spot will matter more than the order — see [[Dynasty]].

- 2024-03-04 — According to **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]] (*Feel It or F**k It*): highlights Harrison's own stated reason for skipping combine drills — "I'm actually preparing for real football" — calling it the best justification he's heard from any prospect for opting out of testing. See [[NFL Combine and Pro Day Skepticism]].

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]) [Dynasty]: same landing-spot risk framing as [[Malik Nabers]] and [[Rome Odunze]] — flagged as a likely fit for critical-need top-10 teams (Tennessee, Giants, New England), with the same dynasty caution that a weak offensive landing spot could suppress his rookie-year outlook regardless of pre-draft grade.

- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast on the 2024 WR class A-Z ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): his release game will need refinement against press man. He has violent hands and a patient-but-sudden footwork combination — "not far away" from consistently winning separation — but shows wasted movement out of his stance, and against top corners, opening his chest too early to the defender could let a savvy pro cornerback reroute or jam him off his route. The (not-so-)hidden strength of his game is that he's physical enough, with enough deep speed on late/vertical routes, to keep working through contact — a terrific quality, but with narrow margin for error against savvy corners: he needs to be on schedule early in his routes for it to show up, or he'll need a quarterback with a lot of faith in him to fit the ball in anyway — the same risk profile Waldman describes for [[Keon Coleman]].

- 2024-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-26]]) [Dynasty]: full profile now live; declines to call him a clear-cut No. 1, framing him and [[Malik Nabers]] as "very different" prospect types rather than one being flatly better — Harrison a "pure vertical X receiver" (78.9% of sampled snaps outside/on the line) versus Nabers's slot-heavy explosiveness. Career-high 16.8% double-coverage rate, the highest in Harmon's four-plus-class database, with a 70% success rate beating those doubles — also the best mark among doubled players. Success rates: 83rd percentile vs. man, 87th percentile vs. press (pushes back on public criticism of his press-coverage tape), 80th percentile vs. zone; 85.2% on slants, 82% on corners, 62% on nines. Compares him to [[Ja'Marr Chase]] as the last true No. 1-X-receiver prospect and says Harrison's profile is "a little bit better" coming in as a separator/technician. Grades him tier one and is genuinely torn between ranking Harrison or [[Rome Odunze]] as his class WR1, with "a damn good chance" he lands on Odunze.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): in a
  discussion of top-10 landing spots, gives Harrison the "slight edge" as the
  draft class's number-one receiver specifically for Arizona — "the evaluation
  is so clean... he's a legitimate difference maker from day one." Projects
  him paired with [[Trey McBride]] and [[Michael Wilson]] in an Arizona passing
  game that "now has something." Discusses moving him inside off pure X reps
  on occasion (given his size/separation traits) while sliding [[Michael Wilson]]
  into the X role — a reversal of how the previous staff misused [[Marquise Brown]]
  running in-breaking/slant routes not suited to his game. Notes: this "slight
  edge to Harrison" call runs counter to Harmon's 2024-03-26 full-profile lean
  toward [[Rome Odunze]] as his personal class WR1 — the hosts remain genuinely
  split between the two even after full profiles were live.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]), reacting to [[Rome Odunze]] visiting the Cardinals: reads Harrison as physically and stylistically closer to the DeAndre Hopkins/Larry Fitzgerald archetype — a "super great player" who does what he does very well but is more limited overall (lacking great top-end speed) compared to Odunze's more complete, Davante-Adams-type skill set.
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]) [Dynasty]: named Marvin Harrison Jr. -- his top-ranked player in the class -- best overall route runner when including the "big three," edging out [[Rome Odunze]] (whom he calls the class's leader in success rate vs. man/press with an all-green route tree) on overall polish and alignment versatility. Cites a 78.7% overall success rate, 83.2% vs. zone, 74% vs. man, and a 25.6% press rate faced on his sample routes. Singles out his slant-route technique as "a thing of beauty" that should translate to easy rookie touchdowns, crediting precise, well-timed releases that make him "weirdly polished" for a college player -- attributing some of that polish to being the son of a former NFL receiver. Notes he wins primarily as a true X (78.9% of snaps outside, 78.9% on the line) but believes he can win from any alignment.
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: rated Harrison Jr.'s 2024 opportunity as the best of the rookie WR class's top three, given Kyler Murray as his quarterback and Trey McBride plus other decent receivers taking pressure off him as 'the man.' He projected a floor of roughly 800 yards and 8-10 touchdowns even in a modest season — 'still good enough to be a starting wide receiver in most fantasy leagues' — while noting Harrison likely won't hit a 1,400-yard ceiling in year one specifically because the offense doesn't need to force volume to him the way Malik Nabers' Giants situation might.
- 2024-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-30]]) [Dynasty][Best Ball]: Harmon calls Harrison Jr. his No. 1 ranked prospect over his last four draft classes, an elite press-man beater as a pure X with essentially no weaknesses, and says he could be a tier-one dynasty receiver within two months of the season. On redraft/best ball, Harmon and Koh (untracked) place him around WR8-9, behind CeeDee Lamb, Ja'Marr Chase, Tyreek Hill, Justin Jefferson, Amon-Ra St. Brown, Puka Nacua, A.J. Brown and Garrett Wilson, but ahead of Chris Olave, Mike Evans and Deebo Samuel in Harmon's eyes. They project a conservative floor of ~130 targets on a Cardinals offense they peg at ~500 pass attempts (25% target share), translating to an 'easy' 80 catches, 1,100-1,200 yards, and 8-10 touchdowns with Kyler Murray, who they note closed 2023 on a strong note (6:1 TD:INT, 100+ passer rating over his final three games). Both hosts urge fans not to fade him as a rookie, calling him a top-15 WR bare minimum given quarterback quality relative to draft classmates landing with Gardner Minshew, Anthony Richardson, or worse.
