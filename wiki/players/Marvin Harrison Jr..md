# Marvin Harrison Jr.

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023, when Harrison was a draft-eligible Ohio State
> receiver and had not yet been drafted. Treat as historical pre-draft scouting
> context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]: prefers [[Malik Nabers]] "by a healthy margin," while being explicit that this is not a pan — Harrison "could very well end up being the most productive rookie in this class if he finds the right fit." His two named weaknesses: "he doesn't take contact over the middle of the field, he doesn't protect himself well enough," and "he's not as good after the catch as people think he is." He is also "not the mover that Nabers is, which I think is going to limit his route tree initially." Angelo's compressed verdict: "I don't think he's as good of a player as he is a prospect."
- 2023-12-21 — **Angelo**, same episode, on deployment and ceiling: at 6'3"–6'4" with real speed he will win on go balls and posts immediately ("very Big Bird"), and he is "a very unique linear athlete because of his speed and how good of an accelerator he is," which is where the higher ceiling comes from. But until he proves he can beat NFL No. 1 corners one-on-one, "he's not going to be a true [X]" — Angelo would move him into the slot early to acclimate. His stated frustration is with the discourse rather than the player: "there's this aura around Harrison Jr. right now that I just don't understand as much."
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, the technique case behind the learning-curve call, framed as "concern relative to the buzz." **(1) Releases:** playing outside, "he opens his chest up too often to the defender as he's trying to work off the line and doesn't provide a move" — he owns footwork releases but doesn't always use them, and gets pinned to the boundary or fails to overcome contact. Waldman cites Penn State and Michigan in 2022 and Georgia at the end of 2023. **(2) Catch windows:** he waits for the latest possible arrival, often turning it into an over-the-shoulder grab rather than catching at a high point with an overhand position — where a Chris Olave will vary which window he attacks depending on coverage. Waldman offers two possible motives (maximizing YAC by turning downfield, or comfort) without claiming to know which. **(3) Attack position:** "he can catch overhand, but he likes to catch underhand — that's his default attack position," which lets the ball into his frame, invites break-ups against tight coverage, and produces drops "you wouldn't expect a prospect of his skills" to have. Waldman's exceptions to the underhand rule are Terry McLaurin among current players and Marvin Harrison Sr. historically ("everything you would say don't do — but he never dropped the ball"), with the caveat that "you afford yourself a little leeway if you do anything at 99th percentile." He calls all three items correctable.
- 2023-12-21 — **Both hosts**, same episode, the non-evaluative wrinkle: there was live speculation Harrison might return to Ohio State for a senior season on NIL money. Raised as a scenario that would "dramatically shift what some of these front offices are seeing," not as a prediction. See [[NIL and Player Development]].
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): first Reception Perception charting look, an explicitly early and non-final 3-game sample ("cannot hold me responsible... this is not the final product"). Estimated 6'4"/205 lb. Praises his release and how he gets in and out of breaks against man coverage for his size, and says his press-coverage success rate through three games is on pace to be the best of the last four draft classes if it holds. Two flagged technique items, both graded "good, not elite": can be a step slow getting out of breaks specifically against zone coverage, and occasionally lets his size create overconfidence at the catch point in contested situations rather than winning cleanly on technique. Explicitly not built like his more slender father, Marvin Harrison Sr. — "a damn pterodactyl out there." Independently corroborates Waldman/Angelo's read that he isn't yet a proven true X against elite man coverage, while agreeing the ceiling case is obvious. See [[Reception Perception Methodology]].

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]), on combine-workout skepticism generally: cites Harrison — who will skip all combine workouts and is also going without an agent — as the correct call for a prospect who's "done all that he needs to do" on film. Argues that gaming a workout (e.g., cutting weight to look faster in a drill that isn't representative of his actual game) would only cost him development time on things that matter more. See [[NFL Combine and Pro Day Skepticism]].
- 2024-02-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-27]]): a more skeptical gloss on the same decision than Waldman's the day before — Harmon's "antenna is raised" specifically by the addition of skipping his Ohio State pro day too (the "trifecta," on top of no agent and no combine), a step beyond precedents like DeVonta Smith/Jalen Waddle skipping only the combine. Says he personally has no questions about Harrison that combine drills would answer, and cites reported testing numbers (6'3"/206 lb, 380 lb bench for 22 reps at 225, a 3.94 pro shuttle, 10'9" broad jump, 23 mph top GPS speed, via Albert Breer) as evidence teams already have what they need. Still plans to give top-10 rankings to all three of Harrison, [[Malik Nabers]] and [[Rome Odunze]], declining to rank them 1-2-3 against each other and arguing landing spot will matter more than the order — see [[Dynasty]].
