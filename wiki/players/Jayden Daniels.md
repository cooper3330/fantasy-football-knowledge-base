# Jayden Daniels

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. Team/role are as of that
> date and are almost certainly stale — treat as historical pre-draft
> scouting context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: "feel it" — sets a floor of at least a second-contract NFL starter, with a real chance to clear that bar. Draws the inevitable [[Lamar Jackson]] comparison explicitly, while grading Daniels a notch below Jackson in pocket play and explosiveness — "in the neighborhood" rather than equal. Not calling him a Day 1 high-level starter, but expects a real opportunity to start early and grow into the role.
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]) [Dynasty]: agrees Daniels would benefit from a bench year, but for a specific, narrower reason than a general physical-development case — a "slender" build plays a small part, but the larger issue is that NFL defenses will show him disguised coverage looks he hasn't had to process with confidence yet, and that gap is what a redshirt year would actually address. Grades him ahead of [[Drake Maye]] on how well he's already processing the field ("done a better job of processing" than Maye "has seen... but not necessarily done well"), while still wanting only a one-year runway for Daniels versus a two-year one for Maye. Reiterates Daniels as part of the top-three quarterback group mocks are consistently producing alongside [[Caleb Williams]] and Maye.

- 2024-03-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-08]]) [Dynasty], solo cast on the full QB class: flags his processing of coverage on multi-read progressions as the big debate point — lacked timing and patience with too many routes based on the coverage, and repeatedly turned away from a spot just as the receiver came open. Calls it "comically tragic timing," the same issue Dak Prescott had at Mississippi State, and thinks Daniels needs a scheme with surrounding talent that lets him find early success on quick decisions and gradually build confidence with progression reads — not necessarily easy without an experienced infrastructure like the one Prescott had in Dallas, or bench time like [[Jordan Love]] got behind Aaron Rodgers. If Daniels earns the playing time to develop those skills, or his team gives him training wheels (less than [[Lamar Jackson]] needed in Baltimore), Waldman thinks he has the tools and accuracy to become a competent NFL starter — "and may even be a very good fantasy option because of his running upside."


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: grouped with [[Michael Penix Jr.]] as the vertical-throwing fits Minnesota's [[Justin Jefferson]]/[[T.J. Hockenson]] pass game would benefit from most among the post-[[Kirk Cousins]] quarterback options being discussed.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: floats him as a possible New England trade-back target at No. 3 if the Patriots decide to pass on [[Drake Maye]] — seeing Daniels as the more athletic, pocket-mobile quarterback fit for a team that wants to spread the ball around.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]): adds Daniels to his "coach killer" candidate list (see [[Coach Killer Prospects]]) — pushes back on an automatic [[Lamar Jackson]] parallel, arguing Jackson was actually the best pure pocket passer in that class on tape and got an unnecessarily long "baby step" runway in Baltimore; worries the league will either rush Daniels expecting fast processing he doesn't yet have, or over-correct and stair-step him so long he never sheds the label. On tape: flashes multi-read progressions but is consistently "a half-beat too late" recognizing when a route has cleared and a hitch would spring it open — the same issue Waldman has flagged in [[Dak Prescott]] at Mississippi State. Calls him a more limited runner than Jackson, closer to a Marcus Mariota outcome (not tracked). Reads Washington (established receivers, room to sit) as a much better fit than New England (weapon-starved, will rush him). [[Brandon Angelo]], same episode: notes Daniels's loaded 2023 LSU receiving corps — [[Malik Nabers]] and [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] — created a "perfect storm" that could mask timing issues either way, since neither receiver needed pinpoint timing to win (Nabers on YAC, Thomas on separation).
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and untracked co-host Bob Harris ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]) [Best Ball]: The two disagree on which rookie QB is most likely to finish a top-12 fantasy QB1 in 2024. Bob Harris picks Jayden Daniels, expecting him to get the most early opportunities and a scheme built around his legs plus some vertical passing, though he stops short of calling him a true rookie QB1. Waldman instead names Caleb Williams as most likely (see [[Caleb Williams]]), but separately says he personally likes Daniels' higher rushing floor best -- comparing his fit with [[Terry McLaurin]] and [[Jahan Dotson]] to Kyler Murray's early Kliff Kingsbury seasons -- and projects more weekly QB1-spike weeks from Daniels even while agreeing Williams' talent and offensive-line upgrades are legitimate.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is buying Jayden Daniels as Washington's Week 1 starter over Marcus Mariota, giving Mariota an over/under of three to four games played (if not started) before Daniels takes over — 'unless Daniels struggles mightily.' He reiterates QB1 upside driven by rushing, calling Daniels a 'lottery ticket' comparable to [[Anthony Richardson]] for rushing equity, though unlike Richardson, Daniels came into the league already carrying a big-arm passing reputation. Waldman frames Daniels as a low-end fantasy QB1 this year on rushing-aided production alone.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo picks Daniels as a player who will look great in shorts during camp — praising his spin and ball placement — but expects a rocky rookie season because Washington's supporting cast beyond [[Terry McLaurin]] and Kliff Kingsbury's offense project to finish in the bottom third of the league in scoring, unlike [[Caleb Williams]]'s much stronger Chicago infrastructure. He notes McLaurin isn't the true WR1 he'd want for a rookie QB, and that beyond him Washington lacks trustworthy weapons ([[Jahan Dotson]], [[Ben Sinnott]], late-career [[Austin Ekeler]], [[Brian Robinson Jr.]]). Waldman agrees, adding that some evaluators are overestimating Daniels' current field-processing level — he expects improvement but not a year-one leap.
- 2024-06-18 — Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) says he is drafting Jayden Daniels as his QB1 in Washington and has him ranked 11th at the position, calling him 'this year's Anthony Richardson' - a cheap lottery ticket - because the list of backups he's comfortable playing after Daniels is long; Harris ranks Daniels ahead of Bryce Young. According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]): Waldman is more skeptical, arguing Daniels' processing is 'a little overrated' and questioning OC Kliff Kingsbury's NFL track record; he expects Kingsbury to scheme around Daniels with misdirection, RPOs and simplified reads rather than ask him to diagnose the field, comparing his likely outcome to Marcus Mariota rather than Kyler Murray or Lamar Jackson, and warning defenses will 'get fooled once, but not twice' by scheme-heavy answers. Waldman does credit Washington's better offensive line and receiving corps relative to Carolina's last year. Harris counters that Kingsbury got Kyler Murray to a top-2/3 QB finish in his second season and isn't worried about a Kingsbury ceiling on a mobile passer.
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he's tempering enthusiasm on Daniels relative to the market, saying 'people are overrating' him and 'not factoring in him being a rookie nearly enough' -- possibly skewed by Anthony Richardson's small-sample hype. His math: only two rookie QBs in the last decade have rushed 75+ times while hitting 300 pass attempts -- Kyler Murray and Josh Allen -- and they scored 17.3 and 17.8 fantasy points/game as rookies; Daniels' current ADP implies a 17.1 point/game expectation, meaning he's being drafted near his likely floor rather than at a discount. His stance: fine as a double-digit-round pick given the rushing floor (2,000+ rushing yards each of his last two college seasons), but he'll fade Daniels if his price climbs into low-end QB1 territory (~QB10), arguing rookie QBs 'generally do suck in fantasy football' and pointing to CJ Stroud, who didn't hit 19 points/game in his acclaimed rookie year.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon said Daniels 'throws the ball well downfield' and is 'a strong vertical passer,' which maps well stylistically to [[Terry McLaurin]]'s skill set, but noted he has 'long-term questions' about Daniels overall. Harmon's central worry is scheme-and-protection dependent: if Washington's bottom-five offensive line (per guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn, not tracked) can't hold up on true drop-backs, it limits how much the Daniels-McLaurin connection can be maximized, making a heavier run-first, condensed-formation approach under new OC Kliff Kingsbury important for Daniels' rookie development.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): if forced to choose between the two rookies for a top-10 fantasy quarterback finish, Waldman takes a healthy Daniels over [[Caleb Williams]], with the caveat that you pay more for Daniels and that the case rests on rushing production. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) took the other side of a proposed friendly bet, arguing Williams is the better player with the better receiving corps, though he acknowledged positive Washington camp talk including on [[Terry McLaurin]]. Waldman's stated risk on Daniels is durability — he warned Daniels may not be that same downfield runner after a couple of big hits and invoked Marcus Mariota as the cautionary path, saying 'be careful' about counting on the legs to hold up across a season.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he is 'totally feeling' the Robert Griffin III 2.0 comp — both straight-line runners who did not always know how to protect themselves, and Daniels has shown no sign of learning it, with Dan Quinn putting him on double secret probation for failing to slide in the first two preseason games and [[Austin Ekeler]] publicly saying he needs to tell Daniels to give himself up. Waldman does not think the processing will look any faster against complex defenses, so he expects Washington to simplify to one-read and occasional two-read concepts, which may work this year. His verdict on price is mixed within the same segment: he first says people are going a little overboard on where he is being drafted, then agrees with Football Guys colleague Dave Kluge that Daniels is one of three league-winning-upside QBs alongside [[Anthony Richardson]] (rounds 5-7) and [[Kyler Murray]], and concludes Daniels gives the least upside of the three but is appropriately valued in round 9-10, so it is worth a shot.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): he is going 'all in' on not being sold on Daniels. Watching the preseason he saw a lot of first-read throws and RPOs — Cliff Kingsbury at its finest — a scheme that supports what Daniels does best and hides his weakness, but that does not develop him as an NFL quarterback. Waldman splits with Greg Cosell and Doug Farrar, who thought Daniels a better processor than expected; Waldman says he is a beat to a beat-and-a-half slow between first-to-second and second-to-third reads, and misses route/coverage combinations where one hitch would let him anticipate a deep crosser or over route instead of turning his back and spinning out to run. He also flags Daniels running into brick walls again in preseason, [[Austin Ekeler]] publicly telling him that taking hits is the back's job, and Washington's thin supporting cast — [[Terry McLaurin]], an unsettled No. 2, [[Zach Ertz]] likely starting. Comp: Marcus Mariota — a hot start of three-to-five big games with three-to-five touchdown noise, then defenses adjust. [[Brandon Angelo]] agreed the system is not as quarterback-friendly as advertised.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): 'fuck it' on Daniels as a year-one fantasy starter. Waldman does not believe in Cliff Kingsbury's predictable, low-motion offense, does not believe in the receiving corps, and does not think Daniels is a good manipulator or processor at this stage. He dismisses the preseason hype as a false positive — one-read plays against defenders who will be in the UFL within 18 months — and compares the profile to [[Will Levis]] and Marcus Mariota. He calls Daniels a Robert Griffin III / Mariota straight-line runner who needs open field, less able in the pocket and a worse processor than [[Kyler Murray]], a QB Waldman has never loved. He says he may not even give [[Justin Fields]]-like early production; if Waldman is wrong it will be because Daniels gets away with running. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) pushed back hard, citing Murray's rookie year under Kingsbury (3,722 yards, 20 TD, 12 INT, 500 rushing yards, QB9) and QB2 with 24 points per game in year two — the 'Konami code' argument that running QBs score regardless. Waldman's counter-worry is whether the slender Daniels will avoid contact enough to survive.
- 2024-08-27 — According to guest Derek Klassen (The Athletic; not a tracked expert) and host James Koh (not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): both push back on the near-universal preseason hype. Koh says the community is treating Daniels as if 2024 will be a repeat of [[C.J. Stroud]]'s 2023 and warns that once first-string defenders and real defensive coordinators show up he may get stuffed. Klassen says he had more questions about Daniels than most, and pointed to a second preseason game rep against Miami where a boundary-side pressure came and Daniels never tried to replace the blitz, just throwing a go ball to the opposite side — 'probably bad process' he refused to excuse as Kliff Kingsbury coaching him to get the ball out. On the positive side Klassen's charting has Daniels excellent in the one-to-five-yard area on hitches and flats, and good on slot fades and go balls outside the numbers — exactly what Kingsbury calls. The caveats: Washington may lack the personnel to run any of it well, and has arguably the league's worst offensive line.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman confirmed he is a big fan of Daniels' passing ability, 'especially downfield — it's great when he does that,' while naming his reservation precisely: the quickness of the decision-making. Untracked co-host Bob Harris said he does not necessarily disagree on the decision-making concern but agrees on the downfield ability, and joked that Daniels will be a recurring battleground between the two of them this season. The context was whether [[Dyami Brown]] can be a usable three-receiver-lineup piece off Daniels' deep ball.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Going Deep)]]): Daniels is his own 'hot start, slower finish' pick. Waldman expects Kliff Kingsbury to lean on play action, RPOs and misdirection off a strong running game to put Daniels in half-field reads early, which should produce fast production. The concern is the counter-punch: once defenses take that away, Daniels has to prove at NFL level that he can get to a second and third read and avoid running into a brick wall. Waldman frames the fork explicitly — is this a [[Robert Griffin III]]/Marcus Mariota outcome, or a quarterback who can read the full field and make good decisions with his legs as a bonus rather than a crutch. He is 'a little worried' about that transition mid-season.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Film and Theory)]]) [Dynasty]: Daniels is the storyline he is most looking forward to this season — he made him his first-round rookie pick in a one-quarterback dynasty league where he already rosters [[Patrick Mahomes]], [[Dak Prescott]], [[Deshaun Watson]] and [[Bryce Young]], so it was a pure want rather than a need. The open question he wants answered is how much of Daniels is the system: he frames it as whether Daniels turns out to be a Marcus Mariota or [[Will Levis]] type of player whose production is scheme-dependent, versus something genuinely new. Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) shared the enthusiasm, saying he prefers the potentially revelatory prospect to the safer one.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Daniels's most reliable weekly fantasy value early on is garbage time, and Waldman feels that. Washington's defense may not be good, the offensive line is a problem, and Daniels's pocket management means many plays are schemed to a second read he isn't yet quick to reach. The running and the deep ball are clearly there, but stringing it together for big yardage games won't happen often; instead, when the team falls behind he'll get easy underneath throws and be allowed to run for contained 10-15-20-yard gains. That plays as a solid QB2 you can stream in one-quarterback leagues, provided he isn't taking a beating as a runner — Waldman was glad to see the modified tumble slide but would prefer an actual slide, and more deep passes. He notes fantasy points were not the problem in week one.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): a rocky debut in which Harmon says Daniels got the ball out in 2.35 seconds — the opposite of the live-armed, attack-every-blade-of-grass playmaker he was billed as — and he compares the feel to [[Anthony Richardson]]'s Colts debut a year earlier while stressing the two are not the same (Daniels has an absurd amount of college starting experience, Richardson had almost none). Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is blunter on accuracy: the LSU ball placement was fine, but in game one he missed receivers 'by a wide, wide margin,' including a wide-open [[Terry McLaurin]] down the sideline that was out of the area code, and says he's worried about the long-term accuracy. Both split the blame with the situation: Harmon has 'bad vibes' about the whole Washington operation, calls the receiver room outside McLaurin brutal, says the two leading receivers were the running backs, and blames Cliff Kingsbury's unchanged scheme. Koh: 'that's how broken franchises stay broken.'
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-12]]): Daniels' debut indicated he will be a good garbage-time quarterback — Washington will trail, defenses will play softer looks in second halves, and he should regularly bank 60-90 rushing yards plus underneath work and the occasional score. Waldman calls that a [[Kyler Murray]]-esque data profile: good fantasy QB numbers that may not support any one Commanders receiver enough to keep them relevant. He liked the deep ball and the first read, saw little second-read work with a lot manufactured, and noted he sometimes held the ball too long, which is typical of rookies — so there is hope, but Waldman is not ready to call him the top-five redraft value people are drafting late. Harstad (NOT tracked) sees much more fantasy hope than NFL hope, comparing it to a vintage 2022 [[Justin Fields]] game (QB6 on ~150 passing yards per game); [Dynasty] he says such a season would leave him feeling good about 2024-2026 but concerned about 2027.
