# Blake Watson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]), on Shrine Game prospects: a transfer (from Old Dominion to Memphis) Waldman calls "very underrated," comped to a Giovanni Bernard/[[Austin Ekeler]] archetype — explicitly speculates Bernard would have had a much different career had he entered the league in Ekeler's draft class instead of his own. Praised as a good all-around back: can catch, runs effectively between the tackles, and is a pretty good blocker. If he tests well athletically at the combine, Waldman thinks he could go earlier than people currently expect.
- 2024-02-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]) [Dynasty]: refines the archetype call — "an aspiring Gio Bernard," someone who plays a little bigger than his size, good pad level, a legitimately good receiver in the open field, and effective enough between the tackles as a tight-crease runner. Explicit ceiling calibration: doesn't project as the next [[Austin Ekeler]] or reach Bernard's own level, but is the type of player who could get there if everything breaks right; more realistically, a role player who could "carve out a role if he's consistent." Cites Memphis's running back pipeline (Tony Pollard, Kenneth Gainwell, and others) as a program that continually churns out quality if unspectacular backs, with Watson as the latest example.

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]): notes Watson was **not** invited to the combine, unlike similarly-profiled [[Deshaun Fenwick]], and reads this as likely reflecting limited team interest rather than a real ceiling signal — per The Athletic's Chad Reuter (not tracked; ASR rendered this "Chad Ryder"), teams submit invite request lists and Watson apparently didn't draw enough of them, though Reuter also notes three third-round players were snubbed from the combine the year before, so the invite list isn't infallible. Restates the Gio Bernard comp, adding that Bernard's real value, properly contextualized, is "closer to Austin Ekeler than people may want to think."
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast: reaffirms Watson as agile and quick with a "gliding" quality to his acceleration; lacks true power but graded well on the RSP's own contact-metrics tracking. Understands how to attack half a defender's frame and keeps his knees/feet high to slip leg shots — traits Waldman expects to translate well both in space and between the tackles.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): grades him the best actual pass-catcher of the class's receiving backs (behind [[Dylan Laube]] and [[Will Shipley]] in overall tier), though his route running still needs work to reach their level. The question is whether his NFL ceiling lands closer to Chase Edmonds or to a Gio Bernard-turned-[[Austin Ekeler]] outcome — Angelo notes Gio Bernard himself may have been "eight to ten years too early" for the modern league. Runs well in zone or gap schemes, identifies problems early, and is efficient with his solutions; lacks true power but knows how to take on half a defender's frame in tight quarters.

- 2024-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Feel It or F@#k It* ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-01]]): calls him underrated — not among draft-analyst circles necessarily, but certainly relative to broader awareness. Also not 200 lbs, but physical for his size, runs with good pad level, breaks tackles, and is excellent in the open field for quickness and speed; reportedly ran a 4.39 at his pro day (relayed by co-host **Bob Harris**, not a tracked expert). Grades him "probably the best pass catcher in this draft," though his route running still needs to improve; already a very good pass protector. Restates the Gio Bernard comp from earlier episodes, saying Bernard would have looked like [[Austin Ekeler]] had he entered the league ~8 years later — "Blake Watson might be right on time."
- 2024-05-02 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): Blake Watson was his top UDFA pick, landing in a Sean Payton scheme that specifically needs a running back who can run angle and option routes and beat linebackers in coverage — a skill Payton prized in Drew Brees-era backs and wants from [[Bo Nix]]'s catchable, post-catch-friendly throws. Waldman argued Watson is the only back in Denver's room who offers that receiving skill set: not [[Javonte Williams]], not [[Audric Estime]], and not Jaleel McLaughlin, whom Waldman called a good open-field, Tarik Cohen-esque player but lacking Watson's timing and pace as a route runner. Waldman said Watson can provide that receiving dimension from day one if called upon.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo likes Watson in Denver given Sean Payton's own comments that every back he brings in earns a real role through competition — Angelo cites Payton's history with [[Alvin Kamara]] in New Orleans as the model: Kamara was buried behind Adrian Peterson as a rookie and fumbled/bounced runs outside too often until Payton pushed him to hit holes inside decisively, at which point Kamara took off and Peterson was released. Payton has since said Watson isn't a Kamara clone but shares similarities to that preferred archetype. Angelo ranks Watson one spot ahead of teammate [[Audric Estime]] on his board and flags the ADP gap as the reason to prefer him — Watson currently goes around pick 59 (end of round 5) versus roughly pick 36 for Estime. He expects Denver's backfield to shake out as either a Watson or Estime lead role, or for the team to add someone else entirely, since he believes [[Javonte Williams]] doesn't fit what Payton wants from a decisive, timing-based runner.
