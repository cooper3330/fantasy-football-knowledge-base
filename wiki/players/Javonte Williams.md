# Javonte Williams

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]): feels Williams as a real value right now, theorizing last year's underwhelming return from his catastrophic multi-ligament knee injury reflected him never getting back to full speed rather than a talent decline — the same injury-not-skill framing he applies to [[Tony Pollard]], [[Austin Ekeler]] and [[Travis Kelce]] elsewhere in this episode. Floats a possible one-two punch with backfield-mate [[Samaje Perine]] in Denver's backfield, though the specific complementary-role upside for Perine is left underexplored. Explicit caveat: multi-ligament tears like his can take a full extra year to shake off, so a slow or unconvincing summer would signal he may never quite be the same player.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo says it 'smells like' Javonte Williams isn't the fit Sean Payton wants in Denver's backfield — he can't 'see the picture' the way [[Blake Watson]] or [[Audric Estime]] might, and Payton's scheme leans on a timing-based decisiveness that Williams' downhill instincts reportedly haven't shown.
- 2024-07-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]): Williams is genuinely on the roster bubble in Denver. Bob Harris (not tracked) stacks the reporting — Cecil Lammey (not tracked) said a month earlier that [[Jaleel McLaughlin]] looked like the top back and questioned whether Williams makes the roster; Troy Renck of the Denver Post wrote that [[Samaje Perine]] and Williams are fighting for the same spot; Parker Gabriel told Harris the outcomes range from Williams and Perine again being the top two to neither making the team. Waldman's read is scheme and provenance: Sean Payton wants more patience in setting up runs and simply does not like Williams's fit, and Payton is from the Parcells school of 'his guys' — Williams is the only back in the room he did not pick. Better landing spots Waldman names: Buffalo if [[Ray Davis]] disappoints, the Giants for gap-scheme work, Cleveland behind [[Nick Chubb]], or Dallas.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]): a change of mind upward. Williams came into camp in great shape having lost weight, and Waldman weights the report because his coach called him a totally different player and outside observers he trusts — he names Cecil Lammey (not a tracked expert) on the Broncos beat — substantiate it. He now thinks Williams is poised to be the man in Denver and moved him up enough to call him a low-end starter with upside, where previously he was unsure. The caveat is the offense: he does not expect a good Denver ground game unless [[Bo Nix]] is good out of the gate and forces defenses to respect the pass.
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]): a deliberately hedged verdict. Bob Harris (not tracked) offers that 'when he's not fumbling he looks good'; Waldman will only go as far as 'he's looking better,' saying he saw moments he liked. The two settle on the compromise that whether you call him good depends on where you think he is coming from — Waldman's position is improvement from a low base, not a return to form.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]): asked whether Denver already has its 2025 starting running back on the roster, Waldman said 'fuck it' — no. He allowed that Williams is 'obviously running harder and a little bit better' than earlier in the year, but expects him gone in the offseason: 'I don't think they're going to resign him even if he's having moments that look good.' Waldman's read is that Denver dips into a well-stocked incoming rookie RB class to pair with [[Audric Estime]] and [[Jaleel McLaughlin]] rather than committing to Williams. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) framed the question and did not push back. [Dynasty]
- 2024-11-14 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14 (Age Cliffs and Dented Cans)]] [Dynasty]: Williams is his third dented can alongside [[Najee Harris]] and [[DeAndre Swift]] — the trio that was ranked as top-three-to-five dynasty backs a few years ago, which he thought was premature wish-casting at the time. His diagnosis is a measurement problem: broken tackles are the easiest element of back play to quantify superficially and are sticky year to year, so the industry priced Williams at the top because tackle-breaking is his hallmark and it was the one thing anyone could measure — but it turns out not to be the be-all end-all of the position. He is explicit that Williams 'was never as good as people wanted him to be' and will not be a star. Still, he is an unrestricted free agent after the season, some team will give him a shot, and Harstad would not be at all surprised if he follows the Swift path: written off, lands in a muddy new situation, plays well enough to earn a larger share, and finishes a top-15 to top-20 back a year from now.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]): Waldman says 'fuck it' on Dallas signing Williams and [[Miles Sanders]], and is not excited from a fantasy perspective. He concedes Williams is young enough to be a defensible risk-reward bet, but flags the ACL tear and that Williams was never a clean fit for Denver's run scheme, leaving open how he gels with Dallas's. On Sanders, he says he has 'always needed a compass to figure out where to go' and is best as a reserve — which Dallas may in fairness need. His preferred alternative: for similar money Dallas could have signed [[Nick Chubb]] and [[J.K. Dobbins]] and substantially upgraded the backfield, with one or both emerging. He adds the caveat that the offensive line may make the whole question moot, and jokes the real winners are angry Cowboys fans.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]): 'fuck it' on Williams finishing as the Cowboys' lead back. Waldman expects he may start as the lead back but doubts he ends the year as Dallas's most productive runner. The threat he names is rookie [[Jaydon Blue]], who offers more big-play ability, is a really good pass catcher and is a smart runner — with the caveat that Blue has to hold onto the football. Waldman also notes [[Phil Mafah]] could squeeze into the committee if Williams is slow getting the job done, since Mafah can run over people and is not a bad receiver in his own right, with [[Miles Sanders]] as a semi-serviceable body in the mix. He expects Williams to be part of a committee rather than a bell cow.
- 2025-05-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Williams at RB38 in the tenth round is reasonable on paper, but Waldman says 'fuck it' with [[Jaydon Blue]] in the equation — he would let others take Williams as the lead back and spend his shot on Blue several rounds later. Two reasons: Blue is the more promising runner and has likely retained more athletic ability than Williams, and the Dallas offensive line is one of the league's worst and mid-rebuild (he allowed the Falcons' quick pre-season rebuild before Devonta Freeman's big year as the rare counterexample). Waldman still projects Williams to lead the backfield in carries, targets and catches: about 178 attempts for 657 yards (3.7 YPC) and three touchdowns, plus roughly 40 catches for 225 yards. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) has shares at the 10-11 turn and called both Dallas backs values.
- 2025-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris's number three offense he may be wrong about is the Dallas backfield — specifically, what if the Cowboys have been telling us all along that it is just Javonte Williams and nobody is listening. He is careful not to oversell: he does not think Williams played well last year and cannot separate whether Denver's run game failed because every back was bad or because the situation was bad, and he is not pouncing on Williams. But the Cowboys' actions suggest they think he still has life, and no Dallas back goes in single-digit rounds in his drafts. Guest Sigmund Bloom (Footballguys, not tracked) adds that Williams is the assignment-sound, pass-blocking veteran the [[Ezekiel Elliott]] role called for, and does not have to be much better than Elliott for the team to talk itself into running the whole playbook with him — maybe no burst or wiggle left after the injury, but competent behind that line and alongside that passing game.
- 2025-08-22 — According to Cousin Josh, a recurring guest on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast]] and not a tracked expert ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Williams wins the Carlos Hyde Out of the NFL Award — Josh predicts he will not be playing in the league next season, despite being only 25, on a one-year deal with the Cowboys and going around round 10. He notes both he and [[Chris Harris]] were high on Williams last year, and is explicit that this is not irrational hatred: he says Williams seems like a really good dude and simply does not think the play is there. Harris frames it as a clear-eyed prediction rather than a joke pick, and notes the award exists because Josh once lost a bet predicting Carlos Hyde would be out of the NFL.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: The Dallas beat writers got fairly decisive in August and Williams at least seemed to win the backfield battle kind of conclusively — [[Jaydon Blue]] got helium then missed weeks with a bad ankle, [[Miles Sanders]] was silent, and [[Phil Mafah]] went to the practice squad. Despite that, Williams went only about a round ahead of Blue in most drafts, which Harris says is not reflective of how the August news broke, though he understands it because a lot of people, himself included, got burned by Williams last year.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-04]]): Williams gets the Deebo Samuel Bounce Back Huggy Award — Harris says a little piece of his heart still believes Williams isn't as bad as his detractors claim. He acknowledges the advanced efficiency metrics that paint Williams as an inefficient runner who will never be the same after the knee injury, but says he doesn't really believe those metrics and that it is hard to judge a back when an entire team's run game is bottled up: everyone else in Denver's backfield last year, including [[Jaleel McLaughlin]] and [[Audric Estime]], posted worse numbers in those same efficiency metrics. He isn't mad the Broncos moved on, and concedes the right answer may be that none of those backs were any good. Still, at the cost of a 10th-round pick, Harris calls Williams a worthwhile investment to find out — while cautioning 'maybe not so much tonight against the Eagles'.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Williams, whom Harris had given his 'bounce back huggy award' on Thursday's podcast, 'was fine' in the opener — two goal-line touchdowns, and a nice job on power runs when the Dallas line got push, which it did legitimately, though Harris repeatedly caveats that Jalen Carter's pre-snap ejection removed Philadelphia's best interior defender. When the line didn't win or the defense got his shoulders turned, 'he didn't do a lot,' and he vanished with the rest of the offense after the thunderstorm. Harris explicitly declines to call it 'some massive victory or some electric performance,' but the goal-line role is the fantasy-relevant piece, especially with [[Miles Sanders]] fumbling in the red zone and [[Jaydon Blue]] inactive.
- 2025-09-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-12]]): Williams is his number four, a positive change of mind. He concedes this is largely a volume argument — everyone came into the season with a matrix of possibilities for someone else emerging in the Dallas backfield, and the Cowboys instead came out and declared Williams their back — but says volume matters here, especially with the coach already saying [[Jaydon Blue]] needs to do more consistent things to be active on game day, and Blue apparently inactive in Week 2 with his ankle not the reason. Harris says he is not shocked given what Blue was at Texas as a player. Crucially he is also relieved Williams looked good: he never thought Williams was a bad football player in Denver, just that the results were terrible, as they were for every Denver back. His conclusion is that Javonte Williams as your flex absolutely works. He notes Williams was a flag player for him last year, and Josh had him as a warp zone player, so the vindication comes a year late.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-22]]): 'feel it' — Williams is for real this year. Waldman said he looks like Tar Heel Javonte Williams again: running hard, bleeding yards after contact, making smart decisions. He identified the run offense and the offensive line as the strength of this Cowboys team, which they will need with [[CeeDee Lamb]] possibly out. He stopped short of calling Williams a long-term RB1 — 'I don't know if he's going to stay that way' — but said he can definitely be a solid RB2 and owners should be happy with that. Waldman added that he is almost encouraged Dallas has not made [[Jaydon Blue]] active, since his Blue investments were always made on the assumption of a slow roll, but conceded that if Williams keeps playing like this those investments may never pay off.
- 2025-09-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-26]]): both hosts use Williams as trade currency — Josh offers him for [[Tetairoa McMillan]], Harris offers him one-for-one for [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] — and both explicitly say this does not mean they dislike him; they think he's good. The logic is that he was a lucky later-round pick capitalizing on big stats, and in a running-back-scarce market that makes him the piece other managers actually want. Harris rates the Williams-for-McMillan offer fair but not strong, and suggests adding the receiver McMillan would be replacing if it isn't enough.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: an explicit reversal. Harris said he gave up on Williams in the summer — he was alone on an island arguing against drafting him in the middle rounds during his last Denver year, was unconvinced by the [[Jaleel McLaughlin]] pitch, and concluded the right answer was none of the Denver backs. Now he says Williams looks really great at whatever this version of him is: probably not the breakaway threat he once was, but not slow, with some nice long runs, the ability to make a move and create space, and notably physical play, on display in the Sunday night Green Bay game. Harris carried some shares by attrition rather than conviction, and framed it as reassurance that his talent evaluation has not completely eroded, while allowing that the Dallas offense humming is partly why he looks so good. Priced $6,200 on DraftKings.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Williams was the direct beneficiary of the week's biggest bench-or-not question. With four of five Cowboys offensive line starters out, Harris's Yacht Club chat repeatedly asked whether to sit him; his answer was that he doesn't get away from regular fantasy starters over missing offensive linemen, while conceding 'that can go badly' and that you can't see the future. It did not go badly — Williams pounded in a second-quarter touchdown, ripped a 66-yard draw play (the longest run of his career) on which he was run down from behind short of the end zone, and added a second touchdown in garbage time on a sprint-out pass. There was no [[Miles Sanders]] in the game, and rookie [[Jaydon Blue]] played 11 snaps, five of them while competitive.
- 2025-10-09 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys; NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-09]]) [Dynasty]: Williams fits a running back archetype Harstad actively buys in rebuilds — highly drafted, showed enough to be interesting, but on the whole a disappointment in his first stint, and therefore heavily discounted. He grouped him with [[J.K. Dobbins]] and [[Leonard Fournette]] as 'classic dented cans' and said the play is to acquire cheaply and see whether there is a second act, because these are the assets that let a rebuilding roster gain value rather than merely hold it.
- 2025-10-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-10]]): Williams is his number one do-not-trade-away despite the insane start, a position Andy Behrens (Yahoo, NOT a tracked expert) also endorsed on Harris's Wednesday paywall show. He is RB2 in standard and RB4 in PPR and roughly fifth among backs in scrimmage yards, with 17 receptions on 19 targets — a genuine three-down role. Harris is honest that it won't stay this good because six scrimmage touchdowns are driving much of it, and he doesn't claim credit since neither host made the call strongly preseason; he ranks Williams well short of RB2 rest-of-season but 'completely usable.' Guest Cousin Josh had him for the same reason: the peak-Denver broken-tackle ability is back, he isn't afraid of [[Jaydon Blue]], [[Miles Sanders]] is dinged up and no threat, and even at 80% of this he is a scarce-position back on a good offense with a brutal defense that will have to keep scoring. Harris pushes back that a bad defense argues for the passing game, and that game flow is unknowable anyway — if the offense is good, take the attached back and be damned. Harris takes Williams over [[David Montgomery]], [[Alvin Kamara]], [[J.K. Dobbins]] and, hesitantly, [[Kenneth Walker III]].
- 2025-10-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-14 (Week 7 Waiver Wire & More W6 Film)]]): after 135 rush yards against the Jets the prior week, Williams managed just 29 yards against Carolina plus 5 catches for 5 yards including two hopeless check-down screens that lost yardage -- Harris says it was the first time all season Williams had utterly no place to run, against what may be a sneaky-tough Carolina run front. Harris says he's 'bummed' about the down week but wouldn't be out on Williams by any means, especially since no other Dallas back ([[Jaydon Blue]]: 3 carries, 0 yards) did anything either.
- 2025-10-17 — According to co-host Josh (not tracked) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-17]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Josh put Dallas in his top 'driverless wagon' tier -- a tier-one quarterback, two productive receivers, a top tight end, and Williams as a plug-and-play lead back who has been productive stepping into the role. Harris pushed back that the Cowboys' offense may be more opponent-dependent than truly automatic, noting the running game did little the previous week and voicing skepticism that all of [[CeeDee Lamb]], [[George Pickens]], [[Jake Ferguson]] and Williams stay great every week. Asked directly if he's buying Williams at current value, Josh said he's a hold -- not seeking him out to acquire, but also not selling him for 75 cents on the dollar off one game given how hard bell-cow roles are to find.
- 2025-10-24 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: names Javonte Williams his fantasy MVP through the midseason mark over the more obvious pick of [[Jonathan Taylor]], taking a 'wonky' value-based view rather than a raw-production one. Williams is currently the RB5 and the number six overall player in VBD, despite being the number 98 pick on average in fantasy drafts -- effectively a ninth-round flier who was 'left for dead after Denver' and, before this season, would have been considered behind [[Jaydon Blue]] and [[Miles Sanders]] on the depth chart. Harris admits he was skeptical of Williams last year and calls this outcome 'a little vindicated' now that he's shown he can be a talented every-down back, though he's careful not to claim retroactive credit. He calls Williams a 'rock solid pure RB1' so far but flags it remains to be seen how it holds up the rest of the season.
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-10-27]]) [Redraft]: had a 'revenge game' against his former team Denver, converting short-yardage runs and scoring twice, including a meaningless garbage-time goal-line touchdown late.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-04]]): Williams finished 15 for 83 on Monday night and Harris's complaint is with the play calling, not the back — huge lanes on the game's first two plays, the run working well all night, and Dallas getting away from it far too easily despite holding the ball. He singles out a third-quarter spin move that made an unblocked backfield defender miss entirely and turned into a 10-plus-yard run. The mark against him: he did not help himself with a game-sealing fumble after a catch at the very end. Harris will not rank Cowboys weapons out of starter territory over one bad night, but notes it is not foolproof, and Dallas is on bye before a Monday nighter in Vegas.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06 (Players We Think We Know)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Williams is who Waldman always thought he was — a good back who needs talent around him and the right scheme fit, not a back who creates it himself. He didn't fit Sean Payton's system and was slow reading those blocks; he runs Dallas's scheme well. He is a bruiser, which carries an availability question, and Waldman's summary is 'a good back when he was available to play', pegging him as a low-end RB1 despite rankings placing him higher this year. Co-host Adam Harstad (NOT tracked) added that Williams got a bad rap in reverse: early analytics could only confidently quantify tackle breaking, which is Williams' single best skill, so he and [[DeAndre Swift]] briefly became top-three dynasty backs for being exceptional at the one measurable thing. Harstad: it wasn't his fault the market got ahead of its skis.
- 2025-12-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-15]]): Williams scored a goal-line touchdown after a couple of tries, then left with a shoulder injury and was questionable to return (ceding time to Malik Davis), but came back for the third quarter and became 'basically the main thing that worked for Dallas,' logging 15 carries (9 in the second half) for 78 second-half yards. Harris's takeaway: 'keep using your running back' when he's producing like that in a close game, and says Williams is 'locked in as long as his injury is okay.'
- 2026-02-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-16]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman says 'f**k it' on the Cowboys prioritizing Williams's re-signing -- he played well, but Waldman credits the resurgent Dallas offensive line more than Williams individually, comping him to [[Miles Sanders]] as a solid-but-replaceable role player teams keep around 'just in case' rather than a true lead-back priority. Waldman doesn't think Williams would beat out [[Jaydon Blue]] in a stronger backfield, and specifically says he would not play ahead of [[R.J. Harvey]] in Denver, because Sean Payton reportedly felt Williams was 'a beat slow' diagnosing his zone-heavy scheme. Waldman's view: the Cowboys are better off finding a cheaper back and spending resources protecting the offensive line and receivers instead, though they could still bring Williams back if price and timing work.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Andy Behrens (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-05]]): Williams re-signed with Dallas rather than hitting the market, capping off what both hosts call a spectacular career-reclamation season after being written off in Denver. Harris takes some credit for staying on him during the bad Denver years, arguing the advanced/expected-yards metrics that made him look bad there were misleading -- 'expected yards is a terrible stat' that can't capture a player being miscast. Both project him as a borderline RB1 again in Dallas's improved offense, agreeing it's a good outcome for both player and team even though some preferred Breece Hall or Kenneth Walker III there instead.
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-22 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Dynasty]: Bob Harris (untracked co-host) is 'still pretty keen' on Williams as the main back, and Waldman agrees Williams is 'head and shoulders' the main back entering the season over backups [[Jaydon Blue]] and [[Phil Mafah]], despite camp buzz about both backups.
- 2026-07-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Jake Seeley ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: unlike last year's muddled Cowboys backfield battle (a Miles Sanders/Williams competition plus rookie hype around Jaydon Blue), Williams is now considered locked in as the starter after signing a new contract, and both analysts expect the Cowboys backfield to generate little fantasy-relevant camp news beyond Williams himself.
- 2026-08-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-04]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris bumped Williams to an A- in power for the 2026 almanac, calling him a 'pile-moving' back and tackle-breaker who 'ran mean' for Dallas last year -- he recalls a long run against the Jets where Williams shed a defensive tackle wrapped around both legs and broke free for 60-plus yards, though he didn't score. Harris doesn't think the Cowboys anticipated this level of play when they gave Williams the same free-agent contract as Miles Sanders and also drafted Jaydon Blue and Phil Mafah, but credits the team for realizing what they had. Harris frames him as a close-in touchdown thumper and 'good physical player' he'd be thrilled to take in the 4th round of a redraft, and would still consider in the 3rd -- while cautioning 'nobody has a backfield to himself' anymore, and Malik Davis will periodically annoy fantasy managers rostering Williams on this Cowboys backfield.
- 2026-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Cousin Josh (untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-14]]): both gave Williams a 4, with Harris noting he doesn't view him as an especially strong talent but sees a real path for him to miss time given his injury history -- otherwise the pick is fine.
- 2026-08-24 — According to Patrick Doherty (Roto-World, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-24]]): Williams is his #4 bust. Doherty views last year's roughly 1,200-yard season as a statistical outlier rather than something the Cowboys should have paid roughly $24M to run back, and calls Williams 'stunningly inefficient' as a receiver -- under 4 yards per catch, among the worst pass-catching seasons by a running back Doherty says he's seen. He believes rookie Jaydon Blue is finally poised to cut into Williams's passing-down work: unlike last year, when the Cowboys publicly downplayed Blue all summer before he flopped, this camp the coaching staff 'cannot stop heaping praise' on Blue and clearly wants a true third-down complement. Doherty is 'full fading' Williams as an RB2 at his current ADP, and also flagged that beat reporters tabbing camp addition Malik Davis as the current RB2 could be more smoke than signal. [[Chris Harris]] pushed back, saying he's fine drafting Williams in the third round even if Blue emerges -- comparing the risk to last year's [[TreVeyon Henderson]] situation -- preferring the aggressive RB2 swing over a 'safe' pick.
- 2026-09-01 — According to guest Ben Gretsch (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-09-01]]): he worries he is too low on Javonte Williams, whom he's fading as a likely 'dead zone' running back per research from JJ Zachariason (not tracked) on backs with poor receiving efficiency in that ADP range historically busting -- Williams's yards-per-route-run last year was 'horribly low,' below 0.5. But he flags the counterargument: Williams now has essentially zero competition after the Cowboys cut depth pieces and kept only [[Malik Davis]] plus a waiver-claim receiving specialist behind him, he's the clear lead back in a good scoring offense, and league-wide touchdown rates rose last year (new kickoff rules, more fourth-down aggression) even as yardage fell -- Gretsch projects 12-14 touchdowns for Williams and worries he's 'missing the forest for the trees.' [[Chris Harris]] disagreed and remains higher on Williams, noting a guest on a prior show (Pat Doherty, not tracked) called Williams a bust just over a week earlier and pointed to [[Jaydon Blue]] instead -- a take that 'didn't age well' once Dallas cut Blue. Harris shares some concern that Williams isn't trusted in the passing game (reportedly struggles catching screens), creating touchdown-dependent boom-bust risk plus injury exposure from a heavy workload.
