# Joe Mixon

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): expects Cincinnati to move on from Mixon this offseason (per Pro Football Network reporting), noting the same reports circulated last year before Mixon apparently took a pay cut to stay — uncertain whether he'd do that again. Names Dallas as his ideal landing spot on pure skill-set grounds: Mixon offers the explosiveness, power and receiving ability that fans hoped [[Tony Pollard]] would provide but hasn't fully delivered, to the point Waldman jokes that combining Rico Dowdle (not a tracked page) with Pollard and "a little fairy dust" roughly produces a Joe Mixon. Calls a Dallas/Mixon pairing "the elixir" for both team and player over the next couple of seasons.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: "loves" Mixon's landing spot in Houston now that the trade is official — argues public perception of Mixon's off-field reputation gets conflated with his on-field play, and that his breakaway-rate criticism (Cincinnati's stated reason for moving on) matters less than his still-present burst and efficient-yardage ability. Calls him a complete, do-it-all back — a terrific pass receiver — who should benefit from a real passing attack around [[C.J. Stroud]].


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: going around RB17-21 in early best balls, calls it a great value — expects the volume to be there in Houston's passing attack regardless of his breakaway-run-rate criticism, "give him enough chances and he's going to be fine."
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Pushing back on co-host Bob Harris's (untracked) suggestion that Joe Mixon's run as a starter is also finished, Waldman disagrees -- he expects Mixon to be Houston's true lead back in 2024, while acknowledging that every backfield, even ones with a clear lead runner, carves out some complementary work for a second back.
- 2024-07-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]): asked for a percentage on Mixon delivering RB1 production, he says 75–80%, and calls him a low-end RB1 'at the very least' if Houston's offensive line gives him what Cincinnati's could not. The argument is volume and role, not explosiveness: Houston ranked about 29th rushing last year and [[Devin Singletary]] still got weekly-starter volume, Mixon has a long record of top-12 finishes and good red-zone work, and the quarterback is not a goal-line rushing threat. Waldman leans on coordinator Bobby Slowik calling Mixon a workhorse — coach-speak about volume from the San Francisco tree (he notes Klint Kubiak saying similar about [[Alvin Kamara]]) means McCaffrey-style usage, and [[Christian McCaffrey]] led the NFL in touches last year. The caveat, from Footballguys' Zareh Kantzabedian, is the lowest breakaway rate since 2020 among top-12 backs. Waldman calls him a doubles-and-triples hitter and an all-around back in the mold of Ezekiel Elliott.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT a tracked expert) names Mixon his 'profile says no, situation says yes' pick for a possible career year. McFarland's case: Mixon's skill set has clearly deteriorated — missed tackles forced and yards after contact are all down, and at 28.1 on opening day the cliff could be there — but he lands in Bobby Slowik's Shanahan-tree offense with [[Nico Collins]], [[Stefon Diggs]], [[Tank Dell]] and [[Dalton Schultz]], and if [[C.J. Stroud]] doesn't regress that offense should score a lot of touchdowns. McFarland compares the upside to Curtis Martin's late-career Jets season, notes coaches have trusted Mixon on all downs for four to five years, and sees neither [[Dameon Pierce]] nor Jawhar Jordan as a real threat. Waldman agrees Mixon is reliable in the playbook while granting he may no longer have his old explosion.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: after a 159-yard week one, one of his best games as a pro, Waldman thinks Mixon can join [[Saquon Barkley]] in the top five. The difference from Cincinnati is what happens at the second level: he was routinely reaching the secondary with downhill momentum and his pads squared into a safety's shoulder, and had two runs he nearly broke for huge gains. Waldman frames Houston as the AFC version of the Eagles offense — multiple weapons spreading the field plus a dynamic quarterback — with Mixon leveraging it. On age he pushes back: 28 to 29 is generally the athletic prime, and if Mixon declines it will more likely be recovery speed than ability. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) noted Mixon was RB6 last year on roughly 18 touches a game in Cincinnati and has consistently finished in RB1 proximity.
