# Joe Mixon

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): expects Cincinnati to move on from Mixon this offseason (per Pro Football Network reporting), noting the same reports circulated last year before Mixon apparently took a pay cut to stay — uncertain whether he'd do that again. Names Dallas as his ideal landing spot on pure skill-set grounds: Mixon offers the explosiveness, power and receiving ability that fans hoped [[Tony Pollard]] would provide but hasn't fully delivered, to the point Waldman jokes that combining Rico Dowdle (not a tracked page) with Pollard and "a little fairy dust" roughly produces a Joe Mixon. Calls a Dallas/Mixon pairing "the elixir" for both team and player over the next couple of seasons.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: "loves" Mixon's landing spot in Houston now that the trade is official — argues public perception of Mixon's off-field reputation gets conflated with his on-field play, and that his breakaway-rate criticism (Cincinnati's stated reason for moving on) matters less than his still-present burst and efficient-yardage ability. Calls him a complete, do-it-all back — a terrific pass receiver — who should benefit from a real passing attack around [[C.J. Stroud]].


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: going around RB17-21 in early best balls, calls it a great value — expects the volume to be there in Houston's passing attack regardless of his breakaway-run-rate criticism, "give him enough chances and he's going to be fine."
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Pushing back on co-host Bob Harris's (untracked) suggestion that Joe Mixon's run as a starter is also finished, Waldman disagrees -- he expects Mixon to be Houston's true lead back in 2024, while acknowledging that every backfield, even ones with a clear lead runner, carves out some complementary work for a second back.
- 2024-07-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]): asked for a percentage on Mixon delivering RB1 production, he says 75–80%, and calls him a low-end RB1 'at the very least' if Houston's offensive line gives him what Cincinnati's could not. The argument is volume and role, not explosiveness: Houston ranked about 29th rushing last year and [[Devin Singletary]] still got weekly-starter volume, Mixon has a long record of top-12 finishes and good red-zone work, and the quarterback is not a goal-line rushing threat. Waldman leans on coordinator Bobby Slowik calling Mixon a workhorse — coach-speak about volume from the San Francisco tree (he notes Klint Kubiak saying similar about [[Alvin Kamara]]) means McCaffrey-style usage, and [[Christian McCaffrey]] led the NFL in touches last year. The caveat, from Footballguys' Zareh Kantzabedian, is the lowest breakaway rate since 2020 among top-12 backs. Waldman calls him a doubles-and-triples hitter and an all-around back in the mold of Ezekiel Elliott.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT a tracked expert) names Mixon his 'profile says no, situation says yes' pick for a possible career year. McFarland's case: Mixon's skill set has clearly deteriorated — missed tackles forced and yards after contact are all down, and at 28.1 on opening day the cliff could be there — but he lands in Bobby Slowik's Shanahan-tree offense with [[Nico Collins]], [[Stefon Diggs]], [[Tank Dell]] and [[Dalton Schultz]], and if [[C.J. Stroud]] doesn't regress that offense should score a lot of touchdowns. McFarland compares the upside to Curtis Martin's late-career Jets season, notes coaches have trusted Mixon on all downs for four to five years, and sees neither [[Dameon Pierce]] nor Jawhar Jordan as a real threat. Waldman agrees Mixon is reliable in the playbook while granting he may no longer have his old explosion.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: after a 159-yard week one, one of his best games as a pro, Waldman thinks Mixon can join [[Saquon Barkley]] in the top five. The difference from Cincinnati is what happens at the second level: he was routinely reaching the secondary with downhill momentum and his pads squared into a safety's shoulder, and had two runs he nearly broke for huge gains. Waldman frames Houston as the AFC version of the Eagles offense — multiple weapons spreading the field plus a dynamic quarterback — with Mixon leveraging it. On age he pushes back: 28 to 29 is generally the athletic prime, and if Mixon declines it will more likely be recovery speed than ability. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) noted Mixon was RB6 last year on roughly 18 touches a game in Cincinnati and has consistently finished in RB1 proximity.
- 2024-11-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-26]]): Mixon's worst game of the season — 14 carries for 22 yards, a 1.6 yards-per-carry average — after running hot all year. Harmon attributes it to the Tennessee front rather than to Mixon, singling out Jeffery Simmons and T'Vondre Sweat for dominating the line of scrimmage and taking the run away entirely, and praises the Titans defense for holding firm despite losing one of the better secondary players in the NFL. He folds it into a broader concern: Koh was early in asking whether the Houston offense was actually good even with the full complement of [[Nico Collins]], [[Stefon Diggs]] and [[Tank Dell]], and Harmon now concedes every Houston game looks concerning.
- 2024-12-05 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-05]]): Mixon is the season's cleanest illustration of the one workload effect that does replicate. Citing Jason Lisk's research that backs with 30 carries are likelier to get hurt in following weeks than backs with 25 — and, tellingly, that 30 carries in a close game is more dangerous than 30 in a blowout — Harstad said he tweeted the concern in real time after Mixon carried 30 times in a tight Week 1 Texans win, and Mixon was injured the following week and missed time. The stated mechanism is that the first ~20 carries are roughly free and each one after carries an increasing cost, and coaches only push past a back's known limit when the leverage is high. Harstad framed it as a short-term effect, explicitly not evidence of long-term career mileage damage.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]): presented with Mixon finishing weeks 1-17 as RB13 on the season and RB6 in points per game, Waldman says yes, draft him as an RB1 next year — mainly because he can handle the volume. He gets dinged up, comes back, and keeps absorbing the workload, and Waldman calls him a good all-around player. On the incoming-rookie threat, he is dismissive: Houston may find someone to spell him, but he expects it to go like the [[James Conner]] and [[Trey Benson]] situation, where people talked all offseason about a mid-season takeover that never came. Mixon, in his view, is one of those backs who fights off a challenger for another year — the same pattern he sees with [[Kyren Williams]] and [[Blake Corum]].
- 2025-01-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-21]]): Mixon had a great season and a great fantasy season and was productive in the playoffs, but Houston's run game was a bottom-three unit by success rate even with him on the field. Harmon explicitly does not blame Mixon for that — even granting he is not prime Mixon — and traces it back to the offensive line and to protection communication, which also undercuts the under-center play-action shot plays the Shanahan-tree offense wants to run.
- 2025-02-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-04]]): the Texans were one of the league's worst run teams even after upgrading to Mixon — 30th in success rate in games Mixon started and finished in 2024 — which Harmon attributes largely to offensive line execution rather than the back. He expects Houston to stay mostly in zone concepts under Nick Caley because Mixon was pretty good on those, and sees fixing the run game as the central job of the new staff. Koh (not tracked) thought Mixon looked good in spots but says he has to be slowing down at this stage of his career, and both flag his contract as a live question given Houston had roughly $381k in cap space at the time of recording.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): Mixon remains locked in as Houston's back despite the age questions and the arrival of [[Woody Marks]]. Waldman noted he missed some time in 2024 but was fairly durable, came back quickly from a high ankle sprain and racked up tons of work, and said he expects more of the same and is drafting Mixon as such — while allowing that a dynasty draft is where he would get speculative about the succession.
- 2025-06-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-02]]): asked whether a 29-year-old with 1,800 career carries — second-most among active backs behind [[Derrick Henry]] — worries him, Waldman answers with an unambiguous no concerns at all. He projects Mixon for about 15 games rather than a full 17 given his age, but with over 260 carries, nearly 1,100 rushing yards, 12 rushing touchdowns, two receiving touchdowns and around 39 catches for 300 yards — roughly 1,400 total yards and 14 touchdowns. At an RB16 ADP he says he would be very happy with that, and notes the projection is downstream of him expecting a Texans rebound overall. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) supplies the 2024 baseline: 1,016 rushing yards, 4.1 per carry, 36 catches, 11 touchdowns and an RB10 finish despite missing three games with a high ankle sprain he rebounded from quickly. Waldman also cites Mixon's ability to carry a load and move the chains as the thing that keeps [[C.J. Stroud]] on schedule.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): Mixon's ankle is the reason to be concerned rather than the reason to be calm — still in a walking boot well into the offseason, with the team signing [[Nick Chubb]] on top of it. Waldman notes there was no surgery, which leaves the question open of whether this is precautionary or something more, and says a definitive answer is unlikely. He can see an even split, or Chubb starting until Mixon is genuinely ready, or Mixon hurrying out of the boot because he sees Chubb creeping down his back and hurting himself in the process. Bob Harris (not tracked) counters that Houston insider Aaron Wilson keeps calling it minor — while noting Wilson has been saying that since early in the year — and draws the parallel to Christian McCaffrey's 'minor' foot-ankle issue that lingered all summer while most of the industry still ranked him RB1.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): on reports Mixon may not be ready for the start of camp, Waldman's response is 'lo and behold, why do you think they picked [[Nick Chubb]] up?' Houston said Mixon should be ready to participate at some point, though maybe not the first day or two, and Waldman reads 'at some point' as genuinely open-ended — it could be day one or the last week. His guidance is not to be overly optimistic about Mixon playing the entire season.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Mixon is 'firmly on my do-not-draft list' at a top-5-to-top-10 RB ADP. Angelo's medical read: a player in a boot this late is either protecting a surgical site or has a stress-related lower-limb injury, and since Houston has stayed hush-hush on the reason, he infers Mixon likely needed tightrope surgery to stabilize the ankle ligament after rushing back from a December high ankle sprain in three-to-four weeks on what is normally a six-to-eight-week injury. He expects a slow-play ramp toward a healthy playoff push, not a Week 1 workhorse, and notes [[Nick Chubb]], [[Dameon Pierce]] and rookie [[Woody Marks]] can take touches off a nearly 30-year-old back's plate. [[Matt Waldman]] adds that 'should be ready by the first week of camp' routinely becomes PUP and then weeks more, citing [[Christian McCaffrey]] in 2024.
- 2025-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Mixon is the number 6 flag player — a record fifth time on the list and second straight year — because Harris believes the market 'has simply made a living being wrong' about him. The stat case: RB11 in standard and RB16 in PPR last year despite missing three games, two hip-drop tackles and two sprained ankles, and RB9 per game in both formats, with two strong playoff-push games against the Chargers and Chiefs. He is explicit that he lowered Mixon when the vague foot injury news broke, that it may relate to the in-season hip-drop damage, and that if surgery is announced you should not draft him at all. But ADP of 71 (and 90s-110 on some sites) is 'wild' relative to a player who would otherwise go second or third round, with [[Isiah Pacheco]], [[Kaleb Johnson]], [[TreVeyon Henderson]] and [[D'Andre Swift]] all going ahead. Harris says ignore his own third-round rank and take Mixon in the sixth, as he did in an experts draft; he and Andy Behrens (not tracked) could find no explanation for the discount beyond spite.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-15]]): Mixon made the flag list for a fifth time in his career, which Harris jokes should be impossible for one player to be undervalued that often. Cousin Josh (untracked co-host) agreed he was a predictable inclusion — a healthy Mixon would go around the second or third pick in standard, and instead he is going at the end of the sixth or early seventh, so at that price Josh would click the button too. Both flagged the caveat: neither likes taking dinged-up players, the injury information could turn out much more serious than the team has let on, and Harris says this is exactly the reason to draft late and treat Mixon as a flex rather than a lineup anchor.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris publicly reverses the flag-player call he made on Mixon four days earlier. His Thursday argument was 'how dare you' at an RB28 ADP for last year's RB11 (RB9 in standard points per game), on the view that the market was punishing a foot injury nobody had real information about. Then Houston Chronicle veteran Aaron Wilson reported Friday that Mixon is 'not considered a lock to be ready for the season opener' per league sources — buried in a piece whose headline was an interview with owner Cal McNair, which Harris reads as the team laundering bad news. He cut Mixon from RB16 in the Almanac to RB25 standard / RB27 PPR, now roughly market. He will still take him at a sixth-round standard / seventh-round PPR price as a bench stash, not at the ADP in the 50s on Yahoo/ESPN/CBS. Harris ties it back to the recovery from the multiple 2024 high-ankle sprains caused by hip-drop tackles, and admits the embarrassment of the timing rather than defending the old take.
- 2025-08-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Mixon's situation has spiraled since late last week and there is now a real chance he is not available in Week 1 — Harris says it does not feel like he will play. Asked how early he would take him with no further information, Cousin Josh (a recurring guest, not a tracked expert) says round eight; Harris says he could see himself at six, would love to wait longer, and had originally ranked Mixon as a second-rounder on the flag list thinking the worry was silly, but no longer thinks that — so maybe he needs to get comfortable saying seven. The complication he flags is that default draft-room ranks will not catch up to how far Mixon actually falls, so you cannot read the room. The upside case: if Mixon returns Week 2 or 3 and gets 20 touches a game, that is a league winner and massive. The downside case: this could be a Todd Gurley situation where 'you can get left with nothing'. Josh's Baggy Award song for Mixon was Bette Midler's Wind Beneath My Wings, a long-time staple of his roster he is rooting for.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]]): Mixon remains a flag player for Harris despite his situation — he says you have to go deeper into the draft before you think about drafting him, but he still would, taking the chance in the seventh round. He raises this in contrast to [[Jaylen Wright]], whose injury pushed him off the flag list entirely; Mixon stayed on it.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): Mixon's ankle puts him on the NFI list to open the season, which will cost him at least four games — something Harris says had felt like it was coming for the previous ten days or so. He had championed Mixon as a 'flag player' and now ranks him roughly eighth round, apologising directly to listeners who drafted him higher on his recommendation ('do your drafts later in August', but 'that's on me'). His framing: sometimes you take advantage of the market being worry-warts on a player and sometimes you are the one taken advantage of. Whether Mixon returns in October may be signalled by whether Houston trades for another back. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): reading the rewritten Almanac profile, Mixon has been placed on the NFI list and will miss at least the first four games, after ankle sprains that lingered into spring minicamp and a foot injury that cost him several weeks of August practice — Harris suspects the Texans never planned on him practicing. He calls making Mixon a flag player a couple of weeks ago 'a bit of a disaster' and drops him 'well down in the realm of the eighth round', but keeps the flag planted because he expects the market to punish Mixon even further than that. His evaluation of the player is unchanged: never a pure speed guy and 'glumphing' when bottled up, but give him an angle or a cutback lane and only [[Saquon Barkley]] is scarier; the 2024 injuries were hip-drop tackles missed by officials, not age or decay, and Mixon posted 100-plus scrimmage yards and a TD in both playoff games. Risk that he never plays in 2025; chance he is fine by October, with only [[Nick Chubb]] behind him.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris reported no meaningful news on Mixon, whose mid-August draft stock 'took the kind of tumble that makes flags cry'. Mixon is on the NFI list and not eligible to return until week 5, but Harris sees no sign of a public ramp-up and reasons that if Mixon were close, we would be hearing sightings — so with Houston's bye in week 6, the realistic plan may be to hold him out until week 7. When reporters ask Texans management they get PR language about dealing with active players and reassessing after four weeks, and nobody is leaking optimism about his foot or ankle recovery, which Harris called a bummer. He still believes Mixon would be the better solution than Houston's current committee.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): 'the hope still is it's Joe Mixon — they're going to lose a lot of games if they don't get Joe Mixon back, because he's much better than [[Woody Marks]] or [[Nick Chubb]].' The problem is roster mechanics rather than talent: nobody knows anything about the timeline, and with bye weeks starting the following week, managers without IR spots are going to find it very hard to keep holding him. Harris and guest SCOTT FISH (Fantasy Cares, NOT tracked) both waved off the 'Twitter doctors' rumors circulating about the injury.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): Harris relays that Aaron Wilson, who covers the Texans, has been told Mixon 'is not close', so Mixon should not be considered likely to return anytime soon, if at all this year. He calls the whole sequence weird — the NFI placement and the long silence around it. Guest Jeff Erickson (RotoWire, not tracked), who describes himself as the Mixon evangelist 'to my detriment this year', agrees the fact Mixon is not being mentioned at all is the scary part. This is what unlocks [[Woody Marks]] as a clear add.
- 2025-11-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and untracked co-host Bob Harris ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Mixon's 2025 status is unresolved -- reports surfaced that he will not return this season, which Mixon publicly disputed, calling out his agent online over the report. Waldman speculates the Texans could bring Mixon back in 2026 if healthy to pair with [[Woody Marks]], or otherwise roll out the same Marks/[[Nick Chubb]] tandem again; he does not view Marks as a clear standalone lead-back answer.
- 2026-01-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Ben Gretsch (untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-15]]): Mixon turns 30 in July with one year and an $8M base left, zero of it guaranteed, so cutting him saves nearly all of it against a $2M cap charge. Both read the end-of-season leaks -- that Mixon went to unsanctioned doctors and didn't disclose treatments -- as originating from the Texans' camp, not his, effectively the team signaling he isn't coming back; Gretsch calls it 'a frosty relationship' and says he can 'feel certain' Mixon won't return. Harris says Mixon 'may physically just be done,' though neither claims certainty about the injury itself. Gretsch expects Houston to pair Woody Marks -- probably more as a pass-catcher than he was used in 2025 -- with a young addition rather than bring Mixon back.
- 2026-05-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-28 (Going Deep)]]) [Dynasty]: floated as a depth veteran option for a Green Bay backfield needing help behind [[Josh Jacobs]], but Angelo ranked Mixon behind [[Nick Chubb]] as an add, saying 'you have no idea what you're getting' with Mixon right now.
