# Jackson Dart

- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether Dart is draft media's overhyped poster boy, Waldman gives a conditional answer — he is feeling it only if Dart lands in a short passing game with play action, rollouts and a real runway, or in a Carson Wentz-style setup that limits the footwork demands and keeps his deep attempts in the 25-30 yard range. Beyond 30 yards Waldman says the deep game is a 'steaming pile', having charted roughly 25 throws over 30 yards and not liking them. His summary joke: Dart would be good at the game of darts, not at javelin throwing.
- 2025-02-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-27]]): [[Jackson Dart]] is this class's 'doughy-faced guy with upside' that the NFL will talk itself into, and Waldman comps him to [[Drew Lock]] coming out of Missouri in reverse — Lock was erratic from zero to 15 yards but fine beyond, Dart is the opposite. Inside 20 yards, on play action, moving the pocket, throwbacks and quick game, Waldman thinks he can be really good, and his ball placement on stop routes and comebacks is 'pretty damn accurate'. Beyond 25 yards he calls the accuracy an 'utter disaster'; when someone on X posted two Penn State throws as a rebuttal he said find the other 23 plays and he is at 50 percent. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees, drawing the line at 15 yards, and complains the Ole Miss passing game was 'stop route after stop route' that wasted [[Tre Harris]]. Angelo gives Lock a slightly better arm and says it is hard to start in the NFL when your level-three accuracy is that poor.
- 2025-03-28 — According to Cory Kinnan (quarterback charter, not a tracked expert) with [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-28]]): Kinnan cannot find the selling point and says the first-round buzz is 'driving me insane.' He grades a plus-average arm at best, no big-time athleticism, and a system he calls maybe 29 plays that makes life easy yet still leaves Dart late after three years in it. Dart charted 51% on deep balls, second-best of the five charted behind Will Howard, but Kinnan discounts it because 'all he wants to do is throw nine balls anyway.' His comp band is the third-round lottery-ticket group — Davis Mills, Kellen Mond, Malik Willis, Desmond Ridder — even though he expects Dart to go inside the top 32. Harmon floats a Jordan Love-style sit-and-develop plan and then talks himself out of it, saying Dart does not have Love's tools; Kinnan agrees completely.
- 2025-03-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-31]]) [Dynasty]: Dart's accuracy wanes somewhere between 25 and 30 yards, and beyond that it is scattershot — overthrows, underthrows, behind and ahead of wide-open targets. What worries Waldman is that Dart's setup, stance and release lack significant flaws, so there is no obvious fix; the visible symptom is that his feet get too wide when he maneuvers from pressure, when they need to be undisturbed and narrower to deliver at 30 to 45 yards. Developing a precise pocket climb is the path to overcoming it. Waldman, who notes he is not a quarterback coach, comps the mechanics-versus-accuracy mismatch to Jake Locker. The positives are real: he works two to three reads sideline to sideline before pressure arrives, identifies pre-snap interior and edge pressure, handles multiple simultaneous rushers, protects receivers by placing throws away from the nearest defender, throws the ball away when pinned to the boundary, and eats it in the red zone to preserve field goal position. Ideal landing spot is a West Coast scheme with pistol, shotgun, moving pockets off play action and misdirection throwbacks; starting early would be inadvisable unless the surrounding talent keeps him on schedule.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): on Ian Rapoport's framing that it is a matter of 'when, not if' Dart takes over from [[Russell Wilson]] in New York, Waldman reads 'when not if' as an admission that nobody expects the Giants to contend — which he thinks is realistic. If they fall out of the race, Dart plays. But he argues the outcome Brian Daboll and Joe Schoen should want, to keep their jobs, is Wilson dragging them to a wild-card berth, which he thinks is possible if Daboll does not micromanage and lets Wilson freelance — not championship football, but enough to prolong their tenure and bring Dart along next year. Waldman also defends Daboll and Schoen against the fan sentiment about an 18-32-1 record, arguing the losing owes more to the quarterback they were saddled with, now in Indianapolis, than to the coach or GM.
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]): Waldman is more cautious than his guest on Dart starting by week six. His framing is what Brian Daboll thinks he bought in [[Russell Wilson]] — if Daboll knew he was getting a quarterback who throws YOLO balls up the boundary and creates off-script, and believes Wilson raises the ceiling for [[Malik Nabers]] and [[Darius Slayton]] without needing to be overruled the way Arthur Smith did in Pittsburgh, Waldman expects Wilson to hold the job all season, with Dart possibly flashed late as a job-security move for Daboll and Joe Schoen. He adds that a healthy Giants offensive line could make Wilson the antidote, giving Dart the redshirt year Waldman thinks most quarterbacks need. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked) is emphatically feeling it and thinks it comes even earlier — possibly week three or four, because the first three games (at Washington, at Dallas, Kansas City at home) could produce an 0-3 start before a home game against the Chargers; if they don't switch by week four, the schedule from week seven on is brutal enough that it might not happen until much later, and he wonders if Wilson wants to hold the job through the week seven Denver revenge game.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-18]]): asked whether Dart should be the immediate starter over [[Russell Wilson]] after a second straight impressive preseason outing (5-of-6, 62 yards, touchdown on the opening drive), Waldman said 'fuck that' — Dart did in the game exactly what his RSP scouting report said: screens, RPOs and short work, well-executed, with one accurate throw beyond 20 yards but poor accuracy past that range generally. Waldman argued you have to scheme for Dart, whereas Wilson will take the deep shot to [[Malik Nabers]] or [[Darius Slayton]] more often than Brian Daboll may want; combining Dart's execution with Wilson's downfield aggression would make a top-five quarterback. He expects Dart to start eventually — roughly after five to seven weeks if the Giants can't win a game or two — since a rookie handpicked by a staff on the tail end of its arc is the classic life-preserver move for Daboll and Joe Schoen. Bob Harris (not tracked) opened by arguing Dart is the guy and had earned the job.
- 2025-08-28 — In the discussion of quarterback strategy in 14- and 16-team leagues, DENNY CARTER of Rotoworld (guest, NOT a tracked expert) said on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]] [Redraft (Standard)] that a player like Dart becomes really important in those formats: grab him late and stash him, because he is going to take the starting job and Carter thinks he will be pretty fun for fantasy. According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]]): he agrees you can get Dart very late in that kind of format, and frames the whole exercise as scarcity management rather than any philosophical hatred of quarterbacks — in deep leagues the waiver wire will be empty, so you must carry a backup for byes and injuries.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-15]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman issued a pointed warning to [[Malik Nabers]] investors — anything over 25 yards is consistently missing from Dart's game, and Nabers' ceiling depends on either [[Russell Wilson]] holding the job or Dart somehow developing a deep ball ('taken some pills, gotten some shots, did some therapy'). He also does not think Dart is yet as good as Wilson in the pocket or as experienced at changing plays and situations pre-snap. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) countered that the organizational logic still points to Dart eventually, since a coach and GM who drafted him view him as their last hope for job security and will turn to him under losing pressure.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-23]]): both were baffled by the Giants inserting Dart for two to four high-leverage designed-run snaps mid-drive and then removing [[Russell Wilson]] when he had rhythm. Harmon thinks Brian Daboll badly wants to play Dart but is terrified of the matchup — the schedule offers no soft landing — and of doing it while Andrew Thomas is not fully healthy, comparing it to the panic about starting [[Drake Maye]] against Houston. Koh noted most draft analysts saw Dart as a big-time reach with many red flags, and marveled at the irrational confidence of a hot-seat regime spending an early pick on a developmental quarterback. Harmon's worry is structural: if Daboll is fired, the next staff may have no investment in Dart, and he is skeptical Dart is the quarterback who meets [[Malik Nabers]] in the middle.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-29]]): Dart's debut was 'a good start by the standard of what most media think is a good start,' but not by Waldman's. Grading him generously as a rookie, he ran where openings existed and got the ball out quickly, and was accurate enough for receivers to adjust inside 20 yards — but those were throws Waldman expected to be pinpoint, and beyond 25 yards he was not accurate at all, though some misses drew pass interference. The positive: he did not melt, which many rookies do. Waldman's warning is the rookie-hot-start graveyard — [[Marcus Mariota]] and [[Will Levis]] with four-touchdown debuts, [[Drew Lock]], Mitchell Trubisky, [[Desmond Ridder]] — and he hopes Dart does not 'join that boy band.' Bob Harris (not tracked) valued the unexpected win most, but noted Dart took a thumping, has a hamstring issue, and now plays without [[Malik Nabers]], leaving [[Darius Slayton]] and [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] as his lead options; he does not think Dart makes any Giants piece more appealing.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): the Giants beat the Chargers and Harris finds it impossible not to feel some hope watching the franchise turn the page to its second-round rookie — partly the relief of not having to watch [[Russell Wilson]], who played two snaps after a spotter thought Dart stumbled. Dart scored the Giants' first touchdown scrambling up the gut with read-option mixed in, and Harris says he is a pretty good runner with a little of the cheat code in him, though he is probably a little too keyed up looking for contact. The mechanical flag is real: Dart is an absolute mess under center, took only about 10 snaps there out of 57 total offensive snaps, dropped one inside his own five that an offensive lineman luckily recovered, ran 47 snaps in shotgun and attempted zero passes from under center. Harris says that makes the offense predictable — shotgun draws on third-and-one do not work — and that Dart looked a very long way from being able to lead a comeback, even though no interceptions is good.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-30]]): Dart's debut win over the Chargers was 13-of-20 for 111 yards plus 10 carries for 50 yards, many of them designed runs and RPOs, and Harmon calls him poised and composed — he did what his team needed and avoided killer mistakes, though Harmon attributes the result more to the Chargers playing poorly and the Giants' defensive line (Abdul Carter used more off the edge against a line missing Rashawn Slater and Mekhi Becton, with Joe Alt leaving early with a high ankle sprain) than to the Giants playing well. He sees a promising run-game connection between Dart and [[Cam Skattebo]], but with [[Malik Nabers]] out the receiver room is 'bereft,' which caps what the offense can be.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-02]]): the positives from Dart's first start were that he did not melt (Waldman noted Hall of Fame quarterbacks have melted in early starts), found open running lanes, made checks at the line and completed passes. But he saw few pinpoint throws even in the short game and still has not seen Dart throw an accurate pass beyond 25 yards. His Ole Miss read: a one-read-and-run college quarterback whose athleticism matched the level he played at, but at NFL level — Waldman put him a little above Jordan Love athletically, well short of [[Jalen Hurts]] — he is in trouble if defenses neutralize that. Waldman wrote pre-draft that Dart 'needs to learn how to play quarterback better': stop stiff-arming and lowering his shoulder, and move drives brick by brick. [[Brandon Angelo]] added Brian Daboll's personality fits Dart, but with [[Malik Nabers]] out on an ACL and [[Jalen Hyatt]], [[Darius Slayton]], [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] and [[Theo Johnson]] left outside, the design-run volume needed to move the ball will stunt his development as a passer.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-02]]): Harmon is a firm skeptic of the Dart hype after his first career start. He does not take anything away from the win over a tough Chargers defense, but says what the Giants ran out there 'was not NFL offense stuff' — 111 passing yards in a win, smoke and mirrors of the sort Daniel Jones was run out of town for. He credits the Giants' defensive line and the Chargers' collapse after losing tackle Joe Alt far more than Dart, and rejects the framing entirely for fantasy purposes: 'quarterback wins is not a real stat,' so comparing Dart to Geno Smith or C.J. Stroud on the back of one victory is a category error. He allows the fantasy appeal is real and comes from rushing, since New York is leaning on designed runs and RPOs — partly, he argues, because getting Dart on the field helps Brian Daboll save his job. Harmon found Daboll's end-of-game celebration over-the-top.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-06]]): Waldman is still 'feeling' the concern that Dart's accuracy beyond 25 yards is a problem, consistent with his charting — anything under 25 yards looks pretty darn good, everything over 25 looks pretty darn bad. He counted roughly six to nine attempts past 25 yards and Dart hit one, a decently placed back-shoulder; the rest were poorly located even where catchable, creating harder work for his receivers. He singled out a flea-flicker where Dart should have led [[Darius Slayton]] for an easy touchdown, and an interception that was a hero-ball off-platform throw he could not layer over the defender. Bob Harris (not tracked) countered that he does not care about the deep accuracy — Dart was QB12 on the day with near-QB1 numbers, the rushing equity is always there, and the short passing feeds his 20th-round tight end [[Theo Johnson]]; five turnovers in the game were the real cost. Waldman's rejoinder was sustainability: he raises it so you know when to get off the ride once defenses adjust.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: a split verdict. Harris likes the comportment — 'I don't think Dart is like a rah-rah idiot; he runs when he has to, he doesn't freak out about it' — and early on Dart moved the Giants really well, spreading it around to a 14-3 lead with a 20-yard scramble inside the red zone and two touchdowns to [[Theo Johnson]]. Then it fell apart: a badly underthrown flea-flicker deep ball to [[Darius Slayton]] that would have scored, a scramble on which he dropped the ball untouched for a lost fumble, a fourth-down desperation interception and another pick with five minutes left — three turnovers of the Giants' five in a game Harris felt they otherwise dominated.
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-09]]): having charted Dart in college and now in the pros, he has thrown exactly one accurate pass beyond 25 yards from his pitch point -- one -- and that is the central flaw. Everything under 25 yards looks really good because Dart moves well and the staff can use designed movement and play action, but Waldman expects Philadelphia to set a high edge rush to keep him in the pocket, forcing him to move, reset and fire downfield, 'the death knell for that guy's game right now.' He cited an LSU rep where Dart stepped away cleanly from pressure and underthrew an open man by ten yards as typical. His broader read is that Brian Daboll and Joe Schoen signed [[Russell Wilson]] hoping to redshirt Dart, then played him early to save their jobs; if it goes wrong Dart becomes 'the lead singer of a boy band' of found-out young passers alongside [[Desmond Ridder]], [[Kenny Pickett]] and [[Drew Lock]] -- though Waldman hopes he fails that tryout and succeeds in the league. [[Chris Harris]] thought Dart played okay on the Saints film and is more even-keeled than expected, but ranked him QB26 and will not play him, especially with [[Malik Nabers]] out.
- 2025-10-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-10]]): Dart injected real energy into the Giants' upset of the Eagles, but Harris is skeptical of chasing it weekly. Dart has topped 50 rushing yards in three straight games and had 105 yards on his first five throws (then 90 on his next 20) against a Philadelphia defense missing [[Jalen Carter]] and with its only functioning outside corner, Quinyon Mitchell, pulling a hamstring early. Harris likes the running style — not a hair-on-fire scrambler, he sees it well and runs under control — but notes three turnovers the prior week against the Saints, a shocking receiving corps with 'little' Jordan Humphrey as the number one and [[Malik Nabers]] out, and a near-interception on a third-and-12. Dart was momentarily stunned and went to the blue tent. Harris says he will not be ranking Dart as QB7 just for the rushing floor, warns of Linsanity-style New York overreaction, and reminds listeners it was [[Matt Waldman]] on Thursday's show who said Dart isn't ready to be an NFL starter.
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-13]]): Dart is good in this offense but Waldman is not all the way there, because analysts get over their skis on rookie quarterbacks in the first five or six weeks before defenses adjust. The Eagles wanted edge pressure to force him high in the pocket and make him climb and throw, but without their best edge available they could not contain it and Dart broke out through the middle and ran. Waldman rates him a good scrambler who found receivers after breaking the pocket, but flags that his instinct is to run first and then look downfield, and that on several plays stepping up would have produced the same or better result. He is a slightly better runner than [[Baker Mayfield]] and needs to learn Mayfield's lesson about climbing rather than flushing outside; accuracy beyond 25 yards still is not there. Fantasy verdict: solid play with rushing floor, near-QB1 finishes if not for five turnovers. Both hosts praised the energy he brings versus [[Russell Wilson]], while Waldman cautioned that [[Drew Lock]] and Mitchell Trubisky also had good early results.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-04]]): in his first game post-[[Cam Skattebo]], Dart threw for roughly 80 yards through three quarters with the extra hundred arriving in not-quite-garbage time down three scores. Harris grades gently — Dart is a rookie playing with exactly zero skill weapons who are no-brainer NFL starters, and we make all sorts of excuses for other rookie quarterbacks — and credits the moxie: a scramble that gave the offense life before halftime, a rushing touchdown down 17, and a late touchdown pass. His fantasy verdict is that Dart's legs make him a super-flex weapon at the very least; on whether he develops beyond that, Harris says he is a little skeptical but is willing to give him time.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-06]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris builds his DraftKings lineup around a Dart–[[Wan'Dale Robinson]] stack at $5,700 and $5,300, saying Giants–Bears is the game he most wants to target on a weak Week 10 main slate with the Bengals, Cowboys, Packers, Eagles, Steelers, Chargers, Falcons and Colts all off the slate. Guest Jake Trowbridge (not tracked) argues Dart's price still has not caught up to his play — he feels Dart should be priced in the [[Baker Mayfield]] range and is not. Guest Ben Gretch (Stealing Signals, not tracked) makes the structural point earlier in the show that the 2025 Giants offense is centered on Dart, where the 2024 version was centered on the running back position, which is why [[Devin Singletary]]'s do-no-harm profile now fits better than [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]]'s.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-10]]): Dart showed better feet and notably better downfield accuracy before his concussion, including two consecutive throws over 25 yards to [[Theo Johnson]] — one up the seam, one a contested ball at the boundary. Waldman's bar is modest: if Dart can consistently hit the 25-to-35-yard window he does not need to become a 'mad bomber', because the short game is already good enough for him to develop into a good starting quarterback. The caveat is self-preservation — he has been checked for head injuries four times, and Waldman wants him climbing pockets with more control and picking his spots as a runner rather than mixing it up at the end of plays. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) noted Dart finished the week as QB1 at 28.3 fantasy points despite leaving early, is QB2 since taking over, became the first rookie to run for a touchdown in five straight games, and that his 25 design runs lead all quarterbacks despite not starting until Week 4 — a Brian Daboll design choice mirroring the [[Josh Allen]] template.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-11]]): Brian Daboll's firing was justified largely by how he used Dart, calling it 'irresponsible and reckless' to use a rookie as a battering ram on an obvious lost season for a two-win team — Dart has been checked for a concussion four times since the preseason. Harmon argues Daboll placed saving his own job above developing Dart long term, and asks why Joe Schoen or ownership never intervened. Both hosts credit Dart as far better as a passer and far more explosive as a runner than expected, but note he is at or under 200 passing yards in roughly four or five starts, that the pre-draft book called him a developmental passer, and that his specific style narrows the Giants' next-coach pool — few coordinators are licking their chops at that profile, with a Shanahan/McVay-tree hire (Harmon jokes Kubiak, or Joe Brady) the likely direction.
