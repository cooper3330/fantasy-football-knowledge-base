# Jackson Dart

## Expert Takes

- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether Dart is draft media's overhyped poster boy, Waldman gives a conditional answer — he is feeling it only if Dart lands in a short passing game with play action, rollouts and a real runway, or in a Carson Wentz-style setup that limits the footwork demands and keeps his deep attempts in the 25-30 yard range. Beyond 30 yards Waldman says the deep game is a 'steaming pile', having charted roughly 25 throws over 30 yards and not liking them. His summary joke: Dart would be good at the game of darts, not at javelin throwing.
- 2025-02-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-27]]): [[Jackson Dart]] is this class's 'doughy-faced guy with upside' that the NFL will talk itself into, and Waldman comps him to [[Drew Lock]] coming out of Missouri in reverse — Lock was erratic from zero to 15 yards but fine beyond, Dart is the opposite. Inside 20 yards, on play action, moving the pocket, throwbacks and quick game, Waldman thinks he can be really good, and his ball placement on stop routes and comebacks is 'pretty damn accurate'. Beyond 25 yards he calls the accuracy an 'utter disaster'; when someone on X posted two Penn State throws as a rebuttal he said find the other 23 plays and he is at 50 percent. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees, drawing the line at 15 yards, and complains the Ole Miss passing game was 'stop route after stop route' that wasted [[Tre Harris]]. Angelo gives Lock a slightly better arm and says it is hard to start in the NFL when your level-three accuracy is that poor.
- 2025-03-28 — According to Cory Kinnan (quarterback charter, not a tracked expert) with [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-28]]): Kinnan cannot find the selling point and says the first-round buzz is 'driving me insane.' He grades a plus-average arm at best, no big-time athleticism, and a system he calls maybe 29 plays that makes life easy yet still leaves Dart late after three years in it. Dart charted 51% on deep balls, second-best of the five charted behind Will Howard, but Kinnan discounts it because 'all he wants to do is throw nine balls anyway.' His comp band is the third-round lottery-ticket group — Davis Mills, Kellen Mond, Malik Willis, Desmond Ridder — even though he expects Dart to go inside the top 32. Harmon floats a Jordan Love-style sit-and-develop plan and then talks himself out of it, saying Dart does not have Love's tools; Kinnan agrees completely.
- 2025-03-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-31]]) [Dynasty]: Dart's accuracy wanes somewhere between 25 and 30 yards, and beyond that it is scattershot — overthrows, underthrows, behind and ahead of wide-open targets. What worries Waldman is that Dart's setup, stance and release lack significant flaws, so there is no obvious fix; the visible symptom is that his feet get too wide when he maneuvers from pressure, when they need to be undisturbed and narrower to deliver at 30 to 45 yards. Developing a precise pocket climb is the path to overcoming it. Waldman, who notes he is not a quarterback coach, comps the mechanics-versus-accuracy mismatch to Jake Locker. The positives are real: he works two to three reads sideline to sideline before pressure arrives, identifies pre-snap interior and edge pressure, handles multiple simultaneous rushers, protects receivers by placing throws away from the nearest defender, throws the ball away when pinned to the boundary, and eats it in the red zone to preserve field goal position. Ideal landing spot is a West Coast scheme with pistol, shotgun, moving pockets off play action and misdirection throwbacks; starting early would be inadvisable unless the surrounding talent keeps him on schedule.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): on Ian Rapoport's framing that it is a matter of 'when, not if' Dart takes over from [[Russell Wilson]] in New York, Waldman reads 'when not if' as an admission that nobody expects the Giants to contend — which he thinks is realistic. If they fall out of the race, Dart plays. But he argues the outcome Brian Daboll and Joe Schoen should want, to keep their jobs, is Wilson dragging them to a wild-card berth, which he thinks is possible if Daboll does not micromanage and lets Wilson freelance — not championship football, but enough to prolong their tenure and bring Dart along next year. Waldman also defends Daboll and Schoen against the fan sentiment about an 18-32-1 record, arguing the losing owes more to the quarterback they were saddled with, now in Indianapolis, than to the coach or GM.
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]): Waldman is more cautious than his guest on Dart starting by week six. His framing is what Brian Daboll thinks he bought in [[Russell Wilson]] — if Daboll knew he was getting a quarterback who throws YOLO balls up the boundary and creates off-script, and believes Wilson raises the ceiling for [[Malik Nabers]] and [[Darius Slayton]] without needing to be overruled the way Arthur Smith did in Pittsburgh, Waldman expects Wilson to hold the job all season, with Dart possibly flashed late as a job-security move for Daboll and Joe Schoen. He adds that a healthy Giants offensive line could make Wilson the antidote, giving Dart the redshirt year Waldman thinks most quarterbacks need. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked) is emphatically feeling it and thinks it comes even earlier — possibly week three or four, because the first three games (at Washington, at Dallas, Kansas City at home) could produce an 0-3 start before a home game against the Chargers; if they don't switch by week four, the schedule from week seven on is brutal enough that it might not happen until much later, and he wonders if Wilson wants to hold the job through the week seven Denver revenge game.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-18]]): asked whether Dart should be the immediate starter over [[Russell Wilson]] after a second straight impressive preseason outing (5-of-6, 62 yards, touchdown on the opening drive), Waldman said 'fuck that' — Dart did in the game exactly what his RSP scouting report said: screens, RPOs and short work, well-executed, with one accurate throw beyond 20 yards but poor accuracy past that range generally. Waldman argued you have to scheme for Dart, whereas Wilson will take the deep shot to [[Malik Nabers]] or [[Darius Slayton]] more often than Brian Daboll may want; combining Dart's execution with Wilson's downfield aggression would make a top-five quarterback. He expects Dart to start eventually — roughly after five to seven weeks if the Giants can't win a game or two — since a rookie handpicked by a staff on the tail end of its arc is the classic life-preserver move for Daboll and Joe Schoen. Bob Harris (not tracked) opened by arguing Dart is the guy and had earned the job.
- 2025-08-28 — In the discussion of quarterback strategy in 14- and 16-team leagues, DENNY CARTER of Rotoworld (guest, NOT a tracked expert) said on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]] [Redraft (Standard)] that a player like Dart becomes really important in those formats: grab him late and stash him, because he is going to take the starting job and Carter thinks he will be pretty fun for fantasy. According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]]): he agrees you can get Dart very late in that kind of format, and frames the whole exercise as scarcity management rather than any philosophical hatred of quarterbacks — in deep leagues the waiver wire will be empty, so you must carry a backup for byes and injuries.
