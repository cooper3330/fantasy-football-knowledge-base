# Jaylen Wright

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]) [Dynasty]: named one of four backs Angelo grades as early-round talents regardless of actual draft capital, alongside [[Jonathan Brooks]], [[Blake Corum]], and [[Dylan Laube]]. Comps him to a rawer [[Travis Etienne]] (not tracked) coming out of Clemson — real high-ceiling flash including brilliant spin-move plays, undercut by the same flaw Etienne had early: turning through contact too often and absorbing hits he didn't need to take. Same episode, according to [[Matt Waldman]]: rates him lower than Angelo and lower than most public boards — in the same range as [[Blake Watson]] rather than a top-5 back — specifically on ball security and contact-balance concerns against certain shot types that graded a little weaker on tape than expected. Explicitly conditional: if those two things improve, Waldman puts him back in the "reliable committee back" conversation; until then, "gotta prove these two things." Both hosts flag him as an elite high-school-caliber accelerator — Waldman notes a 55-meter time at a listed weight near 210 lbs that both call an excellent mark for his size — whose NFL translation depends on learning to play slower the way Etienne eventually did: weaponizing that acceleration through contact rather than trying to bounce or spin away from it.
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: his most detailed positive take yet — despite a Tevin Coleman-type (not tracked) archetype that historically struggles with it, Wright shows real skill setting up lead blockers and moving linebackers into blocks, giving him "a little more saving grace" than Coleman had at Indiana. Calls his movement "one of the most appealing facets of his game": strong stop-start skills, efficient hip mobility, and footwork that sets up creases and forces missed tackles in space. Specifically flags his high-knee "kicking of his heels to his backside" to extricate his legs from wraps as "one of the most Alvin Kamara-like aspects of his game."
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]): addressed Miami adding a fourth notable back into an already crowded room (De'Von Achane, Raheem Mostert, Christopher Brooks, Salvon Ahmed) by framing Wright as Mostert's eventual replacement as the home-run/change-of-pace threat, given Mostert's age. He said he's 'feeling it' as a football evaluation — having pre-draft-comped Wright's aspiration directly to Mostert — but conceded it's 'not great for fantasy managers' given the added competition for touches. He guessed Christopher Brooks and Salvon Ahmed are likeliest to lose roster spots if Miami prioritizes speed, with Jeff Wilson also at risk due to age, and floated that special-teams value (not just backfield role) explains why some late-round backs land where they do.
