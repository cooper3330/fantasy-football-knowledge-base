# Jacory Croskey-Merritt

## Expert Takes

- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]) [Dynasty]: Croskey-Merritt reminds him a little of [[Aaron Jones]] in style of play and quickness. Around 205-210 pounds, with excellent lateral movement, explosiveness in the short area, the ability to flip his hips and set up blocks reasonably well, and creativity — he alters his body in ways that do not look efficient but let him get skinny and slide away to make do with what is available. Waldman's caveat is that other backs 'run the lemonade stand' better, turning genuinely bad situations into good ones, but says Croskey-Merritt's is not bad. Waldman traced the path from Alabama State to New Mexico, a record-breaking season there, a stop at Ole Miss and now Arizona.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman absolutely feels the comparison that Croskey-Merritt has some [[Aaron Jones]] in his game — not as strong a player as Jones was at UTEP, but not that far away. He is a better receiver than expected, can work downfield and get position to win the ball in the vertical game, has good acceleration and speed with okay quickness, and is very creative: a good gap runner with some zone skill who breaks tackles and sets up blocks. Waldman flags the curious career path — Alabama State, then New Mexico with a good year, then a planned Ole Miss move that fell through, then Arizona where he barely played — but says the snaps he did get look like a good player, and he sees growth potential.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: both said yes on him as a late dynasty rookie-draft target among the less-heralded backs. Waldman described an aspiring [[Aaron Jones]] type — creative and quick, though without quite the receiving chops — and Angelo agreed the movement tools are definitely there.
- 2025-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is 'totally feeling' the sleeper tag. He sees a lot of a young [[Aaron Jones]] in the game, while allowing that Jones was more refined at UTEP than Croskey-Merritt was at Arizona, New Mexico and Alabama State. He has speed, really understands how to use his feet to create, has pretty good contact balance and power for his size, and catches the ball really well. It would not shock Waldman at all if he works his way onto an active roster from a late-round or UDFA position, earns a role and grows from there — this is one of those players who has a better NFL career than college career.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21]]): Waldman 'feels it' on Croskey-Merritt, the wildcat back, and pairs him with another late-round back he sees as very similar — quick, smooth, good decision-maker, capable of real work between the tackles. He projects Croskey-Merritt as the sixth- or seventh-round pick of that pair and the comparable prospect as the undrafted free agent with a chance to make a roster. His fantasy guidance is patient: do not add either to a roster on draft weekend unless drafted, but keep an eye out for whether they earn an active roster spot by September, because running back is the position where somebody rises out of nowhere every year.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: a day-two-caliber back 'without a doubt' who slipped on eligibility rather than tape, and he is going in the fourth and fifth rounds of rookie drafts. Waldman likes the setup — Washington's backs are leaving after this year and the offensive line is upgraded — and says the price only existed because Croskey-Merritt barely played in his final college season. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees, pairing him with [[Jalen Royals]] as the class's two clearest cases of players discounted for missing volume and framework of games rather than for ability.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Best Ball]: he is feeling Croskey-Merritt as a must-have late-round stash at RB81, calling him 'an [[Aaron Jones]] starter kit with a lot of advanced apps in the box that just need to be installed' — fast, shifty, with the peripheral vision to adjust and create. The open question is standard for athletic rookies: how well he adapts to and operates inside an NFL scheme this year. Waldman attributes his draft slide to eligibility rather than talent — he played for three schools and was limited to a small number of reps in his final year before being ruled ineligible, which he believes also cost him a landing spot at Ole Miss; in a different class he would have gone earlier. Waldman named Vikings UDFA Tre Stewart as his other Aaron Jones comp in the class, noting Kevin O'Connell used the same comparison and that Stewart could compete with Ty Chandler for RB3.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Croskey-Merritt is his headline rookie riser — the most talented back in the Washington room and 'not particularly close' given where [[Austin Ekeler]] is in his career, with [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] rated as merely okay, a [[Tyler Allgeier]]-level skill set that makes a good secondary option. Angelo said his tape shows primary-back ability and that Washington needs a primary back, and stressed the caveat that Croskey-Merritt barely played in college because he ran out of eligibility rather than through any failure. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed, said he has looked good in camp, upgraded Robinson slightly to a LeGarrette Blount comp that still reads as a secondary back, and named Croskey-Merritt a player to target in rounds 18-20 of deep drafts [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)] [Dynasty]: a legitimately good back and a very worthwhile sleeper he would take over [[Jaydon Blue]] at the end of drafts. Waldman describes him as 'an Aaron Jones starter kit with some of the advanced features already installed' — a better pass catcher than expected, with great contact balance for his size and excellent burst. The seventh-round slide is a film-availability artifact: he played at New Mexico State where teams had less tape, then his eligibility ran out after only a game or two at Arizona State, and Waldman thinks a normal college path would have made him a third or fourth round pick. He would not be shocked if by year two JCM is a real name out of the backfield. If [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] is gone, Waldman projects 150-180 attempts, 750-800 rushing yards, six to eight touchdowns and 150-250 receiving yards, with [[Austin Ekeler]] around 400 yards as the receiving-down back. [[Chris Harris]] cautions in the outro that if JCM's price gets bid up toward the fourth round, Ekeler at a twelfth-round cost is the better share.
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] apparently on his way out of Washington, Croskey-Merritt and [[Austin Ekeler]] both get bumps, though Harris had not yet moved his ranks and wants the official transaction first. He is openly curious how high the market goes: on one hand everyone races to be hippest and rank him highest, on the other his ADP started so low that the fantasy platforms may function as echo chambers and never let him climb, which could leave him good value. Harris also relays [[Matt Waldman]]'s more skeptical view from the previous day's show — not an overwhelming prospect, something like a lesser [[Aaron Jones]] eventually, and a 'too many moves' back who wants to bounce everything.
- 2025-08-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he moved JCM into his top 100 for the first time — just above [[Austin Ekeler]] in standard, slightly behind Ekeler in PPR, and slightly highest of the Washington backs in standard. He called the move speculative and reactionary rather than news-driven, and explicitly held it in tension with [[Matt Waldman]]'s appearance on the show two days earlier, where Waldman said JCM is good but not a crusher. Harris's counterweight: the decision to eject [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] probably reflects Washington being delighted with their seventh-round pick and believing he can combine with the other backs to replace Robinson. He stressed nobody outside the building knows the split between JCM, Ekeler, Chris Rodriguez and Jeremy McNichols.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]]): Harris wrote the seventh-round rookie a new profile after Washington dumped [[Brian Robinson Jr.]], but says it may be a mistake to assume the backfield entirely belongs to Croskey-Merritt with [[Austin Ekeler]] and [[Chris Rodriguez Jr.]] still there. He wanted to include Croskey-Merritt on the late-round list but ruled him ineligible: the ADPs say 10th-11th round, but that is not what is happening in drafts anymore — the numbers just have not caught up to the phenomenon. Guest JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) confirmed he saw him go in the seventh the day before, and named pass catching as his biggest fear, since Croskey-Merritt was not a pass catcher at all in college and that is what translates to fantasy.
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]): Waldman has been drafting Croskey-Merritt ahead of ADP on the strength of preseason reporting and the people he has talked to, but is explicit that he does not trust it to play out — everyone loved him in the preseason and he was 'the beat writer's delight', yet there is not a lot of substantial evidence behind it. He expects many possible seismic shifts in the Washington backfield before it settles, with [[Austin Ekeler]] still not dead yet (double-digit fantasy points in eight of the 12 games he appeared in last year) and a consistent role, and [[Chris Rodriguez Jr.]] essentially a curiosity for having stuck around a second year.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Going Deep Rookie Receivers and RB Depth Charts)]]): both called him badly overdrafted for 2025 — Waldman said he is going about six rounds earlier than he would pay, and that Croskey-Merritt will need injuries to play up to his ADP, with [[Deebo Samuel]] additionally eating into designed rushing work the way he did in San Francisco alongside [[Christian McCaffrey]]. Angelo's projected Washington pecking order for the season is [[Austin Ekeler]] on most of the work and [[Chris Rodriguez Jr.]] first crack at short yardage and the low red zone. Both, however, are bullish beyond 2025: Angelo said Croskey-Merritt is Washington's starter in 2026 and that he believes he is talented enough to be an NFL starting back, and Waldman agreed it is very easy for him to leapfrog Rodriguez if [[Austin Ekeler]] gets hurt. Waldman noted the depth chart itself is a weak signal, invoking the old third-string-listing of [[Justin Jefferson]] as the caution.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): 'pay the bill' — he feels Croskey-Merritt out-producing [[TreVeyon Henderson]] this season. In Week 1 the Commanders repeatedly gained box advantages with him on the field — 10-on-7 and 9-on-6 looks on the early drives — and all he had to do was press one block and hit the gas, which he did exactly as advertised. Waldman does not expect those box advantages to recur at that rate against better opponents, but argues he has better weapons around him, a better offense and a scheme that gets him into space more often than New England's does for Henderson.

Harris's first live impression is that he is small, so he may not always be the goal-line back, though he did score on a zone-read handoff when the defense jumped at [[Jayden Daniels]]. Harris says Ekeler is not going anywhere. Guest **Sam Gold** (not tracked) liked the limited snaps but would not make him a week-to-week starter, only a desperation flex, noting he is still a seventh-round pick with too many mouths to feed.
