# Rashid Shaheed

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]) [Best Ball]: grades him a "wide receiver four with wide receiver three upside" — a good [[Best Ball]] play but not yet a proven every-week fantasy starter. Genuinely uncertain whether he develops beyond a speed-role ceiling: thinks New Orleans currently sees him "in a box as that speedster" rather than as a developable full-route-tree receiver, and says he won't buy into a bigger role "until we hear a coach in late August say he's been great since the beginning of camp." Floats new OC Klint Kubiak (a longtime Shanahan-coaching-tree assistant) as a wildcard for the role, drawing a tentative historical comp to Ashley Lelie, a deep-threat receiver from earlier in the Shanahan/Kubiak coaching lineage — while immediately hedging that Lelie himself "was also that kind of guy," i.e. never developed beyond the speed-role ceiling either.
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: New Orleans not adding a receiver of consequence signals faith in the Olave/Shaheed/A.T. Perry trio. Harmon pushed back on Shaheed being viewed as just a shot-play/gadget receiver, comparing him to the John Brown mold -- a field-stretcher who also shows signs of being a legitimate route runner rather than purely a designed-touch player.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon likes Shaheed as more than a burner -- a smaller speed-slot type who profiles well -- and notes he'll be featured in Reception Perception's upcoming 'Year 2-4' WR drop in early August. Both hosts observe Shaheed was near the top of the league in visibly frustrated reactions to being left open and not targeted last season, a byproduct of Derek Carr checking down too quickly behind a shaky offensive line; Harmon suggests getting Shaheed more drag-route/leak looks would help. He projects as the Saints' flanker if new OC Clint Kubiak deploys A.T. Perry as the X and Chris Olave motions around.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon called Shaheed 'a sneaky baller' and said he'd fit especially well in new OC Klint Kubiak's full-speed, presnap-motion scheme since his 'bread and butter' is in-breaking routes run with speed rather than breaking routes outside. That projection carries the same offensive-line caveat Harmon and guest analyst Brandon Thorn (not tracked) raised for [[Chris Olave]]: New Orleans' pass protection, reliant on center Eric McCoy and RT Ryan Ramczyk as its only real 'pillars,' could be significantly worse if Ramczyk's knee keeps him out for the season.
- 2024-07-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]): asked whether Shaheed, freshly extended, can emerge as a viable fantasy WR2, Waldman says he thinks he does. The known quantities are deep speed and run-after-catch; the underrated part is that at Cal and early in New Orleans, when they featured him that way, he worked over the middle and made tough plays, and Waldman thinks he is getting better as a route runner every year. With [[Chris Olave]] opposite him, Waldman is enthusiastic — 'especially the value that you can get him at.'
- 2024-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-25]]): Shaheed is a more complete route runner than his reputation — a 74.5% success rate vs press (74th percentile) while facing press on 18.5% of routes, 80% success on posts run at a 15% clip, and a strong nine-route mark. His route chart went viral because every in-breaking route is green and every out-breaking route red; Harmon jokes that receivers line up on both sides so 'Rashid Shaheed can turn left,' but concedes the out-breaking weakness is real and tied to a 76.4% vs zone score (32nd percentile). Koh argues the zone number is depressed by usage, with nearly 40% of routes being nine/post/corner. Harmon says lifting zone to ~78.8% would reach the 51st percentile and support a 100-120 target role; comp is loosely John Brown, and he frames Shaheed as what people wanted [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] to be. Neither would be surprised by a third-year breakout.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]) [Dynasty]: a curiosity rather than a firm call. He had written last year that Shaheed could be the difference in the Saints offense alongside [[Michael Thomas]] and [[Chris Olave]], but the offensive line could not support [[Derek Carr]], the deep passing game broke down and Thomas could not stay healthy, so the offense never clicked despite Shaheed having a good year. Shaheed seems to keep growing, and what Waldman wants to see early this season is how complete a route runner he has become. If he has fully blossomed in year three, that is exciting — making him an early trade-acquisition candidate.
- 2024-09-12 — According to co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-12]]): Shaheed is not a superstar but is 'such an interesting player' schematically for what his speed enables, and his 59-yard Week 1 touchdown from [[Derek Carr]] in the first quarter was the single play that pulled Harstad back in on the Saints passing game. He praises New Orleans for defining receiver roles more clearly than most teams and putting players in positions of strength (the same way they use [[Alvin Kamara]]). [[Matt Waldman]] concurs that Shaheed was the personnel avenue he thought would tie the whole 2023 offense together — the reason he nearly named the Saints offense in his bold predictions before backing off. Caveat from both: it is one play in a 47-10 blowout, and Shaheed had only the one long score.
- 2024-09-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-17]]): Shaheed is the clearest evidence Clint Kubiak's scheme is unlocking existing Saints personnel. Harmon notes he has run only 33 routes total through two games yet ranks ninth in the NFL in receiving yards, with a perfect 158.3 passer rating when targeted and a 77.8% catch rate. He frames Shaheed as unambiguously a splash-play guy rather than a volume receiver, and reminds listeners that Reception Perception has been on him since a strong profile last year — he calls it 'a very pro-Rashid Shaheed podcast' and says Shaheed was a player he drafted in fantasy this year. Efficiency this extreme on this few routes is presented as a testament to the play caller rather than a sustainable per-route expectation.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): buying Shaheed's start, with the caveat that he will not keep catching a post-route touchdown every week and that top-25 NFL receiver status is hard in a crowded league. The 2023 RP profile is strong — a post route on about 15 percent of charted routes at an 80 percent success rate, plus good dig, curl and slant success rates; everything straight or in-breaking is green on his route chart and everything out-breaking is red, which Harmon jokes gets the Zoolander treatment. Most impressive to Koh (not tracked): 74th percentile vs press coverage on 18.5 percent of routes at 180 pounds, which matters because New Orleans plays heavy personnel with tight ends and [[Taysom Hill]], so Shaheed must line up outside rather than in the slot. Harmon calls him picture-perfect for Klint Kubiak's motion-heavy offense with [[Derek Carr]] feeling it.
- 2024-11-22 — According to Adam Harstad, untracked co-host ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]) [Dynasty]: Harstad volunteered Shaheed as a bet he got wrong — he cut [[Kyren Williams]] to hold Shaheed, which bit him big time. His reasoning at the time was that he was a big fan of the talent and saw a non-zero chance Shaheed could take a [[Tyreek Hill]] type leap, while conceding nobody is going to actually be the next Tyreek Hill. He used the example to argue against one-size-fits-all roster rules: archetypes raise your odds from 50% to maybe 55%, so you are still wrong 45% of the time, and his own bench is not purely backup running backs and quarterbacks.
- 2025-01-30 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-30]]: Shaheed was 'popping off every week' in the early stretch of 2024 under Klint Kubiak in New Orleans, with [[Derek Carr]] comfortable pushing the deep ball behind a line that played far above its projected talent — Koh uses him as the template for what Kubiak could unlock with [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] in Seattle. [[Matt Harmon]] flags the health caveat: Shaheed is coming off the lower-body injury, and any read on the New Orleans receiver room under a new staff has to start with whether he and [[Chris Olave]] are healthy.
- 2025-02-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-21]]): Shaheed was in the middle of a genuine breakout before a major meniscus tear with an estimated four-to-six-month return window, which Koh calls 'pretty scary' for 2025 availability. Harmon characterises his role as the specific one [[Derek Carr]]'s deep-ball production was built on — classic cover-three beaters, posts that split safeties — and distinguishes those from the nine routes and deep curls [[Chris Olave]] runs, arguing all deep throws are not created equal. With the Saints' only committed 2025 receivers being Olave, Shaheed, Cedric Wilson, [[Bub Means]], Mason Tipton and Kevin Austin Jr., both hosts frame the room as thin and entirely dependent on two players returning healthy.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]) [Best Ball]: in a one-versus-one with [[Chris Olave]], Waldman takes Shaheed at his WR51 ADP (around the tail end of round eight per Bob Harris) over Olave at WR31 in round five. The reasoning is cost rather than talent — Waldman concedes Olave is the more talented player, but says Shaheed is priced such that he is not counted on as a starter yet could deliver starter value, whereas Olave's price requires quarterback play in New Orleans that Waldman does not trust.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): Shaheed was playing lights-out early in 2024 with [[Derek Carr]], and Waldman's standalone read without that connection is a talented but limited player — essentially a better-hands version of [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]] in how he would be used. His best-case path is [[Tyler Shough]] being good, because a real downfield thrower is what makes Shaheed work. Where the market once priced him as a developing WR2 taken at a WR3-ish cost, he has fallen to roughly WR55, and both Waldman and Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed that is exactly the right price: as variable as it gets, so you take the cheap shot and dump him by the side of the road if it does not hit.
- 2025-05-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-21]]): Harmon pushes back on the speed-merchant label, saying Shaheed is more than that and can be a fully formed receiver — his profile coming out of his second season was really encouraging. The caveat both hosts raise is the injury: co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) notes that Shaheed's season ended with a major lower-leg injury and that speed is exactly what he trades in, so nobody knows what that speed looks like on the other side of it. Harmon says a healthy trio of [[Chris Olave]], Shaheed and [[Brandin Cooks]] would theoretically be a fast, good room centred on Olave, but calls it very fragile given Cooks's time on IR and Olave's concussions.
- 2025-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-30]]): Shaheed is a baller having a nice camp coming off a major injury, and Harmon expects him on the field Week 1. He rates him close enough to [[Chris Olave]] in skill that the comparison is a compliment to Shaheed rather than a knock on Olave, and calls the pair a good enough WR1/WR2 duo. He had not charted Shaheed yet at the time of recording but planned to within a couple of weeks. The concern is the [[Tyler Shough]] fit: like Olave, much of Shaheed's damage comes on deep in-breakers splitting the safeties or crossing a defender's face against single-high zone, exactly the throws Harmon doubts Shough wants to make. Both hosts are also worried about the room's depth — the Saints added only a 32-year-old [[Brandin Cooks]] on a two-year deal despite the pass-catching group being what derailed the team in 2024.
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]): named alongside [[Chris Olave]] as the reason both hosts are 'feeling it' on the Saints producing a top-15 fantasy option beyond [[Alvin Kamara]]. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked) calls Olave and Shaheed 'so good' and says he is willing to lean on receiver talent over quarterback uncertainty — with [[Tyler Shough]] and [[Spencer Rattler]] both willing to push the ball downfield despite turnovers, and a likely bad Saints team playing from behind, one of the two should absorb enough volume to pay off. Waldman agrees but caveats that it depends on neither quarterback having 'the [[Derek Carr]] gene' of throwing hospital balls to receivers.
- 2025-08-25 — According to guest JJ Zachariason (LateRound.com, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]], relayed and endorsed by [[Chris Harris]]: Shaheed is his number five late-round target, drafted past pick 110. Health is the headline — he seems healthy, and four of his first five games last year produced 16-plus PPR points before the injury. JJ calls him a monster efficiency player with yards per route run over 2.0 in two of his first three seasons, a genuine field stretcher, and is intrigued that Kellen Moore's preseason deployment has shown a lower average depth of target closer to the line of scrimmage to let him work after the catch; Moore's offenses have never run fewer than 65 plays per game in six years as a coordinator. The risk is situational: nobody knows who the Saints quarterback will be. Harris agrees the pick is great but wants to see more than deep shots and one-yard crosses on the film, and pushes back on the idea that drafting a player on a possibly-bad offense is a flaw.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-05]]): Harmon said he is 'grossly excited' about Shaheed and [[Chris Olave]] and that he was ahead of consensus on Shaheed in fantasy rankings. The logic is concentration rather than quality: everyone expects the Saints offense to be bottom-five or the worst in the league, but Kellen Moore has generally run a concentrated offense through his top weapons, and New Orleans effectively has only two of them — [[Devaughn Vele]] and [[Brandon Cooks]] aside. He added that [[Spencer Rattler]] will let it rip, pointing to a season-high 14 targets for Olave in the Chargers game Rattler started and finished, and that Shaheed's downfield vertical style fits. The caveat is the opener: he does not consider the Cardinals a soft start and said Rattler is '100 percent' throwing a pick in that game, while still expecting a couple of ripped downfield throws.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'the way you stretch the field, you really burn them all.' Shaheed converted a badly thrown third down early and then took a late-third-quarter play-action bomb from [[Spencer Rattler]] — an over route out of the slot — 87 yards for a touchdown, the longest Saints pass play in nine years, with Harris noting the Giants saw it coming and simply could not catch him.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-06]]): Shaheed, traded from New Orleans to Seattle to reunite with Klint Kubiak, is 'very, very good' and one of the most underrated receivers in the league — he and [[Jakobi Meyers]], both former undrafted free agents, have significantly outplayed better-drafted, better-paid teammates. From fresh in-season charting: his route running is even better now than early in his career, especially against man coverage, where he is a 'really combative, decisive route runner'; he can hang out at X without being exclusively an X, and can also play slot and flanker. Harmon expects him to out-snap [[Tory Horton]] the rest of the season and says you do not trade for a player like this and leave him off the field. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is unconvinced the role exists: Seattle runs 11 personnel just 35.8% of the time, second-lowest in the league, and 29th in passing plays out of 11 — 'they're not changing anything.' Harmon's counter is that Seattle's heavy-set advantage disappears in the playoffs when defenses stop matching in base, so they need three-receiver answers in pure dropback situations. New Orleans got only a fifth and a sixth back.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-06]]): Harris says he would rank Shaheed AHEAD of [[Jakobi Meyers]] for Week 10 if forced to choose one in a PPR flex, and notes the show has liked both players for years. The reason is scheme continuity: Klint Kubiak is the same offensive coordinator Shaheed played under in New Orleans last season, so he should already know the terminology and the plays — a real edge over a receiver learning a new playbook after a Tuesday trade and two days of practice. Guest Ben Gretch (Stealing Signals, not tracked) frames Seattle as an offense that is incredibly efficient at creating chunk plays on low pass volume, which is exactly what Shaheed is; he only needs a couple of targets to post a solid game, and he is especially attractive in non-PPR, half-PPR or distance-bonus formats where Meyers's volume-based scoring matters less. Both caveat that a mid-week trade acquisition is never guaranteed.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-10]]): pushed on whether Shaheed helps [[Cooper Kupp]] more than [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]], Waldman rejected the premise — he helps both, a lot. Aligned to the same side as Kupp, Shaheed's presence creates underneath opportunities for Kupp; aligned opposite, he stretches the safety deep enough that JSN gets to the far flat wide open with the ball in his hands, exactly as happened on Sunday. Waldman also expects more intermediate throws to open up as a byproduct of the vertical threat.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon says people are overreacting to Shaheed's light playing time in his Seattle debut — it was a blowout of Arizona decided almost immediately when [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] went over 50 yards on an early touchdown, he had been traded on the Tuesday before a Sunday game, and even knowing Klint Kubiak's offense from New Orleans the playbook and quarterback are different. He expects a much larger role against the Rams, a team he liked Shaheed's isolated film against with the Saints — getting open consistently against man coverage on the outside while playing all three receiver positions. The caveat is he may never be a full-time every-snap player alongside Smith-Njigba. Pick: over 2.5 catches at plus-146.
