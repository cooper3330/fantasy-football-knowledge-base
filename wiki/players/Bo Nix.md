# Bo Nix

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — Guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Dynasty]: "f\*\*k it," a skeptical outlier take he expects will be unpopular with Oregon fans. His core objection is scheme-driven: Oregon's passing offense is functionally a one-read, run-when-it's-not-open system, and Nix's ball placement forces receivers to adjust to the ball rather than the reverse — the same drop mechanism he flags as a red flag when he sees it elsewhere. Explicitly caveats that the system, not necessarily the player, is responsible for how little full-field reading Nix's tape shows, and names the Senior Bowl as the specific proving ground to watch for evidence he can scan a field and make secondary reads under pressure. Absent that evidence, his working expectation is a quarterback who "will struggle in the NFL" once a defense disguises the picture he's used to seeing.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: an explicit disagreement with guest Daniel Harms's 2024-01-15 skeptical read — a live split between a tracked expert and an untracked guest, not resolved. Waldman: surprised how good Nix has looked moving from a middling Auburn tenure into Oregon's offense — grants the offense manufactures some easy reads, but argues Nix shows more than that "almost every week," reading the field well and making sound pre-snap decisions. Says he "wouldn't be surprised if he's actually one of the three to four best quarterbacks" in the 2024 class capable of starting and developing within their first two NFL seasons — a real reversal in tone from Harms's "will struggle in the NFL" framing a week earlier.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-01]]): a genuine three-way split now exists on Nix. Watching him actually throw live during Senior Bowl week, Angelo "stocked him down" — "I get why teams would take the risk in the first round, but it's again like that Mitch Trubisky syndrome... everything is just a fraction too late." His comp: "what people hoped Mitch Trubisky might be." Grades him at the borderline between a real starting contributor and a player who's only a starter in an emergency — notably closer to guest Daniel Harms's 2024-01-15 skepticism than to Waldman's own 2024-01-22 "top 3-4 in the class" read, making this an unresolved three-way disagreement among Harms (skeptical), Waldman (bullish) and Angelo (skeptical) heading into the draft.

- 2024-02-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-05]]) [Dynasty]: "totally feeling it," with the most detailed bullish case yet, published four days after Angelo's 2024-02-01 Senior Bowl downgrade but not addressing it directly — Waldman does not reference or rebut the live-viewing "Mitch Trubisky syndrome" critique here, so the three-way split logged on 2024-02-01 stands. Grades him as capable of becoming "a competent starter... maybe even a good one." Pre-empts the standard Oregon-scheme objection directly: yes, the offense runs a lot of RPOs, but the tape also shows him making full-field reads, manipulating defenders, and creating both inside and outside structure — "the entire Oregon offense isn't just RPOs." States he'd benefit most from a redshirt rookie year, and explicitly bets he clears the bar Sam Howell (also given a real sit-and-develop runway) set: "I think you'll see Bo Nix rise to the occasion better than Sam Howell."
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]) [Dynasty]: relays a specific outside characterization from **Dan Hatman** (draft evaluator, not a tracked expert, cited from a private conversation rather than as a show guest) — "bubble screen Bo," the question of whether Nix is fundamentally just an improviser off manufactured throws rather than a real full-field passer. Waldman doesn't resolve it either way here: says he "can see it going either way" on whether Nix is actually the class's QB2 that the market hasn't priced in yet, a more hedged framing than his own bullish February 5 read.

- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]) [Dynasty]: names Washington "a really good landing spot" for Nix if the Commanders miss on [[Caleb Williams]], likening him to a "Kirk Cousins-like player" who lacks special off-schedule arm talent but can make every required throw. Praises his pocket movement, strong play-action game, and middle-of-field reads ("bubble screen Bo" undersells him, per a colleague's nickname Waldman disagrees with), plus a maturity/low-mistake decision-making profile that keeps him from forcing throws he shouldn't. Rates him better than [[J.J. McCarthy]] specifically at operating outside the pocket. Ceiling read: "the dreaded game manager, but game manager kind of plus version."

- 2024-03-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-08]]) [Dynasty], solo cast on the full QB class: uses Nix as the season's central case study against age-based scouting discounts — treats knocking him for being an older, more experienced prospect as the same "ham-handed" filtering error as an old 6'2/210 wide receiver height/weight cutoff, since self-awareness tracks a player's skill and desire more than his age or reps. On tape: reads the field better than he's credited for, accounts for defender leverage with his placement and decisions, a willing tight-window thrower who protects his receivers, gets rid of the ball fast off structure, and throws it away rather than forcing it into coverage — "he doesn't get too invested in plays." Adds small manipulative touches (frame positioning after drops/play fakes, pump fakes that hold defenders' eyes). Not the flashiest name on the board, but if the self-awareness translates, Waldman thinks "he could outlast a lot more of the exciting names in this class."
- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]): names Nix, alongside [[Michael Penix Jr.]], as his preferred Denver quarterback options behind Caleb Williams, wanting a QB who moves well in and out of the pocket, avoids inviting pressure, and fits Sean Payton's timing game.
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]): said he preferred Nix as a prospect over J.J. McCarthy, but rates Denver's landing spot as less favorable because Nix's receiving corps is uncertain — reliant on Marvin Mims and Troy Franklin taking a leap, and possibly trading Courtland Sutton, whom Waldman called 'the black hole.' He said Nix isn't 'definitely bad' for Denver but is less likely to level up as fast as McCarthy, projecting more of a 'steady plateau' than a fast breakout.
- 2024-05-02 — According to Brandon Angelo ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]) [Dynasty]: Bo Nix is his top underrated player, calling it a 'hand-in-glove' scheme fit with Sean Payton, whose system is almost entirely first- and second-level, timing-based route concepts — exactly what Nix does at a high level, unlike [[Russell Wilson]], whose off-schedule style never worked in Payton's structure. Angelo compared Nix favorably to what Mitchell Trubisky 'should have been': a more in-control, mature version of that archetype who reined in the hero-ball tendencies that hurt him early at Auburn and rarely makes the big mistake. Angelo said Nix is significantly undervalued in rookie drafts right now, especially in 6-point passing-TD formats, though he flagged the receiving weapons around him (Courtland Sutton 'okay,' Troy Franklin mainly a level-three/vertical beater) as a real question for the offense.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman ranks Nix as the NFL-readiest of the 2024 rookie QB class — a lower ceiling than [[Jayden Daniels]], [[Drake Maye]] and [[J.J. McCarthy]], but already a higher floor, comping the gap to Paxton Lynch's tantalizing physical ceiling versus a lower-ceiling-but-safer Matt Ryan/Kirk Cousins type ('you'd much rather have Matt Ryan or Kirk Cousins'). He argues Sean Payton's timing-based scheme fits Nix's game — quick-hitting underneath routes, similar to how Drew Brees produced with journeyman-caliber weapons like Lance Moore, Devery Henderson and Robert Meacham — and that Nix's 61 college starts and heavy volume give him more functional readiness than his rookie rivals. Waldman thinks Nix could realistically finish as a top-two producer among this rookie QB class in 2024 purely on scheme fit and experience, even without Courtland Sutton being a true alpha receiver.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: both hosts named Bo Nix as the rookie they least want to draft yet keep taking anyway because he sits high on their boards. Waldman gives him a strong infrastructure grade for fitting Sean Payton's scheme, calling him a safe if unsexy QB3 with a low hit rate, projecting a possible QB2 ceiling only if Denver adds weapons. Angelo agrees the profile isn't exciting given Payton's post-Russell Wilson talent deficit, and admits he'd still take Nix at the same range despite the hesitation, noting that fan memory of Payton's Drew Brees-era offense may be inflating expectations for a currently talent-poor Denver roster.
- 2024-05-20 — According to guest Jagger May (Football Guys, untracked) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-20]]): May said he likes Troy Franklin and sees "a lot to like" with Bo Nix, but is deeply skeptical of head coach Sean Payton, comparing him to a returning Jon Gruden — a broadcast-booth retread bringing outdated arrogance into a rebuild without a Hall of Fame quarterback to prop him up, crediting Drew Brees's development more to Marty Schottenheimer than to Payton. [[Matt Waldman]] noted Nix has a "nice timing element" to his game that could help him fit, but agreed the Broncos need more roster help than just a quarterback and a receiver for it to matter as soon as 2024.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): asked whether Denver will ruin Nix by starting him immediately, Waldman said no — he expects Nix to be fine. He checks the ball down well, makes more positive off-script plays than people realize, and reads defenses reasonably well for a rookie. Waldman's projection is a Mac Jones-style rookie year that people find promising, with ups and downs, and the open question is whether Denver can avoid ruining him afterward the way New England did with Jones. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed, citing Sean Payton's track record with quarterbacks and that Nix was Payton's hand-picked pick at 12th overall.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]): Nix is a sleeper specifically in two-quarterback leagues if he manages the game the way he did at Oregon. Waldman explicitly discounts the preseason evidence — preseason football is closer to college football, and once the regular season starts the complexity rises and all the top athletes are on the field at once, which is exactly what overwhelms rookies in September when every opponent would have been the best player on their college team. So he does not expect Nix to manage games as cleanly as he has looked so far. If the management does carry over, Denver could run the ball enough that [[Javonte Williams]] outpaces his price — Waldman frames Nix as the gating factor on that whole offense.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): he would not be shocked if, when the dust settles, Nix starts all 17 games, keeps his head on a swivel, and may not post sexy stats — but that on a season-long basis fantasy managers come out better with Nix than with [[Jayden Daniels]], whose per-game average will likely be much higher but whose games played Waldman doubts given how much contact he takes as a runner. Framed as slow growth from there rather than an upside call.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): named the week-one starter in Denver, Nix is a 'feel it' long-term and a 'fuck it' for fantasy this season — draftable only at the end of a superflex/2QB roster as a dart throw. Waldman projects roughly 3,500-3,600 yards and maybe 20 touchdowns without a strong receiving corps, with a lot of check-downs; he says Nix will play better than his box score. He credits Nix as a fit for what Sean Payton wants — enormous college starting experience, throws the ball away, gets out of bounds, knows where he can and cannot take chances, and has enough mobility to be serviceable without chasing rushing yards. His comp: 'Mitchell Trubisky with maturity.' Bob Harris (not tracked) noted Nix threw about a third of his college passes at or behind the line, which he views as good news for his [[Jaleel McLaughlin]] shares.
- 2024-08-27 — According to guest Derek Klassen (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): now Denver's named starter. Klassen's written player write-up said 'somewhere between Gardner Minshew and Andy Dalton should be a realistic expectation, anything more than that is a cherry on top'. His charting is full of green because Oregon manufactured space extremely well using the college hashes — which do not exist in the NFL — and because Nix does not miss the gimmes that frustrate with [[Anthony Richardson]] or even [[Matthew Stafford]]. The disconnect is what he leaves on the table: a risk-averse passer who takes the first available throw, checks down or scrambles rather than testing intermediate and deep windows. Arm strength is fine straight down the hallway on seams and digs; outside the numbers Klassen sees a [[Baker Mayfield]]-like loss of control and touch. Realistic ceiling: a version of the Teddy Bridgewater offense with running ability, maybe QB12 down the line, not year one. Host James Koh (not tracked) is more optimistic on the Sean Payton fit and 'vibes' after [[Russell Wilson]].
- 2024-09-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-20]]): Denver is running a similar protected, simplified approach to Washington's with [[Jayden Daniels]], but 'it doesn't look as great.' Waldman placed Nix provisionally in the Daniels tier — a QB you slog through year one with, accumulating experience while the staff limits the situations where things can get out of hand — and said Denver is doing okay by him from that standpoint. He caveated that he needs to watch Nix more before drawing the same conclusion firmly. For fantasy purposes he ranks Daniels as the more promising of the two right now, while noting Daniels himself is not ultra-productive.
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-25 (Campus2Canton with Felix Sharpe)]]): Waldman pushed back on the narrative that Nix is only an RPO and dump-off quarterback, saying he also has a chance to be a really good quarterback in this league. He pointed to the Tampa Bay game, where Nix hit a lot of downfield throws early plus timing shots to receivers that had not appeared in the first two weeks. Waldman framed Nix and [[Jayden Daniels]] as perceptions moving in opposite directions that may both come back toward the middle later on.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): Nix will be up and down this year but Waldman thinks he can be a competent quarterback and long term maybe Matt Ryan or [[Kirk Cousins]] decent, depending on how good the weapons are. From the Raiders tape he saw good throws and good in-pocket decisions, with expected rookie problems: receiver miscommunications, and predetermining reads — passing up a skinny post that broke open for 20-30 yards while backed up, to check down to [[Davante Adams]]. Encouragingly, in two of the past three weeks he has taken more downfield shots, which Waldman credits to Denver giving him room to expand rather than throwing everything at him. His analogy: [[Caleb Williams]] is driving a broken-down manual car in Atlanta traffic, [[Jayden Daniels]] has the electric car with the assist button, and Nix sits in between — less skilled than either, but in a better-managed situation. Context: Sean Payton called Nix a bit of a Ferris Bueller after the two barked at each other in the third quarter.
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]) [Dynasty]: Nix is playing better than it looks — a scrappy, hard-competing player whose athleticism to avoid pressure and still find downfield options is admirable behind an offensive line that has not played well, with some completed downfield throws called back. Denver is putting training wheels on him and lifting them situationally. Aside from one bad early overthrow that was intercepted, he hit impressive throws. Waldman's comp: 'basically what people hoped Mitchell Trubisky would become,' which he means as a good outcome given how much better Nix manages the game. Context: 3-3 with no run game worth anything and a young, unspectacular receiver room. Verdict — bullish, and a dynasty bargain you can likely acquire as a moderately priced trade add-in and profit on later. He and Bob Harris both note Sean Payton is clearly invested in him, and that Payton's history with 'his guy' is good; both also brush off the sideline shouting-match discourse as normal high-pressure conflict.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31]]): Nix is QB9 on the season and QB3 for the month of October, outscoring Burrow, Mahomes, Carr and Stafford and at times [[Jayden Daniels]] — with the caveat that one game came against a bad Panthers defense. Angelo, who wrote his pre- and post-draft RSP profile, said Nix offered one of the best floors in the class as an older prospect who can operate an NFL offense, and calls the Sean Payton fit hand-in-glove because Payton is a no-nonsense coach who puts quarterbacks in position to succeed regardless of personnel. Waldman's comp: Mitchell Trubisky with actual field-general refinement — Trubisky looked great between the twenties and was awful inside them, while Nix keeps you out of bad plays. The mistakes Waldman sees are honest ones, more often inaccurate throws than misreads, plus a tendency to drift and flush outside rather than climb the pocket — none of which he considers a major developmental concern. Both say the supporting cast is the ceiling: [[Courtland Sutton]] is a WR2 at this stage, [[Devaughn Vele]] may be the best receiver, and the backfield of [[Javonte Williams]] and [[Audric Estime]] is unresolved. Waldman notes he is no longer just 'bubble screen Bo'.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): Waldman is 'starting to think we might have to go there' on Nix being the best and most productive rookie quarterback of this class right now, taking some joy in it at [[Jayden Daniels]]' expense. He drove Denver down the field against the Chiefs, is making good reads, and Waldman argues that if he had a receiver who did not 'clap-attack' everything the way [[Courtland Sutton]] does, he would be a 300-yard passer more often. The one flaw: Nix is prone to taking sacks against certain interior blitzes he does not yet know how to maneuver, though he handles other kinds of middle pressure fine. Overall he does not make many mistakes, which Waldman calls the biggest thing. On the experience question, Waldman doubts college starts alone transfer (people made the same argument for [[Brock Purdy]]'s five years) — processing speed, pocket maneuvering, ball placement and game management matter more. His comp: 'basically Mitchell Trubisky without the do-do-do' between the 20s, and crucially he has learned from his early-season mistakes.
- 2024-11-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-18]]): Waldman is 'starting to feel it' on Nix as the best rookie quarterback in the NFL, and Bob Harris (not tracked) went further with the numbers — QB4-to-QB6 on the season at 17.7 points per game, and QB4 since week five at 21.3 — arguing Nix is not merely promising but already a viable fantasy QB1, and asking what he would do with [[Justin Herbert]]'s receivers. Waldman's explanation for why the industry missed: logo scouting off bad Auburn teams, the 'RPO Bo' label, and the age smear (the Chris Weinke framing), when in fact these players should have 10-14 years ahead of them. What Nix did better than anyone in the class was manage the game — throwing the ball away when nothing was there, protecting it in the red zone and inside his own 20. Waldman's comp: 'Mitchell Trubisky with sense.' He also mocked Sean Payton's claim that Denver did not know how athletic Nix was, since Nix skipped combine and pro-day movement work with turf toe but the athleticism was on tape.
- 2024-11-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-21]]): Harmon had no strong pre-draft take on Nix but says the September film for both Nix and the Broncos offense was simply not good — cluttered, heavy on max-protect concepts that get only a couple of routes out and do not challenge modern NFL defenses. By late October and November he likes the offense a lot: Nix knows where routes are supposed to be, is accurate, and puts the ball on receivers to set up yards after catch, which matters in this scheme. Statistically he has been one of the best fantasy quarterbacks of late even as his rushing numbers have declined over three or four games. Harmon frames the Sean Payton relationship as a give and take that took months to negotiate. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) praises Nix's command of the offense and week-over-week growth, and concedes he was overdrafted at 12th overall and could probably have been had in the second round — but Denver is making the pick work.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-19 (NFL QB Lunacy)]]): Nix is Waldman's example of the quarterback a coach should leave in the game rather than bench — a guy who will get in Sean Payton's face and argue his read is right. He may be wrong, but he isn't confused, he's decisive and still making good decisions on the whole, so the deft-handed coach lets him play through it. Waldman contrasts that with Sam Darnold telling Adam Gase he was 'seeing ghosts' and getting a blank stare. Separately, Waldman lists Nix among the quarterbacks widely called reaches on draft day — with [[Justin Herbert]], [[Josh Allen]] and to some the [[C.J. Stroud]] pick — who have looked fine, as a warning against overconfidence in the 'Penix was overdrafted' consensus. He notes Nix has been hot and cold but is 'looking pretty good this year.'
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]): Nix and [[Jayden Daniels]] scored essentially the same in Waldman's top five in last year's RSP — both graded as quality NFL starters — but Waldman deliberately positioned himself more excited than the crowd on Nix and more sober than the crowd on Daniels, calling them 'two sides of the same coin.' Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) named Nix alongside Daniels as the 2024 rookies who far exceeded expectations and who will hold fantasy attention going forward, and suggested Nix may be in a better circumstance than any of the class in terms of coaching, with the supporting cast still to be proven. Waldman's caveat is the [[C.J. Stroud]] and [[Baker Mayfield]] lesson: defenses catch up to rookie tape in year two, so expect Nix to be tested in ways he was not as a rookie.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'fuck it' on Nix at Underdog QB7 despite liking his future — Waldman's stated reason is that quarterback development is an ebb-and-flow process over the first two years, and defenses come back better prepared after a full offseason, posing obstacles a young passer has not yet proven he can solve. He expects sophomore challenges that pull Nix's ADP and value down, but not a trap-door collapse: floor is a low-end QB1 with a chance at top-15. Untracked co-host Bob Harris also called QB7 overpriced but said the rushing ability makes Nix a target at a discount — at the tail end of the QB1s he would own 'a shit ton' of shares, and far more of Nix than of [[Patrick Mahomes]].
- 2025-03-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-31]]): revisiting his pre-draft stance a year on, Waldman uses Nix as his case study for why the age knock was misapplied. Media downgraded Nix (and [[Michael Penix Jr.]]) for being old and over-experienced, but Waldman says he has seen far older quarterbacks with NFL experience never show Nix's self-awareness: he already had a feel for off-platform across-body throws, did not get over-invested in plays at Auburn or Oregon, got rid of the ball fast when the pocket broke, and would throw it away on schemed plays rather than force the receiver. He read the field far better than credited, accounted for defender leverage in target decisions and placement, was a willing tight-window thrower who protected his receivers, and showed subtle manipulation — frame positioning after drops, play fakes, pump fakes, holding defenders with his eyes. Not exciting on a highlight reel, but Waldman notes Nix delivered the eighth-best quarterback season of 2024 and one of the 12 best rookie seasons in league history. He also lists Nix among the prospects whose RSP sweet-spot ADP guidance paid off.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Nix is his No. 5 bust. Harris does not rank him anywhere close to a fantasy starter and thinks the late-season touchdown streak tilted the scale badly. He believes the rushing production was not fake and allows Nix could beat four rushing touchdowns, but says Denver wants to be a defense-and-run-game team playing efficient, low-volume passing — a very different Sean Payton offense from the 650-attempt New Orleans years — and the supporting cast, while not horrible, will make Nix do heavy lifting rather than bail him out, which is not where you want a sophomore quarterback. Harris says a sophomore slump is too strong a word, but he does not expect linear progression: a good outcome is repeating the rookie numbers. He is skipping the [[Kyler Murray]]/Nix tier entirely and would sooner dip into the [[Dak Prescott]] or even [[Jared Goff]] tier. Guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not tracked) had expected Nix on the list instead of [[Baker Mayfield]].

The catalogue: an across-the-body throw on the dead run looking for [[Courtland Sutton]] that was intercepted; a second-quarter fumble after he abandoned a deep shot and ran around the pocket; a near pick-six dropped by Amani Hooker; a throw behind his man to end a drive; and another interception on a bad read to open the third quarter.
