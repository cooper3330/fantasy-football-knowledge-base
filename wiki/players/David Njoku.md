# David Njoku

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his name as "David
> Joker"/"the Djoker"; normalized here to the real name of the Browns tight
> end, David Njoku.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]) [Best Ball]: coming off a stretch-run 2023 as effectively "tight end two" alongside Amari Cooper (Njoku's own page) as Cleveland's top two targets, Waldman doesn't think the [[Jerry Jeudy]] trade knocks Njoku down the pecking order. Still grades him a value at his current TE8 best-ball ADP, and expects "a similar rise" by mid-season — possibly earlier, once the Browns try Jeudy in a role and find he's still probably their third option behind [[Amari Cooper]] and Njoku. Cites a strong [[Deshaun Watson]] connection as the reason Njoku holds his role, calling Jeudy more of a "scheme player" who needs a settled, established passing structure to produce.

- 2024-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-21]]): names Njoku (with [[Amari Cooper]]) as the settled identity of Cleveland's offense — "the identity can be Amari Cooper and David Njoku" — with the rest of the receiver room ([[Jerry Jeudy]], [[Elijah Moore]], [[Cedric Tillman]]) cast as supporting characters rather than reliable pieces in their own right.
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): with [[Jameis Winston]] under center and [[Amari Cooper]] traded, Njoku 'remains the guy' — the safest of the four Cleveland pass catchers in play, ahead of [[Elijah Moore]] on floor, [[Cedric Tillman]] on ceiling and [[Jerry Jeudy]]. Waldman's reasoning was schedule-driven: after the Chargers and Saints, Cleveland faces Pittsburgh, Denver, Pittsburgh and Kansas City before a softer Cincinnati/Miami finish, and Njoku is the option least dependent on a friendly matchup.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31]]): [[Jameis Winston]] is doing something no Cleveland quarterback has done in Njoku's career — throwing him contested, go-up-and-get-it balls. Waldman says Njoku has always been a top-end athlete who can jump and control his body, but the Browns only ever ran him on over routes, seams and sails, and no quarterback there had asked him for 50-50 tape since his Miami days; Winston's first throw of the game was an over route into very tight coverage that Njoku extended for, and the touchdown was an inaccurate ball placed high enough for Njoku to go over the defender's back and pluck it. Waldman says not even Joe Flacco used him that way. He frames it as a usage change that matters: when you have that kind of player, you use him that way.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]) [Dynasty]: the tight end of record in Cleveland, but on borrowed time — his contract is up at the end of the season and Kevin Stefanski is going back to a two-tight-end offense. Waldman's evaluation is that Njoku is a high-functioning linear athlete rather than a top route runner: he wins on crossers, seams and over routes that do not break back to the quarterback, builds a head of steam, is agile enough to leap over people, but is not a stop-start cutback player. He expects Njoku to be [[Joe Flacco]]'s security blanket again if Flacco starts, which he thinks is likely, while arguing that [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] hitting makes Njoku easy to replace with a blocking tight end.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]) [Best Ball]: TE13 is a good value and Waldman is not scared off by either the quarterback situation or the arrival of [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] — he thinks Njoku is at least TE13 even with Fannin succeeding, and that [[Joe Flacco]] has a good shot at starting, which he considers the best fantasy outcome for Cleveland's pass catchers given Flacco's 2023 comeback run. His evaluation is that Njoku is a linear player who does his best work in a straight line; most linear players are not good, but a high-end linear one is, and Cleveland feeds him crossers, over routes, seams and corners — angular routes that do not require breaking back to the quarterback or an advanced route toolbox, letting him use size, speed and length. The longer-term risk Waldman flags is contractual: Njoku is not under contract after next season, and a re-signing would be 'ruinous' for Fannin — though he does not expect it.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: a high-end linear athlete rather than a route technician — Njoku can hurdle defenders, is fast and wins contested plays and is fluid in his lane, but does not run the timing routes a [[Travis Kelce]] runs: no seams, zone sits or corner routes. That, Waldman says, is why he thrived with [[Joe Flacco]], who put him on overs and crossers where he could build speed. He expects Njoku to price himself out of Cleveland in 2026 free agency, with the Browns content to hand the receiving-tight-end role to [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] and sign a cheap blocker behind him.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Njoku is 'a high-end linear player' — very good on crossers, seams and corner routes where the break is not back to the quarterback and he can simply run past people, but not good at the bend-and-decelerate routes that break back to the passer. Waldman's conclusion is that this profile caps him around TE5 and keeps him out of the top three on a regular basis even with [[Joe Flacco]] throwing his way on every down. He expects Njoku to stay in line as a blocker in two-tight-end sets, which is precisely why he thinks [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] ends up as Cleveland's best slot option.
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]): Njoku is a great athlete but a linear one — he does not break down well at the top of stems, so he cannot run the wide-receiver-style timing routes, and instead wins on deep overs, corners, seams, crossers and screens. Waldman contrasts him directly with [[Harold Fannin Jr.]], who he believes can run that route tree. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked) adds that Cleveland has for years been asked to use Njoku in the slot role fans want, but keeps plugging him in-line and feeding him the ball on motion across the middle to create after the catch rather than sending him 20-30 yards downfield — while Fannin is being deployed as a receiver from the outset. The implication both draw is that Fannin's usage is the more fantasy-friendly of the two.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): if you were buying Njoku's last go-round in Cleveland for fantasy this year, Week 1 was a blow. He played a lot and did nothing particularly wrong, and Harris can imagine weeks where Njoku gets more looks — but [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] was the one moving with [[Joe Flacco]] on extended plays, and it came out 7-63 Fannin to 3-37 Njoku, which Harris says is about what the tape looked like.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Njoku had a big day against a very hard Minnesota matchup — jumping over a defender on one play and scoring on a third-and-long slant — but Harris is 'concerned that that's small sample size stuff' with [[Dillon Gabriel]] under center, since bad quarterbacks don't make receivers consistent for fantasy even at tight end. His concession: tight end stinks, so taking a shot on Njoku or [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] is 'okay by me.'
- 2025-10-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-14 (Week 7 Waiver Wire & More W6 Film)]]): Njoku took a hard hit in the ribs early against Pittsburgh, stayed in the game, but was in and out of the lineup afterward with what's now being described as a knee issue. Harris says it's unclear what actually happened and expects both Njoku and [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] could end up sidelined or limited, which would be 'annoying' for fantasy purposes.
- 2025-11-21 — According to Chris Harris and Cousin Josh (guest, untracked) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-21]]: Njoku had not practiced by Thursday with a knee injury heading into Week 12, and Harris said if he's ruled out it 'could breathe the faintest of life into' [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] -- though Fannin would still be stuck throwing to [[Shedeur Sanders]]. Separately, discussing 2026 outlook, the hosts noted Njoku is an unrestricted free agent with no guaranteed money on his deal, meaning he is likely gone from Cleveland next year, which is part of the bull case for Fannin's target share rising.
- 2026-02-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-24]]): Cleveland is set to spend $54M in 2026 cap money on three players -- Joel Bitonio, Wyatt Teller and Njoku -- who won't be on the roster next year, effectively confirming Njoku is a cap casualty gone from the Browns as the team rips off the bandage toward a full financial reset ahead of the 2027 QB draft.
- 2026-07-24 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris ranks Njoku TE31, calling him not good or important enough to be a draftable fantasy tight end, but says he's 'still good enough to muck things up for somebody else' -- specifically flagged as a problem for [[Oronde Gadsden II]]'s target share in Cleveland's tight end room. Harris also notes that drafting [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] was 'a great call' by the Browns, with the twist that a year later it is Njoku, not Fannin, still angling to stick around given how fake NFL salary-cap constraints can be in practice.
