# Olamide Zaccheaus

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]) [Dynasty]: a long-time favorite and a preseason riser in his eyes. Observers early in Washington camp had Zaccheaus on the roster bubble; as the preseason has gone on, Waldman sees a team trying to find ways to get him on the field more often. He cites Drew Lieberman (not a tracked expert), who coached Zaccheaus and says he should be a starter in this league, and argues the league passes him over and labels him because of his size and draft capital rather than his play — a scouting-bias point. Depth-chart read: [[Terry McLaurin]] is the top guy, [[Jahan Dotson]] could still be good and [[Luke McCaffrey]] has had a good camp, so Zaccheaus is likely WR4 with a push to WR3 on good play.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: not draftable, but a genuine watch-list name and possibly a late bloomer. Waldman cites WR coach Drew Lieberman — who has worked with [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and [[Dontayvion Wicks]] and has influenced Waldman's own receiver grading over five to six years — as saying Zaccheaus should have started in Atlanta and forced his way from late-round/UDFA status into a deep-threat role, even though deep threat was not the strength of his game. His actual strength is working in space out of the backfield as a slot, underused at Virginia. He caught only 10 balls for 168 yards and two TDs with Philadelphia in 2023, but the Eagles were not a three-receiver offense. Talk of him as the Commanders' WR2 over [[Jahan Dotson]] shows either how good his camp is or how bad Dotson's is. Waldman puts him in the [[Adam Thielen]] / Ed McCaffrey / Derrick Mason bucket of receivers who were slow starters for two to four years and became good.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): asked to pick among Washington's WR3 candidates — Zaccheaus, Dyami Brown and [[Luke McCaffrey]] — Waldman went 'right away, Olamide Zaccheaus.' He is a good vertical player who gets open in that range of the field and is the most versatile of the three; Dyami Brown gives the vertical element but 'not much else.' Waldman's broader point is the archetype: Zaccheaus is the low-draft-capital player who keeps making rosters teams expected him to be fodder for, and whom teams are reticent to hand a bigger role because they have money invested elsewhere — 'I'm talking to you, Atlanta.' He would not be surprised if Zaccheaus starts the whole season and the team decides they found something. He believes confidence in Zaccheaus plus McCaffrey is part of why Washington felt able to trade [[Jahan Dotson]].
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]): Zaccheaus is 'another favorite of mine but on a lower level' — a career backup contributor, a sixth-man type who can start and has done decent things but has never been signed as a perennial starter. Camp reports have him consistently with the ones and assignment-sound, and there is talk he ends up the three-receiver-set guy more than people realize. Waldman's read is that Chicago is keeping him engaged as versatile insurance rather than anointing him: he has the speed to win outside if [[D.J. Moore]] goes down, could fill the [[Rome Odunze]] role, can play the [[Luther Burden III]] slot role, and was a running back at Virginia, so [[Ben Johnson]]'s backfield concepts are available too. Waldman adds that he never understood why Atlanta used him solely as a deep threat. The function of those reps is to push Burden, not to block him.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-18]]): Zaccheaus is the assignment-sound veteran who can give rookie [[Luther Burden III]] a longer runway, and Waldman expects him to start ahead of Burden for part of the season. He argued Chicago may finally use Zaccheaus as he was meant to be used — a partial running back out of the backfield and a yards-after-catch player — rather than as the deep threat Atlanta 'ham-handedly' turned him into after he started whipping people on deep balls as a returner; his UVA tape showed a genuinely good runner and receiver out of the backfield, which Waldman sees fitting the Ben Johnson offense well. The secondary function is motivational: making Burden earn snaps under a 'no block, no rock' standard.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): worth a late-round stab. Waldman's argument is that the Atlanta version of Zaccheaus — a deep threat who made the team on vertical work in camp — is not who he was at Virginia, where he was a converted running back used out of the backfield and in the slot to exploit his YAC ability. That profile fits Ben Johnson's offense and its open space underneath, and he is versatile enough to play outside when needed. Waldman does not think Zaccheaus is the better long-term player than [[Luther Burden III]], but sees Chicago having the luxury of a veteran who can start the first month while Burden learns assignment-sound detail work. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) said Zach of Bear Report laid it out the same way: Zaccheaus early, Burden coming on.
