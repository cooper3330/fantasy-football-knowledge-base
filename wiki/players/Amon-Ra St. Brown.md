# Amon-Ra St. Brown

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]): **feeling it** as a top-5 wide receiver, on the same basis as [[Keenan Allen]] — elite route running rather than speed, wins against both man and zone rather than just zone, and is deployed both inside and outside. Waldman's contrast case is Deebo Samuel, whom he rates a great zone route runner but not a good man route runner. See [[Zone vs Man Route Running]].
- 2023-12-18 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode [Best Ball]: treats St. Brown as the safer of the pair on continuity — "same play caller," where Allen's offense might change again — and says he'll keep drafting both in January best-ball leagues until better information arrives.

- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]], in passing: names St. Brown first among the players who define Detroit's locker room — a roster deliberately built for "an unselfish brand of football that works with those guys' personalities," alongside [[David Montgomery]], Jared Goff and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]. A situation/culture note, not a change to the evaluation above. See [[Player Development and Coachability]].
- 2024-01-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-25]]): ranked #2 of the playoffs' remaining receivers behind [[Brandon Aiyuk]] on pure ability, but "the most valuable receiver to their team... in a runaway" — "if Amon Ra St. Brown was kidnapped, the Lions would be so screwed" despite good complementary talent (Sam LaPorta, Jahmyr Gibbs, Josh Reynolds). Cites real in-season development as an outside receiver specifically — now beating press-man coverage, not just zone — as the reason he's "closer to that complete elite tier," on the cusp with Aiyuk.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): still "a stud" per Harmon, flagged in a roster-building context rather than a talent one — drafted in 2021, entering the last year of his rookie contract and expected to be "up for a big payday pretty soon," one of several Detroit second-contract decisions (alongside a future [[Jared Goff]] extension) that will shape how much cap room is left to add a needed X receiver.

- 2024-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-04]]), in the same historical poor-college-separator study cited for [[Keon Coleman]]: names St. Brown one of only five "inarguable hits" (with [[Cooper Kupp]], [[Rashee Rice]], Tyler Boyd and Juju Smith-Schuster) among 32 charted prospects who finished below the 35th percentile in two of the three main coverage metrics — all five became "big slot receivers" in the NFL despite playing more outside in college. Contrasts St. Brown's clean hand placement and ball-attacking technique favorably against Coleman's tendency toward unnecessary one-handed grabs.
- 2024-05-14 — According to Adam Rank ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-14]]), not a tracked expert: rounds out his top five with [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] alongside [[DJ Moore]], behind the 'undisputed' top three of Tyreek Hill, Justin Jefferson and Ja'Marr Chase. Rank wonders aloud whether St. Brown gets more credit within the fantasy community than from football fans/media at large, calling him underappreciated outside that circle.
- 2024-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-13]]): Amon-Ra St. Brown's reception-perception profile shows continual year-over-year improvement, not a plateau. Post route success rate jumped from 63% (2022) to 81% (2023); dig route from 74% to 87%; and critically his success rate vs. man coverage rose from 60.3% as a rookie to 70.5% in year two, while his success rate vs. press coverage climbed from 52.2% (rookie) to 68.8% (year two) to 75.6% (year three) -- evidence he's evolved past a slot-only zone-beater into a real X/flanker option, now splitting snaps roughly 50-50 outside/slot versus a 60-40 inside-heavy rookie split, comped to [[CeeDee Lamb]]'s pre-breakout usage arc. Harmon calls him 'Bud Light Cooper Cup' for the power-slot mold. In 2023 he caught 119 passes for roughly 1,515 yards (72.6% catch rate) and 10 touchdowns. Harmon stops short of putting him in his 5-7 man elite WR tier (Justin Jefferson, Davante Adams, Stefon Diggs, Tyreek Hill, A.J. Brown, Ja'Marr Chase, Cooper Kupp) because St. Brown still doesn't win consistently downfield -- only 9.3% of his charted routes are nine routes -- and places him instead at the top of a 'superstar' tier just below elite. Projects a medium 2024 outlook of ~1,447 yards/10 TD with a high-side ceiling around 1,700 yards, one of the highest high-side projections in the league. [Best Ball] [Dynasty]
- 2024-07-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-02]]): Harmon holds up St. Brown as the growth-and-expansion ceiling comp for [[Rashee Rice]], noting St. Brown actually ran his fewest career slant routes (21) in his own rookie season and beat zone coverage better than the Juju Smith-Schuster/Jarvis Landry big-slot archetype Rice currently resembles -- the differentiator Harmon points to for a big slot receiver eventually breaking into true WR1 territory.
- 2024-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-30]]): St. Brown is 'top of tier two' — roughly the ninth best receiver in the league, alongside [[Garrett Wilson]] — behind a tier one of 'six and a half' players, the half being [[Brandon Aiyuk]] depending on how many drinks Harmon has had. He is not willing to call him a top-three receiver and jokes that the Lions-fan expectation has reached the point where 'you either die a hero or live long enough to see yourself become the villain', while stressing that a former day-three pick being top-10 in the league is an incredible outcome. He is emphatic St. Brown is not topped out and names two routes to tier one. The [[CeeDee Lamb]] path: Lamb went from 75.6% vs man and 78.4% vs press in 2022 to 77.9% and 81.2% in 2023 while also raising his zone score, achieved through more usage as an X receiver rather than a change of alignment; St. Brown has already been better than Lamb against zone across their careers, so cranking his man and press marks is the same door. The [[Cooper Kupp]] path — Harmon's long-standing comp, 'Bud Light Cooper Kupp' — is that Kupp's tier-one peak came from becoming solidly above average vs press and man plus pushing zone success rates to 91.4% and 87.3% in 2021-22; St. Brown has actually been better than Kupp vs man and press in years two and three, so becoming a downfield zone beater is his unexplored step. Koh notes the 2023 line of 119 receptions on 164 targets and 10 touchdowns.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): St. Brown is the answer to the holdout problem — a receiver with 'absolutely zero concerns.' Waldman's framing is a drafting philosophy as much as a player take: managers try too hard to hit the bullseye and 'split the hair of the flea on the bullseye' chasing a 20-touchdown or record-breaking season, when the better play is a good player with the highest floor and a ceiling within the neighborhood of top-12. Bob Harris (not tracked) said St. Brown is being overlooked as exactly that guy — 'the new Antonio Brown,' someone who finishes at the top every year whether it is first or fifth, and who played through several injuries last season, which he finds encouraging.
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): St. Brown is a superstar and 'top of Tier 2' receiver for him, but a mostly-slot player whom Detroit deliberately moves and schemes for in three-receiver sets — which is why the team still needed an outside possession body after losing [[Josh Reynolds]]. Harmon also invokes St. Brown's career arc as the model for a slot receiver improving against man and press coverage year over year, using it as the benchmark he would be betting on if he expected [[Rashee Rice]] to grow into full-time outside work. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) describes Detroit as building the passing game around its tight end and St. Brown as the possession receiver, with everything else role-specific around them.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): St. Brown's six targets (2 catches, 49 yards, TD) is a clear outlier — only the second time since Week 8 of 2022 that he's seen six or fewer — and Harmon is 'not worried,' calling him a great player and an integral part of the offense. He ran 30 routes to [[Jameson Williams]]' 28 but was out-targeted 9-6. Harmon's structural read is that St. Brown has improved against press every season, clearing a 70% success rate versus press in year three, so Detroit can now trust him on the line of scrimmage; his 2023 snaps-on-the-line rate of 37.9% was already way up from his rookie year and Harmon suspects Week 1 was higher still, letting the Lions play him as a power slot who fits the run while hiding [[Sam LaPorta]] as a blocker. Caveat Harmon states plainly: if Williams stacks performances, it does change the complexion of the distribution, and Williams could climb past fifth-most-important on this offense. His ideal is Detroit keeping ~80% of the Williams usage and giving 20% back to St. Brown.
