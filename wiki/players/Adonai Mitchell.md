# Adonai Mitchell

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-29 — According to guest **Eric Froton** (NBC Sports college football analyst — not a tracked expert) and **James Koh** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, hosted solo by Koh with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]) [Dynasty]: both are "torn" on him. Elite ball-tracking and hand-eye coordination on deep balls at an estimated 6'4"/190 — Froton says he wins late via hand usage, timing and tracking at the catch point — but a thin, largely linear release without much route manipulation, and real short-to-intermediate separation questions: 1.8 yards per route, only three targets behind the line of scrimmage all season, and 3.4 yards after the catch. Froton grades him behind [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and [[Keon Coleman]] in this tier of big receivers because he hasn't shown the "complete package" needed to profile as more than a deep-threat specialist at the next level, and separately flags Texas teammate [[Xavier Worthy]] as a similar size/speed archetype who tests faster.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): needs to refine his release packages against man coverage. Has violent hands — a good, uncommon quality especially among vertically-skilled prospects — but doesn't apply his hand counters consistently when defenders shoot their own hands, and his footwork needs refinement so he attacks the opponent's leverage deep enough into the stem to set up those counters. Speed isn't a problem — he varies it well to bait defenders and attack leverage during stems to set up breaks. Waldman's projection: sharper, flatter breaks plus better releases could make him a more complete receiver rather than just a vertical option.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): names him his first "do not draft" pick if he were an NFL GM. Elite athlete (4.34, 6'3"+) but the issue is lack of intent in his routes and off the ball — flashes lethargic in-cuts that turn into tipped balls or interceptions, and shows up "when he wants to show up." Devastating in the low red zone (a short list of class winners there, alongside 2023's Tank Dell), but a totally different player between the 20s. Risk comp if he doesn't develop within his first 16 months: Denzel Mims or Terrace Marshall Jr. (neither tracked) — hyper-athletic receivers who never developed a mature enough NFL skill set for consistent touches, in a receiver-deep class and league.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on *Reception Perception: The Show* ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-11]]), with co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert): finished full-sample Reception Perception profile — measured 6'2"/205 with a 4.34 40, 39.5" vertical and 11'4" broad jump. Pushes back hard on "lazy"/loafing narratives (including Mitchell's own combine explanation that he doesn't run every route full speed so he can play whole drives), arguing the entire Texas receiver corps does it and it reads as coaching/scheme rather than a personal effort issue; separately notes some teams have flagged personality concerns. 82.8% of his snaps were on the line — the highest of any 2024 prospect Harmon has charted, a true X-receiver alignment profile. Strong man/press success rates (84th/83rd percentile) but a weak 31st-percentile success rate vs. zone, needing faster route landmarks and better leverage/timing understanding. Elite ball-tracking and body control on 35% downfield-route usage (21% go/corner, 10.2% post); poor after the catch, which Harmon argues is an overrated criticism for a true vertical X anyway. 52% of his routes were short/intermediate in-breakers (19.3% digs, 18.2% slants, 14.4% curls) with strong 82.4%/89.5% success on digs/outs — the trait Harmon says most excites him about the ceiling, comparing him favorably in that area to his own cautious [[George Pickens]] comp. Aggressive player comp: [[DeAndre Hopkins]] at his peak, for the same "lulls you to sleep" tempo change into a sharp, late break against man coverage. Best would-fit landing spots named: Kansas City or Buffalo, for a patient, aggressive downfield quarterback; sees him going anywhere from very late Round 1 to mid-Round 2, not the best X receiver in this class but still a clear Day 2-or-better bare minimum given the class's overall receiver depth. See [[Reception Perception Methodology]] and [[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]].
- 2024-04-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-23 (Evaluation vs. Valuation)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman pushes back on first-round valuations of Mitchell, projecting him instead as more likely a mid-second-round pick comparable to [[George Pickens]], due to route-running inconsistency he saw watching Mitchell run routes at the combine — quarterbacks reportedly couldn't count on him breaking at a precise depth on comeback-style routes, and Waldman 'never knew where he was going to be.' He credits Mitchell's ability to take the top off a defense and make plays downfield, but frames the overall pick as a matter of risk: how much growth he still has and how well he can learn detail-oriented route concepts, not raw talent.
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]) [Dynasty]: called Adonai Mitchell the "easy answer" for most likely to disappoint his next team given known volatility in his profile and reported makeup/character questions (including diabetes-management reporting cited from Bob McGinn's Go Long piece), but declined to actually give him the superlative because Harmon likes the player overall and buys into his long-term path to being a productive X receiver -- though he's unsure Mitchell becomes a true WR1. Later also named him as deserving consideration among the class's best pure contested-catch ball winners, alongside [[Xavier Legette]], [[Keon Coleman]] and [[Javon Baker]].
- 2024-05-02 — According to Brandon Angelo ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): Mitchell was the class's most notable draft-slide surprise — Angelo had his line set at pick 17.5 and he fell to day two, going after the less-talented [[Ja'Lynn Polk]]. Angelo said Mitchell lands with a good developmental nucleus in Reggie Wayne and OC Jim Bob Cooter but isn't a 'set it and forget it' Day 1 starter, which he suspects is part of why teams passed on him relative to public boards; the hosts noted NFL teams have interview/background access outside evaluators don't, which can explain a slide like this.
- 2024-05-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-02]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon calls Mitchell landing in Indianapolis in the second round (No. 52 overall) a great fit and great value. The Colts, per Harmon, had two of three field areas settled — [[Josh Downs]] short and [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] intermediate (a player Harmon slots alongside Deebo Samuel/D.J. Moore as a strong but not-quite-true-No.-1) — and needed exactly the vertical, press-beating boundary X Mitchell profiles as. Mitchell ranks 6th in the class in man-coverage success rate (just behind [[Malik Nabers]]) and 5th versus press (just ahead of [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]); his zone score needs work but matters less in a pure boundary role. Harmon notes unspecified coachability/character concerns reported around the league but says he isn't in position to evaluate them personally. He comps Mitchell's evaluation directly to George Pickens — both graded tier-three fringe-round-1/round-2 by Harmon and both went 52nd overall, the same grade Zay Flowers and Jordan Addison got the year before. Per Yahoo's Charles Robinson, the Colts were reportedly heavily interested in [[Xavier Worthy]] in round one before pivoting, having previously let opposing defenses 'squat' on their receivers under Matt Ryan/Gardner Minshew.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: both hosts flag the Mitchell/[[Marshawn Lloyd]] range at the end of the second round as an awkward decision. Waldman says he rarely rosters Mitchell because a leaguemate always drafts him a round earlier, driven by first-round buzz he carried for much of the pre-draft process. If forced to choose, Waldman takes Mitchell when he needs receiver help, but fears he becomes 'bench and hold' like [[Jameson Williams]] or Henry Ruggs — huge tools without the role to cash them in — while Lloyd offers a clearer runway if he's set at receiver. Angelo separately names Mitchell his top pick to sizzle in camp but disappoint as a rookie: he 'really looks the part' with no contact, fast and fluid, but untested against press/man coverage.
- 2024-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (untracked co-host) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-11]]) [Dynasty]: Mitchell (6'2', 205 lbs, 4.40 forty, 39.5-inch vertical) fell to Indianapolis in the second round despite first-round grades around the league. His college tape shows an 84th-percentile success rate vs. man and 83rd vs. press, which Koh calls 'exactly what you want out of your vertical X receiver' -- the missing piece in a Colts room that already had [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] and [[Josh Downs]] but lacked a true outside vertical threat. Both hosts say if Mitchell is ready as a rookie, Indianapolis' WR room is 'way better than people think,' though they flag they don't fully know why he fell in the draft or whether those concerns carry onto the field.
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]): ESPN's Stephen Holder reports Mitchell and [[Alec Pierce]] splitting first-team reps, and Harmon says this is emphatically not the time to freak out about a rookie behind a third-year veteran. Even if Mitchell spends September as the fourth receiver, Harmon argues rookies need time to adjust to NFL speed, NFL route trees and varied alignments they rarely faced in college. He warns against the modern habit of writing a receiver off if he does not produce as a rookie. The upside case is clear: Pierce holds a purely sacrificial role, while Mitchell 'presents the ability to maximize that X-receiver position,' and Harmon says once Mitchell is on the field the whole Colts receiving corps becomes more exciting.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Mitchell's RP prospect profile was the most alignment-limited of any top-two-round prospect in the database from his class — outside on 83% of sample snaps and on the line for 82.8% — with a mostly vertical X route tree (nine, post, dig, out, corner above prospect average; slant within it), and an 84th-percentile success rate versus man and 83rd versus press against just 31st percentile versus zone. That pointed to a straight X role, which fit Indianapolis cleanly. But with [[Josh Downs]] out on a high ankle sprain, the staff has used Mitchell in a variety of formations including the slot, and Harmon believes from camp reporting they concluded he can do more than run deep routes outside. He thinks press-man-beating X remains his primary spot long term, but his movement skills and lull-you-to-sleep route running could support a move-around role that makes him the missing piece in that room.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Going Deep)]]): a notable reversal. Angelo was not a fan coming into the draft, believing an outside role in the NFL would be a disaster for Mitchell. With [[Josh Downs]] out a few weeks with an ankle, Mitchell gets to play the slot in a Shane Steichen scheme favorable to slot receivers — easy targets, two-way goes, confidence-building work. Angelo maintains Mitchell's raw talent sits right there with [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] near the top of the class and that it was production and attitude reports that pushed him down boards; if he cleans that up in this role, he might not come off the field. He rates Mitchell more talented than Downs and a better vertical separator from the slot, if less reliable, which matters because Indianapolis lacks a downfield threat to pair with [[Anthony Richardson]]'s arm — [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] is fine but has not done it and [[Alec Pierce]] has not either. He calls the fit a potential home run.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): an encouraging debut that the box score badly understates. Mitchell was wide open on a touchdown that [[Anthony Richardson]] overthrew and open again on another long fourth-quarter shot that missed; Koh (not tracked) says converting either — let alone both — would have had people's hair on fire. Harmon says Mitchell was consistently separating on the broadcast view and looked schemed for, but finished at just 0.11 yards per route run, which he calls 'not his fault,' though he does wonder whether Mitchell ran the first route exactly as Richardson expected. On alignment, nobody on the Colts ran more than 33.3% of their routes from the slot — [[Michael Pittman Jr.]], Mitchell and [[Alec Pierce]] all rotated through — and Harmon expects [[Josh Downs]] to reclaim that spot when healthy. He'd like to go back and chart this game.
- 2024-09-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-17]]): Harmon liked Mitchell coming into the year but did not have him or [[Josh Downs]] as top-50 players at the position. Through two weeks Mitchell has had splash moments as a route runner but has repeatedly not been in the right place at the right time, and Harmon wonders whether he is simply not on the same page with [[Anthony Richardson]] the way Downs was as a rookie. With Downs hurt, Mitchell has been forced into slot reps to see whether he can hold up there, which Harmon thinks explains some of the inconsistency. Koh (not tracked) raises the roster question directly: can you sit [[Alec Pierce]] for a prospect like Mitchell given the start Pierce is having? Harmon says he is not sure either and expects a decent amount of rotation, with Mitchell's clearest long-term role being over-the-top work alongside Pierce.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Mitchell is the squeeze in a crowded Colts room. Harmon says he has flashed really well through two weeks and that other separation metrics like what they see from him, but there have been rookie mistakes and he looks like he is swimming a little. He is emphatic that the staff is very high on Mitchell and will keep rotating him in because he is very, very talented — but with [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] undroppable, [[Alec Pierce]] needed to stretch defenses and [[Josh Downs]] returning to the slot, Harmon cannot see where the snaps come from in the near term.
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): the return of [[Josh Downs]] gave a resounding answer to the Mitchell-versus-[[Alec Pierce]] snap question and it went against Mitchell — Pierce ran 19 routes to Mitchell's two, and Mitchell played six total snaps for an 11 percent snap share. Harmon acknowledges Mitchell is popping in separation metrics and that there are moments where the route running is good and he gets open, but says he has made a lot of mistakes and has not consistently been in the right place, which has made some [[Anthony Richardson]] misses look worse than they were. Koh treats the split as settled for now rather than a one-week blip.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): Waldman is against the industry consensus on Mitchell. Every analyst he hears is pointing at Mitchell as the Colts receiver to own, and the numbers support it — strong separation metrics and more targets than [[Alec Pierce]] — but Waldman says the quality behind those numbers is poor. Watching the tape, Mitchell is falling down and slipping on routes across multiple games and has dropped passes in multiple games: rookie mistakes. By contrast Pierce is making few mistakes and winning on harder plays. Waldman hedges that separation-and-target profiles sometimes do play out, but his read is that the metrics are flattering Mitchell relative to what is actually on film. This is a direct disagreement with the prevailing separation-based case for Mitchell.
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]): Bob Harris (not tracked) reports Colts beat writers saying Mitchell is not far off — they still view [[Alec Pierce]] as the better player, but think Mitchell matches or beats him on separation. Waldman disagrees on the timeline: he thinks Mitchell is 'a year away,' which in his own frame means not far off but by beat-writer standards is far off. The tells are technical rather than athletic — Mitchell trips and falls on routes he should not, indicative of a player still thinking his way through the offense, and drops passes he should not. The speed looks right; the technique does not yet. He expects Mitchell to be a good one, just not this year.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-31]]): Mitchell 'definitely gets separation' and flashes, but also makes a lot of mistakes — part of why Harmon says there is little positive play from Indianapolis's outside receivers. The interesting angle for him is developmental: the [[Joe Flacco]] insertion means the Colts are trying to develop a quarterback and young receivers at the same time, and Harmon's hope is that a veteran who has thrown to countless receivers tells Mitchell exactly what he expects, speeding up his developmental curve so he is a better player whenever [[Anthony Richardson]] returns. That chemistry, rather than immediate production, is what Harmon says he would be watching for.
- 2024-11-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-29]]): Mitchell's rookie Reception Perception profile says X receiver, and the Colts' early-season deployment worked against him. Alignment 68.7% outside, 65.3% on the line; 84.2% success vs press (highest in the class other than [[Ladd McConkey]], who faced almost none) on 20.7% of sampled routes, 71.4% vs man, but 72% vs zone — tied with [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] for the worst zone mark in the rookie report, and well under Harmon's 77% alarm line for an outside receiver. He argues Indianapolis, thrilled with him in camp, moved him to off-ball slot when [[Josh Downs]] hurt his ankle, leaving a rookie 'treading water at two positions' — hence the drops and misread spots with [[Anthony Richardson]]. Comps: a [[George Pickens]]/[[DeAndre Hopkins]] cross for how he wins, with Brett Kollmann's [[CeeDee Lamb]] comp accepted only for the bendy break-point movement; James Koh floats 'a very poor man's [[Justin Jefferson]].' Long term Harmon sees number-one-receiver skills possibly arriving by year three on the Pickens growth curve, provided the zone number and consistency rise, but concedes a Discord listener's [[Dontayvion Wicks]] comp is apt — a talented fourth receiver blocked by [[Michael Pittman Jr.]], Downs and [[Alec Pierce]]. He wants Mitchell fed more short routes (86.7% on slants, 100% on curls at 10.9% of routes).
- 2025-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-07]]): Harmon is 'certainly not giving up on him' after a rocky rookie year. He reads Mitchell as a pure perimeter player he no longer wants to see in the slot given his movement skills — a man-coverage beater, not a good zone beater, who can win tight-coverage receptions but has issues at the catch point and with drops. He jokes that Mitchell and [[Dontayvion Wicks]] may be the same person: separation against man, mistakes at the catch point, and both blocked by a sacrificial X ([[Alec Pierce]] and [[Romeo Doubs]] respectively). Harmon reconstructs how the slot experiment happened — Pierce had a good camp, [[Josh Downs]] got hurt, the staff loved Mitchell and forced a way to get all three on the field — and says it overloaded his brain. Koh calls the assignment unfair given Mitchell had never played slot at any level, and wants him focused purely on outside X work this offseason, especially with the league short of X receivers.

- 2025-05-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-23]]): Mitchell posted 'the most extreme X-receiver profile' Harmon has charted — 90.5% success on slants, 90.9% on outs, 88.2% on curls and a functional 59% on nine routes, against 50% on posts and corners, sub-70% on digs, and a 14th-percentile 72.6% vs zone. Man (67%) is fine and press (76.9%) is good. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) framed him the same way: excellent on nines, slants and intermediate out/curls, below average everywhere else. Harmon's read is that Indianapolis fell in love with him in camp and, when [[Josh Downs]] hurt his ankle, played him in the slot and at flanker — the one place he should never line up, since he was an 83% on-the-line, 83% outside pure X as a prospect with an already-poor 31st-percentile zone grade. Harmon notes the zone-heavy pure-X historical neighbors run from busts (Jalen Reagor, Kelvin Benjamin, [[Kadarius Toney]]) to 2015 [[Mike Evans]], 2018 A.J. Green and 2024 [[DK Metcalf]] — none of them career-best seasons, but all pure X's. He is also among the worst after-catch players Harmon has charted: in space on only 42.9% of catches and down on first contact 100% of the time, with no power despite loose, fluid movement. Harmon calls him 'a poor man's [[Dontayvion Wicks]]' and doubts he fully displaces [[Alec Pierce]], who is roughly 10% better on nine routes — a year-three question rather than a year-two breakout. Koh argued Kansas City should have taken him over [[Xavier Worthy]]; Harmon pushed back, saying Mitchell's zone weakness would be exposed against the two-high looks the Chiefs see.
- 2025-06-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-06]]): Mitchell is the fourth distinct receiver type in a crowded Colts room and is one of the guys Indianapolis 'should want to get activated' — but Harmon's core point is that with this quarterback situation they simply are not going to get all of these players activated. Koh (not tracked) floated an 'Adonai Mitchell season' half-jokingly off the observation that [[Riley Leonard]]'s best charted routes were slants, curls and nines, i.e. an outside-X vertical profile that would suit Mitchell. Harmon adds that Mitchell and [[Josh Downs]] are middle-of-the-field types relative to [[Alec Pierce]]'s pure vertical role, and suspects this is the last year the whole four-man room is together.
- 2025-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-13]]): Mitchell is an X receiver only — 'I don't want to see that guy play anywhere else other than X' — and at this point a developmental player rather than a 2025 contributor, rotating in behind [[Alec Pierce]]. Harmon frames him as a maybe-breakout candidate for 2026, when Pierce's contract expires, while flagging that who is coaching and playing quarterback for Indianapolis in 2026 is an open question. He was a disaster as a run blocker in 2024 and poor at the finer points of the position, particularly settling versus zone. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) tied that to a broader pattern he sees with Texas receivers doing the bare minimum in the run game.
- 2025-07-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-28]]): Mitchell's drops are technique-oriented, not focus-related like [[Keon Coleman]]'s, and that makes them the harder problem. Waldman describes him as a 'clap attacker' — hands apart rather than meeting together as the arms extend toward the ball, which creates a chance to fight the football. He argues most of the league's low catch-percentage receivers are clap attackers, and that it is so fundamental it should have been coached out before college; it survives because elite athletes thrive in spite of it and get told how good they are until the pros, when the starting job comes under jeopardy. [[Diontae Johnson]] is his example of a player who eventually had to confront it. Waldman thinks Mitchell can turn the corner but says he has not yet acknowledged the problem, and notes Indianapolis kept forcing him into the lineup while he dropped balls with [[Anthony Richardson]].
- 2025-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-01]]): Mitchell is tantalizing talent but a weird profile, and Harmon thinks he is somewhat overrated as a separator because his good numbers come against press on isolated routes. Harmon — normally the analyst asking how to get a receiver snaps from multiple alignments — says the opposite here: he never wants to see Mitchell take snaps anywhere other than X. Indianapolis tried moving him in 2024 and the results were terrible, a 14th-percentile success rate versus zone with no choice routes and no option work. Harmon's prescription is to keep him at X running outs, nines, curls and slants and nothing else, noting Mitchell has said he never played slot going back to high school, which Harmon says the charting confirms. Koh's framing is that a second-round pick being the group's bench player is a marker of how deep the Colts room is.
