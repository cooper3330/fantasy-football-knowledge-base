# Adonai Mitchell

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-29 — According to guest **Eric Froton** (NBC Sports college football analyst — not a tracked expert) and **James Koh** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, hosted solo by Koh with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]) [Dynasty]: both are "torn" on him. Elite ball-tracking and hand-eye coordination on deep balls at an estimated 6'4"/190 — Froton says he wins late via hand usage, timing and tracking at the catch point — but a thin, largely linear release without much route manipulation, and real short-to-intermediate separation questions: 1.8 yards per route, only three targets behind the line of scrimmage all season, and 3.4 yards after the catch. Froton grades him behind [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and [[Keon Coleman]] in this tier of big receivers because he hasn't shown the "complete package" needed to profile as more than a deep-threat specialist at the next level, and separately flags Texas teammate [[Xavier Worthy]] as a similar size/speed archetype who tests faster.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): needs to refine his release packages against man coverage. Has violent hands — a good, uncommon quality especially among vertically-skilled prospects — but doesn't apply his hand counters consistently when defenders shoot their own hands, and his footwork needs refinement so he attacks the opponent's leverage deep enough into the stem to set up those counters. Speed isn't a problem — he varies it well to bait defenders and attack leverage during stems to set up breaks. Waldman's projection: sharper, flatter breaks plus better releases could make him a more complete receiver rather than just a vertical option.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): names him his first "do not draft" pick if he were an NFL GM. Elite athlete (4.34, 6'3"+) but the issue is lack of intent in his routes and off the ball — flashes lethargic in-cuts that turn into tipped balls or interceptions, and shows up "when he wants to show up." Devastating in the low red zone (a short list of class winners there, alongside 2023's Tank Dell), but a totally different player between the 20s. Risk comp if he doesn't develop within his first 16 months: Denzel Mims or Terrace Marshall Jr. (neither tracked) — hyper-athletic receivers who never developed a mature enough NFL skill set for consistent touches, in a receiver-deep class and league.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on *Reception Perception: The Show* ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-11]]), with co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert): finished full-sample Reception Perception profile — measured 6'2"/205 with a 4.34 40, 39.5" vertical and 11'4" broad jump. Pushes back hard on "lazy"/loafing narratives (including Mitchell's own combine explanation that he doesn't run every route full speed so he can play whole drives), arguing the entire Texas receiver corps does it and it reads as coaching/scheme rather than a personal effort issue; separately notes some teams have flagged personality concerns. 82.8% of his snaps were on the line — the highest of any 2024 prospect Harmon has charted, a true X-receiver alignment profile. Strong man/press success rates (84th/83rd percentile) but a weak 31st-percentile success rate vs. zone, needing faster route landmarks and better leverage/timing understanding. Elite ball-tracking and body control on 35% downfield-route usage (21% go/corner, 10.2% post); poor after the catch, which Harmon argues is an overrated criticism for a true vertical X anyway. 52% of his routes were short/intermediate in-breakers (19.3% digs, 18.2% slants, 14.4% curls) with strong 82.4%/89.5% success on digs/outs — the trait Harmon says most excites him about the ceiling, comparing him favorably in that area to his own cautious [[George Pickens]] comp. Aggressive player comp: [[DeAndre Hopkins]] at his peak, for the same "lulls you to sleep" tempo change into a sharp, late break against man coverage. Best would-fit landing spots named: Kansas City or Buffalo, for a patient, aggressive downfield quarterback; sees him going anywhere from very late Round 1 to mid-Round 2, not the best X receiver in this class but still a clear Day 2-or-better bare minimum given the class's overall receiver depth. See [[Reception Perception Methodology]] and [[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]].
- 2024-04-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-23 (Evaluation vs. Valuation)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman pushes back on first-round valuations of Mitchell, projecting him instead as more likely a mid-second-round pick comparable to [[George Pickens]], due to route-running inconsistency he saw watching Mitchell run routes at the combine — quarterbacks reportedly couldn't count on him breaking at a precise depth on comeback-style routes, and Waldman 'never knew where he was going to be.' He credits Mitchell's ability to take the top off a defense and make plays downfield, but frames the overall pick as a matter of risk: how much growth he still has and how well he can learn detail-oriented route concepts, not raw talent.
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]) [Dynasty]: called Adonai Mitchell the "easy answer" for most likely to disappoint his next team given known volatility in his profile and reported makeup/character questions (including diabetes-management reporting cited from Bob McGinn's Go Long piece), but declined to actually give him the superlative because Harmon likes the player overall and buys into his long-term path to being a productive X receiver -- though he's unsure Mitchell becomes a true WR1. Later also named him as deserving consideration among the class's best pure contested-catch ball winners, alongside [[Xavier Legette]], [[Keon Coleman]] and [[Javon Baker]].
- 2024-05-02 — According to Brandon Angelo ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): Mitchell was the class's most notable draft-slide surprise — Angelo had his line set at pick 17.5 and he fell to day two, going after the less-talented [[Ja'Lynn Polk]]. Angelo said Mitchell lands with a good developmental nucleus in Reggie Wayne and OC Jim Bob Cooter but isn't a 'set it and forget it' Day 1 starter, which he suspects is part of why teams passed on him relative to public boards; the hosts noted NFL teams have interview/background access outside evaluators don't, which can explain a slide like this.
- 2024-05-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-02]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon calls Mitchell landing in Indianapolis in the second round (No. 52 overall) a great fit and great value. The Colts, per Harmon, had two of three field areas settled — [[Josh Downs]] short and [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] intermediate (a player Harmon slots alongside Deebo Samuel/D.J. Moore as a strong but not-quite-true-No.-1) — and needed exactly the vertical, press-beating boundary X Mitchell profiles as. Mitchell ranks 6th in the class in man-coverage success rate (just behind [[Malik Nabers]]) and 5th versus press (just ahead of [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]); his zone score needs work but matters less in a pure boundary role. Harmon notes unspecified coachability/character concerns reported around the league but says he isn't in position to evaluate them personally. He comps Mitchell's evaluation directly to George Pickens — both graded tier-three fringe-round-1/round-2 by Harmon and both went 52nd overall, the same grade Zay Flowers and Jordan Addison got the year before. Per Yahoo's Charles Robinson, the Colts were reportedly heavily interested in [[Xavier Worthy]] in round one before pivoting, having previously let opposing defenses 'squat' on their receivers under Matt Ryan/Gardner Minshew.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: both hosts flag the Mitchell/[[Marshawn Lloyd]] range at the end of the second round as an awkward decision. Waldman says he rarely rosters Mitchell because a leaguemate always drafts him a round earlier, driven by first-round buzz he carried for much of the pre-draft process. If forced to choose, Waldman takes Mitchell when he needs receiver help, but fears he becomes 'bench and hold' like [[Jameson Williams]] or Henry Ruggs — huge tools without the role to cash them in — while Lloyd offers a clearer runway if he's set at receiver. Angelo separately names Mitchell his top pick to sizzle in camp but disappoint as a rookie: he 'really looks the part' with no contact, fast and fluid, but untested against press/man coverage.
