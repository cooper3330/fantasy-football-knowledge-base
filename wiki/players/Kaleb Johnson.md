# Kaleb Johnson

## Expert Takes

- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: the back in the class most dependent on landing spot. Angelo calls him a plus-plus-plus athlete relative to [[Trey Sermon]], but a field-based zone runner whose production hinges entirely on cohesive blocking up front — give him a runway and an alley and he is an explosive accelerator who is good enough through contact, but he cannot create behind the line of scrimmage the way [[David Montgomery]] can when hit at or behind it. The comp offered is Rashaad Penny: three, four, five steps of ground and he makes something happen; hit early and he is stuck. Angelo sees a starting-caliber-plus back if he lands in a zone-heavy scheme with a sound offensive mind, and trouble early in his career if he does not. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed, expecting scouts to view him as an obvious wide-zone-specific fit.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'fuck it' at Underdog RB21 relative to [[David Montgomery]], but with a real conditional upside case. Waldman: Johnson is big and fast, and if he lands with a good offense that runs a majority of outside zone he is the kind of back who can post a 'shooting star' number that gets everyone excited. Fit is everything for him — Waldman noted that every analyst has already identified him as an outside zone back, so a heavy outside zone team should take him, and the failure mode is an owner (he joked about Mark Davis) dropping him into a gap scheme instead.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]) [Dynasty]: 'the safest running back in this class.' Waldman notes Johnson is talked about as an outside zone runner and that is his best fit, but he also ran gap plays well — and Arthur Smith, a good run coordinator who can scheme run plays, said the same and called him explosive with quick twitch. The environment supports him: a good Pittsburgh defense, a team that wants to run the ball a fair bit, and a young offensive line the Steelers believe will keep improving. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) asked whether he is the next [[Najee Harris]]; Waldman replied that Harris was a safe play — four straight 1,000-yard seasons behind a bad line. Limits named: [[Jaylen Warren]] takes away receiving work, and Waldman does not believe Mason Rudolph is the answer at quarterback, expecting [[Aaron Rodgers]] to start and open up the run game via the threat he poses.
- 2025-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) led his favorite-landing-spot list with Johnson, noting his ZAP model spat out [[James Conner]] and Le'Veon Bell as top analytical comps — and the Iowa back then landed with the black and gold in Pittsburgh, in the zone scheme his projection needed. He expects [[Jaylen Warren]] to stay involved as a change of pace in 2025 but points out Warren is a free agent next year and that Pittsburgh under Mike Tomlin has historically not been shy about true bell-cow usage; the caveat is coaching churn, with Arthur Smith and even Tomlin not guaranteed to be back. He values the immediate opportunity for confidence and development and is throwing darts at Johnson in redraft. Waldman agreed the fit in Pittsburgh is perfect for what they want to do, and mentioned drafting both Johnson and [[Matthew Golden]] on the same team.
- 2025-08-15 — According to Cousin Josh (untracked co-host of the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast]]) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-15]]): Johnson is his number 3 frag player at a sixth-round ADP of 70, ranked RB35 on his own board. He sees a two-down back who is not especially elusive or fast, with limited receiving ability and admitted pass-protection work to do — which in an [[Aaron Rodgers]] offense he calls a death sentence — and expects something like a 70-30 split early in favour of [[Jaylen Warren]], who he thinks owns passing downs. He objects to spending a sixth on a player who has not taken an NFL snap when [[Tony Pollard]], [[Calvin Ridley]], [[Travis Hunter]], [[Chris Olave]] and [[Jaylen Waddle]] go around there, and says the hero-RB build is where this pick actually kills you. [[Chris Harris]] is also below ADP but less severely, with Warren 27 and Johnson 30 in standard, Warren 26 and Johnson 33 in PPR, and he voiced the bull case: Johnson is exceptional at diagnosing outside zone, exactly what Arthur Smith wants, with good phone-booth burst — a chance he is a Le'Veon Bell-style reactor. Both noted the rookie objection loses sting at a fifth or sixth-round price.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]): Waldman contrasts Pittsburgh's handling of Johnson with Chicago's slow-rolling of [[Luther Burden III]] — the Steelers do want to use Johnson right away as the main guy, though [[Jaylen Warren]] is still there along with veterans the staff could live with but would not feel good about. He notes Mike Tomlin delivered a mild public kick, saying he would like to see Johnson be more comfortable and not think so much, and just let it rip, because they know what he can be; he did not look ready in that preseason game. Waldman treats that as the classic overthinking problem rather than a talent verdict, connecting it to playing at the speed of instinct, and to his [[Nick Chubb]] theory that a rookie back's first game tape needs contextual study rather than box-score reaction.
- 2025-08-18 — According to guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert), on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Johnson is his No. 1 bust and his version of the [[RJ Harvey]] problem — a good rookie back whose role he cannot justify. Doherty has grown steadily more bearish: Johnson is not the third-down back and he doubts he will be the pass protector, while [[Aaron Rodgers]] plays slow as molasses and loves to throw at the goal line, so what is the upside for a two-down rookie in that offense? He notes the ADP has already fallen outside the top 24 backs. [[Chris Harris]] is close but slightly lower, and lays out the bull case he is not making himself: Arthur Smith wanted someone to run his outside-zone offense and Johnson, a thud athlete by scouting reputation, is excellent at exactly that from Iowa — a lot of volume in a boring offense, essentially Najee-plus, at a sixth or seventh-round price. Harris' fear is that Rodgers wins out over Smith and slows the whole thing down. His cousin Josh had Johnson on the previous Friday's bust list for the same reasons.
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: still feeling good and unchanged on Johnson despite a soft second preseason game, projecting 260-280 attempts, close to 1,200 yards and six or seven touchdowns — a genuine fantasy starter at a late-sixth/early-seventh round cost. Waldman clarifies that his standing criticism is of Steelers fans and media, not the organization: he jokes they have made a living being wrong about running backs (souring on Le'Veon Bell after year one, anointing Benny Snell over [[James Conner]]) and expects the same treatment of Johnson. He thinks the offensive line is young and improving, that Johnson may need a ramp-up and might not even start immediately, but will build a case to be the main guy within three to four weeks. He also argues practice is one of the worst ways to judge a runner — they run through car-wash curtains as tacklers — citing Kyle Shanahan that you do not know what you have until extensive real-game situations. [[Chris Harris]] notes his cousin and Pat Doherty of Rotoworld (neither tracked) have both called Johnson a bust, and says Waldman's view is what has informed his own on-air defense.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): 'oh fuck yes' Johnson is better than his preseason buzz. Waldman says the worry from a poor first preseason game was dispelled by the next two, with coaches openly excited, and reads Mike Tomlin's public pressure as normal rookie coaching rather than a red flag — Pittsburgh knows how to coach even if it does not know how to assuage fantasy analysts. He argues draftniks split on Johnson because he is big but not fast, dismissing him as a plodder with poor footwork, and that Arthur Smith — who understands running backs and running-game design — valued him and got 'his guy'. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) is ambivalent: good long-term landing spot, but he has not seen pass protection proven, the season hinges on a 40-plus-year-old [[Aaron Rodgers]] staying upright, and he has been drafting more [[Jaylen Warren]], citing Ed Bouchette's reporting that people in the building saw Warren as the back of the future.
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]): Waldman expects Pittsburgh to hang its hat on Johnson as the two-down, goal-line and closeout complement to the newly extended [[Jaylen Warren]], making a genuine one-two punch likely. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) acknowledged Johnson's camp looked like a slow start but cautioned against panic — rookies routinely take a minute to get up to speed at the NFL level — and reiterated Waldman's frequent point that Johnson is an ideal scheme fit for this Steelers offense. Neither host treated the slow reports as a reason to move him down.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Going Deep Rookie Receivers and RB Depth Charts)]]): Angelo called Johnson extremely talented but 'kind of a near zero' in the passing game and on third downs, so he loses that work to [[Jaylen Warren]]; his trump card is the low red zone, where his power and ability to get north-south fits what Mike Tomlin's Pittsburgh wants — the role that produced three straight 1,000-yard seasons for [[Najee Harris]] — and Angelo sees a future 1,000-yard rushing season there. The near-term brake is [[Aaron Rodgers]]: Angelo and Waldman both argued a rookie cannot keep up with a quarterback who rewrites plays at the line, with Waldman's extended jazz-gig analogy (most quarterbacks are a scripted pop set; Rodgers is an improvised jazz group) and the pair citing [[Alvin Kamara]] with Drew Brees as the rare exception. Waldman reported Tomlin asked early in the preseason for more decisiveness, then Arthur Smith gushed over his last two preseason games. Both want him onboarded slowly rather than set up to fail.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): real bad news. Johnson played two offensive snaps in the game and zero at halftime, with one carry on which he lost yards, though he did draw a personal foul after being drilled out of bounds. Harris does not think you drop him in a 12-team league, since the rookie's ability has not changed and roles move, but he also does not think you can start him in Week 2 at home against Seattle. The Steelers backfield instead ran through [[Kenneth Gainwell]] and [[Jaylen Warren]].
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): Johnson played two offensive snaps, took one carry, did nothing wrong and never returned to the offense — and then, returning a kick in the fourth quarter, touched the ball, did not chase it, and let it roll into the end zone for a Seattle recovery and the winning margin. Harris nominates him for the 'human brain fart' drop. He notes [[Mike Tomlin]] likes a reclamation project and you never say never, but 'right now in a standard-sized league without a deep bench, feels awfully hard to imagine Kaleb Johnson helping you in the short term after this.' That [[Kenneth Gainwell]] — whom Harris calls a void and a black hole — kept playing 25 snaps is itself a commentary on Johnson.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): 'fuck it' on there being more to come this season — not to the amount people are hoping for. He liked seeing Johnson emerge from the doghouse long enough to catch a pass and take a carry on a series, and expects Pittsburgh to feed him a little more with occasional big plays when the opportunities come, but framed the real payoff as next year [Dynasty].
