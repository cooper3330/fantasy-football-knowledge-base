# Stefon Diggs

> ⚠️ Takes below run through early April 2024 (the trade to Houston). Team/role
> are as of that date and are almost certainly stale — treat as historical
> context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-01 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]]: instant gut call is negative on Diggs as a 2024 wide receiver one, pointing to a stretch of his final roughly ten games of 2023 without a 100-yard performance.
- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode [Redraft (Standard)]: complicates the instant read — distinguishes "divorcing the name from the numbers" (fine for a week-to-week lineup call) from ignoring "the history of numbers" that comes attached to an established name. Argues Diggs still becomes a legitimate 2024 value if his ADP falls out of the first round to the 2nd/3rd-round turn, name-checking Adam Harstad's below-ADP-value framework — but cautions against overstating the decline, given how badly a similarly confident down-call on Gabe Davis backfired the year before ("we drafted him like that all last summer").
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): flags a real role/deployment concern ahead of the Chiefs-Bills Divisional Round game — Buffalo moved Diggs into the slot on 43% of his routes in the Wild Card win over Pittsburgh specifically to get him away from CB Joey Porter Jr., and may do the same against Kansas City's L'Jarius Sneed. The counter is that KC's Trent McDuffie is also a strong nickel corner, so there's no clean matchup for him regardless of alignment. Notes Diggs "didn't have a big game" the last time Buffalo and Kansas City met (Week 14, with Sneed shadowing him from the slot on 27 of his routes).
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]): flags real smoke around a behind-the-scenes issue after Buffalo's Divisional Round loss to Kansas City — "there's got to be more to that story," and admits he expected a public blowup that never materialized ("the fuse is delayed"). No specifics given, but notes [[Khalil Shakir]] is now rated above Diggs "in this moment" as a related signal.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): the fullest version of the injury theory yet — Harmon's explicit lean is an unreported oblique/back/abdominal injury, first flagged on the injury report around Week 10, never fully healed, and likely aggravated again in the Wild Card win over Pittsburgh. Cites the specific production collapse as evidence: 11.3 targets, 8 receptions, 93.5 yards/game and 6 TDs over the first 8 games of the season, cratering to 7.9 targets, 4.6 receptions, 43 yards/game and only 1 TD over the last 8 — and a Divisional Round drop where he clearly mistimed and misplayed a deep ball, "something you do when your upper body's not working in conjunction with your lower body." Notes Diggs also shifted increasingly into the slot as the season wore on (regular-season alignment roughly 29% inside, jumping to ~40% inside in the postseason), speculating it may be a way to get him involved without taxing his body as much. On team building: ruled out as a cut candidate ($32M dead-cap hit is "untenable" for a team already in cap trouble); if the issue is purely an injury (not performance decline), Harmon calls him "a great trade candidate," but the likelier outcome is a restructure with an attempt to reintegrate him into the offense. Buffalo is also flagged as needing to add legitimate outside receiver help regardless of what happens with Diggs, since [[Gabe Davis]] "is not a true number two receiver."
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): a real "feel it or f\*\*k it" answer landing closer to "blip on the radar" than to decline, contrary to how it may have read a month earlier — Waldman is explicit he doesn't buy into cliff narratives generally, and while he acknowledges Diggs turns 30, his read of the down stretch is scheme/role-driven rather than physical: Buffalo "probably got figured out a little bit offensively," with Josh Allen visibly still sorting his pecking order among Diggs, [[Gabe Davis]] and [[Khalil Shakir]] and the two not always "on the same page" as a result. Willing to give him "a mulligan" for the stretch but not willing to pay a 2nd/3rd-round price — expects him to keep falling, projects a landing around the [[Tyler Lockett]] range (5th/6th round), and says he'd take that price if it holds. Frames it as a standard post-down-year market inefficiency: some manager in a recurring league won't want to draft last year's disappointment, and that's exactly where the value shows up.

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): sorted into Buffalo's "critical need" wide receiver bucket. Debate over roster security — one host calls it "an absolute slam dunk" Diggs (now 30, "a bit of a mercurial guy") stays on the roster, since trading him wouldn't create cap relief given the dead-cap hit; puts it at "99.3%" he's on the roster in 2024. Regardless of Diggs's status, flags Buffalo as needing a true outside WR2, with this draft class (loaded with outside-caliber receivers, unlike the prior slot-heavy class) as an ideal fit — floats a rookie who could match [[Khalil Shakir]]'s target volume while also threatening from outside.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]), reacting to Buffalo's [[Curtis Samuel]] signing: reaffirm Diggs as a "great player, elite receiver, A-tier separator" at the top of Buffalo's receiver room, and frame the years of thin depth behind him (converted return men like Isaiah McKenzie and Deonte Harty playing real snaps) as the specific gap Samuel now fills as a genuine second/third receiving option.

- 2024-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-04]]), reacting to Buffalo trading Diggs to Houston for a future 2nd-round pick (Buffalo eating a ~$31M dead-cap hit): Harmon is "baffled" by the trade from a Buffalo team-building standpoint, comparing it unfavorably to Kansas City's QB-over-receivers formula ("this isn't Patrick Mahomes and Andy Reid"), but calls it an outright "slam dunk"/"home run" for Houston. Says his still-in-progress 2023 Reception Perception charting (not yet fully finished at time of taping) shows a real decline, not a cliff — still top-tier short/underneath (46.4% of his charted routes were curls or slants, with strong success rates there), but a clear downfield separation drop, most visible on 9-routes/corners: per Next Gen Stats, his passer rating when targeted 20+ air yards fell from 3rd-best in the NFL in 2022 to 66th of 70 qualifying receivers in 2023 (36.2 rating, 1 TD/3 INT), with EPA/target dropping to 47th of 70. Explicitly rules out an unreported second-half injury as the explanation — Diggs "didn't take the bait" when asked directly after the Super Bowl and attributed it to the coordinator/scheme change — and Harmon says his own charting shows no first-half/second-half split, just opponent-quality variance and some near-miss deep connections with Josh Allen. Projects Houston's plan is a veteran "separation route runner" to complete the room (souped-up Robert Woods-style underneath/flanker role, likely to rotate with [[Tank Dell]] between slot and flanker), not a true X; expects [[Nico Collins]] could still lead the Texans in targets over Diggs given the shift, though says target distribution among Diggs/Collins/Tank Dell "could go any which way." On Buffalo's receiver room post-trade: calls it a legitimate entrant in the "worst receiver rooms in the NFL" conversation even accounting for [[Curtis Samuel]] and [[Khalil Shakir]], and floats Buffalo aggressively trading up in the draft for a receiver (names [[Rome Odunze]], [[Malik Nabers]], [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and [[Adonai Mitchell]] as speculative targets) as a real possibility given the extra 2025 second-rounder from this deal. See [[Josh Allen]].


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: with Diggs now going late-second/early-third round in early best balls, calls him "the biggest gamble" of the Houston WR trio specifically because of the age cliff, while still taking shares — betting that in five years the takeaway will be "remember that little downturn Diggs had," not that he fell off a cliff in 2023. Says heavy best-ball drafters should take the shot on Diggs at that price, framing it as a mitigable risk rather than a must-avoid. Separately reads Buffalo's public framing of the trade as sour grapes — translates their "team professionalism" talking points as Diggs wanting Josh Allen held to a higher standard, and speculates Diggs grew frustrated watching Allen not work as hard as peers like Patrick Mahomes and Joe Burrow. See [[Josh Allen]].
- 2024-05-14 — According to Adam Rank ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-14]]), not a tracked expert: comparing receivers to rappers, Rank likens [[Stefon Diggs]] to Drake — 'still good, just past his time' — and says in a straight receiver-skill matchup, [[DJ Moore]] would now beat a prime-level Diggs. Rank isn't calling Diggs finished (noting he's now on his third team) but views him as on the back half of his career and no longer a clear top-five receiver.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman projects Diggs for 114 targets, 932 yards (~11.0 YPC) and 4 touchdowns in Houston — nearly even in target share with [[Nico Collins]] (116) but well behind him in yardage efficiency. He questioned how Diggs sustains his career-average ~160 targets a year in an offense that must also feed Collins and [[Tank Dell]]. He framed Diggs as a genuine wildcard: could land 13 YPC and ~1,100 yards if his per-target efficiency holds, or fall to the 10-11 YPC/~900-yard range if volume erodes further — calling him good value if his price continues to slide, distinct from a Robert Woods-style falloff since target share should hold.
- 2024-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-11]]) [Dynasty]: Diggs' 2023 RP profile dropped in nearly every category versus 2022 -- 97th to 76th percentile vs. man, 96th to 67th vs. zone, an 18% drop-off on nine routes and 22% on digs -- and Harmon says this decline held constant from week one through the finale, so it was not a late-season fade masked by a hot start. His raw 2023 marks (73.4% vs. man, 80.6% vs. zone, 74.6% vs. press) are still 'great numbers for a normal receiver' but well off his own historic best-in-RP-history peak, when he tracked with [[Davante Adams]]-level 78%+ marks in 2022. The specific collapse was on deep routes (nine, corner, dig and post all below average) while short/intermediate routes (slant, curl, flat) stayed elite, which is why Harmon projects him as [[C.J. Stroud]]'s 'third or fourth-down guy' in 2024 rather than the primary vertical threat. He says forecasting WR age cliffs is the thing he's least confident using RP data for -- some receivers plateau usefully (DeAndre Hopkins, Keenan Allen) while others fall off completely (Allen Robinson) or cliff in a single final season (Dez Bryant) -- but leans toward Diggs aging like the former group given his elite technique and work ethic. He does not think Houston needs 'old Diggs' given [[Nico Collins]] and [[Tank Dell]]'s emergence, and expects Diggs to be a secondary piece whose presence draws extra coverage and raises Collins' target quality.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Diggs' 2023 RP profile declined from his peak but was still at a very good level, with the biggest fall coming on deep routes, while he remained successful on short and quick routes — which raises whether his future is as a slot player, something he has not done full time since 2016. Harmon's projection is Diggs as Houston's third-down, backside X running shorter ISO routes with the freedom to just get open, citing [[Josh Allen]] telling him Diggs would take a seven-yard route at six or eight; he wonders how [[C.J. Stroud]] handles that latitude. Koh (not tracked) disagrees on feasibility, arguing Diggs' personality makes a 70% slot role untenable and that after a few games of low-aDOT throws he will need vertical shots; Harmon counters that deep routes from the slot might actually help him. Diggs played 70% outside and 30% slot in 2023.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-12]]): Harmon 'felt great' about Diggs' Week 1 role — 63.6% of routes from the slot, quick-hitting patterns, three first downs on his targets and two touchdowns already. He pushes back hard on analysts dunking on his 1.3 average depth of target, pointing out Diggs had three targets inside the 10-yard line where there are no air yards to get, and that 17 of his 31 routes travelled more than 10 yards downfield. He concedes Diggs has lost a step as a vertical separator, something they discussed all offseason, but says his Reception Perception route success chart from last year was still extremely strong on slants, curls and quick outs, and that is a fine base when [[Nico Collins]] and [[Tank Dell]] can hit downfield. Harmon calls him a threat for 10-plus touchdowns. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) agrees the targeted-air-yards framing is misleading and says the film did not look like a Danny Amendola line-of-scrimmage role.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-10]]): Diggs has been Houston's primary slot receiver and led the team in routes run in Week 5. With [[Nico Collins]] on IR, Harmon floated Diggs kicking back to the perimeter — he has a history there — so [[Xavier Hutchinson]] could work from the big slot, but with the caveat that Diggs 'is not that vertical receiver either', neither the vertical separator Collins is nor the one Diggs himself was in his prime. Harmon still called him a really, really good receiver. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) added that Diggs has never been a good blocking receiver — Koh says he has discussed it with Diggs directly and got a 'sometimes you just go through the motions' answer — which is a further argument for keeping him away from the run-play side.
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]): asked whether Diggs is the same player in Houston as in Buffalo — he caught six or seven with a touchdown in a run-heavy game where [[Joe Mixon]] returned — Waldman says he is still a very good player but is open to the idea that something has changed. His reason: most of Diggs's wins have come on matchup-based, pre-snap advantageous alignments where the route was designed to beat the defender's leverage. What Waldman has not seen is Diggs in the [[Nico Collins]] role, simply beating his man heads-up without a schemed leverage advantage, and he wants to see that before declaring nothing has been lost. He adds the Texans are too smart to put him in that spot often, so the evidence may not come.
- 2024-10-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-15]]): with [[Nico Collins]] out, Diggs is Houston's best receiver right now — Harmon is explicit that this should not be controversial given [[Tank Dell]]'s film. Diggs turns 31 in November and no longer affects the game vertically, so he has extended his prime the way aging perimeter receivers must, kicking inside for layup routes, the role Houston had originally targeted [[Keenan Allen]] for. The Week 6 alignment data surprised the hosts: every Texans receiver — Diggs, Dell, [[Xavier Hutchinson]] and [[John Metchie III]] — ran 70%+ of routes from outside, with nobody above 28% in the slot. Even so, Diggs beat press man coverage at an above-70th-percentile Reception Perception rate, getting in and out of breaks cleanly and taking a good young corner in Christian Gonzalez to the woodshed. Harmon expects the target volume to continue while Collins is out.
- 2025-01-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-21]]): Harmon does not think Diggs is 'that guy' anymore, but suspects he may still return to Houston because at 31 coming off an ACL tear he will not command a big deal and has organizational familiarity and a good relationship with [[C.J. Stroud]] — and even if he is back, Houston needs more pass catchers beyond him. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) disagrees and thinks Houston moves on: Diggs still carries the 'I'm still the guy' mentality that made him great when he bet on himself over [[Adam Thielen]] in Minnesota, and at his age and off the injury that becomes more trouble than it is worth to manage.
- 2025-02-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-04]]): Diggs is expected to move on from Houston and his void contract will give the Texans cap relief. Evaluating him as a free agent, Harmon questions whether he is still a perimeter receiver — he played slot for Houston in 2024, though Harmon suspects that was a function of the other players on the roster and that Diggs probably could still play outside. The negatives he lists are stacked: coming off an injury, an older player, and no speed element at all, which is specifically what a Chip Kelly Raiders offense would need. He allows Houston could bring him back, but not as the answer.
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-13]]): a Dallas signing of [[Stefon Diggs]] is a real one to watch — Harmon says he knows for a fact Diggs and his brother Trevon have fantasized about playing together and this may be the one chance it happens, with neither of their stars shining as bright as at their individual peaks and Diggs coming off injury. He fits Harmon's stated Cowboys need for a low-cost veteran with inside-outside versatility to play next to [[CeeDee Lamb]], though Harmon stresses it does not have to be Diggs specifically. Elsewhere Harmon lists Diggs among the veteran free agents — with [[Davante Adams]], [[DeAndre Hopkins]], [[Amari Cooper]], [[Keenan Allen]], [[Chris Godwin]] and [[Cooper Kupp]] — none of whom are coming off peak seasons, which he thinks strengthens [[Terry McLaurin]]'s market.
- 2025-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-07]]): working through Diggs's 2024 Reception Perception profile, Harmon thinks he 'largely gave them what they were looking for' and played well for Houston before the injury — and that he might now be better on the outside than in the slot Houston used him in, because he remains more of a man-coverage beater at this stage. Harmon calls him 'an alpha' in mentality even if he is no longer a skill-set alpha, meaning he wants an alpha's target share. With the room thin, a return makes some sense, but Harmon is 'not sure that he's a lock' given the injury and a new offensive system. Koh is more cautious: 31 years old, an ACL tear suffered in Week 8, a PUP candidate to open the year, and a hard player to rely on for a team with deep-playoff expectations.
- 2025-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-21]]): after charting Diggs's 2024 Houston sample for a new Reception Perception profile, Harmon says the decline is real but is a decline from one of the best performers of the RP era — Diggs still owns the highest success rate vs man Harmon has ever charted (82.6% in 2020) and posted 71.6% vs man (67th percentile) and 78.8% vs zone in 2024, with a 76th percentile mark vs man press. The rot is downfield and over the middle: nine-route success rate fell from north of 60% every year 2016-2022 to 44% in 2023 and 47.1% in 2024, and the dig route fell from 90.9% in 2022 to 68%/70%. All out-breaking routes (corner, out, comeback, flat) graded above league average, so Harmon wants him as a flanker-slot hybrid around 60/40 out-breaking specialist rather than a full-time slot, noting Houston moved him inside only because [[Nico Collins]] is a pure X and [[Tank Dell]] is too small to block. Big caveat: a non-contact ACL tear in Week 8 at age 31 (32 soon), possible PUP, and the fact he is still unsigned and visiting a 4th-pick team signals teams fear the medicals — Harmon expects a one-year, incentive-laden 'kick the tires' deal. James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) compares the vibe to [[Odell Beckham Jr.]] post-ACL and says he would not count on Diggs weeks 1-5, only maybe week 8 onward, while arguing Dallas should pursue him harder for inside-outside versatility; Harmon counters that the Cowboys will not pay perimeter receivers and that brother Trevon Diggs may himself be a cut candidate.
- 2025-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-28]]): Diggs' three-year New England deal worth up to 69M (26M guaranteed, escapable after one year) is good value for a team that entered the offseason with ~120M in cap room, and Harmon says he is broadly 'post contract' — the structures are fake and teams exit after two years. On the player, he pushes back hard on the slot-only rhetoric: the Diggs he last saw in Houston posted a 71.6% success rate versus man and 71.4% versus press, both 67th percentile, and he only moved inside because Nico Collins is a pure X and Tank Dell is too small for the slot, not out of decline. Harmon projects him as New England's flanker who can also play slot, and the only man-beater on the roster. Caveats: 31 years old off a Week 8 torn ACL, recovery reportedly ahead of schedule but possibly a 50% snap player through Weeks 1-2 and not fully ready until Week 5-6, and Harmon notes injuries accelerate the aging-receiver decline. He also defends Diggs' leadership reputation, calling him beloved in Houston and a fix for an immature Patriots receiver room.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): Diggs is a steal at his WR45 price. He is a good route runner who will do good work whether New England uses him in the slot or at flanker, and Waldman noted there is nobody else on the roster who runs routes or catches the ball as well. The stated risk is squarely the injury and age question: he looks healthy in OTAs and teammates say he looks fantastic, but staying that healthy across a full season as an older player coming off the injury is the bet — one Waldman would take at that cost. Later in the episode he added the historical note that [[Adam Thielen]] was instrumental in teaching Diggs to refine his route running in Minnesota.
- 2025-06-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-02]]): split verdict — 'fuck it' on the off-field noise, 'feel it' on the ACL. On the viral boat video and pink powder, Waldman argues most NFL teams have players doing as much or worse who still play well; coaches know and simply do not want it in the tabloids. He notes the odd bifurcation in Diggs' reputation, from Minnesota teammates' criticism to being a leader on and off the field in Houston. The real problem is medical: Diggs tore the ACL in midseason (October), so Waldman expects it to take a full year to be right and is 'not really trusting him as a starter.' His pre-news projection was 12 games, nearly 90 targets, 60 catches, almost 650 yards and five touchdowns — not starter value, though he allows the ceiling could still be there in a vacuum where the knee is fine. Practically, he says he probably will not be drafting Diggs at all, and suggests waiting to pick him off waivers after a slow start for possible bye-week value. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) finds the WR45 ADP irresistible and notes Diggs did report for the final voluntary OTA.
- 2025-06-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-04]]): Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) discuss the viral Memorial Day boat video and local radio host Scott Zolak floating that a release is a 'real possibility', against Ian Rapoport reporting the Patriots have no plans to cut him. Koh notes the three-year, roughly 63.5 million dollar deal is effectively a one-year deal and that Diggs must pass a physical before his 2025 salary fully guarantees, so he would be surprised but not shocked by a release. Harmon expects Diggs to stay and to have a nice season: he was playing well before the torn ACL, is reportedly tracking well in recovery, and nobody questions his work ethic. Harmon values him as a veteran presence in an immature receiver room, already seen mentoring [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] in OTAs and giving [[Drake Maye]] someone dependable, while cautioning he will never be a superstar receiver again.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]): the Albert Breer report that Diggs is likely ready for Week 1, and his absence from the PUP list, helps his value 'to the public' but should not change a savvy drafter's price — Waldman hopes he falls. His stated reason is the durability question specific to long rehabs: being fit enough to run fast in a straight line now is different from having healed enough to take weekly punishment and regain burst and speed each week, which is why players so often say a year and a half later that they were not truly ready. He half-jokes that Diggs may be so visible in camp because of the boat video and worry about how Mike Vrabel sees him. Bob Harris (not tracked) disagrees on price, noting Diggs was WR41 and that a player who was never worse than a fantasy WR2 from 2017-2023 is a good bet even assuming a month missed; he cites Phil Alexander's workout video showing Diggs cutting well and positive OTA reports, and Football Guys' WR24 points-per-game projection. Waldman's comp for a realistic bounce-back is Jordy Nelson's 2012 — 49 catches, 745 yards, 7 TDs in 12 games — though he doubts Diggs matches the 15.2 yards per catch. On whether Diggs becomes [[Drake Maye]]'s favorite target: 'fuck it' — he sees Diggs as the number two or three, with Demario Douglas possibly leading by target volume and Kyle Williams a surprise candidate if he stops fighting the football.
- 2025-07-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-28]]): 'fuck it' on Diggs as AFC Comeback Player of the Year, mostly because he already gave that answer to [[Nick Chubb]] — he grants Diggs is a legitimate candidate. Waldman relays [[Kendrick Bourne]]'s comments that both had ACL tears, that it took Bourne a full year on the field post-procedure to feel normal, and that Diggs already looks back to normal, with Bourne invoking Adrian Peterson's famously fast return. Waldman stays skeptical for now while allowing he may have to change his mind: given the running and pounding involved, he would rather bet on a running back two years removed from the injury than a receiver less than a year removed.
- 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]): Harmon is 'so impressed' by Diggs' offseason coming off injury and expects him to open week one as a full-time player, one of the two Patriots most likely to see real volume alongside [[Demario Douglas]]. He grants that returning at this level would make Diggs an outlier, then argues Diggs already is one — a fifth-round pick who became one of the best route runners and technicians in the league, and a grinder at the position whatever the off-field noise. Harmon's 2024 charting had Diggs at a 67th percentile success rate versus man and 71.4 percent versus press, so he can still get open, though he does not get down the field as much and projects as a flanker. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is more cautious: an older player coming off injury is not a set-it-and-forget-it piece.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Dr. Deepak Chona of SportsMedAnalytics (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]] (ASR: 'Stefan Digs'): Diggs tore his ACL in late October last year and is now about 10 months out, right at the average full-return timeline, and is trending toward playing week one without limitation. Chona's model still expects only about 85 percent of pre-injury production initially, with the usual four-game ramp-up in target volume and consistency for these clean ACLs, meaning variance settles after four games — so he has a lot of faith in Diggs from week five onward and expects mid-90s percent of pre-injury level around mid-season. On the profile: age works against him, but he had not shown signs of slowing, has six straight thousand-yard seasons, and had not missed many games recently; combined with a clean non-contact ACL and ample recovery time, Chona is optimistic about this one.
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]): Waldman endorses Tom Curran's (NBC Sports) view that Diggs is heading for roughly four-to-six targets a game rather than the eight-to-ten a player of his reputation would command, and that [[Kayshon Boutte]] could outproduce him. His reasoning is trajectory rather than talent denial — Diggs is at the stage of his career where the arrow points down while Boutte's still points up. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) added that beat writer Tom King of the Nashua Telegram has Diggs and Boutte neck-and-neck for target share behind [[Hunter Henry]], with [[Demario Douglas]] also in the mix, which corroborates the demotion in expected volume.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris ranked Diggs WR42 for Week 1 to guest Andy Behrens's WR35, and says people are 'whistling past the graveyard' on a 33-year-old coming off a late-October torn ACL. Harris's read is that a couple of good reps in a joint practice where nobody was getting hit convinced people he is totally back; he expects another shoe to drop in September — swelling, a big hit — and notes the count of midseason-ACL receivers who came back and were fine is 'like 10 or something,' i.e. very low. He did not mind the signing as a Patriots fan but is skeptical Diggs is the fix for the offense, having already seen [[Kyle Williams]] floated as the answer. Both hosts agree zero Patriots receivers in a starting lineup is a fine answer; Behrens said he almost put [[Demario Douglas]] ahead of Diggs and only leaned Diggs because he tilts toward the player with the better best case. Harris framed it as a difference of degree, not a disagreement.
