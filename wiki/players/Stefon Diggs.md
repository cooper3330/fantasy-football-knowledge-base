# Stefon Diggs

> ⚠️ Takes below run through early April 2024 (the trade to Houston). Team/role
> are as of that date and are almost certainly stale — treat as historical
> context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-01 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]]: instant gut call is negative on Diggs as a 2024 wide receiver one, pointing to a stretch of his final roughly ten games of 2023 without a 100-yard performance.
- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode [Redraft (Standard)]: complicates the instant read — distinguishes "divorcing the name from the numbers" (fine for a week-to-week lineup call) from ignoring "the history of numbers" that comes attached to an established name. Argues Diggs still becomes a legitimate 2024 value if his ADP falls out of the first round to the 2nd/3rd-round turn, name-checking Adam Harstad's below-ADP-value framework — but cautions against overstating the decline, given how badly a similarly confident down-call on Gabe Davis backfired the year before ("we drafted him like that all last summer").
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): flags a real role/deployment concern ahead of the Chiefs-Bills Divisional Round game — Buffalo moved Diggs into the slot on 43% of his routes in the Wild Card win over Pittsburgh specifically to get him away from CB Joey Porter Jr., and may do the same against Kansas City's L'Jarius Sneed. The counter is that KC's Trent McDuffie is also a strong nickel corner, so there's no clean matchup for him regardless of alignment. Notes Diggs "didn't have a big game" the last time Buffalo and Kansas City met (Week 14, with Sneed shadowing him from the slot on 27 of his routes).
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]): flags real smoke around a behind-the-scenes issue after Buffalo's Divisional Round loss to Kansas City — "there's got to be more to that story," and admits he expected a public blowup that never materialized ("the fuse is delayed"). No specifics given, but notes [[Khalil Shakir]] is now rated above Diggs "in this moment" as a related signal.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): the fullest version of the injury theory yet — Harmon's explicit lean is an unreported oblique/back/abdominal injury, first flagged on the injury report around Week 10, never fully healed, and likely aggravated again in the Wild Card win over Pittsburgh. Cites the specific production collapse as evidence: 11.3 targets, 8 receptions, 93.5 yards/game and 6 TDs over the first 8 games of the season, cratering to 7.9 targets, 4.6 receptions, 43 yards/game and only 1 TD over the last 8 — and a Divisional Round drop where he clearly mistimed and misplayed a deep ball, "something you do when your upper body's not working in conjunction with your lower body." Notes Diggs also shifted increasingly into the slot as the season wore on (regular-season alignment roughly 29% inside, jumping to ~40% inside in the postseason), speculating it may be a way to get him involved without taxing his body as much. On team building: ruled out as a cut candidate ($32M dead-cap hit is "untenable" for a team already in cap trouble); if the issue is purely an injury (not performance decline), Harmon calls him "a great trade candidate," but the likelier outcome is a restructure with an attempt to reintegrate him into the offense. Buffalo is also flagged as needing to add legitimate outside receiver help regardless of what happens with Diggs, since [[Gabe Davis]] "is not a true number two receiver."
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): a real "feel it or f\*\*k it" answer landing closer to "blip on the radar" than to decline, contrary to how it may have read a month earlier — Waldman is explicit he doesn't buy into cliff narratives generally, and while he acknowledges Diggs turns 30, his read of the down stretch is scheme/role-driven rather than physical: Buffalo "probably got figured out a little bit offensively," with Josh Allen visibly still sorting his pecking order among Diggs, [[Gabe Davis]] and [[Khalil Shakir]] and the two not always "on the same page" as a result. Willing to give him "a mulligan" for the stretch but not willing to pay a 2nd/3rd-round price — expects him to keep falling, projects a landing around the [[Tyler Lockett]] range (5th/6th round), and says he'd take that price if it holds. Frames it as a standard post-down-year market inefficiency: some manager in a recurring league won't want to draft last year's disappointment, and that's exactly where the value shows up.

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): sorted into Buffalo's "critical need" wide receiver bucket. Debate over roster security — one host calls it "an absolute slam dunk" Diggs (now 30, "a bit of a mercurial guy") stays on the roster, since trading him wouldn't create cap relief given the dead-cap hit; puts it at "99.3%" he's on the roster in 2024. Regardless of Diggs's status, flags Buffalo as needing a true outside WR2, with this draft class (loaded with outside-caliber receivers, unlike the prior slot-heavy class) as an ideal fit — floats a rookie who could match [[Khalil Shakir]]'s target volume while also threatening from outside.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]), reacting to Buffalo's [[Curtis Samuel]] signing: reaffirm Diggs as a "great player, elite receiver, A-tier separator" at the top of Buffalo's receiver room, and frame the years of thin depth behind him (converted return men like Isaiah McKenzie and Deonte Harty playing real snaps) as the specific gap Samuel now fills as a genuine second/third receiving option.

- 2024-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-04]]), reacting to Buffalo trading Diggs to Houston for a future 2nd-round pick (Buffalo eating a ~$31M dead-cap hit): Harmon is "baffled" by the trade from a Buffalo team-building standpoint, comparing it unfavorably to Kansas City's QB-over-receivers formula ("this isn't Patrick Mahomes and Andy Reid"), but calls it an outright "slam dunk"/"home run" for Houston. Says his still-in-progress 2023 Reception Perception charting (not yet fully finished at time of taping) shows a real decline, not a cliff — still top-tier short/underneath (46.4% of his charted routes were curls or slants, with strong success rates there), but a clear downfield separation drop, most visible on 9-routes/corners: per Next Gen Stats, his passer rating when targeted 20+ air yards fell from 3rd-best in the NFL in 2022 to 66th of 70 qualifying receivers in 2023 (36.2 rating, 1 TD/3 INT), with EPA/target dropping to 47th of 70. Explicitly rules out an unreported second-half injury as the explanation — Diggs "didn't take the bait" when asked directly after the Super Bowl and attributed it to the coordinator/scheme change — and Harmon says his own charting shows no first-half/second-half split, just opponent-quality variance and some near-miss deep connections with Josh Allen. Projects Houston's plan is a veteran "separation route runner" to complete the room (souped-up Robert Woods-style underneath/flanker role, likely to rotate with [[Tank Dell]] between slot and flanker), not a true X; expects [[Nico Collins]] could still lead the Texans in targets over Diggs given the shift, though says target distribution among Diggs/Collins/Tank Dell "could go any which way." On Buffalo's receiver room post-trade: calls it a legitimate entrant in the "worst receiver rooms in the NFL" conversation even accounting for [[Curtis Samuel]] and [[Khalil Shakir]], and floats Buffalo aggressively trading up in the draft for a receiver (names [[Rome Odunze]], [[Malik Nabers]], [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and [[Adonai Mitchell]] as speculative targets) as a real possibility given the extra 2025 second-rounder from this deal. See [[Josh Allen]].


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: with Diggs now going late-second/early-third round in early best balls, calls him "the biggest gamble" of the Houston WR trio specifically because of the age cliff, while still taking shares — betting that in five years the takeaway will be "remember that little downturn Diggs had," not that he fell off a cliff in 2023. Says heavy best-ball drafters should take the shot on Diggs at that price, framing it as a mitigable risk rather than a must-avoid. Separately reads Buffalo's public framing of the trade as sour grapes — translates their "team professionalism" talking points as Diggs wanting Josh Allen held to a higher standard, and speculates Diggs grew frustrated watching Allen not work as hard as peers like Patrick Mahomes and Joe Burrow. See [[Josh Allen]].
- 2024-05-14 — According to Adam Rank ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-14]]), not a tracked expert: comparing receivers to rappers, Rank likens [[Stefon Diggs]] to Drake — 'still good, just past his time' — and says in a straight receiver-skill matchup, [[DJ Moore]] would now beat a prime-level Diggs. Rank isn't calling Diggs finished (noting he's now on his third team) but views him as on the back half of his career and no longer a clear top-five receiver.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman projects Diggs for 114 targets, 932 yards (~11.0 YPC) and 4 touchdowns in Houston — nearly even in target share with [[Nico Collins]] (116) but well behind him in yardage efficiency. He questioned how Diggs sustains his career-average ~160 targets a year in an offense that must also feed Collins and [[Tank Dell]]. He framed Diggs as a genuine wildcard: could land 13 YPC and ~1,100 yards if his per-target efficiency holds, or fall to the 10-11 YPC/~900-yard range if volume erodes further — calling him good value if his price continues to slide, distinct from a Robert Woods-style falloff since target share should hold.
