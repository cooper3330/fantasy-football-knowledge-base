# Rico Dowdle

## Expert Takes

- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: on whether drafting a Cowboys back is a waste of time, Waldman said Dowdle's cost has been low enough that you can give him a shot. Dowdle is 'the guy that they want things to work out with,' and Waldman believes he has the talent to surprise. The caveat is that both Dowdle and [[Ezekiel Elliott]] are expendable — if Dowdle does not work out, Dallas can fall back on an Elliott-plus-[[Dalvin Cook]] combination. Waldman was more confident about the Dallas passing game than the backfield.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): Dowdle still looks pretty good to Waldman on tape, but the situation caps him — the Dallas offensive line does not look strong and the defense has real questions. Bob Harris (untracked) cited JJ Zachariason's data showing Dowdle's rush share climbing from 42 percent in Week 1 to 62 percent in Week 2, with 22 more snaps than [[Ezekiel Elliott]] in Week 3, possibly game-script related. Waldman's verdict on Dowdle being the better play: 'he's the better play of bad plays,' and he still expects Elliott to take the goal-line work either way. Separately, both agreed the Eagles and Cowboys defenses are weekly matchups to target with a strong ground game — both are playing nickel base and five-man fronts and still cannot stop the run.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): Dowdle is one of the few backs he expects to grow rather than regress. He is averaging 4.1 yards per carry and ranks 27th in PPR, and Waldman thinks the market is down on him mainly because the profile is unexciting — but he is emerging as the back who will take the most touches in that backfield and has become the more reliable option in the short term. Waldman would take a cheap bet on him and lean on him going forward. He explicitly prefers Dowdle to [[Tank Bigsby]], who ranks 37th but is the hotter name, because Bigsby's eight yards per carry is fool's gold built on a handful of long runs while Dowdle's production reflects a stabilizing role.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]): after becoming the first undrafted player with three straight 100-plus-yard rushing games since Arian Foster in 2014, Dowdle has a chance to open 2025 as Dallas's starter — he will probably head into camp as the starter — but the incoming running back class is rich and he will get competition. Waldman expects fantasy analysts to spend the offseason touting whichever back Dallas drafts in rounds one through four, and frames the open question as whether it becomes a [[Kyren Williams]]/[[Blake Corum]]-style situation where the incumbent holds, or a [[Travis Etienne Jr.]]/[[Tank Bigsby]]-style committee. He has long compared Dowdle to Arian Foster and preached patience — 'I didn't think we'd have to be this patient' — and says Dowdle should be one of Dallas's top two backs next year if he stays on the roster. Bob Harris (not tracked) noted Dallas kept him through the injuries, which says they knew what they had.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): watching Carolina's 30-0 win with his own eyes rather than the box score, Harris concluded Dowdle 'is kind of who got the offense going' in the second quarter, and that in the fourth 'I don't think there's any arguing that the Panthers felt like Dowdle had a hot hand and they rode him' — a bunch of consecutive carries down to the five with the game not fully salted, and then, after [[Chuba Hubbard]] came in and caught one down to the one, it was Dowdle who came right back in to score the clinching touchdown. Harris is not declaring a change but calls it an uh-oh moment for Hubbard backers.
- 2025-10-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-03]]): with [[Chuba Hubbard]] ruled out for Week 5 with a calf injury, Dowdle moves 'now higher in the ranks.' Harris notes Dowdle had already been taking a suspiciously bigger part of the Panthers' workload over the previous couple of weeks, which in hindsight reads as Hubbard being managed. [[Trevor Etienne]] is placed in the emergency portion of the ranks behind him.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-06]]): Waldman will half-grant that Dowdle might be better than [[Chuba Hubbard]], but not by enough to take the job away once Hubbard is healthy. He rates Dowdle the more creative runner between the tackles and in traffic, but notes some of the 50-plus-yard creases Dowdle hit would have been house calls for Hubbard, who has more juice and is better in the open field. In college he would have said Dowdle was the better runner hands down; as pros it is too close to call and he leans Hubbard. Bob Harris (not tracked) highlighted Dowdle describing himself post-game as a violent runner — the same word Mike Kaye of the Charlotte Observer and head coach Dave Canales have used about Hubbard — as evidence both backs understand the job requirement. Both expect a more even split going forward with Dowdle having earned more work.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: over 200 rushing yards in [[Chuba Hubbard]]'s absence and, in Harris' words, if he were fast he'd have housed two of them — he was run down from behind by Minkah Fitzpatrick on a third-quarter pitch and again on a fourth-quarter lane up the middle. Harris splits the credit: big credit to the Carolina offensive line's right side, some of it was Miami simply not stopping a run-right call, but he did break tackles and was getting yards before contact. He cramped off in the fourth ([[Trevor Etienne]] handled the winning drive) and returned to run out the clock. On waivers, Dowdle at 59% rostered is the name to chase in a shallow league but Funston would temper the bid — 'splitting half of a Carolina backfield at the most... that's not what I'm emptying my budget on' — while Harris leans toward paying up because one win is worth something and every Hubbard manager should be handcuffing.
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-13]]): asked whether Dowdle is good or the defenses he faced are bad, Waldman answered both are true. He is a long-time Dowdle proponent and stands by the traits: great burst, excellent balance, creative, runs with power and contact balance, and catches the football. He cited Greg Olsen's (not tracked) broadcast point that some backs get stronger as a game goes along and a committee never reveals which back that is, but pushed back on applying it to Dowdle, who by halftime already has a full game's worth of touches and is cramping by late in the game; at South Carolina he could not stay healthy enough to earn late-game volume. The caveat is opponent quality: the Cowboys are not a good defense and neither are the Dolphins. Bob Harris (not tracked) noted the 239 scrimmage yards were the most ever by a player against his former team, and that Carolina keeps winning these Dallas exchanges.
- 2025-10-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-14 (Week 7 Waiver Wire & More W6 Film)]]): Dowdle put up 239 scrimmage yards plus a receiving TD in a revenge game against his former team Dallas -- 30 carries, 34 touches, with Harris noting the amount of clean running room before contact was 'utterly ridiculous.' Harris says he's a good, solid power back and that opposing running backs vs. the Iberflus-led Dallas defense should keep being targeted, but flags the real question is playing-time ramifications once [[Chuba Hubbard]]'s calf heals -- Dowdle's last two huge weeks came against two of the friendliest run defenses (Miami, Dallas), and the next two opponents (Jets, Bills) are tougher.
- 2025-10-16 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Dowdle has been an 'awesome, bright spot' for Carolina, and Angelo compared his running style to a mix of [[Joe Mixon]] and LeVeon Bell -- a creative gap/zone runner who 'creates lemonade out of lemons.' He noted Dallas kept Dowdle as an undrafted free agent through injuries for four straight years rather than cutting him, which Angelo reads as a real talent signal, and pointed out Dowdle topped 1,000 yards last year behind a worse Cowboys line than this year's improved unit. At 28, Angelo doesn't expect a long-term lead role but thinks Dowdle could land a nice one-year payday as a bridge starter elsewhere. Both hosts agreed Dowdle is currently the better back of Carolina's committee, ahead of [[Chuba Hubbard]] at this stage of Hubbard's career, though Hubbard's return to full health should still take load off Dowdle rather than create a full timeshare.
- 2025-10-17 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and co-host Josh (not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-17]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: With [[Chuba Hubbard]] back at limited practice after missing two games with a calf injury, Harris moved Hubbard back into his ranks but is keeping Dowdle ranked higher for this week specifically, reasoning Carolina can afford to bring Hubbard along slowly -- explicitly flagged as speculation, 'pure category two,' until actual usage is seen. Josh grouped the Panthers among his surprise-supportive offenses, noting Carolina 'has been pretty decent the last couple weeks' even before this backfield shakeup, with [[Tetairoa McMillan]] and returning [[Jalen Coker]] also developing as pass-game pieces.
- 2025-10-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-21 (Week 8 Waivers & W7 Reviews)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Dowdle got the second series and finished with more scrimmage yards than [[Chuba Hubbard]] (96 to 55), starting to 'cook' as Carolina tried to pull away late, though he was stuffed on a fourth-and-one screen earlier. Harris said the two backs currently belong side by side in the rankings given the true series-based split, with volume at risk of shrinking if Carolina has to throw more behind [[Andy Dalton]].
- 2025-10-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-28]]): Dowdle played fewer snaps than [[Chuba Hubbard]] (20 vs. 35) and returned the opening kickoff; Harris disputes the narrative that Dowdle has clearly separated as the better back, calling it a product of a couple explosive plays rather than sustained superiority, and expects the timeshare with Hubbard to continue.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-04]]): after re-watching Carolina's upset win in Green Bay as a 13.5-point road underdog, Dowdle was definitively the man and the Panthers were 100% telling the truth with their pre-game leak that he would take over. Harris admits he was wrong not to separate Dowdle from [[Chuba Hubbard]] in his ranks, though he defends the process — his standing line in the yacht-club chat was that it is usage and it is really hard to know in advance. The complication on film: Dowdle was in and out with a groin injury, visibly grabbing at himself after runs and ceding portions of series, which is the only reason Hubbard saw the work he did. Dowdle scored his first touchdown of the year after Hubbard carried to the four, broke a mid-second-quarter weak-side pivot pitch around the right edge, and iced the game with the draw that set up the winning field goal. Harris says the way we look at Carolina's backfield has to adjust accordingly.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): the Saints defense 'absolutely sold out' to stop Dowdle specifically, run-blitzing to flood the middle and playing man outside to dare [[Bryce Young]] to beat them. It failed on the opening drive — 15 plays, seven minutes, seven Dowdle touches, ending in his touchdown, Carolina's only points all day — and worked thereafter. Harris's evaluation is blunt: 'it turns out he's not, like, magical. He's a power back who has okay speed,' and he cannot do much on the edges. Dowdle finished with 21 touches but only 39 yards after that first drive. Harris will not make a fantasy change until he sees a real-life change: 'it's Dowdle, it's [[Tetairoa McMillan]], we're not getting cute.'
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-13 (Paper Champions and All-Timers Across Eras)]]) [Dynasty]: Dowdle is the case study in how little paper value predicted the Carolina backfield. Untracked co-host Adam Harstad raised him as the counterexample to [[Jonathan Brooks]] — two years ago every dynasty manager would have called a Panthers back a championship anchor, and they were right about everything except the name. Waldman notes Dowdle needed four years and an injury to [[Chuba Hubbard]] before the opportunity arrived, and did not reach his 100th career touch until September of his fourth season, despite having posted roughly 1,300 total yards behind what Waldman calls a crappy Dallas offensive line. Waldman also credits the volume itself: heavy touch counts let a back read alignments and play the game within the game, which he thinks flatters Dowdle relative to backs fed three carries a quarter (see [[Touch Volume and In-Game Adaptation]]).
- 2025-11-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-14]]) [Redraft], his #1 trade-away this deadline special: Harris ranks Dowdle 41st overall on his own board versus FantasyPros' 23rd and wants to flip that gap into a real upgrade -- proposing Dowdle plus George Pickens (whose role has cooled 'since Lamb came back') for James Cook, calling Cook 'a receiver you love' with 'the potential to be a monster' once people forget his rough last week; he'd also accept Javonte Williams back but prefers Cook. Harris says he's 'half a step skeptical about Dowdle lasting for the rest of the season, not health-wise, but production-wise,' and separately admits he was wrong to be down on teammate Chuba Hubbard after a bad Wednesday-show take, calling Dowdle 'found money' sitting in a contender's couch cushions worth cashing in before a game where he 'looked human' again against the Saints.
- 2025-11-24 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris said the Panthers overall are a team he's 'not heavily invested in,' but he's 'starting Rico Dowdle every week at the moment' alongside Tetairoa McMillan as the two must-starts on the roster. He noted feeling 'a little shakier' about Dowdle lately, possibly tied to a leg injury, and said he isn't chasing Dowdle's yardage prop for Monday night even though Dowdle remains locked into his season-long lineups.
- 2025-11-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: has 'cooled way down' after the Week 11 explosion, with the Panthers backfield snap count 28 Dowdle to 15 Hubbard on Monday night against San Francisco. On what looked like a big screen gain, Harris notes Dowdle hesitated instead of accelerating past a pursuing defender ('imagine if Rico Dowdle was fast') and settled for 12 yards instead of more. Harris says he'll probably still start Dowdle 'for another week' but 'won't be shocked' if that changes soon, and wouldn't be surprised to see the touches split more evenly with Hubbard going forward if the offense keeps sputtering.
- 2025-12-01 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-01]]): Harris said Rico Dowdle 'didn't really do anything wrong' against the Rams -- he got stuffed early but put together an okay yardage day -- he simply didn't get the well-blocked plays that [[Chuba Hubbard]] found in the second half, finishing with 27 snaps to Hubbard's 36. Harris said this flips what he'd been saying about the pair back in October, and that watching this game 'takes some shine off the idea of Dowdle as a locked-in, no matter what, fantasy playoff contributor' -- he now expects the Panthers backfield to be treated as an even timeshare again, making both backs 'annoying flexes' into their Week 14 bye.
- 2025-12-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-16]]): [[Rico Dowdle]] was clearly the lead back against New Orleans, out-snapping [[Chuba Hubbard]] 33-24 and scoring from in close on Carolina's best drive of the day. Harris said after the bye, Carolina appears to have gone back to Dowdle as the main choice, with Hubbard around to steal looks -- floating that Hubbard's Week 13 workload may have reflected Dowdle playing hurt rather than a real role change.
- 2025-12-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-23]]): per the broadcast production meeting, the Panthers' plan was for Dowdle to get early downs on the first series and [[Chuba Hubbard]] third downs, then flip on the second drive. Dowdle ended up with 30 snaps to Hubbard's 22 -- 'kind of a split job right now with a slight lean to Dowdle' -- but Harris cautions neither did much of anything statistically, so 'I don't think you want to go crazy with him.'
- 2026-01-13 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-13]]): probably won't be back with Carolina next season after frustrated postgame comments about his lack of usage; Harmon wasn't sure Dowdle finds a starting job on the open market either.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Andy Behrens (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-05]]): Carolina, despite creating uncertainty around Chuba Hubbard, did not re-sign Dowdle, surprising both hosts who doubted he'd be expensive to retain. Harris pushes back on midseason hype that made him sound like a star, calling him 'a good number two running back' who will be 'more nuisance than starter' but still a viable flex 'in a league where you take your flex in the sixth round.' He jokes Dowdle might lead the league in carries with modest per-carry results while some backs would have hit home runs on the same touches.
- 2026-03-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman likes Dowdle as an ideal fit in Washington, comping his skill set to a blend of [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] and Austin Ekeler -- he runs with power, shows good quickness, creates in tight spaces and catches the ball well, projecting as a potential every-down back who also works fine in a timeshare if needed. Waldman noted Washington faced Dowdle twice a year with Dallas and likely values what they saw enough to sign him as insurance while last year's rookie develops. Bob Harris (not tracked) added Dowdle's production has been volume-dependent -- he delivers well with bulk touches but underwhelms at half-volume -- and floated Denver (with JK Dobbins's status unclear) or a spot near Kenneth Walker III as alternates, with Washington still the favored landing spot for both.
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Dave Kluge (Footballguys.com, untracked guest) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-12]]) [Dynasty]: Dowdle leaves Carolina for Pittsburgh, pairing with [[Jaylen Warren]]. Kluge argues fantasy managers got 'a little over our skis' on Dowdle last year -- his best stretch was really just two games against the league's two worst run defenses plus a motivated revenge game against Dallas, and once [[Chuba Hubbard]] returned healthy, Hubbard arguably outplayed him. Both flag Pittsburgh's backfield as an 'incomplete puzzle' pending whether [[Aaron Rodgers]] returns; last year the Steelers clearly prioritized pass-blocking backs to protect an immobile 40-something quarterback, which Kluge argues favors Warren on passing downs with Dowdle limited to early-down work. Harris ultimately says he'd rank Warren ahead of Dowdle but recalls how poorly [[Najee Harris]]-timeshares worked out in Pittsburgh before, expecting a similarly muddled new-coaching-staff split.
- 2026-07-31 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-31]]) [Best Ball] [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris and Harmon group Dowdle with [[Jalen Warren]] as a late-RB2/handcuff-tier option they like about equally but can't meaningfully differentiate from camp reports. Harris argues Dowdle profiles as the back who'd benefit most from [[Aaron Rodgers]]'s checkdown-heavy, contact-averse style ('Rico Dowdle can catch those, I've seen it'), and both agree Dowdle is the one who'd fit a 'banger back' role if [[Caleb Johnson]] were ever to push into the mix. Neither expects the Steelers to reveal a real hierarchy in August -- Harmon says he'd need to see Dowdle in danger of not making the roster before taking any Caleb Johnson buzz seriously.
