# Justin Joly

## Expert Takes

- 2024-09-25 — According to guest Felix Sharpe of Campus2Canton (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-25 (Campus2Canton with Felix Sharpe)]]) [Dynasty]: the 6'3', 235-pound UConn transfer is a flex mismatch piece rather than an every-down tight end, comping stylistically to Harrison Bryant — the Mackey winner used as a big slot at Florida Atlantic. Joly chose NC State for OC Robert Anae, whose system has historically fed this archetype (Oronde Gadsden II at Syracuse, Jelani Woods at Virginia). Sharpe loves the route-running and run-after-catch, saying he runs routes like a big wide receiver and is unafraid to extend and pluck away from his frame. He has 14 receptions for 225 yards on the season as NC State's number-two option with a true freshman quarterback, including three catches for 73 yards against Clemson, and was uncoverable for Tennessee while at UConn. Sharpe said if he were 6'5', 242 this would be an easy day-two projection; at his actual size the question is whether the NFL finds a role. [[Matt Waldman]] raised Ed Dickson as a precedent; Sharpe countered that Dickson was a straight-line player and Joly has more, and both noted [[Evan Engram]] came out of Ole Miss at a similar 230-235.
- 2026-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-11]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman says NC State's Joly has the best hands in the entire 2026 tight end class, ahead of [[RJ Maryland]] and Kenyan Sadiq (untracked mention). He wins high points, extends away from his frame, digs out low throws, takes hard hits and still finishes contested catches, and was NC State's go-to target on fourth downs and in clutch spots. Built like an 'overgrown fullback,' he can also stretch the field with some burst and win after the catch, and projects to help on double teams and finesse blocks in a supporting in-line role rather than as a true anchor blocker. Waldman calls his draft and fantasy value volatile because his fit is scheme-dependent: in the right situation he projects Joly as a 700-800 yard, 6-8 touchdown receiver, but in the wrong fit he could end up a 300-400 yard, 3-4 touchdown role player.
- 2026-04-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-06]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls the 2026 tight end class unremarkable in a three-year historical window, but flags NC State (via UConn transfer) TE Justin Joly, 6'3', 241 lbs, as a standout within it. Asked if Joly could become a smaller [[Mark Andrews]], Waldman agrees, and adds he compares favorably to [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] as well -- saying Joly has the best hands of any tight end in the class, is a strong after-the-catch, power-oriented player, and a clutch red-zone weapon who has repeatedly made big plays in difficult moments. He's more of a move tight end than his build (which looks more like a fullback's) suggests, and Waldman isn't sure he has Fannin's downfield ceiling, but sees the same trait Andrews and Fannin share -- stretches where they look like the best receiver on their team's field, not just the best tight end.
- 2026-04-16 — According to Dalton Wasserman (guest draft analyst, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: [[Justin Joly]] is undersized but a really good athlete with strong contested-catch skills and after-the-catch production, consistent across levels of competition at UConn and NC State. Wasserman projects him into a 'move' tight end role similar to what [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] did behind [[David Njoku]] in Cleveland, or the [[Isaiah Likely]]/[[Jonnu Smith]] archetype -- get him the ball four or five times a game via screens and seam shots rather than asking him to block in-line. Wasserman declined to guarantee Joly matches Fannin's rookie breakout when Chris Harris pushed him to, but liked the underlying ball-skills profile enough to flag him as one to track for landing spot.
- 2026-04-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls Denver a 'black hole' for tight ends historically and, despite believing Joly has the talent to be an exception -- especially with Evan Engram unlikely to be around forever -- leans toward 'fuck it' for now, since the Broncos tend to spread minimal usage across multiple tight ends rather than feature one.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-07 (Going Deep with Brandon Angelo)]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo flagged Joly as a strong value in Denver given the roster's thin tertiary-target picture: Evan Engram's contract is up after 2026 and he's turning 33, while [[Adam Trautman]] has two years left on his deal, is turning 30, and hasn't topped 200 receiving yards under Sean Payton. Angelo compared Joly's opportunity to Isaiah Likely's in Baltimore -- outside of Courtland Sutton and rookie Pat Bryant, Denver lacks a clear tertiary answer, and he doesn't expect [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] or [[Troy Franklin]] to reach 600-700 yards. Both hosts agreed tight end is a value pocket worth monitoring in this range of rookie drafts.
- 2026-06-25 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Joly was competitive after the catch, super combative as a blocker, and graded as the best contested-catch tight end in his class -- Angelo compares him closely to [[Isaiah Likely]] and floats him as a potential 'Harold Fannin Jr. of this class' that the industry will regret not being higher on. The situational case: [[Evan Engram]] turns 33 with his contract up after 2027, [[Adam Trautman]] hasn't cleared 200 yards in two seasons, and [[Courtland Sutton]] returns at a big cap number at 32 -- meaning outside of [[Jalen Waddle|Jalen Waddell]], Denver could be hunting for explosive playmakers by 2027 and Joly, currently a free add in every draft, could fill a startable role.
- 2026-08-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-31]]) [Dynasty]: Joly (referred to on-air as 'Dustin Joley') was cut in Denver despite an 88-yard touchdown catch in the preseason finale, and both hosts think the team hopes to re-sign him to the practice squad. Waldman frames him as a terrific pass-catcher who is not a real threat after the catch -- comparing him to 'the equivalent of the undersized tackle in college who plays technically very well but isn't big enough' -- and says he simply doesn't fit what Denver wants physically. He notes Evan Engram has 'played really well' (though Waldman is skeptical that's Engram's ceiling regardless), Adam Trautman is a known quantity, and Lucas Krull is a solid, soft-handed blocker in the A.J. Barner mold, which together squeezed Joly off the roster.
