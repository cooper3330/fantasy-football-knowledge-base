# Josh Cameron

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2025-12-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-08]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman compared Baylor prospect Josh Cameron (listed roughly 6'1', 224) to [[Dontayvion Wicks]] -- strong after the catch, capable of winning targets in the air, and a good route runner on inside-breaking routes. Cameron has one of the largest release-move vocabularies of any non-top prospect Waldman has scouted in a decade, but struggles to actually separate using them, and Waldman flagged a ball-security issue -- he needs to cinch the ball tighter to his body rather than carrying it loosely. Waldman called Cameron a boom-bust evaluation: if he adds nuance to his releases he could become a really good primary receiver quickly, but the bust outcome is a long-term depth contributor with occasional big weeks depending on usage.
- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Cameron has essentially every release-footwork and hand-counter technique on Waldman's press-coverage checklist, but hasn't shown he can turn that technique into actual separation against tight man coverage -- he wins against zone and off coverage, and the craft underscoring his technique 'just isn't there yet' in terms of translating to production.
- 2026-09-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-09-29]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon views Cameron as a 'dirty work specialist' rather than a real threat to [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]'s long-term standing -- an outside-only Baylor product whose college Reception Perception charting graded him raw as a route runner (3rd percentile vs. man, 6th percentile press, 9th percentile zone) but with good contested-catch hands and toughness, 'a bull in the open field.' He's earning early NFL snaps (2 touchdowns, about 30 slot snaps used situationally, strong effort as a blocker) as a Day 3 developmental project rather than a finished receiver, per Harmon's original prospect summary.
- 2026-10-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-10-20]]): [[Josh Cameron]] has emerged as a 'hoss' blocking receiver for Jacksonville who is also good enough as a catcher that he isn't a tell for run-or-pass when he's on the field. Harmon compared the role to '11 and a half personnel,' the archetype [[Cooper Kupp]] and [[Robert Woods]] once filled for the Sean McVay-tree Rams offense that Jaguars play-caller Liam Coen comes from. Harmon noted this physicality and toughness at the catch point and with the ball in his hands didn't show up on Cameron's Baylor tape because he was stuck wide on the perimeter in a scheme-limited college offense, but it has clearly translated, including a go-route block that helped spring a [[Parker Washington]] touchdown against Philadelphia.
- 2026-10-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-10-22 (Going Deep Eagles & 2027 QB Class)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman highlights rookie Josh Cameron as a 'dirty work' receiver who digs out safeties in the run game, and says he's made [[Brian Thomas Jr]] fairly obsolete in that role -- Jacksonville is pulling [[Jakobi Meyers]] off the field in 12 personnel in favor of Cameron specifically because of his run blocking, even with [[Parker Washington]] entrenched as the clear primary target. He comps Cameron to a [[Juwan Jennings]]-type energy-giver who gives big downfield blocks and never takes a play off, and notes Carolina's Tommy Tremble and [[A.J. Barner]] as similar run-game-impact tight ends who won't put up box-score numbers but drive consistent down-to-down offense.
