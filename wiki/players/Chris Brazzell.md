# Chris Brazzell

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2025-12-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-01 (Feel It or Fk It)]]): Waldman called Chris Brazzell II 'a more promising version of George Pickens,' saying Brazzell is better than Pickens was at Georgia both at the catch point and as a route runner, despite Tennessee's scheme using super-wide splits tight to the boundary. He highlighted Brazzell's ability to manipulate his release stem -- widening or diving it to move defenders out of position before accelerating past them -- calling it done in 'an expert sort of way,' and said more patience and suddenness in his movement contrasts could make him an excellent big-play receiver and possibly a future alpha. Waldman flagged a build caveat: listed 6'5' but a 'stick figure' frame comparable to [[Travis Hunter]] and other lanky recent Tennessee receivers, though he still expects an early-career opportunity to contribute.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Brazzell is a rangy, high-variance evaluation -- Waldman's outcome range spans a Christian Watson-type breakout (comparable to Watson's post-ACL 2025 role in Green Bay) down to a Robbie Anderson-type limited-but-employable career. Waldman's mid-range comp is George Pickens, joking Brazzell is 'the winner of the George Pickens look-alike contest' for this class. Both hosts worry teams could pigeonhole him into a rebound/slant/over-route role if he doesn't start fast, which would cap him below what his tape suggests he's capable of.
- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls Brazzell the closest stylistic comp on this year's board to [[George Pickens]] -- elite speed, excellent length and body control to rebound the ball in tight confines, plus focus and toughness to play through contact, with good deceleration as a route runner and ball carrier. Waldman spends significant time pushing back on 'logo scouting' skepticism around Tennessee's recent receiver track record (Dont'e Thornton Jr., Jalen Hyatt, Cedric Tillman, Josh Palmer, Jauan Jennings), arguing the program ran at least three distinct receiver systems rather than one interchangeable scheme, so Tennessee's mixed recent draft history shouldn't be held against Brazzell individually. He views Brazzell as having enough promise to start as a rookie for a team with an immediate need.
- 2026-04-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman ranked Brazzell his WR7, calling him the best of a three-man group he stylistically comps to George Pickens, but said Brazzell is more than a sideline rebounder -- he runs slants and over routes and shows good ball-tracking skill. Waldman pushed back on 'logo scouting,' evaluators discounting a prospect because of a school's recent track record with similar-profile receivers rather than judging the player's own tape, noting Brazzell also played well at Tulane before Tennessee and is a different type of player than the specific Tennessee-lineage comps people default to (e.g. Josh Palmer, Jaylin Lane). He projected Brazzell as a likely future starter with a real chance to start as a rookie.
- 2026-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls Brazzell a 'George Pickens look-alike' -- not as good yet, but ahead of fellow Tennessee alum Ja'Kobi Lane and only slightly behind Pickens -- and says his catching, breaks and weight-drop distinguish him from recent disappointing Tennessee Vols WRs out of the 'Looney Tunes' spread system (Dont'e Thornton, [[Jalen Hyatt]], [[Cedric Tillman]], Josh Palmer, Juwan Jennings), most of whom rarely saw press/outside-boundary looks in that scheme. Harmon agrees Brazzell projects day-two, roughly picks 60-70, praising his loose hips on intermediate/out-breaking routes (the viral out route vs. Georgia) as genuinely representative of his game. Both worry he needs multiple years of seasoning as an X-only, one-side-of-the-field player, and that NFL rosters may move on to the next draft class before he's given that runway.
- 2026-04-09 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-09]]) [Dynasty]: also lower than consensus on Chris Brazzell -- a fluid 6'5' mover with 'all the play speed in the world,' but Angelo's central question is whether he can win above the rim consistently against physical, press-man No. 1 corners. He grouped Brazzell with the historically tiny pool of 6'5'-plus receivers who've posted 1,000-yard NFL seasons -- naming only Calvin Johnson, Randy Moss, Mike Williams and Mike Evans -- calling that list 'very small,' and noting offensive coordinators often don't know how to deploy tall, lean receivers as true No. 1s rather than move pieces in 12 personnel. He comped Brazzell's archetype to [[D.J. Chark]] and said he's a talented player with real upside, but the deployment question is unresolved.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-30 (Round 3 WRs)]]): Carolina took the 6'4', ~200-pound Brazzell 19th in round 3. Harmon flags him as 'an obvious blend of red flags and appealing marks,' including unspecified off-field concerns, plus a skewed-alignment profile from Tennessee's spread ('Looney Tunes') offense -- 95% of his sampled snaps came at right outside receiver, one of the highest rates Harmon has charted in recent classes, though his prior stop at Tulane gives some hope he can adjust better than typical Tennessee products. He's not yet a nuanced separator, but flashed loose hips on horizontal breaking routes: 81% success on the dig (high volume) and 80% on the out route, both areas where Tetairoa McMillan also excelled. Harmon and Koh see him fitting Carolina's oversized WR room (McMillan, Xavier Legette, Jalen Coker) built to give the smaller Bryce Young clearer throwing lanes, and think his speed lets Carolina move McMillan and Coker around rather than pigeonholing them. They also floated that Carolina's best Bryce Young offense historically came from empty/spread sets (11.2 yards/completion, 7.3 adjusted yards/attempt, 96 passer rating out of empty in 2024) and that Brazzell could push Carolina to lean into that more. Both view him as a year-three developmental project given the Tennessee system risk.
- 2026-05-25 — On whether Chris Brazzell is the favorite to win Carolina's WR3 job, Bob Harris (untracked, Football Guys) said he 'feels it,' and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-25]]) [Dynasty] agreed: he views [[John Metchie III]] as not having elevated his pro game (compounded by illness) and [[Xavier Legette]] as adventure-prone catching the ball and a lesser route runner than Brazzell will become, so Brazzell 'could easily win this job' by training camp, or clearly be the guy by season's end. Waldman noted a comp from analyst Stigman Bloom (untracked) to [[George Pickens]] as an interesting stylistic parallel. Both hosts pointed to Carolina's history of swinging and missing at receiver (citing [[Jonathan Mingo]] as a prior miss) and moving on without sunk-cost hesitation, meaning the WR3 job is genuinely open for the taking rather than a formality.
- 2026-06-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-29]]) [Dynasty]: Thinks the OTA buzz on Chris Brazzell is worthy -- he projects as an excellent contested-catch, YAC-capable outside receiver, essentially 'the outside receiver people thought [[Tetairoa McMillan]] was supposed to be,' and should develop fairly quickly into an outside complement to [[Jalen Coker]] (who already beats man coverage) once he wins his camp battle with Xavier Legette. Frames the outlook as contingent on 'rational coaching': if Carolina instead keeps McMillan outside rather than moving him into the Drake London-style big slot, Brazzell stays a boom-bust bench stash rather than a real weekly factor.
- 2026-07-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and untracked guest Jim McCormick ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Discussing Carolina's third-round rookie receiver Chris Brazzell, McCormick describes him as a 'stretched out DeVonta Smith without the Heisman' -- a 6'4', 195-pound vertical/deep-threat archetype with prototypical size-speed measurables but no proven college production, part of what McCormick calls a now-crowded group of similar '6'4', 4.4' receiver types. Neither expects much August news to move his outlook; he's viewed as a project behind [[Tetairoa McMillan]] and [[Jalen Coker]] on the depth chart.
- 2026-08-03 — According to Ian Hartitz (untracked co-host) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-03 (Feel It or Fk It with Ian Hartitz)]]): he gives Brazzell some credit off a strong Tennessee tape (one of only two SEC receivers over 1,000 yards, including a 170-yard, three-touchdown game against Georgia), comparing the tendency to dismiss it to how evaluators shrugged off Jalen Hyatt's big game against Alabama a few years back.
