# Kareem Hunt

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): Waldman said Hunt is 'the one to punch here' in the Kansas City backfield after the Chargers game. Two reasons: Hunt looked really good and fresh, and he is better in passing-down situations than [[Carson Steele]], who dropped a pass and is not as sharp in pass protection. Waldman expects Andy Reid to keep a three-back rotation, with Samaje Perine taking pass-protection duties and Hunt working as the receiver; Hunt 'clearly understands Andy Reid's offense and looked like he had never left,' with great movement in traffic. Waldman noted he was buying cheap Hunt shares all offseason while everyone else paid up for Steele, expecting Hunt to land somewhere. Caveats: age could show if he cannot rebound week to week, and a Clyde Edwards-Helaire return from the non-football injury list could scramble it again.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: asked whether Hunt is Kansas City's best back healthy or not, Waldman said 'I feel that.' He is 'not as quick or as fast as [[Isiah Pacheco]], not the big play guy,' but 'when you watch him, he bounces off of people, runs through people's faces — he is what they need as a between-the-tackles runner.' The value is reliability down to down to get the tough yardage while also finding big creases, even if he does not break them as far as Pacheco. Waldman's structural point: that reliability keeps down-and-distance manageable, keeps game scripts good and 'is making [[Patrick Mahomes]]'s job much easier.'
- 2024-11-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-25]]): Waldman expects Hunt to keep the lead role through the fantasy playoffs even as [[Isiah Pacheco]] returns, because the sensible Kansas City plan is to ease Pacheco back with sparing touches in a Carson Steele-type role and let his leg fully heal ahead of the NFL postseason. Waldman flagged this as the take he is most likely to be wrong on; Bob Harris (not tracked) shared the same hunch.
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]): hold, do not start. Waldman openly says he and Bob Harris were wrong the previous week to expect Hunt to keep the job while [[Isiah Pacheco]] eased back — the Chiefs clearly saw enough in practice. Hunt still had a role in a roughly even split and ran more routes, but Waldman's rule is blunt: if you were starting Kareem Hunt, you are no longer starting Kareem Hunt; if you are thinking of dropping him, keep him unless you have a massive roster hole and comfortable RB depth. If he is dropped, add him speculatively, because Pacheco could get hurt again and Hunt is too valuable a player in too good an offense to forget entirely.
- 2025-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-05]]): Hunt led Kansas City in running-back snaps against Buffalo in the AFC Championship at about 55%, which Harmon says has been the common theme for roughly the past month — ahead of [[Isiah Pacheco]] at 30%, [[Samaje Perine]] at 16% and [[Carson Steele]] at 11%. His Super Bowl rushing-yards line was 45.5 against Pacheco's 22.5, with first-carry odds even at +425 for both. Harmon still describes the overall Chiefs ground game as not good but effective and efficient rather than explosive, and the four-way split as absolutely maddening from a fantasy and betting perspective.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Hunt played 23 snaps to [[Isiah Pacheco]]'s 28 and was clearly both the third-down back, playing basically every third down, and the short-yardage back. Harris says that if you own Pacheco it should worry you, and at worst Hunt looks like the back you would want to own in this backfield, while simultaneously saying he does not think Hunt is that good a player.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): the pre-game chatter that the Chiefs backfield answer was 'the notes you don't hear' — [[Brashard Smith]] — did not hold up; Smith played about 10 offensive snaps. Hunt 'tries real hard and sometimes he scores a touchdown', getting one late, though he lost another to a [[Rashee Rice]] wildcat run and was stuffed at the goal line after a Marquise Brown bomb. Guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert) answers in one word that if [[Isaiah Pacheco]] is again unavailable, 'it's Kareem Hunt and nobody else'. Both expect Pacheco back after the bye, and Harris's instruction on Smith is explicit: 'whatever you do, do not play him'.
