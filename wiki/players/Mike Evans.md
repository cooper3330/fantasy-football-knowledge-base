# Mike Evans

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: coming off an 11th straight 1,000-yard season, Evans's 2024 outlook is entirely a situation question rather than a talent one. If he re-signs with Tampa and keeps Baker Mayfield, Waldman would draft him around his 2023 ADP (third round) or "a little above," expecting "going rate will be cheap again." If he leaves via free agency, Waldman turns notably more cautious, noting "these late-career moves don't always work out for the best" for aging receivers changing situations.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: "an absolute smash spot" for the Divisional Round at Detroit — the Lions allowed the most receiving yards, second-most receptions, and six touchdowns to outside wide receivers between Weeks 10-17 (bottom-12 in completion percentage allowed). Expects a bounce-back after a quiet Wild Card game, on the reasoning that Evans "rarely almost never has two quiet games" in a row.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): explicit free-agency mechanics after Tampa Bay's Divisional Round exit — Harmon says Tampa "cannot allow him to test the open market," expecting a bidding war (floats a minimum 3-year, $50M deal) from receiver-needy teams if he gets there — the Jets, Kansas City, and especially New England ("going way over the top") are named as plausible bidders, with Detroit's $58.6M in cap space and a stated Dan Campbell-culture fit (never a diva, all business) singled out as the best non-Tampa landing spot, a DeAndre-Hopkins-to-Detroit comparison made and rejected on culture-fit grounds. Overall verdict: Tampa likely retains him given its own $47M in cap space, but it's not close to guaranteed.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): independently floated (alongside [[Tee Higgins]] and Michael Pittman Jr., the latter not a tracked page) as one of the top free-agent receiver names Kansas City could pursue to pair with [[Rashee Rice]] — corroborating Harmon's read from six days earlier that Evans will draw serious outside interest if he reaches the market, now with a specific Chiefs connection attached.
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): a new complication on top of the free-agency question — **Tampa Bay OC Dave Canales is leaving to become Carolina's head coach.** Harmon credits Canales's 2023 scheme changes directly for "unleashing" Evans on more big in-breaking and traditional routes rather than the "bunch of go routes" he ran in the prior Byron Leftwich offense, meaning Evans's 2024 outlook is now uncertain on scheme as well as free-agency grounds, even if he re-signs with Tampa.
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): reports a "soft deadline" for a new Tampa Bay deal has reportedly passed (per Jordan Schultz, Bleacher Report) and Evans now looks headed to the open market as an unrestricted free agent — the clearest signal yet, in this wiki, that he actually reaches free agency rather than re-signing before hitting it. Logged mainly for its downstream effect: [[Baker Mayfield]]'s own 2024 outlook is explicitly conditioned on Evans staying in Tampa.
- 2024-02-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-27]]): fresh 2023 Reception Perception charting shows one of his best seasons — 73.1% success rate vs. man coverage (career best), 75.4% vs. press (second-best of his career, trailing only his 79.1% in 2021), and third-best success rate vs. zone. Credits new OC Dave Canales's route-tree redesign: go routes fell from 27% of his routes in 2022 to 16.5% in 2023, slants rose from 12.6% to 18.5%, and simple outs rose from 8% to 15% — and per Next Gen Stats he actually produced more targets/catches/yards/touchdowns on 20+ air-yard routes in 2023 than in a heavier-go-route 2022, despite running fewer deep routes overall. Calls him an underrated, technically excellent route runner miscast as "just a big jump-ball receiver." On free-agency value: projects a deal north of $18M/year, citing Brad Spielberger's (PFF, not tracked) Kenny Golladay 2021 comp as a floor and Davante Adams's Raiders deal as a ceiling comp.

- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]) [Best Ball]: with his new two-year, $52M Tampa Bay deal now official (a 10-year stay, 10 straight 1,000-yard seasons), projects his 2024 ceiling depends entirely on who throws him the football — a rookie starter likely caps him near 1,000 yards and reads as "a down season"; a veteran addition like [[Kirk Cousins]] could sustain his usual production; [[Baker Mayfield]] returning "could do it again." Notes he's currently going in the fourth round of early best ball drafts and expects the price to keep rising now that the signing is confirmed.
- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): calls the finalized two-year, $52M deal ($35M guaranteed) "an absolute W for both sides." Fresh full-season 2023 charting confirms one of his best years: 73.1% success rate vs. man (career best), 75.4% vs. press and 77% vs. zone (both second-best of his career), 83% on slants (career high), 86.8% on out routes. Frames the 2022 dip (72.5% vs. zone) as route-tree misallocation under Byron Leftwich rather than decline — the same shape of dip that preceded real cliffs for Allen Robinson (2021) and Andre Johnson (2014) — but credits OC Dave Canales's redesign (slant rate up from 12.5% to 18.5%, out-route rate up to 15%) for the 2023 rebound. Calls him a "stereotyped," underrated technician rather than just a "nine-route specialist," citing a self-driven climb from ~67-69% success his first four seasons to consistently 70%+ since. Also frames the deal as protecting [[Baker Mayfield]]'s outlook, since losing Evans would have left Tampa with only [[Chris Godwin]] — "a really good two, but not a one" — at receiver.
