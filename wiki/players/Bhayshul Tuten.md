# Bhayshul Tuten

## Expert Takes

- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: the Virginia Tech back is the player he may be too bearish on. He grades below the 50th percentile on consistency across the board — fumbles, stuff rate, failing to recognize simple keys and being out of phase with the run call — but he is a genuine big-play threat whose physical measurables are there while the perceptual-cognitive side is not. Angelo said the right system changes everything: landing in San Francisco to learn pace and tempo behind [[Christian McCaffrey]] under Kyle Shanahan, one of the best outside-zone minds, would make him a potentially dynamic weapon. [[Matt Waldman]] said Angelo is not too bearish but realistic, and cited his own six-year RSP ball-security study: backs falling below a fumble-rate threshold — and Tuten is well below it — produced a top-24 fantasy season only about 10% of the time and a top-10-to-12 season about 3% of the time. Waldman compared a talented young player developing in that San Francisco environment to a young musician sitting in with the Wrecking Crew, noting what it did for [[Brock Purdy]].
- 2025-05-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-19]]) [Best Ball]: Tuten and [[Jaydon Blue]] are the double-digit-round ambiguous-backfield darts Waldman is splitting close to 50/50 — he leaned Blue over the weekend and Tuten on the day of recording, 'it's that close.' The tiebreak on receiving work goes to Blue: Waldman calls Blue the better receiver with more upside to succeed early as a blocker, and characterizes Tuten as a physical talent still learning the nuances of the game, where Blue already shows some. Tuten's advantage is ball security, which has been better than Blue's. Waldman also notes [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] is a good pass catcher who handles that backfield work, and that Jacksonville's line does not inspire confidence despite free-agent additions bulking up the interior; the 4.32-versus-4.38 forty gap he considers immaterial, worth maybe one extra share.
- 2025-06-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Tuten is the back everyone wants to be the guy, and at RB40 Waldman is fading him — he would rather have [[Keon Coleman]], [[David Njoku]] or a bargain-priced [[C.J. Stroud]] than Tuten at that price. He acknowledged the appeal: a very fast 40 at the combine and great size and measurables across the board. The disqualifier is ball security — Waldman said flatly that Tuten does not hold onto the ball well — while still calling him promising. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) admitted he owns Tuten shares and that the 40 time is most of the reason, in the same spirit as his standing joke about 4.4 running back speed.
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: calls Tuten the wild horse of the Jaguars stable and possibly the best-skilled runner they have, but the outcome is close to binary because of ball security. Waldman jokes that Tuten and [[Jaydon Blue]] should be made to run with a greased watermelon — and that you probably would not even need the grease. His framing: either Tuten is a 1,200-yard rusher right out of the gate once somebody ahead of him gets hurt and he holds onto the football, or it is 30 carries and four fumbles and the team says 'next year'. He describes Tuten as [[Jaydon Blue]] on Red Bull who needs to calm down; if he gets it together, there is something there. [[Chris Harris]] is dismissive of the summer hype, noting nobody can claim to know what Tuten will be with two backs in front of him.
- 2025-08-25 — According to guest JJ Zachariason (NOT a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]]: Tuten is his number one late-round dart at around the 13th round, while being explicit that he ranks Tuten third in the Jacksonville backfield behind [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] and [[Tank Bigsby]] today. The thesis is the ambiguous backfield: uncertainty pushes cost down, and someone can emerge, as [[Leonard Fournette]] did in the Tampa Bay committee. He calls Tuten a really high-variance prospect and explicitly refuses to claim Tuten is Liam Coen's [[Bucky Irving]] — not the same player at all — but says the Irving usage shows Coen will eventually feed the most talented runner. Tuten's size-speed profile is similar to [[Kenneth Walker III]], though JJ does not claim the same vision, and he thought Tuten was a good pass catcher coming out. His likeliest shape is a second-half-of-season stash. [[Chris Harris]] endorses taking the shot: you will not get yelled at for a 13th-round pick.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Tuten is the one Jacksonville back Harmon does not have a great read on. He thought Tuten was a 'really super one-dimensional player' coming out of school, but the one thing he does well should be leaned on by Liam Coen — outside zone. If Jacksonville runs it, Harmon said Tuten 'is going to be great', and he could split fantasy-relevant work with [[Travis Etienne Jr.]], though Harmon's overall lean remains Etienne as the lead. Full charting profiles on Tuten, Etienne and [[Tank Bigsby]] are on the Reception Perception site.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Tuten was absent from both hosts' Week 1 top 50, which Harris flagged as a likely mutual mistake; guest Andy Behrens (not tracked) corrected to RB19 on air. Harris's skepticism is about the process behind Tuten's rise rather than the player — people who did not know he existed watched the combine, learned three facts (Travis Etienne's contract situation, Tank Bigsby's status, Tuten's athleticism) and concluded he must be a superstar worth a 10th-round pick. Harris's stated default is 'you know nothing.' Both hosts also mocked the summer's Jacksonville backfield churn — analysts parsing a [[Tank Bigsby]] eyes-emoji tweet for trade intent — and landed back where they were in June: a probable split between Bigsby and [[Travis Etienne Jr.]], with Harris calling Tuten an interesting player and athlete but 'not the guy to start the year.'
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Film & Theory Sequences, Signal, Steelers)]]): [[Bhayshul Tuten]] is the counterexample to the do-not-panic-about-fumbles rule — Waldman said that if Tuten fumbles a fair bit early while sitting in a three-man backfield competition, that is more concerning than the same fumbles from a locked-in talent like [[Ashton Jeanty]], because the coaching staff has ready alternatives and the fumbles give them a reason to use them. Still framed as a caution about a pattern, not a single play.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): Tuten played four snaps while the game was competitive but got back-to-back carries inside the Carolina 15 after [[Travis Etienne Jr.]]'s long run, gaining ground without scoring. With [[Tank Bigsby]] traded, Harris says Tuten could be an okay add in a shallow league but calls it a tough one at 57% rostered — a deep shot for shallow-league purposes, since the third Jacksonville back also matters. Guest Jake Ciely of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) left him off his top five: best case is Tuten taking roughly a third of the touches, the [[Rachaad White]] pattern under Liam Coen, and in shallow leagues you should chase potential top-20-25 backs rather than bench depth. Both agreed [[Quinshon Judkins]] (55% rostered) is the easier bid than Tuten in a shallow league.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris specifically wanted to flag Tuten's touchdown reception, which looked a lot like the [[Bucky Irving]] catch that set up Tampa's winning drive — the rocked-up 5-9, 210-pounder caught it in the flat, put his foot in the ground and stopped, and the defender fell straight past him for an easy score. He also looks strong: on a third-quarter carry a defender basically climbed on his back in the backfield and Tuten shrugged him off for a five-yard gain. The usage caps it, though — Jacksonville's backfield snaps went 46 [[Travis Etienne Jr.]], 18 Tuten, 11 [[LeQuint Allen]] (10 of Allen's 11 snaps on third down, so Allen is the third-down back and the other two split early downs). Harris does not think Tuten is usable in Week 3 in very many leagues, but calls it a positive performance.
- 2025-09-19 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-19]]): Tuten faces 'a real narrow path' as long as [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] stays healthy. The hidden detail in Jacksonville's backfield is that third downs belong to rookie [[LeQuint Allen]] — neither Etienne nor Tuten plays there at all — so Tuten's realistic route to value is the goal line, and Harris has seen Etienne suddenly look good in short yardage too. On talent he is positive: guest Josh Fisch (not a tracked expert) praised a screen play Tuten took a long way, and Harris agreed it was 'very [[Bucky Irving]]-like, except he's big — to be able to do that and be big is a good skill.'
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): Jacksonville's backfield split 36 snaps for [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] to 12 for Tuten, but Tuten got the goal line the first time it came up and scored from a yard out. 'The kid has some oomph to him' — Harris singled out a shotgun draw to start the third quarter where Tuten withstood a hit in the backfield, put a hand on the ground to keep his balance and got six yards basically on his own. The verdict is a handcuff verdict: 'for the moment, he's almost certainly more an ETN handcuff than a startable commodity himself.'

Asked directly whether he values Tuten above [[RJ Harvey]] right now, Harris says yes, though it is close, while acknowledging the market still prefers Harvey.

The backfield stays a committee with Etienne on top. Waldman's caveat is a general one — football is weird and things can change fast in the NFL.

Waldman said Etienne actually played well and got the bulk of red-zone work (two touchdown-scoring sequences to Tuten's one), but Tuten's physicality and explosiveness stood out on tape and support the push toward an even split going forward, health permitting. [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-17 (Week 11 Game Film Review)]]), who watched the game live, said in the moment it looked serious enough that he feared a severe foot injury, so Tuten's near-term usability is very much health-contingent pending updates.
- 2025-11-21 — According to Chris Harris and Cousin Josh (guest, untracked) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-21]] [Dynasty]: both picked Tuten as a 2026 riser after his breakout game, praising him as already the best zone runner on the roster and a contact-seeking, physical runner they compared to [[Jalen Warren]] (with Tuten possibly faster than Warren, per Josh). They noted [[Matt Waldman]] has been a vocal believer in Tuten on social media. The bull case hinges on [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] hitting free agency; if he leaves, Tuten projects to roughly 60-65% of the Jacksonville backfield since head coach Liam Coen and [[Trevor Lawrence]] are staying and the offense should remain stable. Even so, both cautioned the team won't hand him the entire backfield -- a return by [[Najee Harris]] or a drafted rookie, or incumbent [[LeQuint Allen]], could create a 65-35 split -- and neither is ready to call him a guaranteed top-24 dynasty back yet, just a legitimate trade target off a proof-of-concept game.

- 2026-01-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: named Tuten his 'chalkiest' level-jumper for 2026 -- an explosive rookie who was trusted near the goal line and flashed multiple big chunk runs in the playoff loss to Buffalo, fitting the outside-zone, perimeter-heavy scheme Liam Cohen ran with Tampa Bay's backs in 2024. Harmon thinks Jacksonville abandoned the run game too early against Buffalo and that a full offseason plus possible O-line additions could push Tuten into obvious-breakout territory, contingent on what happens with pending free agent [[Travis Etienne Jr.]]. [[Chris Harris]] independently had Tuten at #3 on his own list, calling him a good power runner who is a handful at the goal line, praising his ability to cut back on inside zone ('unlike TreVeyon Henderson, who never has done that in his life') and his open-field speed once he turns the corner on outside zone ('not Jonathan Taylor, but pretty fast'). Harris says Tuten is unlikely to be drafted as a top-2-round fantasy RB but could produce like one.
- 2026-01-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-26]]) [Dynasty]: hold. Waldman comps Tuten to Khalil Herbert -- explosive and rugged for his size, capable of handling a sizable workload despite not being a big back, and a smart, quick runner between the tackles -- while noting Tuten needs more discipline in his decision-making than Herbert showed but is a better receiver out of the backfield. With [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] a pending free agent, Waldman expects Tuten gets the first opportunity at the job even if Jacksonville adds a complementary back, citing team feedback that Tuten is a well-liked, professional worker on and off the field, even if he 'does everything well but nothing great.'

The hoped-for outcome for Tuten backers is that he becomes the goal-line and passing-down piece, but with [[LeQuint Allen]] also drawing passing-down work, they compare the situation to Arizona's muddled backfield and say it's too unclear to commit to any one back pre-draft -- best approached by taking whichever is cheapest in ADP. Harris notes a fuller 'Film Futures' breakdown on Tuten is coming to the show's subscriber feed.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Tuten's touch share in Jacksonville looks likely to be capped by Liam Coen's approach -- pointing to what [[Chris Rodriguez Jr.]] did in a similar role at Kentucky under Coen, Waldman thinks the low red-zone/goal-line work goes to Rodriguez, leaving Tuten with between-the-20s work and limited pass-catching opportunities, since Tuten doesn't profile as a prolific pass-catching back. Waldman compared his likely role to a 'Keith Mitchell-plus' big-play specialist -- frustrating for fantasy -- and noted [[LeQuint Allen]] profiles as a strong third-down back, further squeezing Tuten to roughly 38-42% of the backfield work, not enough for Waldman to bet on sustainable fantasy production.
