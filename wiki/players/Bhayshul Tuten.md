# Bhayshul Tuten

## Expert Takes

- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: the Virginia Tech back is the player he may be too bearish on. He grades below the 50th percentile on consistency across the board — fumbles, stuff rate, failing to recognize simple keys and being out of phase with the run call — but he is a genuine big-play threat whose physical measurables are there while the perceptual-cognitive side is not. Angelo said the right system changes everything: landing in San Francisco to learn pace and tempo behind [[Christian McCaffrey]] under Kyle Shanahan, one of the best outside-zone minds, would make him a potentially dynamic weapon. [[Matt Waldman]] said Angelo is not too bearish but realistic, and cited his own six-year RSP ball-security study: backs falling below a fumble-rate threshold — and Tuten is well below it — produced a top-24 fantasy season only about 10% of the time and a top-10-to-12 season about 3% of the time. Waldman compared a talented young player developing in that San Francisco environment to a young musician sitting in with the Wrecking Crew, noting what it did for [[Brock Purdy]].
- 2025-05-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-19]]) [Best Ball]: Tuten and [[Jaydon Blue]] are the double-digit-round ambiguous-backfield darts Waldman is splitting close to 50/50 — he leaned Blue over the weekend and Tuten on the day of recording, 'it's that close.' The tiebreak on receiving work goes to Blue: Waldman calls Blue the better receiver with more upside to succeed early as a blocker, and characterizes Tuten as a physical talent still learning the nuances of the game, where Blue already shows some. Tuten's advantage is ball security, which has been better than Blue's. Waldman also notes [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] is a good pass catcher who handles that backfield work, and that Jacksonville's line does not inspire confidence despite free-agent additions bulking up the interior; the 4.32-versus-4.38 forty gap he considers immaterial, worth maybe one extra share.
- 2025-06-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Tuten is the back everyone wants to be the guy, and at RB40 Waldman is fading him — he would rather have [[Keon Coleman]], [[David Njoku]] or a bargain-priced [[C.J. Stroud]] than Tuten at that price. He acknowledged the appeal: a very fast 40 at the combine and great size and measurables across the board. The disqualifier is ball security — Waldman said flatly that Tuten does not hold onto the ball well — while still calling him promising. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) admitted he owns Tuten shares and that the 40 time is most of the reason, in the same spirit as his standing joke about 4.4 running back speed.
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: calls Tuten the wild horse of the Jaguars stable and possibly the best-skilled runner they have, but the outcome is close to binary because of ball security. Waldman jokes that Tuten and [[Jaydon Blue]] should be made to run with a greased watermelon — and that you probably would not even need the grease. His framing: either Tuten is a 1,200-yard rusher right out of the gate once somebody ahead of him gets hurt and he holds onto the football, or it is 30 carries and four fumbles and the team says 'next year'. He describes Tuten as [[Jaydon Blue]] on Red Bull who needs to calm down; if he gets it together, there is something there. [[Chris Harris]] is dismissive of the summer hype, noting nobody can claim to know what Tuten will be with two backs in front of him.
- 2025-08-25 — According to guest JJ Zachariason (NOT a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]]: Tuten is his number one late-round dart at around the 13th round, while being explicit that he ranks Tuten third in the Jacksonville backfield behind [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] and [[Tank Bigsby]] today. The thesis is the ambiguous backfield: uncertainty pushes cost down, and someone can emerge, as [[Leonard Fournette]] did in the Tampa Bay committee. He calls Tuten a really high-variance prospect and explicitly refuses to claim Tuten is Liam Coen's [[Bucky Irving]] — not the same player at all — but says the Irving usage shows Coen will eventually feed the most talented runner. Tuten's size-speed profile is similar to [[Kenneth Walker III]], though JJ does not claim the same vision, and he thought Tuten was a good pass catcher coming out. His likeliest shape is a second-half-of-season stash. [[Chris Harris]] endorses taking the shot: you will not get yelled at for a 13th-round pick.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Tuten is the one Jacksonville back Harmon does not have a great read on. He thought Tuten was a 'really super one-dimensional player' coming out of school, but the one thing he does well should be leaned on by Liam Coen — outside zone. If Jacksonville runs it, Harmon said Tuten 'is going to be great', and he could split fantasy-relevant work with [[Travis Etienne Jr.]], though Harmon's overall lean remains Etienne as the lead. Full charting profiles on Tuten, Etienne and [[Tank Bigsby]] are on the Reception Perception site.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Tuten was absent from both hosts' Week 1 top 50, which Harris flagged as a likely mutual mistake; guest Andy Behrens (not tracked) corrected to RB19 on air. Harris's skepticism is about the process behind Tuten's rise rather than the player — people who did not know he existed watched the combine, learned three facts (Travis Etienne's contract situation, Tank Bigsby's status, Tuten's athleticism) and concluded he must be a superstar worth a 10th-round pick. Harris's stated default is 'you know nothing.' Both hosts also mocked the summer's Jacksonville backfield churn — analysts parsing a [[Tank Bigsby]] eyes-emoji tweet for trade intent — and landed back where they were in June: a probable split between Bigsby and [[Travis Etienne Jr.]], with Harris calling Tuten an interesting player and athlete but 'not the guy to start the year.'
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Film & Theory Sequences, Signal, Steelers)]]): [[Bhayshul Tuten]] is the counterexample to the do-not-panic-about-fumbles rule — Waldman said that if Tuten fumbles a fair bit early while sitting in a three-man backfield competition, that is more concerning than the same fumbles from a locked-in talent like [[Ashton Jeanty]], because the coaching staff has ready alternatives and the fumbles give them a reason to use them. Still framed as a caution about a pattern, not a single play.
