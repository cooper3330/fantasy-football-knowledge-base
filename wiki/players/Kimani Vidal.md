# Kimani Vidal

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name as "Kamani Vidal"; normalized here to the real name of the Troy
> running back, Kimani Vidal.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]) [Dynasty]: named as a mid-to-late-pick who could become a viable committee contributor if he survives the perception attached to his likely Day 3 draft capital, avoids mistakes in camp, and earns real opportunity. Profile: 5'7", ~215 lbs, a powerful runner with a low center of gravity and real movement skill, plus decent decision-making on tape. A standout in Senior Bowl pass-protection reps specifically — both hosts flag it as a real "earned his stripes" moment. [[Matt Waldman]], same episode: agrees the low center of gravity and movement skills are genuine translatable traits, and floats a fourth-round outcome as plausible given how he could be viewed once teams see the tape.

- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]) [Dynasty]: reaffirms he has Vidal "as a top 10 running back" in the 2024 class, while noting he was still surprised by Vidal's 4.46 combine 40 time given that ranking. See [[NFL Combine and Pro Day Skepticism]] for Waldman's broader process for weighing an unexpectedly fast workout number.
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: praises Vidal's conservative, reliable decision-making (avoids bouncing/cutting back plays coaches want kept in structure) and blitz-pickup skill — moves incrementally well to influence a blitzer's angle and dissuade him before contact. Sharpest line: people whose "hopes and dreams" are tied up in [[DeAndre Swift]] are "unknowingly projecting much of Vidal's actual game" onto their idea of what Swift is, since Swift "isn't actually all that they see" — Vidal "has a chance to be" that quality player.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]] [Dynasty]: extends the [[DeAndre Swift]] comparison from 2024-03-23 with a real-estate analogy — Vidal is "essentially what people see when they look at DeAndre Swift but are wrong about," at a smaller school with likely low draft capital who'll have to prove himself more. Ranks the archetype's ceiling as Maurice Jones-Drew ("prime real estate"), Ahmad Bradshaw next, DeAndre Swift as "the mid-rent place," and places Vidal's likely outcome somewhere between Ahmad Bradshaw and Swift.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): extends the [[DeAndre Swift]] comp again — most fantasy GMs equate Swift with pure production rather than seeing him as a scheme-and-circumstance-dependent player with real flaws, and what they're "deludedly" projecting onto Swift is actually a more accurate description of Vidal, who has better decision-making, more versatility, better acceleration, tackle-breaking and pass-pro skills, and needs work only as a receiver. Swift's edge over Vidal for now is draft capital, not talent. [[Brandon Angelo]], same episode: rates Vidal highly among this class's backs for playing an efficient brand of football — one of few in the class with high-end reps you have to rewind — more efficient than dynamic compared to sexier runners like [[Marshawn Lloyd]] and [[Trey Benson]], who Angelo says could reach Vidal's current level in a few years if they develop. Notes the market gap: Lloyd likely gets drafted round two, Vidal pushed to round five or six, purely on draft-capital perception rather than tape.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]): restates the market gap in blunter terms — "pound for pound, Kimani Vidal is a better running back than [[Marshawn Lloyd]] is," a read he thinks anyone who watches and understands the position will agree with, but draft capital will tell a different story: Vidal probably a fourth-, fifth-, or sixth-round pick with a short leash, versus Lloyd as a likely second-rounder and possibly the first back off the board with a longer leash purely because of that capital. See [[League Trend Cycles and Market Inefficiency]].
- 2024-05-02 — According to Brandon Angelo and Matt Waldman on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]: both hosts rate Kimani Vidal as one of the more underrated backs in the class after day-three draft capital didn't change their evaluation. Waldman has him 17th on his post-draft board while ADP across nine mocks/drafts he tracked ranged from pick 27 to 41, averaging roughly the 34th pick (a 12-team third-rounder) — a 17-spot gap he called huge. Angelo praised Vidal as an every-down back: 'short but not small,' able to run through and carry piles, an adequate pass catcher who's good in both gap and zone schemes, can make people miss, and is a solid pass protector if he keeps his head up more. Both compared him to a more in-control version of [[DeAndre Swift]]. The Chargers adding J.K. Dobbins is seen as a stopgap, since incumbent Isaiah Spiller 'still hasn't figured it out' and Gus Edwards is scheme-dependent on Greg Roman; Waldman said he wouldn't be surprised if Vidal leads the room in carries as soon as next year.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is buying Vidal as a cheap dynasty investment behind a shallow Chargers backfield — J.K. Dobbins has a poor injury track record, Gus Edwards is more productive than his 'not a true feature back' reputation suggests but is aging, and Isaiah Spiller hasn't shown much, with the team also drafting another back to push Vidal. Waldman comps Vidal's size to Austin Ekeler (maybe a touch bigger), and frames him as a low-cost, low-risk dynasty stash worth taking a shot on given the muddled depth chart ahead of him.
