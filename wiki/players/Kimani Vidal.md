# Kimani Vidal

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name as "Kamani Vidal"; normalized here to the real name of the Troy
> running back, Kimani Vidal.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]) [Dynasty]: named as a mid-to-late-pick who could become a viable committee contributor if he survives the perception attached to his likely Day 3 draft capital, avoids mistakes in camp, and earns real opportunity. Profile: 5'7", ~215 lbs, a powerful runner with a low center of gravity and real movement skill, plus decent decision-making on tape. A standout in Senior Bowl pass-protection reps specifically — both hosts flag it as a real "earned his stripes" moment. [[Matt Waldman]], same episode: agrees the low center of gravity and movement skills are genuine translatable traits, and floats a fourth-round outcome as plausible given how he could be viewed once teams see the tape.

- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]) [Dynasty]: reaffirms he has Vidal "as a top 10 running back" in the 2024 class, while noting he was still surprised by Vidal's 4.46 combine 40 time given that ranking. See [[NFL Combine and Pro Day Skepticism]] for Waldman's broader process for weighing an unexpectedly fast workout number.
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: praises Vidal's conservative, reliable decision-making (avoids bouncing/cutting back plays coaches want kept in structure) and blitz-pickup skill — moves incrementally well to influence a blitzer's angle and dissuade him before contact. Sharpest line: people whose "hopes and dreams" are tied up in [[DeAndre Swift]] are "unknowingly projecting much of Vidal's actual game" onto their idea of what Swift is, since Swift "isn't actually all that they see" — Vidal "has a chance to be" that quality player.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]] [Dynasty]: extends the [[DeAndre Swift]] comparison from 2024-03-23 with a real-estate analogy — Vidal is "essentially what people see when they look at DeAndre Swift but are wrong about," at a smaller school with likely low draft capital who'll have to prove himself more. Ranks the archetype's ceiling as Maurice Jones-Drew ("prime real estate"), Ahmad Bradshaw next, DeAndre Swift as "the mid-rent place," and places Vidal's likely outcome somewhere between Ahmad Bradshaw and Swift.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): extends the [[DeAndre Swift]] comp again — most fantasy GMs equate Swift with pure production rather than seeing him as a scheme-and-circumstance-dependent player with real flaws, and what they're "deludedly" projecting onto Swift is actually a more accurate description of Vidal, who has better decision-making, more versatility, better acceleration, tackle-breaking and pass-pro skills, and needs work only as a receiver. Swift's edge over Vidal for now is draft capital, not talent. [[Brandon Angelo]], same episode: rates Vidal highly among this class's backs for playing an efficient brand of football — one of few in the class with high-end reps you have to rewind — more efficient than dynamic compared to sexier runners like [[Marshawn Lloyd]] and [[Trey Benson]], who Angelo says could reach Vidal's current level in a few years if they develop. Notes the market gap: Lloyd likely gets drafted round two, Vidal pushed to round five or six, purely on draft-capital perception rather than tape.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]): restates the market gap in blunter terms — "pound for pound, Kimani Vidal is a better running back than [[Marshawn Lloyd]] is," a read he thinks anyone who watches and understands the position will agree with, but draft capital will tell a different story: Vidal probably a fourth-, fifth-, or sixth-round pick with a short leash, versus Lloyd as a likely second-rounder and possibly the first back off the board with a longer leash purely because of that capital. See [[League Trend Cycles and Market Inefficiency]].
- 2024-05-02 — According to Brandon Angelo and Matt Waldman on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]: both hosts rate Kimani Vidal as one of the more underrated backs in the class after day-three draft capital didn't change their evaluation. Waldman has him 17th on his post-draft board while ADP across nine mocks/drafts he tracked ranged from pick 27 to 41, averaging roughly the 34th pick (a 12-team third-rounder) — a 17-spot gap he called huge. Angelo praised Vidal as an every-down back: 'short but not small,' able to run through and carry piles, an adequate pass catcher who's good in both gap and zone schemes, can make people miss, and is a solid pass protector if he keeps his head up more. Both compared him to a more in-control version of [[DeAndre Swift]]. The Chargers adding J.K. Dobbins is seen as a stopgap, since incumbent Isaiah Spiller 'still hasn't figured it out' and Gus Edwards is scheme-dependent on Greg Roman; Waldman said he wouldn't be surprised if Vidal leads the room in carries as soon as next year.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is buying Vidal as a cheap dynasty investment behind a shallow Chargers backfield — J.K. Dobbins has a poor injury track record, Gus Edwards is more productive than his 'not a true feature back' reputation suggests but is aging, and Isaiah Spiller hasn't shown much, with the team also drafting another back to push Vidal. Waldman comps Vidal's size to Austin Ekeler (maybe a touch bigger), and frames him as a low-cost, low-risk dynasty stash worth taking a shot on given the muddled depth chart ahead of him.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: everyone's preseason darling, but 'fuck them' in redraft — a waiver-wire watch-list name, not a draft pick. Daniel Popper's report in The Athletic that made people dismissive of Vidal matches what Waldman has heard from day one: Joe Reedy has told him three times that Vidal is a special teams guy. Waldman's framing is that neither Vidal nor [[Dillon Johnson]] will get the majority of reps, but both have the talent to produce if called upon, so they belong on a short watch list rather than a draft board. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) adds the list of comparable watch-list league-winners — Olandis Gary, Mike Anderson, Domanick Davis, Steve Slaton, Peyton Hillis, Arian Foster, Justin Forsett, Spencer Ware, C.J. Anderson, David Johnson, Alex Collins, [[Cordarrelle Patterson]].
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Going Deep)]]) [Dynasty]: interesting but capped. Angelo expects Jim Harbaugh — with Greg Roman pushing too — to take a back inside the first 40 picks of the strong 2025 class, naming [[Ollie Gordon II]] as the Harbaugh dream fit, because [[J.K. Dobbins]] and [[Gus Edwards]] are both near the tail end of their careers. Vidal is a nice fit and could push for a role if he sees the field enough this year, but Angelo does not think he is dynamic enough to hold a lead job or keep Harbaugh away from an early-round back. In a live keeper-league decision Angelo ranked [[Dylan Laube]] and [[Andrei Iosivas]] ahead of Vidal on starter upside, and [[Matt Waldman]] took Laube and Iosivas.
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]): an explicit reversal — Waldman calls it his 'anti-victory lap,' having been dismissive of Vidal because Hassan Haskins was ahead of him and there was no path to touches. With [[Gus Edwards]] on IR for four weeks there is now a path, and Vidal is easily his pick as the [[J.K. Dobbins]] handcuff after four carries for 11 yards plus a 40-yard touchdown on a wheel route. He describes a short but well-built runner who breaks tackles, looked strong on short-yardage carries, ran well outside, showed burst and caught the ball fine. What impressed him most, as it did at Troy, is pass protection — one rep taking a defensive end all the way around the quarterback with great position and footwork, offset by a missed cut block on Nik Bonitto that Waldman blames on a bad situation rather than his technique. Haskins keeps a special teams edge; Waldman would take a share in deep leagues.
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): 'feeling it' on Vidal as a future starter, but only just — he is 'on that Mason-Dixon line.' There is enough there to call him a future starter, yet Waldman gets worried by Tim Hightower vibes, meaning a future journeyman who starts for parts of seasons rather than a locked-in lead back. What he wants to see before going further: Vidal breaking a run for more than seven yards.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]): in a feel-one/fuck-one/ignore-one between Vidal, [[Dylan Laube]] and [[Marcus Yarns]], Waldman comes down on Vidal as the 'fuck it', saying he will continue slandering Vidal until the day he dies unless Vidal proves him wrong — which he says he is eager for. Bob Harris (not tracked) counters that he still holds Vidal shares, though if forced to choose he would go where Waldman went.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the No. 1 Week 6 waiver add for both Harris and guest Brandon Funston of The Athletic (not tracked), with [[Omarion Hampton]] on IR and the Chargers down two starting running backs. Funston's case: Vidal played ahead of Hassan Haskins last year, is the better passing-game back and receptions pay the bills, so he has the higher ceiling — but he isn't trusted in pass protection, which matters badly with the Chargers' tackles out, and Vidal appeared alongside Trey Lance rather than the starters. Funston expects a platoon or hot hand, recommends a contingency bid of roughly $15 in a 12-team league dropping the same player, and says he isn't getting rich here. Harris ordered them the same way and adds a caveat on environment: an offense that looked awesome for three weeks has lost three tackles and blew a lead because it couldn't protect at all.
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-13]]): Miami is bad, but Vidal is better than his labels — Waldman is a defender, while cautioning he is not as good as this week's line of 18 carries for 124 yards (nearly seven per carry), three catches for 14 and a receiving touchdown. He liked Vidal as a rookie sleeper alongside JJ Zachariason (not tracked), comping him to [[DeAndre Swift]] with better decision-making and more genuine burst, since Swift's long speed outruns his burst and he does not always reach the third level. Vidal profits from the Chargers' intact interior trio on gap plays, less so on zone, though the Chargers run mostly gap. Waldman blamed the lost path on [[J.K. Dobbins]] staying healthy, rookie-year sporadic touches, the [[Najee Harris]]/[[Omarion Hampton]] additions, mixed camp reports, and Jim Harbaugh's outmoded three-yards-and-a-cloud-of-dust taste in backs, arguing Harbaugh needs Kyle Shanahan's rule of not knowing what you have until live game action. Bob Harris (not tracked) said [[Hassan Haskins]] could have gained half those yards on one foot against Miami.
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Chris Raybon (guest, not a tracked expert; [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-13]]): Vidal broke out with roughly 100 rushing yards on 43 snaps against a bottom-ranked Dolphins run defense, an 'Ekeler-esque' outing that flipped the expected Chargers backfield hierarchy after reports had favored [[Hassan Haskins]]. Raybon was skeptical the role holds given a crowded, unpredictable Chargers offense that spreads touches across many receivers and tight ends -- he said he's 'trying to sell' Vidal now while his value is high, and Harris agreed selling makes sense given the matchup-inflated numbers.
- 2025-10-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Jake Seeley (The Athletic, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-14 (Week 7 Waiver Wire & More W6 Film)]]): Vidal is the #1 Week 7 waiver target for both hosts. Both had trusted a bad pre-game report suggesting Hassan Haskins would lead the backfield and avoided both Chargers and Cardinals backfields as unusable -- costing them Vidal, who then looked, per Harris, comparable to [[Austin Ekeler]] and outplayed the doubts about his pass-blocking. Seeley uses Vidal to make a broader point that pass-blocking is overweighted as a reason to bench a rookie back given how little even elite pass-blocking prospects (he cites [[TreVeyon Henderson]]) actually play. Both project Vidal as a weekly RB2/3 going forward barring a trade for another back, and recommend an aggressive FAAB bid -- Seeley suggests 30-40%+ of remaining budget, even up to 50%, given how thin the running back pool is right now.
- 2025-10-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]], with guest Dave Kluge of FootballGuys.com (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-16 (Week 7 Flex Decisions)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: after Kimani Vidal was the show's No. 1 waiver add and posted 124 total yards (72 before contact) on 18 carries against a bad Miami run defense, both hosts were reluctant to credit the talent. Kluge said he 'couldn't draw anything' from the tape, joking Vidal was 'just running through holes you could back an 18-wheeler into,' and ranked him RB25; Harris had him RB27, agreeing he's essentially a replacement-level back elevated by a good Chargers offensive line and scheme (Kluge compared his size/style to Austin Ekeler). Both flagged a tougher Colts matchup this week and warned it's possible the Chargers lean on power/kick-out runs that could tip some work back to [[Hassan Haskins]]. Neither is advocating benching a real player for Vidal off one good game against a bad defense.
- 2025-10-24 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: is skeptical of reading too much into Kimani Vidal's big Thursday-night game (127 scrimmage yards against the Vikings, up from just 35 against the Colts the prior week), noting the broadcast crew's gushing over Vidal ignored that he wasn't even part of the Chargers' plans to start the season and still wears a uniform number (30) that could be swapped for Austin Ekeler's without anyone noticing -- but the current Chargers offense isn't built to feature one cornerstone back the way Ekeler's old role was. Harris likes Vidal's archetype (short but not small, a 'thumper' with good shifting ability) and says the performance gets him 'further into starting consideration,' but stops short of calling him a locked-in top-15 rest-of-season option yet.
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-27]]) [Redraft]: Waldman leaned 'fuck it,' essentially 50/50, on [[Kimani Vidal]] locking down a co-starter role long-term -- he thinks the Chargers are more 'stuck with' him than sold on him as a talent, but conceded that once he's in rhythm he can play well, something they didn't see from him last year. He framed it as an opportunity-and-fit story rather than a talent breakout, and put roughly 50-50 odds that [[Najee Harris]], on a one-year deal, isn't brought back next year, which would open a clearer path for Vidal to start.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): a really nice performance that 'feels so much like the Austin Ekeler days where you know he's not as good as the numbers and he's a passenger more than he's a driver' — the line has to open it up for him, and it did in the second half. Guest Jamie Eisenberg of CBS Fantasy (not tracked) calls it an up-and-down five-game stretch, three good and two bad, and admits he would have sat Vidal if he had a better option; he treats him as a mid-tier RB2, notes the Chargers barely throw to him, and says plainly 'we overhyped him as a prospect' — the plan is to hand the job back to [[Omarion Hampton]] when he returns from injury.
- 2025-11-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-14]]) [Redraft], his #2 trade-away this deadline special: Vidal is coming off a big week (108 total yards and a TD two weeks ago, meh last week) and gets Jacksonville this week in a good matchup, but Harris says the looming return of Omarion Hampton is 'hanging over' his value and he wants out now. Josh pushed back that Vidal's owner won't get much for a 'one-week-on, one-week-off' back and floated only a 'ponies for a horse' return like a Troy Franklin add-on, joking he'd rather trade for George Kittle (FantasyPros ranks Vidal 87th, Kittle 69th) than take Vidal off Harris's hands; Harris didn't take that deal, calling it too rich for a bye-week decision.
- 2025-12-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-08]]): With [[Omarion Hampton]] returning for the Monday night game against the Eagles, Harris expects Vidal's fantasy relevance to fade quickly, though he stopped short of guaranteeing a timeline for the transition.
- 2025-12-09 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Vidal started and out-snapped Omarion Hampton 49-22 (16-3 on third downs), and made the game's big third-down conversion, but struggled badly in pass protection -- asked to block 14 times and repeatedly beaten by N'Kobe Dean. Harris expects Vidal to remain close to Hampton in the rankings for Week 15, creating an annoying flex call for managers rostering either back.
- 2025-12-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-15]]): Vidal started against Kansas City but the touch count between him and [[Omarion Hampton]] is now 'far more equal' (39 snaps to Hampton's 23), which Harris again attributes to Vidal's role as the pass-blocking back in obvious passing situations for a Chargers offense that was down multiple linemen.
- 2025-12-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-26]]): Vidal has not practiced because of a neck injury, and Harris said he took him out of his running back ranks entirely.
- 2026-01-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-05]]) [Dynasty]: floated as a candidate to get another chance to prove he can handle the zone-runner complementary role next to [[Omarion Hampton]], in a Chargers backfield Waldman sees as needing exactly that skill set based on what [[Najee Harris]] provided this year.
- 2026-07-09 — According to James Koh (co-host, not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-09]]): in a Chargers backfield he calls a deep trio, Vidal remains the more likely true complementary/passing-down back behind [[Omarion Hampton]] over [[Keaton Mitchell]], comparing the group's roles to Baltimore's, with Vidal in the 'Justice Hill' part.
- 2026-07-24 — According to Andy Behrens (untracked co-host) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Behrens calls Vidal's role 'the legitimate big variable' in the Chargers backfield -- for anyone fully bought into [[Omarion Hampton]], Vidal's camp usage is the thing to track, since meaningful work for Vidal would cut into Hampton's share rather than [[Keaton Mitchell]]'s already-defined role. Neither host expects real clarity here before Week 1.
- 2026-09-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-09-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris pushed back on calling the Chargers backfield a clean 'two-headed monster' around [[Omarion Hampton]], noting Vidal 'is standing right over there waving hello' and was one of the five most important players to the Chargers making the 2025 playoffs -- reason to be cautious projecting a clean workload for either Hampton or an injured [[Keaton Mitchell]].
