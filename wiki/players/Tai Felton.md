# Tai Felton

## Expert Takes

- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]) [Dynasty]: asked to disabuse Bob Harris (untracked) of the appeal of Felton's 4.37 forty at 6-1, 183, Waldman calls him the most frustrating receiver he is studying. From release through stem to break point Felton looks like a future primary receiver — then the ball arrives and you don't know what you'll get. He lists essentially every catch-technique failure at once: clap attacks, using the wrong attack for the ball's trajectory, allowing the ball into his frame rather than attacking at its earliest point, failing to earn ideal position, letting the ball through his hands, and focus drops. Any two of those would stall development. His plan is to root for Felton but hope he falls to the end of a rookie draft, stash him on a big roster for 12 to 18 months, and cut bait if there is no improvement.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): the Maryland receiver is a high-ceiling prospect with a trapdoor leading to a deep drop. He is speedy with a lot of route-running skill and a wide array of release maneuvers and setups, and needs to refine the smaller details so everything executes with precision and believability. Waldman is explicit that the refinement is not the trapdoor — the trapdoor is all the different ways Felton knows how to drop a football, and that is the thing he has to fix. See [[Catch Technique and Ball Tracking]].
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21 (Film & Data with Dwain McFarland)]]): Felton 'should be one of the best three receivers in this class based on how he runs routes' — but Waldman drops him because of his hands, saying Felton finds every possible way to drop a ball and that it is a bad habit rather than a lapse, which in Waldman's framework means it needs fixing at the next level rather than just refocusing. He used Felton as the extreme reference point when discussing [[Kyle Williams]]' catch issues.
- 2025-04-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-29]]): Felton going with the last pick of round three was earlier than Harmon expected, ahead of day-three guys he ranked higher such as [[Jalen Royals]] and [[Elic Ayomanor]]. Profile: 6-1, 183 with a 4.37, 73% of Maryland snaps outside and 51.7% on the line but reps at both X and flanker on both sides of the field, 96 catches in 2024 (top five among FBS pass catchers), 59th-percentile vs man and 80.2% vs zone, good separation on slants, curls, flats and outs, and real after-catch ability (broke a single tackle on 37% of in-space attempts, multiple on 13%). The problems are that the timed speed does not translate to vertical separation and the catch point is a real issue — poor contested catch rate and six drops in the RP sample, with games where drops snowballed, the same flaw that pushed Ayomanor to round four. Harmon projects a speed slot and low-volume role, comparing the usage to Brandon Powell as a pet player of this coaching tree, with possible kick-return work; both hosts agree Minnesota needed a third-receiver upgrade over [[Jalen Nailor]], whose contract is up.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Harmon says he has warmed on the pick the more he has thought about it. His initial reaction was that Minnesota, short on picks and already holding [[Justin Jefferson]] and [[Jordan Addison]], did not need another receiver — but he prefers a team adding a dimension to one sitting on its hands, an explicit contrast with Atlanta and Arizona, who he says simply ran their offenses back. He does not project Felton as a future star, but thinks he brings run-after-catch ability Addison does not, and was good on screens — useful when the priority is getting [[J.J. McCarthy]] up and running. Koh (not tracked) cites the measurables: 6-foot-1, 183, third round out of Maryland, 4.37 with a 39.5-inch vertical.
- 2025-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-18]]): Harmon and Koh were both a little surprised Felton went as early as the third round, and while they like him, Harmon reads his fit as a slot role rather than the outside 11-personnel piece Minnesota actually needs while [[Jordan Addison]] is suspended — which is part of why he expects the Vikings to lean run-heavy early in 2025.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Felton, the last pick of the third round, went a little higher than Harmon expected but interests him as a possible replacement for what Minnesota wanted from [[Rondale Moore]], who is out for the season with another preseason injury. His charting shows great success rates on short routes — curls, slants, flats, comebacks — plus a good out route and an 80.2% success rate vs zone, but nothing downfield, terrible contested-catch numbers and real drop issues. He is 6-1, 183 pounds, ran a 4.37 and caught 96 passes in his final college season. Harmon's pitch is a get-the-ball-in-his-hands, break-tackles-after-the-catch role that could add the singles and doubles Minnesota lacked in a 2024 offense built on home runs and triples. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) counters that those are cheap yards you cannot sustain drives with. Harmon calls him a very deep name to keep an eye on.
- 2026-01-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-26]]) [Dynasty]: on the border between feel it and fuck it. Felton runs good routes, has nice speed, and is skilled after the catch, but Waldman says the catch point has often been 'a carnival' for him -- comping him to early-career Diontae Johnson, though he sees Felton as a more promising route runner than Johnson was at the same stage. He projects as Minnesota's number-three or -four receiver at best, complicated further by [[Jordan Addison]]'s reported off-field issues and Minnesota's unresolved quarterback situation.
- 2026-02-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-16]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman says Felton's realistic ceiling is 'Addison-like,' but flags a real catch-point issue in his game -- comping the specific flaw to what [[Garrett Nussmeier]] deals with at quarterback -- that creates 'a bigger trap door to his floor' than his route-running talent and explosiveness would otherwise suggest.
- 2026-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-19]]) [Dynasty]: Felton 'didn't really show much' as a rookie, playing behind pending free agent Jalen Nailor and veteran Adam Thielen (who was later traded to Carolina and finished the year with Pittsburgh). Harmon says Minnesota drafted him aggressively for a late-third-rounder -- he had him valued more like a late-fourth/fifth option, defensible mainly as a get-the-ball-in-space, run-after-catch third receiver. He's not ruling out a Felton breakout given Minnesota's downfield-oriented, well-designed passing scheme and its need for a player who can create after the catch (a role neither Addison nor Jefferson fills), but isn't putting a real bet on it happening, noting it comes back to who plays quarterback in 2026.
