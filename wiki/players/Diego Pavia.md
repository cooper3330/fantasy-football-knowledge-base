# Diego Pavia

## Expert Takes

- 2026-04-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-13]]) [Dynasty]: talent-wise, Pavia absolutely 'can' become a top-three quarterback in this draft class -- but 'will he?' gets a firm no, because NFL teams appear to be surfacing character/makeup concerns about him and Waldman doesn't know how much weight to put on those reports given how inconsistent teams' interview evaluation processes are. On tape, calls Pavia's processing speed very strong and his play aggressive and physical for his 5'10', 207-lb size, joking he plays like he thinks he's a fullback at the end of SEC runs. Draws a Baker Mayfield attitude comp but more extreme -- 'where Baker was drinking wine spritzers, Diego's doing the vodka and Red Bull.' Sees his realistic outcome as a journeyman starter or a high-value backup capable of occasionally ripping a defense open, contingent on a team tolerating the off-field noise.
- 2026-05-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-04]]) [Dynasty]: calls Diego Pavia one of the top five quarterbacks in this class 'outside of the off-field stuff,' framing his three-year Ravens deal -- no guaranteed money, no signing bonus -- as a talent-acknowledging but behavior-contingent contract given how the NFL views his off-field reputation. If Pavia behaves, Waldman says Baltimore lands one of the better backup QBs they've had in a while, with [[Joe Fagnano]] able to develop slowly behind him on the bench as a third option.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-07 (Going Deep with Brandon Angelo)]]) [Dynasty]: Pavia is the last quarterback on Waldman's board he'd actually consider drafting, and he wouldn't take him even in a deep dynasty league -- he'd rather add Pavia off waivers as a free agent later. His read on Baltimore's plan: a three-year deal signals the Ravens see real talent and view him as a possible QB2, but with no guarantees they could move on within six months depending on his off-field behavior. Waldman's full late-quarterback order behind Fernando Mendoza: [[Ty Simpson]] (a good sit-and-develop spot, comped to Jordan Love), then Carson Beck, Garrett Nussmeier and [[Cole Payton]] (all 'no thank you'), then [[Drew Allar]] -- who Waldman argues is a bigger red flag rather than a mitigant, because Penn State's scheme never let him develop full-field reads or a varied route tree needed at the next level -- and finally Pavia as the last name worth knowing.
