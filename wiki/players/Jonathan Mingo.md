# Jonathan Mingo

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: per a Dan Hindery note Harstad cites, Mingo and [[Jayden Reed]] were the *only* two rookie receivers in the 2023 class listed as full-time starters heading into Week 1 — which should have boosted his [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] score. Instead his usage score is one of the highest in the class while his efficiency is "bottom 15," an extreme split driven by a historically bad Carolina passing situation (a rookie quarterback, a fired head coach mid-season, and a veteran backup who didn't play). Waldman: liked Mingo a lot pre-draft but wanted more development time, and is "revising my opinion of him pretty heavily downwards" through the course of the year — not on talent, but because a coaching change usually means the incoming staff drafts its own receiver and leaves the previous regime's rookie behind. Comparable statistical profiles cited: Zay Jones (closest match), Corey Davis, Tajae Sharpe, Darius Heyward-Bay.

- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): reiterates a pre-draft view — the only role Mingo can succeed
  in is a "power slot" role, the same spot [[Adam Thielen]] currently
  occupies for Carolina. Notes the front office that drafted Mingo (Frank
  Reich, since fired) may not be as invested in him under new GM Dan Morgan
  (previously the assistant GM), and it's unclear whether the staff tries to
  find him a role alongside newly added [[Diontae Johnson]] and a likely
  drafted receiver, or simply moves on from him.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): a firmly
  negative update on his rookie-year flanker/outside usage — his 2023 film
  playing off the ball was "as bad" as the other disaster-level rookie
  receivers discussed in the episode, only marginally ahead of [[Quentin Johnston]]
  and [[Marvin Mims Jr.]]. Harmon doesn't think he can consistently beat man
  or press coverage on the outside, and argues his only functional role is the
  "power slot" job [[Adam Thielen]] currently holds — direct disagreement with
  a suggestion (from co-host James Koh) that Mingo could play a flanker role
  off the ball. Ranked behind [[Adam Thielen]] as a "number two" for Carolina.

- 2024-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-04]]), discussing [[Keon Coleman]]'s prospect profile: reiterates Mingo, like Coleman, fits the "poor college separator who needs to become a big slot receiver" archetype, but rates Coleman a better overall prospect than Mingo was a year earlier — Coleman shows more contested-catch ability and after-the-catch juice than Mingo did.
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]): Mingo is drawing positive camp reports and is listed as the WR3 behind [[Diontae Johnson]] and [[Adam Thielen]], ahead of [[Xavier Legette]]. Harmon buys it 'a little bit' — his charting says the rookie year was not as bad as the consensus thinks, better than a [[Quentin Johnston]]-level season, and the realistic outcome is something like [[Josh Palmer]], who was bad as a rookie and is now a fine, non-negative NFL receiver. He liked Mingo as a big-slot candidate and still thinks that beats outside, doubting he will ever be a high-quality target earner as an outside guy; functional is the bar. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is harsher — 43-of-85 for 418 yards, no touchdowns, 9.7 yards per reception, shaky hands and no tackle-breaking — calling him at best a bench player, though he credits Mingo for calling Steve Smith for help this offseason.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]) [Dynasty]: Mingo is a buy-curious name and an example of the 'what have you done for me lately' dynamic that writes off young receivers after one year. Waldman likes the size and tracking ability and says Mingo is fast enough to get downfield on play action and win the ball in tight coverage; what was missing is rapport with the quarterback, being on the same page reading coverage, and added sophistication against top defenders in tight windows — things that take time. Upside comp: a young Anquan Boldin-type presence if it all clicks. He has had a strong camp. The question is whether he can be WR3 there, since Carolina will try to push [[Xavier Legette]] onto the field — but Waldman asks whether Legette can stay healthy and be more than a schemed-up screen/RPO/fade player. He would trade for Mingo on good early signs.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman is feeling Mingo as the second-year player people wrote off too fast, and the reason is coachability rather than opportunity. Where a lot of young players think they have made it once they reach the NFL, Mingo knew he had a bad rookie year and actively sought out someone who would tell him 'the unvarnished truth' about himself — Steve Smith — and worked with him. Waldman says all you hear out of Panthers camp is that Mingo's routes look much better and that he has had one of the strongest camps among the offensive skill players, with Terrace Marshall sent packing, a better offensive line, and [[Diontae Johnson]] and [[Adam Thielen]] able to threaten defenses on route ability alone. His projection: maybe not a fantasy WR2, but he could threaten WR3 status and is a rosterable player who gives starter-worthy games in two-receiver sets some weeks.
