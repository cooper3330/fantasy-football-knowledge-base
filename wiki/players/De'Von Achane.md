# De'Von Achane

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]]) [Dynasty]: both flag him as a real overrated-in-2024 risk despite a spectacular rookie efficiency profile — "we haven't seen enough from him as a full-time player" to trust the per-touch numbers holding at volume. Harms's mechanism: he's excellent getting to the edge in space in Mike McDaniel's Shanahan-tree scheme, but is a merely good (not great) between-the-tackles runner, and defenses that take away the edge (as Kansas City's run defense did in the frigid Wild Card game, after doing the same to other backs in Germany earlier in the season) neutralize him; comps him to a boom/bust, breakaway-speed-plus-checkdown-volume Travis Etienne 2023 profile rather than a true three-down bell cow. Already battled three separate injuries as a rookie, a durability flag layered on top of the touch-competition question if Miami's other backs (Raheem Mostert, Jeff Wilson) stay involved. Verdict: fine value in the 4th–5th round range, dangerous priced as a top-3-to-top-5 dynasty running back, which both say they've already seen happen in early market chatter. Christopher Brooks flagged as a depth name worth a late-round dynasty look as Miami's Gus Edwards-style contingency if Mostert/Wilson age out.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: "fuck it" on Achane going as a top-10 RB in early best balls — recognizes the talent and offense but calls it "a little bit too much flash," wants to see him stay healthy for a full season before trusting the price, and flags a real touch-competition concern now that Miami gave [[Raheem Mostert]] a $9M extension through 2025, confirming Mostert will play a central role in 2024. At RB10 you're passing on a top receiver or a back with a more secure full-time role. Also flags [[Christopher Brooks]] over Jeff Wilson (not tracked) as the real "third guy" in Miami's backfield.
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Achane is his RB7 against an RB11 market ADP -- roughly a full round of value. He projects about a 40% rush share (up from ~35% last year) despite Jaylen Wright's presence, arguing Miami's draft pick was 'more of a Raheem Mostert thing than an Achane thing,' with Wright more likely an injury-contingent dart throw than a real threat to Achane's role. If Achane's rush share climbs to 50% -- which 20 NFL backs hit last year, not a rarity -- JJ projects him as a top-6 fantasy RB. He grouped Achane with Jahmyr Gibbs as backs who thrive on efficiency rather than pure volume, meaning a committee partner (Mostert, David Montgomery) matters less as long as Achane stays efficient in what he called a 'perfect scenario' Mike McDaniel system for his skill set.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): the hosts disagree on the regression, not the player. Guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) is 'in' on Achane, projecting 5.0 yards per carry down from 7.9 and arguing good players regress to a career average rather than the 3.8 league mean (comp: Jamaal Charles). Waldman projects 6.0. McFarland is deliberately conservative on workload — 41% of attempts and 47% of routes versus 40%/53% in games with [[Raheem Mostert]] last year — and that still yields RB13 at a round 2-3 turn price; at 50% of carries and 55% of routes he becomes RB4, and the NFL-average lead back gets 57% of carries. Waldman had been worried about training-room time but says Achane's frame can carry more weight (comps [[Austin Ekeler]], Terrell Davis, Clinton Portis) and that Mike McDaniel saying Achane is forcing their hand moves him to adjust upward.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): Waldman is warming to Achane as Miami's lead back on the added weight and the staff's stated intent to find more ways to get him on the field, but drew a sharp distinction — Achane will lead the backfield in fantasy production, not in touches. He expects [[Raheem Mostert]] or one of [[Jaylen Wright]], Jeff Wilson, [[Christopher Brooks]] or Salvon Ahmed to absorb the north-south role while Achane gets the east-west open-space work, making him the rare high-efficiency-per-touch outlier who produces without the snap count fantasy managers usually demand. He does expect the split to be closer than last year. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) disagreed on price, calling the RB7-to-RB8 second-round cost an overstatement and preferring Mostert in the seventh or eighth round.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]) [Dynasty]: Achane is a player he is actively changing his mind on and moving up his board — he says he nudged Achane slightly above [[Raheem Mostert]] over the weekend. Last year Mostert was 'Mr. Inside' and Achane 'Mr. Outside', running draws and the [[DeAndre Swift]]-style open-field specials Miami manufactures with motion and condensed looks to create kick-return lanes through big gaps; Waldman now expects Miami to give Achane more traditional interior work too, producing closer to an even split with Mostert, roles included, in the mold of [[Tony Pollard]] and [[Tyjae Spears]]. Caveat: the volume will not resemble [[Derrick Henry]], [[Saquon Barkley]] or [[Brian Robinson Jr.]], but he still expects a valuable fantasy asset.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): Achane was fantastic and looked like the player people were drafting. The caution is on return on investment — Waldman says neither Achane nor [[Raheem Mostert]] is going to pay off what was spent, and that Achane, taken at roughly second-round draft capital, would have to make an incredible run from here to return that value, whereas Mostert has the better chance of returning his cost. He also grants Achane is the more creative runner of the two, with Mostert the savvier.
- 2024-12-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-12]]): Miami has no business being bottom-three in neutral pass rate alongside Philadelphia and Green Bay, because unlike those two its run game does not justify it — Harmon pointed to Achane, the team's lead back, averaging 3.9 yards per carry, and said Miami should simply open the passing game up the rest of the season given its receiver talent. A brief aside within a broader segment on run-heavy teams with good receivers.
- 2024-12-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-17]]): Achane is averaging 3.9 yards per carry ([[Raheem Mostert]] 3.4), and Harmon argues the heavy dose of designed screens and manufactured touches in the passing game exists precisely *because* Miami's run game is terrible — those touches function as extensions of the run game rather than as a receiving role by choice. James Koh (not a tracked expert) said he loves the design work for Achane but thinks Miami is spamming it, at the cost of drop-back passing to [[Tyreek Hill]] and [[Jaylen Waddle]]. Koh also placed Achane, with [[Jonnu Smith]], as too often the first read in the progression.
- 2025-05-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-19]]): Achane at RB7 ADP is a fair price. Waldman explains the 2023 hype (7.8 yards per carry, but only 11 games and about 100 rushes, aided by how Miami's offense stressed defenses) and why the drop to 4.5 YPC in 2024 is not a red flag — defenses adjusted, 4.5 is still very strong, and he more than doubled essentially everything he did in the passing game while playing all 17 games behind a beat-up line Miami has since addressed. His comp is [[Austin Ekeler]], slightly lighter, with more receiving volume: at 5-9 and 180-190 pounds Miami will always limit his between-the-tackles work and he will not close games like [[Derrick Henry]], but Waldman sees a proven top-10 back worth roughly 1,500 total yards and says he is good to go in any format, best in PPR.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Achane sits with [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] at the top of the 'weapon back' pyramid — a back who genuinely alters defensive spacing from the slot or out wide — but his 2024 receiving usage was historic and partly a function of Miami's quarterback situation, which Angelo said got downplayed, including the [[Tua Tagovailoa]] absence. He therefore expects Achane to 'help others more than himself' in 2025 and to be the sacrificial lamb more than fantasy managers would like, while still anchoring an outside-zone-based run game. The named beneficiary is [[Jaylen Waddle]], whom Angelo expects to get more looks and to overtake [[Tyreek Hill]]. On the positive side, Angelo listed Achane at roughly 195 pounds and argued that profile could play a decade because he plays in space rather than absorbing interior punishment [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-13]]): Achane going as a first-round pick in practice (Footballguys ADP tool lists 14) is his number four surprise, paired with [[Bucky Irving]] — 'I love Irving, I don't love Achane.' Harris's objection is that the market races to the top with these small backs while pumping the brakes on every other running back over committee risk, assuming Achane will hold roughly 80% of the workload when he doubts the team will use him that way. He reads Achane's 2024 the same way he reads [[Brock Bowers]]: production that was the byproduct of a truly unhealthy Dolphins offense in the second half of the season, not a repeatable role. He stops short of calling him a bust, saying he is not positive which of the two he would fade.
- 2025-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris grants Achane elite change of direction and jitterbug quickness but calls him 'indisputably tiny boned,' which is why he wants [[Jaylen Wright]] rostered as an alternative, and suspects [[Jaylen Wright]] may actually have better vision. On price he is openly negative: Achane is the one Dolphin immune to the market's Miami discount, drafted in the early second and sometimes the first after being a late-first pick last year, and Harris does not love him there because his production leaned on 'the junk diet of 100 horrible checkdowns' that the market is assuming simply continues. Guest Sigmund Bloom (Footballguys, not tracked) agreed Achane is a good candidate to absorb some of the cheap PPR volume vacated by [[Jonnu Smith]], but framed the whole Miami offense as a car teetering on a cliff edge.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Achane suffered a lower-body pulled muscle; Mike McDaniel said he will be out 'between days and weeks' but is not concerned about Week 1 availability. Harris has no reason to think McDaniel is lying and assumes Achane plays the opener, but calls it a red flag, and uses it to needle the first-round Achane argument built on youth — that taking him over [[Josh Jacobs]], [[Jonathan Taylor]] or [[Derrick Henry]] is safe because he is young and will therefore stay healthy. Separately, guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert) said Achane was the one miss on his 2024 bust list, fine in the first half before going bananas in the second, and defends him against the same touchdown-regression case he makes on [[James Cook]]: Achane is a positive precisely because Miami is not running a real offense, and he caught 70 passes.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Dr. Deepak Chona of SportsMedAnalytics (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]] (ASR: 'Devin A-Chan'): Miami's positive messaging on Achane's calf strain cannot be taken at face value — as the Niners showed last year, it is not in a team's interest to tell the truth — so the objective signal is return-to-practice timing. The average calf strain takes two to three weeks, most players return without much of a dip, and if the severity really is mild as Mike McDaniel said, you would expect about 90 percent by week one; Chona called a reported three-week absence (CBS, and more projection than sourced report) a little bold, since most calf strains do not take six weeks to resolve, and the most common outcome still has Achane playing week one. He would not expect to see him in preseason or practice until close to week one prep, but limited practice work then means he plays. The counterweight: the more severe a calf strain, the higher the re-injury risk, and with a young explosive player you stay conservative to avoid an Achilles rupture (the Tyrese Haliburton pathway). He believes the diagnosis itself given the acute in-game mechanism — a Christian McCaffrey-style rebranding of tendinitis as a calf issue is possible in theory but unlikely here — and said the real unknown is severity grade.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): Harris went under 875.5 rushing yards and said flatly that he does not have anything like a first-round fantasy grade on Achane — not even a second-round grade — calling him a really fun, exciting, but flawed player. Guest Chris Raybon (Action Network, not tracked) also took the under, projecting roughly 777 yards even using his standard median assumption that a running back misses about two and a half games; Achane is already starting the year hurt, the offensive line is a big concern, and Achane needs splash plays that defenses will now be ready for and the line cannot manufacture. Raybon was broadly down on Miami, taking their under 7.5 wins on 'bad vibes, bad talent'.
- 2025-08-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the calf injury that has cost Achane a couple of weeks of camp is being called precautionary by the Dolphins, who say he will play Week 1. Harris passes the news along without pushback. The related note is that [[Jaylen Wright]] will not play Week 1 and Jeff Wilson has landed back in Miami after being cut by the 49ers — Harris says Wilson may be active but you would not start him, and the only real reason to care is tracking who the handcuff behind Achane actually is, something he suggests only matters in very deep or Super League formats.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): Harris rates Achane an 8 out of 10 — 'the world knows that I'm terrified of' him — while recognizing the upside; guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) is right there at 7. The stated risks are the calf injury, an offense that may not want him on the goal line with [[Ollie Gordon II]] around, and above all Bell's structural argument: the thing that produced Achane's fantasy value, like [[Jonnu Smith]]'s, was a broken offense built on three-yard passes because the quarterback could not throw it 12 yards downfield. If Miami schemes up a competent NFL offense, those touches do not exist — so drafting Achane is asking for the offense to be incompetent again. Harris says that is exactly how he feels, while allowing it could still go right with big plays as it did two years ago.
- 2025-09-01 — According to Bob Harris (co-host, NOT a tracked expert) on ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]): Achane is scheduled to practice this week but nothing has been seen yet, and Harris urges caution — he invokes [[Christian McCaffrey]] at this point last year, good to go until he wasn't, while explicitly declining to draw a one-to-one comparison. His concern is the silence: until something is actually seen on the field, investors should be watching out. [[Matt Waldman]] did not dispute the framing, and both hosts used the uncertainty as the setup for discussing [[Ollie Gordon II]] as the only healthy back on Miami's active roster.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-04]]): Achane wins the 'player you simply don't want to think about anymore' Huggy. Harris says he doesn't know the Dolphins' plans, doesn't know whether Achane's barking calf will be an issue, and notes that everyone predicting a Miami disaster makes him inclined to believe it won't be. His own position is split and he states both halves: he doesn't think Achane is that great a player even though he is an obviously insane athlete, and he had him rated below where he was actually drafted — around the first-round border — but he never put him on any bust, film-room-zero or similar list, because he respects what can go right with that speed and quickness. He is mainly exhausted by the argument and dislikes the bear case resting on certainty that the Dolphins will be terrible. His conclusion is to stop with the Achane takes and just watch what happens.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): Achane 'had a really good day whenever they weren't asking him to run directly up the gut' — Harris says he is still not good at that and it is not his fault, he is not very big. He scored on a red-zone arrow route by dusting Robert Spillane and then showed a nice physical side powering through two defenders, and Miami kept feeding him screens. Harris explicitly retires the summer theory that the screen volume was a symptom of a sick offense: 'they look pretty healthy and you got a lot of screens to Achane.' He nearly won the game on a late catch down the left sideline but had stepped out of bounds — poor situational awareness, though he was cutting and moving. Harris warns the opponent was bad but says this breathes life into Achane, [[Tyreek Hill]] and maybe [[Jaylen Waddle]].
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest Scott Pianowski of Yahoo Fantasy (not a tracked expert) said Miami got Achane far more involved in the passing game this season and that he has been 'fantastic' — on pace for roughly 970 receiving yards — calling him 'dynamite'. Pianowski framed this as Miami playing to [[Tua Tagovailoa]]'s strengths of getting the ball out quickly, which he sees as a structural negative for [[Tyreek Hill]]'s downfield role. Both hosts also noted Achane not stepping out of bounds was the play that decided Miami's win over New England, and Harris flagged that New England's linebacker group being asked to cover Achane out of the backfield was a mismatch.
- 2025-09-19 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-19]]): after charting the Thursday night loss in Buffalo, Achane stays in all lineups even though Miami's offense is no longer the 2023 version — defenses have solved Mike McDaniel's blend of outside zone and horizontal passing, and the offensive line can no longer make the big outside zone runs work. In Miami's conservative mode Achane is a primary target on screens (seven catches), which Harris frames as usage rather than only 'a symptom of a pathetic, suffering offense.' He praised a patient fourth-quarter inside zone run where Achane waited for the line to clear and cut back up the middle instead of bouncing left, and saw less bouncing than in week two versus New England, with only one bounce-out-of-the-hole 'inefficient' rep on the last drive. Caveat: he took heavy punishment for 'such a little guy' — 'tiny bones' — and Miami may hand some of those hits to [[Ollie Gordon II]].
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): Achane is probably the actual biggest beneficiary of the [[Tyreek Hill]] injury, on top of already being valued highly. He touched it 21 times against the Jets and his nine-yard touchdown run was 'a man's run' — two ankle-breaking cuts on the same carry before he powered it in, though Harris grants the tackling was poor. Caveats: a fourth-quarter fumble that Miami recovered, and [[Ollie Gordon II]] occasionally taking red zone work. Harris's broader point is that every Dolphin now has an opponent-dependent ceiling — Panthers next week is a lovely spot, Chargers after less so — but Achane has a nice usage floor underneath that, so if he stays upright you will be happy.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: tough sledding against a Carolina front that was very stout up the middle — 46 scrimmage yards, with Achane 'kind of dashing himself against the rocks'. Harris repeats his physical read on the player: 'he is tiny-boned.' The day was saved by a wonderful red-zone fade touchdown where Achane had to go dead-legged to get his toes inbounds. Verdict: lucky for the touchdown, but we will take it.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): a rare episode of Harris praising Achane's usage rather than complaining about it — 'much less hopeless up the middle stuff' until Miami was killing the clock with a lead. He got outside zone with a crack-seal for a big gain, was split out wide for a red zone screen, and produced two breakaway touchdown runs (a juke past a backup safety, then a pitch left) that Miami ended up needing. Harris also flags that it was [[Jaylen Wright]], not Ollie Gordon II, who worked as the immediate understudy on the second series. His summary of the Dolphins for fantasy: 'all we want out of Miami is Achane and [[Jaylen Waddle]]' — both start in Week 11 against Washington in Spain.
