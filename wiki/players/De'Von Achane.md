# De'Von Achane

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]]) [Dynasty]: both flag him as a real overrated-in-2024 risk despite a spectacular rookie efficiency profile — "we haven't seen enough from him as a full-time player" to trust the per-touch numbers holding at volume. Harms's mechanism: he's excellent getting to the edge in space in Mike McDaniel's Shanahan-tree scheme, but is a merely good (not great) between-the-tackles runner, and defenses that take away the edge (as Kansas City's run defense did in the frigid Wild Card game, after doing the same to other backs in Germany earlier in the season) neutralize him; comps him to a boom/bust, breakaway-speed-plus-checkdown-volume Travis Etienne 2023 profile rather than a true three-down bell cow. Already battled three separate injuries as a rookie, a durability flag layered on top of the touch-competition question if Miami's other backs (Raheem Mostert, Jeff Wilson) stay involved. Verdict: fine value in the 4th–5th round range, dangerous priced as a top-3-to-top-5 dynasty running back, which both say they've already seen happen in early market chatter. Christopher Brooks flagged as a depth name worth a late-round dynasty look as Miami's Gus Edwards-style contingency if Mostert/Wilson age out.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: "fuck it" on Achane going as a top-10 RB in early best balls — recognizes the talent and offense but calls it "a little bit too much flash," wants to see him stay healthy for a full season before trusting the price, and flags a real touch-competition concern now that Miami gave [[Raheem Mostert]] a $9M extension through 2025, confirming Mostert will play a central role in 2024. At RB10 you're passing on a top receiver or a back with a more secure full-time role. Also flags [[Christopher Brooks]] over Jeff Wilson (not tracked) as the real "third guy" in Miami's backfield.
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Achane is his RB7 against an RB11 market ADP -- roughly a full round of value. He projects about a 40% rush share (up from ~35% last year) despite Jaylen Wright's presence, arguing Miami's draft pick was 'more of a Raheem Mostert thing than an Achane thing,' with Wright more likely an injury-contingent dart throw than a real threat to Achane's role. If Achane's rush share climbs to 50% -- which 20 NFL backs hit last year, not a rarity -- JJ projects him as a top-6 fantasy RB. He grouped Achane with Jahmyr Gibbs as backs who thrive on efficiency rather than pure volume, meaning a committee partner (Mostert, David Montgomery) matters less as long as Achane stays efficient in what he called a 'perfect scenario' Mike McDaniel system for his skill set.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): the hosts disagree on the regression, not the player. Guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) is 'in' on Achane, projecting 5.0 yards per carry down from 7.9 and arguing good players regress to a career average rather than the 3.8 league mean (comp: Jamaal Charles). Waldman projects 6.0. McFarland is deliberately conservative on workload — 41% of attempts and 47% of routes versus 40%/53% in games with [[Raheem Mostert]] last year — and that still yields RB13 at a round 2-3 turn price; at 50% of carries and 55% of routes he becomes RB4, and the NFL-average lead back gets 57% of carries. Waldman had been worried about training-room time but says Achane's frame can carry more weight (comps [[Austin Ekeler]], Terrell Davis, Clinton Portis) and that Mike McDaniel saying Achane is forcing their hand moves him to adjust upward.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): Waldman is warming to Achane as Miami's lead back on the added weight and the staff's stated intent to find more ways to get him on the field, but drew a sharp distinction — Achane will lead the backfield in fantasy production, not in touches. He expects [[Raheem Mostert]] or one of [[Jaylen Wright]], Jeff Wilson, [[Christopher Brooks]] or Salvon Ahmed to absorb the north-south role while Achane gets the east-west open-space work, making him the rare high-efficiency-per-touch outlier who produces without the snap count fantasy managers usually demand. He does expect the split to be closer than last year. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) disagreed on price, calling the RB7-to-RB8 second-round cost an overstatement and preferring Mostert in the seventh or eighth round.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]) [Dynasty]: Achane is a player he is actively changing his mind on and moving up his board — he says he nudged Achane slightly above [[Raheem Mostert]] over the weekend. Last year Mostert was 'Mr. Inside' and Achane 'Mr. Outside', running draws and the [[DeAndre Swift]]-style open-field specials Miami manufactures with motion and condensed looks to create kick-return lanes through big gaps; Waldman now expects Miami to give Achane more traditional interior work too, producing closer to an even split with Mostert, roles included, in the mold of [[Tony Pollard]] and [[Tyjae Spears]]. Caveat: the volume will not resemble [[Derrick Henry]], [[Saquon Barkley]] or [[Brian Robinson Jr.]], but he still expects a valuable fantasy asset.
