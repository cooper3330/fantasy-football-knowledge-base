# Dak Prescott

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): "Dak Prescott has always been a good quarterback, and I would say a good quarterback is one that when you give him the talent, he can give you elite production." Frames him as a boom/bust "prevailing wind" player whose public evaluation swings with his supporting cast and coaching, rather than a player whose own level of play actually swings that much. Credits Mike McCarthy's play-calling (after retaking duties from Kellen Moore) as a strong scheme fit, with Prescott himself saying it's the most comfortable he's felt in an offense — framed as a "second chances" story for both player and coach. See [[Scheme vs Talent]].
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]) [Dynasty]: after Dallas's Wild Card blowout loss, frames Dak as having hit a ceiling relative to [[Jordan Love]] — "we have beyond run up on the ceiling here with Dak Prescott" — while still crediting a strong statistical season (led the NFL in touchdowns and completions) and noting the Cowboys face a real contract conversation given his rising cap figure. Distinguishes Dak's bad game from [[Tua Tagovailoa]]'s: "he was bad for him," not "straight up bad" in absolute terms.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: on Dallas not reworking Prescott's contract (a $4M reduction, $59M to $55M, that has constrained their free agency) — says he feels "okay" about it, distinguishing the public perception that Dak is the reason the team hasn't won more from his own read that it isn't really true. Expects pressure to mount toward moving on if Dallas doesn't advance further (with Trey Lance as a depth option), but cautions that moving on from "one of the best" — "a top-10 quarterback" — "to plunge into the unknown" is probably not the best course of action.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]): asked whether Dallas's new coach will have an elite quarterback, Waldman said Prescott can put up elite numbers but is not quite elite — a very good player, comped to Matt Ryan in his heyday, who was capable of elite production because of the strength of the players around him. His read on the Cowboys' frustration is that when everyone is healthy the offense is close to that level but never quite there, so a fan base with high annual expectations keeps having the rug pulled. Bottom line for fantasy purposes: Prescott is 'more than good enough' and does not need to be elite for managers to be excited about him as their quarterback.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: at Underdog QB16 Waldman said he wanted to 'feel' both Prescott and [[C.J. Stroud]] and would happily roster Prescott at that price, but forced to split he takes Stroud — his tiebreaker is that 'Jerry wants what Jerry wants', and the ownership's track record means the retool may not work out as intended. Untracked co-host Bob Harris leaned the same way for a different reason: he does not know what to expect from the Cowboys, and Prescott has finished as low as roughly a QB3 in a year, though he granted Prescott still delivers 5,000-yard upside and noted he has been less eager to run since his major injury.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]) [Best Ball]: an emphatic yes on Prescott at QB18 in the 11th round — 'ridiculously low' for a quarterback who has finished as a QB1 in six pro seasons, every full season he has played, and who was QB3 as recently as 2023. Waldman's argument is that you can nitpick the offensive coordinator change and the thin supporting cast, but at an 11th-round cost the downside is trivial: if it does not work out you have lost almost nothing unless you specifically missed that year's [[Puka Nacua]] with the pick, and those odds are very low. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) framed the question around whether the OC change and limited weapons cap him; Waldman's answer was that proven production at that price does not need to be defended.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): at roughly QB18, Prescott is a value. The logic is that even if [[George Pickens]] does not help his own career as much as you would like, he is a legitimate downfield threat who must be covered, and that vertical stretch lets Prescott spread the ball around underneath. Waldman pointed to the supporting pieces that benefit from the coverage attention — [[Jalen Tolbert]] able to work downfield without the top defenders on him, and [[KaVontae Turpin]] looking good in the slot as a big-play option. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) added the Next Gen Stats note that Turpin hit 22.36 mph last season, the fastest recorded speed in the NFL.
- 2025-05-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Prescott at QB16 ADP is 'a quarterback two with tremendous upside' and Waldman pairs him with [[Brock Purdy]] as a complete quarterback plan — 'just take Brock Purdy and Dak Prescott and you're probably pretty good to go.' He projects a rebound of about 4,500 yards and over 30 touchdowns, and says he would push that to 4,900-5,000 if the offensive line were better, having looked at what Prescott has produced behind bad lines before. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) added that Prescott has finished QB1 six times, has done so every year he played a full season, once led the NFL with 36 touchdown passes and finished QB3 as recently as 2023. The line remains the stated cap on his ceiling.
- 2025-06-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-05]]): a rebound season is likely — roughly 4,000–4,200 yards and near 30 touchdowns — provided the offensive line holds up. The hinge is Tyron Smith moving inside to guard: Waldman likes the move for extending a very good player's career, but inside he faces more powerful rushers, and if his durability is already compromised that becomes a bigger problem because Dallas is leaning on him as the starter in the middle. Waldman stresses that interior pressure, not blitzes, is what breaks quarterbacks — completion percentage, turnover rate and yards per attempt are always at their lowest against pressure generated by base coverages without extra rushers. If Smith holds up and the rest of the line is even marginally better, he does not see how Prescott fails to rebound, with [[George Pickens]] a big part of it.
- 2025-08-14 — According to Sigmund Bloom of Footballguys (not a tracked expert) with [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Dallas is Bloom's number four offense the market may be wrong about. Prescott has been a top-five or top-six fantasy quarterback multiple times, and in the season he dislocated his ankle four games in he was pacing toward 5,000-plus yards and near 40 touchdowns, rivaling [[Joe Burrow]]'s numbers. Bloom would draft him ahead of [[Bo Nix]] and [[Justin Fields]] and possibly even [[Patrick Mahomes]], whom the market is projecting to do something he has not done in a while, and thinks the whole passing game — [[CeeDee Lamb]] as a possible WR2 overall, [[George Pickens]], [[Jake Ferguson]], even [[KaVontae Turpin]] — is underpriced. [[Chris Harris]] partly disagrees: Dak 'was bad even before he got hurt last year,' though he wonders how much of that was Mike McCarthy, since Brian Schottenheimer shared play-calling duties at times. Harris agrees a lot of negative assumptions are baked into the price and that Dak is great value if they are wrong, but is skeptical the 'knucklehead' Pickens evens the keel.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): the whole Dallas offense outside [[CeeDee Lamb]] is Harris's number four 'might be wrong about' — he does not have Prescott as a top-12 quarterback and acknowledges he is 'really low' on him. His reasoning is 2024 evidence: even before Dak got hurt, with the same play-caller, the offense stunk and only Lamb was good. He is trying not to be bewildered by a second half that 'meant nothing', but concedes things change year to year and that a Schottenheimer play-calling change might matter, and that a team this recently a wagon could just be ready again. Ben Gretch (guest, NOT tracked) is likewise 'lukewarm', rating Dak very average. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Prescott was 'good before the storm' and 'in control early' — one near-interception on the second drive that [[CeeDee Lamb]] saved — but 'pretty lousy' after the hour-plus thunderstorm delay, including an interception thrown directly to Quinyon Mitchell while being hit that Mitchell dropped. Harris is careful with the variables: Jalen Carter was ejected before the first offensive snap for spitting on Prescott, the Philadelphia defense was already depleted, and the weather delay stopped all scoring. He also pushes back on the offseason consensus that Dallas under Brian Schottenheimer is destined to be pass-heavy — this was 27 attempts to 22 rushes, and 'take away seven throws on the final emergency drive,' with the run game arguably more important to the first four productive drives. He puts a pin in the pass-rate question rather than declaring.
