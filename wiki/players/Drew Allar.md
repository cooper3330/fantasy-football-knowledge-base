# Drew Allar

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Allar is not an undervalued potential rookie-draft steal even with a good landing spot -- Waldman's blunt answer was 'no.' He described Allar as a 'robo-quarterback,' built and throwing the way teams want a prototype to look, but with what Waldman called a 'TRS-80 processor' for how he currently reads and processes the game -- a significant, hard-to-fix mental gap that outweighs his physical tools right now.
- 2026-04-16 — According to Dalton Wasserman (guest draft analyst, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: in a three-way comparison with [[Cade Klubnick]] and [[Carson Beck]], some evaluators give [[Drew Allar]] the highest ceiling because of his arm -- Wasserman noted he and Taylen Green have the two strongest arms in this draft class -- but called his accuracy 'all over the place' and filed him as developmental, behind Klubnick on his personal ranking of the three faded blue-chips.
- 2026-04-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-20]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman expects Drew Allar to be overdrafted -- possibly as early as Day 2 (Round 4-5) -- purely on the NFL's bias toward big, strong, fast quarterback frames (6'5', 230 lbs), calling that a poor match for what the film actually shows. He does not think Allar is worth that draft capital based on tape and projects him more as an undrafted-free-agent type who needs reps in the UFL or a lower league to build fundamentals and playing time before an NFL team tries to rush him into a lineup.
- 2026-05-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-04]]) [Dynasty]: pushes back on the idea that Drew Allar's rookie opportunity alone justifies drafting him, calling that rationale illogical. Waldman has no strong grade for Allar at all -- 'not even a bad grade' -- and rejects the argument that Penn State 'limited' him as a point in his favor: if the team didn't use his capability, evaluators never got to see it, so the excuse actually cuts against him, not for him. Waldman expects the Steelers will turn to Will Howard before Allar, and cautions that a hot start from any rookie QB (he cites Will Levis and Marcus Mariota as cautionary comps) doesn't make a career -- he wants 18-30 games before drawing conclusions. His blanket advice on early-playing-time rookie QBs: avoid unless you're a trade-savvy GM planning to sell high into inflated hype, since sunk-cost bias usually gets these players rushed onto the field before they're ready, which makes outcomes worse more often than not.
- 2026-05-11 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-11]]) [Dynasty]: on Drew Allar's minicamp technique drills (which drew commentary from Kurt Warner about the purpose of the drills), Waldman called the criticism overblown -- while he didn't think Allar was a strong pick at the range Dallas took him, deliberately slow, precise mechanical work is how any player rebuilds technique and muscle memory, comparing it to how musicians drill slowly before speed comes. He framed it as Mike McCarthy 'reprogramming' Allar's footwork and rhythm from how he did things in college, which necessarily looks awkward and slow at first. Waldman noted Warner has his own divergent, self-taught technique views (and a paid Patreon promoting them), so some of Warner's public critique should be read with that incentive in mind, though his Hall of Fame background still carries weight.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-17]]): Asked whether the Steelers will have to consider starting Allar at some point this year, Waldman said 'fuck it' -- pushing back on the premise. He noted Allar threw the ball where it needed to be and got some big plays from his receivers, but wasn't facing defenses or extended exposure beyond what he saw at Penn State. Waldman attributed the push to start him to 'overexcited young analysts who have nice haircuts' chasing social media relevance -- guys who may be strong on X's and O's but have shortcomings evaluating actual player talent, which is only part of the equation.
