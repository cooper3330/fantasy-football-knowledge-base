# Brian Thomas Jr.

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — Guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Dynasty]: rejects the idea that he's a top-5 receiver in this specific (unusually deep) 2024 class, but says he'd be a "fringe top-5" prospect in most other draft classes. Strengths: an excellent downfield/deep-threat profile with strong sideline body control and ball-tracking over the shoulder, plus good hand technique at the catch point — drawing an explicit contrast with [[Quentin Johnston]] and Marquez Valdes-Scantling, whose shared flaw is catching the ball into their chest/body rather than away from it, which Harms calls a mechanical cause of drops. Concerns: limited route-running polish and after-the-catch shiftiness, caveated by the fact he shared an LSU offense with [[Malik Nabers]] and may simply not have been schemed to do more — the same "do the harder thing" caution Waldman and Adam Harstad apply elsewhere (see [[Role Difficulty and Replaceability]]), with Chris Olave's limited Ohio State route tree offered as a precedent for a receiver succeeding in the NFL beyond what his college usage showed.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): first Reception Perception charting numbers (explicitly caveated 3-game early sample). Primarily an outside/X alignment (61.7% on the line of scrimmage, split roughly evenly left/right), a good press-coverage beater (~73-74% early success rate), but a heavily concentrated route tree — nine/go routes, curls and slants combine for 70.4% of all charted routes, which Harmon flags as an area needing real development. Better after the catch than expected (broke or extended past first contact on the majority of in-space touches), and led the nation in touchdowns (17, on a modest 1,100 yards). Grades him a real fit on Harmon's running "Devontae Parker axis of X receiver" archetype — closer to a [[Tee Higgins]]-bucket possession X than a true speed threat — and floats a speculative Detroit fit given the Lions' stated need at that exact spot (see [[Jared Goff]]), while noting he's unlikely to actually fall that far.
- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]), in a Kansas City offseason-outlook segment: a second speculative landing-spot fit floated, on top of the earlier Detroit mention — "one of those handful of routes as a vertical player in the Chiefs offense is exactly what they need," pointing to his big-frame, go-route-heavy profile as a match for Kansas City's outside-X vacancy, though Harmon notes it would require a trade-up to get into range.

- 2024-02-29 — According to guest **Eric Froton** (NBC Sports college football analyst — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, hosted solo by James Koh with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]) [Dynasty]: names him the highest-upside prospect of a four-player "second tier" big-receiver group ([[Keon Coleman]], [[Xavier Legette]], [[Adonai Mitchell]]), citing a frame-sturdiness edge over similarly-built [[Troy Franklin]] and elite contested/deep production — a 78% catch rate on 14 targets of 20+ yards (best in the class), 11 of those 14 catches going for touchdowns, and a 152.7 opposing-QB passer rating when targeted. Wants to see him add weight (from ~195 to the low 200s) to fully translate; calls him a genuine three-level winner rather than just a deep-ball specialist.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): good spatial awareness at the boundary and buys room from the sideline so his quarterback gets easier throws — something Waldman notes a lot of young receivers don't do well. Wiry strength to shrug off pursuit in the open field, finishes runs strong, and has quick, precise feet to make defenders miss. "Good prospect."
- 2024-04-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-02]]): full-sample Reception Perception profile, superseding the 2024-02-01 early read. Size/speed shows up fully on tape, not just at the combine — 69.1% of snaps on the line of scrimmage, only 13.1% in the slot, a true outside X alignment. Route tree remains heavily concentrated (67.1% slant/curl/nine), which Harmon attributes to LSU's scheme (slot fades to [[Malik Nabers]] forcing Thomas into underneath curl/hitch work) rather than a limitation of his own — draws a direct DK Metcalf comp for that same early-career pattern. Elite marks versus press (73.5% success rate) and on the nine route specifically (72% success rate, "bonkers" for his size); 80.4% success rate versus zone. Saw very little extra defensive attention (only 2.5% of routes doubled) because he was third in the defensive pecking order behind Nabers and a scrambling Jayden Daniels. Contested-catch ability graded well (72.7% catch rate on a modest 15.9% contested-target share) and only a 2.9% drop rate; better after the catch than the profile suggests (broken tackle/evaded first contact on 48% of in-space touches). Calls him "the actualized version of what people wanted [[Christian Watson]] or [[Quentin Johnston]] to be" — same size/speed archetype (also comps to Martavis Bryant) but with the polish those two lacked; a "plug and play" Day 1 X starter for any NFL offense. Grades him a clear tier-two prospect, 4th overall in his class rankings behind the top three. Floats speculative fits with the Colts (mid-teens, pairing with [[Michael Pittman Jr.]]) or Dallas (pairing with [[CeeDee Lamb]]). Despite the bullish profile, lands on a surprising median-outcome projection: not a lock true WR1, but "a really really good number two" comparable to [[Tee Higgins]] — high floor (plays every snap, dangerous vertically and after the catch) but may never see a true 140-target WR1 role.

- 2024-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-30]]): Harmon says Thomas is underdiscussed relative to the top-three receivers, but likes the Jaguars landing spot given Jacksonville's bottom-five 57% completion rate to outside WRs in 2023. Charted numbers: 70.1% success rate vs. man, 80.4% vs. zone (impressive given a heavy vertical route diet -- nines, posts, corners), and 73.9% vs. press, sixth-best in the class -- with Harmon noting Thomas's tape improved markedly from early to late season, suggesting he's still ascending. Measurables: 6'3"/209, 4.33 forty, 38.5-inch vertical, 10.6-foot broad; led the FBS with 17 touchdowns in 2023. Harmon doesn't expect Thomas to carry a Marvin-Harrison-level 25% target share as a rookie behind [[Christian Kirk]] and [[Gabe Davis]], but projects him to out-target Davis and, by year two, to be Jacksonville's clear lead target-getter and Trevor Lawrence's long-term No. 1 receiver, comparing his rookie usage (67% slant/curl/nine routes) to DK Metcalf's rookie season.
- 2024-05-02 — According to Brandon Angelo ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): his top 'overrated based on fit' pick. Angelo said Thomas was a secondary option to [[Malik Nabers]] at LSU with a skill set similar to [[Quentin Johnston]] — needs the ball schemed to him early in the route, ideally from the slot, comparable to early-career JuJu Smith-Schuster in Pittsburgh — and called him one of the most underrated after-the-catch athletes in the class for his re-acceleration, length and stop-start ability. The problem is fit: Jacksonville has heavy target competition in [[Christian Kirk]] (Evan Engram caught 100+ balls last year) with Kirk in the slot and [[Gabe Davis]] at X, relegating Thomas to an outside role without much of the motion/schemed touches Angelo thinks he needs, and Jacksonville lacks a receiver like Nabers to draw coverage away from him.

Harmon calls Thomas a good player who's ready for year-one impact but not yet 'the dude' given the target competition.

- 2024-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-11]]) [Dynasty]: charted Brian Thomas Jr. as a stripped-down X-receiver prospect at LSU -- over 67% of his routes were nine, slant or curl -- but he dominated within that limited tree: 72% success rate on the nine route, 83% on the curl and 82% on the slant, clearing Harmon's usual benchmark thresholds (roughly 70% vs. man, 80% vs. zone) on all three levels (underneath, intermediate, deep). 13.1% of his sample snaps came from the slot, with the rest split between flanker and X (69.1% on the line), so Harmon projects a hybrid X/flanker role that will need to expand beyond that route tree as he develops. - 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-12]]): Thomas is the rookie receiver who slipped in valuation — the 2024 class talk centered on a big three when it should have been a big five, with Thomas and [[Xavier Worthy]] in that tier. Waldman grades this as the best receiver class he has seen since 2014 (Beckham, Watkins, Evans, Cooks, Adams), with the caveat that landing spots and maturity are big ifs and potential is not reality. On tape he saw good route running against both zone and man: Thomas read not only his own defender's leverage but the nearby defender's, manipulating both, and 'told stories with his routes' that forced defenders off their spots to create separation. Waldman contrasts that nuance with [[Gabriel Davis]], who wins on scheme. Conclusion: he looks like the Brian Thomas Jr. of LSU and can grow from there, and could end up the Jaguars' primary receiver by the end of 2024 if not by 2025 — a promising development this early.

- 2024-10-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-01]]): Harmon expected Thomas to be more developmental behind [[Evan Engram]] and [[Christian Kirk]], but injuries and the fact that [[Gabe Davis]] is not a target-commanding receiver forced Jacksonville to lean on him and he has responded — targeted on 35% of his routes in Week 4, with a well-run double move for a touchdown against rookie corner Kamari Lassiter. - 2024-10-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Thomas is exceeding his own pre-draft projection — 8 targets, 5 catches, 122 yards and a touchdown for his first 100-yard game, on pace for roughly 1,350 yards and 10 touchdowns at a 65% catch rate per Koh. Charting the last two games, Harmon has him at only 24% vertical routes (nine/post/corner), with the curl his most-run route at 22% and an 83% success rate, and his best work coming on digs against man coverage — clean releases, precise depth, selling outside before breaking in. He is split roughly evenly on and behind the line of scrimmage, mostly flanker with ~24% slot, because [[Gabe Davis]] absorbs the sacrificial X role. Caveats: sub-70% success rate vs. zone in a small sample, which Harmon says must improve for a true WR1 label, and the fact everyone beats the Colts. He believes Thomas will outproduce [[Rome Odunze]] and may out-gain [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], and that the pre-draft Martavis Bryant / [[Christian Watson]] / [[Quentin Johnston]] archetype comp has been blown away.

- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-13]]): the Jaguars did not want Thomas pinned as a pure X receiver and tried to move him around the formation, but as Harmon puts it, he could not really run those over-the-middle routes all season. Thomas has also been in and out of the lineup along with [[Travis Hunter]] and [[Brenton Strange]] — Koh challenges anyone to find a successful franchise whose top three pass catchers are all this unavailable alongside a quarterback in [[Trevor Lawrence]] who has not established himself. Harmon frames 'can they get BTJ back up to speed' as one of the central open questions for the offense, while saying he is otherwise comfortable with Liam Coen's staff and design.
