# Jalen Tolbert

## Expert Takes

- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon liked Tolbert as a prospect -- he had him ranked in the early Day 3/Round 4 range, and Tolbert went a bit higher -- off a strong small-sample RP profile from South Alabama, but flags him as a 'Skyy Moore bucket' risk: small samples against lower-level competition can produce inflated RP results that don't translate early in a career, which is roughly what happened as a rookie (36 targets, 22 catches, 268 yards, no real impact). He now wonders if Tolbert is simply a slower developer who flashed late last season and could be readied to start given Dallas's total inaction at receiver this offseason, but stopped short of a confident projection either way.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman rejected breakout buzz on Tolbert, projecting only incremental growth behind CeeDee Lamb, Brandon Cooks and Jake Ferguson as Dallas' top three pass-catching options. He also expects Dallas' backfield (Deuce Vaughn, Rico Dowdle) to soak up meaningful receiving work, pointing to Ezekiel Elliott's sizable receiving-yardage total at running back last year in Foxborough as the kind of volume that can go to backs instead of a WR4. His projection lands Tolbert around 450-500 yards on 35-45 catches, with real upside only if Lamb or Cooks misses time and forces the offense to feed him.
- 2024-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-16]]): Harmon's RP chart on Tolbert (South Alabama, a four-game rather than typical eight-game sample) graded 59th percentile vs. man, 62nd vs. zone and 74th vs. press, which supported his eventual late-third/early-fourth-round pre-draft grade -- the range he actually went in. Harmon's stated ceiling comp is a 'Marvin Jones-ish' complementary X: mostly vertical and tight-coverage wins, but mistake-prone. Tolbert played 249 routes for 22 catches in 2023 (after touching the ball twice as a rookie) and is projected for roughly 56 targets in 2024 as Dallas's presumptive number three behind [[CeeDee Lamb]] and [[Brandon Cooks]]; both hosts flagged buzz around 2023 seventh-round pick Jalen Brooks (not a tracked-page player) as a threat to that role, and expressed skepticism that Dallas meaningfully addressed the WR room at all this offseason. [Dynasty] [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]): Harmon will not write off the Tolbert third-year-leap buzz, mostly because he liked the RP prospect profile — decent size, over six feet, route running he compared to Marvin Jones, a 70.5% success rate versus man and 81.2% versus zone, hitting the 70/80 benchmark he wants, with an almost entirely green route tree (only the slant and nine grading yellow). Small-school evaluation tilts it somewhat. He notes Tolbert took snaps ahead of a declining [[Michael Gallup]] late in 2023 (36 targets, 22 catches, 268 yards, two TDs) and calls the grade an incomplete on 39 career targets. The caution is motivated reasoning: Dallas badly needs it, because beyond [[CeeDee Lamb]] the room is thin, [[Brandin Cooks]] is a three at this point, and both hosts call the offense fragile — Koh (not tracked) says an injury to Lamb or [[Dak Prescott]] would sink the season.
- 2024-09-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-26]]): the second of his two off-radar receivers, offered with hedging — Tolbert has 'flashed a little bit' and Harmon does not think he is a great player, but it has been a slow burn and Dallas now trusts him on the field with [[Dak Prescott]], throwing him chunk plays down the field. Harmon's case is need-based: he does not believe [[Brandon Cooks]] can still affect the game as a vertical outside receiver, and Dallas wants [[CeeDee Lamb]] getting slot reps, so someone has to earn true targets as an outside boundary receiver. Koh adds that Tolbert had an intriguing collegiate profile. The open question Harmon poses is whether Tolbert becomes a genuine target earner outside over the course of the season.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-10]]): asked to buy or sell Tolbert's 10-target, 7-catch, 87-yard, one-TD breakout, Harmon says he's buying it a little. He frames Tolbert as a coming cautionary tale against writing off receivers who do nothing as rookies — two catches as a rookie, yet Dallas stuck with him, never replaced him and talked him up, which Harmon reads as signal. Charting Giants and Steelers, he was surprised Tolbert wasn't in the [[Michael Gallup]] X role: 66.7% of snaps off the line, 36% slot, with slants and curls over 50% of his routes and success rates of 87.8% vs zone and 73.7% vs man — both clearing Harmon's good-NFL-receiver benchmarks of 80% zone / 70% man. He credits some of it to Tolbert being a smart player who executes (praising him running the game-winner into the end zone rather than flattening the route) and some to schemed space created by [[CeeDee Lamb]] kicking to X, and expects the zone number to come down. Comp: Marvin Jones, a good No. 2 across from a superstar — though he needs to show downfield ability Jones had. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) added a 70.4% catch rate and 12.4 yards per reception over the last four games against a schedule including Baltimore and New Orleans.
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-13]]): [[Jalen Tolbert]], entering the last year of his rookie deal, is an okay and useful player but 'probably like a three' — the same mold as [[Wan'Dale Robinson]], someone you can throw out as a third receiver without embarrassment. Harmon is explicit that Tolbert was not up to the task of being a true second option to [[CeeDee Lamb]], nor was it fair to expect him to be. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) pushed back slightly in Tolbert's favor, arguing he is more versatile than Robinson because he can line up outside and run more of a route tree, though less dangerous after the catch.
- 2025-06-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-06]]): Tolbert is 'not a bad player' who was miscast and overcast as the WR2 last year, especially with [[CeeDee Lamb]] injured — Dallas did not want him as their only outside presence. Harmon stops short of calling 2024 a breakout, describing it as a bit of a breakthrough when he settled in as a solid starting outside receiver, and says he is certainly useful as the third piece behind Lamb and [[George Pickens]] with [[Jake Ferguson]] at tight end. Koh (not tracked) noted spring reports of Tolbert looking good in Dallas.
- 2025-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-08]]): Harmon plays 'a bit of an apologist' for Tolbert — a nice player, not a special one, who can be a third receiver you are not embarrassed by. Older small-school prospect out of South Alabama with a solid Reception Perception prospect profile; Harmon comped him to [[Marvin Jones]], a jack of all trades and master of none, which is roughly what he has become. A two-game 2024 sample against Pittsburgh and the Giants had him at 66.7% of snaps off the line of scrimmage and 36.6% in the slot. His value is as a movable third body who can run the slants and curls Dallas asks for and act as a [[Sacrificial X Receiver]] clearing space so [[George Pickens]] and [[CeeDee Lamb]] can be deployed together — the [[Romeo Doubs]] / [[Kayshon Boutte]] role. Harmon calls him a nice route runner but not explosive.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-23]]): Tolbert has been used as a 'big-time flanker' for Dallas and is running a 46.7% slot rate, which Harmon flagged as ahead of [[CeeDee Lamb]]'s own rate this season — a quirk of how Dallas had been deploying Lamb. His verdict is measured: 'I think Tolbert's okay, like I think he's an okay wide receiver three.' With Lamb out on a high ankle sprain, Harmon does not see Tolbert as the player who moves the needle, expecting the added volume to concentrate on [[George Pickens]] and [[Jake Ferguson]] instead.
