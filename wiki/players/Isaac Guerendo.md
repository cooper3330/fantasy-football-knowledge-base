## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: frames Guerendo as an either/or prospect — either a hidden gem overshadowed at a "big school" program the way Priest Holmes was by Ricky Williams or Terrell Davis was by Garrison Hearst (none tracked), or a player who simply "wasn't a college star" for a reason — and guesses the truth lands somewhere in between. Physically has the dimensions, explosion, and movement of a feature back; lacks star-caliber power and contact balance as a tackle breaker but is competent (not easy to bring down). The right scheme fit could help him become an early contributor.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): named, alongside Anthony McFarland (not tracked), as an unproven-upside flyer-type back Waldman would rather pass on in the fifth-to-seventh round range in favor of a more proven, efficient back like [[Dillon Johnson]].
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether it's better to be Guerendo behind Christian McCaffrey or Braelon Allen behind Breece Hall with the Jets, chose McCaffrey's backfield outright, citing San Francisco's scheme and surrounding talent (including Trent Williams anchoring the line). He compared Guerendo to 'Elijah Mitchell 2.0' with a bit more speed, noting he projects best as a gap-scheme runner but that the 49ers' outside zone is simplified enough for backs that it effectively plays like a gap scheme, which should let his skill set translate well.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman compared 4th-round pick [[Isaac Guerendo]]'s size/speed/power/footwork profile to Todd Gurley -- 'does he have Todd Gurley's level of speed and acceleration? It's not that far away,' though he clarified that doesn't make him Gurley in overall ability. He noted Guerendo improved every year behind a loaded Wisconsin depth chart (Jonathan Taylor, Braelon Allen) and could win the 49ers' No. 2 job behind [[Christian McCaffrey]] over injury-prone Elijah Mitchell and Jordan Mason if he shows he can pass-protect in camp, invoking the Shanahan-tree history of unheralded backs (Mike Anderson, Brock Purdy) breaking out unexpectedly. He called Guerendo 'a dart throw' right now and a better dynasty stash than redraft asset.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Guerendo is named as the immediate next man up if [[Jordan Mason]] fumbles or misses a hole, in Waldman's argument that drafting any 49ers reserve back is a waste of time — Kyle Shanahan's perfectionism means the job rotates rather than transfers, and after Guerendo the search continues to the practice squad and waiver wire. Waldman's prescription is to take the 49ers backfield off the waiver wire week to week rather than pay a draft or FAAB premium for it.
