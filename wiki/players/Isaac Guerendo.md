# Isaac Guerendo

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name as "Isaac Garendo"; normalized here to the real name of the Louisville
> running back, Isaac Guerendo.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: frames Guerendo as an either/or prospect — either a hidden gem overshadowed at a "big school" program the way Priest Holmes was by Ricky Williams or Terrell Davis was by Garrison Hearst (none tracked), or a player who simply "wasn't a college star" for a reason — and guesses the truth lands somewhere in between. Physically has the dimensions, explosion, and movement of a feature back; lacks star-caliber power and contact balance as a tackle breaker but is competent (not easy to bring down). The right scheme fit could help him become an early contributor.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): named, alongside Anthony McFarland (not tracked), as an unproven-upside flyer-type back Waldman would rather pass on in the fifth-to-seventh round range in favor of a more proven, efficient back like [[Dillon Johnson]].
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether it's better to be Guerendo behind Christian McCaffrey or Braelon Allen behind Breece Hall with the Jets, chose McCaffrey's backfield outright, citing San Francisco's scheme and surrounding talent (including Trent Williams anchoring the line). He compared Guerendo to 'Elijah Mitchell 2.0' with a bit more speed, noting he projects best as a gap-scheme runner but that the 49ers' outside zone is simplified enough for backs that it effectively plays like a gap scheme, which should let his skill set translate well.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman compared 4th-round pick [[Isaac Guerendo]]'s size/speed/power/footwork profile to Todd Gurley -- 'does he have Todd Gurley's level of speed and acceleration? It's not that far away,' though he clarified that doesn't make him Gurley in overall ability. He noted Guerendo improved every year behind a loaded Wisconsin depth chart (Jonathan Taylor, Braelon Allen) and could win the 49ers' No. 2 job behind [[Christian McCaffrey]] over injury-prone Elijah Mitchell and Jordan Mason if he shows he can pass-protect in camp, invoking the Shanahan-tree history of unheralded backs (Mike Anderson, Brock Purdy) breaking out unexpectedly. He called Guerendo 'a dart throw' right now and a better dynasty stash than redraft asset.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Guerendo is named as the immediate next man up if [[Jordan Mason]] fumbles or misses a hole, in Waldman's argument that drafting any 49ers reserve back is a waste of time — Kyle Shanahan's perfectionism means the job rotates rather than transfers, and after Guerendo the search continues to the practice squad and waiver wire. Waldman's prescription is to take the 49ers backfield off the waiver wire week to week rather than pay a draft or FAAB premium for it.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]) [Dynasty]: moderately excited, not more — Waldman told a listener holding Guerendo shares to keep an eye on him because the size and speed are visible and he did what he was supposed to do in those offenses, but that he is not yet fully well-rounded in the passing game the way [[Jordan Mason]] can be as a blocker and probably as an assignment-sound player. His image for the level of enthusiasm: you are not in full pants-off mode yet, but you can start untying the shoelaces so you can make that move later.
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]): for managers who missed [[Jordan Mason]] after the [[Christian McCaffrey]] injury, Guerendo is worth a waiver share but is not a one-for-one replacement — he is 'good enough to help you,' not Mason. He is not the receiver Mason is and is still figuring out how Kyle Shanahan wants the system run, with moments of hesitance and a lack of attack. The physical profile is there: big, strong, fast and a violent runner when he wants to be; he needs to be more decisive. If that develops, Waldman thinks he could be roughly one-to-one between the tackles down the stretch, but not as an all-around back.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-19]]): a Week 16 worry. Guerendo did not practice Wednesday with a hamstring injury after playing 77% of the snaps and being the only 49ers back to take a carry the week before, having already entered that game nursing an injury. Koh flagged that hamstrings have been a chronic problem for him dating to college — they cost him availability collegiately and professionally and derailed his training camp. Harmon used it to make a broader complaint about a 'generational Shanahan problem': running one back at 80-90% of snaps every week rather than mixing in other bodies, which he says runs these players into the dirt.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Guerendo 'probably will start the year as the RB2' behind [[Christian McCaffrey]] and is very talented, but he is up and down as a player, and Waldman noted his 2024 emergence came only after he was RB3 or RB4 behind [[Jordan Mason]] and even Patrick Taylor. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) disagreed on the price: he thinks you pay a real handcuff premium for Guerendo with McCaffrey coming off injury, and Guerendo has not shown he stays healthy — Harris called it 'Elijah Mitchell part two,' recalling a mock where he badly overpaid for [[Elijah Mitchell]] as a handcuff. Harris said he is injury agnostic but will not overpay for a maybe, so he would ride McCaffrey without a handcuff and try to get [[Jordan James]] later.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]]): the 49ers trading for [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] and immediately declaring him the RB2 is, in Harris's words, more shocking as a Guerendo indictment than anything else, and he dropped Guerendo to deep-flyer territory in his ranks. He attributes it to a combination of not being reliable, always being hurt, and 'probably some of the film problems' he has been raising about Guerendo on the show for the past several months — so this is a confirmation of a long-running skepticism rather than a reversal. Guerendo now sits alongside [[Jaylen Wright]] and [[Ollie Gordon II]] in the same deep-flyer band.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): the 49ers' trade for [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] and Kyle Shanahan's immediate announcement that Robinson is the RB2 behind [[Christian McCaffrey]] puts the 'oft-injured' Guerendo on the outs — Robinson, not Guerendo, is now the back who would inherit the workload if McCaffrey goes down.
