- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]): McMillan is the 2025 receiver he thinks is overhyped — not a bust call, since his score is good enough that combine cross-checking could push him into Waldman's top five to ten, but right now he is not there. Waldman likes [[Savion Williams]] more, says McMillan clearly catches well but that his route running is between tiers, and sees him as more of a specialist than an every-down primary starter — a [[Mike Williams]] type with a niche. [[Brandon Angelo]] names McMillan as his own too-much-buzz pick and agrees on the routes: good at the stem sometimes, but too much drifting, not as clean and concise as an NFL starter needs. Angelo's real worry is fit — he expects a top-10 pick in a thin receiver class, which means being asked to be the WR1 against number-one corners from day one; his realistic ceiling is a [[Tee Higgins]]-type secondary receiver, which is fine but easy to overvalue at that cost.

- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): McMillan's releases need more craft — he has a lot of maneuvers in the toolbox but almost none carry the contrast between patience and suddenness that beats NFL press man. Waldman also wants a better pre-snap plan and alternatives to it: when a defender plays unexpected leverage McMillan stays too goal-focused on the bad plan and gets pinned at the boundary. The sharpest line is conditional praise — if he did not have lapses with clap attacks he would earn an elite pass-catching score and rank among the best pure receivers in the class. He shows great highs at the catch point, but extends his hands in directions requiring significant adjustment while accounting for a defender. Fix that and Waldman thinks he could be 'pretty unstoppable' at the catch point. See [[Catch Technique and Ball Tracking]].
