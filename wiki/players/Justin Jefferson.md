# Justin Jefferson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: **feeling it** with Nick Mullens at quarterback. Calls Jefferson "one of the best three route runners in the NFL" and an acrobatic, high-flying finisher, and says he "put on an absolute clinic on even just three routes" the prior week. The practical ruling: bump him one tier down from his absolute WR1-overall ceiling because of the quarterback, but he stays a clear start. The reliability of the route running is exactly what lets a backup quarterback fit balls into tight windows — see [[Weak Quarterback Play and Receiver Value]].

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]): says he's "loved Justin Jefferson's game since LSU," and while he'd take [[Ja'Marr Chase]] "ever so slightly" ahead of him right now in a dynasty startup — purely on Chase's better quarterback certainty — he'd "run to the podium" to grab Jefferson if someone took Chase first. Frames the two as effectively 1-2 with a negligible gap. Notes Jefferson made backup Nick Mullens (not tracked) look serviceable when healthy last season, reinforcing that the QB-agnostic talent read still holds even as the situational edge favors Chase.

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: with [[Kirk Cousins]] signing in Atlanta and Minnesota moving to a bridge-veteran-plus-likely-rookie quarterback plan, says simply "I think Justin Jefferson will be fine" regardless of who ends up throwing him the ball — contrasted directly with [[Jordan Addison]], who he expects to suffer more from the downgrade at quarterback.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: admits some irritation that Jefferson won't get "the most ideal of outcomes" now that [[Kirk Cousins]] has officially left Minnesota for Atlanta, though he notes that doesn't necessarily mean bad things for a player this good.
- 2024-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: calls [[Justin Jefferson]] deserving of a market-altering extension (likely ~$30-35M/year) given his historic per-game production (98 yds/game, 68% career catch rate, 31 TDs in 60 games), and pegs the holdup as a negotiation over guaranteed money rather than any doubt about his value.
- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]): notes Jefferson ranked 3rd in fantasy points per game over Weeks 14-18 last season despite a QB carousel of Josh Dobbs/Jaren Hall/Nick Mullens, and expects him to remain highly productive whether Minnesota starts [[Sam Darnold]] or a rookie.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: on the day Jefferson signed the NFL's richest receiver contract (~$35M/year, making him the league's highest-paid WR), Waldman argued the market is overstating concern about rookie QB J.J. McCarthy as a limiting factor. He cited Amari Cooper's 100+ catch, WR18 finish on the NFL's worst-scoring offense with Bryce Young, and Davante Adams' WR10 finish over age 30, as proof elite receivers survive shaky QB play; he called the QB worry 'more a mental thing than an actual thing.' Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) countered that Jefferson was WR3 in per-week scoring over his final month last year playing with backups Josh Dobbs, Jaren Hall and Nick Mullens — real evidence the concern isn't purely psychological. Both agreed Jefferson going anywhere WR1-4 is reasonable, with CeeDee Lamb and Ja'Marr Chase also in that tier discussion; Waldman personally has Lamb as his WR1.
- 2024-07-22 — According to Bob Harris (co-host, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]: Jefferson is the least of his worries in a Minnesota offense with quarterback uncertainty — after returning from the hamstring injury late last season he was third in the league in points per game across five games thrown by a combination of Josh Dobbs, Jaren Hall and Nick Mullens. [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]) agrees he likes the offense and the coaching, and expects whoever plays quarterback — [[Sam Darnold]] or eventually [[J.J. McCarthy]] — to be coached up enough to get it done, adding that a young quarterback with that level of surrounding talent does not have to overthink much.
- 2024-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-30]]): asked whether Jefferson is the best receiver in the league, Harmon says 'hell yeah' without qualification and adds he has been for a while, maybe the best route runner and best contested-catch receiver in the league. He calls 2023 Jefferson's worst Reception Perception season and it was still 76.6% success rate vs man; he attributes the dip partly to sampling the first four and last four games, with the return games coming straight off injury before he was on a full snap load. Harmon's headline projection is volume: with Minnesota's depth chart 'falling off a cliff' after [[Jordan Addison]], [[T.J. Hockenson]] hurt and either [[Sam Darnold]] or a rookie under center, he thinks Jefferson has a real shot to lead the NFL in target share and reach a 200-target season. He would take the over on 40.6% first-read target share — the figure [[Davante Adams]] led the league with in 2023 per Fantasy Points — against Jefferson's own 33.2%, and floats 45%. His reasoning: a rookie or journeyman QB makes 18 the first read maybe 50% of the time and the first read on the concept 80-85% of the time. He points at the Nick Mullens stretch as proof the targets come regardless of quarterback quality — 'not good football, but enthralling football'. Koh does the math: 12 targets a game over 17 games is 204, and given this depth chart that does not sound crazy.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): Jefferson is a great player and Waldman will not argue with anyone who ranks him WR1 — he sees a very clear top two of Jefferson and [[Ja'Marr Chase]] in a tier above the rest of the league. His tiebreaker goes to Chase, whom he graded head and shoulders above the rest of that draft class, judging Chase the better athlete with more speed and short-area burst and more physicality at the catch point, with route running roughly even. He liked Jefferson a lot pre-draft and calls the separation between them splitting hairs.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14 (Age Cliffs and Dented Cans)]]: the hosts have swapped sides on the best-receiver question over two years. Adam Harstad (NOT tracked) was previously on [[Ja'Marr Chase]] but says Jefferson 'wore me down' — he wanted it not to be Jefferson on aesthetic grounds and now concludes 'it has to be Jefferson.' What won him over was watching Jefferson excel with different quarterbacks, which separates the player from his context — a separation he says is always hard to make and that he needed to see rather than assume. [[Matt Waldman]] has Jefferson 1-2 with Chase but is leaning Chase, calling the gap close enough that he cannot fault anyone for preferring Jefferson. Waldman credits Jefferson as a route runner nearly equal to Chase, tough over the middle and in contested-catch areas, and able to play all three positions. Both noted Philadelphia passed on him out of LSU believing he was slot-only.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: not concerned — Jefferson can beat anybody — but he called A.J. Terrell one of the genuine shadow corners who travels with the number one receiver, and while he does not think Terrell is at the peak of a few seasons ago, he would not give Jefferson a clean edge either. His scheme note: Terrell has essentially not taken a slot snap this year and is a pure boundary corner, so if he were Kevin O'Connell he would push Jefferson inside as much as possible, up from his roughly 30 percent slot rate. Atlanta is dead last in completion percentage allowed to receivers lined up outside. He and James Koh (co-host, not tracked) both raised that the beneficiary is often the secondary receiver — [[Jordan Addison]] here — citing [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]] going 3-for-3 for 109 and two scores in week 10 and [[Diontae Johnson]] drawing 10 targets for 78 and a touchdown in week 6 against Atlanta.
- 2024-12-12 — According to guest Patrick Peterson (former NFL cornerback, not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-12]]: the week 12 line against Chicago — five targets, two catches, 27 yards — was not Jaylon Johnson shutting Jefferson down. Johnson had consistent safety help over the top, which pushed Jefferson underneath, and Peterson does not think the Bears can commit to that again with [[Jordan Addison]] producing and the rematch indoors rather than outdoors. [[Matt Harmon]] added that Matt Eberflus, who called that game, is no longer with the team. Peterson's broader point was that a defense answers a receiver like Jefferson through offseason roster construction — stacking multiple genuine cover players at corner, safety and linebacker to survive a division — not through a single matchup plan.
- 2025-01-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-09]]): Jefferson wins 2024 Best Deep Ball Wide Receiver. He drew 22 targets of 20-plus air yards with a 64% catch rate on them — which Harmon calls crazy for that depth — and 1.55 EPA per target, and Koh adds he was sixth in the NFL in total deep-pass yards per Next Gen Stats. Harmon highlights the weeks 12 through 17 stretch as when Jefferson was consistently collecting massive downfield plays, especially slicing up zone coverage alongside [[Jordan Addison]], and credits the connection with [[Sam Darnold]] as special to watch. The lone caveat is week 18, when nothing went right for the Minnesota offense in what became the NFC North title decider. Jefferson plays mostly X, as do most of the receivers in this deep-ball category.
- 2025-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with [[Jordan Addison]] facing a likely suspension, Minnesota's receiver depth chart behind Jefferson is 'dangerously shallow' — [[Jalen Nailor]] was a 45% slot player in 2024 and [[Tai Felton]] projects as a slot — so Koh (not tracked) suggested Jefferson could see 20 targets per game over the first three weeks and said he is comfortable taking him at WR1 or WR2 for the early-season security blanket. Harmon pushes back on the volume estimate: he expects Minnesota to run more and throw maybe 28 times a game to protect rookie [[J.J. McCarthy]], so the raw target count may not get there. Harmon says he has been waffling between Jefferson and [[CeeDee Lamb]] for the WR2 spot behind [[Ja'Marr Chase]], whom he thinks should go first overall.
- 2025-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the hosts disagree. Guest Sigmund Bloom (Footballguys, not tracked) says he would not take Jefferson earlier than sixth or seventh overall given the expected run-balance shift and [[J.J. McCarthy]]'s rookie-treatment offense, and would not call you crazy for preferring [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Ashton Jeanty]], [[Derrick Henry]] or [[Malik Nabers]]. Harris disagrees: he is still taking Jefferson in the middle of the first round and is not that worried, because Jefferson is good enough that even at 80 catches he would still post about 1,400 yards and 10 touchdowns.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Jefferson is the offense — Harmon says he could walk off the bus with any quarterback and put up 100 yards a game, and expects him peppered with targets, but the surrounding cast is thin with [[Jordan Addison]] suspended three games and a hamstring injury costing Jefferson training-camp reps with new starter [[J.J. McCarthy]]. Harmon thinks the real key is Minnesota's run game and play-action, helped by interior line investment in Ryan Kelly, Will Fries and Donovan Jackson plus [[Jordan Mason]]. On the FanDuel most-receiving-touchdowns market he passes on Jefferson at +900 ([[Ja'Marr Chase]] favored at +700), preferring longer odds like [[Nico Collins]] at +2000 or [[A.J. Brown]] at +2200, reasoning that if Minnesota regresses hard Jefferson is not scoring 15-17 touchdowns. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) floats a 12-loss season as within the range of outcomes; Harmon calls that too harsh and says McCarthy would have to truly stink, given how productive [[Sam Darnold]] was there.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]]: the switch from Sam Darnold to [[J.J. McCarthy]] should cost Jefferson some quality downfield targets, because Minnesota is moving toward bootlegs and short easy completions rather than the deep play-action shots that made their air yards per target among the league's highest. Nguyen was explicit that this is not a fade — 'I would not say that I would be fading Justin Jefferson at all' — only that the structure of the offense is different enough to trim the volume of high-value deep looks.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): Harris rates Jefferson a 3 out of 10, saying he would love to give him a 1 because 'he's awesome' but cannot because of the quarterback. Guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates him a 5, arguing Minnesota needs to catch lightning in a bottle twice after [[Sam Darnold]], now with a first-year [[J.J. McCarthy]], that he would prefer [[Jordan Addison]] available opposite him rather than [[Adam Thielen]], and that the hamstring means 'the injury seal is a little bit broken' — a phrase Harris mocks as the bag of things you say when you need something to say, before conceding the point reduces to 'a tweaked hamstring can tweak again'. Bell wants a first-round pick as close to 100% healthy as possible given in-season wear and tear; Harris is less worried about early-August tweaks than about the QB.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Jefferson 'is the one who saved this game' for Minnesota in London, reeling in a couple of long catches to start the second half and making the play of the game with 1:35 left deep down the right sideline where 'the throw isn't good and Jefferson just goes and somehow gets it, because he's a freak.' Harris's broader point is that the Vikings' quarterback play is the problem, not Jefferson: he did not want to submit this tape as evidence in a pro-Carson Wentz argument, citing Wentz running around 'like a lunatic' on third and long, taking a shot to his non-throwing shoulder that nearly forced in rookie [[Max Brosmer]], and taking two big third-down sacks in a three-point game.
- 2025-11-11 — According to co-host James Koh (not tracked) and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-11]]): Jefferson's 4-of-12 for 37 yards was his lowest yardage in 14 games, a 33% catch rate and 3.08 yards per target. On the online chatter about his effort, Koh says the loafing after the interception was a bad look on that single play; Harmon counters that questioning receiver effort is a bad idea about 90% of the time because you cannot see the assignment, and he saw no loafing across the game. The exculpatory number: per Fantasy Points, only three players had a sub-50% catchable target rate in Week 10 and two were Vikings — Jefferson at 42% (5 catchable on 12 targets) and [[Jordan Addison]] lowest at 33%, with [[Darnell Mooney]] at 38%. Harmon's conclusion: even a supposedly quarterback-proof elite separator and contested-catch winner is only quarterback-proof until he isn't, and this will go down as a disappointing statistical season — seventh in receiving yards at 686 (76 per game) on 84 targets.
