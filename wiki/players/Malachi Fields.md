# Malachi Fields

## Expert Takes

- 2026-02-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-02]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said 'feel it' on Fields projecting as a future NFL starter, calling him a good X-receiver who can go up and get contested catches but isn't a one-dimensional contested-catch-only player -- he runs decent routes and can give a team a 'full-fledged game.' Waldman said his draft stock should only rise as the pre-draft process continues.
- 2026-03-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-02]]) [Dynasty]: Fields ran the second-slowest 40 among receivers at 4.61 (just ahead of BYU's Chase Roberts at 4.65), and Waldman thinks that number will scare off decision-makers enough to sink earlier chatter of a late-first-round grade -- 'that number concerns me and I think it will concern everybody else.' He argued the numbers that matter more are Fields's 38-inch vertical, 4.35 20-shuttle (still starter range for initial get-off) and an elite 6.98 three-cone, which point to real suddenness in and out of breaks that matches his tape. Comped him at roughly 6'4 to Plaxico Burress as a big receiver who can win on quickness, acceleration and leaping despite unspectacular long speed. Bottom line: 'four-six-one is not a killer but it may kill his draft capital,' shifting him from priority target to sleeper-profile pick.
- 2026-03-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-03]]): Malachi Fields ran a disappointing 4.6 40 at the combine, confirming pre-existing questions about his long speed on tape, and also had drops in the gauntlet drill. Harmon still likes him as a player but now downgrades him to more of a third-round-caliber prospect rather than the higher marks some evaluators had given him, arguing 'you can't take a wide receiver 4.6 speed in the first two rounds unless you produced unbelievable numbers' -- pointing to Allen Robinson and Cooper Kupp as 4.6-40 guys who nonetheless put up huge college production, which Fields (at Virginia and Notre Dame, not prolific passing environments) did not match.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Fields is a case study for why raw testing numbers need size context -- his 4.61 40 looks pedestrian in isolation, but at 6'4', 218 pounds with three-cone and 20-yard-split numbers that are 'pretty darn good' for his frame, he's an outlier worth evaluating relative to his own size class rather than to smaller, faster receivers.
- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman uses Fields as a case study for why raw RAS/Combine percentiles mislead -- Fields ran a 4.61 40, only two-hundredths of a second off Waldman's 4.59 starter-tier threshold, a gap smaller than the Combine's own measurement error. His compensatory profile is stronger than the headline number suggests: a 4.35 20-yard shuttle (solidly starter-tier for separation) and an elite 6.98 three-cone, which Waldman says maps better to release-footwork and out-of-break acceleration than straight-line speed does. He may not win pure go-routes, but his size, strength and initial quickness should let him earn separation and sustain position through the catch point within the first 10-15 yards, the way most 'speed' routes actually get won in his framework.
- 2026-04-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman ranked Notre Dame's Malachi Fields (a converted quarterback via Virginia) his WR3, saying his floor is a better version of Michael Pittman with real upside beyond that -- 'ceiling' comps floating around include Nico Collins, though Waldman thinks that undersells him. He dismissed a poor 40 time as irrelevant given elite initial acceleration, change-of-direction quickness and vertical leap, and relayed a Dave Kluge report that Fields was the best receiver on the field at Senior Bowl practices, separating cleanly at the break point against tight coverage -- which Waldman called far more meaningful than empty highlight-reel line-of-scrimmage moves. He called Fields a refined route technician with strength and size who could be a second- or third-round value, and said in a direct comparison he sees Fields and [[Denzel Boston]] as much closer in talent than consensus boards suggest, with Fields likely the better bargain two rounds later.
- 2026-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: Fields is a top-five prospect for Waldman despite a poor Relative Athletic Score (4.6-range forty) -- Waldman argues RAS percentile doesn't set a skill floor, and Fields' elite three-cone, size, contested-catch ability (88% catch rate on off-target adjustments, per Harmon's charting on a large sample) and after-contact power outweigh straight-line speed concerns. Both compare him structurally to [[Keon Coleman]] -- near-identical Reception Perception splits (Fields: 24th %ile vs. man, 63rd vs. zone, 25th vs. press; Coleman: 20th, 59th, 19th) -- and worry a team will misuse him as an X since he doesn't separate well enough there, the mistake Waldman says doomed Coleman in Buffalo; the better outcome is a power-slot/off-ball role like [[Nico Collins]] or Alec Pierce grew into over years. Untracked analyst JJ Zachariason's guide flags Fields turning 24 in July, an 'older breakout' profile Harmon says narrows his margin for error. Waldman: Fields is the guy in his top five most likely to fall out of it post-draft based on fit.
- 2026-04-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman defends the Giants' pick of Fields as good despite fan disappointment that he 'only' runs a 4.6 rather than burner speed. He cites Fields' size, play strength, ability to win contested catches downfield, and underrated quickness and short-area change of direction that should generate YAC and separation versus press. He calls Fields a very good route runner and a strong complement to [[Malik Nabers]], projecting a potential Ja'Marr Chase/Tee Higgins-caliber duo if things break right. Waldman says he barely moved Fields down his board -- the only real concern is whether QB [[Jackson Dart]] can push the ball downfield consistently, which he frames as a swing factor that could make this era take off quickly or fizzle just as fast.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-30 (Round 3 WRs)]]): the Giants traded up (giving up multiple picks including a 2027 fourth) to take the 6'4', 218-pound Fields 10th in round 3. He ran a poor 4.61 40 (tight-end speed) despite a 38-inch vertical and 10'4' broad; Lance Zierlein comped him to Michael Pittman, which Harmon isn't fully sold on. Fields played quarterback in high school and is still new to receiver, which Harmon and Koh think shows up as 'quarterback traits' -- diagnosing zone coverage, working back to the ball, attacking leverage -- rather than as a refined route runner. RP data: below the 35th percentile in success rate vs. man/press, but 63rd percentile (81.6%) vs. zone, with his curl/stop route (81% success) his best. Harmon thinks he could blur the line between a volatile deep threat and a power slot like the piece James wrote on poor-separator outcomes describes, wanting him at ~30% slot snaps like Pittman gets. Koh disagrees on usage, arguing head coach John Harbaugh and Matt Nagy ('not a good talent evaluator') will just plant him as a boundary X given his frame, pushing Darius Slayton to a sacrificial-X/flanker role and leaving Malik Nabers (not fully healthy to start the season) to roam. Both agree Fields projects well as a boundary/contested-catch weapon paired with rookie QB Jackson Dart, who they say loves throwing contested go routes and deep stop routes to receivers who can climb the ladder.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-07 (Going Deep with Brandon Angelo)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman has Fields ninth overall in this rookie class, higher than [[Brandon Angelo]], betting Fields sees significant early usage on shallow and intermediate routes (crossers, digs) while [[Malik Nabers]] handles the deep stuff -- partly because Nabers is still working back from his ACL tear and the Giants may want him running fewer high-speed comeback/YAC routes early. Fields' combination of quickness and power after the catch, plus his size as a target, could offset [[Jackson Dart]]'s well-documented struggles throwing 20-25-plus yards downfield; Waldman noted Dart could still develop behind a stronger 2027 quarterback class if he doesn't improve. Waldman is also skeptical of the Dart/Matt Naggy pairing, citing Naggy's failure to modernize his scheme in Kansas City. Angelo agreed with the shallow-usage projection for Fields but was overall less bullish on him given his own concerns about Dart's ceiling.
- 2026-05-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-18]]) [Dynasty]: asked if rookie Fields is a bad fit in New York, Waldman said fuck it -- he's actually a good fit, because he can work the middle of the field, win contested catches, and win after the catch, all three things the Giants need after losing [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] and with [[Malik Nabers]] compromised. He projects 800-900 receiving yards for Fields this year if things break right, with more upside possible, though it hinges on whether [[Jackson Dart]] can support the passing game.
- 2026-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-28]]): with [[Malik Nabers]] likely out for much of 2026, Fields is set up for early, frequent playing time in a Giants offense Harmon expects to lean into heavier 12/13/21/22 personnel with fullback Pat Ricard and Isaiah Likely added. Harmon notes John Harbaugh specifically wanted Fields in the draft, and that his game -- strong blocking, ability to win on curls/stops/digs/slants over the middle, and contested-catch ability even without elite straight-line separation -- fits a run-heavy, bully-ball scheme well. He jokes Fields is essentially 'playing at Notre Dame again' given the expected role. Harmon frames this as a real opportunity bump for production purposes even though he still expects the Giants passing game overall to be worse off without Nabers healthy.
- 2026-06-02 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-06-02]]) [Dynasty]: amid the Giants' crowded, chaotic WR room (an [[Odell Beckham Jr.]] signing, a JuJu Smith-Schuster addition, Darius Slayton, Darnell Mooney, Calvin Austin III, Braxton Berrios, and an injured/uncertain [[Malik Nabers]] all on the roster), Harmon says he 'will remain more interested in what a guy like Malachi Fields can do for this team' than in the veteran additions -- a notable vote of confidence given how unsettled the depth chart looks entering 2026.
- 2026-06-25 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: if the Giants passing game needs juice early because [[Malik Nabers]] isn't ready until November or later, Fields is the one Angelo wants more looks for -- he offers long-term role potential, and a healthy trio of Nabers, [[Isaiah Likely]] and Fields would be a strong long-term outcome, 'you could do worse.'
- 2026-06-25 — According to RP quarterback charter Alfredo Brown ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-06-25]]): despite not testing well at the combine, Fields is 'actually a very good deep ball tracker' who 'was crushing' at the Senior Bowl and in one-on-ones, and profiles as a contested-catch big body who fits Jackson Dart's preference for posts, corners and nines. Brown flagged him as one of three similarly-built Giants targets (with Isaiah Likely and Theo Johnson) who lack true man-coverage separation.
- 2026-08-10 — According to Bob Harris and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-10]]) [Dynasty]: after being cautious on Fields earlier given a wide-open Giants WR2 competition among retreads and free agents, Waldman is coming around -- he's a good route runner for the college level (maybe better than perceived) and a strong contested-catch player who wins with size, length and initial acceleration/short-area quickness. Both hosts prefer Fields over veteran retreads Odell Beckham Jr. and JuJu Smith-Schuster for the WR2 role, with Calvin Austin III in the mix as a slot option and health questions still surrounding Malik Nabers. Waldman sees Fields as a good target/inaccuracy eraser for Jackson Dart and worth a late-draft flier -- especially as a complement if you can't land [[Rashad Bateman]] and haven't loaded up on receivers early.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is much higher on Fields than Harris's board reflects (outside the top-80 redraft receivers) -- Waldman moved him ahead of [[Jacoby Lane]], [[Ted Hurst]], and [[Omar Cooper Jr.]] live on the show, suggesting he should be closer to 72nd than 87th. Unlike the other big, raw rookies discussed this episode, Waldman says Fields is a genuinely better route runner who can run intermediate timing routes with flat breaks and good technique, has excellent short-area quickness and initial acceleration, wins downfield with size once he gets position, and breaks tackles after the catch. Waldman is banking on [[Jaxson Dart]] disappointing (his 'Kyler Murray-itis' comp) and [[Jameis Winston]] taking over under center, at which point Waldman thinks Fields becomes a real waiver-wire target the way Winston once peppered [[Michael Thomas]] in New Orleans.
