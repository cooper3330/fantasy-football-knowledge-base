# Jahmyr Gibbs

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]): takes an explicit victory lap — "everything I said about Jahmyr Gibbs this summer is 100% correct," noting he "took a lot of abuse" for it. His 2023 argument was a consistency argument about pricing, not a knock on [[Bijan Robinson]]: if draft capital and situation justified Robinson as a fantasy first-rounder, Gibbs deserved a third-round pick or better on the same logic. Gibbs finished the segment as roughly RB9, ahead of Robinson at RB11.
- 2023-12-18 — **Matt Waldman**, same episode [Best Ball]: immediately turns the call around for 2024 — because the market will now pay a premium for Gibbs, he expects the value to sit with Robinson instead. This is a valuation call; he does not walk back the talent evaluation.
- 2023-12-18 — Risk flagged by both hosts, same episode: Ben Johnson was a hot head-coaching candidate (Carolina interest noted), so Gibbs's 2024 offense and play-caller were not settled.
- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]] (recorded before the *Film and Theory* episode below, which cites it): two things, both new. First, the maturity marker — watching Gibbs "slipping and sliding out of tackles," Angelo's note is that when the run isn't there, "he protected himself and went down, and he's okay doing that. Young players a lot of times are not okay making that step. They want to break the big run every time." The named counter-case is [[Breece Hall]]. Second, the scheme leverage: Detroit is "much more successful with Gibbs on the field — not because Gibbs is so much better a player than [[David Montgomery]], but because defenses have to think a lot more when Gibbs is out there." Montgomery "very rarely will break a 25-plus-yard run"; Gibbs widens the range of outcomes a defense has to cover. Angelo also names Gibbs's **re-acceleration** as the specific thing a linebacker dreads. Framework: [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]].
- 2023-12-21 — **Angelo**, same episode, on fit: names Gibbs ("listen to him speak") among the players Detroit drafted to a cultural mold — coachable, unselfish — alongside [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[David Montgomery]] and Jared Goff. See [[Player Development and Coachability]].
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): lists Gibbs among the backs defined by change of direction in tight space at speed ([[Christian McCaffrey]], [[James Cook]], [[Austin Ekeler]], [[Lamar Jackson]]) — the trait Waldman argues cannot survive being scaled up to 210-plus pounds. Corroborates the existing evaluation rather than moving it. See [[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]].
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]): a methodology note rather than a new grade — reveals part of how the original pre-draft evaluation formed. Watching Lions RBs coach Scotty Montgomery's pre-draft interactions with Gibbs, "the enthusiasm and the excitement and the way they hit it right off," shaded Waldman's view of the fit positively before the draft even happened — offered as a general evaluation principle (intangibles/relationship fit matter, not just tape) rather than new information about Gibbs's play. See [[Player Development and Coachability]].
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): a brief victory-lap callback after Detroit's NFC Championship loss — "some of us were really in on him from the get-go" — paired again with [[David Montgomery]] as a duo that "gives you all the pieces," part of the case that Detroit's core is set up for continued success regardless of coordinator churn.

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: pushes back on the pass-catching hype from his rookie year — "we talked about... this guy's going to be a big-time pass catcher in the league... but they just kind of really used him as a running back" — cited alongside [[Saquon Barkley]] as a caution that even highly-touted receiving backs rarely have as much passing-game impact as expected.
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]): While breaking down Detroit's offense, Harmon said he 'loves' David Montgomery and thought the Lions were right to give him the bulk of 2023 touches (235 to Gibbs' 234), but wants to see the team find more ways to get the ball into Gibbs' hands going forward.

- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): pushed back on offseason chatter about Gibbs seeing a bigger receiving role (including slot usage), projecting only about 61 catches, 454 yards receiving -- not a 700-yard-receiver breakout -- because the Lions want Gibbs and David Montgomery to work as their outside-the-numbers options while Amon-Ra St. Brown and Sam LaPorta stay inside; downfield outside production is more likely to come from Donovan Peoples-Jones/Jameson Williams. He projects Gibbs at roughly 1,500 total yards and 10-12 touchdowns -- 'RB1 caliber' but not a top-three overall fantasy talent, ranking him RB4 in his consensus rankings, closely bunched with [[Saquon Barkley]] given both have strong offensive lines. He says Gibbs's receiving role gives him a somewhat higher floor, but pushes back on fans who 'fall in love' with his speed and write off Montgomery, arguing Montgomery remains a high-floor top-3-to-5 back even as Gibbs's touches grow -- while conceding Gibbs's ceiling is still the higher of the two.

That toughness is exactly what Dan Campbell values, and Angelo says it will endear Gibbs to Detroit the way [[David Montgomery]], [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and [[Sam LaPorta]] have been. He frames the pick in context: Detroit traded [[DeAndre Swift]] on draft night — a back known for missing assignments and keys and not playing hard down to down — and drafted Gibbs in the first round despite already pairing Montgomery and Swift, a move widely questioned at the time.
