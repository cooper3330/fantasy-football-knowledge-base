# Jahmyr Gibbs

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]): takes an explicit victory lap — "everything I said about Jahmyr Gibbs this summer is 100% correct," noting he "took a lot of abuse" for it. His 2023 argument was a consistency argument about pricing, not a knock on [[Bijan Robinson]]: if draft capital and situation justified Robinson as a fantasy first-rounder, Gibbs deserved a third-round pick or better on the same logic. Gibbs finished the segment as roughly RB9, ahead of Robinson at RB11.
- 2023-12-18 — **Matt Waldman**, same episode [Best Ball]: immediately turns the call around for 2024 — because the market will now pay a premium for Gibbs, he expects the value to sit with Robinson instead. This is a valuation call; he does not walk back the talent evaluation.
- 2023-12-18 — Risk flagged by both hosts, same episode: Ben Johnson was a hot head-coaching candidate (Carolina interest noted), so Gibbs's 2024 offense and play-caller were not settled.
- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]] (recorded before the *Film and Theory* episode below, which cites it): two things, both new. First, the maturity marker — watching Gibbs "slipping and sliding out of tackles," Angelo's note is that when the run isn't there, "he protected himself and went down, and he's okay doing that. Young players a lot of times are not okay making that step. They want to break the big run every time." The named counter-case is [[Breece Hall]]. Second, the scheme leverage: Detroit is "much more successful with Gibbs on the field — not because Gibbs is so much better a player than [[David Montgomery]], but because defenses have to think a lot more when Gibbs is out there." Montgomery "very rarely will break a 25-plus-yard run"; Gibbs widens the range of outcomes a defense has to cover. Angelo also names Gibbs's **re-acceleration** as the specific thing a linebacker dreads. Framework: [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]].
- 2023-12-21 — **Angelo**, same episode, on fit: names Gibbs ("listen to him speak") among the players Detroit drafted to a cultural mold — coachable, unselfish — alongside [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[David Montgomery]] and Jared Goff. See [[Player Development and Coachability]].
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): lists Gibbs among the backs defined by change of direction in tight space at speed ([[Christian McCaffrey]], [[James Cook]], [[Austin Ekeler]], [[Lamar Jackson]]) — the trait Waldman argues cannot survive being scaled up to 210-plus pounds. Corroborates the existing evaluation rather than moving it. See [[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]].
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]): a methodology note rather than a new grade — reveals part of how the original pre-draft evaluation formed. Watching Lions RBs coach Scotty Montgomery's pre-draft interactions with Gibbs, "the enthusiasm and the excitement and the way they hit it right off," shaded Waldman's view of the fit positively before the draft even happened — offered as a general evaluation principle (intangibles/relationship fit matter, not just tape) rather than new information about Gibbs's play. See [[Player Development and Coachability]].
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): a brief victory-lap callback after Detroit's NFC Championship loss — "some of us were really in on him from the get-go" — paired again with [[David Montgomery]] as a duo that "gives you all the pieces," part of the case that Detroit's core is set up for continued success regardless of coordinator churn.

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: pushes back on the pass-catching hype from his rookie year — "we talked about... this guy's going to be a big-time pass catcher in the league... but they just kind of really used him as a running back" — cited alongside [[Saquon Barkley]] as a caution that even highly-touted receiving backs rarely have as much passing-game impact as expected.
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]): While breaking down Detroit's offense, Harmon said he 'loves' David Montgomery and thought the Lions were right to give him the bulk of 2023 touches (235 to Gibbs' 234), but wants to see the team find more ways to get the ball into Gibbs' hands going forward.
- 2024-05-20 — According to guest Jagger May (Football Guys, untracked) and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-20]]) [Dynasty]: with the Lions signaling an increased workload for Jahmyr Gibbs in 2024, both still rate him a top-five dynasty running back and note he and [[David Montgomery]] rank at the top of the league in explosive-run rate together — but they pushed back on using the workload news to declare Montgomery obsolete, since Gibbs isn't the between-the-tackles, goal-line hammer that a Jared Goff-led, non-running-QB offense also needs.
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): pushed back on offseason chatter about Gibbs seeing a bigger receiving role (including slot usage), projecting only about 61 catches, 454 yards receiving -- not a 700-yard-receiver breakout -- because the Lions want Gibbs and David Montgomery to work as their outside-the-numbers options while Amon-Ra St. Brown and Sam LaPorta stay inside; downfield outside production is more likely to come from Donovan Peoples-Jones/Jameson Williams. He projects Gibbs at roughly 1,500 total yards and 10-12 touchdowns -- 'RB1 caliber' but not a top-three overall fantasy talent, ranking him RB4 in his consensus rankings, closely bunched with [[Saquon Barkley]] given both have strong offensive lines. Bob Harris agreed, saying he won't get 'over enthusiastic' about the receiving-role hype until he sees it.
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman says Gibbs has an 'outside shot' at RB1-overall only if [[David Montgomery]] gets hurt, and ranks him lowest of the group discussed (behind [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Breece Hall]] and [[Bijan Robinson]]) for that specific outcome, despite Gibbs going higher in ADP than Montgomery. He says Gibbs's receiving role gives him a somewhat higher floor, but pushes back on fans who 'fall in love' with his speed and write off Montgomery, arguing Montgomery remains a high-floor top-3-to-5 back even as Gibbs's touches grow -- while conceding Gibbs's ceiling is still the higher of the two.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): revisiting his draft-night take, Angelo says the number one thing he said about Gibbs beyond the physical talent was that he is tough as hell — a tough out and a physical player who runs with an attitude despite being only a bit over 200 pounds. That toughness is exactly what Dan Campbell values, and Angelo says it will endear Gibbs to Detroit the way [[David Montgomery]], [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and [[Sam LaPorta]] have been. He frames the pick in context: Detroit traded [[DeAndre Swift]] on draft night — a back known for missing assignments and keys and not playing hard down to down — and drafted Gibbs in the first round despite already pairing Montgomery and Swift, a move widely questioned at the time.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]): news broke during the show that [[David Montgomery]] is out indefinitely with an apparent MCL injury — 'so get your Gibbs on.' Waldman treats Gibbs as the automatic beneficiary and the clear fantasy-playoff play, with Craig Reynolds the popular secondary add and [[Sione Vaki]] the cheap luxury-spot alternative if you want a lottery ticket rather than the obvious name.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Gibbs is his favorite running back to watch in the league right now — when he reaches the gap between guard and tackle he shoots through it, and Harmon expects a lot of big plays against a Washington defense that has struggled against the run. He rated Gibbs a level above [[Bucky Irving]], who he still called a great player. Washington gloved up the interior against Tampa Bay, but Harmon says you cannot sell out against Detroit's interior because the Lions are so good at getting to the perimeter. James Koh (not a tracked expert) guessed Gibbs could see the ball 30 times, though [[David Montgomery]] is likely back. Harmon's caveat when taking the under on a 55.5 total: there are only so many explosive plays available while grinding clock.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman thinks the industry has 'a little [[Tony Pollard]] syndrome' about Gibbs — Gibbs is a higher-end version of Pollard for electricity and big-play spark, but neither is a back you want north of 260 carries regularly, and Detroit's late-season lean on him was situational rather than the plan. He argues the Lions want [[David Montgomery]] taking the punishing work. Untracked co-host Bob Harris flagged that Footballguys' projections (Jason Wood's) have Gibbs as RB1 and the overall 1.01, which Harris called 'interestingly aggressive' and not something he is totally against, but he thinks it overlooks Montgomery still being there. Harris noted the possible Detroit coordinator brain drain may be overstated. Waldman added a price note: he drafted Gibbs in round four as a rookie and felt good about it, but would not have taken [[Bijan Robinson]] in round one.
- 2025-05-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-26]]): Waldman is 'a little more concerned' about [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] than [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] under new coordinator John Morton, and he understands why Gibbs is sliding — he has seen drafts where [[Ashton Jeanty]] goes ahead of him, which are not drafts Waldman would make, but he grants the argument that Jeanty has a clearer path to workload because [[David Montgomery]] still exists. He stresses the committee has not slowed either Lion down; both have produced at a high level together. His own caveat is a draft-capital one: if he is picking very early and thinks he can get Montgomery as an RB3 later, he might prefer another high-upside player in that early first-round range. Bottom line — nobody in Detroit is play-caller proof, but he does not expect Gibbs to slow down this year.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]): reports that Gibbs is lining up at receiver more should not raise his price — he is already drafted to his ceiling as roughly RB3 overall, so there is not much higher to go. Waldman even argues it could cut the other way: if Detroit spreads him out and targets him like a receiver, [[Jared Goff]] starts thinking of him as a receiver, and Gibbs probably will not execute those routes as well as a true wideout would. His read is that the alignment change is about stressing and tricking defenses, which creates opportunities for [[Sam LaPorta]], [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and [[Isaac TeSlaa]] more than for Gibbs himself. Bob Harris (not tracked) adds the bear case that Gibbs' 2024 value leaned heavily on 20 touchdowns, which is not a sticky stat, and that there are universes where he, [[Saquon Barkley]] and [[Bijan Robinson]] all miss expectations.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Gibbs is the archetype of the weapon back — split out to the slot he becomes a coverage dictator, which takes pressure off [[Jared Goff]] by telling the quarterback what he is looking at, something Angelo said is under-discussed. On 2025 volume, he called Gibbs 'neutral': he cannot project higher after Gibbs absorbed so many carries over the final six weeks in [[David Montgomery]]'s absence, though he expects more pass-catching involvement than Ben Johnson used him for. [[Matt Waldman]] also landed on neutral. Angelo added a contract note — Gibbs is likely headed for potentially the richest running back contract in NFL history, which is what squeezes Detroit's ability to also pay [[Jameson Williams]] and Sam LaPorta [Dynasty].
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): Harris rates Gibbs a 4 out of 10 on risk — he is the satellite half of a platoon, much better than [[Tyler Allgeier]] and with a great ceiling, but Harris allows for the coordinator having mattered and for [[David Montgomery]] (the only Lion who gets hurt, which actually helps Gibbs) staying healthy. Guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) initially rated him a 1, reasoning that if Chase, Bijan and Barkley are off the board there is no risk in taking Gibbs at four; pushed to evaluate him in a vacuum, Bell revised to a 3, granting valid concerns about the Detroit situation. Bell has Gibbs as his RB2 and says he would flip Gibbs to a 4 if it meant passing on Barkley. Harris notes the exercise does not capture upside.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Detroit, not Green Bay, is the story of this game, and the offense looked real broken, with a bottled-up run game and a hundred check-down screens to Gibbs that went nowhere. Guest **Sam Gold** (not tracked) explains the design: the Packers played a lot of two-deep cover-two and cover-four to take the deep ball away from [[Jameson Williams]] and funnel everything underneath, which is why [[Sam LaPorta]] had nine targets and Gibbs got roughly 10 check-down throws that were always kind of open but kind of not, while pressure came from the four-man rush and Micah Parsons. Gold's larger worry is the offensive line no longer being elite after Frank Ragnow's retirement, plus play calling that felt vanilla without Ben Johnson. Both stress it is one data point against a good defense: Harris says this is not the moment to sell or bench Lions you drafted high.
