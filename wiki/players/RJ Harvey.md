# RJ Harvey

## Expert Takes

- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: he likes Harvey a lot and thinks he can be a contributor, but suspects that is where it stops. Angelo credits explosiveness and effectiveness in open areas, while questioning whether the high-end talent is there to start for multiple years — noting that going back to 2019 very few backs are still starters in the league at all. His projected outcome is a career contributor in the mould of [[Devin Singletary]]: bounces around, not fancy, but a reliable piece.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): Harvey is the back he would target if Baltimore adds to its run game, praising his RPO and read-option work — holding the end and making good decisions — plus a play-speed dimension that adds something the Ravens' top-three rushing attack does not already have, even though he is a smaller back. Angelo's key point is class context: Harvey is 'probably a second-round type talent in most classes, early third', but in this depth he might go in the fourth. [[Matt Waldman]] extends it — Baltimore could take Harvey to play the Justice Hill/Keaton Mitchell role and still add a bigger back like [[Damien Martinez]] in the same range for a true A/B pairing behind the 31-year-old [[Derrick Henry]].
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]): 'feel it' — the market is getting ahead of itself. Harvey went inside RB2 territory before the end of the third round in a post-draft mock, and Waldman says he will rise on his own board from a low pre-draft grade but that people are seduced by his speed and quickness. He likes Harvey's hands and calls him a good zone runner but not yet a good gap runner, though he thinks those are reasonably easy fixes. The bigger caution is Sean Payton: Payton is good at picking backs but ran three at once with Ingram, Pierre Thomas and Ivory, praised [[Blake Watson]] as an [[Alvin Kamara]] reminder, and does not care about draft capital — he will ride Harvey hard, and if Harvey tries to out-athlete everyone without discipline the capital will not save him. Waldman notes Kamara only clicked after being yelled at for bouncing runs, and thinks Kamara was the exception, not the rule. He is fine taking Harvey in round three or four but not as an RB3 overall, explicitly disagreeing with Derek Brown (not tracked).
- 2025-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest JJ Zachariason (Late Round Draft Guide, not a tracked expert) called Harvey a neutral pre-draft prospect with an okay-but-not-elite receiving profile who landed in an unbelievable spot — Sean Payton's teams have never ranked worse than sixth in RB target share across his 13 seasons since 2011, and [[Javonte Williams]] drew 70 targets there last year — with [[Jaleel McLaughlin]] a change-of-pace and [[Audric Estime]] no pass catcher at all. Waldman's film view: Harvey is poor in pass protection, a 'one-and-done' blocker late to diagnose blitzes, but a good pass catcher who attacks away from his frame, with correctable Terrell Owens-style focus drops; oddly a better zone than gap runner who bounces gap plays and gets away with it on quickness. Waldman projects roughly 75 targets, 58 catches, just under 200 carries at 4.25-4.3 yards per attempt, about 1,200-1,300 total yards. Both put him around RB18-19; both reject the [[Alvin Kamara]] comp — Zachariason said he is 'more Pierre Thomas than Kamara,' a speed-score back rather than Kamara's balance and receiving profile.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): Harvey's upside comp on his best days is Tarik Cohen, with a mid-range Darren Sproles as the realistic middle and Ito Smith — the last player briefly anointed the next Sproles — as the downside. What he must improve is the gap game, red-zone and goal-line work, and ball security, all of which can get worse while he processes the NFL game, which is exactly why a veteran like [[J.K. Dobbins]] alongside him is a good thing. Waldman projects about 140 carries for roughly 600 yards and a rushing touchdown, plus 38 receptions for around 300 yards — about 900 scrimmage yards, so a nice add whose projection is modest. Sweet spot: draft him as an RB3 or RB4, not an RB2. Bob Harris (not tracked) notes 'the Riz is gone' — post-draft hype is fading and the price should keep sliding. Waldman cites Dave Kluge's framing of Dobbins as the Pierre Thomas to Harvey's Sproles and says he could see it.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): Sean Payton's praise — 'he's doing well, we haven't given him his joker wings or any of that yet' — signals real progress in the receiving role, but also reinforces that [[J.K. Dobbins]] is Denver's number one back. Waldman explicitly reverses part of his own post-draft move: he had bumped Harvey up in the RSP post-draft publication when Denver had nobody else who profiled as a compelling 1A, and with Dobbins signed he now places Harvey back where he had him pre-draft — a source of occasional fantasy production rather than a back you lean on. The reasons for the slow ramp are film-based: Harvey is not a good pass blocker, ball security has been an issue that Waldman expects to surface more in games than in practice, and immature decision-making on carry management may show up in the red and black zones, bouncing plays outside or cutting back where there is no cutback. He is fast and shifty and not a badly flawed decision-maker overall, and the Dobbins tandem is called a great learning opportunity.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harvey is his No. 3 bust and the only rookie on his list. Harris is blunt that he does not know Harvey from Adam and doubts anyone advocating a fifth-round (sometimes fourth-round) price knew who he was before the draft, since he played at Central Florida — when Harris had [[Matt Waldman]] on pre-draft to discuss 20 running backs, Harvey never came up. He questions the third-down-back thesis: Harvey has barely pass blocked and never caught more than about 20 passes in a college season, and he is already 24, so there may not be much more coming. Harris lists prior near-third-round backs who did not deliver — [[Trey Benson]], [[Blake Corum]], [[Marshawn Lloyd]], [[Zach Charbonnet]], [[Kendre Miller]], [[Tyjae Spears]], [[Tank Bigsby]], [[Trey Sermon]], [[A.J. Dillon]] — as the base rate. Guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not tracked) allows Harvey has significant upside but notes the absence of any camp drumbeat, positive or negative, plus plenty of positive [[J.K. Dobbins]] press including third-down talk, and says he cannot project Harvey's Week 1 role at all under Sean Payton.
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: an excellent pass receiver whose quickness, more than his straight speed, is the best part of his game, and a good schematic fit for Sean Payton. The oddity is that he is a better zone runner than gap runner — he attempts strange cutbacks on gap plays where you are not supposed to cut back at all — and Waldman thinks it will take him a while to process things more intelligently. Projection: roughly 900 total yards, in the same neighbourhood as [[TreVeyon Henderson]] but with fewer touchdowns, with [[J.K. Dobbins]] capable of 1,100-1,200 yards as a good all-around back if he can stay intact. Waldman defends Harvey's receiving projection against the low-target-count objection, citing [[Jonathan Taylor]], Melvin Gordon and [[Leonard Fournette]] as backs whose catching technique on limited reps told you more than their target totals did.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): 'feeling it' that Harvey is better than his preseason buzz. Waldman still expects [[J.K. Dobbins]] to have the biggest role and rates him as a player and talent, but thinks Harvey will do far more as a receiver, called his running 'just fine' on tape with pretty good decision-making, and described him as a dynamic player who should produce some solid fantasy games as the No. 2 and grow into a bigger role — and if Dobbins gets hurt, Denver may force Harvey into volume he is up to handling. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) disagreed on where the value sits: he has accumulated far more Dobbins over camp as the cheaper piece of the same puzzle, sometimes four rounds later, and said he is looking elsewhere at Harvey's price range.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): the Denver backfield went 37-27 in snaps for [[J.K. Dobbins]] over Harvey and 18-7 in touches for Dobbins — and Tyler Badie touched the ball before Harvey did. Harvey's moment came with nine minutes left, a 50-yard carry showing good speed that set up a Dobbins red-zone run. Harris allows that things can change and it was Harvey's first NFL game, but says it is hard to trust 'the six-foot invisible rabbit' in a fantasy lineup just yet.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): Harvey played 17 snaps to [[J.K. Dobbins]]'s 25 and, more tellingly, got six touches to Dobbins's 15 — and the alternate back who made the big play was actually Tyler Badie on a third-quarter screen. Harris allows that Denver would not have spent a second-round pick on Harvey if they were not going to use him, and says he cannot yet tell you Harvey can't play, only what the first two games look like: 'hard for me to view him as more than a supplemental running back right now while Dobbins has kind of looked like the man.' He is not sure how you could get Harvey into a Week 3 lineup.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harvey — whom Harris nicknamed 'the invisible six-foot rabbit' — started against the Chargers and played more than [[J.K. Dobbins]] on the first two series, both three-and-outs, but Harris said flatly that he has not seen Harvey make anybody miss at all yet. By the start of the third quarter Denver moved on to Dobbins, who immediately broke a 41-yard run. A negative usage and evaluation signal for a rookie who was being drafted as the eventual lead back.
- 2025-09-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-26]]): Harris says the market still likes Harvey more than [[Bhayshul Tuten]] but that the market has been really stupid about Harvey for basically the entire year; he himself values Tuten slightly higher. He allows that a 2-1 or 3-0 team might reasonably buy Harvey low and store him, and concedes he cannot promise these rookies don't take over their jobs and look great in the second half — but equally, they have not been usable in fantasy yet and could stay that way all year.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): Harris reads Sean Payton's fourth-quarter game management as deliberate development work — Payton used the final three quarters as 'a lesson in physicality' for his own team and to get the rookie Harvey, whom Harris calls 'the six-foot rabbit', a bunch more touches, including short targets where Harvey is obviously not comfortable. That meant Denver could have delivered the death blow far earlier but instead ran and punted repeatedly at 21-3. Harvey did convert a garbage-time touchdown with [[Bo Nix]] at the very end, after [[J.K. Dobbins]] had already hit 100 yards and been pulled, and was the target on a missed deep connection in the first half (Aikman thought Harvey pulled up; Harris is unsure).
- 2025-11-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the Raiders game showed 'the danger of the low-volume touchdown streak' — Harvey finished with six touches for 18 yards. Harris jokes that Harvey is 'the six-foot invisible rabbit' and that [[Sean Payton]] might have him on his fantasy team, because Denver's one snap inside the five was a play-action screen to Harvey that was poorly blocked and lost yardage, with [[J.K. Dobbins]] then leaving the field before the touchdown throw to [[Troy Franklin]]. The take: Harvey's fantasy value is riding almost entirely on scoring, and the underlying usage does not support it when the touchdowns stop.
