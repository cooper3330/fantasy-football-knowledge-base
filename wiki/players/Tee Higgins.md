# Tee Higgins

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: acknowledges a disappointing 2023 overall but points out Higgins had been **WR21 since [[Jake Browning]] took over** — the quarterback change did not sink him. The qualifier: expect boom/bust rather than steady production, because Browning can't deliver the on-the-move deep ball 25–40 yards downfield that Joe Burrow throws for Higgins to go up and get.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): free-agency context — named one of the top three receivers plausibly available in free agency (with [[Mike Evans]] and Michael Pittman Jr., not a tracked page), though Cincinnati is expected to use the franchise tag on him. Waldman notes that hasn't stopped other teams from talking about him anyway, floating incoming Tennessee HC Brian Callahan (Higgins's outgoing Cincinnati OC) as someone with informed thoughts on his value.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]), a specific trade scenario rather than a talent read: floats [[Jared Goff]]'s Detroit as a team that could "take a big swing" and trade a first-round pick for him if Cincinnati tags him and then looks to move him, given Detroit's stated need at X receiver — while conceding it's unlikely to actually happen ("he probably won't" reach that market at all).
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): the franchise-tag question remains unresolved this close to the deadline — Waldman leans toward expecting the tag but explicitly hedges ("who knows, funny things happen"). Notes his dynasty read on [[Ja'Marr Chase]] doesn't hinge on Higgins staying or going either way.
- 2024-02-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-27]]): fresh 2023 Reception Perception charting shows a down year — 68.7% success rate vs. man coverage (his lowest since his rookie year), 68.8% vs. press (career low), 73.9% vs. zone (career low), plus a career-low 55% catch rate and 656 yards after back-to-back 1,000-yard seasons. Harmon partly attributes it to playing through injury but won't fully excuse it. Draws a firm talent-tier line: Higgins is "top-20-ish" but not a "transformative" player like [[Tyreek Hill]] or [[A.J. Brown]] were when they forced trades, and has no proof of concept as a true WR1 without [[Ja'Marr Chase]] — more a "vertical throwback X" who wins contested catches than a true X. Koh is surprised Cincinnati didn't push harder for an extension while his value was down rather than tagging him; both expect the Bengals to listen on trade offers (~late-1st/early-2nd capital, Carolina's No. 33 pick floated) but see no realistic path to a Tyreek/A.J. Brown-level trade return.

- 2024-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-07]]): brief
  follow-up — expects Higgins to be officially franchise-tagged by
  Cincinnati (not yet confirmed at recording time) and floats a possible
  tag-and-trade, without landing on a firm outlook. Reiterates the down 2023
  season is "excusable" as injury rather than a talent decline. Asked to pick
  Higgins or [[Nico Collins]] straight-up, Harmon says the public perception
  of a talent gap between them "hasn't quite caught up" to reality — Collins's
  2023 season was better than any season either player has had, and Harmon
  says he's "very tempted" to take Collins right now if forced to choose,
  while still calling both "great number ones" as outside X receivers.

- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]): responds to Higgins requesting a trade from Cincinnati (filed the same day) — "you're not a wide receiver one, T. Higgins, you're fantasy people" saying that label. Grades him a "very good wide receiver two" who should stay in Cincinnati, "his best opportunity to compete," unless he somehow lands with Kansas City (which Waldman doubts, since the Chiefs need a field-stretcher more than a contested-catch/over-the-middle receiver — a role [[Rashee Rice]] already fills). Comps him against Mike Williams (not a tracked page) as the standard he's "aspiring to be" — not as strong a runner after the catch, almost as good at the catch point, maybe not quite as good a route runner — and says Higgins "wants great player money" for what's really WR2-level play.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast on the 2024 WR draft class ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): a direct message during a tangent on career-span economics — "you're on a team that is going to give you probably the best shot you're going to get in your career to get to the Super Bowl and win it," urging Higgins to stay with Cincinnati and [[Joe Burrow]]/[[Ja'Marr Chase]] rather than chase maximum free-agent money. Acknowledges Higgins deserves good money and likely already has enough for generational wealth if he manages it well, and hopes Higgins won't regret leaving 30 years from now.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]): restates the "Robin, not Batman" framing from his earlier trade-request take — players like Higgins "are a better Robin than a Batman, and that's totally okay," and fans should "stop listening to the fantasy analyst telling you you're a wide receiver one." Frames it as part of a broader NFL shift toward a two-alpha receiver system (citing Brian Callahan's Titans core of Hopkins/Ridley as the model) rather than a single true WR1 funnel. Named again in the same episode as [[Keon Coleman]]'s outcome comp if Coleman lands with [[Joe Burrow]] in a Higgins-replacement role.

- 2024-04-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-15]]: "feeling" Higgins being a Bengal this year — he dropped his trade request the day before recording, after seeking a long-term deal that wasn't progressing. Waldman reads it as someone realizing the wide receiver market (fresh Jefferson/Chase deals) is about to reset in his favor if he plays 2024 on the $21.9M franchise tag rather than forcing a move now, and calls staying with [[Joe Burrow]] his best realistic shot at a Super Bowl.
- 2024-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-16]]): unlike Aiyuk, [[Tee Higgins]] appears resigned to playing on Cincinnati's franchise tag after requesting a trade; Harmon says the Bengals would be smart to keep and extend him long-term alongside [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Joe Burrow]] as their core.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]): Waldman thinks a Tee Higgins trade this year is plausible if Jermaine Burton develops into a fit for Zac Taylor's over-the-middle, after-the-catch scheme, calling Higgins a great boundary jump-ball receiver and route runner but less versatile than what Cincinnati may ultimately want; Waldman comps Ja'Marr Chase to a higher-ceiling DeAndre Hopkins and projects Burton as more of a Will Fuller-type field-stretcher if he pans out. Waldman calls this likely Higgins' 'swan song' in Cincinnati regardless, since the Bengals can't realistically pay both Chase and Higgins at the roughly $30M/year top-of-market range. He expects Higgins to play out 2024 on the franchise tag (about $21.8M guaranteed) before landing a lucrative deal elsewhere in 2025, potentially in the Amon-Ra St. Brown range given how fast the market is inflating.
- 2024-06-18 — According to James Koh (co-host, not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-18]]), on [[Tee Higgins]] finally signing his 2024 franchise tag tender with the Cincinnati Bengals: Koh views Higgins as 'a tier below' the truly elite/superstar receivers, but says he 'could be some team's number one' even though he's 'probably not an ideal number one.' Koh believes Higgins could have commanded top-market money on the open market or via trade, and criticizes the franchise tag system as letting the Bengals keep him for a season with no long-term commitment.
- 2024-07-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-04]]) [Dynasty]: the hosts expect Cincinnati to let Higgins walk or trade him mid-season rather than re-sign him, which would open significant target volume beyond [[Ja'Marr Chase]] for [[Jermaine Burton]], [[Andrei Iosivas]], and [[Charlie Jones]] to split. On durability, Angelo argued larger-framed receivers like Higgins carry more mass and force through their joints and tendons and are more prone to soft-tissue injury than smaller players, contrasting him with the much healthier career of [[DeVonta Smith]], and said the deciding factor for Higgins is always whether he stays healthy through the playoffs, which has varied year to year. If traded, both floated contenders needing a proven second receiver as plausible landing spots -- the Jets (if Rodgers needs a trusted target), the Bengals themselves, the Steelers, or a healthy [[Christian Kirk]]-led Falcons.
- 2024-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-23]]): Harmon is deliberately below consensus, ranking Higgins 13th on his Yahoo list of the top 15 receivers 25 and under — behind [[Nico Collins]] (6), [[Jaylen Waddle]] (7), [[Puka Nacua]] (8), [[Drake London]] (9), [[DeVonta Smith]] (10), [[Chris Olave]] (11) and [[Tank Dell]] (12), with [[Zay Flowers]] just behind at 14 — while still calling him a top-24 NFL receiver and a very good No. 2. 2023 charting was a career low across the board (50th percentile vs man, 19th vs zone, 57th vs press), which Harmon excuses as nagging injuries rather than decline at 25; he treats the 2021-22 version (72.2% and 71.4% vs man, 72.4% and 73.5% vs press) as the real player. His skepticism is about transportability: a mostly vertical boundary X running nines, corners and outs with good-not-great contested-catch work needs a quarterback with arm talent and nerve, so Higgins would go crazy with [[Will Levis]] but might not be maximized with [[Bryce Young]]. James Koh (not tracked) disagreed directly, arguing Higgins' style works league-wide and that he elevates any offense regardless of quarterback, and would take him over Flowers; Harmon also called [[Michael Pittman Jr.]]'s 2022 charting comparable or better and leaned Pittman, while conceding it is close.
- 2024-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): Higgins is a good receiver but [[Ja'Marr Chase]] far clears him as a player, and with Chase out Higgins slides up to be Cincinnati's number one X receiver while also moving around. Harmon thinks it is very unlikely Higgins is on the team in 2025 — he is playing on the franchise tag with essentially no extension talk, just a one-year run at a Super Bowl. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) argues that even without Chase this remains a functioning wide receiver room, unlike Washington or Dallas, because Higgins can anchor outside with [[Jermaine Burton]] and [[Andrei Iosivas]] filling around him.
- 2024-11-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-18]]): asked whether Higgins's best future is in Cincinnati, Waldman split it — financially no, he should take his chances on the open market, but football-wise yes. His reasoning is supply: there are maybe six or seven quarterbacks of [[Joe Burrow]]'s quality in the league and they already have good receivers, so the quality of targets and volume Higgins gets even as the option opposite [[Ja'Marr Chase]] is hard to replace. On the player himself Waldman raved about the hands and ball attack, describing a catch he intended to feature in his Footballguys top-10 where the ball was three-quarters of the way to Higgins with his hands still at his sides — he was not expecting it that fast because of pressure — and he still snatched it in front of his face mask, where most receivers would have had it lodged in the mask or bounce off.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]) [Dynasty]: Higgins is the clear top receiver in the 2025 free agent class and the only thing left for him to prove down the stretch is health — Harmon also wondered whether a Bengals team falling out of the playoff race keeps playing a franchise-tagged player at all. He expects Higgins to reach the open market since a second tag is implausible, but said he is nervous about him as a 30-plus-million-per-year receiver: from his own recent charting he does not see Higgins in the tier of [[Tyreek Hill]], [[Brandon Aiyuk]], [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[A.J. Brown]], [[CeeDee Lamb]] and [[Justin Jefferson]], and doubts he reaches the 35-million Jefferson/Lamb territory. Landing-spot talk: New England is the fit he can already picture, the Raiders sit second in cap space, and while Chargers-plus-[[Ladd McConkey]]-plus-[[Justin Herbert]] is fun in theory, Harmon flatly does not see the Jim Harbaugh / Joe Hortiz brain trust paying a receiver 30 million. He also tied it to Cincinnati still owing [[Ja'Marr Chase]] an extension.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]): purely on money, Higgins should take the free-agent payday in Carolina, New England or Las Vegas — but Waldman argues he would be wiser to stay in Cincinnati. His evaluation: Higgins is 'an excellent wide receiver two,' what people hoped Mike Williams would be, roughly a top-15-to-20 NFL receiver; people calling him a WR1 misunderstand receiver play, since much of his production is aided by [[Ja'Marr Chase]] drawing coverage and by [[Joe Burrow]]. Elsewhere he would be asked to do more as a route runner and after-catch playmaker than Waldman believes he can, so his label and long-term earnings would fall after the one big payday. He also thinks Cincinnati's offense can contend like the San Diego Chargers of their youth despite an awful defense — Higgins's best title chance is staying.
- 2025-01-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-09]]): Higgins wins 2024 Best Ball Winner. Harmon flags this award as explicitly unquantified — 'no stats, pure vibes' — based on the sheer number of plays Higgins makes in tight contested coverage, and concedes he may be biased by one week 18 grab. The bulk of the segment is a free agency argument: Harmon finds pairing Higgins with a young rising quarterback like [[Drake Maye]] intriguing, is less sold on a [[Bryce Young]] fit, but ultimately wants him to stay in Cincinnati. The reasoning is a skill argument about the thrower — there is an art to throwing a receiver into a winnable contested situation rather than simply into coverage, and [[Joe Burrow]] and Higgins have played together long enough that Higgins knows the timing and placement and when to go get it. Harmon's summary: when he was on the field, Higgins was the best ball winner in the league this year.
- 2025-01-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-13]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon does not believe Higgins gets out of Cincinnati, and names him the top free agent receiver available ahead of [[Chris Godwin]]. He calls Higgins probably the best ball winner in the NFL last season — a conclusion he reached doing a receiver awards series with James Koh (NOT a tracked expert) — and says [[Keon Coleman]] is a similar ball winner who might get there but currently has less nuance and separation in his game. Harmon does add a caveat on the tier question: he is not convinced Higgins is a better football player than [[Ladd McConkey]]. [[Matt Waldman]] frames Higgins as a nice but specialized player relative to McConkey's completeness, and argues that Higgins staying is Cincinnati's best chance at playoff life because that team wins with offense as long as [[Joe Burrow]] is there.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Higgins will not reach the open market — Harmon expects Cincinnati to franchise tag him a second time and try to work out a long-term deal, and said he charts tagged players anyway because they stay in the news. His reasoning is organizational rather than statistical: it would be a bold move for Mike Brown to give the middle finger to [[Joe Burrow]], the most important person in the building, who has publicly said you cannot let good players walk, and Harmon does not think the offense works without both Higgins and [[Ja'Marr Chase]]. James Koh (not a tracked expert) initially guessed Higgins would hit free agency before agreeing the tag is likelier. Harmon named him first when listing a 2025 free-agent class he otherwise called a past-time list.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-23]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon slotted Higgins second in the complementary number-two tier, behind [[DeVonta Smith]] and ahead of [[Jaylen Waddle]] and [[Jordan Addison]], and was explicit that he still sees him as a two rather than a latent number one. The reasoning is athletic: Higgins is not as dangerous in the open field as the others in the group and does not offer the zero-to-sixty ability Harmon wants from a true alpha, and he grades as a good-not-great separator — he 'more than crosses the threshold' of a good starting perimeter receiver but is not one of the high-end separators. Harmon flagged he was mid-way through finishing Higgins's Reception Perception charting for the season, so the take could move, and acknowledged Higgins is going to get paid.
- 2025-02-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-03]]): good chance the Bengals franchise-tag Higgins and keep both him and [[Ja'Marr Chase]], with the second tag costing 120% of a prior salary already north of $20M. Waldman has never bought the 'Higgins could be a WR1 elsewhere' framing — he calls him 'a damn fine wide receiver two in the role of a wide receiver two,' but not the guy you want beating man coverage on timing routes as a true WR1, and thinks most NFL teams understand that better than fans and some media do. He also frames it from Higgins's side: there is nowhere offering big money plus a two-to-three-year Super Bowl window, so staying in Cincinnati is the best path to a legacy as a winner.
- 2025-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-05]]): Harmon has no conviction on Higgins' future and says he flips week to week — one week he cannot see Cincinnati letting him leave, the next he notes this is the one team where a receiver might actually bounce, because on the open market Higgins would become a top-five paid receiver even if he is not a top-five receiver. He dismisses the Bengals-fan argument that firing his agent signals a hometown discount: the next agent who tells a client to take less money will be the first. The franchise tag remains available. Harmon stresses the fantasy-relevant consequence: Higgins can play both flanker and X, and that flexibility is a big part of what unlocked [[Ja'Marr Chase]]'s career season.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): asked about Higgins to the receiver-needy Packers, Waldman says 'f**k it' — he would rather Green Bay bring [[Davante Adams]] back to finish his career as the WR1. His reasoning is a role argument, not a talent knock: Higgins is an excellent WR2 and would be a No. 1 by target volume without [[Ja'Marr Chase]], but he does not run WR1 routes — he is not the timing-route specialist with hard breaks working back to the quarterback on curls, deep outs and in-cuts, and good safeties cover him well on those. Waldman's analogy: assuming a great baker is a great chef confuses two different mediums, and Higgins is the baker of wide receivers. That makes him underrated as a WR1 in name but a different job than the one a receiver-needy team is shopping for.
- 2025-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-05]]): Higgins was franchise-tagged for a second straight year at 120% of the prior tag, roughly $26M for 2025, and is openly unhappy about it. Harmon puts the odds Higgins is on the Bengals roster in 2025 at 85-90%, but will not rule out a tag-and-trade if talks break down, and says he struggles to see Higgins actually playing under the tag. His read from Zac Taylor's and Duke Tobin's Combine availabilities is that the front office is in lockstep and the holdup is ownership: the Browns' — Bengals' — family ownership is 'house rich and cash poor', and any guaranteed money must sit in escrow, with [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and Trey Hendrickson also queued up. Cincinnati likely must finish Chase's deal first. James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) argued the Bengals have no backup plan — [[Jermaine Burton]] was supposed to be it — so they are effectively forced to keep Higgins.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]): Higgins signed a $115 million contract, making him the league's highest-paid WR2, alongside the [[Ja'Marr Chase]] extension. Waldman 'feels it' — he argues Cincinnati had no choice, because if they had let Higgins walk to fund defense they had no comparable receiver to replace him and would have blunted the only unit carrying the team. He frames the Bengals as a modern Dan Fouts-era Chargers, all offense and no defense, likely to score enormously without winning enough. Bob Harris (untracked co-host) calls the question moot for fantasy purposes: the team spending on skill players is unambiguously good for [[Joe Burrow]], Chase and Higgins production, and the extensions lower both cap numbers in 2025.
- 2025-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-19]]): Higgins re-signed for four years and $115M (~$29M AAV, first two years reportedly guaranteed) and, in Harmon's view, plainly took less to stay — with Arizona at $63M and the Chargers at $54M in cap space he would have cleared [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and possibly [[A.J. Brown]]'s $32M. Koh estimates ~$15M left on the table and is surprised Higgins did not chase being 'the man' elsewhere; Harmon counters that the grass is not always greener and doubts Higgins is a true No. 1 anywhere, calling him instead maybe the best No. 2 receiver in the league and a tier or two below [[Ja'Marr Chase]]. Charting: 2024 was his best season — 79.1% of snaps outside but only 63.7% on the line, 72.4% success vs man (improved every year of his career from 68% in 2020) and 78% vs press (86th percentile), 61% on the nine, 91% on the curl, and seventh among receivers at 2.57 yards per route run from the slot. Caveats: 656 then 911 receiving yards in back-to-back sub-1,000 seasons, five games missed in each of the last two years.
- 2025-07-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-09]]): Higgins is the template for a maximized number two — 2024 Reception Perception marks of 72.4% versus man, 74.8% versus zone and 78.6% versus press, meaning he is not a consistent zone separator either, but it does not matter because he plays X and wins against press and man, which frees [[Ja'Marr Chase]] to move around the formation. Harmon uses him as the direct comparison for what [[George Pickens]] could be in Dallas next to [[CeeDee Lamb]], noting the two receivers' 2024 charting profiles are within shouting distance of each other even if their statistical output has not been similar.
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-13]]): with Higgins re-signed, Cincinnati may have the best receiver duo in the league, though Harmon would personally take [[DeVonta Smith]] over Higgins — explicitly framed as 'no slight' to Higgins, just a preference. Higgins is the size-and-press piece that lets the Bengals run an old-school vertical attack without scheming receivers open: 86th percentile success rate versus press while aligned on the line of scrimmage on 63.7% of his sampled snaps. He played 12 games in 2024, averaging roughly nine targets a game when available, for about 1,150 yards and 10 touchdowns. Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) both wished Cincinnati would manufacture more designed targets for Higgins, but accept that [[Joe Burrow]] wants to play this way and the talent lets them. Harmon also credited Burrow with drawing a line in the sand to keep the pair together.
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-20]]): Higgins ranks third among the league's best number-two receivers. 72.4% success versus man and 78.6% versus press in 2024, a classic X archetype you can throw up to even though Cincinnati moves him and [[Ja'Marr Chase]] around rather than pinning him at X. Harmon argues Higgins is undersold as an intermediate separator and has genuinely improved there — a 91.3% success rate on the curl, working back to the quarterback reliably against man, on top of the expected nine, post and corner production. He may be the best contested catch receiver in the NFL: an 81.8% contested catch rate at high volume and consistently good across his career, with a catch radius like [[George Pickens]]'s but better focus, timing and tracking. Harmon still puts [[DeVonta Smith]] ahead, conceding that could be his separation bias, and says Smith, Higgins and [[Chris Godwin]] are effectively interchangeable at the top.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates Higgins a 3 out of 10 — the last chance to get a really good piece of a Bengals offense he will elevate players for, with contingent upside on top, since if [[Ja'Marr Chase]] misses time Higgins is a top-five fantasy receiver. Harris rates him a 5, arguing Higgins gets hurt more often than the average NFL player and is a bit mercurial and come-and-go: 'I think he's a knucklehead, but he has a lot of ceiling'. Both note the big contract is now signed — 'he's a paid knucklehead now'. Harris grants Bell's contingent-upside point as one he had not fully weighted.
