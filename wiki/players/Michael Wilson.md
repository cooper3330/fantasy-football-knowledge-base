# Michael Wilson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Dynasty]: was "pretty keen on him heading in," describing him as a different kind of piece in the Arizona receiver room, and thinks that reading held up — rookie-year role limits and health issues (a recurring problem going back to Stanford) kept him from rising further, nearly hitting 100 yards in the season finale. Calls him a genuinely good route runner in the mold of an "aspiring Michael Thomas," and puts top-24 wide receiver value in play for 2024 if he can stay on the field the full season — the biggest swing factor after health being whether [[Kyler Murray]] is the full-season starting quarterback.
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: Harstad's [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] scores him higher than his draft slot and limited pre-season profile would suggest, with among the highest usage rates in the class; the comp pool below him (Keelan Cole, Donnie Avery) skews weaker than the pool above (Jarvis Landry, D.J. Moore, Brandon Cooks) — several of which are unqualified NFL-starter hits, not just complicated ones. Waldman assigns him an archetype comp of Allen Hurns and, to a lesser degree, Robert Woods — a high-volume possession profile with a real chance at a multi-year strong-volume stretch.

  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): named the
  most promising player in one of the league's worst receiver rooms — good
  rookie film supports him as a "non-embarrassing" starting outside receiver
  and a good number-two next to a rookie-drafted [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], who
  the hosts pair with Arizona at #4 in a top-10 mock scenario. Also flagged as
  a better fit at true X than [[Marquise Brown]], who the previous Arizona
  staff mis-deployed running Marvin Harrison-style in-breaking/slant routes.
- 2024-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-06]]): Wilson was far more solid as a rookie than people realize, and his profile carries real similarities to [[Michael Pittman Jr.]]'s rookie-year RP profile. He was used early as a flanker and near power-slot, a role Harmon expects Arizona to keep exploiting as a big slot, but the important data point is the final three games after Hollywood Brown went down, all with [[Kyler Murray]]: Wilson took 81.8% of sampled snaps outside and 87.3% on the line of scrimmage, and Murray was willing to throw him the ball in tight coverage as the X, with varying success. Harmon says that matters because a functional part-time X lets Arizona move [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] around the formation. He also names a step forward from Wilson as one condition that could make Arizona the division's best receiver room.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-10]]): Harmon liked that Wilson handled X-receiver duties over the final three games of 2023 after [[Marquise Brown]] went out, with [[Kyler Murray]] trusting him enough to throw him jump balls. His expectation was that Wilson having played well in that role would let Arizona move [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] around — and that hasn't happened so far. He notes Wilson is one of the Cardinals who does get the easy looks Harrison never sees, alongside [[Trey McBride]] and [[Greg Dortch]], and that Dortch being a slot-only player further constrains Arizona's personnel deployment.
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]): a little underrated. Waldman says with a better quarterback than [[Kyler Murray]] he might be a top 15-20 annual player and the ultimate WR2 option — not in the way [[Tee Higgins]] wins, but more like an [[Adam Thielen]] type playing the number two beside a [[Justin Jefferson]]-caliber alpha. Bob Harris (not tracked) noted Arizona moved Wilson into the slot more this past week with [[Zay Jones]] back in the mix, which could give him slightly more visibility.
- 2025-02-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-26]]): Wilson did not take the step Harmon expected in 2024, which he called a shame because he thought Wilson had a quietly underrated rookie season in 2023 — including a stretch at the end of that year when [[Marquise Brown]] got hurt and Arizona simply put Wilson at X, where he did all right. Harmon's verdict is measured: not much production to show for 2024, but 'he's fine', a receiver you can get away with starting as your number two, and Harmon would happily upgrade over him if the chance came. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) liked the value angle, pointing out Wilson carries only a $1.5 million cap hit and that he and Harrison are the only receivers under contract with 'a bunch of nobodies' behind them.
- 2025-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-21]]): Harmon still likes Wilson and thinks his quiet 2024 was mostly an offense problem rather than a player problem — he is fine, just not good enough that Arizona should pass on a receiver who falls to them. He had expected Wilson to be the player who gave the Cardinals X-flanker flexibility: as a rookie he was used in a [[Michael Pittman Jr.]]-ish way, then was tossed into the X spot when [[Marquise Brown]] went down and had some okay moments with [[Kyler Murray]] down the stretch, but Arizona simply never did that with him again in 2024 and Harmon does not know why. For [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] to be deployed more flexibly, Harmon says Wilson has to take a step.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Wilson's rookie Reception Perception profile was promising rather than star-making — not a future superstar, but clearly a startable NFL receiver, with his best work coming against press man. Harmon points out he handled X-receiver duties for the 2023 Cardinals down the stretch after [[Marquise Brown]] went down and had decent, if uneven, moments there, which Harmon expected to matter once [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] arrived because it meant Arizona would not have to park Harrison at X — and then they did exactly that anyway. Koh (not tracked) says Wilson's 2024 route tree was badly truncated and wants Arizona to ask more of him. Both see the Cardinals as having run the offense back on personnel, which Harmon argues invites regression rather than progression.
- 2025-06-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-04]]): Wilson is on Harmon's shortlist for the year-three receiver series, possibly for the August 'last call' rather than next week's drop. His rookie Reception Perception profile showed 18th percentile against zone but 63rd against man and 65th against press — solid for a rookie — and he graded well on curls, outs and corners, giving him some X-receiver verticality; but 52.8 percent success rate on nine routes is below average, so he is not the field-stretcher Arizona needs. Harmon compares him to a poor man's [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] in how he has been used off the line and in the slot, and separately to a [[Romeo Doubs]]-ish X: effective when on the same page with the quarterback rather than through vertical speed. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is more bullish, calling him a 14th-15th round dart throw who could be an 850-950 yard player if he takes a year-three jump. Both frame him as the one extra piece Arizona needs to unlock [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]].
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): Wilson has said he wants to raise his yards per catch from 11.7 to the 13-15 range, and Waldman argues that is largely not on him — it is a [[Kyler Murray]] problem. With a quarterback willing to climb one or two steps in the pocket and work to the second or third read, Waldman thinks Wilson goes from a roughly 600-yard receiver to an 800-900-yard receiver. He runs the counterfactuals explicitly: as Tampa Bay's number three, in Cleveland with [[Joe Flacco]], as the Rams' number two opposite [[Puka Nacua]], or with [[C.J. Stroud]] — anyone who can climb a pocket makes Wilson better, especially now that he lines up opposite [[Davante Adams]].
- 2025-07-23 — According to James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]: Wilson is an unsung player for the Cardinals — not a top-30 receiver, but in 2023 he played a good amount of X and played it well enough to justify being moved around. [[Matt Harmon]] agrees he expected Arizona to use Wilson as the X so they could weaponize [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] elsewhere, and it simply never happened in 2024. Harmon notes Wilson played about 60% of his rookie snaps on the line of scrimmage, inflated by the back half of the year after Marquise Brown got hurt. Both hosts see Wilson's usage as the swing factor for whether Harrison gets freed off the line, alongside Arizona's preference for heavy personnel with Tip Reiman blocking for the run game.
