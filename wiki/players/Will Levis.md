# Will Levis

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): reacting
  to Tennessee signing [[Calvin Ridley]] to throw to Levis. Harmon's framing:
  on a rookie quarterback contract, a team should "spend money with reckless
  abandon" to get a real evaluation of the player before committing further —
  which is why he's fine with the Titans overpaying at receiver here rather
  than running Levis out with a bare cupboard (previously just [[DeAndre
  Hopkins]] and [[Treylon Burks]], who "is not going to work out"). Skeptical
  of the idea Tennessee should instead trade up for J.J. McCarthy and bench
  Levis: "how much of an upgrade is J.J. McCarthy over Will Levis? I'm not
  that sure." Both hosts agree it would be a very Tennessee outcome to trade
  up for McCarthy only to have him bust.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman expects a more varied Tennessee passing attack under new coach Brian Callahan and is 'feeling it' on Levis throwing four-touchdown games this season, but is skeptical he'll do it often enough to matter for fantasy value — he'd bank on three such games rather than more — and expects an equal or greater number of multi-interception games, given AFC South defenses will make him pay ('everybody's got a plan until they get hit in the mouth').
- 2024-06-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-20]]), citing charter Derek Klassen's RP profile: Levis' route distribution skewed heavily toward screen and then post/nine/corner/out routes — an aggressive, downfield-first thrower who 'would not throw the ball away,' staying in the pocket to sling it deep, which Harmon comps to Ryan Tannehill's willingness to hold the ball and take hits rather than bail. With Brian Callahan calling plays (fresh off coordinating Cincinnati, where Joe Burrow's own preference drove the vertical, shotgun-empty scheme), Harmon expects Levis to get more of a 'Jake Browning-style' offense — Cincinnati's more conservative, ball-control approach after Burrow's injury — paired with Levis' cannon arm, which he thinks 'could be interesting' for everybody involved rather than a pure high-volume deep-shot offense.
- 2024-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-11]]): with [[Calvin Ridley]] and [[DeAndre Hopkins]] now atop Tennessee's receiver room, plus tight end [[Chigoziem Okonkwo]] as a pass-catching option, Harmon says he'd 'be surprised' if Levis doesn't take a step forward in 2024, adding that any struggles 'won't be because of his pass-catching options'; he also noted Levis 'showed some tools' as a rookie.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): the hosts split slightly. Waldman calls 3,500 yards Levis' ceiling and has him at it, with about 18 touchdowns and no path to 20 even with [[Calvin Ridley]] and [[DeAndre Hopkins]]; he says the run game has to pace the offense, and later that he would rather draft [[Bryce Young]] than Levis every day of the week because Young's ceiling is far higher. Guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) sits a touch higher at 3,700 yards and a 60% pass rate — under the 63-65% others project, since Brian Callahan's Cincinnati rate came with [[Joe Burrow]]. McFarland's core finding is that the number one driver of neutral-situation pass rate is simply having a good quarterback, and Levis is not ready to drop back 65% of the time. Waldman's watching note: the intent is there, but throws are airmailed by ten yards.
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh, not a tracked expert ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]): using Derrik Klassen's QB charting on receptionperception.com, Levis threw a dig on only 5% of his throws as a rookie despite being relatively successful there — Koh expects that to change under new HC Brian Callahan, whose Cincinnati offense posted the second-highest pass rate over expected in 2023 even with a backup quarterback. Levis has real accuracy concerns but graded best on in-breaking routes (curls, digs, posts) and the nine, and was among the most aggressive deep passers in the league. Harmon sees strong overlap with [[Calvin Ridley]]'s route tree and is excited about that connection, with an improved offensive line (Lloyd Cushenberry, J.C. Latham, Peter Skoronski) potentially letting Tennessee outkick expectations.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): asked whether Levis is a 'field general', Waldman says 'f**k it' — 'sometimes he's like a field major... more often I think he's maybe like a field corporal, maybe a buck private.' The core indictment is ball security: 'Will Levis does not cherish the football. He does not value the football. He's careless with the ball, and hence the offense is going to be a mess,' visible in both the opener and Week 2, including a red-zone throw to Tyjae Spears he should have eaten as a sack. Waldman credits him as a good vertical thrower who can thread the needle downfield, but says he needs Brian Callahan to scheme opportunities so the supporting cast creates for him rather than the reverse. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) went further, calling him a 'mayonnaise salesman' and questioning the early endorsement money as a sign of misplaced priorities. The fantasy cost is fewer offensive plays for the whole Titans offense.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): fixing Tennessee's passing game (6th fewest passing yards in the NFL) is complicated in practice but simple in theory — you have to get Levis to chill out. Harmon says he has compared Levis to this generation's [[Jameis Winston]] since he first saw him in the NFL: tantalizing moments, not a consistent down-to-down quarterback, and a league leader in what Harmon calls insane turnover screenshots. He is fine with Brian Callahan coaching Levis hard on the sideline after the Week 2 fumble, noting Callahan came up around Peyton Manning. Harmon adds that pass protection has to improve around rookie J.C. Latham and a young line, and that Levis's DNA is to go big-game hunting rather than take the available dig route — co-host James Koh (not tracked) wondered aloud whether the problem is coordinator or quarterback.
- 2024-09-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-20]]): a mixed bag. Levis is not playing badly enough to be benched on accuracy or big-play ability, and Waldman grants 'there's some talent there' — but the decision-making at key leverage points, the black zone and red zone and when to take an off-script risk, has been rough and repeatedly reckless despite being coached on it. Like [[Bryce Young]], he is on his second scheme in two years, which has to mitigate the read on him. The distinguishing failure versus [[Caleb Williams]]: Levis makes one mistake and things start to fall apart, and you begin worrying he goes into reckless mode. In a perfect world Waldman would bench him for a few games purely on principle, so he absorbs the consequences, with the team publicly backing him rather than hanging him out to dry — he sees Brian Callahan's public handling (raised by Ross Tucker) as having a valid point. The fear if it continues: 'his career could drown.'
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]): Waldman is skeptical of Tennessee's stated long-term commitment. His argument is structural: if Levis keeps playing hero ball and the response is to shorten the offense rather than bench him, the Titans get progressively more conservative, receivers stop running the full route tree, and eventually nobody will want to play there — he cites a frustrated postgame interview from [[Calvin Ridley]] as an early sign. Even when they did go downfield, Levis threw it up for grabs while acknowledging he knows he has to be better. Waldman's prescription is benching as a teaching tool: there is a point where you manage what a player can do, and a point where accommodating him hurts the whole team — 'sometimes you have to discipline your kids,' and Tennessee is not.
- 2024-10-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-15]]): Levis is 30th in drop-back success rate, ahead of only [[Bo Nix]] and [[Deshaun Watson]], and Harmon says he is bad enough to sink Tennessee's entire season — he went 0-for-8 targeting [[Calvin Ridley]], with one throw landing so far off that Ridley was not even in the broadcast frame. Harmon questions the offseason premise: Levis showed something as a rookie but never unquestioned-starter stuff, so pairing him with a non-threatening backup in Mason Rudolph and no real contingency was a risky build, especially with veterans like Ridley, [[DeAndre Hopkins]], [[Tony Pollard]] and [[Tyler Boyd]] imported on the cheap-quarterback theory. Koh (not tracked) argues ownership rode Levis on the memory of his four-touchdown debut and that a backup's only job is not turning the ball over, which he says Rudolph and Jimmy Garoppolo fail at. Harmon would have used the shoulder injury as an out to sit him; anything past 15 yards was a struggle.
- 2024-10-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-24]]): Harmon relayed hearing that Levis is actually a smart quarterback who knows where the right reads are but cannot get out of his own way — which he said is a worse failure mode than a quarterback who simply does not care. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) was harsher: Levis has been 'so bad you have no chance of winning a football game', pointing to two mind-melting turnovers in the loss to a Bears team that scored no offensive touchdown. Both traced Brian Callahan's loss of faith to the week-three sideline screaming match, with Harmon framing it as a three-stage breakup — a training-camp inkling, the week-three confrontation, and the shoulder injury as the exit — after which Tennessee turned to Mason Rudolph and began selling off veterans ([[DeAndre Hopkins]], Ernest Jones). Harmon believes the shoulder injury is legitimate. Context for downgrading every Titans pass catcher.
- 2025-02-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-03]]) [Dynasty]: 'ultimately fuck it,' though he is open to being wrong. News that Levis plans to work this offseason with Jordan Palmer on mechanics and pocket presence does not move Waldman, because from what he has scouted the work will address symptoms rather than the real problem — processing information at a level that lets him be more dynamic and fluid conceptually. He added a structural point: Palmer will never publicly admit a paying client's issue is conceptual, because that is a death knell with teams — nobody knows how to fix it — which is also why teams struggle to draft quarterbacks at all, hoping it clicks while they work the physical and technical symptoms. Levis still has to eliminate the boneheaded plays that wreck a game every two to three weeks, and Waldman notes [[Shedeur Sanders]] or [[Cam Ward]] could arrive with Tennessee's No. 1 overall pick.
