# Jonah Coleman

## Expert Takes

- 2026-02-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-09]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is a firm fade on Coleman as a top-five back, saying 'I don't understand the love for this guy.' He credits size and tackle-breaking but notes Washington's offensive line created huge holes on tape, and Coleman struggled to beat linebackers in space he should be winning -- a trait Waldman worries will show up badly at the combine's speed/acceleration testing. His projection: a bench/rotational contributor who can offer isolated value, not the early-round starter his public rankings suggest. Waldman jokes his own grade divides published rankings 'by two, three, or four.'
- 2026-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-01]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said Coleman, once past the line of scrimmage, needs to learn to leverage his size and get downhill fast rather than bouncing plays outside or cutting against the grain -- he isn't explosive or agile enough to make that work and gets tackled too easily for his size. Waldman wants to see him stick his foot in the ground and force pursuit into bad angles instead.
- 2026-04-09 — According to Paul Pertichese, guest analyst on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-09]] (not a tracked expert) [Dynasty]: Jonah Coleman stylistically resembles Doug Martin ('muscle hamster' build and running style) to Pertichese, though he doesn't project to see the field at as high a level as Martin did nor have Martin's career. Coleman skipped the entire pre-draft testing process while healthy, which worries Pertichese that he may test poorly -- his tape reads as average play speed, burst and quickness. He projects as more of an early-down committee or depth piece than a difference-maker, with Pertichese explicitly saying he likes Coleman less than some other evaluators do.
- 2026-04-09 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-09]]) [Dynasty]: names Jonah Coleman, alongside [[DeMond Claiborne]], as one of the two best low-red-zone runners in the 2026 class, though the two get there very differently in how they convert positive yardage into touchdowns.
- 2026-04-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-13]]) [Dynasty]: he thinks Coleman is overrated relative to a consensus that has the 5'8', 220-lb back as a top five-to-seven running back in the class -- says he's 'alone on an island' well below that. His grade says Coleman makes an NFL roster and could carve out a situational role, but he isn't sold on the acceleration, speed or vision needed to hold a starting job over a full season. Also pushes back on Coleman's reputation as a strong pass protector, arguing evaluators grade blocking on outcome rather than technique -- citing head-dropping, a weak punch and overextending into blocks, the same flaw he says got Deonta Foreman overrated as a blocker years ago. Says Coleman needs to show more explosiveness and faster downhill decisiveness on off-guard/off-tackle runs, and that his receiving work (screens, wide routes) could be a legitimate outlet if the rest develops.
- 2026-04-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is skeptical of Denver's 4th-round pick of Coleman, comping him to LenDale White -- a bowling-ball back who lacks burst, doesn't run with good pad level downhill, and whose broken tackles came mostly against lower-level competition and arm-tackle attempts through big holes; his pass protection gets in the way of defenders but lacks real technique. He notes Sean Payton has a mixed history drafting backs (pointing out Payton reportedly didn't even want to draft [[Alvin Kamara]] in New Orleans) and rates [[Royce Freeman]], an earlier Payton pick, ahead of Coleman. Waldman doesn't think [[JK Dobbins]] is going anywhere as long as he's healthy, and is unsure whether the pick is really an indictment of [[RJ Harvey]] given how stylistically different the two backs are -- floating that Denver may simply be hedging with short-yardage/goal-line insurance rather than threatening Harvey's role. Waldman says he's 'not all that impressed' and suggests fading the pick could be the right contrarian play.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-07 (Going Deep with Brandon Angelo)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman has Coleman overrated by roughly a round and a half, grading him at pick 36 (end of third) against a much higher common rookie-draft ADP. His case: outside of undrafted [[Alvin Kamara]] -- whom Sean Payton initially thought was 'soft' before Kamara's game completely turned around -- Payton has never hit on a running back drafted after the fourth round; his earliest RB picks were Reggie Bush and a second-round Mark Ingram. Denver's backfield is already crowded with [[Audric Estime]], [[R.J. Harvey]] and free-agent addition [[J.K. Dobbins]]. Waldman compares Coleman's play speed to 'molasses,' says he gets caught by linebackers, lacks real contact balance and explosion despite a power reputation, and that his pass protection is more effort than technique; he attributes much of Coleman's college production to strong offensive line play. [[Brandon Angelo]] agreed Coleman projects as a Royce Freeman/Tyler Allgeier/Jamal Williams-type committee back rather than a lead back, useful only in a high-volume, goal-line-needy rushing offense -- which Denver, with Dobbins entrenched, is not.
- 2026-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-28]]): Coleman was the most consistent running back Harmon has charted across two full years of RB charting, generating positive yardage reliably with real big-play juice mixed in, particularly strong in zone scheme. He contrasts this directly with [[R.J. Harvey]], calling Harvey a 'home run hitter' who 'strikes out a lot' -- Harmon believes that boom/bust inconsistency is exactly what drove Denver to draft Coleman as a fix. Harmon also relays from his own Yahoo interview with Coleman that he takes pride in the little things -- ball security (only one college fumble, in his final season) and reading blocks well -- which projects as a coaches' favorite skillset. Harmon expects Coleman to step in as a true 1A if [[J.K. Dobbins]] (long injury history) misses time, forming what he called 'a really interesting combo' with Harvey.
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-22 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Dynasty]: agrees Coleman is 'making a case' for playing time relative to a Broncos backfield where [[R.J. Harvey]] is more of a return specialist/situational back, but cautions Sean Payton is 'a little fickle,' and Coleman's role could be lighter coming off surgery for a torn labrum. Waldman credits Coleman with showing solid pass-protection recognition and receiving ability out of the backfield, but is skeptical the power-runner profile people are projecting from his shorts-and-T-shirt look will translate to real tackle-breaking production, noting it didn't fully translate at Washington. He says Coleman will have a role, but its size won't be known until live game reps.
- 2026-07-24 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-24]]) [Dynasty]: Harris says some dynasty-minded 'futurist' analysts who only care about multi-year outlooks are already 'messing around with' Coleman and moving on from the [[R.J. Harvey]] era, but Harris personally thinks [[J.K. Dobbins]] is 'just too good' for Coleman to be a real near-term factor, dismissing any goal-line or camp buzz around Coleman as noise for now.
- 2026-08-03 — According to Ian Hartitz (untracked co-host) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-03 (Feel It or Fk It with Ian Hartitz)]]): he's 'fuck it' on Coleman taking over the Denver backfield from JK Dobbins by November, citing his own pre-draft 'Little Things' study grading every running back's pass protection, career fumble rate and career drop rate -- Coleman graded best in the class, highlighted by a viral pass-pro clip flattening a defender, plus real receiving usage at Washington and enough size (220 pounds) to mix in on short yardage. He still expects Dobbins to be the lead back for now given his contract, and doesn't think Coleman or RJ Harvey individually become someone to feel good about unless Dobbins misses time, since they'd likely split touches with each other rather than one clearly taking over.
- 2026-08-13 — According to James Koh (untracked co-host) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-13]]): Koh contrasts Jonah Coleman with backfield mate [[RJ Harvey]] as the more consistent, if less explosive, option behind an injury-prone J.K. Dobbins -- he expects coach Sean Payton to lean on Coleman's ability to 'keep the offense on track,' and says if Dobbins goes down, he's betting on Coleman over Harvey to earn the coaching staff's trust, even though Harvey carries the higher ceiling.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman wasn't a fan pre-draft and remains skeptical despite camp buzz, noting that Denver coach Sean Payton likes to use player archetypes and name-comps that get fans overly excited before people realize Payton runs distinct three-back committees. Coleman can find and set up an open crease and has the size to run through arm tackles, but Waldman didn't see the burst, long speed, or contact-balance/tackle-breaking ability of a back like [[Rhamondre Stevenson]], and thinks he should be a harder runner than he showed. Waldman expects [[J.K. Dobbins]] to remain the lead back with Coleman getting an early short-yardage or red-zone role, though Waldman thinks even that role ultimately goes to Dobbins.
- 2026-08-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-28]]), relaying [[Matt Waldman]]'s view: Waldman told Harris he doesn't understand the hype on Coleman and thinks 'he kind of stinks.' Coleman is nonetheless climbing draft boards, which Harris attributes to Sean Payton -- who Harris says 'simply cannot be trusted in what he says about his players' -- talking Coleman up. Harris says he is staying away from Coleman on that basis.
