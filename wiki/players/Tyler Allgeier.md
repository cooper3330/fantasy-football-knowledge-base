# Tyler Allgeier

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Dynasty]: expects [[Bijan Robinson]] to get the bulk of Atlanta's new-coaching-staff workload increase in 2024, but flags Allgeier as a real complementary piece worth "a dash of" draft capital in his own right — he "plays really well when in the system they put him in."
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): with Atlanta's new OC hire (Zach Robinson, from the Rams) bringing a man-heavy run scheme, Harmon expects [[Bijan Robinson]] to be the clear lead, but still expects Allgeier to "get involved" as a complementary piece rather than being phased out entirely.
- 2024-11-22 — According to Adam Harstad, untracked co-host ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]) [Dynasty]: Harstad likes watching the Falcons scale back Allgeier's snaps as [[Bijan Robinson]]'s role grows — not because it hurts Allgeier's outlook but because he hopes leaguemates read the shrinking workload as a reason to move on. In his view Allgeier has clearly passed the organizational-commitment test and has looked good when given opportunities. He doubts Allgeier ever reaches waivers outright, but flags him as a buy at a discount around next year's rookie draft when roster crunch pushes managers to clear room for shiny new toys — the classic later-round back with future opportunity whom the organization loves.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Allgeier played only 18 snaps and Harris cannot understand how he still got 10 carries out of it, calling it come-on-man usage that should belong to [[Bijan Robinson]]. He is not mad at the 10 for 24 line given Tampa's good run defense, but the split reflects Atlanta being too clever with alignments rather than any Allgeier merit.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest John Daigle (Establish The Run, not tracked) made Allgeier his number-two Week 3 waiver add at 30% rostered — the add you make if you need an immediate RB3 or flex rather than a no-standalone-value stash like [[Blake Corum]], [[Woody Marks]] or [[Chris Rodriguez Jr.]] — because he sits behind [[Bijan Robinson]] in a friendly spot against a Carolina team that let [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] run for a career-high 143 yards in Week 1, with [[Bryce Young]] struggling and Atlanta's defense playing well; Daigle projects 10-15 carries. Harris flags his usual objection to projecting game scripts, but agrees the outcome range is real: 10 and 16 carries in the two wins, unproductive against Tampa (Harris kept wanting Bijan back in) but effective in heavy garbage time against Minnesota. Harris expects him ranked around RB35 and among the highest of the backup backs.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris' No. 5 and guest Brandon Funston's No. 4 Week 6 waiver target, 31% rostered. Harris' pitch is deliberately modest: 'if you find yourself in a bad situation, I think he's startable... it isn't great and you're not going to be happy, but as your flex, it can be Tyler Allgeier.' Funston adds the volume case — a couple of games with 16 carries, though completely out of the passing game — and rates him near the top of any next-man-up handcuff list, finding the end zone even in a shared role with Bijan Robinson. Both attribute the 31% availability to managers dropping handcuffs to survive bye weeks. Harris groups him with [[Kendre Miller]] as a good power back who would be useful, not a superstar, in a pure starting role.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): Allgeier scored twice, but Harris reads the usage as closer/fresher-back rotation rather than a goal-line takeover. The first came after a run from the 22 down to the one with no personnel change — a good run, stay in, get another run. The second sequence started near midfield with two minutes left: Robinson got a carry, came out, and Allgeier stayed in for a pass to [[Kyle Pitts]] plus three straight runs from the 19. Harris's month-long counts show Allgeier trailing [[Bijan Robinson]] 13-23 on red zone snaps; both of Allgeier's inside-the-five snaps came Sunday. He concedes you are allowed to be freaked out that both backs worked, but calls the 'Allgeier has all the goal line work' claim false.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): Allgeier was the number one Week 11 waiver add for both Harris and guest **Brandon Funston** (The Athletic, not tracked), at 33 percent rostered. Harris pushes back on the goal-line narrative - the Germany score against the Colts was the only carry Atlanta has had inside the three-yard line in the past month, so it is thin evidence of a defined role. The real question he poses is whether you can start him with [[Bijan Robinson]] healthy: 'Is he [[David Montgomery]]? No', but both agreed he is a flex play, with six touchdowns, about a third of the rushing carries, and Carolina at home giving up around five yards per carry. Funston adds context that [[Michael Penix Jr.]] has been struggling and Raheem Morris looks to be limiting his passing exposure with a conservative, run-heavy approach. Harris: 'is he going to be the second-highest second-banana running back in everybody's ranks? Yes.'
