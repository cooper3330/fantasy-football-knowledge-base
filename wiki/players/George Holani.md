# George Holani

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-12]]) [Dynasty]: one of three underrated backs Waldman flags this episode (with [[Dylan Laube]] and [[Daijun Edwards]]), and explicitly the highest-variance of the three — "the most boom-bust... the highest upside, the deepest floor." Injury history is the real risk: an MCL injury cost him most of one season, then an unspecified lower-leg injury cost six games the following year. When healthy, the flash is real — Waldman cites a screen pass against the nation's top-ranked (UCLA) rushing defense in 2023 where he "made seven guys miss in the open field." Good pass catcher with pass-protection skills good enough to project growth into a role player; would need to add weight to profile as a viable NFL committee back.
- 2024-02-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]) [Dynasty]: names him his own pick for a back who can make a roster as a late-round selection and develop into a reliable reserve — 5'11", 205 lbs, a good receiver with real contact balance. Projects a practice-squad path in year one, developing along the lines of Jeremy McNichols or Salvon Ahmed (neither tracked pages), with a bit more upside as a runner than either of those two showed — possibly a situational third-down back in the mold of Jalen Richard (not a tracked page).
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: backs up the "underrated" tag with charted contact-balance data — broke 6-of-9 (67%) tracked hits from defensive linemen, 7-for-7 from linebackers, and 10-of-14 (71%) from defensive backs, 79% overall across a 24-hit sample. A 12% broken-tackle rate specifically on linebacker wraps, which Waldman places in the same tier as [[Jonathan Brooks]], [[Trey Benson]] and [[Rasheen Ali]].

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]): names him one of his favorite off-radar prospects — third straight year Holani's been in the RSP after Waldman twice guessed wrong about his draft eligibility. Cites a UCLA-game viewing where Holani showed better contact balance and quickness than expected, "makes people miss and breaks tackles" more than credited for. Updated career-shape comp: more of a Jeff Wilson with a little extra pop, rather than the more explicit change-of-pace/scat-back framing from earlier reads.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]) [Dynasty]: 'fuck it unless he makes this roster' for redraft, but a dynasty player to watch. Holani is fighting Kenny McIntosh for the Seattle RB3 job and Waldman expects McIntosh may edge him out, leaving Holani cut and looking for work elsewhere — which would not be a bad outcome, because a number of teams could want him. The strong camp is notable: good size, good power, contact balance, speed and quickness, good movement, and a very good pass catcher. Michael-Shawn Dugar of The Athletic also flags him as an excellent blocker, and Waldman notes those little things are often what decides whether a back makes a roster.
- 2024-11-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]) [Dynasty]: Holani is a keep-an-eye-on name for Waldman. He noted the Boise State back kept [[Ashton Jeanty]] from being a full-time player for a stretch, crediting versatility, good contact balance, decent quickness and speed, and smart running, and said he nearly beat out Kenny McIntosh for Seattle's third role. Waldman's angle is that even if he is stuck in Seattle he may find another team and a better spot, pointing to Seattle's history of moving on from serviceable backs who then found roster spots elsewhere.
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]): Waldman says Holani deserves attention and denies it is a reaction to him beating out [[Damien Martinez]] — he flagged Holani as a guy to watch last year too, when Seattle kept Kenny McIntosh over him and put Holani on the practice squad. His case is roster-construction logic: Holani offers more of the [[Kenneth Walker III]] element, a scat back who can still run between the tackles and create in the open field, plus real special-teams value that Martinez does not provide. In the spice drawer of the running back room, Holani duplicates Walker while Martinez duplicates [[Zach Charbonnet]]. Bob Harris (not tracked) noted Holani ran hard through camp.
- 2026-01-22 — According to James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-22]]) [Best Ball]: with [[Zach Charbonnet]] out for the season with a torn ACL, Koh sarcastically predicted Seattle would keep leaning on backup Holani for goal-to-go and short-yardage work over the red-hot [[Kenneth Walker III]] -- 'they're going to give George Holani the football around the goal line' -- calling it consistent with a season-long pattern of underusing Walker in that role.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-07]]) [Dynasty/Best Ball]: says he's personally excited about his own deep dynasty shares of Holani, projecting he can fill the 'inside grinder' outside-zone-backup role that Emmanuel Wilson held previously in Seattle. Harmon points to a real signal from the playoffs: per PFF charting, Holani logged 11 pass-block reps in the conference championship game versus only 4 for Kenneth Walker III, which Harmon reads as the coaching staff trusting Holani's pass protection over Walker's -- the same pass-pro trust gap that cost Walker snaps to Zach Charbonnet during the season despite Walker being viewed as the more explosive athlete of the two.
- 2026-05-11 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-11]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman feels it on George Holani as a deep-league sleeper and top waiver-wire watch in Seattle's backfield -- he's worked his way from special teamer to low-end contributor, has good burst, breaks tackles, and is an excellent receiver, and Waldman said he's earned staff trust despite entering the league neck-and-neck with (since ACL-injured) Kenny McIntosh, whom the coaching staff has called the more explosive back. With [[Zach Charbonnet]] not expected to be a real factor 'until late October, if at all,' Waldman expects an [[Emmanuel Wilson]]-and-[[Jadarian Price]] rotation to lead early, but said Holani has the size to be an every-down back if either of those two gets hurt, and he isn't seeing Holani drafted nearly enough right now relative to Wilson.
- 2026-07-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-06]]) [Dynasty, Best Ball]: Waldman feels Holani has a legitimate shot at fantasy relevance in 2026, arguing he's actually the better player than Kenny McIntosh despite years of narrowly trading the RB3 role back and forth, and that recent public OTA reports show the team trusting him with first-team reps. He praises Holani's contact balance, quickness, ability to make defenders miss in tight space, and receiving chops for a back 'big enough' to work inside. Waldman's realistic path: Holani becomes the primary beneficiary if Jadarian Price doesn't seize the role at cutdowns and Emmanuel Wilson stays behind, giving him a bridge role until Zach Charbonnet returns. Currently RB73 (277th overall) and trending up in ADP, Waldman calls him a worthwhile 20th-round Best Ball reach, ranking his near-term workload path above both Jaydon Blue and Jalen Wright in that backfield mix despite Blue having more long-term talent.
- 2026-07-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: amid the crowded Seattle backfield discussion, Waldman flagged that [[George Holani]] is getting first-team running back reps in practice and is the most acclimated player in the offense entering his third year there, making him a real threat to siphon touches from [[Jadarian Price]] if Price struggles with pass protection or ball security early on.
- 2026-07-21 — According to guest Ben Gretsch (Stealing Signals newsletter, not a tracked expert), discussed with [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-21]]): Holani has gotten notable positive offseason buzz in Seattle despite having done little on offense in his career (mostly special teams/depth work). Gretsch is skeptical it means much, framing it as likely offseason fluff, and said the real test comes once camp starts in August -- if the Holani chatter dries up and coverage becomes all about [[Jadarian Price]], that confirms the buzz was empty. Harris noted Holani likely won't even get drafted in most fantasy leagues, so he's purely a name to watch for a change in team messaging rather than a fantasy asset right now.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-17]]): Waldman noted Holani is starting to draw buzz in Seattle after not making the roster as a rookie behind Kenny McIntosh, who is now looking for work elsewhere -- a path he thinks [[Miles Montgomery]] in New England could follow.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]) [Dynasty]: Holani, who came in with no meaningful draft capital, has started camp as one of Seattle's top two backs. Waldman likes him as a well-rounded player who can pass protect, run inside, and catch the ball, with enough quickness and power to hold a real role in what Waldman expects to be a crowded Seahawks backfield alongside [[Jadarian Price]] and Emmanuel Wilson.
- 2026-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-27]]) [Dynasty]: Harris called Holani a personal 'binky,' a first-time super-deep-sleeper pick he's stashed in his 'La Liga Lebowski' dynasty league for about a year and a half in case of backfield carnage, even though he ranks him behind [[Emmanuel Wilson]], Zach Charbonnet and rookie Jadarian Price on the Seahawks depth chart and isn't sure Holani even makes the roster. Jake Seeley disagreed on the internal order, saying reports are split roughly evenly between beat writers favoring Holani versus Wilson, and cautioned against trusting any single reporter's 'this guy looks amazing' takes without corroboration.
