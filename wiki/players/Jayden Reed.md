# Jayden Reed

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]): a promising option who "may wind up one of those players" — meaning the [[Keenan Allen]] / [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] mold of a receiver who wins on route running against both man and zone and can be used inside and outside. The stated difference in Reed's favor: unlike those two, "Reed actually has speed." Waldman's qualifier is situational rather than about the player — how far it goes depends on the offense he ends up in.
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): names Reed — with A.J. Brown, [[Justin Jefferson]] and Chris Olave — as one of the receivers he was "higher on as good fits immediately than most in the industry," citing them as evidence that his rebuilt wide receiver evaluation system is working. A track-record claim about the analyst rather than new analysis of the player; useful mainly as a note on how confident Waldman is in this specific call.
- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): says he'd rank Reed above [[Tank Dell]] and considers slotting him ahead of [[Puka Nacua]] and [[Zay Flowers]] in his 2023 rookie wide receiver tier, behind only [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] and [[Jordan Addison]]. Explicitly: "I wouldn't trade Jayden Reed for any of them." Calls him "a baller" with "some Stefon Diggs potential to his game," and sets the floor comp at Laveranues Coles — a six-figure-yardage, multi-franchise career-length floor, not a bust risk.

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Dynasty]: grouped with [[Christian Watson]] and [[Romeo Doubs]] as Green Bay's fantasy-relevant "big three," with speed the trait that separates Reed and Watson from the more physical [[Dontayvion Wicks]]. No downgrade implied — Wicks is framed as a threat to Doubs's and Watson's roles, not Reed's.
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: Adam Harstad's [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] doesn't include rushing production, and even so Reed still ranks 32nd of ~190-200 qualifiers; adding his rushing work (roughly 120 yards, 2 TDs on the ground, clearing a "40 targets + 60 rush yards" threshold that only a handful of the best rookie-WR seasons since 2006 have cleared, including CeeDee Lamb, Deebo Samuel and Percy Harvin) is read as a positive signal on top of an already-strong model score. Waldman names Reed (with [[Puka Nacua]]) as one of his three biggest post-draft rookie-WR value calls, and reaffirms he'd take Reed over [[Zay Flowers]] straight up.
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]): calls him Green Bay's best receiver outright — he didn't record a catch in the Wild Card win over Dallas and "didn't need to," a testament to how much the offense produces without needing to funnel volume through him.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Best Ball]: "looks like a potential difference maker," relaying a Mike Florio comp of a "Wisconsin Deebo" (Deebo Samuel) vibe to his game. Grouped again with [[Dontayvion Wicks]], [[Romeo Doubs]] and [[Christian Watson]] in a crowded Green Bay receiver room where Waldman's stated draft strategy is to take the cheapest piece rather than pay a premium for any one name.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]) [Dynasty]: the strongest ceiling call yet — asked directly whether Reed can grow into an [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]-style role (primary inside receiver, powerful at the catch point, real juice after the catch), Harmon says yes: "quite crispy" as a route runner, with legitimate man-coverage-beating ability that graded out in college and in his Reception Perception rookie profile. Wants to see him used outside more rather than pigeonholed into rub-route/schemed-open looks. Calls him the receiver with the best odds of the group to become Green Bay's true number one, with [[Dontayvion Wicks]] the only other real candidate.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: currently the priciest of Green Bay's receiver corps, going as high as WR3 in early best balls — but still dirt cheap relative to a strong 2023 stretch run (was arguably the team's WR1 down the stretch), and Waldman says he'll have "many, many shares."
- 2024-05-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-21]]): despite liking [[Dontayvion Wicks]]'s well-rounded profile, Harmon still installs Reed as the odds-on favorite to be Green Bay's best receiver in 2024, calling him a player with a 'multi-layered skill set' who was more than the 'slot-only gadgety' rookie he sometimes looked like on tape.
- 2024-05-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-30]]): Reed's 2023 Reception Perception profile posted a 72% success rate vs. man, 82% vs. zone, and 76.9% vs. press (a small sample at only 7% of routes, but consistent with his 77.8% college press number, 87th percentile). Harmon called the profile among the most positive of the rookie class and argued there is little in the data suggesting he needs to stay limited to a slot/gadget role (66.7% of snaps in the slot, plus 10.8% pre-snap motion from the backfield) — he beats man press better than Deebo Samuel despite lacking Deebo's athletic profile, per Harmon, pushing back on the "poor man's Deebo" comp. Reed excelled on in-breaking routes: 83% success on digs, ~86% on slants (his most-run route), 80% on posts. Weaknesses: below NFL average on nine routes (only 4.3% of his sampled routes were nine routes), curls, and out routes — areas Harmon said, if improved, could push him toward a Terry McLaurin-level outside receiver. Contested catch rate was weak: targeted on 16.9% of sampled routes, caught only 33.3% of those. Co-host James Koh (untracked) flagged a mild worry Reed becomes "the next Curtis Samuel or Elijah Moore" — a clearly talented player miscast into a gadget role by his team, given the Packers' Christian Watson/Romeo Doubs/Dontayvion Wicks size at outside receiver. Both hosts agreed Reed should be a top-two target earner in Green Bay regardless of alignment, with Harmon adding Dontayvion Wicks is the only real threat to that status if he takes a leap.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman called Reed the safest, most established and highest-priced of Green Bay's receiver trio ([[Christian Watson]], [[Dontayvion Wicks]], Reed), and the one least likely to swing much in either direction. He projects roughly 100 targets, 65-75 catches, 800-900 yards and 8-10 touchdowns, comparing his role and profile to 'a Stefon Diggs starter kit,' with room to outperform that if Jordan Love's efficiency ticks up. Waldman framed Reed as the 'go for the established name, not the value' pick of the group.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon called Reed 'the best player last year on film' among Green Bay's crowded WR corps, praising his after-catch ability, and grouped him with [[Dontayvion Wicks]] and Christian Watson as receivers who could see more 'manufactured' work if the offensive line's uncertain left tackle/right guard battle (rookie first-rounder Jordan Morgan vs. Rasheed Walker vs. Sean Rhyan) fails to gel and limits true drop-back passing. Harmon said he feels generically good about all of Green Bay's skill players because of Jordan Love and OC Matt LaFleur's scheme, but which specific receiver benefits most in fantasy production remains unresolved heading into camp.
- 2024-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-30]]): Reed is the favorite and 'should be' the top target earner in a Green Bay room where all four receivers are 'good to some degree'. His rookie Reception Perception profile confirmed what Harmon liked as a prospect — 71st percentile success rate vs man, 77% vs zone (Harmon wants slot receivers over 80% vs zone), and a small-sample but excellent 76.9% vs press, an 82nd percentile mark. Harmon says the Packers offense was demonstrably at its best when Reed was a featured player and calls him easily their best separator; the risk is that he played almost exclusively in three-receiver sets as a rookie, so if he is a 50-60% snap player in two-WR looks he becomes the riskiest of the group to draft at his price. Harmon argues it would be 'ill-advisable' not to make him a near-every-snap player given his press and man ability, and warns he is likely to get pigeonholed as a gadget create-a-touch player when the charting says he can do much more. Koh agrees Reed is the top target getter and calls him ascending and tough at the catch point, but notes he needs Watson and Doubs around him to clear space. Koh's own projections have Reed at 108 targets, 973 yards, seven touchdowns with a 1,300-yard ceiling. Both flag his brief camp PUP stint.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Harmon says that in any other situation he would rank Reed as roughly a top-20 fantasy receiver and definitely a WR2 — his prospect profile checked the man and press success-rate boxes as a good outside receiver, and his rookie Reception Perception came in at a 72.2% success rate versus man and 81.7% versus zone, winning on quick-hitters but also intermediate digs, posts and corners. The blocker is playing time: he cannot rank Reed there because the Green Bay rotation is unknowable with [[Dontayvion Wicks]], [[Christian Watson]] and [[Romeo Doubs]] all deserving snaps, plus two good tight ends on a team that was among the heaviest 12-personnel users. Reed primarily runs from the slot. Harmon says Reed is too good to take off the field, but that is not the same as knowing the coaching staff agrees.
- 2024-10-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-01]]): Reed is 'a superstar receiver' and the best Packers receiver drafted since [[Davante Adams]], a rare non-size-threshold pick for an organization Harmon calls size freaks. His 2024 charting: 72% success vs man, ~82% vs zone, both above Harmon's 70/80 benchmarks. He is not being used on 'Mickey Mouse' underneath routes — an 8.3 aDOT overall and 13.8 in the two games with [[Jordan Love]], over 130 yards in those games, a 158.3 passer rating when targeted and all three contested targets caught. That last point matters because Reed's rookie RP profile flagged contested catching as the one real concern (contested target on 16.9% of sampled looks, only 33% brought in) as the swing factor on his ceiling. Harmon is ready to ask whether Reed is better than [[Chris Olave]], [[Drake London]] or even [[Garrett Wilson]] and sees him heading toward top-12 receiver status. Koh pushed back — he loves Reed but says the sample is too small and Reed plays the easier inside spot, with over 70% of snaps in the slot and only 23% out wide.
- 2024-10-10 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert), co-host with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): Reed is a player Harstad has been in on since before his rookie year hit the field, and he still is — but the current production is not repeatable. It is not smoke and mirrors, there is genuine skill behind it, but Reed is not going to keep hitting big plays at this rate week in and week out. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees the 19.7 yards per reception is going away quickly while arguing Reed is still a top-15 receiver, because he wins multiple ways in the manner Green Bay uses him, comparable to how [[Deebo Samuel]] is deployed; Waldman accepts Harstad's case against a top-five finish. Harstad contrasts him with [[Rashid Shaheed]], whose game genuinely is the huge yards-per-reception average — Reed can do the deep stuff but is not a one-trick deep-shot pony.
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Reed remains Harmon's pick as Green Bay's best receiver and his personal favorite in the room — 'if I just had to line everybody up, who's the guy I want, I do think it is Jayden Reed' — which is why he sells [[Romeo Doubs]] as the team's best overall. The caveat is hands: Reed leads the team with six drops on the year and dropped roughly two more on Sunday, so Doubs is the more reliable catcher. Usage has him as the flanker-slot at 83.3% route participation, about 73% in the slot and 27% outside; Harmon says he and Doubs should rarely leave the field while [[Christian Watson]] (deep threat, 15 air yards per target) and [[Dontayvion Wicks]] rotate at ~50% of routes, and that the room is too deep and too good for anyone to be iced out or consistently featured.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): emphatically yes, Reed is Green Bay's go-to receiver, and Waldman pushes back on respected analysts calling him a limited player or a [[Deebo Samuel]] type who is good but not elite. He sees potential to become an elite route runner and cited a back-shoulder fade where Reed accelerated at the top of the stem to gain a half step and sell the vertical before turning suddenly for the catch. Adds that he is a good bad-weather player, a good runner after the catch, and that his routes are better than people think. His long-standing comp: a [[Stefon Diggs]] starter kit with all the pieces needed to develop into the advanced version. Prediction: when the dust settles Reed is the one who gets a second contract in Green Bay, or the one who makes Green Bay regret letting him go. The caveat is workload — he has cleared a 20% target share only twice this season and needs more opportunity, which Waldman half-jokingly pins on [[Jordan Love]].
- 2024-11-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-26]]) [Dynasty]: the quiet stretch (over 30 receiving yards in only two of his last six games) is real but is not a skill issue. Harmon publicly walks back his early-season enthusiasm, saying he got 'over his skis' on dynasty rankings after the big Jordan Love games and wrongly expected Reed to reach tier two of NFL receivers by now — he calls Reed the best receiver on the team and a very good NFL starter, but not a funnel-everything-through-him player, and notes most players never become that. The cause is environment: Green Bay is dead last in neutral pass rate coming out of the bye with a run game Harmon praises highly. Koh disagrees on usage, calling a sub-70% snap share in four of five games a misallocation of resources and arguing Reed can win as a flanker off the line, not just a slot. Harmon expects big but volatile games down the stretch.
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]): underrated, and specifically underrated by data-first analysts. Waldman argues the current trend of leaning on the tracking data that Dwain McFarland and JJ Zachariason work with is fine, but some players slip through those cracks because you have to watch the tape to see how they function in the offense. On film Reed is in a [[Deebo Samuel]]-esque role — a better route runner than Deebo, not used as heavily in the run game, but Green Bay is unafraid to use him that way in the red zone — so his combined variety of ways to win is greater than Deebo's. He calls Reed the primary receiver in the offense, just not always used at the level you would want, a WR1 on good weeks and a WR4 on bad ones, hence the ideal WR3. Bob Harris (not tracked) said that describes the whole Packers receiving corps in a season where [[Jordan Love]] has had few top-10 weeks, so the trickle-down is whack-a-mole.
- 2024-12-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-10]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon explicitly walks back his early-season push, saying he got over his skis trying to shove Reed into the superstar tier and up the dynasty ranks alongside [[Drake London]] and [[Chris Olave]]. The early-season usage — routes on over 80% of dropbacks, heavy intermediate work, lining up out wide — has not continued: Reed played over 70% of snaps in four of the first seven games and zero times in the last six, just 50% on the Thursday night loss to Detroit, where he drew one target and caught nothing despite the Lions being a slot-funnel defense. Harmon predicted it once Detroit lost interior linemen and went heavy. He still calls Reed clearly the team's best separator per RP, better than [[Christian Watson]], [[Romeo Doubs]] and [[Dontayvion Wicks]], and notes rookie marks of 71st percentile success rate vs man and 77th vs zone — very good but not superstar. Koh is more indignant: Reed is on pace for only 77 targets and 906 yards at 15.75 yards per reception, ranks 57th in targets behind [[Tre Tucker]], [[Chris Godwin]], [[Jalen Tolbert]], [[Michael Wilson]] and [[Noah Brown]], and leads all receivers in rushing (128 yards, 13 carries, a TD) while sitting as WR12 in fantasy. Harmon's conclusion: Green Bay does not treat him as a number one, so defenses will not either, and more individual proof is needed before calling him one.
- 2024-12-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-31]]): Reed is 'still clearly their best receiver' and the best man-coverage beater in Green Bay, but Harmon caps him as a 'really, really good number two' rather than a true No. 1 — he is not as good as [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) pushes harder: Reed has not played 80% of the offensive snaps in a single game all season and is often under 60% over the last two months, which Koh calls 'role player adjacent' and 'a complete misuse of resources.' Against Minnesota's heavy man coverage he ran 77% of first-half dropbacks but only 61.9% in the second half, with Malik Heath and Bo Melton rotating in after a Reed drop. Both note his drop problem in critical spots; Harmon adds the offense looked smoothest in the second half of 2023 when Reed was the featured player, and wonders if the staff has lost trust in him.
- 2025-01-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-09]]): Reed is the recurring 'why isn't he getting the ball' case of the episode, picking up honorable mentions for deep ball and for zone coverage and leading a contested-catch split. Deep: 75% offensive success rate on 20-plus air yard throws (first among receivers with double-digit deep targets), 1.96 EPA per deep target, 11 of 14 deep targets caught for a 78.5% catch rate against a 34.6% league average, and 446 downfield receiving yards, fourth-most in the NFL. Zone: third in yards per route run behind [[Puka Nacua]] and [[Ja'Marr Chase]], an 86% catch rate and a 145 passer rating when targeted in zone, the best in the league, on 648 yards and four touchdowns. Contested: a 60% catch rate that led the NFL, though on only 10 targets. The counterweight is drops — a 14.7% drop rate, 11 drops on 75 targets per Fantasy Points — and Harmon points out, counterintuitively, that almost all of them came on throws under 10 air yards, not downfield. Both hosts argue he is pigeonholed as a slot-only player when his prospect profile had him inside on just 26% of sampled snaps; Koh wants him used as a flanker off the line, more intermediate in-breakers and outside work, and Harmon agrees he would benefit from an expanded role. Reed's opportunity may grow with [[Christian Watson]] out on a torn ACL.
- 2025-01-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-14]]): Harmon and Koh both argue Green Bay should feature Reed as the closest thing they have to a number one — he gave real effort and made plays when targeted in the wild card loss before getting hurt, but disappears for long stretches while the offense manufactures designer shots for [[Dontayvion Wicks]] or Bo Melton. Harmon's framing is that Green Bay has a bunch of good receivers and no needle-mover, and that they need to 'cut down the developmental path instead of widening it' — narrow the rotation, empower one guy, and give [[Jordan Love]] a safety blanket he can feed eight to ten times a game. Koh says the offense has looked best when Love simply worked to Reed and [[Romeo Doubs]] with the tight ends mixed in. Both frame a bigger Reed role as the key 2025 offseason ask.

James Koh (not tracked) says he will go to his grave saying Reed was horrifically underutilized in 2024 and that his snap rate makes no sense, wanting him on the field 90% of the time; both note his drop issues last season as a real blemish. - 2025-04-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-29]]): Harmon's loudest point of the episode is that Reed is being misused as a gadget jet-sweep player when the charting says he is a route runner — an 87th-percentile success rate vs press as a prospect, a 25.9% contested target rate with a 64% contested catch rate, and no create-a-touch profile at all. He rereads his own prospect write-up on air: Reed could find his way into the [[Terry McLaurin]] / [[Stefon Diggs]] axis of smaller separators if he keeps honing his craft and improves as a finisher, but if he levels off he lands nearer the Russell Gage tier — a solid number-three target. - 2025-05-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-30]]) [Dynasty]: Reed sits in tier four, effectively back-to-back with [[Matthew Golden]] — a placement Harmon calls 'a classic hedge ranking' and admits is off consensus, since DLF startup ADP has Golden at WR32 and Reed at WR47. - 2025-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-13]]) [Dynasty]: Reed's 2024 Reception Perception profile flatly disproves the 'gadget receiver' label — 67% success on the nine, 81.6% on the post, ~73% on the corner, 79% on digs and 79% on slants, with those deep routes about a third of his route tree. Per Rich Rebar (Sharp Football, not tracked), Reed ran just seven routes from one- or two-receiver sets across two seasons. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) argued furiously that Green Bay is being boneheaded and Reed must play more; Harmon partly agreed but cautioned that [[Elijah Moore]] and [[Curtis Samuel]] show a profile like this can stay stuck in a gadget role forever.

Harmon is 'pretty skeptical' and expects a notification within a month that Reed is headed to IR for surgery.

- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-11]]): Reed is playing on a busted foot and ran a route on only 52.2% of dropbacks in Week 1, but was targeted on 41.7% of his routes for 3.75 yards per route run with a touchdown — 'never on the field, but when he's on the field he's getting that football'. Harmon says he was higher than most on Reed as a prospect, similar to where he had [[Emeka Egbuka]], and thinks Reed has outperformed his billing while Green Bay has capped his role. On the FanDuel prop he likes the over 3.5 receptions at plus-138 despite Vegas leaning under, citing the target rate, Green Bay needing to keep pace with Washington and Washington's cornerback issues; he also flags that Detroit gave up a lot of slot production last year, which may inflate the Week 1 line.
- 2025-09-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-12]]): Reed made a spectacular first-quarter catch in the right corner of the end zone on a brilliant bomb from [[Jordan Love]], but the play was overturned for offensive holding and he landed hard on his shoulder and was immediately ruled out in obvious pain. It turned out to be a broken collarbone. Harris says that does not instantly rule him out for the whole season, but he will have a hard time getting back in the next couple of months and helping fantasy teams during the fantasy regular season. His roster advice: stash Reed if you have an IR spot, otherwise you will probably need the roster spot once byes start.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-13]]): Green Bay misses Reed badly — he was the offense's layup and YAC target, extremely efficient on a per-route and per-touch basis, and with [[Tucker Kraft]] also out there is now nobody to dump the ball to in the flat for nine or ten yards after the catch, which compounds the volatility of [[Jordan Love]]. Harmon says he has been asking for more Reed for three years and it has not happened, and he does not expect it to. He uses Reed as the cautionary case for his own bias toward small, undersized separators — the same bucket as [[Jaylin Noel]] and [[Josh Downs]] — who get pigeonholed as part-time players, while noting [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] proves those boxes are sometimes wrong.
