# Josh Palmer

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): called "a
  non-embarrassing number three receiver" who now, after the [[Keenan Allen]]
  trade, might have to be the Chargers' de facto number one — a big reason the
  hosts name the Chargers' receiver room (Palmer plus [[Quentin Johnston]]) the
  single worst in the NFL. Acknowledges "Josh Palmer bros" expecting a 2024
  breakout given the added opportunity, but is noncommittal ("who knows?").
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]): Waldman calls Josh Palmer a proven, capable contributor in a complementary rather than lead role for the Chargers, with his hold on snaps this year dependent on efficiency given Jim Harbaugh/Greg Roman's expected run-heavy offense.
- 2024-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-13]]): Harmon floats Josh Palmer as the Chargers' likely 'sacrificial' X receiver -- not a great scheme fit for the role, but capable of a professional, C-plus-level job there similar to how Romeo Doubs (untracked comp) is used as Green Bay's functional WR3, which would let the offense deploy more athletically interesting players like Quentin Johnston and Ladd McConkey around him. [Dynasty]
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon described Palmer as a 'non-needle-mover' receiver but a solid, reliable piece in a Chargers passing game both hosts expect to lean heavily on play-action within a run-first, 60/40 split under Jim Harbaugh and Greg Roman, given guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn's (not tracked) 7th-ranked offensive line grade for LA, anchored by LT Rashawn Slater and rookie RT Joe Alt.
- 2024-07-29 — According to Bob Harris (co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]: he expects to invest more in Palmer than in [[Ladd McConkey]] purely on price, calling himself a cheapskate — Palmer is available around WR50-something and it is 'not out of the range of possibilities' that he leads the Chargers in receptions. He stresses this is not a disagreement with [[Matt Waldman]]'s view that McConkey is the team's WR1; Waldman's counter is the Porsche-vs-Toyota analogy — at similar prices, take the better car.
- 2024-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-01]]): Palmer is a player you can get by with as a No. 2 but is an ideal No. 3. Asked by co-host James Koh (not tracked) whether a pass offense can function with Palmer at 110-120 targets, Harmon answers it can be functional and even efficient, but probably not high volume. He also argues Palmer, not [[Ladd McConkey]], is the better fit for the run-game-adjacent slot snaps under Greg Roman because he has more size to set the edge as a blocker. Koh is mixed on Palmer overall but concedes he could lead the Chargers in yards on more downfield usage, and floats a 900-yard season if the staff misuses McConkey as a low-aDOT slot. Harmon notes he still owes subscribers a Palmer profile before the August 26 last call.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: on the claim Palmer will lead the Chargers in targets, Waldman said he feels it only if it is confined to outside receivers — he expects Palmer to lead Chargers outside receivers in targets while [[Ladd McConkey]] still leads all Chargers receivers, a position Waldman said he staked out in May and is 'ready to die on.' He also pushed back on the market fear that Los Angeles simply will not throw: they will run a lot, but the defense and the AFC West schedule mean they will also throw plenty — he conceded you can lean on running backs against the Raiders and Broncos, but not against the Chiefs or the rest of the league. Untracked co-host Bob Harris agreed Palmer will be busy.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): Palmer led the Chargers in routes run with 26 but Harmon calls him, without meaning disrespect, 'a fine NFL number three wide receiver' and 'just a guy.' His specific criticism is route detail on downfield throws from [[Justin Herbert]] — a couple of attempts looked like bad quarterback decisions when the actual issue was Palmer being slightly off his landmark or running a route through when he should have settled. Harmon's conclusion is that those snaps would be better spent on [[Ladd McConkey]], who he thinks can handle downfield work.
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]): the top candidate to absorb volume if [[Ladd McConkey]]'s knee keeps him out. Waldman expects a [[Quentin Johnston]], Palmer and Derius Davis rotation with Palmer the likeliest to see the extra targets; Davis is the sneaky watch-only name rather than an add, because like McConkey he can win one-on-one. He is cooler on Simi Fehoko, who does not offer much after the catch and is a lesser route runner outside of zone coverage.
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]) on Palmer signing with Buffalo for three years, $36M — the same deal as [[Darius Slayton]]: Harmon was not a fan early in Palmer's career but thinks he has rounded into a solid number three, and can do a lot of what [[Mack Hollins]] did for Buffalo last year as a flanker, in a souped-up version — a good dirty-work player, a blocker, tough, able to move inside and out. He hopes it lets [[Khalil Shakir]] kick outside on some concepts. Koh calls it a sneaky good signing, notes Palmer is still only 26, and says he is an upgrade on [[Amari Cooper]]'s 2024, while conceding it is not a huge needle-mover in a group that also has Shakir, [[Keon Coleman]] and [[Curtis Samuel]].
- 2025-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-18]]): Harmon says Palmer 'can have a role' in Buffalo but is not necessarily a great separator at the X position, so he does not solve the Bills' downfield problem for [[Keon Coleman]]. He plans to chart Palmer for the site around the August last-call drop and is interested to see what it yields.
- 2025-07-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-28]]) [Best Ball]: 'fuck it' on the claim that Palmer will be a top-three target getter in Buffalo, but keep eyes peeled because it will be close. Waldman argues the Bills deliberately distribute rather than feed one alpha — his read is they looked at the film and decided not to give [[Josh Allen]] a [[Stefon Diggs]] he might fixate on instead of working progressions. Palmer fits the quick-hitting, run-after-catch role and is still big enough to win selected contested routes, but Waldman projects him competing for the No. 3/No. 4 role, neck and neck with [[Keon Coleman]] and behind [[Khalil Shakir]] and [[Dalton Kincaid]]. Top-three targets is his ceiling, not his floor, unless Shakir gets hurt. Waldman calls him an excellent late best ball pick given the real (if not high-end) contract; Bob Harris (untracked co-host) agrees on best ball but is 'not that interested' in redraft, where Shakir is the piece to own and Palmer is depth.
- 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]): Harmon pushes back on outlets calling Palmer a great separator — he charted four 2024 games and does not see it. Palmer is good on some routes, specifically posts and digs, roughly the big in-breaking role Buffalo wanted from [[Amari Cooper]], and has some vertical ability, but Harmon sees no full-field separation. He projects him as a quality role player who has to get healthy and build chemistry with [[Josh Allen]], the classic 10-11 million a year receiver you can put on the field and not be embarrassed. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) uses Palmer to make a wider point that fans oversell functional role players in a world where you are either great or you suck; Harmon agrees, comparing it to naming [[Romeo Doubs]] the most average starting receiver in the league and getting flamed for what was meant as a compliment.
- 2025-08-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Palmer is a repeat super-deep sleeper — he was also on the 2023 list — and Harris calls himself 'a Josh Palmer kind of guy' whose film he has liked for years, while admitting the dude is 26 now and it is getting late. He is the likeliest name on the list to actually matter in fantasy, though still outside the top 180 on every major platform. Playing with [[Josh Allen]] is a hell of an opportunity: the Bills supported no reliable fantasy receivers last year other than possibly [[Khalil Shakir]], but Harris argues that need not be permanent, and if [[Keon Coleman]] does not improve on a bad rookie year, Palmer was the acquisition to be the outside pro they hoped [[Amari Cooper]] would be for the stretch run. Scouting: fast and smooth, a big guy who is faster than he looks, some insane hands catches on film, but not a run-through-the-defense player, not a super-elite route runner or deep sprinter or change-of-direction guy — straight-line downfield speed is the tool. The caveat is that pairing him with [[Justin Herbert]] was supposed to work and mostly did not; even amid Chargers injuries last year Palmer often could not crack the starting lineup, so Harris cannot promise he earns major target share, only that the team switch might jog it.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]]): Palmer was on Harris's super deep sleeper list — players he probably would not draft in a 12-team league — for essentially the same Buffalo touchdown-regression reason as [[Keon Coleman]]. Guest JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) goes a step further, saying he does not mind Palmer late at all and considers him a value, and would not be upset if someone called out Palmer instead of Coleman, particularly with [[Khalil Shakir]] banged up.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): mixed but ultimately positive on the Bills opener. Harris flagged a play where Palmer gave up on a pass and nearly had it intercepted as a result, which he was ready to hold against him, but after [[Derrick Henry]]'s fumble both Palmer and [[Keon Coleman]] became heroes of the comeback, with Palmer making the big catch on the winning drive after Baltimore's questionable fourth-and-short decision near midfield.
- 2026-06-16 — According to Matt Bove ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-06-16]]): Josh Allen doesn't yet have chemistry with Josh Palmer, and Bove currently projects him as the Bills' number four or five receiver behind DJ Moore, Khalil Shakir, Keon Coleman and rookie Skylar Bell. Palmer had a promising start last year (nice catches vs. the Ravens, a big first drive on Monday Night Football vs. the Falcons) before a game-ending injury derailed him and he never recovered momentum, described as 'really inconsistent.' Bove grouped Palmer with Coleman as candidates to be used as decoy/'sacrificial X' receivers to open up DJ Moore and Khalil Shakir underneath.
