# Derrick Henry

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]] (recorded before the *Film and Theory* episode below): the most fantasy-actionable knock on Henry in the wiki so far, and it is about *stopping* him rather than tackling him. "If you watch the Tennessee Titans, if the run game isn't working with Derrick Henry, it ain't working. You're not fixing it mid-game — they've keyed it. If they have a ton of the box on Henry at this stage in his career, it can be hard to get anything going." His contrast: a [[James Cook]] / [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] / [[Christian McCaffrey]] type "can still produce in a different way" once the base plan is taken away. Angelo also inverts the tackling question — asked who an NFL linebacker least wants to bring down, prime Henry or the movers, "the answer might surprise people," because size is a known quantity and "McCaffrey's feet" and Gibbs's re-acceleration are not.
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, the qualifier in Henry's favor: the one place you would still rather not see him is the fourth quarter, "if he's getting over 15 to 18 touches and he's already pounded you into the ground and you are now tired and beaten up and are making business decisions." But he extends the caveat to the movers too — late in a game "if they're on a roll, you're always guessing with them," whereas with Henry "you're not guessing. You're just praying that you can hit him low." See [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]].
- 2023-12-21 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host on *RSP Film and Theory* — not a tracked expert in this wiki) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]: uses Henry as the standing warning against drafting an archetype off one outlier. His account of the emergence: in Henry's first Tennessee seasons, splitting work with Dion Lewis, "he looks pretty good but he doesn't look like an All-Pro" — closer to "a slightly better, more evolved version of Brandon Jacobs," big and strong in a way that jumped off the page. The change was Tennessee committing to him full-time, after which he "just exploded."
- 2023-12-21 — **Harstad**, same episode, the actual point: at the time, Henry's breakout was widely read as proof the league had over-corrected toward undersized backs and that big power backs were the counter — "ignoring, of course, that Derrick Henry is a **one of one**. Oh, the solution is just draft the next Derrick Henry." [[Matt Waldman]]'s reply: "get a Stradivarius — they're only $19.95 at Walmart." Both hosts treat him as unreplicable and therefore useless as a trend signal, while accepting that the *cyclical* argument (a big back can exploit a league of nickel defenses and tweener linebackers) is sound in general. See [[League Trend Cycles and Market Inefficiency]].
- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: "I don't know that his story has been written yet" — wouldn't draft Henry as a low-end RB1 for 2024, but would happily take him as one of the "dead zone" round backs, expecting more value there than the market will likely price in. Explicitly rooting for a landing spot with the Ravens (alongside Gus Edwards, Keaton Mitchell, and [[Lamar Jackson]]); flags that a hypothetical free-agent move to the Rams instead would be a real risk to [[Kyren Williams]]'s outlook there.
- 2024-01-08 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]: floats Dallas as a second possible free-agent landing spot alongside [[Tony Pollard]]. Waldman's reply keeps his stated preference for the Ravens fit, while agreeing Dallas "could be a good place for him too."
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: reaffirms the Ravens hope from three weeks earlier — "I think he's got another year left. I hope he lands in Baltimore" — and separately floats a [[Josh Jacobs]]-to-Baltimore scenario as an equally exciting outcome. Either back landing with [[Lamar Jackson]]'s offense is "massive" in his framing.

- 2024-02-05 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-05]] [Best Ball]: reports drafting Henry in the seventh round of his first full best-ball mock of the cycle and calls it a market miss — "come on, people, what are we thinking here? He's going somewhere." According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode: agrees a "course correction" is coming once Henry lands somewhere, and repeats his standing Ravens wish-cast from January, explicit that a Baltimore landing spot is "the ideal" outcome. Both hosts frame him as a real value at a seventh-or-eighth-round best-ball price given he was RB12 in 2023 — "a lottery ticket... who it looks like he can still get something done."
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the redraft price keeps sliding earlier as free agency approaches — currently going in the fifth round in Waldman's live drafts, which he calls a likely "huge steal": "based on my eyeballs from last year, he can still play a little bit," and he says he'd have considered Henry in the third round on talent alone. The one real downside case he flags is landing as a committee/situational back for a team that already has an established starter and just wants cheap playoff-fresh depth. Expands the landing-spot list beyond the standing Ravens wish-cast to include the Chargers (new Jim Harbaugh regime), Dallas (replacing [[Tony Pollard]]'s workhorse role) and Philadelphia (replacing the departing DeAndre Swift) — with his closing line on the free-agent RB market generally: "there are two backs... Derrick Henry and Josh Jacobs — they're getting starting gigs somewhere, and if they don't, somebody's stupid."

- 2024-03-01 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host on *RSP Film and Theory* — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-01]] [Dynasty]: ranks Henry fourth of the "Big Four" 2024 free-agent backs (behind [[Josh Jacobs]], [[Austin Ekeler]] and [[Tony Pollard]]) purely on risk — he's "the oldest," and Harstad's own aging research says older backs are more likely to have already crossed the decline threshold. But separately gives him the highest *upside* of the four: "peak Derrick Henry is a Hall of Fame level running back" in a way Jacobs and Ekeler have never been. Digs into NFL Next Gen Stats rush-yards-over-expected per attempt and finds it essentially flat across 2021–2023 (~0.1 yards over expectation all three years, down from ~1.1 in 2018–2020) — concludes the per-touch efficiency drop he'd expected to see isn't actually there, so the 2023 down year reads as a lighter role rather than a real skill decline.
- 2024-03-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode [Dynasty]: agrees with Harstad's read from film — Henry's reduced 2023 role (ceding pass-protection/passing-down work to [[Tyjae Spears]] from Week 1, including Spears's rookie debut in a loud Superdome) looks like a deliberate plan by Tennessee to save Henry's legs for the stretch run rather than evidence of decline. On tape, still shows burst and ability to win short-area/perimeter bursts against NFL linebackers and even beat corners with angles on him — "I'm not as wary about him in that regard." Reiterates that system and role will decide his value: happy to bet on him if he lands with a team that just hands him 250–300 carries and the dirty/red-zone work (the role Tony Pollard inherited and struggled with in Dallas, previously Zeke Elliott's job) — explicitly, a Ravens landing spot — but "forget it" if he winds up a committee specialist in an Atlanta-style offense.
- 2024-03-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Adam Harstad**, same episode [Dynasty]: from a pure fantasy-value standpoint both rank Henry fourth of the four (behind Jacobs, Ekeler, Pollard); from an NFL-team-building standpoint Waldman says he'd flip the order and put Henry first. Waldman's "Column A / Column B" framing: Ekeler and Pollard are the receiving-back column, Jacobs and Henry the bell-cow column — an ideal roster construction takes one from each.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]] [Best Ball]: the standing Ravens wish-cast finally comes true, and both hosts call it the happiest move of the free-agency cycle. Waldman cites his own 2023 tape review (posted to X/Twitter) and 2023 speed data — 7th-fastest tracked time in the NFL, one of his best breakaway-run rates — as proof "this guy hasn't lost anything" beyond playing behind a mediocre Tennessee line; expects a good offensive line plus [[Lamar Jackson]]'s extra man in the box, less early-down RPO work, and real value as a screen/swing-pass option behind Jackson's play-action. Still ranks him outside the RB1 range in ADP terms but says that's a market inefficiency to exploit — "the king has not been deposed" — and plans heavy [[Best Ball]] exposure.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: both hosts are bullish on Henry in Baltimore, an offense that led (or was near the league lead in) running back carries the last two years (364 in 2023, 358 two-year average). Waldman projects 250 carries, 'knocking on the door' of 1,300 yards and 15 touchdowns, calling 280-300 carries plausible but not what he's underwriting. He noted no proven backup on the roster — Rasheen Ali is unproven, Keaton Mitchell is still recovering, Justice Hill is coming off injury — meaning Baltimore would sign a veteran free agent rather than lean on that depth if Henry got hurt, which Waldman reads as confirmation of a heavy planned workload in what is 'not a long-term acquisition' at 30 years old. He noted Henry is no longer a discount at RB9 ADP but is still a fine third-round pick.
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Best Ball]: Both reiterate enthusiasm for Henry in Baltimore that they say dates back to last October, noting the Ravens led the NFL in rushing attempts last year and that neither Keaton Mitchell (not ready for Week 1) nor depth pieces Rasheen Ali and Justice Hill threaten his workload. They call his price 'ridiculously reasonable' given he's going as late as the third round in redraft, and group him with [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Breece Hall]], [[Bijan Robinson]] and [[Saquon Barkley]] as the 'safest' backs in the class -- each paired with a strong offensive line capable of both spreading the field and compressing it against lighter defensive personnel.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]) [Dynasty]: Henry is 'rocking the world' in Baltimore, and Waldman disclosed that roughly three weeks earlier he acquired Henry for a second-round pick in a dynasty league in a win-now situation — Sigmund Bloom got a similar offer and called him 20 minutes later realizing how cheap it was. Waldman's framing is that the market badly underpriced Henry entering the season and that the Ravens' scheme fit made him a bargain win-now buy; the offense has out-rushed opponents by 100-plus yards in all five games, and Waldman noted it has not all been easy, citing Hubbard getting a safety on Henry in the first half of the Bengals game. He pairs Henry with [[Saquon Barkley]] as the one-two punch this show had been recommending all offseason.
- 2024-10-10 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert), co-host with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): Henry is grouped with [[Saquon Barkley]] as an incredible back who is nonetheless overproducing. Both are averaging around six yards per carry, both are among the best backs in the NFL, and Harstad thinks both are plausible future Hall of Famers — but he expects a moderate decline in efficiency from here and says the current level should not be treated as the baseline. The mechanism he gives is the same one he applies league-wide: yards per carry is dominated by a small number of long runs, and the repeatable input is straight-line speed rather than overall quality, so even elite backs cannot sustain outlier averages. No injury or role concern was raised. See [[Yards Per Carry as Noise]].
- 2024-10-24 — According to co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-24]]) [Dynasty]: Henry is the standing example of the aging-curve fallacy. The deterministic read is that he was over 5.0 yards per carry and over 100 yards per game at 25 and 26, that his yards per game and yards per carry have trended down for three straight years to 4.2, and that another year older therefore means another year worse until he washes out at replacement level. Harstad argues that is simply not how careers work — of the top 50 fantasy wide receivers of the last 30 to 50 years, roughly half were better in their last relevant season than in their second-to-last. Careers are closer to random walks than curves, and production is only loosely correlated with a player's craft. Waldman agrees, framing the profit in dynasty as betting against everyone else's assumed orderly curve.
- 2024-11-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-07]]): Waldman recalled the early-career hubbub after Paul Kuharski pointed out Henry flailing through a footwork drill on the first day of training camp, with observers concluding he had bad feet and poor footwork. Waldman brought former NFL running back Chad Spann (not a tracked expert) onto the show to explain that footwork drills come in dozens of variations with a defined progression, and Henry was simply unfamiliar with that particular one and learning something new. Waldman offers it as a standing caution about reading camp clips as talent evidence rather than as a snapshot of where a player sits on a learning curve.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Henry is the pressure point in Baltimore-Buffalo. In the earlier meeting he ran for 199 yards at 8.3 yards per carry in a 35-10 Baltimore win, and against Pittsburgh in the wild-card round Baltimore's quarterback-plus-back rushing total was the largest in a playoff game since Colin Kaepernick and Frank Gore against Green Bay in 2013. Harmon likes Henry over 19.5 yards on his longest rush at -120, calling that price serious respect and a function of the matchup. He expects Baltimore to split time between under-center looks Henry is good at and the pistol and shotgun looks [[Lamar Jackson]] prefers. James Koh (not a tracked expert) countered that it is hard to beat the same team twice the same way and that Buffalo will have self-scouted and prepared a counterpunch aimed squarely at stopping Henry.
- 2025-01-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-21]]): Henry is the biggest 'teeter' on a Baltimore roster Harmon otherwise expects to run it back, because he will be 31 and the offense's sustainability partly depends on him repeating. Harmon says Henry has already 'put a middle finger up at history' and at typical running back age cliffs, so the Ravens will probably just bet on him doing it again — but adds the caveat that what has come before is not a guarantee of what will be.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'feel' Henry over [[Ashton Jeanty]] at Underdog RB5 — we know the line he is running behind, the quarterback, the offense and what he did last year, and Waldman does not think he is going to slow down. His framing on taking a rookie over him: 'when you come at the king, you better not miss.' Untracked co-host Bob Harris agreed and added that Next Gen Stats showed no sign of decline in Henry's 20-plus mile-an-hour runs.
- 2025-05-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-19]]) [Dynasty]: after Baltimore's two-year, $30M extension, Waldman is not scared of Henry at RB6 despite him turning 32 during the season — he projects about 305 attempts, 850 yards and 16 touchdowns and another top-five running back finish, and says he will bet on it, noting he traded for Henry in two dynasty leagues last year expecting one more win-now year and is now probably getting two. His reasoning: Henry has repeatedly handled a high workload, and in 2024 he ran behind one of the best lines and with the best quarterback of his career, both of which return. Stated caveat: if he gets hurt the crowd will call it age and no one will be able to argue back. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) added Henry still owns two of the top-20 Next Gen top speeds at 247 pounds, while citing Andy Hicks that age is undefeated. Waldman recommends handcuffing with [[Justice Hill]], and warns Baltimore may sign [[Nick Chubb]] or [[J.K. Dobbins]] if Henry goes down.
- 2025-08-20 — According to guest Scott Pianowski (Yahoo Fantasy, not a tracked expert) on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Henry is one of three round-two safe options, because in an offense where [[Lamar Jackson]] is the player defenses fear most, Henry runs through the light boxes that creates — 5.9 yards per carry last season plus cheap touchdowns, with the accepted trade-off that he will not catch many passes. Harris pushes back on the availability: he has never seen Henry get to the second round and just took him in the first of a half-PPR draft, though Pianowski insists it has happened in his drafts including the Kings Classic. Harris also contrasts Henry's workload with [[Kyren Williams]]'s, saying Williams's usage is actually crazier despite the reputation, and calls them very different players.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates Henry a 2 out of 10 — until we actually see it, the age-cliff concern is not there for him. Harris rates him a 3 and agrees completely, explicitly recanting his own prior stance: he believes he gave Henry something like an 8 in a similar exercise a year earlier coming out of Tennessee, with 'alarm bells going off', and calls himself an idiot for it now. Harris also drafted Henry in the first round of a DraftKings best ball draft the night before [Best Ball].
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Henry is on two of his teams, both first-round picks in the middle to tail end of round one, which he calls unrevolutionary and exactly where Henry should have gone. In the St. Jude charity league, picking eighth, the plan was Henry in the first and [[Josh Jacobs]] in the second; that failed because listeners knew the plan and Jacobs went at pick 13, so he pivoted to [[Puka Nacua]] in the second. He says he still would have taken Henry over Jacobs but has the two close together.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Henry delivered in a huge way before the brutal fourth-quarter fumble that cost Baltimore the game. What Harris found most notable is that Henry did not need his usual act of grinding the defense down over a game; he went crazy early, with a monster pitch-out carry in the second quarter, a run where Taylor Rapp had him dead to rights and Henry simply laughed and kept going, and another burst late in the half, giving him eight carries for over 100 yards. He housed a long run on the Ravens' final scoring possession. Harris's open question is whether that says the Bills' run defense is a problem at the point of attack or the Ravens' rush attack and offensive line are that good, which he says bears watching but is unknowable after one week.
