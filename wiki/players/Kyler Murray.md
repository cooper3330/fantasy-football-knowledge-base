# Kyler Murray

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]): used as the foil for [[Lamar Jackson]]'s pocket comfort, and referred to as a standing view ("we've talked about Kyler Murray"). Waldman's charge is that Murray's internal clock is set by proximity rather than by the play: "when somebody gets within three yards of him, he runs as if a bomb has blown up in the pocket." The contrast is Jackson, who Waldman says has always been able to operate "in the tightest of pockets" and would "dodge the shrapnel and still throw the ball downfield." Offered as an illustration in the pocket-poise discussion rather than as a ranking or start/sit call.

**Note:** the one live evaluative claim here is negative and narrow — pocket
discipline under near pressure. Nothing in this episode addresses Murray's
2023 ACL return, role, or fantasy price.
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: Waldman likes Murray as a value at QB7 ADP, citing the Drew Petzing scheme he's now had a year in, the addition of [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] on top of Trey McBride, and rushing equity -- Footballguys projects him for 91 carries, fifth-most among QBs. He notes Murray flashed real ceiling before, finishing QB2 in 2020 under Kliff Kingsbury with two top-10 finishes in his first two seasons. But when directly compared to [[Trevor Lawrence]], Waldman says he would rather have Lawrence at QB7 and take Murray instead at Lawrence's current ADP of QB14, preferring Lawrence's larger sample of proven production over Murray's flashes.
- 2024-08-27 — According to guest Derek Klassen (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): bullish on a 2024 bounce-back. Klassen argues people forget how good Murray is because of injury and a bad end to the Kingsbury era, and that last year's counting stats hide strong late-season play — he was throwing insane balls to [[Trey McBride]] and down the field to 'smurf receivers' like Marquise Brown who simply do not finish contested reps. He also credits the schematic shift under Drew Petzing to more under-center snaps and hard play action rather than pure gun RPO, which suits Murray's arm, plus growth into a more mature pocket manager. Conditions for a leap: another good [[Trey McBride]] year, a slightly better offensive line, a step from [[Michael Wilson]] and [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] being as good as advertised. Host James Koh (not tracked) adds that Arizona's underrated pass-catcher depth — Harrison at X, Wilson as flanker, [[Greg Dortch]] in the slot, McBride at tight end — means defenses cannot cheat a safety into the box.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Film and Theory)]]) [Dynasty]: he went back through Murray's three healthy, meaningful full seasons and found that [[DeAndre Hopkins]] is the only receiver Murray ever supported inside the top 24, and that was as a WR4 finish — the basis for Waldman's doubt that Murray can support multiple fantasy-relevant wideouts at once. He adds that Murray has been the third-worst in passing yardage among top-12 fantasy QB seasons, with roughly 33% of his fantasy production coming from rushing; he scores enough, but the 'why' behind it is that he does not stay in the pocket long enough and bleeds plays out off-script. Adam Harstad (Footballguys, not a tracked expert) agreed on the mechanism while rejecting the standard [[Russell Wilson]] comp — both are short, but Wilson works inside the pocket hunting throwing lanes while Murray leaves the pocket immediately to see, and Harstad argued that is not an offense that supports receivers running precise routes in structure.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Murray is a 'fantasy points black hole' — fine as an individual streaming option because of his rushing, but overrated as a lifter of his receivers. Because he does not climb the pocket unless forced, he pins himself to one side of the field, invites pressure, and makes it hard to access his full receiver stable. Waldman's evidence: three fantasy-caliber seasons in five years, all top-12, with nearly 31 percent of his fantasy points from his legs, ninth-rated as a passer but third-worst in yardage, and exactly one top-12 receiver across those years ([[DeAndre Hopkins]] as WR4 in a shortened season; the others finished roughly 25th and 36th). He calls him a garbage-time quarterback and expects only one of [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] or [[Trey McBride]] to survive. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) disagreed on the conclusion, arguing the path to 4,000 passing and 500 rushing yards is still there and he is drafted around QB7 anyway.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-19]]): in a segment on Fran Tarkenton, both hosts settled on Murray rather than [[Patrick Mahomes]] as the closest modern stylistic comp for Tarkenton — co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) said 'the modern Fran Tarkenton is not Patrick Mahomes... he's more Kyler,' with the caveat that Tarkenton was clearly better than Murray, 'especially as a passer,' and that the comp is about inclinations and tendencies: playing off-script, extending plays and creating outside the design rather than operating within it. Waldman called it a great comp and added early-career, Dan Reeves-era John Elway and Russell Wilson to the same mixture. The implicit knock is the one Harstad applied to Tarkenton: a quarterback whose creativity comes with a refusal to stay in structure leaves yardage and offensive efficiency on the table.
- 2024-09-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-26 (Win-Now Trades and Jayden Daniels)]]): Murray is a fantasy 'black hole' in the basketball sense — the ball goes in and does not come back out to anyone in a way that sustains a pass catcher. Waldman's supporting stat: other than [[DeAndre Hopkins]] finishing as the WR9 one season, Murray has not had a wide receiver finish better than WR26 in his three full seasons as a starter in which he played most of the games, with a partial [[Zach Ertz]] season as the only other startable pass catcher. He says the pattern is repeating in 2024 — Murray either spreads it around because he is hunting whoever is open or does not feed anyone, with one strong [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] game two weeks earlier not followed up. Waldman raises it as the Cliff Kingsbury-offense concern he now carries over to Washington's pass catchers under Kingsbury.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): teed up on whether Murray is overrated, Waldman said yes emphatically. He discounted the big game against the Rams because the Rams defense 'sucks' and cannot cover on the back end, and noted Murray supported exactly one receiver in it — Marvin Harrison Jr., and barely. His central claim is that Murray cannot support more than one receiver unless the defense is really bad: he wastes time and wastes his teammates' effort, is unbelievably inefficient in how he goes about things, and shows he is capable of more efficiency but chooses not to be at times. Waldman allowed that Murray can be a good quarterback and admires his skills, but said he would rather have Jared Goff because he knows what Goff gives down to down and that Goff will not waste a good offensive line. His forward-looking call: he does not think Murray has the career length as a starter that people expect over the next five years.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14 (Age Cliffs and Dented Cans)]] [Dynasty]: the hosts disagreed openly. Adam Harstad (NOT tracked) had Murray as a dented can and thinks the buy window has now closed — back to full price after a hot start that looked as good as it scored. He dismissed the Call of Duty narrative ('you don't get to be where Kyler Murray is without being devoted to the game') and rejected the idea that failing to improve from year two to three means a player is capped. [[Matt Waldman]] disagreed and said he 'loves to hate' Murray: a James Harden-style black hole who has supported only about two fantasy-worthy starters in his career, who bails on plays, outruns his protection, runs his receivers ragged, and publicly blamed more experienced teammates like [[DeAndre Hopkins]]. Waldman doubts Murray can get [[Trey McBride]] and [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] to 80-100 yards in the same game and calls him overrated — while conceding he is the QB7 in fantasy right now. Both agreed Murray raises a bad offense's floor more than [[Trevor Lawrence]] but raises its ceiling less; Harstad compared his out-of-structure instinct to Fran Tarkenton.
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]): properly rated as a fantasy asset and overrated in reality. Waldman puts him on the lower end of the top 15, roughly QB8 to QB15 — he creates yardage, he will have multi-touchdown weeks, he is capable enough as a thrower and runner — but says flatly that he is not a field general worth a damn. Asked whether that makes him overrated in reality, Waldman answered without a doubt: he made ridiculous throws in the most recent loss, was a turnover machine, and lost a game Arizona was leading. His formulation is that Kyler is [[Kirk Cousins]] with legs, the hubris turned up a notch and the mistakes blamed on everybody else. He doubts many teams would actually trade for him — joking with Bob Harris (not tracked) that only the Browns would, because they trade for anybody.
- 2024-12-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-09]]): Murray, QB8 at 17.5 points per game, is the quintessential 'bad' fantasy black hole — Waldman says 'oh fuck yes' and does not believe [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] will ever be a top-12 fantasy receiver with Murray unless [[Trey McBride]] gets hurt, and even then he expects the targets to shift to [[Michael Wilson]] rather than Harrison. The criticism is specific: Murray throws too many YOLO balls, does not read the field pre-snap well, and cannot fit the ball with timing into a receiver who has minimal separation the way [[Brock Purdy]] or [[Josh Allen]] can; instead he creates space by tucking and running. Waldman's line: 'I don't think Kyler trusts anybody but himself.' Noted he threw two interceptions Sunday, the first time in his career with multiple picks in consecutive games.
- 2024-12-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-30]]): Murray is 'a skilled quarterback' but 'not a very good quarterback,' a distinction Waldman says is meaningful. His specific complaint is that Murray gets his weapons yardage but is not a touchdown maker as a thrower and makes mistakes at the wrong moments — his image of it is a player raising his hand to say he scored from 50 yards away while the team still loses the game. He points to [[Trey McBride]] going touchdownless until the final week despite elite volume, including a stretch where Arizona threw him four end-zone targets in one game that went uncaught. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) teases that a Murray hater is in the room and argues Murray may still be better than the quarterbacks [[Brock Bowers]] played with.

[[Matt Waldman]] runs his standing argument that Murray is a good fantasy quarterback but not a good NFL quarterback: he spreads the ball around, and only once has he supported two fantasy-worthy receivers inside the top 24 at the position, which was the DeAndre Hopkins year — and Waldman weighs heavily that Hopkins did not want to keep playing with him, treating that as veteran receiver play giving a thumbs down. Harmon counters only lightly, noting Hopkins produced with Brian Hoyer and Ryan Mallett too, so his standards for supporting quarterbacks are not obviously high.

- 2025-02-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-26]]): Murray is a genuine starting quarterback but one who imposes 'a hard ceiling' on the offense — Harmon's issue is not the contract, it is whether you can win with him. He described Murray as stuck in the middle of the league yet inconsistent within that band, vacillating between very high and very low moments, which he called more frustrating than a steady mid-tier starter because it kills the hope that the right environment elevates him from mid to genuinely high production. Harmon still thinks Murray is a quarterback you can win with if the surroundings improve, and said fixing what is around him is Arizona's job this offseason. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) disagreed on the market, saying he has argued all offseason that Arizona would move Murray for a merely good — not Godfather — offer, reading the tea leaves against the team's public stance; Harmon countered that nobody really wants to buy in on Murray, though he agreed the three-year money is actually quite attractive with the cap rising.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): asked whether the Cardinals will be better in 2025 if Murray runs more as he has said he intends to, Waldman answers 'fuck no.' His argument is historical and structural — when has Murray running more produced a winning season, and when has he supported multiple receivers on a regular basis? Waldman says running more just stresses the offensive line and the receivers further, or means Murray is ignoring chances to create quickly and efficiently from the pocket for bigger plays. He allows Arizona could be two or three games better, a seven- or eight-win team, but not a genuinely good one. Waldman notes he happily rosters Murray in fantasy some years even while dismissing the real-team upside — a clean split between [[Fantasy Quarterback Value vs NFL Quarterback Quality]].
- 2025-05-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-14]]): Harmon calls Murray objectively a starting NFL quarterback and at worst a B-plus passer, but not top ten — and warns against being binary about it, because some bad quarterbacks pepper their number one target while a competent one may not. His specific concern is structural rather than about talent: Murray is too short to see the deep-intermediate middle of the field, which is exactly where [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]'s best route lives (87.5% success rate on the dig, his most-run route, yet frequently not thrown to). Reception Perception's quarterback data has Murray throwing a dig on just 3% of attempts in 2023, down from 2.9% in 2022, with 0% comebacks and 4.9% posts. Harmon compares him to [[Russell Wilson]], who never worked the middle of the field, and notes the exceptions — [[Bryce Young]] and Drew Brees — had big interior offensive lines helping their sight lines, whereas Arizona added nothing to the line or the skill group. He also flags that Murray and Harrison need to build chemistry, invoking [[Derek Carr]] and [[Chris Olave]] as a pair who never got on the same page despite the receiver being good.
- 2025-06-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-02]]): a conditional yes at a QB9 ADP. If Waldman believed Murray would get 600-700 rushing yards and six or seven rushing touchdowns, he would have him QB7-QB9; instead he currently has him closer to QB13-QB14, because Murray reported something 'clicking' in the knee off the 2022 ACL without any surgical procedure, and is simply hoping it works out. Waldman's memorable framing: that 'kind of sounds like someone who buys time in the pocket for 20 seconds and hopes things will work out for them, and you have a losing record' — and he says flatly he does not trust Murray to manage himself or his team well, adding he is not high on Murray as an NFL player even where he likes the fantasy profile. His projection is roughly a 25-touchdown player with over 4,000 yards of offense, which is startable, and he concedes that if Murray is healthy enough to reach 600-800 rushing yards with six to eight rushing scores, he is automatically a top-10 fantasy quarterback. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) notes Murray ran 78 times for 572 yards despite the knee and averaged 24.2 points per game as QB2 in 2020.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): he rejects OC Drew Petzing's stated plan to lean further into Murray's scrambling and off-schedule creation — 'I like that idea in theory,' but the problem is Murray already goes off script far too much and is not wise about when to use it. Waldman argues that bleeding plays long outside the pocket ruins timing, creates pressure, wastes blockers who had their man controlled once the QB breaks containment, and forces receivers to re-route. The consequence he cites for fantasy: Murray has supported more than one fantasy option in the receiving game only once in his seven-year career, with year eight now underway. 'I don't think more of what he f**ks up doing is the answer' — he suspects Petzing has watched too many highlights. Until Murray becomes more efficient inside the pocket he will keep capping his pass catchers, and Waldman thinks Petzing goes down with the ship first if Murray does not change, since Arizona may still be paying enough of Murray's deal for him to survive.
