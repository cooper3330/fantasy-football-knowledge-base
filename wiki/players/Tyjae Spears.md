# Tyjae Spears

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his name as "TyJay
> Spears"; normalized here to his real name, Tyjae Spears.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-05]] [Redraft (Standard)]: stock is rising regardless, on the strength of his receiving work out of the backfield — he "played pretty well even with that crappy offensive line performance" as a rookie. Two separate tailwinds stack on top of that baseline: new offensive line coach Bill Callahan (father of HC Brian Callahan) arriving to fix one of the league's worst lines, and a probable [[Derrick Henry]] departure in free agency clearing the workload. Waldman's explicit value comp is Cleveland's Jerome Ford, who delivered real fantasy production behind an injury-depleted line — with the caveat "I think Tyjae Spears is more talented than Jerome Ford." Ceiling case if the Titans also address the line (free agency, trade, or draft): a "top-24 back" floor even before accounting for further growth; downside case if Henry somehow stays: muddier but still rising given the O-line investment alone.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: [[Tony Pollard]]'s signing is "short-term fuck it" for Spears's redraft value, which he expects to keep dropping — but calls Spears a buy-low, "Bob Harris value special" for later in drafts, since the Pollard deal reads as insurance rather than a full commitment away from Spears if he outplays Pollard.

- 2024-04-15 — According to **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) and [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-15]] [Best Ball]: with new Titans OC Nick Holz calling Spears and [[Tony Pollard]] "interchangeable, 1A/1B," Harris takes Spears "as the bargain" — better pass protector, equal-or-better receiver, slightly better inside runner — while Waldman holds few shares of either back at current prices and prefers other value at the same cost.
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]): Spears will be 'one of my highest rostered running backs this year,' still favoring him over free-agent addition Tony Pollard. He cited Spears' 14.8% target share per game last year as the 6th-best rookie RB target share since 2011 (behind only CMC, Saquon Barkley, Alvin Kamara, Bijan Robinson and Tarik Cohen, who was RB15 in his second year), plus a trend that RBs in the RB25-42 range past year five of their career hit at a worse rate than younger backs -- Pollard enters year six. New OC Brian Callahan has called Spears and Pollard 'interchangeable,' running a straight timeshare rather than divided roles, which JJ likes because it lets the better player win the job. Waldman separately noted the Titans should be more pass-heavy under Callahan with Calvin Ridley added and Will Levis pushing the ball downfield ('no governor'), boosting both backs' value, and relayed that Spears drew heavy Week 1 rookie-year pass-protection reps against blitzing NFL defenders and handled them one-on-one -- evidence a defensive-minded, former pass-rusher head coach in Mike Vrabel trusted him early, which Waldman treats as a strong signal of real talent.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): Brian Callahan is talking up Spears' strong offseason, but Waldman flatly rejects Callahan's earlier stated preference for an even three-way split in Tennessee — 'f**k that, I'm not believing that.' He treats it as Callahan describing a scheme ideal rather than a usage forecast, and expects reality to be Spears and [[Tony Pollard]] continuing to split carries, plausibly close to evenly between the two. On talent, Waldman is bullish: he sees Spears as a back capable of 1,200-1,300 rushing yards, and 1,500-1,600 total yards if the volume were there — but says it may be another year or two before that volume arrives.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): Spears might be headed for injured reserve. Harris had already lowered him significantly in his ranks when the high ankle sprain was announced. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): Spears goes on IR with a high ankle sprain and will miss at least four games. In his absence rookie [[Kalel Mullings]] looks like the number two back behind [[Tony Pollard]].
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): Harris slotted Spears fifth on his Week 4 waiver list at the last moment, purely as a running-back stash at 30 percent rostered — 'if I'm trying to hoard running back assets, I'll put him at number five,' while admitting 'I don't know that I feel awesome about having the second running back in Tennessee' and that 'probably the way it's going to work out is that Spears is just going to be annoying.' The case is a change-of-pace one: of him and [[Tony Pollard]], 'Spears seems likelier to develop an explosive play out of nothing,' whereas Pollard needs a runway. Guest SCOTT FISH (Fantasy Cares, NOT tracked) had Spears as an honorable mention around six or seven, right alongside [[Ollie Gordon II]].
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): Spears is Harris's number-two Week 5 waiver add, over guest Jeff Erickson's preferred [[Kendre Miller]]. Reasoning: Spears could be back as soon as this week, [[Tony Pollard]] has the work but nothing has worked in Tennessee, so the new guy coming in could actually take the job if something starts clicking — and Spears is a pass-catcher too, which Miller is not. Harris concedes the Titans are not a better offensive environment than the Saints, and that at 32% rostered Spears is unavailable in many leagues while Miller at 5% is available everywhere, which is Erickson's counterargument.
- 2025-10-14 — According to guest Jake Seeley (The Athletic, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-14 (Week 7 Waiver Wire & More W6 Film)]]): Seeley ranks Spears his #2 Week 7 waiver add, chasing upside -- he's already the passing-game/explosive-play option over [[Tony Pollard]] and Seeley compares the split to the Najee Harris/Jaylen Warren dynamic where the league preferred the higher-ceiling passing-down back. Seeley floats the possibility Tennessee could eventually push Pollard toward a 60/40 or worse split behind Spears, even calling out the coaching change as a minor factor, though he flags Spears could also just remain a bench piece. Harris participates in the discussion without ranking Spears as highly.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-20]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman conceded Spears will likely never be an every-down NFL lead back -- with his ACL history, teams are scared to feed him a real workload and he projects to stay in a change-of-pace role. He called it disappointing given the tape: 'when he runs the football, this guy's a fucking monster,' but doesn't see a team getting comfortable enough with the injury risk to hand him lead-back touches.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Spears was clearly a distant backup in this game, getting only four touches while [[Tony Pollard]] handled the bulk of the competitive-snap work in Tennessee's loss to New England.
- 2026-07-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman feels Spears is talented enough to be a starting-caliber, potentially high-end back -- 'a near-elite pass catcher' -- but says the Titans clearly don't trust him with the workload the tape suggests they should, comparing it to the inverse of Indianapolis not limiting [[Jonathan Taylor]]. He's not drafting Spears this year: with [[Tony Pollard]] entrenched and [[Nicholas Singleton]] added as a rookie, Spears projects as a top-50 back on paper whose expectations should stay minimal barring an opening, and he's also missed nine games over the past two seasons with durability concerns.
- 2026-09-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-09-07]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman gives a 'mitigated' fuck-it on this finally being Spears' breakout year -- he sees Spears realistically as a top-25 back rather than the top-15 role fantasy managers hoped for, because Tony Pollard remains too solid and heavily used when healthy to hand Spears a real workload, with Nicholas Singleton also in the mix. Waldman says if he ran a team that just needed a back for a year or two, he would rather trade for Spears and 'ride him till the wheels fell off' than watch Tennessee's slow-torture timeshare continue to waste his talent. Bob Harris (untracked co-host) says he never invested heavily in Spears even at his cheapest price points because Pollard's usage has been too reliable to bet against for weekly production.
