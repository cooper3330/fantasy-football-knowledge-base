# Garrett Wilson

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-26]]): discussed in the context of the Jets signing [[Mike Williams]] to play X. Wilson has told Harmon directly that he prefers working the slot, where he gets freedom to run post and corner routes as option routes; had big rookie-season plays as a slot receiver but was pushed into more perimeter/X-type usage in 2023 because the Jets had no other real outside receiver behind him (not Randall Cobb or [[Xavier Gipson]], neither a proven perimeter option). Harmon reads the Williams signing as freeing Wilson to move around the formation again, though he'd still want the Jets to add more receiver help even after it — "wouldn't blink" at the Jets taking a receiver 10th overall.
- 2024-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]], same episode, discussing [[Malik Nabers]]'s prospect comps: compares Nabers to Wilson as a fellow "walking explosive play," but notes Wilson was the better zone-coverage separator of the two as a college prospect. Recalls having similar "wild horse"/refinement concerns about Wilson's own pre-draft profile, yet Wilson still finished as the top-ranked receiver of his 2022 draft class (ahead of [[Drake London]] and Chris Olave, neither a tracked page) by the end of his rookie season — floated as a hopeful precedent for how Nabers's career could unfold.
- 2024-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-13]]): Garrett Wilson's charted numbers are elite-tier despite ugly box-score efficiency caused by Jets quarterback play, not talent -- 95 catches for just over 1,000 yards but only 3 touchdowns and a 56.5% catch rate in 2023, which Harmon attributes to Zach Wilson's pressure issues and the offensive design, not Wilson's game. His success rates are 78.6% vs. man, 82% vs. zone, 81.1% vs. press (93rd-96th percentile). As a rookie he hit 81.2% success rate vs. press, joining CeeDee Lamb, Michael Thomas, Tyreek Hill, Ja'Marr Chase, Justin Jefferson and Odell Beckham Jr. as the only rookies in RP history to clear 80%. In year two he cleared the 95th percentile success rate vs. man, a group that includes Stefon Diggs, Antonio Brown, Davante Adams, A.J. Brown, Justin Jefferson, Tyler Lockett and Terry McLaurin at their peaks. He also posted 91% on curls and 90% on slants (up from 84% as a rookie). Harmon flags a real weakness: contested-catch rate under 70% both seasons on a 27.2%-then-29.4% contested-target rate, and directly comps this to Justin Jefferson's year-two contested-catch dip before Jefferson's year-three breakout under a new coordinator -- floating that Wilson could take a similar leap if he gets even league-average-plus quarterback play (hopefully a full season of Aaron Rodgers). Projects medium 2024 numbers of 86 catches/1,337 yards/9 TD with a 1,700-yard high-side ceiling, the 7th-highest high-side projection at receiver leaguewide behind Ja'Marr Chase, CeeDee Lamb, Amon-Ra St. Brown, Tyreek Hill, Justin Jefferson and Puka Nacua. Not yet placed in Harmon's elite tier -- he wants to see a full statistically dominant season materialize first. [Best Ball] [Dynasty]

- 2024-07-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-09]]) [Best Ball]: Harmon called Garrett Wilson 'the most obvious guy that could break into the elite tier this year,' with man/press/zone success rates of 96th/93rd/82nd percentile that 'check every box' for the profile RP looks for, and put him in the same 2,000-yard-upside conversation as [[CeeDee Lamb]], [[Tyreek Hill]] and [[Justin Jefferson]] alongside [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[Ja'Marr Chase]], [[A.J. - 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): Waldman had Wilson fourth or fifth on his receiver board, then talked himself down after new RSP charting on catch technique. Wilson has flaws both with hand placement and with tracking the football — and tracking, Waldman says, is the one trait that rarely gets fixed later ([[Davante Adams]] being a rare exception). Waldman still likes the overall player and the big moments, and notes Wilson has never had a quarterback of [[Aaron Rodgers]]' caliber, nor one who would demand contested, tight-coverage, odd-angle catches at volume — so he has not yet truly been tested against his pre-draft evaluation. Conclusion: value him as a low-end WR1 / high-end WR2 rather than a top-five receiver. High-end comp [[CeeDee Lamb]]; low-end comp [[Diontae Johnson]] — a target-earner whose production never fully translates.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]): the televised sideline argument between Wilson and [[Aaron Rodgers]] is meaningful for fantasy managers but not alarmingly so — with Rodgers having missed OTA time, the pre-snap and early post-snap tweaking he demands has not been rehearsed, so friction is expected and will take reps to resolve. Waldman had Wilson ranked a top-five WR at Footballguys and has now lowered him slightly. The deeper concern comes from his own RSP research, written up in a recent Gut Check column, on ball-tracking correlating to fantasy production: [[Davante Adams]] was a rare exception who had serial tracking problems and improved past them, whereas Wilson did not have serial flaws but did show lapses tracking and attacking the ball. That puts Wilson in WR2 or low-end WR1 land rather than threatening elite production, because Rodgers will challenge him with contested targets he did not always win at Ohio State.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): Waldman had Wilson as high as fourth overall before camp and now lists him WR11, the drop driven not by talent doubts but by reports that he and [[Aaron Rodgers]] are not yet on the same page. His frame is developmental: it took [[Davante Adams]] three years to make the high-level conceptual adjustments Rodgers wants, and Wilson has never played with a quarterback capable of demanding them — his experience is [[Zach Wilson]], whose weakness was exactly that. Waldman said Rodgers told him personally about a week earlier that Wilson has the same drive, desire and talent as Adams, while stressing Wilson is not Adams yet. He expects them to work it out and for Wilson to be very good. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) said he should have taken Wilson over [[Puka Nacua]] at the first-round turn.
