- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman sees a fork for Hurst -- if he refines his routes and adds a bit more mass, he could become an A.J. Green-style big-play primary receiver running a full route tree; if he bulks up further with a more limited route tree, he's more of a [[Tee Higgins]]-style boundary possession receiver. He's already effective with speed turns and weight drop on breaks but needs more route setups and a real patience-and-suddenness contrast in his releases to be compelling at the next level.
- 2026-04-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-06]]) [Dynasty]: on 6'4', 206 lb wide receiver Ted Hurst, who isn't highly ranked publicly, Waldman says he is draft-worthy and projects a range of outcomes. Best case, if his route game develops, is an aspiring A.J. Green type -- a reliable route runner who wins at the catch point and could be a solid WR1 for stretches, though Waldman doesn't think he reaches A.J. Green's caliber. A middle outcome comps him to [[Christian Watson]]. If his route stems, coverage-reading, and ability to drop weight don't develop, Waldman sees a [[Tee Higgins]]-like floor -- a good receiver who wins contested balls and produces on screens/RPOs via athletic ability and matchups, but doesn't dictate a defense's coverage the way a true alpha does.
- 2026-04-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-13]]) [Dynasty]: the 6'4' Georgia State receiver has real upside beyond his projected role-player value if his route running gets refined, which he thinks is plausible given flashes of dynamic athleticism. Ceiling comp is an 'upper-middle-class' A.J. Green -- not 1,400-1,500-yard seasons but a workable 1,000-1,100 yards and six-to-eight touchdowns as a fantasy starter, with a chance at one monster year off an injury elsewhere or strong quarterback play. Floor comp is a contested-catch 'go up and get it' outside option in the mold of [[Tee Higgins]] or [[Mike Williams]] -- still fantasy-relevant at his peak. Noted he was set to visit the Giants, Packers and Chiefs the week of the episode.
- 2026-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: Georgia State's Ted Hurst (6'4, 206) ran a 4.42 40 with a 36.5' vertical and a standout 11'3 broad jump (97th percentile), after a two-year stretch of 71 rec/1,004 yards/6 TD then 56 rec/961 yards/9 TD against low-level competition (though the sample includes one game each vs. Ole Miss and Vanderbilt). Harmon calls him a fighter with strong ball skills and mindset at the catch point but inconsistent hands with too many drops. RP charting shows a slasher profile rather than a true vertical X: below-average nine-route and curl success but strong dig (81%), post (80%) and slant (84%) success. Coverage splits: 67% vs. man, 75% vs. zone, and a surprisingly high 72% vs. press that Harmon says outkicks the tape -- he dislikes Hurst's release package and thinks the number is more about lower-level competition than clean process, wanting footwork tightened up. He compares Hurst's projection to [[Tory Horton]] -- not ready to line up as an X, but could contribute early on deep posts/overs or even in a Z role while he develops his stack-and-go game. Notably his OC at Georgia State is former Browns head coach Hugh Jackson.
- 2026-05-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-05]]) [Dynasty]: Hurst (Georgia State, 6'4', 206 lbs, 4.42 40, 36.5' vertical, 11'3' broad) landed in a crowded Bucs room with [[Chris Godwin]], [[Emeka Egbuka]] and [[Jalen McMillan]], none of whom profile as true X-receiver size, which Harmon says explains a late-third pick on that specific body type. His charting shows a mixed profile: excellent on slants (84% success) and digs (80%), strong hands and ball tracking, and a fighter in contested-catch situations, but a below-70% success rate on curls/go routes and an 8.2% drop rate, with focus lapses at the catch point. Harmon projects him as a developmental, 2027-and-beyond bet who could be sprinkled in as an X this year running horizontal-breaking routes (digs/slants/outs) rather than the straight-line vertical tree, drawing a loose stylistic parallel to early-career [[George Pickens]] or [[Alec Pierce]] usage rather than to [[Mike Evans]] himself. Harmon jokingly but repeatedly predicted Hurst for '1,000 yards every year' in Tampa, flagged on-air as a bit for the show rather than a serious near-term ranking claim.
- 2026-06-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-08]]): Waldman thinks Ted Hurst could see north of 50 targets in the Bucs offense as a 'distant number three' behind [[Chris Godwin]] and [[Emeka Egbuka]], with real streaming/emergency upside if injuries hit ahead of him, but he's not a startable option in a normal week.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]): Hurst is another big, fast rookie people are buzzing about, but Waldman says his role will be limited early because he's not an advanced route runner. If he becomes the kind of relentless worker Waldman comps to Tom Brady or Stefon Diggs and figures out how to accelerate, Waldman thinks he could develop a bit of A.J. Green-level skill down the road; short-term, Waldman compares him more to a T.J. Higgins/big-bodied Mike Williams type -- a boundary go-up-and-win guy who isn't yet a refined timing-route runner -- and doesn't see him becoming an 800-900-yard receiver this year given Tampa Bay's existing weapons.
