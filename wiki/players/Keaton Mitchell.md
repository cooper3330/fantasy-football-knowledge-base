# Keaton Mitchell

## Expert Takes

- 2025-08-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)] [Dynasty]: Mitchell makes Harris's super-deep sleeper list for the third consecutive year — first named in 2023 before [[Derrick Henry]] was a Raven, when he was a very fast, smaller-framed back who got hurt in his first camp, debuted halfway through his rookie year, went off in a blowout against the Seahawks, worked as a satellite back, then tore his ACL that December. Harris put him on the 2024 list hoping he would become the passing-down alternative to Henry, but Mitchell only got back to game-level fitness around Week 17. He is healthy again and was talked up in early August as a big-play threat and 'jitterbug' guy, but Harris no longer expects him to displace [[Justice Hill]], because Mitchell has not proved himself in pass protection the way Hill has — he may be more of a special-teams player. Touches are hard to project; it would probably take an injury to Henry or Hill for him to matter. The bull case: Baltimore has no other truly quick or truly fast back (Henry is very fast once going, not at the start), and the offense could be good enough to support two fantasy backs. Explicitly not a draft recommendation in normal leagues.
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-10-27]]): 'our old pal,' a deep sleeper, got a pure speed pitch play for 25 yards down the sideline in the third quarter behind [[Derrick Henry]].
- 2025-12-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-01 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman remains a Keaton Mitchell believer, calling him very fast and expecting a real shot in a committee role once [[Derrick Henry]] moves on, but both he and Bob Harris (untracked) agreed Baltimore will likely also add a bigger back and that Mitchell's clear opportunity is still 'another year away.'
- 2025-12-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: both pushed back on giving Mitchell more work over [[Derrick Henry]]. Waldman argued availability is itself a talent Mitchell hasn't proven -- he broke a big run and then immediately left the game injured. He likes Mitchell as a change-of-pace big-play threat and a bench asset for bye-week/emergency fantasy starts, but said he offers boom-or-bust production (an occasional explosive play sandwiched by stretches of minimal touches) versus Henry's more predictable weekly floor, and noted Baltimore would be losing by much more right now without Henry given Lamar Jackson's injury and other roster issues.
- 2026-01-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: named Mitchell his #5 level-jumper, noting the Ravens backfield finally settled into a clean 'two-thirds Derrick Henry, one-third Keaton Mitchell' split late in the year as the Justice Hill experiment faded. Harris says Mitchell is not a power back like Tuten but a lightning, home-run-hitter type, and compared his ceiling to [[De'Von Achane]] -- arguing the talent gap between the two isn't as large as their outputs suggest, and that Achane simply found the right offensive mind in Mike McDaniel, which usually doesn't happen for backs this size. With Mitchell now a year further removed from his knee injury and Henry aging, Harris thinks a new coaching staff -- possibly even McDaniel himself if he lands the Ravens job instead of the Chargers OC post -- could unlock a bigger role than the 'splash player' usage the outgoing staff limited him to.
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Dave Kluge (Footballguys.com, untracked guest) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-12]]) [Best Ball]: Mitchell, a longtime deep-sleeper on the show, leaves Baltimore (who didn't tender him at a low dollar figure) for the Chargers under Mike McDaniel, getting notably more money than Baltimore offered. Both hosts flag [[De'Von Achane]] as the natural aspirational comp -- a small, electric, surprisingly sturdy back in a McDaniel offense -- but caution against assuming Mitchell simply becomes the next Achane; Kluge notes he wrote the same 'this is arbitrage on Achane' comp about Mitchell in his 2024 dynasty rankings, before Achane developed into a clearly better player than his rookie flashes suggested. Both plan to draft Mitchell heavily as a cheap, high-upside late flier assuming the Chargers' offensive line is fixed, provided he stays healthy (he's been hurt often).
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris said he's 'always been a fan' of [[Keaton Mitchell]] going back to his rookie flashes in Baltimore before injury, and likes his fresh start in a crowded Chargers backfield alongside [[Omarion Hampton]] and [[Kimani Vidal]], calling him fully healthy now and 'not a bad late-round flyer.' He noted the role picture is still murky given Hampton's own small sample of receiving usage.
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-22 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the swing factor in the Omarion Hampton redraft-value debate. If Mitchell stays healthy, Waldman believes he cuts into Hampton's volume similarly to how Devon Achane carved into Raheem Mostert's role in Mike McDaniel's Miami offense, making Mitchell 'a junior version' of that arrangement -- though Waldman cautions it may not translate one-to-one from McDaniel's old Miami offense to the new Los Angeles scheme.
- 2026-07-09 — According to James Koh (co-host, not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-09]]): despite online 'Keaton Mitchell insurgent' buzz -- fueled by reports that new Chargers OC Mike McDaniel put up literal wanted posters for Mitchell and fullback Alec Ingold before the hire -- Koh is skeptical Mitchell gets real volume behind [[Omarion Hampton]]. He researched head coach Jim Harbaugh's running back usage back to his 2008 Stanford teams and found that of nine backs who led Harbaugh squads in carries, seven were at least 210 lbs and four were 220-plus; Mitchell, at roughly 180 lbs, doesn't fit that mold, and Koh argued a 'control freak' like Harbaugh will have real say over personnel even with McDaniel calling plays. He expects Mitchell to be 'sprinkled in' for occasional explosive plays, as he was in Baltimore, rather than earn a real committee share, partly because he isn't viewed as a competent pass protector -- which is why Koh still views [[Kimani Vidal]] as the more likely true complementary back.
- 2026-07-24 — According to Chris Harris and Andy Behrens (untracked co-host) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-24]]) [Best Ball]: the hosts flagged Mitchell as a name they're already higher on than the market's [[Omarion Hampton]] hype, having first discussed him on their members-only Yacht Club show. Harris calls him one of those 'bird-boned' backs -- explosive, hits the hole hard -- who will never be a featured, full-workload runner, more of an 'every fourth series' change-of-pace piece who 'definitely gets a slice,' guessing something like 20% of snaps. Behrens says the real swing factor to watch in camp isn't Mitchell's role, which he considers settled, but how much work [[Kimani Vidal]] gets, since Hampton 'needs all the rest of the work' to justify his current ADP. Both agree camp reports are unlikely to teach them anything new here barring an injury.
- 2026-08-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris named Keaton Mitchell a post-hype sleeper, describing him as a lottery-ticket back who probably never takes over a full workload but has drawn fresh hype since landing with the Chargers. Harris said he doesn't buy the coach-speak or the 'underwear Olympics' testing hype around Omarion Hampton -- 'I don't find him as fast' on film -- and thinks Hampton's speed has been overvalued, while Mitchell is genuinely fast. He expects Hampton to get the most touches when everyone's healthy in a blended backfield, but wants a 'little chance' at Mitchell getting into the Austin Ekeler business with 3-5 carries and 2-3 catches per game, aided by a Greg McDaniel-run offense (Harris says McDaniel specifically wanted Mitchell) and a healthier offensive line -- the Chargers scored 25.3 points/game with Joe Alt playing versus 18.3 without him last year. He cautioned that if Hampton misses time, the touches likely split with Kimani Vidal rather than funnel to Mitchell alone.
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-20]]) [Dynasty]: both call Mitchell a big sleeper in the Chargers backfield, citing his per-touch average as one of the best in the league and a stylistic fit for Mike McDaniel's outside-zone scheme that [[Omarion Hampton]] doesn't have.
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Mitchell is number nine on the Flag list, his third-plus appearance across Harris's post-hype sleeper, players-we-love and level-jumpers lists. Harris calls him a blast and thinks he played really well late last season in Baltimore; he doesn't know the Chargers' plan for him and isn't sure he'd inherit the full workload even if [[Omarion Hampton]] got hurt again, but likes him as the speed piece of a potentially explosive offense, comparing the upside scenario to wanting the change-of-pace back on the 2023 Dolphins. He notes Harris isn't fully sold on Hampton either. ADP is a mess across platforms -- Yahoo has Mitchell at 193, CBS at 165, and FootballGuys' composite closer to the 120s -- so Harris's advice is simply to grab him whenever you're convinced, since the exact double-digit round doesn't matter much.
- 2026-09-01 — According to guest Ben Gretsch (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-09-01]]) [Best Ball]: he remains high on Keaton Mitchell despite only 59 carries last year, citing NFL player-tracking data showing Mitchell hit 20+ mph a league-leading seven times on those 59 carries -- more raw instances than [[Jonathan Taylor]] and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] (tied at six each) or [[De'Von Achane]] (five), despite those backs getting hundreds of carries. He notes Mitchell carried a real workload at East Carolina and sees a fit for Mike McDaniel's speed-in-space, H-back-style usage, pointing to camp video of Mitchell split out wide running go routes against linebackers before he got banged up. Gretsch groups Mitchell with [[Jonathan Brooks]] and [[Marshawn Lloyd]] as high-upside, high-efficiency backs he may be overrating collectively given all three carry real injury risk, at a position where injury rates have generally fallen (hip-drop tackle rule). [[Chris Harris]] agreed, calling Mitchell one of his long-running 'flag' plays, noting Mitchell is the cheapest of the three efficiency backs and has the best home-run/flex ceiling even if his floor as a true workhorse is lower than Lloyd's or Brooks's, and pointed out the Ravens have never before aggressively committed to featuring him the way this looks poised to happen.
- 2026-09-03 — According to Denny Carter (RotoWorld, untracked) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-09-03]]): Denny floated Mitchell as another candidate for this year's 'James White PPR scam' role, and Harris agreed Mitchell is 'my guy,' noting head coach [[Mike McDaniel]] is clearly invested in him -- but Harris flagged that Mitchell is currently dealing with an injury and said he doesn't actually know how serious it is, calling that the key swing factor on his value right now.
- 2026-09-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-09-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman is feeling Mitchell as a fantasy RB3, calling him the most dynamic, game-breaking runner on his new roster and expecting the coaching staff to scheme him touches both up the middle and outside, including draw plays and passing-down looks that get him the ball in space for early chunk yardage. He warns this is a classic profile where 'everyone chases him early' before fading by around Week 7, so he's worth riding while hot but not worth trading away other running back value to acquire.
- 2026-09-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-09-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: several listeners asked about Mitchell as a deep-league Week 1 flex; Harris says he's coming off what sounds like a multi-week minor injury with an unclear plan, and in a normal-sized league isn't worth starting -- he'd rather use receivers like Khalil Shakir, Jalen Coker, or Matthew Golden in that flex slot, or wait on [[TreVeyon Henderson]] or a [[Brock Bowers]] replacement instead. He also flagged that [[Kimani Vidal]] isn't erased from the Chargers backfield even with [[Omarion Hampton]] in the mix, calling Vidal one of the five most important players to the Chargers' 2025 playoff push -- adding uncertainty to how much rushing work Mitchell would get even healthy.
- 2026-09-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-09-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: [[Keaton Mitchell]] has been limited in practice with a hamstring issue Wednesday and Thursday; even if he's active, Harris said his role is unclear and he isn't 'looking for a reason to use him in Week 1.'
