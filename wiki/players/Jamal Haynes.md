# Jamal Haynes

## Expert Takes

- 2026-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-30]]): Waldman has 'a crush on' Jamal Haynes, a converted slot receiver (with zero catches/targets at receiver two years ago) now a 190-pound Georgia Tech running back. If Haynes adds 15-20 pounds and settles in around the size of a young Aaron Jones (about 195 pounds at UTEP), Waldman says 'look the fuck out' -- he runs with excellent pad level, literally pushing linebackers downfield, and Waldman would use his tape to shame bigger backs like [[Chip Trainum]] and [[Adam Randall]] on effort. He also runs sharper routes and catches better than Randall did at a similar career stage despite the unsuccessful slot-receiver background. Bob Harris (untracked co-host) confirms Haynes ran a 4.48-second 40, which Waldman calls starter-caliber if not truly elite speed, and both project him as a potential fantasy-relevant committee contributor.
- 2026-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-01]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman's central question on Haynes is whether he can make a living between the tackles; the outlook improves if he can add 10-15 pounds to reach an [[Aaron Jones]]-type frame. As a college slot receiver for most of his career, Waldman expects Haynes will get a chance to add quick-twitch muscle, projecting a path like Kyle Williams, Aaron Jones and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] -- backs who entered under 200 pounds and added weight in the league. If Haynes reaches 205-210 pounds while keeping his explosion, Waldman said there are real starter traits to mine: setting up pullers and double teams in gap/zone schemes, strong leverage reading, dynamic speed/acceleration/quickness, and wise game-script decision-making.
- 2026-05-11 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-11]]) [Dynasty]: asked to pick between Jamal Haynes and [[Tahj Brooks]] in Cincinnati, Waldman said Haynes is the one worth monitoring -- he could be 'as explosive of a back as Chase Brown,' is a pretty good pass catcher, and runs hard relative to his explosiveness, making him the closer archetype match if the Bengals want a redundancy plan behind Chase Brown. He contrasted Haynes with Brooks (more of a Samaje Perine-type build with more lateral than straight-line explosion but similar receiving ability), and framed Samaje Perine as likely on his last legs at the position, opening a real path for whichever of the two develops.
