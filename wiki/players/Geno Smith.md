# Geno Smith

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: in a competition framing against newly acquired [[Sam Howell]], says that when everybody's playing their best, "Geno Smith wins this in a cakewalk" — but argues Smith is perceived as shakier under pressure than he actually is, since he's genuinely good at moving around and finding open men; contrasts that with Howell being credited as tougher against pressure mainly because he's willing to throw into it, arguing the two quarterbacks would draw identical praise/criticism if you "switched jerseys."

- 2024-04-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-15]]: "f\*\*k it" on calling him a clear frontline NFL starter right now — two years ago he looked to be trending that way, but last season's dip was worsened by an early offensive-line injury and reported front-office/scheme turmoil between Pete Carroll and the offensive coordinator. Reads new OC Ryan Grubb's arrival and the [[Sam Howell]] acquisition as real, unresolved competition rather than a lock for Smith, a shift down from his 2024-03-25 "wins in a cakewalk when healthy" framing.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: both hosts are 'totally feeling' Smith as a rebound/value play, attributing his down 2023 to a Seattle offensive line ravaged by tackle injuries rather than a talent decline, with the surrounding skill talent still intact. New OC Ryan Grubb is noted for a pass-heavy scheme in his prior stop in Washington. Waldman likes Smith as a QB2 in start-one leagues or a high-upside Superflex piece for managers punting the position early, and laid out Underdog's QB13-23 tier — Brock Purdy, Tua Tagovailoa, Trevor Lawrence, Jared Goff, Justin Herbert, Kirk Cousins, Matthew Stafford, Aaron Rodgers, Deshaun Watson, Baker Mayfield, then Smith at QB23 — saying he'd be comfortable with any of them if he waits at the position.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]): Waldman expects a rebound season for Geno Smith under new OC Ryan Grubb, arguing much of last year's struggle traced to losing both starting tackles in Week 1 with no adequate depth behind them - a common problem across the league that badly hurt a Pete Carroll-style play-action, drop-back scheme. Waldman says Smith has always been a 'heady' processor whose post-snap coverage recognition should improve with better protection and weapons, comparing his prior spot to Mac Jones' situation in New England rather than a genuinely limited passer. With Jaxon Smith-Njigba, Tyler Lockett and DK Metcalf all viable, Waldman projects Seattle to support a clear fantasy WR1 or two strong WR2s plus a good WR3, which could make Smith a top-15 and possibly top-10 fantasy QB in 2024.
- 2024-08-27 — According to guest Derek Klassen (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): Smith is his pick for the quarterback most likely to surprise. Klassen says 30-plus touchdowns is absolutely on the table if Seattle's offensive line is anything but bottom five — even 18th in pass protection would do — and calls a ranking like Mike Sando's QB tiers putting Smith around 23rd alongside [[Deshaun Watson]] 'completely bonkers' and 'aggressively crazy'. His supporting argument: [[DK Metcalf]] is still good even where he frustrates Klassen as a player, [[Tyler Lockett]] still has something left, and [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] has become underrated. The stated risk is the coordinator — Klassen and host James Koh (not tracked) both question how much of Washington's college offense first-time NFL play caller Ryan Grubb actually authored.
- 2024-12-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-17]]): Smith has 'done a lot to keep the disaster at bay' behind what Harmon calls possibly the league's worst pass-protecting offensive line — a point he raised specifically to dispute Chris Collinsworth's on-air praise of Seattle's line. Harmon is explicit that he is not a member of the hardcore Geno fan club, but says the [[Sam Howell]] relief appearance was the clearest possible demonstration of Smith's value: with Geno the offense is 'a little volatile', without him it is 'toast'. He also flags that Seattle's replacement center, who had played well since Connor Williams retired, was hurt in this game, causing snap-count problems on top of the protection issues.
- 2025-01-21 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Harmon]]'s show ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-21]]): Seattle takes a big step back next season because Smith is getting up there in age and has not been aging well. Harmon's own framing was milder — he called the Seahawks 'probably mid' within a division of mid teams — and neither host disputed the other.
- 2025-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-30]]): Geno is genuinely good in the true drop-back game, which is often the weak spot for Shanahan-branch offenses that excel at under-center play-action design, so the Klint Kubiak marriage could be a good mix — and Harmon points out Geno already had success in Shane Waldron's version of the same tree in his first year starting. The caveat is fit and ownership: Geno is 'not this coaching staff's guy,' he is Pete Carroll's guy, so Harmon wonders aloud whether Carroll calls Seattle from Las Vegas to ask how available he is. Both hosts prefer that reunion to a [[Russell Wilson]] one, saying there is more love in the Carroll-Geno relationship. Koh (not a tracked expert) counters that he would not want to start over at ground zero at quarterback, and thinks Seattle's offense goes 'to the moon' if Geno stays healthy.
- 2025-02-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-03]]): 'feel it' that Smith and Seattle work out an extension. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) set the table — Smith led all qualified quarterbacks with an 81.8 on-target rate, his deal runs through 2025, he wants a raise from $12.7M, the team could move on cheaply, and Mike Macdonald has said he wants him back. Waldman expects it to get done because Seattle now wants to run the ball more and has realized a good run game complements its defense better than throwing it around the yard, and because Smith, looking realistically at his own career, should see this as his best chance at a couple more years as a starter — the same realism that got him to work in the first place.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): 'fuck it' on Smith being a long-term fantasy difference maker after the trade to Las Vegas, but he called him a major organizational difference maker. The Raiders now have an actual starter-worthy talent with an existing relationship with the head coach, which buys draft flexibility — they can take the quarterback they actually want and let him sit, or gamble later in the draft, rather than reaching and ending up with the 2024 disaster after missing on [[Michael Penix Jr.]]. Waldman framed him as a good bridge starter for a team that needed exactly that. Downstream, he sees the arrival helping [[Brock Bowers]], [[Jakobi Meyers]] and the run game by putting the offense on schedule.
- 2025-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-12]]): traded to Las Vegas for a third-round pick and reportedly seeking Matthew Stafford money at $45M+/yr. Harmon calls Smith certifiably a bare-minimum top-15 quarterback who gives you top-10 moments, likes the move for the Raiders because no rookie or remaining veteran free agent comes close to that tier, and respects Pete Carroll simply trying to win rather than running an Aidan O'Connell/Gardner Minshew camp battle. He also says Smith's age and back-breaking turnovers get glossed over, and admits real risk in committing multiple years and $40M+. James Koh (not a tracked expert) disagrees sharply: he calls it a classic Raider overpay for an aging player off two good seasons, notes Smith is 35 with a wildly inconsistent career, and points to 21 touchdowns against 15 interceptions in 2024 despite a 70.4% completion rate and pass catchers of [[DK Metcalf]], [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] and [[Tyler Lockett]]. Koh says he would rather take a short-term Rodgers or Jameis deal and draft a bridge, and predicts they will look back in six months and say they saw it coming. He would change his tune if Las Vegas adds [[Tetairoa McMillan]] alongside [[Jakobi Meyers]] and [[Brock Bowers]]. Both agree Seattle moving on to get younger and cheaper is defensible.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Smith at QB22 is real value, and a good illustration of why to shop outside the top 15 at quarterback — the margin between those passers and QB8 through QB12 is small enough to play weekly matchups profitably. He projects over 68% completions and a 4,000-yard season, helped by play-action off what he expects to be a strong Chip Kelly run game. Waldman notes the primary receiver is [[Brock Bowers]], who can go up and get it, with [[Jack Bech]] offering some of that and [[Jakobi Meyers]] good in contested situations — a group of unsung rather than underrated pieces — plus 500–600 receiving yards out of [[Ashton Jeanty]] and [[Raheem Mostert]]. He also credits Smith's three straight 3,500-yard, 20-touchdown Seattle seasons while acknowledging he has had issues.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: bad Geno was the main reason for a snoozy Vegas offensive output. He threw an interception on the first offensive play of the game and was picked three times with no touchdowns, and was lucky not to be picked in the end zone in the first quarter as well; he threw an arm punt into double coverage before halftime and looked for [[Jakobi Meyers]] into double coverage in the fourth. Harris's core objection is that he is too aggressive throwing deep into tight windows. There will be games this year where Geno goes wild for 400 yards and three touchdowns and you kick yourself for not starting him — but the reason you generally do not start him in a single-quarterback league is that bad Geno is so bad. He also badly overthrew an open [[Brock Bowers]] in the end zone.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): the Bears game was about hanging around long enough for Geno to Geno, and he obliged with three interceptions. The first, on the opening drive around midfield, he stepped up and tried [[Jakobi Meyers]] on a dig with what Harris calls elite blindness — he does not see the safety standing in the middle of the field. The second was over the middle again with no awareness of a safety standing there. On the third, a minute into the third quarter, he did see the man-coverage defender trailing Meyers but threw it behind Meyers, more to the defender than the receiver. The knock-on effect: Pete Carroll got in someone's ear and the second-half play calling went very conservative, heavy run, a draw on third-and-three because they were terrified of their quarterback, and a blocked long field goal to end it.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'a tough offense to like.' Smith drove Vegas right down the field on the opening possession and had good moments — checking to a speed option for [[Ashton Jeanty]], a bomb to [[Tre Tucker]] down to the Colts 10 — but then threw an end-zone pass deflected into the air for an easy interception and, down 23 in the second half, an awful throw straight into double coverage that was nearly a pick six. Harris' summary judgement is comparative: unlike [[Daniel Jones]], Smith 'is not salvaging himself with a new team'. Guest Brandon Funston (not tracked) made the same point about [[Sam Darnold]] replacing him in Seattle: 'they both make at least one of those plays a game.'
- 2025-10-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-21]]): Geno Smith and the Raiders' offense were lifeless in a 31-0 loss to Kansas City, managing only 30 offensive plays -- the fewest by any NFL offense in a game since at least 2002, per Nate Tice, with Daniel Jeremiah noting the All-22 review of every Raiders offensive snap took under 5 minutes. Harmon said this 'could not have gone any worse' for Smith, that at 35 years old the Raiders are 'definitely looking for a quarterback after this year,' and pinned the collapse on a regressed offensive line (Jackson Powers-Johnson, DJ Glaze), a nonexistent run game, and a directionless pairing between Pete Carroll and OC Chip Kelly (the NFL's highest-paid OC) that he doubts survives the season.
- 2025-11-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-11-18]]): Harris walks back his own prior recommendation that Smith would make a good streamer, admitting the take was wrong. Smith finished with 240 yards and a touchdown that Harris says looks fine on paper only because Dallas played basura-time defense for most of the second half -- 'that guy stinks and did not take advantage really.' He notes offensive coordinator Chip Kelly abandoned the run almost entirely (three first-half rush attempts, the fewest by any Raiders team in 17 years) while trailing big.
- 2025-11-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-25]]): had chances to hit Tre Tucker on multiple deep touchdown shots but missed them, and generally held the ball too long, took sacks on fourth downs instead of throwing the ball away, and made 'so many bad plays' behind a Raiders offensive line that allowed 10 sacks to Cleveland's pass rush (led by Myles Garrett). Harris says Smith 'just does not have it in him to be consistent anymore,' while acknowledging he still shows flashes of a beautiful throw on occasion.
- 2025-12-09 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-09]]): Smith struggled behind a bad Raiders offensive line, backpedaling into sacks or throwaways for most of the game after an early scoring drive, and took an unnecessary sack that let Bo Nix launch his own Hail Mary before halftime. Smith was hurt late in the third quarter and replaced by Kenny Pickett.
- 2025-12-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-12]]): Smith has not practiced this week, and Harris said 'the wide assumption seems to be it's going to be a Kenny Pickett game' for the Raiders in Week 15; given how Smith has played over the past month, Harris called the change 'a lateral move' rather than a downgrade. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-12]]): Smith returns to the Jets, where he began his career as a bad second-round rookie starter in 2013. Harris credits him with reinventing himself for maybe two good years in Seattle before diminishing returns (a 10-win 2024 that wasn't really because of him) and a rough 55-sack season in Las Vegas last year. His read: the NFL simply doesn't have enough qualified starting quarterbacks, so Smith becomes 'Justin Fields for a year' in New York. With the Jets picking No. 2 overall and reportedly not planning to take a quarterback there, Smith likely opens the year as the starter -- which Harris says doesn't sound great for [[Garrett Wilson]] and [[Breece Hall]], though probably better than what New York ran out last season.
- 2026-05-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-18]]) [Redraft]: Waldman pushed back on the idea that leaving a bad Seattle offense for the Raiders wasn't an upgrade for Smith, noting Smith actually posted QB1 fantasy value in two of his first three years starting in Seattle despite a Seahawks organization that never prioritized offensive line investment until last year. He expects better Raiders quarterback play this season if the retooled offensive line (a stated team priority) holds up, which also supports more 12/13-personnel usage benefiting [[Ashton Jeanty]]. In a separate Jets segment, Waldman called Smith 'a much better signal caller' and diagnostician than Justin Fields or Zach Wilson, projecting he'll unlock more explosive plays for [[Breece Hall]].
- 2026-06-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-29]]) [Dynasty] [Redraft (Standard)]: Calls Geno Smith 'at the very least... an upgrade' over the Jets' prior QB room of [[Justin Fields]], Brady Cook and [[Tyrod Taylor]], noting he led the league in completion percentage twice in three years in Seattle -- real upside if the offensive line, which 'looks good on paper,' holds up. Doesn't want to get too far in front of the move but has 'high hopes' for pieces around him including [[Garrett Wilson]] and [[Michael Wilson]]. Notes conflicting reports on off-field issues from earlier in Smith's career that haven't resurfaced publicly.
- 2026-07-17 — According to Patrick Doherty (Rotoworld, not a tracked expert) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-17]]): Harris calls Smith's 2025 season with the Raiders possibly the worst quarterback season he's ever seen from an established, long-time starter, while acknowledging most of the blame lies with the roster around him. Both doubt his new landing spot fixes anything -- Frank Reich and Aaron Glenn are not the Shanahan-tree-caliber upgrade that could realistically rescue him -- and neither analyst believes Smith is capable of 'conspiring to save' [[Garrett Wilson]]'s fantasy value in New York.
- 2026-08-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Geno Smith reportedly looked like a mess in Jets-Buccaneers joint practices, which Harris said doesn't surprise him given how Smith played with the Raiders last year -- he treated it as reinforcing an existing concern rather than a new one.
