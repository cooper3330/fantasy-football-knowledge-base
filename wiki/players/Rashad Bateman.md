# Rashad Bateman

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Best Ball]: "a super value play" if he's priced cheaply in 2024 drafts — [[Lamar Jackson]] can get him the ball, and Baltimore invested a first-round pick in him. The limiting factor: Mark Andrews and Isaiah Likely are both non-blocking tight ends who likely can't share the field, which caps how much of the passing game Baltimore can build around a third weapon like Bateman. Availability has been the bigger issue than talent so far, and he "hasn't been the playmaker" some expected, but Waldman would take best-ball shares at a discount.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): reinforces the availability read post-AFC-Championship in three words — "Rashad Bateman, always hurt" — cited as one of the reasons Baltimore's receiver room outside [[Lamar Jackson]] himself was gutted all season, alongside Odell Beckham Jr.'s part-time role and Mark Andrews's own injury absence.
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): a mixed update — made it through the full season healthy, which is genuinely notable given the availability concern, and "I love the flashes I saw from him." But stops short of calling him a proven key contributor for 2024 without the Ravens adding more around him in the receiver room.

- 2024-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-26]]): says Baltimore's staff still values Bateman as a starting outside receiver and doesn't expect them to use a first-round pick on a receiver as a result, though the team has shown interest in free agent Michael Gallup (not a tracked page) as complementary X depth.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): Harmon
  ranks Baltimore's "fragile" group ahead of the Falcons and Panthers largely
  on his own bias toward Bateman, admitting it up front — "people are going to
  hate me for this... that's my Bateman bias." Still "the candle is lit" for
  him as a starting outside receiver, with some rough moments to start 2023
  but improved play as a possession receiver as the season went on. Considers
  the receiver room (Bateman plus Zay Flowers and Nelson Agholor, not a
  tracked page) thin on paper behind that hope.
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon is 'back on the Bateman bandwagon,' noting the Ravens extended him through 2026 after his agent's error forfeited his fifth-year option, which he reads as a real vote of confidence. He credits Bateman with visible route-running growth as last season progressed despite a rough start, missing only one game (hamstring) after entering the year unsure he'd even play coming off a foot injury; there were still drops and inconsistency. Harmon compares his profile to a [[Keenan Allen]]-mold underneath route runner rather than a downfield X, and notes the team told GM Eric DaCosta they drafted Tez Walker specifically as a downfield strider so Bateman and Flowers can work the short/intermediate tree. He projects Bateman for a 'quality season' around 600 yards behind Zay Flowers -- not a target-share breakout given Derrick Henry, Mark Andrews and a run-funneled offense -- while explicitly separating 'good in RP' from 'good in fantasy'; the receiving cast behind Flowers/Bateman is thin, which opens a path to more target share for Bateman specifically.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host, not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-18]]): both hosts hope the [[Baltimore Ravens]] use [[Rashad Bateman]] more as a 'big slot' / short-to-intermediate separator in 2024 rather than the outside go-route/curl-heavy X role he played in 2023, arguing that's not his best fit alongside [[Zay Flowers]] also working the slot. They see [[Devontez Walker]], if he develops, as a more natural fit for the vertical outside X role the offense needs, freeing Bateman for more varied route work.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman is fully bought into [[Rashad Bateman]] at his current WR74 price, calling him a likely NFL starting receiver even with [[Derrick Henry]] and Zay Flowers drawing volume, and said he would take Bateman over everyone else discussed in the segment except maybe [[Javon Baker]] -- and possibly even over Baker in many drafts -- citing his after-the-catch ability and route running. He noted the team reinvested in Bateman this offseason despite past injuries and system instability, and pointed out that [[Matt Harmon]] (tracked, Reception Perception) remains high on Bateman as well, having not lost faith in the player.
- 2024-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-23]]): Bateman is the most pressing question in Baltimore's room and Harmon is openly a believer — 'I'm such a big Bateman guy.' His 2023 charting showed poor separation early while he came back from a foot injury so serious he thought he would miss the season, but from the Lions game onward he was a good quality separator and sustained it; the route-running film is good. The open questions are whether he wins the ball: inconsistent hands and poor timing at the catch point on contested throws, which Harmon suspects is reps and confidence post-injury. Usage, not availability, is the ceiling — he played 16 games but cracked 80% snaps only four times, and over his last five games ran 47/44/54/72/47%. He caught just 32 balls for 367 yards. He turns 25, and Baltimore has signalled belief in him consistently since the combine, with John Harbaugh calling him a player they expect to be a top receiver in the league after highlight-reel camp catches the day of taping. James Koh (not tracked) loved him at Minnesota as a bully X who played bigger than 6'1', 200 lbs and rates him a possible high-end No. 2 beside [[Zay Flowers]] — health being the whole bet.
- 2024-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): Harmon says he loves Bateman, calls him a very good player and expects a nice season if he can stay healthy, running out there as a Ravens starter — health being the stated caveat. He notes the Ravens took a long time to recognize Bateman was getting open consistently, only really talking about it late last season, which he attributes to Todd Monken's first year in the system. If [[Lamar Jackson]] is unlocked in year two of the scheme, Harmon and James Koh (not tracked) agree the beneficiaries are Bateman and [[Zay Flowers]].
- 2024-10-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-15]]): 82.4% of Bateman's receptions this season have gone for a first down or a touchdown, fourth in the NFL behind [[A.J. Brown]], [[Nico Collins]] and [[DeMarcus Robinson]], and tied with [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] — a stat Harmon offers with a wry note that the fourth overall pick and Bateman are 'basically the same player' by that measure, while Koh (not tracked) is baffled by DeMarcus Robinson's placement. Structurally, Harmon's point is that Bateman and [[Zay Flowers]] give Baltimore two receivers who can win against man coverage on the outside, which is what allows Todd Monken to throw out of condensed formations and marry the run and pass game. Efficiency, not volume, is the case — no target-share claim was made.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-31]]): Bateman has played well over the past four or five weeks and Harmon called him a 'Reception Perception darling,' noting the big downfield scramble-drill catch against Tampa Bay — while ribbing him for the third-and-long drop he blamed on the sun against Cleveland, also on a scramble drill rather than a called route. Deployment-wise Bateman is a pure outside receiver, on the perimeter for 93.8 percent of his snaps with almost no slot work, doing what Harmon calls [[Sacrificial X Receiver]] duty while still being trusted in one-on-ones; Flowers by contrast is roughly a 60/40 outside-to-slot player. Harmon argued Baltimore did not desperately need a receiver because of Bateman's play, and doubted he is the man who comes off the field for [[Diontae Johnson]] — 'the Ravens like Rashad Bateman' and he has been productive — though he conceded someone in the room likely becomes rotational.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]): Bateman is finally being used properly. What earned him first-round capital at Minnesota was not just YAC as an over-the-middle receiver — the part-time role Baltimore had been limiting him to — but the ability to stretch the field, win contested downfield throws and then add yards after the catch; at Minnesota he and Tyler Johnson were a one-two punch and Bateman was the guy you threw deep to. Waldman credits Todd Monken's offense for letting [[Lamar Jackson]] throw downfield and using Bateman multidimensionally, which creates defensive binds that make life easier on Bateman and on the whole Ravens offense. He calls the decision to re-sign him and pay a little more 'great wisdom' — the Ravens knew what was there and had not exploited it.
- 2025-01-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-07]]): Harmon cited Bateman as the player he has most obviously kept a candle lit for, saying he always believed there was a much higher ceiling to reach and that most people had totally given up on him — including the widespread assumption that [[Diontae Johnson]] would take his starting job in Baltimore. He noted the outcome vindicated the patience: Bateman finished with nine touchdowns on the season while Johnson ended up on a different team. He raised it as the template for staying patient with [[Jahan Dotson]].
- 2025-01-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-21]]) [Dynasty]: 2024 was a breakthrough year for Bateman, and Harmon puts him in Baltimore's set top-three pass-catcher group with [[Zay Flowers]] and [[Isaiah Likely]], all three on the upswing. On the divisional-round interception he does not assign fault — on the All-22 Bateman wins off the line immediately and breaks toward the sideline anticipating the safety crashing, while [[Lamar Jackson]] throws as if the route keeps working inside; Harmon calls it a miscommunication against blitz-heavy man coverage Baltimore was not expecting.
- 2025-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-05]]): Harmon calls Bateman 'a really, really good starting outside receiver' and part of a Baltimore duo with [[Zay Flowers]] that is good enough to win a Super Bowl given [[Lamar Jackson]]. James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) countered that Bateman's long injury history — he got hurt in the Bills playoff game — makes expecting 16-17 games plus a postseason run unrealistic at this stage of his career, which is why Baltimore must add role-player depth rather than count on the top two staying upright.
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]): Harmon says he really likes Bateman and, with [[Zay Flowers]], the duo is 'good enough' — but the Ravens needed a third option, filled by [[DeAndre Hopkins]]. Koh sees Bateman's role as more of a downfield boundary player running deeper posts than an every-down chain mover. Harmon notes Bateman is the Ravens' X receiver, which leaves an open question about who plays the slot now that Hopkins — a career X — is in the building.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Bateman's usage is, in Harmon's word, crazy — he ran 444 routes last year (43rd in the NFL per TruMedia) and was in the slot on just 7.7% of them. Harmon scrolled his full route-participation list and had to go all the way to [[Javon Baker]] at 177th (40 routes) to find another receiver in single digits, and notes that even dominant X receivers usually take 20-25% of snaps inside. For context he cites [[Terry McLaurin]] at 18% under Cliff Kingsbury, which he already mocks as too low. He liked Bateman as a potential slot early in his career and thinks he should be moved inside more, while acknowledging Baltimore's logic — Bateman is the better press-coverage option than [[Zay Flowers]] (41.7% slot) — and that heavy two-tight-end usage limits slot snaps anyway. Bateman had four catches for 128 yards on 38 slot routes.
- 2025-06-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-06]]): Bateman's three-year, $37M extension ($20M guaranteed) is a good move for both sides and a second straight show of faith after the 2024 deal that the Ravens needed to sign to control his rights once an agent misstep cost him his fifth-year option. It moves him from under $6.5M per year — below [[Kendrick Bourne]] and this version of [[Brandin Cooks]] — into [[Dyami Brown]], [[Josh Palmer]] and [[Darius Slayton]] territory. Harmon says Reception Perception saw him playing really well in isolation all along, he never gave up the starting job even after the [[Diontae Johnson]] trade, and 2024 (17 games, 756 yards, nine touchdowns after four in his prior three years combined) was his best season on every efficiency metric. On the over/unders: over 50 receptions, but under on repeating a 16.8 yards-per-reception average and over on 72 targets — same ballpark output accumulated differently — and over 6.5 touchdowns. He notes Bateman ran only 7.7% of snaps from the slot, unusual for a player whose biggest weeks came against cover two, splitting safeties on corner routes, while [[Zay Flowers]] was the movable chess piece attacking single-high. Koh (not tracked) pressed the [[Dynasty]] counterpoint that Bateman managers want a pass-first offense; Harmon rejects it — you're almost certainly downgrading quarterback, and few passing environments are more efficient than Baltimore's, run-heavy as it will remain with [[Lamar Jackson]] and [[Derrick Henry]].
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Bateman is one of Angelo's two picks from the Hopkins/Likely/Bateman trio, alongside [[Isaiah Likely]], with the caveat that Baltimore's passing game likely still revolves around [[Zay Flowers]]. [[Matt Waldman]] calls himself a big Bateman fan but wonders aloud whether the Ravens kick him outside as more of a field-stretching X and install [[DeAndre Hopkins]] as the big slot in more sets — a usage change that would raise Bateman's aDOT while ceding high-value red-zone snaps.
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-13]]): Bateman is Harmon's favorite play in the division — he took the over on a 550.5-yard FanDuel line, arguing it undersells a player who already posted 756 yards on 45 receptions in 2024. Among receivers with 40-plus catches, Bateman led the league with a first down or touchdown on 77.8% of receptions, just ahead of [[A.J. Brown]], plus a 40% explosive catch rate, all while taking only 7.7% of snaps from the slot — dead last in that group, with the nearest comparables ([[Quentin Johnston]], [[Romeo Doubs]], [[Kayshon Boutte]], [[DK Metcalf]], [[Courtland Sutton]]) all true or sacrificial Xs. Harmon calls him a strong technician (78.4% versus press in 2023, better vertically in 2024) who played slot in college and should move around. Risk is health: foot injuries kept him under this line in three of four seasons, and Harmon thinks Vegas is fairly pricing that. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) argued he is closer to 1,000 yards than 550; Harmon agreed he beats it comfortably but doubts 1,000 given Baltimore's run rate and tight-end usage. Both noted early [[Lamar Jackson]] chemistry issues that improved on scramble drills last season.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: bold prediction that Bateman outproduces [[Zay Flowers]] in 2025 by fantasy points, receiving yards or whatever measure you prefer. Harmon called Bateman an ascending player coming off a very good charting year, Baltimore's best press-coverage receiver and a big-play threat on the outside who he expects to move around the formation more this season. He promised Bateman's charting data on site before Week 1 even if a full profile does not land. Harmon stressed the two are very close in talent and that this is not a knock on Flowers. James Koh (not tracked) pushed back with the gap: Flowers had 1,059 yards and 74 catches to Bateman's 756 and 45, so Bateman needs roughly a 50% jump — though Koh conceded Bateman had nine total touchdowns to Flowers's four receiving and that their air-yard shares are closer than people realize.
- 2026-02-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-24]]): Bateman's 2025 was brutal -- 19 catches for 224 yards, less than Puka Nacua's 225-yard output in a single Week 16 overtime game. Charting has him at just 9.8% slot snaps, 4th-lowest among high-snap WRs (only Kayshawn Boutte, Kyle Williams and George Pickens were lower), meaning he almost never runs routes over the middle even though Lamar Jackson's biggest strength is attacking the intermediate middle -- a structural mismatch Harmon calls genuinely 'weird.' He's a good route runner who separates well but is inconsistent at the catch point, offers little after the catch, isn't a willing run blocker, and has lost explosiveness to a string of injuries. Harmon (joking that his 'therapist' has told him to stop talking about Bateman) says he needs a trade and a fresh start with a team that will actually move him around all three receiver spots instead of using him as a static X-only piece.
- 2026-05-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (untracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-05]]) [Dynasty]: with a new Ravens coaching staff replacing the regime that drafted him, both hosts expect Bateman -- a route runner but not a ball winner, undersized for an X at 6'0', 193 -- to be pushed toward a rotational role or even traded, calling the player-team fit one that 'needs a divorce.' He is coming off an injury-limited, disappointing catch total last season despite playing nearly every snap in 2024. Harmon notes he was already rotating with Devontae Walker and others before this staff change, and expects most of Baltimore's receiver room besides Flowers to rotate in 2026.
- 2026-05-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-18]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said he doesn't think Bateman will ever return to his first-round draft value in Baltimore, because the Ravens' passing scheme routes receivers through the middle of the field and doesn't let him win contested catches on the boundary -- the exact skill that made him excellent at Minnesota.
- 2026-07-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-06]]) [Dynasty, Redraft]: Waldman feels Bateman rebounds in 2026, pushing back on frustrated fans ready to write him off after drops or missed plays -- arguing that reaction is often more about bettors losing money than sound evaluation. He says Bateman remains the Ravens' best route runner and most dynamic athlete among their receivers, and a strong contested-catch option, with the front office still committed to using him as the WR2. Reports that rookie [[Jacoby Lane]] or Elijah Surratt could threaten his standing don't concern Waldman -- he doesn't see either as strong enough yet to unseat him, comparing that competition tier more to a Fred Gibson/Joshua Palmer level. He caps expectations at fantasy WR3/WR4 with occasional big games rather than true WR2 fantasy output, given the target competition atop Baltimore's passing game.
- 2026-07-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-20]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman said [[Rashad Bateman]] is effectively Baltimore's only hope at receiver this year given [[Elijah Surratt]] profiles more like a 'Joshua Palmer type' and [[Jacoby Lane]] still needs to learn to stop terminating routes early despite flashing acrobatic catch ability -- and that neither is likely to beat out Devontae Walker anyway. He pointed to Bateman's 2024 career high of 756 yards and nine touchdowns as the bar, and with an ADP around WR92/pick 269 called him a 'free square' in best ball drafts, saying he might even take him ahead of [[Malik Nabers]] at that range given Bateman's talent and how cheaply he can be cut loose if it doesn't work out.
- 2026-07-31 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-31]]) [Dynasty] [Redraft (Standard)]: Bateman remains Baltimore's nominal number-two receiver, but both hosts frame this year as truly his last chance to lock down the role after a poor 2025 season -- Harmon says this year's rookie draft class (see [[Jacoby Lane]] and [[Elijah Surratt]]) reads like contingency planning for Bateman's departure as soon as next year. Harris jokes 'my therapist says I'm not allowed to talk about Rashad Bateman anymore,' admits he's put Bateman on a $1 salary-cap goofball roster in a deep league, but is explicit he is not recommending Bateman as a real redraft pick. Both agree Bateman was a good, meaningful contributor in 2024 and deserves the runway, and Harmon separately hopes Bateman moves off the boundary and plays more off-ball if Lane emerges at X, since Harmon doesn't think X suits Bateman's skill set.
- 2026-08-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-10]]) [Dynasty] [Best Ball]: citing conversations with multiple Ravens beat writers (Jonah Schaefer, Brian Wacker), Waldman says not to sleep on Bateman as Baltimore's real receiving-corps story. He notes Bateman's 700-plus-yard season, touchdown-making ability, strong after-the-catch play, contested-catch skill and solid middle-of-field route running, tempered by injury history and offenses that haven't leveraged him well -- some of Bateman's best games historically came only when Lamar Jackson had to freelance off a bad game plan. New OC Declan Doyle is moving Bateman around the formation, which Waldman reads as evidence of real trust and part of why Baltimore moved on from its old receiver corps. At ADP rounds 18-20, Waldman calls him a safe, high-floor late pick in the mold of a Rico Dowdle-type value -- the exact kind of proven role player worth pairing with a riskier bet like [[Luther Burden III]].
- 2026-08-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-10]]): Harris reiterates he is done believing in Rashad Bateman as a fantasy asset -- 'we are not going to do this with Rashad Bateman again' -- acknowledging that [[Matt Harmon]] is a fan of his but saying he won't be drafting him, and probably won't draft either Ravens rookie receiver ([[Jacoby Lane]] included), in most leagues.
- 2026-08-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-11]]) [Dynasty]: Bateman told reporters he's 'moving all over the place' under new OC Declan Doyle after years as an almost purely static X receiver — Harmon has charted him at just 10.3% career slot rate, third-lowest of 180 qualifying WRs (500+ routes) since 2021, behind only Terry McLaurin and Michael Gallup, with 80%+ on-ball alignment in each of the last three charted seasons. Harmon has long felt Bateman's actual skill set (the Ravens' best separator, no longer a vertical threat, never a true contested-catch winner) profiles more like a Keenan Allen-type slot/Z than a true X, and says he's buying the report that his alignment finally diversifies in 2026 — especially with rookie [[Jaylin Lane]] potentially absorbing X-receiver reps, including taking over the X job outright in 12 personnel if Bateman (who 'always' seems to get hurt) misses time.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]): Waldman still takes Bateman as an 18th-19th round dart even with rookie [[Jacoby Lane]] threatening his role, liking that Baltimore is moving him around the formation -- a low-cost bet on him becoming a real No. 2 target in a retooled offense.
