# Kalen Black

## Expert Takes

- 2026-05-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-04]]) [Dynasty]: is fully bought in on Kalen Black as the 49ers' second-rated back on their own board, drafted ahead of expectation. Black attacks creases at the earliest point, anticipates blocking well on the off-tackle zone and inside zone concepts Kyle Shanahan's staff runs, finishes runs strong and blocks well. Waldman rejects the popular Trey Sermon comparison as shallow -- it's built on shared (lack of) speed rather than shared body type and style -- and instead likens Black's decisive, violent-at-the-point running to what made Elijah Mitchell translate well in this scheme despite not being a zone runner in college; Sermon, by contrast, ran too patiently for Shanahan's taste. Comparing Black to fellow 49ers back [[Jordan James]], Waldman gives Black a slight current edge as the more decisive, anticipatory, physical runner, while conceding James may be the more versatile/creative player -- though the most recent tape on each (Black's from December, James barely played last year) makes it a close call that could shift after an offseason of development.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-07]]) [Dynasty]: the biggest running back reach of the 2026 draft, going 90th overall (3rd round) to San Francisco out of Indiana -- a pick Harmon says sparked the 'consensus big board' discourse online. Charted green in three of four scheme quadrants: posted a 76% outside-run success rate, the third-highest Harmon has charted over two years behind only tiers occupied by Ashton Jeanty/RJ Harvey-caliber backs. But he ran zone on only 11% of his college carries, so it's unclear whether that outside success translates to zone runs at the NFL level. Explosiveness is the concern: just a 13% explosive play rate, well below the ~20% benchmark, and he didn't break many tackles -- Harmon describes his tape as monotonous, gap-scheme, fall-forward-for-4-yards reps, with a standout 65-yard touchdown against Oregon in the CFP and a peak game against Miami in the national title. Pro day measurables (5'9', 208 lbs, 4.45 40, 37.5' vertical) were better than Harmon expected. Verdict: 'a classic fifth round draft pick' -- defensible as a late-4th/5th rounder, not a 3rd-rounder, and 'if the Niners really wanted a grinder that could turn out solid gains and nothing more, they got their guy.' Context: the 6th mid-round RB reach of the Shanahan/Lynch era (Joe Williams, Trey Sermon, Tyrion Davis-Price, Isaac Guerendo, Jordan James), none of whom has topped 500 career rushing yards as a 49er. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) floats a theory -- explicitly his own speculation, not something Harmon confirms -- that Shanahan may deploy Black as a designated early-down grinder specifically to pull touches off Christian McCaffrey's plate in 2026.
- 2026-07-21 — According to Ben Gretsch (Stealing Signals newsletter, not a tracked expert), discussed with [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-21]]): Black is a 2026 49ers rookie running back that Gretsch says 'everyone' considers a huge reach/overdraft, with a profile that isn't great and no real established receiving pedigree. Both hosts said they have essentially no confidence San Francisco will clarify anything about a [[Christian McCaffrey]] backup plan in August, and Black remains a total question mark alongside [[Jordan James]] for handcuff purposes.
- 2026-07-31 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-31]]) [Best Ball] [Dynasty]: Kalen Black, San Francisco's surprise third-round rookie running back, has 'the obvious dotted line' drawn to him as the leading candidate to be [[Christian McCaffrey]]'s backup after Isaac Guerendo (shoulder surgery) and Jordan James (fractured rib, out 2-4 weeks) were both sidelined early in camp and Khalil Herbert washed out as a serious option. Harris cautions this is Shanahan football and the team is unlikely to reveal much about backfield pecking order unless CMC actually gets hurt, but he has already moved Black up into late-round draftable territory across all formats and says Black would be his own top choice as a CMC handcuff/insurance pick right now.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]) [Dynasty]: Pre-draft Waldman had Black as RB17 and comped him to [[Jordan James]] on the same team, or a lesser version of [[Sean Tucker]] -- a hard runner who can catch on screens, attacks at the end of runs (which many bigger backs don't do well), and is versatile and smart even without top-end speed, in the mold of [[Kyren Williams]]. With [[Christian McCaffrey]] not practicing, [[Khalil Herbert]] healthy but not a passing-down back, and both Black (abductor strain) and James (broken rib) hurt, Waldman says Herbert is currently the 'camp body,' while Black could seize the job long-term only if McCaffrey suffers a long-term injury or retires -- San Francisco's Shanahan-tree backfield, per Waldman, has always been a 'throw three against the wall and see which one sticks' approach.
- 2026-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-27]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris has actually drafted Black 'a bunch' in standard leagues and has him inside his top 150, betting he is the current favorite to be the 49ers' next-man-up if [[Christian McCaffrey]] is hurt, since Kyle Shanahan's system 'makes good running backs great' without needing elite talent. Guest Jake Seeley of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) pushed back, arguing it's still unclear whether Black or [[Jordan James]] would get the earlier chance, and that he hasn't drafted either as of this recording, preferring to wait for a definitive depth-chart signal close to Week 1 before spending a bench spot. Seeley noted [[Matt Waldman]] comps Black and James to each other, which he found unhelpful for separating them; the key swing factor cited was that James broke a rib and missed roughly a month, which is part of why Harris currently leans Black.
