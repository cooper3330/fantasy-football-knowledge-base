# Sam Darnold

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast -
  2024-03-19]]: firmly rejects a Baker-Mayfield-style redemption framing for
  Darnold's move to Minnesota — "no, no, not remotely." Argues the "ghosts"
  from his early-career struggles will follow him rather than being erased by
  scenery, and pins real blame on the unnamed early-career coach (described
  as the one who kept "locking him in that crowded closet" in the pocket) more
  than on Darnold himself. Verdict: "Darnold will be a placeholder for
  somebody else at best."
- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]): skeptical beyond a modest 300-500 yard boost from playing with [[Justin Jefferson]], calling him more of a 'Joe Flacco bridge to the next Lamar Jackson' and questioning whether he's solved his career-long struggles reading coverage in the middle of the field.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman projects Darnold for four to six games as Minnesota's starter before [[J.J. McCarthy]] takes over, citing the Vikings' better overall roster than what Darnold has played with previously as a reason some analysts hold modest hope for a Baker Mayfield-style bounce-back stretch, even while expecting the McCarthy transition mid-season.
- 2024-07-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]): the obvious answer is 'fuck no' given what Darnold has shown, but Waldman refuses to fully dismiss him, citing Josh Dobbs as proof players can grow. The traits are real — creative under pressure, fends off rushers physically, makes tough throws on the move, scrambles, fits the ball into tight windows, fearless, big arm, yardage when he breaks the pocket. The unresolved question is whether he can finally read underneath coverage and blitz and coverage disguises, and whether pressure has permanently cost him confidence, leaving him second- and third-guessing. If he has moved past that with [[Justin Jefferson]], [[T.J. Hockenson]] and [[Jordan Addison]] (suspension permitting), Waldman says he could be 'a hidden star' and still sees a path to top-15 fantasy production at his late cost — though he would not want him if he started going as a top-15 quarterback. Bob Harris (not tracked) limits him to two-QB leagues or a week-one waiver claim. [[J.J. McCarthy]] is considered a long shot to open the season starting.
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): with J.J. McCarthy out for the season, Harmon pushes back hard on the enthusiasm for Darnold. He allows the Baker Mayfield or Geno Smith path is possible and that Kevin O'Connell is one of the better play callers in the league — the best coaching Darnold has had — but argues Minnesota is in real danger of a 'lost at sea' season by early-to-mid October with no rookie to turn to, and no appetite for Nick Mullins or Jaren Hall. His supporting-cast caveats: [[T.J. Hockenson]] starts on PUP for at least four weeks and likely longer, the interior offensive line is a question mark even with Christian Darrisaw at tackle, [[Aaron Jones]] is not a lock for 17 games, and Jalen Nailor as the third receiver is not a powerhouse. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) argues Darnold was always going to start Week 1 regardless of McCarthy; the two trade jokes about 'Darnold sickos' versus 'Darnold oligarchs,' with Harmon conceding he does not care much either way.
- 2024-09-03 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): hot take that Darnold leads Minnesota to the playoffs at 11 wins out of the NFC North, citing Kevin O'Connell as a certified good coach, [[Justin Jefferson]] and [[Jordan Addison]] as by far the best receivers Darnold has had, [[T.J. Hockenson]] returning from injury, and Darnold having matured. [[Matt Harmon]] is firmly against: since 2018, of 67 quarterbacks with at least 500 dropbacks, Darnold ranks 58th in EPA per play and 53rd in success rate on over 2,000 plays — a large sample and pretty much none of it good. Harmon predicts he is benched by Week 8 for Nick Mullens, and needles the 'quarterback oligarchy' of podcasters excusing Darnold's record under Adam Gase and Matt Rhule when nobody was that enthusiastic about [[Baker Mayfield]] last year despite Mayfield grading in the 30s in EPA per play.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): Darnold's Vikings debut was 19-of-24 for 208 yards and two touchdowns, completing each of his first 12 passes — per Next Gen Stats the odds of that given each throw's completion probability were 0.8%. Harmon (whom co-host James Koh, not tracked, teases as one of the media 'Sam Darnold oligarchs' alongside Mina Kimes and Nate Tice) grants full credit but wants to 'check in with me in October,' pointing at the Week 5 London game against his old Jets team as the referendum. Koh counters with the precedent: Darnold's first four games in Carolina in 2021 produced a 68% completion rate, five TDs to three INTs and 300 passing yards a game before it 'absolutely fell apart' — these are the highs, the question is whether he avoids the lows. Both agree the supporting cast (Kevin O'Connell's coaching, [[Justin Jefferson]], a healthy [[Aaron Jones]] running well) is the best he's had by a mile.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): Waldman is careful to say he has not yet watched Darnold's tape, so he holds the take loosely and promises a fuller answer in a column. What he will say is that good coaching matters and Kevin O'Connell has done a good job scheming this offense — he cites the play call that sprang [[Justin Jefferson]] for a 97-yard touchdown. He expects Darnold to keep playing well but not to jump into QB1 territory from around QB14, especially with Jefferson out and possibly [[Jordan Addison]] too, and with Houston, Green Bay, the Jets and Detroit upcoming. Useful in superflex and as a streaming option in a bind. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) pushed the mirage case: Darnold starts hot everywhere he goes and then reality sets in.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): Kevin O'Connell gets only about 25 to 30 percent of the credit for Darnold's turnaround — coaches can call quarterback-friendly plays and avoid putting a passer in situations he is bad at, but the rest belongs to Darnold. Waldman's framing is that Darnold did not hide from the world after the poundings he took as a Jet: he is no longer a traumatized quarterback and he has kept working at his craft. The caveat is pointed — Waldman still sees him as a slightly more mature, slightly elevated version of [[Will Levis]], saying he watched the Houston game and kept seeing throws that Levis gets yelled at for. He used the segment to argue the broader case that NFL coaches do not develop players, especially quarterbacks, and that owners keep hiring the hot coordinator name the networks promote.
- 2024-10-24 — According to co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-24]]): Darnold is the clearest illustration of feedback environments. With the Jets he was the golden boy and savior of the franchise who could not do anything wrong; after washing out he became a bust who could not do anything right. Harstad speculates the secret in Minnesota is that they told him plainly when he played poorly and when he played well, held standards and expectations regardless of who he had been before, and pushed him to meet them rather than settling. He ties it to Mike Tomlin's line that when a player is struggling it is the coaching that is struggling. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees, adding that developing any craft requires knowing when you did poorly and when you did well.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): asked whether Darnold is really any different from the Jets version, Waldman says better and more experienced but with the same core flaws intact — a season-high 290 yards, three touchdowns and 28-of-34 completions came alongside two bad interceptions and a lost fumble. His diagnosed blind spot is unchanged: defenders dropping and disguising, specifically a linebacker dropping into coverage or a slot corner sliding over, reads he still does not see. Waldman calls him a gunslinger who will make hero plays and says he has gotten wiser and toned down the worst of it, but he will never be listed among the best quarterbacks in football. Verdict: a competent starter who makes some gutsy plays — he 'feels it' at that level and no higher.
- 2024-11-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]]): Darnold threw three interceptions and no touchdowns in Week 10 against one of the worst defenses in the league while winning 12-7, and Harmon uses him as a case study in long-term vision rather than a player evaluation. His point is that Minnesota is smart precisely because it never seriously entertained extending Darnold during a 5-1 start — they have a first-round quarterback in [[J.J. McCarthy]] and are running what they themselves call a competitive rebuild. A prisoner-of-the-moment franchise would have paid Darnold in October and then arrived at this Week 10 game holding the bag. Koh (not a tracked expert) had posted about Darnold's interceptions over the weekend and Harmon teases him about it being 'his guy'.
- 2024-12-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-09]]): after five touchdowns, 347 yards and a 157.9 rating (career highs), with five throws of 20-plus yards including completions of 52, 49 and 42, Waldman is 'almost feeling it' — Darnold looks like USC Darnold, moving in the pocket and buying time with a little Big Ben Roethlisberger to it. He grants the throws to [[Jordan Addison]] and [[Justin Jefferson]] were not pinpoint but were placed where only the receiver could make the play, and argues that is knowing what you have in your receiving corps rather than YOLO. He stops short of promoting Darnold into a top-five quarterback tier and warns the questions return when a defense can clamp down on the receiver talent — hard with [[T.J. Hockenson]] also lurking. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) thought he looked phenomenal and credited Kevin O'Connell and the coach/GM combination.
- 2024-12-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-10]]): Harmon concedes he was very wrong about Minnesota's season, referencing a hot-takes episode where Koh predicted 12 wins for Darnold's Vikings and Harmon joked he would quit if it happened; Harmon says he only ever saw a path to about 10. He credits time on task with Kevin O'Connell for Darnold's improvement, arguing he now has a much better grasp of the offense and is more willing to pull the trigger on downfield shots, which is the bigger driver of [[Jordan Addison]]'s surge than any change in Addison's route chart. Koh agrees Darnold was up and down early but has been consistently very good over the last month. Harmon's caveat is unchanged: as long as Darnold avoids the crushing mistakes, this offense has a lot of matchup advantages.

McCarthy]] develop behind the scenes so the team carries two potentially good quarterbacks. Waldman's warning is about environment: if Darnold goes to New Orleans he will look like [[Derek Carr]], i.e. 'Derek Carr part two.' He credits Kevin O'Connell for understanding his players' strengths, and also gives [[Kyle Shanahan]]-level credit to Shanahan for bringing Darnold in and talking him up — that endorsement from 'the best offensive mind in the league' likely gave Darnold the confidence not to quit after years of being told his career was over.

He pushed back on a colleague's Super-Bowl-appearance-count argument for quarterback-first team building, saying contention windows (playoff entries, division titles, at least one playoff win over a span of years) are a better measure of a good roster build than championship counts alone.
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon says Darnold's 2024 Vikings season has 'aged really well' even though it was viewed as a bumpy, debated ride at the time -- he uses it as evidence that a merely competent (not superstar) quarterback can be enough to fuel elite receiver production, in direct contrast to his skepticism on [[Kyler Murray]] doing the same for [[Justin Jefferson]] in Minnesota.
- 2026-09-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-09-10]]): Darnold left Seattle's Week 1 win over the Patriots with a hip injury after taking a sack, walking to the medical tent and then to the locker room; Harris does not expect a quick return. With Darnold out, Drew Lock took over and the offense went scoreless in the first half before Lock loosened up in the fourth quarter. Harris also renewed his standing skepticism of Darnold as a quarterback -- 'I still don't really think Sam Darnold is great,' noting he was a passenger on last year's Super Bowl champion team and didn't play well in that game -- while acknowledging the ring is permanent regardless. The bigger fantasy question raised is Jaxon Smith-Njigba's ceiling if Darnold misses real time.
