# Jadarian Price

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2026-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-01]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said Price has mobile hips to adjust stride length and pace and set up rushing lanes, keeps his knees and feet high through low shots, and possesses curvilinear speed so he doesn't have to rely on hard cuts. His flagged issue: he needs to keep his feet tighter when setting up moves near the line of scrimmage, since he currently runs from a place of imbalance there that limits his other skills.
- 2026-04-09 — According to Paul Pertichese, guest analyst on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-09]] (not a tracked expert) [Dynasty]: Jadarian Price is the consensus RB2 in the 2026 class -- also of Notre Dame, alongside [[Jeremiah Love]] -- with strong vision, patience and natural run instincts, plus a shifty, quick-footed style (not a true stop-start burner) and kick-return experience that shows open-field ability. His receiving profile is a total unknown because Notre Dame simply didn't throw to its backs when Love was on the field, which Pertichese compared to the uncertainty around Kenneth Walker III pre-draft, who became an above-average pass catcher. Pertichese projects Price as part of a committee with lead-back upside, but flagged the classic 'second guy in a great backfield gets an unfair bump' risk, citing Sony Michel, Felix Jones and Roschon Johnson as cautionary parallels. Guest analyst Eric Edholm, on the Yacht Club subscriber show, independently also rated Price his RB2.
- 2026-05-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-04]]) [Dynasty]: expects Jadarian Price to lead Seattle's backfield touches, but not to run away with the job outright -- he projects a committee that still leaves meaningful volume for [[Emmanuel Wilson]] behind him.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-07]]) [Dynasty]: an explosive outside-zone runner but a serious question mark inside and in pass protection. Charted a 69% success rate on outside zone (19 carries) and 75% success rate on all outside runs regardless of scheme -- some of the best numbers Harmon has posted across 25 charted RBs -- plus a 26% explosive play rate, the highest he has charted for any back over the last two seasons. Between the tackles in man gap he posted just a 44.4% success rate, well below prospect average, and Harmon graded him an average-to-below-average tackle breaker at the point of attack, citing repeated 'stab tackles' bringing him down. His biggest red flag is pass protection: Harmon calls it 'a huge red flag' -- Price leaves his feet, doesn't square up, whiffs on blocks, and is especially late diagnosing blitzes, which Harmon expects Mike Macdonald's staff to address immediately or limit his snaps. Lands in Seattle with a wide-open runway: Zach Charbonnet is recovering from a late-January ACL tear with no surgery until roughly February 20th (see [[Zach Charbonnet]]), and the only other depth is Emmanuel Wilson and George Holani. Harmon frames a Price-over-Love rookie-year outproduction bet as 'pretty much an opportunity and ecosystem based bet,' not one he'd make, and flags that some of his college success-rate numbers were inflated by garbage-time work in blowouts. Also questions whether first-time playcaller Brian Fleury (promoted from TE coach) can sustain Clint Kubiak's outside-zone scheme at a high level. Comps him to John Schneider's preferred explosive-back archetype (Rashaad Penny, Chris Carson, C.J. Prosise, Kenneth Walker III) and notes Seattle reportedly tried hard to trade out of the pick before taking him.
- 2026-05-11 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-11]]) [Dynasty]: in Seattle's crowded backfield, Waldman said Jadarian Price's current ADP is 'pretty reasonable' given a likely split, with [[Emmanuel Wilson]] projected to get the bulk of the early-season load alongside him while [[Zach Charbonnet]] recovers -- he grouped Price with Wilson as the 'generic' pieces most drafters are responsibly rostering, while flagging [[George Holani]] as the more overlooked name in the same backfield.
- 2026-05-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-25]]) [Dynasty]: reacting to former Washington GM Scot McCloughan's comment that Jadarian Price could be better than [[Jeremiah Love]], Waldman said decision-making -- in-the-moment adjustments plus pre-snap defensive reads -- along with footwork development and pass protection are what separate backs within a tier of each other, and 'Price has a chance to be better than Love, especially from a decision-making and pass-protection standpoint.' Both had chances to show this at Notre Dame, but Waldman said Love's footwork and vision remain ahead right now, so 'I'd still go Love, but I think it will be closer than you think.' Waldman credited McCloughan's scouting track record (Frank Gore, [[De'Zhaun Stribling]], Michael Crabtree) as reason to take the comment seriously rather than dismiss it.
- 2026-07-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman takes 'fuck it' on Seattle's ground game being a good bet unless drafting the lowest-priced viable option. Beat-writer hype has crowned [[Jadarian Price]] the presumptive starter off draft capital alone, but he said Price still has to prove he can pass-protect, catch passes and handle between-the-tackles decision-making before that assumption holds. Current ADP has him at RB26, which Waldman called paying for a 'running back three' outcome with upside to more -- a profile he compared to [[Bhayshul Tuten]] going one pick ahead, while saying he'd rather have [[DeAndre Swift]] two picks ahead of both. He flagged [[Zach Charbonnet]] and [[George Holani]] as real threats to the workload, calling this an unsettled 'carousel' backfield better bought late than early.
- 2026-07-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Ben Gretsch (Stealing Signals newsletter, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Price, taken at the end of round one as a 1B-type complement/kick returner behind Notre Dame teammate Jeremiah Love, is the one Seahawks back the fantasy market will actually draft, likely around the 5th-6th round given his draft capital. Both agreed the Seahawks are extremely unlikely to say anything negative about him barring injury, so direct team buzz on Price is low-signal; instead they'll watch whether hype around [[George Holani]] and [[Emmanuel Wilson]] fades once camp opens -- if coverage becomes 'all Price, all the time,' that's the real tell he's locking down the meaningful role. Harris said he doesn't expect the market to overhype Price the way it will Jeremiah Love, meaning a mid-round stab on him carries limited downside if it doesn't pan out.
- 2026-08-03 — According to guest Dave Richard (CBS Fantasy, not a tracked expert) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-03]]): Price was the #1 training-camp story for both hosts independently. He took first-team reps in Seattle's first full-pad scrimmage and looked good, and the idea that Emmanuel Wilson would be a real threat to him 'now feels foolish in light of everything we've seen.' Both stress the drafting case hinges entirely on [[Zach Charbonnet]]'s recovery timeline from a January ACL tear - Harris doesn't expect Charbonnet back Week 1 (though it's 'not for sure') and Richard frames a Price pick as only justified if he's clearly the feature back, since Charbonnet's eventual role (goal-line, physical, high-value touches) would cut directly into Price's fantasy value once he returns. Harris has Price at RB25 and could see him as high as RB27; Richard is in the same range and worries Price's ADP could get 'rich' if positive camp buzz keeps building, potentially into round four - both expect to move roughly in lockstep since they're reading the same beat reports.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]) [Dynasty]: Price has been banged up in Seahawks camp, and with [[Zach Charbonnet]] unlikely to be right off his ACL, Price's pass protection needs work -- when things at the line get dicey he can 'stifle himself' with his footwork and reactions, limiting his ability to accelerate to either side. Waldman still calls him a nice dynasty prospect who could be a lead or every-down back in the right context, but says he was overpriced pre-draft, and that Seattle looks like a two-or-three-headed committee with [[George Holani]] and Emmanuel Wilson also earning real reps. Waldman would only take the risk in the 7th-8th round or later, and says he'd rather spend an earlier pick elsewhere than on a back likely stuck in a three-way committee.
