# Cam Ward

## Expert Takes

- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]) [Dynasty]: Ward is his favorite college watch of the summer and, on the pre-2024-film evidence, better than Shedeur Sanders and Carson Beck in the upcoming quarterback class. Angelo credits throws to all three levels, anticipation, touch and layering of the ball, and good instincts in the pocket for when to bail. He frames Ward as the same kind of undersold riser [[Michael Penix Jr.]] was a year earlier — a first-round pick most people did not have that high — and calls him a possible Heisman dark horse, noting the transfer path from Incarnate Word to Washington State to Miami. Angelo also likes the surrounding roster (Xavier Restrepo, Damien Martinez) and thinks Miami could win the ACC.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Going Deep)]]): after the blow-up game at Florida, Ward is the most talented quarterback in the 2025 cycle in Angelo's view. The open question from Washington State was whether the off-script brilliance could coexist with on-schedule play — stepping up, fitting the ball into tight windows, making the right decision — and Angelo says the Swamp performance in a hostile environment showed those flashes. He singles out a play where Ward spins left, floats toward the boundary and flicks it 30 yards downfield in stride across the end zone, arguing only eight to ten NFL quarterbacks could make it. He compares the pressure-feel and escapability to Russell Wilson and [[Caleb Williams]]. Caveats: there are still bonehead plays in his game, and Angelo expects Shedeur Sanders or another to go first overall, with Ward landing top-10 to top-12 at the latest.
- 2024-09-11 — According to guest co-host Felix Sharpe of Campus to Canton (NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-11]]) [Dynasty]: Ward is the biggest riser in college football and would be a Heisman finalist if the ceremony were held today. Sharpe traced the arc with downfield numbers — at Washington State in 2022 he completed 56% between 10-19 yards and just 26% on throws of 20-plus; in 2023 that moved to 66% and 40%; through two 2024 games at Miami it is 73% and 75%. Sharpe credits experience (fifth season, third year of power-four ball), the game slowing down, and the best supporting cast he has had. He compares Ward's pressure-avoidance to [[Bryce Young]] and notes he will hold the ball seven or eight seconds. Waldman pushed back hard — two games does not turn a day-three pick into a day-one pick — and asked what Ward actually learned. Both agreed the remaining flaw is that he will not take the free throw: he passes up the open flat route or three-step-drop slant to chase hero ball.
- 2025-02-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-27]]): both have [[Cam Ward]] as the clear QB1 of the 2025 class. Angelo says nothing has changed since August and sees a 'pretty sizable gap' — Ward does things nobody else in the class can with off-script efficiency, extending plays through contact, changing arm angle and throw tempo, and 'climb mechanisms' where he finds the soft spot in the pocket, accelerates then decelerates for a touch throw (he cites a play at Cal). Waldman agrees Ward will very likely be his top quarterback but wants to review a reactivity to pressure he saw a few times, and notes Dan Hatman's complaint that Miami's offense was 'so much four verticals'; he plans to study the Washington State tape. Both caveat that Miami's awful defense forced shootout, down-three-scores throws that clip-cutters misjudge. Waldman dismisses the 8 3/4-inch hand concern, comparing to [[Joe Burrow]] at nine inches, and notes Ward is an academic Heisman winner.
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]): Ward has a better chance than [[Shedeur Sanders]] of surviving as an immediate NFL starter without the 'he's a bust' label attaching, because he is a creative player off structure and his athletic ability should translate. Waldman's framing is that Sanders needs a really strong nucleus around him to play to his capability, whereas Ward would like that but will not completely fall apart without it. On landing spots, Waldman said any of the Giants, Raiders or Saints work for Ward, but he would love to see Ward sit in Las Vegas behind [[Russell Wilson]] or [[Sam Darnold]] for a couple of years; if thrust into New Orleans he is still the better option than Sanders because he can move and throw receivers open, and would be what the Saints hoped they got from [[Spencer Rattler]] and then some. Both Ward and Sanders did little at the Combine itself.
- 2025-03-28 — According to Cory Kinnan (quarterback charter, not a tracked expert) with [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-28]]): the charting inverts Ward's reputation. Despite being seen as a deep-ball artist, his deep success rate over the last eight games was 42% — by far the worst of the five charted (Will Howard 75%, Shedeur Sanders 60%, Jalen Milroe 60%, Jackson Dart 51%) — while he graded near 76% intermediate and 82% middle-of-field, and PFF credited him with 18 turnover-worthy plays. Kinnan calls him the 'dopamine hit' quarterback addicted to hero ball, citing the Cal and Virginia Tech games where Miami trailed by multiple scores because of him and won because of him, and argues middle-of-field passing has the second-widest outcome spread after deep, so being good there is a real edge. His Josh Allen framing is developmental: early Allen's deep ball was poor too, and three explosive plays a game still wins games. Harmon says Ward's arm talent shows most driving outs and digs; both question the Brian Callahan fit given Callahan's in-structure, Burrow-and-Carr lineage, and Koh floats a Jared Goff-style intermediate offense as the path, with Harmon adding a Jordan Love comp.
- 2025-03-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-31]]) [Dynasty]: Ward has the arm talent to start in the NFL immediately and can make most or all pocket throws off a variety of platforms, manipulating defenders with well-executed ball fakes and pump fakes — the play-action game is where his drop footwork looks most fluid and cohesive. Waldman cautions coaches against tinkering with Ward's long, whip-like winding release: it is quick, generates velocity, and is not the source of his accuracy problems. The real work is in the lower body — from pistol and shotgun his drop footwork is fragmented, stop-and-go, and lacking fluid pacing at both Washington State and Miami. It has not yet killed his timing, but the NFL's tighter coverage and earlier pressure demand precision, so Waldman sees a compelling case to bench him early and rebuild the technique, especially with a wider timing-pass menu than the RPO/screen/four-verticals diet he ran in college. Decision-making high points include splitting defenders downfield from a clean pocket, reading leverage, and throwing the moon ball; the lapse is peripheral awareness in the red zone, the most compressed area where mistakes carry the most gravity — he will throw the ball away elsewhere but not there.
- 2025-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether Ward is the safest rookie quarterback to survive a full year one start (likely if Tennessee is all-in on him), Waldman says 'fuck it' — he does not feel any quarterback in the class is that safe, and of his top three ([[Cam Ward]], [[Jalen Milroe]], [[Shedeur Sanders]]) he pegs Ward with the LOWEST floor and Milroe with the highest upside. Ward's benefits are mobility, off-script work and the ability to split coverage, but his Washington State and Miami tape is heavily RPO, screen and four-verticals — concepts that usually end in an off-script play. His drop footwork needs a lot of work, and Waldman's fear is that if the timing routes and his drops are not on the same page, it degenerates into mistake-ridden football; being good off script in college does not automatically transfer.
- 2025-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-01]]): citing Reception Perception quarterback charter Corey Kinnan's profile, Ward grades as an aggressive downfield thrower with the touch to layer throws and the arm talent to drive the ball over the middle — 93% success on digs, 95% on curls and 84% on slants. Harmon's point is the overlay with [[Calvin Ridley]], whose best route grades are on exactly those base slants, curls and deeper in-breakers, and he argues Ridley beats expectations if Ward is good. He also notes Ward threw to tight ends a lot at Miami, which could finally unlock [[Chigoziem Okonkwo]], and that [[Xavier Restrepo]] already has chemistry with him from Miami. The caveats Harmon states plainly: rookie quarterbacks are always an unknown, and Tennessee is still a year or two away from winning games.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]): guest Dan Hatman (Scouting Academy, NOT a tracked expert) said he is a fan of Ward but wishes there were an Alex Smith in front of him. In a truthful setting Hatman believes Ward would admit there are things he would like to tighten up, and thinks Brian Callahan would say the same. Because Ward was better than a healthy [[Will Levis]], he gets the steep on-ramp — no slow development, play right now, and try to win games — the opposite of the runway Andy Reid built for [[Patrick Mahomes]] behind Alex Smith. Hatman puts him in the same bucket as [[Anthony Richardson]] and [[Caleb Williams]]: talented, needing development, and given none of the time.
- 2025-08-21 — According to Jake Ciely of The Athletic (guest, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]] [Redraft (Standard)]: Ward was his No. 5 super-deep sleeper and the only name on his list inside the top 200, pushing outside the top 180 because the class was seen as a terrible quarterback draft. Ciely would rather gamble on Ward than on the [[Matthew Stafford]]s of the world at the end of drafts: the preseason showed aggressive downfield throwing, a big arm that puts it on target, and rushing upside, and three to five hundred rushing yards plus a few scores is what pushed [[Baker Mayfield]] to a top-three finish. At around 4,000-plus passing with rushing on top he would be a high-end QB2. [[Chris Harris]] added his own upside comp from synthesizing draft-expert work: [[Jordan Love]] — big arm, inconsistent mechanics, accuracy that wanes, footwork questions, a player who may need coaching to take fewer chances rather than more.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): guest Ben Gretch (Stealing Signals, NOT tracked) came away from the preseason excited — Ward stands back there ripping throws, the quick release scouts flagged showed up immediately, and he feels like a gunslinger-distributor who gets the ball out and where it needs to be, which Tennessee did not have with [[Will Levis]]. Gretch is careful to separate fantasy fun from quality ('I don't know necessarily how great he's going to be') and acknowledges rookie-QB data says it is hard to support skill players, but argues Tennessee was so basement-level in 2024 that Ward barely has to do anything to be an improvement and let Brian Callahan run his offense. Harris is more negative on the surroundings: 'maybe, maybe' Ward lifts the offense just by not being Levis, but he does not like the head coach or the play-calling at all. [Redraft (Standard)]
