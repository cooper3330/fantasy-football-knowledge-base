# James Cook

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]): asked directly "is James Cook elite?", answers that he's certainly playing that way — RB5 in PPR at the time, off a strong game against a good Dallas defense. Waldman had him rated highly as a rookie on system fit. His read on the delay: Buffalo was cautious early because they weren't confident in his passing-game knowledge, and Cook himself pushed too hard as a runner in ways that produced mistakes. 2023 was "an acclimation year" — he's more comfortable, thinking less, and it shows in his play. Floats him as a candidate to become "the next Austin Ekler type" if Ekler declines.
- 2023-12-18 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode: the goal-line concern is answered — Cook doesn't need carries inside the five to score, because touchdowns come from distance in an explosive offense, even with Josh Allen functioning as the team's best goal-line back.
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]) (recorded before the *Film and Theory* episode below, which cites it): the film rep that seeds the whole framework. Waldman pulls up a Georgia run against Tennessee — two pullers, penetration in the backfield, two unblocked defenders off the edge, Cook taking the exchange from Stetson Bennett without even having hands on the ball — and Cook splits the inside hip of his puller and a defender coming off a missed block, making both miss "like he ran through a phone booth with two people reaching for him," with no deceleration and no downshift, then gains five or six more. Waldman's standard for it: "if you circle this play and ask how many can do this, you can count them on one hand."
- 2023-12-21 — **Brandon Angelo** (co-host — **not** a tracked expert), same episode, the size argument attached to that rep (the ASR carries no speaker labels through the screen-share; the movement-mechanics register and Waldman's own attribution on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]] both point to Angelo): "this wouldn't be possible if James Cook was 210, 205 — this type of movement isn't possible with your 205-plus-pound [back]. You can't move that quickly with that much flexibility in that lower half and then produce force getting up field." His projection from it: the NFL is moving toward this build, and these backs are getting more work on short down-and-distance. He also puts Cook, with [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], in the group "becoming the engine of their own offense." See [[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]] and [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]].
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): names Cook in the group of backs whose separator is changing direction inside tight creases at speed — with [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], Austin Ekler and [[Lamar Jackson]] — and notes he sits "a little bit on the lower end of that weight tier," which Waldman treats as part of the mechanism rather than a risk. See [[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]].
- 2023-12-21 — **Waldman**, same episode [Dynasty]: uses Cook as his own worked example of judging an evaluation too early. He was publicly excited about Cook's fit in Buffalo as a rookie, Cook did little in year one, and he heard "he didn't do anything last year, I'm not buying your work" — including from dynasty managers who passed on Cook because of it. His advice is process-level: misses "may not turn out to be misses after just one year or two years." Reinforces the 12-18 read of 2023 as an acclimation year.
