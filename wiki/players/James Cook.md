# James Cook

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]): asked directly "is James Cook elite?", answers that he's certainly playing that way — RB5 in PPR at the time, off a strong game against a good Dallas defense. Waldman had him rated highly as a rookie on system fit. His read on the delay: Buffalo was cautious early because they weren't confident in his passing-game knowledge, and Cook himself pushed too hard as a runner in ways that produced mistakes. 2023 was "an acclimation year" — he's more comfortable, thinking less, and it shows in his play. Floats him as a candidate to become "the next [[Austin Ekeler]] type" if Ekler declines.
- 2023-12-18 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode: the goal-line concern is answered — Cook doesn't need carries inside the five to score, because touchdowns come from distance in an explosive offense, even with Josh Allen functioning as the team's best goal-line back.
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]) (recorded before the *Film and Theory* episode below, which cites it): the film rep that seeds the whole framework. Waldman pulls up a Georgia run against Tennessee — two pullers, penetration in the backfield, two unblocked defenders off the edge, Cook taking the exchange from Stetson Bennett without even having hands on the ball — and Cook splits the inside hip of his puller and a defender coming off a missed block, making both miss "like he ran through a phone booth with two people reaching for him," with no deceleration and no downshift, then gains five or six more. Waldman's standard for it: "if you circle this play and ask how many can do this, you can count them on one hand."
- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]], same episode, the size argument attached to that rep (the ASR carries no speaker labels through the screen-share; the movement-mechanics register and Waldman's own attribution on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]] both point to Angelo): "this wouldn't be possible if James Cook was 210, 205 — this type of movement isn't possible with your 205-plus-pound [back]. You can't move that quickly with that much flexibility in that lower half and then produce force getting up field." His projection from it: the NFL is moving toward this build, and these backs are getting more work on short down-and-distance. He also puts Cook, with [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], in the group "becoming the engine of their own offense." See [[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]] and [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]].
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): names Cook in the group of backs whose separator is changing direction inside tight creases at speed — with [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], [[Austin Ekeler]] and [[Lamar Jackson]] — and notes he sits "a little bit on the lower end of that weight tier," which Waldman treats as part of the mechanism rather than a risk. See [[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]].
- 2023-12-21 — **Waldman**, same episode [Dynasty]: uses Cook as his own worked example of judging an evaluation too early. He was publicly excited about Cook's fit in Buffalo as a rookie, Cook did little in year one, and he heard "he didn't do anything last year, I'm not buying your work" — including from dynasty managers who passed on Cook because of it. His advice is process-level: misses "may not turn out to be misses after just one year or two years." Reinforces the 12-18 read of 2023 as an acclimation year.

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: "a real shot" at top-12 running back value in 2024 (finished 2023 around RB14-15 in points per game, top-10 in total points). Credits Buffalo's usage pattern for the delayed breakout — cautious as a rookie ("put a governor on him"), fully unleashed in year two despite flirting with Damien Harris and a midseason Leonard Fournette addition — and repeats the "next Austin Ekeler" framing. Flags his outlook as partly tied to OC Joe Brady's return, which appeared likely as of this taping.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]): feeling good on Cook (going as a tail-end RB1/early RB2) even as Buffalo adds a bigger complementary back who could take some short-yardage/red-zone work — doesn't think it takes away much, since Cook has shown he can hold up between the tackles on a real workload. Says 2023 is likely close to his ceiling and expects it to hold steady even with an added complementary piece, since Waldman doesn't expect Buffalo's addition to be a true Jonathan Brooks/Blake Corum-caliber lead-back threat, more a pass-protection-limited complement.
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]): downplayed the threat to Cook's touches from the Ray Davis pick, arguing Josh Allen's own habit of vulturing goal-line carries already suppresses touchdown work for any Bills running back more than a rookie committee mate would. He expects Cook to have 'another good season' as Buffalo's clear lead back in 2024, with Davis carving out only a situational short-yardage role behind him.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon highlighted that Cook posted a 2.4 average depth of target last season -- noting only he and [[Saquon Barkley]] have topped a 2.0 aDOT among running backs over the last two years -- calling him a real weapon in the Bills' pass game as new OC Joe Brady looks to lessen the load on [[Josh Allen]]'s shoulders. Guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn (not tracked) added that Buffalo's ascending offensive line (13th-ranked, with LT Dion Dawkins coming off his best season and added size at guard/center) should keep opening rush lanes for both Cook and power back Ray Davis, reinforcing a run-first shift under Brady.
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]): underrated for his RB1 tier. Waldman recalls watching him at Georgia and thinking there was an outside chance he became a Chris Johnson-type talent; he lacks Johnson's peak contact balance but is as nifty in tight spaces, with great balance in and out of cuts and the ability to make defenders miss with multiple moves — efficient and dynamic. Behind a decent run-blocking unit that explains a 4.7 yards-per-carry average: he breaks long plays and maximizes the creases created for him. He is good in the passing game despite Buffalo splitting work three ways with Ray Davis and Ty Johnson, and is by far their best between-the-tackles runner. Bob Harris (not tracked) agreed he has a blind spot on Cook because [[Josh Allen]] absorbs all the attention in that offense.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]): Cook is a Chris Johnson comp in skills but emphatically not in role. Johnson inherited Eddie George's heavy-duty carry share in Tennessee; Cook gets roughly half that volume, with Waldman noting Cook has taken fewer than half the snaps over the last month while Ray Davis and Ty Johnson eat into the workload and [[Josh Allen]] absorbs the goal-line and scramble rushing. Waldman still credits the big-play ability, slipperiness, agility and speed — 'he is like him, he is not him' — but says the match of skills to role is not strong enough to put Cook in a Chris Johnson tier. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) said every time he fades Cook he gets burned, and that he has repeatedly picked wrong between Cook and [[Chuba Hubbard]] this season.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Buffalo's run game has been awesome and Cook is a big part of whether they can go blow for blow with Baltimore's explosive run game, but Harmon does not think they will be able to activate it against that front. James Koh (not a tracked expert) piggybacked with a receiving angle: he does not mind the over on a 13.5 to 14.5 receiving-yard line for Cook, since Baltimore has been so difficult to run on that Buffalo may get him involved through the air instead. Koh noted Ty Johnson is the designated pass-catching back and that he would be surprised if Ray Davis is available, which concentrates the backfield snaps; he would also take the over on any Ty Johnson receiving prop that opens.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-23]]): Harmon expects some Cook receiving work in the AFC Championship, especially if Buffalo can split him out, though he notes that has been inconsistent — at times successful, at other times he is 'just a good dump-off option'. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) added the more concerning trend: Buffalo has taken a lot of the pass-catching opportunities away from Cook in recent weeks and given them to Ty Johnson, and he wonders whether Johnson gets more involved here. Koh cited the Week 11 win over Kansas City as the template — 15 routes, six targets, five catches for just seven yards, volume without production.
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): Cook is 'such a good fit' in Buffalo's gap-scheme-heavy power run game — a fit that began showing late in the prior season and became obvious this year. Harmon's point is counter-intuitive: gap concepts usually call for a big tempo back grinding at a defense, and instead the Bills drop a roughly 200-pound home-run hitter into those concepts and it works. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) called him 'an evolved player' and noted Buffalo's best stretch in the AFC Championship came when they hammered the ball with him; Harmon adds he had a good day even against what he rates one of the NFL's best run defenses.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): among his wildest draft-weekend predictions is Cook being traded, because he does not believe Buffalo will pay him. [[Matt Waldman]] names New England as the team with the cap space that would, since [[Rhamondre Stevenson]] is 'just a guy' — while immediately noting the counterargument that a Cook-like player is available in the third round of this class, or even the fifth, which is precisely why a team may not pay the veteran price.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Cook is a yes on the weapon-back question and the Bills may need it, because Angelo called him legitimately their best skill player and one of the better receivers in the league at the position, in a receiver room he said contains no one who would start on most teams — [[Khalil Shakir]] is a good slot but not a number-one, [[Josh Palmer]] and [[Elijah Moore]] are 'just guys,' and the best vertical threat might be [[Kelly Akharaiyi]], who probably will not make the roster. [[Matt Waldman]] floated the theory that Joe Brady was hired partly to deliberately flatten the target hierarchy so [[Josh Allen]] cannot lock onto a security blanket; Angelo said there has to be legitimacy to it, since on paper that skill group is not Super-Bowl caliber [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-13]]): Cook's hold-in ended with a new Bills deal worth $30 million guaranteed — top-10-ish for the position without touching the upper echelon — leaving plenty of time to round into game shape and reassume 'the tip of the spear' in Buffalo's offense with [[Josh Allen]]. Harris notes ADP had slid to roughly 34th overall (end of the third round) but says he never lowered Cook from his peak rank, holding him at 20 overall in standard and 27 in PPR, and he had been happily taking Cook in the third in 'wise guy' drafts. He expects the price to snap back inside the second round now that the contract is done, so the edge is a race against ADP catching up before your draft.
- 2025-08-18 — According to guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert), on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Cook is his No. 5 bust. Cook had nine combined touchdowns across his first two seasons and then doubled his career total with 18 last year, which Doherty calls a fluke, but the deeper problem is snaps — Cook played 47.7% of Buffalo's snaps and Doherty sees no path to the 60% range. He loses early downs to [[Ray Davis]], third downs to Ty Johnson and goal-line work to [[Josh Allen]], so he has competition in essentially every game situation, which is worse than raw touchdown regression; he is being drafted RB13–16 as an RB1/2 borderline. [[Chris Harris]] is 'more in' on Cook: he accepts 18 touchdowns will not repeat but invokes the [[Jalen Hurts]]/[[Saquon Barkley]] principle that a good offense leaves enough, and cites his Almanac dancing metric — Cook gained zero or fewer yards on 33 of 207 carries versus [[De'Von Achane]]'s 49 of 203 — while pointing to Cook's AFC Championship fourth-and-one option-pitch touchdown as evidence for a 180-pound back.
- 2025-09-19 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-19]]): 'James Cook is always a starter.' Harris says the preseason doom and gloom — people nominating Cook as a bust and a regression candidate — has simply not happened through three weeks, and he was very good again against Miami, adding a one-yard touchdown after [[Dalton Kincaid]] was stopped at the goal line. In arguing against Kirk Herbstreit's line about all the 'toys' [[Josh Allen]] has, Harris grants that Cook is the one skill player who qualifies; comparing Allen to prime Cam Newton (who had Steve Smith, Greg Olsen, DeAngelo Williams and Jonathan Stewart), he calls the rest of Buffalo's supporting cast 'kind of just a bunch of guys.'
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): Cook was wonderful against New Orleans and central to the methodical nine-minute drive to open the second half that turned the game, which ended in a [[Josh Allen]] rushing touchdown. Harris says Cook did 'a lovely homage to [[Alvin Kamara]]' with some powerful running, and notes he has now scored a touchdown in eight straight games. Along with Allen, Cook is one of only two Bills Harris expects consistent fantasy value from.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Cook was 'bottled up' under 50 scrimmage yards against the Patriots, but Harris explicitly declines to read anything into it — 'it's not like anybody else was any good' in the Buffalo offense that night. He notes the structural ceiling issue instead: when the Bills got to the one down 20-10, [[Ray Davis]] was on the field, but with [[Josh Allen]] chasing another MVP 'they were never handing that ball off' to Davis, Cook or anyone else.
