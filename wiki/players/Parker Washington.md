# Parker Washington

## Expert Takes

- 2024-07-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-04]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said Washington built rapport with [[Trevor Lawrence]] late last year (after Christian Kirk's injury) and is his top target of the young receivers discussed, calling him more ready to play right now than [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and comparing his role to a 'Walmart version' of Robert Woods -- an extension of the run game who wins zone coverage and can become an off-script security blanket for Lawrence. Both hosts said Washington is a contested-catch monster who can win at multiple levels and could realistically be Jacksonville's second- or third-leading target-getter behind [[Christian Kirk]] and [[Evan Engram]], with Waldman floating a long-term Hines Ward-type comp if he keeps developing. Waldman contrasted him with [[Gabriel Davis]], who wins as a scheme-dependent third-level threat but is drop-prone and inconsistent.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): Washington is a reasonable late-round dart throw in 20-round formats and belongs high on a waiver-monitor list through late camp. Waldman's timing argument is the substance: early camp produces highlight athletic plays that inflate everybody, while the later weeks are when offenses test whether receivers can adjust to what quarterbacks want against more complex defenses and game-planned looks — the period when [[Aaron Rodgers]] publicly warned [[Romeo Doubs]] and the 2022 Packers receivers that they would not play if they could not adjust with him. That is when Washington's ability to sync with [[Trevor Lawrence]] should show, and where an injury ahead of him could make him a pre-season waiver add. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) said he holds shares, thought Washington acquitted himself well when thrown into the fray as a rookie, and joked his [Dynasty] shares will still be there in 20 years.
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): with [[Christian Kirk]] done for the year, it could be Washington's season. He has good rapport with [[Trevor Lawrence]], is a good underneath and contested-catch receiver with skills after the catch, and profiles as an aspiring Hines Ward type. Waldman conceded it is partly process of elimination — Devin Duvernay out, [[Gabe Davis]] looking out, [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] with a chest injury — and expects more two-tight-end sets with [[Brenton Strange]] and [[Evan Engram]] while Kirk is out. He still sees a strong chance of good production in the coming weeks, landing as a WR4 or matchup-dependent WR3. Bob Harris (not tracked) said he traded Washington away roughly a week earlier and is not thrilled by the Jaguars' passing attack.
- 2024-10-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-29]]): Washington is the one genuinely interesting name left in Jacksonville, but only from the slot — Harmon says the Jaguars have nobody who can fill even half of [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]'s outside void. As a prospect Harmon liked him a little: reliable, good hands, able to make plays after the catch, and a sensible direct backup to [[Christian Kirk]] on this roster. He had flagged before the deadline that trading Kirk would signal the team believed Washington could play that role; with Kirk now out for the season, he says, 'we're about to find out.'
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]): watch, do not rush to add. Waldman says he recommended Washington eight to ten weeks earlier when [[Christian Kirk]] got hurt, and this six-catch, 100-yard breakout was the first game he was actually worth anything. He wants to see whether Mac Jones — terrible in the two games after [[Trevor Lawrence]] went down, but serviceable and okay in this one — keeps slinging it before committing. He calls Washington not a high priority right now, flags the Jets next and then the Raiders and Titans as the games actually worth targeting, and says a decent showing versus the Jets makes him an add. Bob Harris (not tracked) said he would roster a player after a breakout like that regardless, admitting he is simply greedy that way.
- 2024-12-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-03]]): a split verdict on Parker Washington's 12-target, six-catch, 103-yard, one-touchdown game against Houston — 'I think viable NFL receiver, I don't know if he's a viable NFL starter.' Harmon liked him as a deep-sleeper prospect for his hands and his play after the catch, and says the hands have flashed more in year two, but he would not want to walk into 2025 with Washington as the clear-cut starting slot; he expects Jacksonville to move on from [[Christian Kirk]], keep [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] as the top outside receiver, and for a new coaching staff to add a slot. If it fell to Washington anyway, Harmon said that would not be the worst thing. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) noted the six catches tied a career high and the 103 yards was easily a career best, calling it a spike game.
- 2024-12-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-30]]) [Dynasty]: Washington could well earn a second-contract starting job because he makes a lot of eye-popping plays. Waldman's comp is a potentially better version of [[Jauan Jennings]] — he thinks Washington has a chance to be the better route runner, is as good or better as a contested-catch player, and is at least as good after the catch. The likely path is unglamorous: he gets labeled as the third, fourth or fifth option on Jacksonville's depth chart, and only when the contract expires does another team look at him and decide it needs a slot who can also play flanker. Waldman's projection is a low-end starter for someone on his second deal, framed as a case of a team seeing more in a player than the market does.
- 2025-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-07]]): with [[Christian Kirk]] set to be released, Washington is likely Jacksonville's primary slot in three-receiver sets, and Harmon thinks 'he's got a little something something to him' — a player nobody will discuss when talking about Jaguars receivers but worth keeping an eye on. He is explicit about the ceiling: 'I don't know if he's a future superstar or anything.' Koh points out Washington posted 32 catches for 390 yards and three touchdowns at 12.2 yards per reception, higher than [[Gabe Davis]] managed despite nobody confusing Washington for a big-play receiver. Both hosts agree the Jaguars still need a more reliable second option regardless of how you feel about Washington, and that Jacksonville is a sneaky candidate to add a receiver in free agency or on day two of the draft.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): 'feel it' that Washington can be a real contributor alongside [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] if Jacksonville makes no major additions. The case: he has rapport with [[Trevor Lawrence]], wins contested targets — probably one of his better skills — is good after the catch, and works the underneath zones well. The caveat is that Jacksonville has to add a lot after moving on from [[Evan Engram]], [[Christian Kirk]] and Devin Duvernay, and Waldman expects them to draft both a WR2 type and a tight end from a rich class, so Washington may only take up the slack rather than hold a locked role.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21]]): given a straight choice between Washington and rookie [[Xavier Restrepo]], Waldman 'feels' Washington. His reasoning is that Washington offers more at the catch point, more after the catch, is the better athlete, and can give a team some work outside if needed or as matchups dictate — whereas Restrepo he sees as a lesser Braxton Berrios. Waldman's stance is comparative rather than an endorsement of Washington as a fantasy starter.
- 2025-06-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: an unprompted deep-sleeper flag — Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) teased that Washington is Waldman's secret crush. Waldman said Washington has a little Hines Ward in him, is not super fast, but may be the best contested-catch receiver on the Jaguars and possibly the best in the AFC South outside of [[Nico Collins]]. He also rates him a nice blocker in bunch sets, which matters given Liam Coen's talk of jack-of-all-trades slot roles. The catch is opportunity: Washington has to beat out [[Dyami Brown]] for the third receiver spot, and Waldman conceded it is 'probably nada' — but he will be high on Waldman's waiver-wire list, and he floated Washington and Brown combining for 150 targets.
- 2025-07-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-28]]): 'feel it' — Washington remains an underrated sleeper, and Waldman notes the coaching staff has shifted to talking him up after the offseason hype went to [[Dyami Brown]]. With two vertical stretchers already on the roster, Waldman argues Jacksonville needs an underneath receiver who makes tough catches and gets tough yardage after them, can occasionally split the middle and win contested plays — and he grades Washington above Brown as a contested catcher, as a runner after contact and as a zone route runner. Bob Harris (untracked) adds that Brown is better known for his spring work than his summer and fall work, and that Washington is worth watching but requires going pretty deep in a normal redraft league.
- 2025-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-01]]): Harmon agrees with James Koh's read that Washington is essentially a full-time slot player and says he likes him in that role. The value is structural rather than individual — because Washington covers the slot and [[Dyami Brown]] covers sacrificial X, Jacksonville is not forced to lean on Brown as an every-down player and can rotate [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and [[Travis Hunter]] through all three receiver alignments. Harmon specifically notes that Brown and Washington being very different types is a big part of why the depth works, and that a competent third and fourth receiver is what makes Hunter's two-way training-camp usage viable. Harmon does wonder aloud whether all these players can actually get reps and who ends up as the fourth receiver.
- 2025-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-25]]): Washington drew 11 targets in Week 3 and has played more offensive snaps than [[Travis Hunter]], because Liam Coen's stated policy is that a receiver injury is absorbed by the next receiver rather than by moving Hunter around — so [[Dyami Brown]] going down promoted Washington. The detail Harmon says he had not realized is that Washington has lined up outside on 70% of his snaps despite being an essentially slot-only player last year and profiling best there, and Coen's comments imply he keeps playing outside. Harmon likes him — 'a nice little player,' pretty good — but frames him alongside the traded-for Tim Patrick as a role player holding down the other spots while Hunter masters the F position, rather than as a target earner in his own right. Koh (not tracked) was the one pressing on whether the volume bump is buyable.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-02]]): Washington is 'my jam' and, with [[Brenton Strange]], one of Jacksonville's best players after [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] — but the hosts agreed he and Strange are staying-power role players rather than stars ('those two don't sniff Hunter'). Waldman's comps: Washington in the 1990s Bears would be Marty Booker or slightly better; in Pittsburgh, a middle-class Hines Ward. He is a good slot who could play flanker in an offense designed to feed the flanker, with run-after-catch, space and contested end-zone value — a Doug Baldwin-with-Russell Wilson archetype who could post a 1,000-1,100-yard season with a veteran quarterback like [[Aaron Rodgers]] or [[Matthew Stafford]] who builds rapport. With [[Trevor Lawrence]] and this design, that upside is not accessible.
- 2025-11-04 — According to Jeff Erickson of RotoWire, a guest and not a tracked expert ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-04]]): Washington is his number one Week 10 waiver add, picked up as soon as [[Travis Hunter]] was hurt on Friday, with [[Dyami Brown]] and [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] also going down in that game suggesting his usage goes through the roof. Erickson is under no misconception that he is a game breaker and thinks [[Tory Horton]] is the better and more dynamic route runner — he just thinks he can actually use Washington this week. [[Chris Harris]] left him off entirely and may regret it: he calls Washington a bog-average guy, nothing special, but concedes the balance of ability against opportunity, and that any receiver with quote-unquote guaranteed double-digit targets belongs in your lineup — the difficulty being you cannot make that guarantee until everyone else is confirmed out. Both flag the trip to Houston as a real problem.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-04]]): Washington is a legit player who has been overstretched this season into a role that does not fit — Harmon cites games with a 20-yard average depth of target as too much for him — but points back to his own pre-draft write-up, where he liked Washington as a utility slot player. With [[Travis Hunter]] on IR and [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] unable to work the middle in his current alignment while Dyami Brown (concussion) is more of a vertical player, Harmon expects Washington to be Jacksonville's most-used slot receiver going forward and thinks he can at least pick up the slack.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06]]): both call him a buy. Waldman reads Liam Coen as having wanted tall, fast, quick-twitch field-stretchers and having only begrudgingly accepted Washington after injuries — but Washington had a great camp, made plays on special teams and returns, and is the one receiver with a consistent rapport with [[Trevor Lawrence]], whose accuracy misses Washington's catch radius and toughness erase. Waldman comps him to Hines Ward: not a great route runner, but a tough underneath zone player who runs after the catch and runs routes just good enough, currently filling tight end, flanker and slot roles at once. Angelo says the market is suppressed on him because people assume [[Jakobi Meyers]] takes the slot, but Coen said publicly Meyers will work the boundary — so Washington should play good ball down the stretch. The caveat is what happens to the pecking order when [[Travis Hunter]] and [[Brenton Strange]] return.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-06]]): Washington's breakout game came because he finally played the slot for the first time this season, at 60% of his passing snaps inside, which Harmon says matches his prospect profile as a 5-10, 200-pound receiver who 'should be a slot receiver.' Liam Coen said after the [[Jakobi Meyers]] trade that Washington stays in the slot and Meyers starts outside, so Harmon reads the acquisition as not displacing him — the incumbent already knows the motion-heavy F role that takes time to learn. Harmon frames that continuity as the reason the Jaguars did not simply install their new veteran inside where he had been playing over 50% slot in Las Vegas.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-06]]): Harris is unimpressed with the player but respects the role — 'I don't really know what he is but he seems like just another guy to this point in his career' — while conceding there is 'zero question' [[Jakobi Meyers]] is the better football player. For Week 10 only, Harris still ranks Washington one spot AHEAD of Meyers by the powers of deduction: with [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and [[Travis Hunter]] out, Washington is the guy already able to catch a bunch of five-yard passes in [[Trevor Lawrence]]'s offense. Guest Ben Gretch (Stealing Signals, not tracked) says Jacksonville has been 'really using a lot of' Washington and finds him interesting, but thinks Meyers's skill set is better and that he may get up to speed quickly. In the DFS segment Harris flagged Washington at $4,700 against Houston as a clever cheap play.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-13]]): a long-time favourite he never thought was a great player but always saw as scrappy enough to climb into a role — which is exactly what has happened over the past few weeks. Waldman comps him to a more modern-day Hines Ward: physical for his size, a good contested-catch option who runs after the catch, with good rapport with [[Trevor Lawrence]]. The argument is scheme-driven: with Lawrence's vertical limitations, Jacksonville has to live underneath and in the intermediate middle, which is Washington's territory, and Waldman expects red-zone value too because Chargers linebackers blow assignments and get distracted. [[Chris Harris]] agrees, noting Washington returned a punt for a touchdown last week and reportedly tweaked a hamstring but finished the game and Harris suspects it is soreness rather than a pull. Harris ranked Washington ahead of [[Jakobi Meyers]] and did not rank [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] at all; both agree the slot is the safest role in that offense, and Waldman still takes Washington over [[Michael Wilson]] and even if Brian Thomas is healthy.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-13]]): Washington made the rare Jacksonville play worth pointing at, a sweet catch on Derek Stingley Jr., and Harmon says he likes the player but thinks he has been miscast — used as a vertical outside option because [[Travis Hunter]] was being worked at the interior F spot between slot and flanker, while the staff did not want [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] locked in as a pure X. Harmon calls the whole Jacksonville receiver room bizarre in construction this year. James Koh agrees Washington is a nice player, and Harmon adds the caveat that he should probably be a third or rotational receiver on a good offense rather than a mainstay, which is part of why he is unsure who Jacksonville's building-block skill players actually are.
