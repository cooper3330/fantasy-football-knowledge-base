# Rome Odunze

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. Team/role are as of that
> date and are almost certainly stale — treat as historical pre-draft
> scouting context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): an early (3-game, explicitly non-final) Reception Perception look, and the biggest surprise of the trio charted alongside [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] and [[Malik Nabers]] — "I don't think I have a weakness for him through two, three games." Estimated 6'3"/215 lb. Elite off the line against heavy press coverage, a "smooth separator," and — despite a reputation for winning a lot of contested catches — no real separation concerns on tape, which Harmon attributes to quarterback Michael Penix Jr. trusting him in contested spots rather than to a lack of true separation. Plays all three receiver alignments (X/flanker/slot) plus full-speed pre-snap motion. NFL Draft analyst Daniel Jeremiah (not a tracked expert) independently gave Odunze the same grade as Harrison Jr., which Harmon says he "can see why" after charting him himself. Not yet ranked among the three prospects by either host — "I don't know if I will have him ranked number one... but I see why he would be" — but all three are treated as tier-one, top-10-worthy prospects if the early numbers hold. See [[Reception Perception Methodology]].
- 2024-02-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-27]]): grouped with [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] and [[Malik Nabers]] as one of three receivers earning top-10 rankings this class; Harmon declines to rank the trio 1-2-3, expecting landing spot to matter more than the pre-draft order among them — see [[Dynasty]].

- 2024-02-29 — According to **Kevin Knight** (Falcons reporter, SB Nation's Falcoholic — guest, not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]): says Atlanta would be very tempted to take him (or [[Malik Nabers]]) at 8th overall if he falls, given the Falcons' near-empty receiver room outside [[Drake London]]. Notes Mel Kiper's mock had Odunze going 6th to the Giants at the time, but flags a real chance he slides to Atlanta at 8.

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]) [Dynasty]: same landing-spot risk framing as [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] and [[Malik Nabers]] — flagged as a likely fit for critical-need top-10 teams (Tennessee, Giants, New England), with the same dynasty caution that a weak offensive landing spot could suppress his rookie-year outlook regardless of pre-draft grade.

- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): when he gets jammed at the line, he's shown he can counter quickly enough to still get into his route. He'll lose some interactions like every receiver does, but the ability to win a route even after the defender earned an early advantage is something a lot of young and veteran receivers alike don't do well — a special quality.

- 2024-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-26]]) [Dynasty]: full profile now live, and the episode's headline take — argues Odunze should be viewed as closer to WR1 than the public-consensus WR3, "not even close" to WR4. Took 84% of sampled snaps outside, on the line of scrimmage 67.4% of the time; posted an above-average success rate on every charted route type, a "full green" route tree Harmon had previously only seen approached by [[Chris Olave]] and [[Garrett Wilson]]. Success rates: 76.2% (87th percentile) vs. man, 92nd percentile vs. press, 83.5% vs. zone. Zero drops over the 8-game sample, 88.9% contested-catch rate — attributes the high contested-target volume to quarterback [[Michael Penix Jr.]] trusting him in tight windows rather than an inability to separate, the same pattern he cites for [[Terry McLaurin]] and [[Drake London]], contrasted with a genuine catch-point mess like [[Quentin Johnston]]. Player comp: [[Davante Adams]] — says "probably my number one-ranked receiver of the last four draft classes" is a real possibility. Notes Daniel Jeremiah and Nate Tice (not tracked) also have him WR2 on film, while JJ Zachariason's (not tracked) statistical model has him WR3; pushes back on "dynasty community" chatter tiering [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]/[[Malik Nabers]] above him, arguing all three belong in the same tier. Flags the Chargers, Giants, Falcons, Bears and Jets (picks 5-10) as plausible landing spots, with Tennessee likely out after trading for [[Calvin Ridley]].

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]), discussing the Chargers' No. 5 pick: while Angelo grades [[Malik Nabers]] as his highest-graded receiver in the cycle overall, he thinks Odunze specifically fits [[Justin Herbert]] "like a glove" as a true three-level X, since Herbert has never had a consistent X-receiver option ([[Mike Williams]] often injured and more of a one-level player).

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): continued
  momentum toward Odunze as the class's WR1 — cites PFF-adjacent explosive-play
  data (analysts Arjun Menon and John Kosco, not tracked): over the last two
  college seasons Odunze posted 72 explosive (15+ yard) plays at a 43.1% rate —
  more than both [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] (68 plays, 47.2%) and [[Malik Nabers]]
  (66 plays, 40.9%) in raw count, ahead of Troy Franklin (not a tracked page,
  42.3%). Long-term prospect comparison vs. [[Drake London]]: while London "is
  who he's been" as a proven NFL player, the hosts say Odunze's prospect
  profile grades slightly better and give him the "easy" edge on generating
  explosive downfield plays specifically — the trait Harmon calls "what makes
  special players special." On landing spot: for the Bears at 9, would slot in
  as a true X alongside [[D.J. Moore]] and [[Keenan Allen]], immediately making
  Chicago's a top-tier receiver room; for the Falcons, projects an "all in"
  scenario pairing him with [[Drake London]] and [[Kirk Cousins]].


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]), reacting to Odunze visiting the Cardinals: likes the idea that Arizona could move down and back up to land Odunze instead of [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] — sees Odunze as more of a complete, Davante-Adams-type receiver who can play all three alignments, while grading Harrison as closer to the DeAndre Hopkins/Larry Fitzgerald archetype (great at what he does but a more limited overall skill set given his lack of great top-end speed).

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on *Reception Perception: The Show* ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-11]]), pushing back on co-host James Koh's Big 12-discount framing for [[Adonai Mitchell]]: says he pushed back on similar "discount his success rate because he played in the Pac-12" chatter around Odunze, calling it "jumping the shark" — distinct from his genuine Big 12-specific skepticism about Texas/TCU/Oklahoma-style offenses.
- 2024-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: in a hypothetical Bills trade-up scenario, gives [[Rome Odunze]] a base-case comp of Allen Robinson and an aggressive-case comp of Davante Adams, calling him a potential generational X-receiver worth surrendering multiple future first-round picks for.
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]) [Dynasty]: unequivocally named Rome Odunze the class's best contested-catch winner, citing a class-high 88.9% contested-catch rate on a 23.4% contested-target share -- "first by a lot" -- and said it would be "irresponsible" to give the award to anyone else. Also cited Odunze as number one in success rate vs. man and press with a fully green route tree, making him a strong candidate for best overall route runner too. After re-listening to a Kevin Clark interview with Odunze, Harmon was struck that Odunze credited timing, late hands and deceptive hand placement -- not just size/strength -- for winning 50/50 balls, which Harmon says signals elite technical mastery and makes him a near-certain hit as a contested-catch weapon at the next level. [[Ja'Lynn Polk]], his college teammate, finished second in class contested-catch rate.
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: argued that while Marvin Harrison Jr. has the best immediate 2024 opportunity of the top-3 rookie WR class, Odunze may have the better three-year outlook in Chicago because he inherits better receivers to learn from and a more efficient early offense, plus what Waldman sees as a better long-term quarterback in Caleb Williams if Williams develops as expected. He cautioned Odunze may be 'a lower-volume, high-efficiency guy' in year one, meaning Malik Nabers could outproduce him in 2024 even with a worse quarterback situation.
- 2024-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-30]]): Odunze was Harmon's second-best receiver in the 2024 class, a player with no true weakness who can line up at all three receiver spots. Harmon expects Odunze to play primarily as a true vertical X for Chicago -- more so than [[DJ Moore]], who Harmon thinks profiles as a pre-snap movable weapon -- while [[Keenan Allen]] plays a reduced 25% of his snaps outside as he ages, pushing both Moore and Odunze inside on obvious mismatches. Co-host James Koh (untracked) notes Chicago was fifth-fewest outside WR receptions/10th-fewest yards in 2023 despite bottom-10 attempts, so the addition slots into real need. Both hosts call the Odunze/Moore/Allen trio a top-five, arguably top-three receiving corps in the league on paper for [[Caleb Williams]], who reportedly pushed the front office to draft him.
- 2024-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (untracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-07]]) [Best Ball]: both hosts say they would leave Vegas's 675.5-yard line alone -- Koh's own median projection (50 rec, 654 yards, 5 TDs) lands almost exactly on it, and his high case is only 1,040 yards/8 TDs. They call it fantasy-wise 'one of the worst spots he could have landed,' even while liking the real-life fit. Koh projects only 310 total targets to Chicago WRs in 2024 (up from a league-bottom-two 259 last year under Justin Fields, but still below league average), and both flag a crowded room (DJ Moore, Keenan Allen, Odunze all rostered in early ADP) plus a run-heavy identity under Matt Eberflus (DeAndre Swift, Khalil Herbert, Roschon Johnson) and a much-improved defense that could suppress game-script passing volume. Harmon also notes OC Shane Waldron's stock dipped after a rough 2023 in Seattle, adding QB-development risk on top of the target competition.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman still slots Rome Odunze as a potential low-end WR1 or WR3 in Chicago, but pushed back on Bob Harris's (untracked, RSP Cast co-host) assumption that new OC Shane Waldron's Seattle pedigree means Odunze inherits a Jaxon Smith-Njigba-style slot role. Waldman argues Odunze is naturally an outside receiver while Njigba is a slot player who can play outside, and that Waldron's slot-driven Rams-style scheme was constrained in Seattle by Pete Carroll's run-first philosophy and by losing both starting tackles, which forced more conservative, tight-end-heavy protection looks. With Waldron now running his own offense in Chicago, Waldman expects a 'truer' version of that scheme to emerge, but still projects Odunze as an outside X-type target rather than a Njigba replica.
- 2024-05-14 — According to Adam Rank ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-14]]), Chicago Bears fan and NFL Network personality, not a tracked expert: despite [[Rome Odunze]] not having played a single NFL snap, 'it drips off the film' that he's a baller — Rank says his tape grades 'all green, no red' across levels. Rank ranks the Bears' new receiver room of Odunze, [[DJ Moore]] and [[Keenan Allen]] among the three best trios in the NFL, citing positional versatility: Allen is aging and limited mostly to the slot, Moore can play some X, and Odunze projects to handle the bulk of the X-receiver snaps, giving Chicago answers on short, intermediate and deep routes. Caveat acknowledged by both Rank and host James Koh: this is pure pre-snap film projection with zero regular-season NFL data behind it yet.
