# Michael Pittman Jr.

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024 — treat as historical context until
> superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]) [Best Ball]: calls him the top free-agent wide receiver value if he remains a Colt, currently going in the late third round of early best ball drafts at roughly the same price as [[Mike Evans]], "maybe a little before." Argues that could actually be better value than Evans because of [[Anthony Richardson]]'s breakout upside — "if he's healthy enough, [Richardson] could very well make a big leap next year," which would lift Pittman's per-target value. Says watching Pittman play through 2023 "was phenomenal" even with Gardner Minshew at quarterback, and expects the Colts' offensive future — tied to Richardson's development — to be bright.

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): sorts the Colts into the "substantial need" wide receiver bucket, arguing they still need a receiver who can win on the outside against press coverage — something neither Pittman ("a big receiver" but "not really a downfield burner") nor Alec Pierce (not a tracked page) has consistently provided. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) disagreed, arguing the Colts' receiver room is fine "in totality" given the group's youth and developmental trajectory.

- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]) [Best Ball]: with his new deal finalized (three years, reported $71.5M with $46M guaranteed), grades him "probably a low-end wide receiver one" playing with [[Anthony Richardson]] — a contested-catch, strong over-the-middle receiver who gives Richardson "just enough dimensions" to target often, with the added note that Richardson's own superstar breakout "could happen as early as this year."

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: reacting to his finalized three-year, ~$70M deal ($46M guaranteed) — calls it a team-friendly "good deal" for the Colts, pricing him in the [[Terry McLaurin]]/[[Deebo Samuel]]/DK Metcalf (not tracked) contract tier rather than the A.J. Brown/Cooper Kupp/Stefon Diggs tier. States plainly "I do think Michael Pittman is a true one" — a legitimate No. 1 receiver and "the most underrated receiver in the NFL" the last few years — comparing his usage profile to a "Bud Light version" of peak Michael Thomas (not tracked) with the Saints: 61.4% of his 2023 charted routes were slants/curls/digs, with a 74.3%/74.2%/74.8% success rate vs. man coverage across three straight seasons and over 80% vs. both zone and press in each of those years. Attributes his lack of explosive/downfield plays to a QB carousel — Matt Ryan, Carson Wentz, Gardner Minshew, [[Anthony Richardson]], a different starting quarterback every year of his career — rather than a talent limitation, and says "don't be surprised" if Pittman, entering his age-27 season, has a "banner year" in 2024 that puts him in the top-10 wide receiver conversation. See [[Reception Perception Methodology]].
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon cites Pittman as the clearest example of coaching deployment being the tiebreaker for a receiver's season -- Shane Steichen maximized him in Indianapolis even through inconsistent quarterback play from Gardner Minshew and Anthony Richardson, illustrating how a well-designed role can outweigh volatile QB circumstances.
- 2024-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-11]]) [Dynasty]: Pittman gets 'trashed in some circles' over low yards-per-target but Harmon calls him 'a darn good player,' citing a consistent success-rate climb after his rookie year -- 71.6% vs. man in 2020, then 74.3%, 74.2% and 74.8% across the next three seasons, plus 80.6% vs. press and 80.8% vs. zone in 2023. Harmon calls him 'good enough to be a number one anywhere,' despite years of bad quarterback play (Matt Ryan's arm 'completely shot,' Philip Rivers with 'nothing in the tank' in Indianapolis), and is bullish that better play under [[Anthony Richardson]] finally lets Pittman win more of the vertical, non-go-route shots -- corner and post routes -- he's already shown he can win. James Koh (untracked) agreed, comparing his route tree (slant/curl/dig-heavy) to Michael Thomas's and calling him a locked-in number one receiver.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Harmon has always been higher on Pittman than national consensus and still ranks him in his tier three of receivers, though as the lowest-ranked player in that tier, and he has ended up drafting him a lot because the market's shared hesitancy lets him fall. His supporting evidence: across the two games [[Anthony Richardson]] started and finished last year Pittman was still far and away the most targeted player at roughly 32-33% first-read target share, and 35% across the full season, so it was not merely [[Gardner Minshew]] feeding him — the RPO nature of the offense means the first read gets the ball. Koh (not tracked) is more skeptical, saying he was all-in on Pittman at the start of the process but now expects the gulf between the Colts' first, second, third and fourth receivers to be much narrower than he anticipated.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): co-host James Koh (not tracked) notes Pittman tied [[Alec Pierce]] for the team lead with 18 routes run and drew eight targets on those routes — good volume — but converted only four for 31 yards, and expects better days ahead. Harmon uses Pittman's 2023 usage as the contrast case for Washington: in the games [[Anthony Richardson]] started as a rookie, Pittman was the first-read target over and over, at a 32–35% first-read target share, which is what a team invested in its young quarterback looks like. In Week 1 2024 no Colt ran more than 33.3% of his routes from the slot, with Pittman, [[Adonai Mitchell]] and Pierce rotating there until [[Josh Downs]] returns.
- 2024-09-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-17]]): Pittman was not a clear-cut WR2 for Harmon coming into the year — he had him on the fringes, at the very back end of WR2 territory — and he is lowering expectations for every Indianapolis receiver now. He expects Pittman to remain the leading target-getter and the leader in routes run, and to have his days, but not to put together a consistently productive season, because [[Anthony Richardson]] is squarely in a developmental stage and the season is about developing him. Harmon is explicit that he likes the player and that the room is a fantastic, well-constructed one; Pittman's job in his projected split is anything and everything intermediate, with [[Josh Downs]] underneath and [[Alec Pierce]] over the top. The arrival of Pierce as a genuine fourth playable option lowers the production floor for everyone in the room.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Harmon expects Pittman to go down as a disappointing fantasy pick this year, for two reasons — [[Anthony Richardson]]'s development means the passing game will not be consistent or clean, and the receiver room is crowded with [[Alec Pierce]], [[Adonai Mitchell]] and [[Josh Downs]] when healthy. He says he had Pittman as the last player in his third tier of fantasy receivers and did not draft much of him, while stressing he is not worried about Pittman as an individual talent and that you cannot take him off the field. Koh (not tracked) says his own process — that a developmental young quarterback locks onto whoever is open most — was right but he picked the wrong player, failing to account for Richardson's willingness to lock onto Pierce downfield.
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]): Pittman was rumored to be headed for IR and then played anyway, but 'he did not look well' — he played well without looking like a player whose body will hold up long term if the team keeps deploying him this way. Waldman's expectation is that [[Alec Pierce]] ends up absorbing the Pittman role, which he flags as potentially a significant change in the Indianapolis receiver hierarchy.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31]]): Pittman should not be on the field. Angelo says the back injury had him headed for IR two weeks earlier and that what is playing on Sundays is 'a non-starting caliber player' — he cannot come back to the football, cannot keep his feet in a route, and looks like he is running in mud; Angelo notes broadcast analysts (Chase Daniel or Dan Orlovsky) made the same point that number 11 should not be playing. Waldman ties it to the win-now mandate: a team chasing a weak AFC South is pushing a hurt X receiver onto the field and then blaming the rookie quarterback for the passing game. Both frame Indianapolis as a 12-personnel team with no true X, leaving [[Josh Downs]] (a good separator, but a slot) as the only reliable down-to-down option.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-31]]): Harmon gives Pittman 'a ton of credit for being tough and gutting it through this back injury' but says plainly that he is not the same player as last year or even earlier this season — he was reportedly about to go on IR before suddenly starting, and 'you don't go from about to be on IR to 100 percent like that.' He put a rough Week 8 performance partly down to the injury. Harmon also identified a scheme mismatch independent of health: because [[Anthony Richardson]] cannot be run as often as the design wants, the offense leaned into deep shots rather than the quick-hitting game, and that does not suit Pittman. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) pushed back hard on the takes blaming Pittman for Richardson's struggles, calling it shocking.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): Pittman should not be on the field at all right now — Waldman explicitly endorses [[Brandon Angelo]]'s point from the prior week that the injury makes him unplayable, and attributes his continued usage to Jim Irsay's win-now pressure forcing or heavily influencing him to stay out there. The output backed it up: one catch for 14 yards against Minnesota. Waldman frames this as an ownership problem rather than a talent problem, of a piece with the [[Anthony Richardson]] benching — the organization is spending down assets to chase immediate wins. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) noted the collective expectation that [[Joe Flacco]] would cure all the ills of the Colts receivers did not materialize for anyone.
- 2025-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-07]]): Pittman is 'really good — a guy I think you can get away with as your number one receiver', but like [[Calvin Ridley]] he is an ideal-world number two. He finished with 69 catches, behind [[Josh Downs]]'s 72. Harmon says Pittman played through injuries in 2024 and did not play his best football because of it, is a Chris Ballard 'drafted, developed, rewarded with the contract' locker-room favorite, and carries the biggest cap figure in a room with three players behind him who deserve snaps — so a trade is 'probably not likely but not completely off the table'. Koh pushes back on moving him: there are not many 6-4 receivers with that reliability, hands, versatility inside and out, and homegrown standing, and calls it a tough sell — he would rather Indianapolis make a much lesser deal moving [[Alec Pierce]].
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]): Pittman is 'good — probably a low-end one, ideally a number two'. Harmon's larger complaint is deployment, not talent: Indianapolis in 2024 asked him to run go routes on the boundary in a long-developing passing game, and 'what about Michael Pittman's game has ever said that he could do that?' He would rather see the quick-game, RPO-heavy scheme Steichen ran with Gardner Minshew. Koh notes Harmon has been above consensus on Pittman for years, and Harmon does not walk that back — he is skeptical of the Colts operation generally, less so of the pass catchers.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]) [Best Ball]: Pittman going as WR53, behind [[Rashid Shaheed]], is an absolute 'feel it' — Waldman is taking that whether the quarterback ends up being [[Daniel Jones]] or [[Anthony Richardson]], and he thinks it will be Richardson. His central argument is that Pittman played 2024 through a back injury he probably should not have played through, at one point unable to reach up and catch balls cleanly, so he has an excuse none of the other receivers in that ADP range have. Bob Harris (not tracked) recalls Pittman was expected to land on IR early with broken bones in his back and instead played all year. Waldman also expects the Colts offense overall to be more conducive to fantasy production.
- 2025-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-04]]): Pittman is Harmon's deployment blueprint for big receivers who cannot consistently beat man or press — an outside receiver who is used a lot off the line of scrimmage, in bunches and condensed sets, freed up on crossers, digs and slants rather than being parked as the isolated boundary X. He names Pittman as the comp for [[Jayden Higgins]] if Higgins is going to work as an outside receiver, with caveats: Higgins is probably faster in a straight line, while Pittman is far more fluid and a better separator on slants and digs. Harmon also calls [[Tetairoa McMillan]] a 'souped-up Michael Pittman', making Pittman the archetype anchor for this whole class of big-bodied wideouts.
- 2025-06-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-06]]) in an over/under game: he takes the under 'across the board' — under 80 receptions, under 950 yards, under 5.5 touchdowns. He stresses he is a big Pittman fan, 'a great number two receiver' who has been a fringe one at times, and that 2024 (111 targets, 69-808-3 in 16 games) was a down year played through a back injury. The bearish case is environment, not player: Harmon straight up thinks [[Josh Downs]] is the better player and would be the favorite to lead the team in catches and yards, and he can't imagine two Colts receivers clearing 900 with [[Alec Pierce]], [[Adonai Mitchell]] and first-round tight end [[Tyler Warren]] also needing activation under an unsettled quarterback. Koh (not tracked) countered with Pittman's volume history — 88, 99 and 109 catches from 2021-23 with 1,150 yards in 2023 — noting he has delivered whenever the volume was there. Harmon also suspects this is the last hurrah for the room, with Pierce expiring and Pittman a possible contract out.
- 2025-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-13]]): Pittman has caught 'a little bit too much flack' inside the [[Josh Downs]] hype — Harmon calls him a rock-solid pro and a player Reception Perception has consistently liked, noting he played 2024 through a back injury. Used mostly as a flanker, but Harmon says Pittman has shown enough ability to beat press man that if it were his call he would put Pittman at X in two-receiver sets with Downs as the flanker, rather than the alignment the Colts seem to prefer. In 11 personnel Harmon expects [[Alec Pierce]] at X, Pittman at flanker and Downs in the slot. He also rates Pittman a decent run blocker. With [[Daniel Jones]] expected to start and more RPOs and quick game likely, Harmon sees the offense leaning on Pittman and Downs.
- 2025-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-01]]): Pittman is a rock-solid number two you can get away with starting as your number one, and Harmon does not want people forgetting about him after a down 2024 that he attributes to the back injury Pittman gutted through as a team leader. From 2021 to 2023 Pittman cleared 74% success versus man in three straight seasons, posted 83.7%, 82.5% and 80.8% versus zone, and was over 80% versus press all three years. He is not a full-field vertical player the way [[Nico Collins]] is, but has good hands in contested situations and excellent short-area route work — Harmon offers a 'poor man's [[Michael Thomas]]' or poor man's [[Keenan Allen]] comp, bigger receivers who separate underneath. Co-host James Koh goes further, calling Pittman the guy who stirs the drink in Indianapolis on size and inside-outside versatility, and says the group gets thin fast without him. Both agree the ceiling is gated by quarterback play.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]): asked if Pittman is 'way overrated,' Waldman says overrated, yes. He goes back to USC, where he saw a limited route runner featured on crossings, hooks and go-up-and-get-it verticals; Pittman is good after the catch and does those things well, but Waldman does not consider him a true WR1 and says Indianapolis has stuck its quarterbacks with a group lacking a singular primary ball winner. His comp: a nice [[Tee Higgins]] type, and he does not even think Pittman is as good as Higgins — certainly not a [[Davante Adams]]-style primary. Context: the Colts' depth chart released that Monday listed [[Daniel Jones]] 'or' [[Anthony Richardson]] at quarterback. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) declines to argue, saying the Colts are full of players who fall just short of expectations and that aside from a healthy [[Jonathan Taylor]] nobody on the offense disappoints less often than not.
- 2025-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Pittman is the number 9 flag player and a post-hype sleeper — a good player 'caught in a parade of stink' at quarterback his whole career, with the [[Anthony Richardson]] / [[Daniel Jones]] question fully baked into a roughly 10th-round ADP that Harris calls 'punishment beyond punishment.' He points out [[Jerry Jeudy]] goes more than four rounds earlier with arguably a worse quarterback room, and that stripping out blowout [[Jameis Winston]] games leaves Jeudy at WR57 as of October 26 last year. Harris frames Pittman as a rock-solid big-bodied possession receiver who can win down the field despite no separation speed, citing a road-game-winning catch in Baltimore, and a tough leader who played through a back injury. Risk: a wasted bench pick cut by the byes. Guest Sigmund Bloom (Footballguys, not tracked) added that Pittman would be a third- or fourth-round pick in a different offense.
- 2025-09-03 — According to James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]] [Redraft (Standard)]: bold prediction that Pittman sets a career high with 1,200+ receiving yards, because [[Daniel Jones]] is sadly the most functional quarterback of his career after dead-armed Philip Rivers and Matt Ryan and Gardner Minshew — with the caveat that the 2024 back injury has to hold up. [[Matt Harmon]] backed the player: from 2021-2023 Pittman charted 74.3%, 74.2% and 74.8% success versus man and over 80% versus zone and press in all three, comparable to Keenan Allen or Michael Thomas at times, and he was seventh in the NFL in first-read target share in 2023. Harmon's condition is that you must be out on [[Tyler Warren]], [[Josh Downs]], [[Alec Pierce]] and [[Adonai Mitchell]] — which Koh accepted. Harmon added there is real contingent value here if Downs gets hurt, and framed it as the eye of Sauron moving past proven good players toward the shiny new thing.
