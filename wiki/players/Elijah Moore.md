# Elijah Moore

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): part of
  Cleveland's run of cheap "distressed asset" wide receiver trades over three
  offseasons (with [[Amari Cooper]] and [[Jerry Jeudy]]) — total compensation
  for all three across those trades was just two fifth-round picks, a
  sixth-round pick, and a pick swap. Harmon: "a good player," and beats press
  and zone coverage better than Jerry Jeudy does, but not on his own a
  reason to skip adding more receiver help — "not a guy that's just like, oh
  yeah, we got Elijah Moore, we don't have to touch the receiver." Ideally
  played off the ball / in the slot given his size, though he has X-receiver
  reps from his rookie season with the Jets. Projected behind [[Amari Cooper]]
  in the pecking order — "probably more of a three than a two" — with the
  whole receiver room's outlook tied to whether [[Deshaun Watson]] plays like
  he did in 2023.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: names Moore (with [[Cedric Tillman]]) as one of the two in-house options who could push [[Jerry Jeudy]] down to a clear third receiving option in Cleveland.

- 2024-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-21]]): amid reports that Moore could play outside this season while [[Jerry Jeudy]] moves to the slot, Harmon says he'd "much rather see Elijah Moore run real receiver routes" outside — arguing the better player of the two should get the more valuable X/flanker role, not Jeudy, since Jeudy is a weak zone-coverage receiver ill-suited to the slot. Calls Moore "a little bit of an enigma" and reiterates skepticism of Cleveland's "distressed asset" wide receiver strategy — beyond [[Amari Cooper]] and [[David Njoku]], "not a lot of reliability in this offense," with Moore, Jeudy and [[Cedric Tillman]] needing to be supporting characters rather than the offense's identity. Notes Moore previously "fell out of favor" with the Jets by "doing some stupid stuff."
- 2024-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-23]]): Moore is 'a number two at best' but a viable one, and Harmon insists his lack of targets is not a separation problem — his charting shows real route running (2021: 70% vs man, 80% vs zone; 2022: 72% vs man, 77% vs zone, 73% vs press). 2023 with Cleveland: career-high 59 catches on 104 targets for 640 yards, two touchdowns, 56.7% catch rate. Harmon blames three things: Moore damaging his own standing by tweeting for a trade during a five-game Jets win streak ('when you're Elijah Moore, pipe down' — he compares the self-inflicted career damage to Anthony Miller); Cleveland miscasting him early last season as a gadget/backfield touch player when he was always a pure vertical and mid-range slot, never a create-a-touch type; and bad quarterback play, since you are lucky to get one productive receiver out of a [[Deshaun Watson]] or [[Zach Wilson]] offense. Harmon notes his best stretch came with Joe Flacco's vertical passing (12 targets vs the Rams) before a scary concussion ended his season. James Koh (not tracked) was more forgiving on the Jets complaints given Wilson's quality, and rated Moore ahead of [[Jerry Jeudy]] on film, tape and production.
