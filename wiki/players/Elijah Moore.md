# Elijah Moore

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): part of
  Cleveland's run of cheap "distressed asset" wide receiver trades over three
  offseasons (with [[Amari Cooper]] and [[Jerry Jeudy]]) — total compensation
  for all three across those trades was just two fifth-round picks, a
  sixth-round pick, and a pick swap. Harmon: "a good player," and beats press
  and zone coverage better than Jerry Jeudy does, but not on his own a
  reason to skip adding more receiver help — "not a guy that's just like, oh
  yeah, we got Elijah Moore, we don't have to touch the receiver." Ideally
  played off the ball / in the slot given his size, though he has X-receiver
  reps from his rookie season with the Jets. Projected behind [[Amari Cooper]]
  in the pecking order — "probably more of a three than a two" — with the
  whole receiver room's outlook tied to whether [[Deshaun Watson]] plays like
  he did in 2023.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: names Moore (with [[Cedric Tillman]]) as one of the two in-house options who could push [[Jerry Jeudy]] down to a clear third receiving option in Cleveland.

- 2024-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-21]]): amid reports that Moore could play outside this season while [[Jerry Jeudy]] moves to the slot, Harmon says he'd "much rather see Elijah Moore run real receiver routes" outside — arguing the better player of the two should get the more valuable X/flanker role, not Jeudy, since Jeudy is a weak zone-coverage receiver ill-suited to the slot. Calls Moore "a little bit of an enigma" and reiterates skepticism of Cleveland's "distressed asset" wide receiver strategy — beyond [[Amari Cooper]] and [[David Njoku]], "not a lot of reliability in this offense," with Moore, Jeudy and [[Cedric Tillman]] needing to be supporting characters rather than the offense's identity. Notes Moore previously "fell out of favor" with the Jets by "doing some stupid stuff."
- 2024-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-23]]): Moore is 'a number two at best' but a viable one, and Harmon insists his lack of targets is not a separation problem — his charting shows real route running (2021: 70% vs man, 80% vs zone; 2022: 72% vs man, 77% vs zone, 73% vs press). 2023 with Cleveland: career-high 59 catches on 104 targets for 640 yards, two touchdowns, 56.7% catch rate. Harmon blames three things: Moore damaging his own standing by tweeting for a trade during a five-game Jets win streak ('when you're Elijah Moore, pipe down' — he compares the self-inflicted career damage to Anthony Miller); Cleveland miscasting him early last season as a gadget/backfield touch player when he was always a pure vertical and mid-range slot, never a create-a-touch type; and bad quarterback play, since you are lucky to get one productive receiver out of a [[Deshaun Watson]] or [[Zach Wilson]] offense. Harmon notes his best stretch came with Joe Flacco's vertical passing (12 targets vs the Rams) before a scary concussion ended his season. James Koh (not tracked) was more forgiving on the Jets complaints given Wilson's quality, and rated Moore ahead of [[Jerry Jeudy]] on film, tape and production.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Moore has three drops on a 23.1 percent drop rate to start 2024 and Harmon calls him not the most reliable player, with a role that is weird from a usage standpoint. He is nonetheless close to [[Amari Cooper]] and [[Jerry Jeudy]] in first-read targets and has a legit role — a solid player with clear flaws, in a room Harmon thinks is capped entirely by [[Deshaun Watson]].
- 2024-10-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-17]]): Harmon liked Moore as a prospect and cites his strong rookie-year Reception Perception numbers, but says he 'hasn't really done anything of note since then' and that he now basically knows who Moore is. Moore is Cleveland's main slot and Harmon wants him kept there as a vertical slot option rather than promoted to the vacated X after the [[Amari Cooper]] trade — he is not an X candidate because of size. Harmon generalized the point: smaller receivers post great success rates vs zone but are not real ball winners, so 'yeah you're open, but who's going to really take that throw?' — and with [[Deshaun Watson]] the answer is nobody. He also flagged the Jets experiment of kicking Moore outside as a deployment he hated at the time and cites it as a standing example of why inside-to-outside conversions fail.
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): of the Browns pass catchers in play after [[Amari Cooper]]'s exit, Moore is the highest-floor option behind [[David Njoku]], because he benefits most from slot work, manufactured touches and zone looks. That makes him the safer weekly play than [[Cedric Tillman]] (higher ceiling, much lower floor) or [[Jerry Jeudy]], though Waldman framed the whole group as viable 'right now until we see how that's going to settle' and told listeners to pick by matchup given the Pittsburgh/Denver/Pittsburgh/Kansas City stretch ahead.
- 2024-10-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-29]]): Moore played well from the slot in the Browns' win over Baltimore, and Harmon says the depressed efficiency metrics — 'he should not be averaging .6 yards per route run' — were a quarterback problem, not a Moore problem. He concedes Moore may never be as good as he thought as a prospect but insists 'he does not suck' and that he definitely gets open; Koh (not tracked) says nobody on a podcast has been higher on Moore, calling him a natural separator he wanted in Green Bay opposite [[Davante Adams]] and a player who could handle all three receiver spots. Both note Baltimore was badly banged up in the secondary (Marlon Humphrey out) and is a far more generous pass defense than last year, and Harmon flags a brutal upcoming Cleveland schedule.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31]]): Moore is underrated in this setup. Cleveland is aligning him outside and [[Jameis Winston]] hit him repeatedly on deep outs; Waldman says Moore is a good enough route runner to run the timing routes and can function outside in this scheme, and would not be surprised if Moore ends up outplaying [[Jerry Jeudy]]. He still rates [[Cedric Tillman]] as the top of the two. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees Moore is the steadier player of the Moore/Jeudy pair — he calls Jeudy a 'freestyle' receiver in the Dante Pettis mold who never developed into what the industry wanted — and says Moore never got a fair shake in New York and profiles as a really solid number two or number three. Waldman notes the one ugly-looking Winston throw people mocked was a miscommunication where Moore cut off his route and doubled back.
- 2024-11-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-21]]): Moore is enjoying a resurgence with [[Jameis Winston]] throwing, and Harmon — a long-standing Moore backer, calling this 'an Elijah Moore podcast' — points to the Browns-Saints game where Winston was letting it rip to Moore on downfield routes. That is the confirmation of what Harmon has always argued: Moore is not just a gadgety slot guy but should be a real deal route runner down the field. He is still primarily the slot receiver in Cleveland, with [[Cedric Tillman]] at X and [[Jerry Jeudy]] at flanker. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) adds that most everybody had written Moore off, that he has been efficient, and that he likes how Cleveland is deploying him all over the formation. Harmon's caveat: Winston inflates the whole passing game's statistics.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]) [Dynasty]: Moore is quietly one of the more interesting impending free agent receivers. He posted 14 targets and 111 receiving yards against Denver at about 9.43 air yards per target, made real contested catches along the sideline rather than only the free plays [[Jameis Winston]] created, and is now roughly a 53/43 slot-to-outside split — Harmon said we can finally call him a vertical slot, a genuine change in usage from his earlier career. He projects a Jakobi Meyers-style contract, roughly three to four years at 11 to 13 million per, and wants him to be 'next year's [[Darnell Mooney]]' — a bargain in the right ecosystem. Fits floated: Houston (behind [[Nico Collins]] with [[Stefon Diggs]] leaving, alongside a hoped-for [[Tank Dell]] rebound), Denver if [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] does not seize the job, and Tennessee opposite [[Calvin Ridley]]. Caveat Harmon named: the value only holds if the new team uses him downfield and does not repeat the gadget/backfield experiments that wasted him in Cleveland.
- 2025-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-19]]): Harmon names Detroit as a plausible landing spot for Moore in free agency — not as a true perimeter guy, though he thinks Moore can play the perimeter — because he would bring speed and versatility as a supporting piece behind [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and [[Jameson Williams]]. He frames Moore as a second-act player who can still give a team something on his second contract and says he is very interested to see which team ends up being the Elijah Moore team. James Koh (not tracked) liked the fit but pushed back on size: with Williams thin and St. Brown not big, adding another smaller receiver could create a size problem in Detroit, and Harmon agreed the Lions probably care at least a little, since [[Tim Patrick]] and Allen Robinson were the in-season adds and represent the type they want.
- 2025-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-07]]): Moore is the kind of low-cost free-agent addition Houston should be shopping for instead of the top of a weak market, and Harmon endorses Koh's suggestion — 'I do like your Elijah Moore recommendation here'. The reasoning is quarterback fit: [[C.J. Stroud]] is one of the passers who can activate a smaller route-runner archetype, with the talent and confidence to rip out-breaking throws down the field, exactly what made the [[Tank Dell]] connection work as a rookie. Harmon argues Moore is far more that type of player than 'the manufactured nonsense' Cleveland used him with. Koh agrees he can fill several roles in Houston, though he concedes Moore is not the dirty-work interior player the Texans most need.
- 2025-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-07]]): Harmon loved the Bills signing and pushed back on the 'another slot receiver' response — he thinks Moore can be more than a slot, citing 75.2% and 72.7% success rate versus man in his first two seasons plus 73.3% and 75.8% versus press, and an 81st-percentile success rate versus man as a rookie when he ran 72.8% of snaps on the line and 72.3% outside as effectively the Jets' X. Route detail: 86.2% on the curl as a rookie, and per Koh 81.3% on the out and 77.8% on the comeback in 2022, plus a strong nine route. The risk Harmon names is that he is small and few quarterbacks will take deep outside throws to a smaller player — the last one who would was [[Joe Flacco]] — though [[Josh Allen]] qualifies. Both hosts agreed the fantasy outlook is poor because the room is crowded with [[Khalil Shakir]], [[Keon Coleman]], [[Josh Palmer]] and a run-heavy attack, and Harmon believes only one of Moore or [[Curtis Samuel]] wins the job. Harmon floated Moore having a Jeudy-style year-four/five breakthrough while conceding it may be hopium.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Harmon calls himself the biggest Elijah Moore apologist alive and still says the idea of Moore as Buffalo's X receiver is ridiculous — he needs to be in the slot the majority of the time, or at most a flanker off the line where he can move around. Harmon uses Moore as his standing proof-of-concept that a small receiver *can* get open as an X: Moore took the vast majority of his rookie-year snaps on the line of scrimmage in his Reception Perception profile and separated constantly, but the Jets' only quarterback willing to throw those windows was [[Joe Flacco]], and the same held early in year two when [[Garrett Wilson]] played the slot. Buffalo already has [[Khalil Shakir]] inside, which further squeezes the role.
- 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]): with [[Khalil Shakir]] on a high ankle sprain and [[Josh Palmer]] and [[Curtis Samuel]] banged up, Harmon says Moore 'definitely has an opportunity to make this roster' and is openly rooting for it. Of every Buffalo receiver he has charted, Moore has the best separation in terms of routes and explosiveness and the best man-coverage-beating ability of the group — which matters given Harmon's view that the Bills have no reliable one-on-one winner. He still frames the room as five usable guys — [[Keon Coleman]], Shakir, Palmer, Moore, Samuel — with no superstars.
- 2026-01-29 — According to JJ Zachariason (not tracked) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-29]] [Dynasty]: Moore is one of Zachariason's cited misses - an awesome route runner and separator, but at well under 180 pounds he can't block in the run game or consistently win intermediate/over-the-middle throws against zone coverage, which matters more now that offenses blend 11-and-a-half/12 personnel. He notes Moore's only real quarterback believer has been Joe Flacco; most starters won't throw the tight, contested middle-of-field looks Moore's game is built on.
- 2026-04-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-14]]): Moore is grouped alongside [[Dontayvion Wicks]] and Hollywood Brown as part of the Eagles' newly assembled receiver corps meant to collectively replace [[DeVonta Smith]]'s vacated WR2 production once Smith moves into the #1 role.
