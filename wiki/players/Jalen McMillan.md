# Jalen McMillan

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast on the 2024 WR class A-Z ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): has enough skill to become a weekly contributor who can do extensive work in a starting lineup early in his career, if he lands the right scheme and depth-chart fit. A smooth operator with releases and route setups, he creates after the catch with or without blockers — makes the first man miss, sometimes the second — and sets up his lead blocker well in space. The flaw: in higher-traffic situations with multiple blockers, he doesn't always recognize when he has a wide-open lane and stay patient behind his blocks, and Waldman has seen him veer into unblocked defenders when he had a clear path to the end zone (still scored anyway, just made it harder on himself). Washington teammate of [[Ja'Lynn Polk]].
- 2024-04-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-23]]) [Dynasty]: McMillan tested very similarly to teammate [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] (6'1"/197, 4.47 40 — actually faster than Polk — 37" vertical, 10'7" broad) but Harmon felt he played slower on tape. To avoid another 'Rasheed Rice situation,' Harmon sampled early healthy 2023 games plus 2022 tape rather than McMillan's injury-affected midseason 2023 stretch. Alignment was almost exclusively slot (83.1% slot snaps, 77.5% off-line, only 4.8% backfield), and downfield numbers were poor: 48% success on the nine, 47% on the post (run on 12% of routes) — which co-host James Koh (untracked) called 'bonkers bad,' calling the profile a 'non-starter' as a slot-only player; Harmon pushed back on that framing but shared the concern. Zone success was 74.1%, well below Harmon's ~80% threshold for slot viability, though man coverage success (69.2%) and slant/dig marks (85%+/83%) were strong. Harmon compared his projected role to Hunter Renfrow — a player Harmon has long felt is overrated by the public. Production dropped from a strong healthy 2022 (79-1,096-9) to an injury-hampered 2023 (560 yards, 5 TD); Harmon attributed the flip partly to Polk developing and passing him on the depth chart rather than pure health, and currently projects McMillan as a Day 3 pick, well behind Polk's Day 2/priority-Round-2 grade.
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman expects Jalen McMillan to push immediately for the Buccaneers' WR3 role, and says he graded McMillan slightly ahead of [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] in this WR class, projecting a Chris Godwin-style long-term ceiling -- capable of working inside with real outside utility too. He points to draft capital as the tell: Tampa Bay spent significantly better draft capital on McMillan this year than they spent on [[Trey Palmer]] last year, signaling the front office doesn't view Palmer as their long-term slot answer even after Palmer's improved 2023 receiving season. Waldman flags Palmer and Rakim Jarrett as camp names who could still push past McMillan for a year if either has a strong camp, but McMillan remains his preferred long-term dynasty asset of the group.
- 2024-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-25]]): McMillan charts as a slot-majority player — 83.1% of sampled snaps from the slot, 4.8% in the backfield, 77.5% aligned behind the line of scrimmage — but his coverage data cuts the other way, at the 49th percentile vs man and only the 19th vs zone. Harmon says that split is exactly why Tampa seems intent on moving him outside rather than into the slot, since [[Chris Godwin]] is going back inside and [[Mike Evans]] owns the X; McMillan could take flanker snaps. He expects him to win on a small handful of routes — slants, digs, over-the-middle work — and compares the odd profile to [[Demario Douglas]], a man-beating slot whose chart looks slot-ish but whose coverage data does not. Koh notes the third-round capital, 6-foot-1, 200 pounds, a 4.47 and a 37-inch vertical, and that a 2023 knee injury muddied his final college year.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): McMillan is his long-term monitor — in three to five years, maybe one of the better number-two receivers in the NFL, comped to a 'Darius Slayton plus' type, which Angelo means as a compliment. He expects McMillan to start immediately in three-receiver sets alongside [[Chris Godwin]] and [[Mike Evans]], with Godwin bumping into the slot and Evans on one side at X, leaving McMillan to move around at Z — in Angelo's opinion his most natural position. The caveat is distribution: it only works if [[Baker Mayfield]] can feed all three. Angelo's pitch is a player who grows into a larger role while getting real game experience from day one.
- 2024-10-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-24]]): Harmon charted one McMillan game (week seven vs. Baltimore) and came away mixed-to-negative — low success rates versus both man and zone, undisciplined routes with a lot of wasted motion at the top of the stem, and a sense that the staff got irritated with him. He played 56% of snaps on the line and was outside on 68% in that game, a college slot being asked to work as the flanker so [[Chris Godwin]] could stay inside. Harmon noted McMillan profiled as more of a man-coverage beater than a zone beater inside per his Reception Perception prospect profile, and said he did not love his vertical routes in college and does not love them in the NFL. He also flagged the mismatch with [[Baker Mayfield]], who has mastered the system's precise out-breaking timing while McMillan is 'kind of all over the place'. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) was blunter that [[Sterling Shepard]] has outplayed him. Harmon: no big breakout expected in the next few weeks.
- 2024-12-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-03]]): a mixed but mostly negative read — Harmon says 'Jalen McMillan's film isn't bad' when listing sleepers, but in the same segment says 'McMillan has been a mess' as part of the disappointing Washington trio alongside [[Ja'Lynn Polk]], with [[Rome Odunze]] set to one side as fine. He grouped McMillan with [[Luke McCaffrey]], [[Troy Franklin]], [[Jalen Coker]] and [[Adonai Mitchell]] as the class's thin, largely incomplete depth tier.
- 2025-01-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-07]]): McMillan was a mess on film early — bad enough that Tampa Bay was phasing him out for [[Sterling Shepard]] — but Harmon calls him one of the most improved receivers of the season. Since Week 14 he ranks fourth among rookies at 1.93 yards per route run, behind [[Brian Thomas Jr.]], [[Malik Nabers]] and [[Ladd McConkey]]. Koh (not tracked) added the last five games: 6.2 targets and 4.8 receptions per game, a 77% catch rate, 63 yards per game, 13.2 yards per reception, 316 yards and seven touchdowns. Harmon flagged that the production is touchdown-driven and questioned sustainability, projecting him as a WR3 rather than a WR2 — the room is better with [[Mike Evans]], [[Chris Godwin]] and McMillan back. He wants McMillan used mostly outside as a speed/man-beater, not in the slot, matching a college profile that beat man better than zone.
- 2025-01-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-13]]) [Dynasty]: fuck it on McMillan as a lower-middle-class [[Chris Godwin]], mostly because Harmon holds Godwin so high and because the two do not play the game the same way — he is skeptical McMillan becomes a true number two. He allows that from a pure fantasy-results perspective a lower-middle-class Godwin season is achievable, but Godwin's actual role is not a fit for him. Harmon credits Liam Coen — this was his first inkling Coen would become his favorite play caller of the season — for taking a prospect who charted as a whatever slot receiver and making him a speed flanker: Reception Perception had McMillan as a man-to-man beater rather than a consistent zone winner, and once he became a routine contributor he won down the field against man. Harmon's projection is a good WR3 candidate.
- 2025-02-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-21]]): a genuine in-season reversal. Harmon liked the prospect profile as a late-third/early-fourth value — an almost pure college slot who beat man better than zone — and thought moving him to flanker in the Liam Coen offense was inspired, but called his early rookie film 'straight up terrible,' unphysical and leaving plays on the field, to the point Tampa benched him for [[Sterling Shepard]]. From week 12 on the light came on: 2.55 yards per route run against man coverage, though he stayed unproductive against zone, with the motion-versus-man usage unlocking his big-play ability. James Koh (not tracked) added the box-score version — last five games, 6.2 targets and 4.8 receptions per game, a 77.4% catch rate, 13.17 yards per reception, seven touchdowns, a 1,074-yard 17-game pace. Harmon's projection is nonetheless a very good third receiver, not a two, and he warns the production may not survive a coordinator downgrade.
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]): McMillan is best deployed as a wide receiver three and a Z/motion player, not a slot, despite playing slot in college — at Washington he actually charted a better success rate vs man than vs zone, so moving him outside to attack man coverage off pre-snap motion makes sense. Harmon has not yet charted his end-of-season sample, but warns he struggles at the catch point and is not a physical receiver, and that a lot of his production came off designed motion looks; the touchdown rate is unsustainable. Koh contrasts a 48% catch rate over his first eight games ('he was terrible') with his last five games of 2024 — five receptions, 63 yards per game, seven touchdowns, 77.4% catch rate — and asks which is the real player; both land on somewhere in between. Harmon likes how the trio of [[Mike Evans]], [[Chris Godwin]] and McMillan complement each other.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman agrees with Sigmund Bloom (untracked) that McMillan, the late-season fantasy darling of 2024, is a free-agency loser now that [[Chris Godwin]] has re-signed in Tampa Bay on a three-year deal. His reasoning is that Tampa will still feature the tight end, which siphons opportunities away from McMillan on top of Godwin's return alongside [[Mike Evans]]. He discloses he holds best ball shares of McMillan but says he had felt all along that Godwin was coming back, so the outcome is unsurprising. Bob Harris (untracked) reminds listeners Godwin was the overall WR2 in fantasy through the first seven weeks of 2024 before the dislocated ankle ended his season.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-04-28]]): drafting [[Emeka Egbuka]] is not Tampa souring on McMillan so much as being realistic about him. Harmon warns against the trap of assuming a strong late-season finish means the next step is a bigger role — sometimes the usage a team just found is the best use case. His evaluation: McMillan improved a lot over the year after awful early tape, but he is not a physical player, needs to be schemed open to a degree, can beat man coverage on deeper routes, and will not give consistent separation underneath. Harmon thinks he is better against man than zone, and credits Tampa for finding the right job — a stretch player working off the pre-snap motion and motion-at-the-snap that Liam Coen used last year. Because [[Baker Mayfield]] gets fritzy under pressure and needs layups, the quick separator role goes to Egbuka instead.
- 2025-05-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-23]]): McMillan is the most encouraging of the three struggling rookies but Harmon caps him as a future number three receiver, not a number two. Overall Reception Perception grades were poor — 24th percentile vs man and press, 20th vs zone — and his slant (60.7%) and curl (54.8%) numbers are bottom-20 all-time across roughly 500 charted players, an inversion of the 85.7% slant success he showed as a Washington prospect. What Harmon likes is Tampa Bay's alignment call: an 83.1% slot player in college who took 67.7% of his sampled rookie snaps on the perimeter as a flanker, often in motion, producing 63% on nines, 82% on corners and 72.7% on posts despite sub-50% downfield numbers as a prospect. Harmon credits the speed that let him beat man horizontally in college being expressed vertically. He is emphatic that the late-season production — 13 games, 58 targets, 37 catches, 461 yards, eight touchdowns, with seven scores and a 77.4% catch rate over the final five games, which co-host James Koh (not tracked) notes won fantasy championships — was Liam Coen scheming him wide open off motion, not separation he created. Harmon praises Tampa for drafting [[Emeka Egbuka]] in the first round rather than over-crediting that finish, and notes they built the post-injury offense around [[Cade Otten]] for four weeks, not McMillan. Limitation: he pushes vertically and works back on curls with a defender in his hip pocket, with little route-running nuance.
- 2025-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-30]]): McMillan sits at the 24th percentile or worse versus man, zone and press, which Harmon stresses does not mean he is bad — it means he is not a full-field player and has to be used to his strengths. Those are downfield: an obnoxiously high 81.8% corner route success rate, a good nine route number, 63% on the post and over 70% on the dig, because he gets snappy at the break point once he is rolling, which Harmon likens to a [[Jerry Jeudy]] type. He should not be running short routes, where he is not decisive enough. Tampa moved him from a college slot role to primary outside receiver as a rookie to real success thanks to condensed formations and heavy motion, and he closed 2024 strongly with the room decimated by injury. Harmon sees him as the number three and as a good complement to [[Emeka Egbuka]], who provides the layups McMillan does not; if [[Mike Evans]] leaves after this season, Godwin/McMillan/Egbuka is still a nice one-two-three. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) adds that McMillan and Egbuka have similar combine numbers but are not comparable in the zero-to-five-yard range.
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-20]]): McMillan is placed on IR and, per Rick Stroud of the Tampa Times, may be held out past the Bucs' week nine bye. Harmon's evaluation is that McMillan's role is his role — a really good WR3 rather than a true WR2 — because his Reception Perception charting last year showed him beating man coverage on the downfield stuff (nine, post, dig, corner, out) but not separating well enough on base routes like slants, curls and comebacks to sustain WR2 production. Losing him costs Tampa the motion usage he was given more than raw target volume. Harmon is not 'super worried' about the Bucs offense, but stacks the loss with [[Chris Godwin]]'s ankle and Liam Cohen's departure, which is why he is now fully on [[Emeka Egbuka]].
- 2025-12-18 — According to Matt Harmon and James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-12-18]]): Jalen McMillan's return in week 15 was a notable factor in Tampa Bay's offense 'feeling smoother' -- he played ahead of both Tez Johnson and Sterling Shepard and posted 17.5 air yards per target, the class-leading downfield mark in that receiver room, which Harmon credited with stretching the field and opening up underneath space for everyone else. Both hosts acknowledged it was only his first game back after a serious injury that had caused people (including themselves) to 'forget' about him, but called it a genuine hit for a third-round pick given how much he opened up the deep passing game in that single outing.
- 2026-02-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-27]]): McMillan playing four games at all was 'a win' given the severity of a preseason neck injury that had people wondering if he'd play football again. Harmon says he flashed well down the stretch, at times even running ahead of [[Emeka Egbuka]] from a route-tree perspective in the final month, and both hosts view the Godwin/Egbuka/McMillan/Tez Johnson receiver group as a strong, deep committee even if [[Mike Evans]] leaves in free agency.
- 2026-06-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-08]]): Waldman ranks Jalen McMillan behind [[Ted Hurst]] in the Bucs receiver pecking order since Hurst fits best in the slot role Godwin otherwise occupies, viewing McMillan as a break-glass-in-emergency option who could 'star' if the offense needs him but isn't fantasy-relevant in a normal week.
- 2026-07-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris notes Jalen McMillan suffered a frightening neck injury in the 2025 preseason that was considered career-threatening and cost him most of the season, but he returned to play in December, suggesting he's healthy now. Harris ranks him third in Tampa's receiver pecking order behind [[Emeka Egbuka]] and [[Chris Godwin]], and says definitively confirming McMillan's health and role in August will be difficult -- Tampa never plays two-receiver sets, so all three receivers are always on the field together, muddying any read on true role importance until Week 1.
- 2026-08-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-07]]): McMillan has a knee issue and has missed multiple camp practices while chasing what Harris frames as the Buccaneers' true 'number three' receiver job -- a role that, in the modern passing game, can function as the de facto number one in any given week. Rookie Ted Hurst has reportedly looked good in the meantime. Harris is filing this away to track but isn't moving McMillan in his ranks as of August 7th.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]): McMillan has been battling a bad knee throughout Buccaneers training camp, opening a door for rookie [[Ted Hurst]] to compete for a bigger early role.
