# Woody Marks

- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: an underrated receiving back with some [[Dalvin Cook]] 'isms' in his game. Waldman credits very quick hand reactions — he wins the ball when a quarterback puts it on him too hot — an ability to make awkward catches, tracking over the shoulder, boundary work on outside routes, and the burst to threaten down the field. He is not accelerating past cornerbacks or most safeties, but once he reaches the secondary he will flip the field 40-60 yards because he makes people miss, runs through tackles and is fast enough to be a problem. The projected role is a Jerick McKinnon-plus contributor — more versatile than McKinnon, maybe less athletic — with the NFL toughness and savvy to develop.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): asked whether Marks is a year from a featured role with [[Joe Mixon]] aging, Waldman said 'fuck it' despite liking the skills. He needs proof the organization is high enough on Marks to use him as a featured player, and he expects coaches to type him as a scat-back who is featured in space as part of a thunder-and-lightning committee — he noted you could swap Marks for [[Dylan Sampson]] in Cleveland alongside [[Quinshon Judkins]] and still have an exciting one-two punch. So Marks becomes a fixture and a fantasy-worthy player, but an RB3 rather than an RB1. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) countered that Houston backs keep appearing from nowhere; Waldman conceded he is a one-year lottery ticket you sell high on volume, not ability.
- 2025-06-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-02]]): a featured role for Marks is 'a possibility but not a probability.' He likes Marks as a player and thinks the skills are there to become a [[Tony Pollard]]-style play, but the open question is whether he can hold up at that volume. The path is blocked two ways: [[Dameon Pierce]] played well late last season and, if he is still on the roster taking relief carries early, Waldman would lean Pierce as the first-level replacement for [[Joe Mixon]] with Marks merely competing with Dare Ogunbowale for passing-down work. If Pierce is out of the picture, Marks could become a [[Kyren Williams]]-type play — and in an outside-zone scheme the receiving skills matter less, since McVay-style offenses use play-action off outside zone rather than targeting the back. Waldman's projection if both Ogunbowale and Pierce clear out: roughly 95-100 touches, 400-450 yards and a few touchdowns, which is bye-week value. His caveat: a good late-round upside play, but do not let him clog a roster all year if the picture stays cloudy with Pierce involved. Waldman also flags it as puzzling that Pierce's strong Florida receiving skills have never been used.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): with [[Joe Mixon]] on NFI, Harris slots the rookie Marks right next to [[Dameon Pierce]] in standard and above him in PPR, and argues 'the smart way to play this might just in fact be Woody Marks' precisely because he is the unknown — we think we know Pierce and [[Nick Chubb]] already. In his Almanac profile Harris gave Marks a Kenneth Gainwell comparison, which he concedes 'is not very flattering' and suggests a satellite back who would not make much noise (especially with Dare Ogunbowale still in Houston), but he also invoked the sprinter-soldier [[Bucky Irving]] as the upside outcome. Nobody knows until Week 1 in LA against the Rams. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Marks, 9% rostered, is Harris's number three waiver name and was guest BRANDON FUNSTON's number six, just off his list. Harris admits he knows nothing about Marks and has no idea whether he is good — the appeal is process of elimination, since he is 'pretty sure' about the current version of [[Nick Chubb]] and never thought [[Dameon Pierce]] was that good. His plan is to watch whether Marks gets on the field against a good Rams defense, because that would tell you the coaches think he is good. Funston notes Marks is known for receiving acumen and probably profiles as a third-down back, which in PPR is the more impactful role, and that some people see [[Bucky Irving]]-style overlap — but he worries about Houston overall: one of the worst offensive lines, a messy backfield, and unproven pass catchers outside [[Nico Collins]], and says something tells him it ends as a train wreck.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris's number-five Week 3 waiver add (6% rostered in Yahoo leagues), explicitly a stash and a bet on [[Joe Mixon]] never coming back. Marks played only 13 offensive snaps — more than Dare Ogunbowale, while [[Nick Chubb]] played 24 and Dameon Pierce was inactive — but the flashes were real: one electric 37-yard run after the catch, a good seven-yard carry up the gut, and a good blitz pickup. Harris recalls writing in the almanac that the dream for Marks is that he could one day be a [[Bucky Irving]] type, and says that on the 37-yarder you could believe it. Guest John Daigle (Establish The Run, not tracked) also had Marks among his honorable mentions and grouped him with the no-standalone-value stashes ([[Blake Corum]], [[Chris Rodriguez Jr.]]).
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris repeated his Tuesday-show recommendation of Marks as a speculative deep add, essentially by process of elimination among Houston's non-[[Joe Mixon]] options — [[Nick Chubb]] scored in week 2 but 'doesn't look special at all', and Dare Ogunbowale was freight-trained in pass protection against Tampa Bay on Monday night. Harris said Marks by contrast acquitted himself pretty well as a blocker, which is the specific reason he expects Marks may earn more of a look going forward. He was clear the ceiling is limited while Mixon's return remains unresolved, and that Mixon would still be the better solution for Houston.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): the Texans looked like they were searching in the backfield — Marks got a red-zone carry at the end of a long second-quarter drive and several more in the fourth, finishing with 29 snaps to [[Nick Chubb]]'s 30 and touches of 12-7 in Chubb's favor. Harris thought Marks looked physical for his smaller size but could not start him yet in a lineup with no room to run. Guest SCOTT FISH (Fantasy Cares, NOT a tracked expert) had Marks third on his waiver list: he hates the offensive line but says usage, snaps and touches have all trended up, Marks looks exciting on tape and runs more routes than the other Houston backs for PPR chances, Dameon Pierce did not get an offensive snap, and 'we may be on limited time with Chubb' given the yards per carry, two injuries and age. Harris also had Marks third, with the upside comp being 'what if he could ever be like half of what [[Bucky Irving]] is' — hard for a little guy, and no sure thing.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): Marks is the unanimous number-one Week 5 waiver add for Harris and guest Jeff Erickson (RotoWire, not tracked), at 32% rostered. He scored twice against Tennessee, the second on a sharp foot-in-the-ground stop-go move, and took a flat pass for the first score on the first play either team ran in the red zone — Harris stresses Marks is in there on passing downs, which is the edge over [[Kendre Miller]]. Snap count was 38 Marks to 26 [[Nick Chubb]], with 30 offensive snaps against Jacksonville the week before, so usage is trending. They disagree on price: Erickson says 35-40% of budget in a 12-team home league (over half in NFFC 20-man rosters), while Harris would consider going over 50%, arguing you could be lucking into a season-long starter — though he respects the circumspection given a bad Houston offensive line and a possible [[Christian McCaffrey]]-style injury making [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] available later.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-02]]): after Marks's two-touchdown waiver-wire week, Angelo expects him to be the touch leader going forward in an interesting tandem with [[Nick Chubb]] — he is a good secondary or tertiary pass-game option (his strength at USC), has been better than anticipated in pass protection, and has impressed on first and second down with good second-level decisions and missed tackles forced. [[Matt Waldman]] charted the game from the running back perspective: 22 touches to 15 for Marks over Chubb, but five Marks carries went for two yards or less against only three for Chubb, and Chubb had a 21-yard run wiped out by a marginal backside holding call — so Marks's 22-yard edge in output was thinner than it looked. Waldman still agreed the split edge goes to Marks on business logic: with two backs roughly equal, you develop the young one and keep the veteran as old reliable. He cautioned the surrounding narrative is 'a little bit more hyperbole' than the tape supports.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Marks was the waiver add of the week and, with hindsight on the [[Bucky Irving]] and Arizona news, clearly the right FAAB target — but Harris warned against the coverage cycle in which everyone races to be 'the most in on Woody Marks.' He ranks Marks RB21, just ahead of [[Chase Brown]] at RB24; Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) has Marks RB18 ahead of Brown at 19. Both stressed the one-spot gap is a direction-of-travel call, not a declaration that Marks is guaranteed to outscore Brown. Harris noted Marks got more snaps as the game went on but [[Nick Chubb]] still started, and that a runaway script against Tennessee inflated the explosion; Bell added the Texans are a team openly searching for offensive answers, which favors the newer option. Marks was also the consensus DFS chalk at $5,400.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-06]]): Waldman says 'fuck it' to Marks overtaking [[Nick Chubb]] this year — this stays a split. His film argument is that the good runs Marks had came with convoys where he went untouched and had to do nothing but run in a straight line, while Chubb was making cutbacks and stiff-arming the biggest man on the field against the same defense. Waldman allows Marks is a playable asset in a timeshare and remains more of a receiving-down back who will get more work. Bob Harris (not tracked) agreed, saying the market's Week 4 enthusiasm ignored that Chubb never went anywhere, and that expecting a [[Michael Carter]]-, [[Rachaad White]]- or [[Rico Dowdle]]-style full takeover was the wrong expectation to bring to Marks.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): 'good is not what happened to those who added Woody Marks on waivers' — seven carries for 24 yards and zero catches. Harris reiterates that he tried to temper expectations: after the Titans shutout he saw good signs and a couple of fun runs, but the widely-repeated notion that Marks 'had dispatched [[Nick Chubb]]' was incorrect, and he ranked Marks ahead of Chubb this week by only a few spots. Chubb in fact started, and three Houston backs carried on the opening drive, the third being fullback British Brooks. Marks's best chance was a stuff from the five after a [[Nico Collins]] intermediate post, plus a good run wiped out by holding while it was still 10-3.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06 (Players We Think We Know)]]) [Dynasty]: Marks is a player Waldman thinks he knows and most people don't. His comp is [[Tyjae Spears]] for this offense — a good player on occasion who can probably give you RB2 value, possibly as a committee back, but Waldman explicitly rejects the idea he turns into an [[Austin Ekeler]]. His phrasing: good, but not that good.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): the Jacksonville game 'was much more the Woody Marks show', including in close - Marks took a carry to the one and then a pivot pitch on the goal line for a touchdown, and the snap count was a whopping 54 for Marks to 10 for [[Nick Chubb]]. Harris still caps the enthusiasm because of the state of the Houston offensive line: 'we don't want to be super aggressive with Marks, but at least as a flex, you certainly want him over Nick Chubb'. In the waiver segment he and guest **Brandon Funston** (The Athletic, not tracked) agreed that in a 10-team league you should have better options - Funston preferred [[Trey Benson]]'s ceiling, and Harris said chasing the Texans backs is not a profitable enterprise in a 12-team league either, though in 14-team leagues you sometimes have no choice.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-13]]): Marks is RB24 since Week 6 and RB29 overall in PPR, with 21 touches for 119 yards and a couple of scores against Tennessee and an 81-yard, touchdown outing against the Jaguars. Waldman says Houston is deliberately feeding him — [[Nick Chubb]] is still in the mix but far less — and with a close game script against the Titans, and [[Davis Mills]] under center, he could see 18 to 22 touches; the Titans defense is not an easy matchup and Jeffrey Simmons should be back off the bye, but the run game stays viable. [[Chris Harris]] agrees and says this is the first week he has put major separation between Marks and Chubb, with Marks firmly in his flex territory, noting Marks got the goal-line chances last week even without converting. Asked to choose between Marks and [[R.J. Harvey]] for Week 11, Waldman narrowly took Marks on game script.
