# Brock Bowers

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-05]]) [Dynasty]: leaning "feel it" on Bowers becoming the next big fantasy-producing rookie tight end, though states he's genuinely on the fence. Profile: a "move tight end" in the [[Sam LaPorta]] mold who needs the right scheme fit rather than a plug-and-play talent — strong after the catch, can run through contact, but has an underrated technique flaw on one specific target type (high-and-away throws, where he "claps" the ball with both hands and drops it), and is not yet a technically sound blocker. Floats a Jim Harbaugh-to-the-Chargers landing spot as a positive scheme fit if Harbaugh installs more two-tight-end sets. Explicit ceiling calibration: probably won't top LaPorta's or Kyle Pitts's rookie-year production, but should still have real fantasy value — closer to a [[Dalton Kincaid]] outcome (underused on some route types as a rookie, but still finished as a top-12 fantasy tight end).
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]) [Dynasty]: the Chargers landing spot gets a firmer scheme endorsement now that the pick is real rather than speculative (LA holds the 5th overall selection) — not too early to take him there at all "if you have a cogent idea of how you're going to use him," pointing to how aggressively new OC Greg Roman used Mark Andrews in Baltimore as a direct positional/archetype comp for how Bowers would be deployed, with the explicit caveat that Bowers is "not quite as rugged in the open field" as Andrews. Illustrated with a live anecdote: co-host **Bob Harris** (not a tracked expert) drafted Bowers 11th round in an early best-ball mock and had him sniped away, underscoring how little draft capital the market is currently pricing him at.

- 2024-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-30]] [Dynasty]: "you gotta view Bowers through a similar lens as Sam LaPorta" — a move tight end with competency in specific blocking roles whose primary job is wreaking havoc as a receiver and runner after the catch, ranked TE3 in this RSP class. LaPorta comped as "Evan Ingram without the technical flaws," while Bowers's game is comped to Vernon Davis mixed with Aaron Hernandez's versatility. Named, alongside [[Cade Stover]], as the class's most pro-ready tight end if his scheme uses him as a receiver/H-back rather than an in-line blocker.

- 2024-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Feel It or F@#k It* ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-01]]): "feeling" the possibility that Bowers breaks Mike Ditka's 1961 rookie tight end receiving-yardage record (1,076 yards) — but that's contingent on landing spot. In a 1.5-PPR league specifically, says Bowers should be a top-three pick, "if not the top pick," given how many ways he produces: efficient and dynamic after the catch, able to re-accelerate to beat corners on the same side of the field, useful in the screen game, and sets up his own blocks well — praised as making a good athlete like [[David Njoku]] "look like a lumbering guy with cement in his shoes." His one real flaw as a receiver: in contested catch situations he sometimes "clap attacks" the football rather than securing it cleanly; if that doesn't improve he risks becoming a [[Courtland Sutton]]-type good-not-great fantasy option who drops one or two a game, but Waldman expects him to work through it. With the right landing spot, has a chance to be a top-five fantasy tight end as a rookie and the talent equivalent of prime Vernon Davis mixed with Aaron Hernandez — "a pretty strong statement with those two guys."
- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]) [Dynasty]: names Kansas City as his ideal landing spot for Bowers so he can develop behind [[Patrick Mahomes]] and [[Travis Kelce]] rather than start immediately; co-host Bob Harris relays reported buzz (via NJ.com's Andy Vasquez, untracked) that the Jets could realistically take Bowers if he's available.
- 2024-04-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-23 (Evaluation vs. Valuation)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman flatly rejects the media narrative valuing Bowers as only a mid-first-round prospect, saying there is 'no chance' he'd grade him that conservatively and that Bowers should be considered among the best players in the whole draft regardless of position, unsurprised if he goes as early as 5th (Chargers) or 8th (Atlanta). He praises Bowers's toughness for returning from tightrope surgery to help push Georgia deeper into the playoff race, his excellent body control and agility, and blocking good enough to justify using him in 12 personnel or even without a fullback on the field. Waldman closes with a 'I know it when I see it' framing — Bowers is simply a great football player, full stop. Chad Reuter (guest, not tracked) concurred he's 'pretty good, too.'
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman described Brock Bowers as 'more of a fullback playing tight end with wide receiver skills' -- an H-back type -- and said the Raiders' apparent plan to use [[Michael Mayer]] as the primary blocker while lining Bowers up detached from the formation more often makes Bowers a boom-bust fantasy option in 2024, not a lock for immediate top-five TE production. He voiced real concern the Las Vegas coaching staff (invoking a 'Dumb and Dumber' jab at the franchise) may not use Bowers' skill set to its full potential this year, but noted the team spent the 13th overall pick on him and doesn't expect them to let that investment 'waste away' -- if the offense opens up, Bowers' zone route running and red-zone ability give him real top-five-TE upside.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]): if the Raiders staff make good on their stated line that tight ends must block, Bowers could be the biggest waste of a pick on the roster — but Waldman does not think that is likely. He expects Bowers to be used as a wide receiver, effectively Las Vegas's third receiving option, which is what pushes [[Tre Tucker]] down to fourth in the pecking order.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): Waldman is starting to believe the top-12 fantasy tight end price on Bowers. He dismissed the worry sparked by the Raiders' tight ends coach saying he views tight ends as blockers first, arguing that coach would not keep his job benching a player drafted that early, and noting Bowers has been close to unstoppable in camp on pure athletic ability — with the caveat that this gets harder once coverage looks become realistic. Waldman expects a varied role in which Bowers is not a true in-line tight end most of the time, helped by [[Michael Gallup]] retiring, leaving [[Tre Tucker]] and [[Jakobi Meyers]] alongside [[Davante Adams]]. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) relayed that Kirk Morrison, who spent significant time at Raiders camp, saw zero drops from Bowers.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): Bowers plays a bigger role than people think. Angelo's baseline is Cole Kmet's strong 2023, and he says Bowers is a lot more than Kmet — a Frankenstein of Vernon Davis and Aaron Hernandez — expecting manufactured touches as a ball carrier plus downfield usage in an offense that will not be efficient with [[Gardner Minshew]] or [[Aidan O'Connell]] at quarterback, and that badly needs a shot in the arm to keep up with teams scoring 30. He notes Bowers and [[Davante Adams]] are the ones on the field for 90-plus percent of snaps, and he also likes watching [[Tre Tucker]]. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed and added the mechanism: Minshew throws to the middle of the field and lacks a drive-the-ball arm, and in Minshew's six starts with Dallas Goedert as a comparable middle-of-field target Goedert averaged about five targets, four receptions and 62.5 yards per game — a 1,000-yard, 68-catch, five-touchdown pace. Waldman thinks Adams suffers at Bowers' expense and that [[Jakobi Meyers]] is about to be leapfrogged. Waldman also expects Minshew to start the first half of the year for political reasons before Antonio Pierce gives O'Connell time to watch and learn.
