# Brock Bowers

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-05]]) [Dynasty]: leaning "feel it" on Bowers becoming the next big fantasy-producing rookie tight end, though states he's genuinely on the fence. Profile: a "move tight end" in the [[Sam LaPorta]] mold who needs the right scheme fit rather than a plug-and-play talent — strong after the catch, can run through contact, but has an underrated technique flaw on one specific target type (high-and-away throws, where he "claps" the ball with both hands and drops it), and is not yet a technically sound blocker. Floats a Jim Harbaugh-to-the-Chargers landing spot as a positive scheme fit if Harbaugh installs more two-tight-end sets. Explicit ceiling calibration: probably won't top LaPorta's or Kyle Pitts's rookie-year production, but should still have real fantasy value — closer to a [[Dalton Kincaid]] outcome (underused on some route types as a rookie, but still finished as a top-12 fantasy tight end).
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]) [Dynasty]: the Chargers landing spot gets a firmer scheme endorsement now that the pick is real rather than speculative (LA holds the 5th overall selection) — not too early to take him there at all "if you have a cogent idea of how you're going to use him," pointing to how aggressively new OC Greg Roman used Mark Andrews in Baltimore as a direct positional/archetype comp for how Bowers would be deployed, with the explicit caveat that Bowers is "not quite as rugged in the open field" as Andrews. Illustrated with a live anecdote: co-host **Bob Harris** (not a tracked expert) drafted Bowers 11th round in an early best-ball mock and had him sniped away, underscoring how little draft capital the market is currently pricing him at.

- 2024-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-30]] [Dynasty]: "you gotta view Bowers through a similar lens as Sam LaPorta" — a move tight end with competency in specific blocking roles whose primary job is wreaking havoc as a receiver and runner after the catch, ranked TE3 in this RSP class. LaPorta comped as "Evan Ingram without the technical flaws," while Bowers's game is comped to Vernon Davis mixed with Aaron Hernandez's versatility. Named, alongside [[Cade Stover]], as the class's most pro-ready tight end if his scheme uses him as a receiver/H-back rather than an in-line blocker.

- 2024-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Feel It or F@#k It* ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-01]]): "feeling" the possibility that Bowers breaks Mike Ditka's 1961 rookie tight end receiving-yardage record (1,076 yards) — but that's contingent on landing spot. In a 1.5-PPR league specifically, says Bowers should be a top-three pick, "if not the top pick," given how many ways he produces: efficient and dynamic after the catch, able to re-accelerate to beat corners on the same side of the field, useful in the screen game, and sets up his own blocks well — praised as making a good athlete like [[David Njoku]] "look like a lumbering guy with cement in his shoes." His one real flaw as a receiver: in contested catch situations he sometimes "clap attacks" the football rather than securing it cleanly; if that doesn't improve he risks becoming a [[Courtland Sutton]]-type good-not-great fantasy option who drops one or two a game, but Waldman expects him to work through it. With the right landing spot, has a chance to be a top-five fantasy tight end as a rookie and the talent equivalent of prime Vernon Davis mixed with Aaron Hernandez — "a pretty strong statement with those two guys."
- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]) [Dynasty]: names Kansas City as his ideal landing spot for Bowers so he can develop behind [[Patrick Mahomes]] and [[Travis Kelce]] rather than start immediately; co-host Bob Harris relays reported buzz (via NJ.com's Andy Vasquez, untracked) that the Jets could realistically take Bowers if he's available.
- 2024-04-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-23 (Evaluation vs. Valuation)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman flatly rejects the media narrative valuing Bowers as only a mid-first-round prospect, saying there is 'no chance' he'd grade him that conservatively and that Bowers should be considered among the best players in the whole draft regardless of position, unsurprised if he goes as early as 5th (Chargers) or 8th (Atlanta). He praises Bowers's toughness for returning from tightrope surgery to help push Georgia deeper into the playoff race, his excellent body control and agility, and blocking good enough to justify using him in 12 personnel or even without a fullback on the field. Waldman closes with a 'I know it when I see it' framing — Bowers is simply a great football player, full stop. Chad Reuter (guest, not tracked) concurred he's 'pretty good, too.'
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman described Brock Bowers as 'more of a fullback playing tight end with wide receiver skills' -- an H-back type -- and said the Raiders' apparent plan to use [[Michael Mayer]] as the primary blocker while lining Bowers up detached from the formation more often makes Bowers a boom-bust fantasy option in 2024, not a lock for immediate top-five TE production. He voiced real concern the Las Vegas coaching staff (invoking a 'Dumb and Dumber' jab at the franchise) may not use Bowers' skill set to its full potential this year, but noted the team spent the 13th overall pick on him and doesn't expect them to let that investment 'waste away' -- if the offense opens up, Bowers' zone route running and red-zone ability give him real top-five-TE upside.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]): if the Raiders staff make good on their stated line that tight ends must block, Bowers could be the biggest waste of a pick on the roster — but Waldman does not think that is likely. He expects Bowers to be used as a wide receiver, effectively Las Vegas's third receiving option, which is what pushes [[Tre Tucker]] down to fourth in the pecking order.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): Waldman is starting to believe the top-12 fantasy tight end price on Bowers. He dismissed the worry sparked by the Raiders' tight ends coach saying he views tight ends as blockers first, arguing that coach would not keep his job benching a player drafted that early, and noting Bowers has been close to unstoppable in camp on pure athletic ability — with the caveat that this gets harder once coverage looks become realistic. Waldman expects a varied role in which Bowers is not a true in-line tight end most of the time, helped by [[Michael Gallup]] retiring, leaving [[Tre Tucker]] and [[Jakobi Meyers]] alongside [[Davante Adams]]. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) relayed that Kirk Morrison, who spent significant time at Raiders camp, saw zero drops from Bowers.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): Bowers plays a bigger role than people think. Angelo's baseline is Cole Kmet's strong 2023, and he says Bowers is a lot more than Kmet — a Frankenstein of Vernon Davis and Aaron Hernandez — expecting manufactured touches as a ball carrier plus downfield usage in an offense that will not be efficient with [[Gardner Minshew]] or [[Aidan O'Connell]] at quarterback, and that badly needs a shot in the arm to keep up with teams scoring 30. He notes Bowers and [[Davante Adams]] are the ones on the field for 90-plus percent of snaps, and he also likes watching [[Tre Tucker]]. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed and added the mechanism: Minshew throws to the middle of the field and lacks a drive-the-ball arm, and in Minshew's six starts with Dallas Goedert as a comparable middle-of-field target Goedert averaged about five targets, four receptions and 62.5 yards per game — a 1,000-yard, 68-catch, five-touchdown pace. Waldman thinks Adams suffers at Bowers' expense and that [[Jakobi Meyers]] is about to be leapfrogged. Waldman also expects Minshew to start the first half of the year for political reasons before Antonio Pierce gives O'Connell time to watch and learn.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): Bowers as the overall TE3 at 18.8 fantasy points per game is what Waldman expected — he had him as a top-12 tight end preseason, with the quarterback the only real question. Once [[Gardner Minshew]] won the job, Waldman pointed to Minshew's multi-game stretch with [[Dallas Goedert]] as the template and said a strong, possibly 1,000-yard rookie season was live. On the Week 2 tape he saw good rapport on scramble drills, Minshew unafraid to target him, the after-the-catch skill you want, and 'some nice anticipation plays over the middle' where Bowers adjusted to the ball like a receiver. Explicitly the positive counterweight to his [[Dalton Kincaid]] skepticism in the same segment.
- 2024-10-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-03]]): Bowers has been the most dynamic Raiders pass catcher after [[Davante Adams]], leading the team in the short-intermediate area on option and layup routes while Adams took the deep tree and a 34% first-read target share. Harmon's concern if Adams is traded is not Bowers's volume but his target efficiency: it is much harder to find space for Bowers underneath without Adams pulling coverage away, and neither [[Jakobi Meyers]] nor [[Tre Tucker]] dictates coverage. Koh notes the week-4 game without Adams already fit the worry — a season-high 81% snap share but Bowers's worst game, three targets, two catches, 19 yards. Harmon's constructive answer is to move Bowers and Meyers around, using Bowers's ability to align in the slot or on the line.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): presented with the flat statement that Bowers is the best receiving tight end in football, Waldman agreed and said he wishes he had more shares. He qualified it after a beat: [[George Kittle]] and [[Travis Kelce]] are still up there, and as a pure route runner Bowers may be a step below them — but as an athlete he is above all of them, and he will get there. The larger point Waldman made was self-critical: he preaches all year that a less-than-ideal landing spot should not scare you off a really good receiver, because talent tends to rise above it, and he let Las Vegas' circumstances cloud his view of Bowers' ability. He thanked Bowers for 'taking me to the woodshed'.
- 2024-10-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-17]]): Bowers is effectively the Raiders' slot receiver, which is why [[Jakobi Meyers]] cannot move inside. Harmon expects an 'obnoxious amount of targets' post-[[Davante Adams]] trade but is 'a little concerned' about the quality of them — he loved the first two weeks precisely because Adams drew cloud coverage and hard doubles as the X, opening the middle of the field for Bowers to run crossers and stick routes in what Harmon compares to [[Michael Pittman Jr.]]'s usage under [[Gardner Minshew]], who fed Pittman the best season of his career on that exact distribution. With Aidan O'Connell under center and no Adams to bend coverage, Harmon expects it to look different. He grants Bowers is great while calling the surrounding offense not a real offense at all.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: posed as a one-answer question — will Bowers retain and sustain starter-level volume without top weapons in Las Vegas — Waldman said he already has. [[Davante Adams]] 'had one foot out the door' for weeks before the trade and Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) noted [[Jakobi Meyers]] had been invisible for two weeks, yet Las Vegas keeps scheming plays to get Bowers open. The evaluative core: 'he runs routes like a wide receiver — you can see that on his tape,' so the quick-hitting throws are always available. What he loses without surrounding talent is the huge plays and soft matchups. As long as [[Gardner Minshew]] gives them a chance to win, 'even though he may also be giving them reasons to lose at the same time, he's going to find Bowers early and often.'
- 2024-10-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-24]]) [Dynasty]: on film Bowers is closer to Aaron Hernandez's all-around talent than most tight ends Waldman has evaluated, though he will never reach that level and will never be a great in-line blocker — but he blocks better than [[Sam LaPorta]], is stronger, and is more dynamic as a receiver at the catch point and after the catch, while LaPorta is the better one-on-one route runner. Waldman calls Jordan Reed the best comp (a true H-back with grace, speed and separation off the line) and says Bowers leans finesse like Keith Jackson rather than Shockey's run-you-over style. Key caveat: without [[Davante Adams]], Las Vegas is schemed to use Bowers as an extension of the run game on easy throws rather than as a downfield option, and [[Gardner Minshew]] sits on the Mason-Dixon line between good and bad quarterback play — Waldman would take Bowers over [[Travis Kelce]] right now if he were in Kansas City with [[Patrick Mahomes]]. Co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, not a tracked expert) disagrees on the historical ranking: Mike Ditka's 1961 (1,076 yards, 12 TDs in 14 games) is untouchable, Shockey opened more doors for the 2002 Giants than Bowers is for the 2024 Raiders, and Bowers is currently basically the same player as Jordan Reed, trending toward third-best rookie tight end season ever behind Ditka and Shockey. Both agree Bowers is ahead of LaPorta and [[Kyle Pitts]] through the first half of a rookie season, and that from a fantasy/PPR perspective Bowers will eclipse Shockey's production.
- 2024-12-30 — According to Bob Harris (co-host, NOT a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-30]]): Bowers is the centerpiece of his argument that pass catchers do not need good quarterback play. Bowers broke Mike Ditka's 63-year-old rookie tight end receiving yardage record (1,076) with 1,144 yards and counting, passed the rookie record for receptions at any position (105, set in 2023) and broke the Raiders single-season reception record set by Darren Waller, all while playing with a series of nondescript quarterbacks. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees with the broader argument and says he has always ignored advice to avoid receivers with poor quarterback play, especially rookies, but adds the caveat that a ceiling difference exists — Bowers, [[Trey McBride]] and [[Malik Nabers]] would all be nearer career years with high-quality quarterbacks feeding them more effectively.
- 2025-01-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-06]]): co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) argued the Raiders' offensive staff — Luke Getsy and the Turners' predecessors, then O'Connell — plus [[Gardner Minshew]] deserve credit for not getting in the way of Bowers breaking Mike Ditka's rookie tight end record and setting the all-time rookie reception mark at any position, and used it as a partial defense of the situation Antonio Pierce oversaw. Waldman pushed back on how much credit the environment deserves, noting [[Kyle Pitts]] also had a very good first season and what has happened since, and that [[Trey McBride]] should be putting up numbers like that but is saddled with his quarterback situation. Both agreed Bowers himself is the main reason for the record.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]) [Dynasty]: asked to start a franchise with Bowers or [[Trey McBride]], Waldman takes Bowers, but stressed it is close and that he would be very happy with McBride. Bowers has 'that little bit extra' explosion; the rest is context — Las Vegas essentially fell apart around him, cycling three quarterbacks and losing [[Davante Adams]], which put even more pressure on getting him the ball, and the Raiders still succeeded in doing so. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed, noting Bowers 'does not look the part' physically but carries a best-player-on-the-field vibe every time he watches. In the Raiders rebuild segment, Waldman named Bowers and Max Crosby as essentially the only two pieces on the roster to build around.
- 2025-02-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-04]]): the Chip Kelly hire in Las Vegas is a schematic positive for Bowers. Harmon walked back a joking tweet about Bowers in the 'Jordan Matthews role' but stands by the substance — Kelly's short passing game is built to activate a movable chess piece as the first read on RPO and play-action concepts, which is how the Raiders were already using Bowers early in 2024, comparing it to how [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] has been deployed. He points to Jeremy Kerley leading Kelly's 2016 49ers in targets (115 targets, 64 catches, just over 600 yards) as the archetype of who eats on quick-hitting concepts in this system, and expects Bowers to thrive specifically on big in-breaking routes while flexing between slot and tight end. Caveat: Harmon notes the Raiders have no quarterback and no perimeter threat to clear space underneath.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): the [[Geno Smith]] trade does not limit Bowers at all — Waldman said Bowers is 'pretty much playing at his ceiling' already, and if anything the quarterback upgrade opens the door to another strong year and a solid sophomore season rather than a slump. The mechanism is the offense operating more on schedule with a legitimate starter under center.
- 2025-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-07]]): Bowers is the exception to Harmon's tight-end skepticism — a player who can line up everywhere and do everything, unlike [[Kyle Pitts]] or [[Dalton Kincaid]]. But Harmon added a genuine caveat rather than a blanket endorsement: he is not saying Bowers is overrated, yet part of him wonders what happens if Las Vegas ever adds real receiving threats on the perimeter. If the Raiders had two good perimeter receivers, he questions whether Bowers still commands roughly 150 targets, because most NFL passing games conceptually do not flow through the tight end. The upside case therefore leans partly on the surrounding room staying thin.
- 2025-06-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-04]]): with Chip Kelly saying the Raiders want to 'remain amorphous' and reporting that Bowers and [[Michael Mayer]] could get slot and outside receiver work, Harmon backs Bowers as the piece that makes it credible — he was over two yards per route run on wide targets last year, so he can genuinely line up out there. Answering co-host James Koh's (not tracked) objection that the amorphous approach only ever worked for New England because they had Gronkowski, Harmon's response is that Las Vegas has Bowers. He concedes Gronkowski was a far better inline blocker and calls him the best tight end in history, but argues Bowers, as a smaller and more athletic player, may move around the formation better than Gronkowski could.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): he is buying high on Bowers at a mid-second-round ADP after the historic rookie season, because Bowers should only improve and now has a better quarterback in [[Geno Smith]]. The structural argument is the second tight end: with Michael Mayer, defenses knew he was the blocker or short-range zone option and not a man-coverage threat, so alignments told them nothing; adding [[Jack Bech]] as a credible move tight end/blocker means two-tight-end looks create genuine confusion and open Bowers up. Waldman projects close to 1,200 receiving yards and bordering on 10 touchdowns, expecting Bowers to lead the team in receiving yards by two or three hundred. He owns shares at the mid-second and a first-round share or two from early in the season, and expects first-round cost in tight end premium. Caveat: he concedes other tight ends get you close enough at cheaper prices, so the leverage argument, not the projection, is what justifies the price.
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-13]]): his Almanac profile opens 'Don't draft him' — not a knock on the player but on the price. Harris calls Bowers essentially a big slot receiver (79 of 112 catches came from the slot or split wide), not an elite route runner but a 243-pounder who 'runs like a deer', great balance, a tackle breaker. He credits the record rookie year (breaking Ditka's 63-year-old rookie receiving-yard mark) while arguing the volume was a symptom of a 'sclerotic' Raiders offense: over a quarter of their terrible quarterbacks' passes and 16%+ of all offensive plays went to Bowers, the highest share of any 2024 pass catcher — and the team still went 4-13 with a bottom-five offense, then replaced the staff with [[Pete Carroll]] and [[Geno Smith]]. See [[Value-Based Drafting and Early Tight End Cost]] for the numbers.
- 2025-08-15 — According to Cousin Josh (untracked co-host of the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast]]) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-15]]): Bowers is his number 1 frag player at ADP 16 — explicitly not a knock on the player, whom he loves and would happily own in [[Dynasty]], but on the roster structure. The value-based-drafting math: Bowers scored 141 points as TE2 and beat replacement-level [[David Njoku]] at TE11 by 66 points, while the receivers and backs going around pick 16 ([[A.J. Brown]], [[Drake London]], [[Josh Jacobs]]) can beat their own replacement level by far more. He also notes [[George Kittle]] outscored Bowers last year and goes 20 picks later, and [[Trey McBride]] 15 picks later. [[Chris Harris]] went further: investing heavily at tight end is a losing strategy, you do not win championships by counting the positions you won, and tight end premium does not fix it — an extra half point per catch is only about 30 points of edge for Bowers over Njoku against a gap measured in hundreds for [[Ja'Marr Chase]] or [[Saquon Barkley]]. Only a two-tight-end superflex would create real scarcity, and that is a miserable league. Josh prefers acquiring Bowers by mid-season trade once his roster is playoff-bound.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Bowers is his No. 2 bust purely on draft cost, not talent — Harris says he loves Bowers, calls him a really great player and would be thrilled if his Patriots had him. His position is categorical: no tight end should ever be taken in the first or second round; it is bad math. You are not in fantasy to win a position, and winning tight end every week means losing another. He credits the market for pushing Bowers mostly into the second round rather than the first, where 'the hipsters' take him, and cites [[Travis Kelce]] at fifth overall a couple of years ago as a worse version of the same error. Guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not tracked) would not be caught dead taking Bowers in the second but does not find it insane, noting [[Trey McBride]] can reach that tier and [[George Kittle]] finished as TE1 last year — and framing the math as a legendary tight end season being about 1,200 yards, which is the floor for the receivers going in that range.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]]: Bowers is a feature piece of the Raiders offense alongside [[Ashton Jeanty]], with the receivers as complementary pieces. Nguyen stressed Bowers is not a true in-line tight end but a slot-receiver type — an archetype that can end up as merely a 'slow slot' — yet Bowers has proven he is a matchup problem who produces without being a real blocker. His 2024 production came with no help at all: the Raiders were horrendous running the ball, so he had no play-action threat and was simply beating his matchups. Nguyen added that if [[Michael Mayer]] develops into a genuinely good edge blocker, Bowers can flank or wing outside him and move around more, making Las Vegas more dangerous running to the edge.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): Bowers is guest Ben Gretch's (Stealing Signals, NOT tracked) number four 'might be wrong about' — Gretch is very in, citing the rookie receiving record (breaking [[Puka Nacua]]'s from the year before, more catches than any rookie at any position), a true-freshman year at Georgia where he led a national title team in receiving over [[Ladd McConkey]], [[Adonai Mitchell]] and [[Jermaine Burton]] roughly two to one (800s vs 400s), and improvement in receiving yards per game each college year. He thinks the trajectory is potentially Hall of Fame, but worries about the year-two pullback seen with [[Sam LaPorta]] and [[Kyle Pitts]], a new coordinator and quarterback, and that [[Trey McBride]] existing as an elite second tight end undercuts the price. Harris laughs that Bowers is on his bust list too — 'I would never ever take a tight end at 15': over the past decade, only 4 of 24 tight ends drafted in the first three rounds outperformed their ADP on value-based drafting. His point is Bowers must gap the replacement-level TE9/TE10, not the TE2, and even Bowers' record-setting rookie year with five touchdowns graded out as roughly the 26th pick in PPR. He concedes 11 touchdowns instead of five changes the maths. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): Harris initially rates Bowers an 8 out of 10 but is explicit that the number is entirely about the cost to his roster build of taking a tight end in the second round, not about the player, whom he loves. Guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates him a 2, arguing Bowers could lead the NFL in receptions and that the hand-wringing over split snaps with [[Michael Mayer]] in practice misreads the usage — the plays where Bowers is not lined up at tight end are him in the backfield, out wide or in the slot, all in service of getting him the ball. Harris concedes on air that as a pure player evaluation 'two is probably closer to the right answer than eight' and says he is off on that one.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Bowers opened the game as the centerpiece, with play action to him for 23 yards on the Raiders' first play of a drive that looked like the absolute best case. He then dropped one in the two-minute drill before the half. The important item is that he limped off late in the third quarter with a knee issue and did not return, though he told reporters he was okay and expected to play Week 2 against the Chargers. Harris's response is a wait-and-see.
