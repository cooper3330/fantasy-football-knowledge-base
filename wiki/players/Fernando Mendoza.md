# Fernando Mendoza

## Expert Takes

- 2026-02-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-02]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether Mendoza projects as 'good, good enough, or destined for disaster,' Waldman landed between good and good enough, while not ruling out a rough rookie year if his offensive line doesn't hold up. From what he'd seen in press clippings ahead of starting full film study the next day, he described Mendoza as a good decision-maker who processes information reasonably well and profiles as a 'field general' type -- a [[Kirk Cousins]]-like fit for a Kubiak-style scheme built to make quarterbacks look field-general friendly -- though it could take him a couple of years to get there.
- 2026-02-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-09]]) [Dynasty]: Mendoza may be the highest-graded quarterback he's evaluated in six years, including Caleb Williams's class. Waldman frames the difference as ceiling versus safety: Williams has the higher physical/technical upside to create with arm and legs, but Mendoza 'might be the safer pick' because his trapdoor is smaller. His standout trait is intermediate accuracy -- the 25-35 yard throws between the linebackers and umbrella coverage -- plus mobility (clocked around 19-20 mph, comparable to a good college RB) and pocket climbing/off-platform throwing. Sack concerns are overstated per Waldman: Indiana's scheme often left him with no checkdowns or hot routes, forcing him to buy time behind a leaky line. He compares Mendoza's gangly running style to Bernie Kosar but with real speed. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) flagged Mendoza's toughness -- a national title game touchdown run through injury -- as appealing; Waldman agreed on late-game physicality but wants him sliding more in low-leverage first-half spots, pushing back on the 'Mahomes tucks people all the time' narrative as exaggerated. Likely lands in Las Vegas with Clint Kubiak, where the offensive line needs real work.
- 2026-02-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: Mendoza was his biggest 'surprise in a good way' this cycle -- Waldman hadn't studied him closely until popping on 2024 Cal tape, and came away rating him the number one quarterback he's evaluated in the past six years. He praised aggressive downfield play, a 'good enough' (not great) arm suited well to play-action, and the ability to stay on-script and throw accurately off-platform. The key separator from field-tilted 'whiteboard' quarterbacks like Garrett Nussmeier and Carson Beck: Mendoza doesn't fall apart when a throw has to go into a window with two or three possible defenders -- Waldman said you can 'bet on him' there, where you'd bet against Nussmeier or Beck. He comped Mendoza to a slightly more athletic [[Dak Prescott]], or a Joe Burrow/Matt Ryan blend with more conceptual polish than what people project onto [[Drake May]], plus underrated speed (around 20 mph) despite a gangly running style. [[Brandon Angelo]] added that Mendoza looks far faster live on TV than his tape alone suggests. Framed against [[Caleb Williams]]: Williams is the higher-ceiling, higher-bust-risk option, Mendoza the steadier, more projectable floor.
- 2026-03-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-09]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is fully on board with Mendoza landing in Las Vegas to sit behind a bridge QB for 2026, likening it to a Patrick Mahomes-style rookie redshirt -- he expects Mendoza to see real game action only in the final two or three games (or on injury) before taking over in 2027. Waldman cited Tom Brady's belief in letting quarterbacks sit, plus a Drew Brees theory that a QB needs roughly 50 total games between college and the pros to reveal what he really is, using Brock Purdy's heavy college workload and quick NFL acclimation versus Sam Darnold's long, winding path as contrasting examples of the same idea. Waldman separately said he'd rather Las Vegas start Kyler Murray on a one-year minimum deal in front of Mendoza than pay Malik Willis $20-30M/year, since Mendoza doesn't need a stylistic mentor and Murray could even serve as a cautionary 'reverse barometer.'
- 2026-03-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (untracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-10]]): [[Fernando Mendoza]], the Indiana product anticipated to go first overall to the Raiders, is set to pair with new OC Clint Kubiak's staff -- including new OC/QB coach Andrew Janocko, who previously coached Derek Carr, Kirk Cousins and Justin Fields. Both hosts liked the coaching pairing and framed it as a fresh start after a dysfunctional 2025 in which competing front-office/coaching visions derailed the receiving corps. No specific evaluation of Mendoza's own tape was offered beyond the staff fit.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Mendoza is the safest quarterback prospect in the class -- not a blue-chip talent like Caleb Williams, but a Dak Prescott-type processor per Angelo, who reads the field well, makes quick decisions and is physically tougher than his arm strength alone suggests, with an early-Prescott-like willingness to take on unnecessary hits as a runner. Waldman said Mendoza is a better decision-maker in the pocket than Drake May without quite the arm, and gave him as high a grade as any quarterback prospect in the past four to five years, projecting he can survive a bad situation the way Caleb Williams did as a rookie. Both flagged the Raiders' post-Max Crosby-trade offensive-line uncertainty as a real risk but remain optimistic about his floor as a starter. In superflex rookie drafts, both have him as the pick at 1.02/1.04 once the top two receivers (Tate/Lemon) are gone.
- 2026-04-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-06]]) [Dynasty]: responding to co-host Bob Harris relaying a colleague's 'as elite as it gets' take on Indiana-to-Raiders QB Fernando Mendoza, Waldman says he's closer to 'feel it' than 'fuck it.' Of the quarterbacks he's scouted over the past seven years, Mendoza is one of only two he's felt could start right away and survive a bad situation. He has 35 college starts across two programs and is nearly a season away from the 50-start threshold Waldman references (via the Drew Brees idea) as the point a QB has truly seen enough to play high-level football. Waldman praises his arm talent, mobility, ability to throw on the move and off-script, play-action fit, accuracy in clutch situations, and toughness (cites a title-game touchdown run), and reports pro-day observers thought he looked bigger (6'5', 236, possibly +5-10 lbs). His comp sits between Andrew Luck and Justin Herbert -- moves more like Luck, not as fluid as Herbert, but flashes Herbert's best moments with fewer of Herbert's mistakes.
- 2026-04-09 — According to Paul Pertichese, guest analyst on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-09]] (not a tracked expert) [Dynasty, Superflex]: Asked whether superflex/2-QB dynasty formats change the calculus toward Fernando Mendoza over [[Jeremiah Love]], Pertichese said he'd be tempted a bit more than by WR [[Carnell Tate]], but not enough to actually take Mendoza over Love, because Mendoza lacks rushing 'cheat code' ability -- his best-case outcome is roughly QB6-QB10 production, which would likely require a Joe Burrow-level 38-40 TD season to hit. Pertichese personally comps Mendoza's ceiling to a Jared Goff/Matt Ryan type: a good, not elite-elite, passer.
- 2026-04-16 — According to Dalton Wasserman (guest draft analyst, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: [[Fernando Mendoza]] isn't in the Trevor Lawrence/Caleb Williams prospect tier but projects at the top of the 2024 QB2 group (Michael Penix Jr., J.J. McCarthy, Bo Nix), and there's a case he'd have gone ahead of Cam Ward last year. Wasserman's top selling point is Mendoza's year-over-year progression from his first year at Cal through his final season at Indiana -- footwork, mechanics and decision-making all improved incrementally, which is why his accuracy graded so well this season. Projection: a top-8-to-10 quarterback by the end of his rookie contract, the same range prime Kirk Cousins occupied -- notable since Cousins will mentor him as a veteran on the Raiders. The real swing factor isn't Mendoza but GM John Spytek building the offensive line, finding a true number-one receiver (Brock Bowers already helps at tight end), and a defense that keeps Mendoza from having to carry the team early -- plus head coach Clint Kubiak, whom Wasserman rates below several other NFL play-callers. Both hosts flagged one risk: Mendoza's willingness to stand in and take hits to protect his footwork/accuracy could get him hurt or degrade his mechanics if the line struggles.
- 2026-05-11 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-11]]) [Dynasty]: in a superflex trade scenario where an owner holding [[Lamar Jackson]] and [[Aaron Rodgers]] moved up to the 1.02 to draft Mendoza and was then offered [[Cam Ward]] plus an ancillary piece like Gunnar Helm in return for Mendoza, Waldman said he'd still hold Mendoza 'probably every time' in that spot if he were still building. He downplayed the notion that Ward's mobility is a real separator, noting Mendoza is mobile in his own right even though his rushing production at Indiana looked awkward -- part of the broader knock (echoed with [[Brendan Soresby]]'s comps) that Mendoza 'looked like Bernie Kosar with a jet pack' running the ball despite piling up yardage. Waldman continues to treat Mendoza as a top dynasty rookie QB target.
- 2026-07-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-20]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman expects [[Fernando Mendoza]] to take over as the Raiders' starter only once [[Kirk Cousins]] and Las Vegas fall out of AFC West contention, likely mid-to-late season, and said whether he still has access to targets like [[Brock Bowers]] at that point is the key thing to track.
- 2026-07-24 — According to Chris Harris and Andy Behrens (untracked co-host) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-24]]) [Dynasty]: Harris comps Mendoza to Jared Goff as a prospect (calling it 'maybe even a little aggressive'), describing him as an option/half-field-read quarterback in the mold of an immobile Jalen Hurts, and noting he struggled specifically against the tougher 'meat grinder' defenses he faced in college despite an outstanding, 'craziest season of our lifetimes' final year. Both hosts think the Raiders' public messaging that they want [[Kirk Cousins]] to start the whole season is designed to avoid a daily quarterback-competition news cycle rather than evidence Mendoza can't win the job; Harris cites Jackson Dart forcing his way into a starting job for the Giants last year as a comp and sees no reason Mendoza, the No. 1 overall pick, couldn't do the same with a strong camp/preseason. Behrens says he'd get more interested in the Raiders' skill-position pieces if Mendoza wins or nearly wins the job, since a competent rookie starter would be better for the offense's outlook than 'late-career Kirk Cousins' -- even though they note late-Cousins can still be usable, citing his track record propping up Kyle Pitts.
