# Zach Charbonnet

## Expert Takes

- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman called [[Zach Charbonnet]] 'not bad' behind [[Kenneth Walker III]] in Seattle's backfield but sees him primarily as a cap on Walker's ceiling rather than a standout ceiling play himself, and expects both backs to keep sliding in ADP as new OC Ryan Grubb's pass-heavy scheme (imported from Washington) draws more camp buzz through the summer.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: temper the Charbonnet enthusiasm. Bob Condotta of the Seattle Times told Waldman to forget about him — as long as [[Kenneth Walker III]] is healthy, you will not see anybody else — and Waldman thinks Charbonnet investors will not like the outcome, while [[Kenneth Walker III]] is probably being under-drafted. This corroborates his own RSP data work: running his prospect grades against fantasy success rates over six years, Charbonnet was one of the players whose chance of hitting looked weaker than his (good) prospect grade implied on the correlation data. The standing caveat is that NFL depth matters because injuries happen.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): Charbonnet is the known quantity in the Seattle backfield — 'you know exactly what you're going to get' — in contrast to [[Kenneth Walker III]], who Waldman says presses and swings for the home run behind a poor offensive line. Because Charbonnet is nipping at Walker's heels, Waldman said it is very possible Charbonnet ends up the better of the two in Klint Kubiak's scheme, even while calling Walker the superior perimeter fit on talent.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]): Waldman is projecting a Seattle committee, driven by how well Charbonnet played last year and by reporting that the staff is concerned about [[Kenneth Walker III]] staying healthy — enough that he wants Charbonnet on the field more. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) pushes back hard: per Greg Bell of the Tacoma News Tribune this is a Walker featured role with Charbonnet spelling him, and Mike Clay's split across 11 shared games was 19% of carries and 8% of targets for Charbonnet — worth about 6.5 fantasy points per game — against Walker's 68% and 14%. Harris also notes the only positive Charbonnet quote he has seen came on a day Walker was sitting out, and says a good weekend practice does not move him off Walker. Both agree Charbonnet is capable if something happens or the roles evolve.
- 2025-08-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he deliberately did not move the Seattle ranks despite the new offensive coordinator's flattering, innocuous comment about Charbonnet and a beat reporter's follow-on that he will have a significant role, which the fantasy world was already inflating into Charbonnet-is-the-starter. Harris's read is that the number two back does have a significant role and the rest is interpreting coach speak. He repeated his Almanac line — Charbonnet is [[Kenneth Walker III]] but fast, or rather what would happen if Walker lost all his speed — while acknowledging Charbonnet has had a good August and Walker has missed a lot of it with a foot injury. He said a genuine leak naming Charbonnet the starter would force a ranks change.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon called Charbonnet a really good back who steps in whenever [[Kenneth Walker III]] is unavailable, but noted Seattle has consistently declined to mix him in under both the Pete Carroll staff and the current one — it is Walker until he gets hurt, with Charbonnet on the shelf. Harmon questioned that approach given Walker's injury history, but the practical read is that Charbonnet's standalone value stays contingent.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Charbonnet is ranked well behind [[Kenneth Walker III]] for Week 1 by both Harris and guest Andy Behrens (not tracked), who treat the practice reports of Charbonnet running ahead of Walker as exactly the kind of preseason cue that should not move a rank. Harris cited it as his example of bad analysis — a bullet point recited quickly and confidently — and said he will not rank Charbonnet as the starter until he sees it in a game that counts. Behrens allowed the possibility of a shock in which Walker is no longer the starter, but said he is siding with the player he believes is both the incumbent and the superior runner.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Film & Theory Sequences, Signal, Steelers)]]): Waldman used [[Zach Charbonnet]] as his example of a clear-cut handcuff worth spending aggressively on. In a hypothetical where Kenneth Walker III went down for the year, Charbonnet is a clean, unambiguous successor and going super strong early with FAAB is justified — as opposed to a muddy Rams-style scenario where a Kyren Williams injury would leave Blake Corum and Jarquez Hunter competing, with managers going all-in on one or the other and probably wasting budget. Harstad agreed that an injury with an obvious beneficiary is exactly the situation where a fast, aggressive reaction is merited even though the general lesson of the episode is not to overreact.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Charbonnet, whom Harris likes to call Sab Cav, played 29 snaps to 20 for [[Kenneth Walker III]], snaked away the goal-line touchdown, and still out-played him. Guest **Sam Gold** (not tracked) says what worries him most is that Seattle clearly seems to trust Charbonnet in the red zone and gave him those snaps, which means far more touchdown opportunity going forward. Gold's film note is that Charbonnet is at his best when he can build a full head of steam and hit top speed, which showed on a few early runs of six to eight extra yards where he made a man miss by lowering a shoulder or bouncing off a tackle, opportunities Walker simply did not get in this game.
