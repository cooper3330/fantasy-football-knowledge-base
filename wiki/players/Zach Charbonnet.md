# Zach Charbonnet

## Expert Takes

- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman called [[Zach Charbonnet]] 'not bad' behind [[Kenneth Walker III]] in Seattle's backfield but sees him primarily as a cap on Walker's ceiling rather than a standout ceiling play himself, and expects both backs to keep sliding in ADP as new OC Ryan Grubb's pass-heavy scheme (imported from Washington) draws more camp buzz through the summer.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: temper the Charbonnet enthusiasm. Bob Condotta of the Seattle Times told Waldman to forget about him — as long as [[Kenneth Walker III]] is healthy, you will not see anybody else — and Waldman thinks Charbonnet investors will not like the outcome, while [[Kenneth Walker III]] is probably being under-drafted. This corroborates his own RSP data work: running his prospect grades against fantasy success rates over six years, Charbonnet was one of the players whose chance of hitting looked weaker than his (good) prospect grade implied on the correlation data. The standing caveat is that NFL depth matters because injuries happen.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): Charbonnet is the known quantity in the Seattle backfield — 'you know exactly what you're going to get' — in contrast to [[Kenneth Walker III]], who Waldman says presses and swings for the home run behind a poor offensive line. Because Charbonnet is nipping at Walker's heels, Waldman said it is very possible Charbonnet ends up the better of the two in Klint Kubiak's scheme, even while calling Walker the superior perimeter fit on talent.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]): Waldman is projecting a Seattle committee, driven by how well Charbonnet played last year and by reporting that the staff is concerned about [[Kenneth Walker III]] staying healthy — enough that he wants Charbonnet on the field more. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) pushes back hard: per Greg Bell of the Tacoma News Tribune this is a Walker featured role with Charbonnet spelling him, and Mike Clay's split across 11 shared games was 19% of carries and 8% of targets for Charbonnet — worth about 6.5 fantasy points per game — against Walker's 68% and 14%. Harris also notes the only positive Charbonnet quote he has seen came on a day Walker was sitting out, and says a good weekend practice does not move him off Walker. Both agree Charbonnet is capable if something happens or the roles evolve.
- 2025-08-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he deliberately did not move the Seattle ranks despite the new offensive coordinator's flattering, innocuous comment about Charbonnet and a beat reporter's follow-on that he will have a significant role, which the fantasy world was already inflating into Charbonnet-is-the-starter. Harris's read is that the number two back does have a significant role and the rest is interpreting coach speak. He repeated his Almanac line — Charbonnet is [[Kenneth Walker III]] but fast, or rather what would happen if Walker lost all his speed — while acknowledging Charbonnet has had a good August and Walker has missed a lot of it with a foot injury. He said a genuine leak naming Charbonnet the starter would force a ranks change.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon called Charbonnet a really good back who steps in whenever [[Kenneth Walker III]] is unavailable, but noted Seattle has consistently declined to mix him in under both the Pete Carroll staff and the current one — it is Walker until he gets hurt, with Charbonnet on the shelf. Harmon questioned that approach given Walker's injury history, but the practical read is that Charbonnet's standalone value stays contingent.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Charbonnet is ranked well behind [[Kenneth Walker III]] for Week 1 by both Harris and guest Andy Behrens (not tracked), who treat the practice reports of Charbonnet running ahead of Walker as exactly the kind of preseason cue that should not move a rank. Harris cited it as his example of bad analysis — a bullet point recited quickly and confidently — and said he will not rank Charbonnet as the starter until he sees it in a game that counts. Behrens allowed the possibility of a shock in which Walker is no longer the starter, but said he is siding with the player he believes is both the incumbent and the superior runner.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Film & Theory Sequences, Signal, Steelers)]]): Waldman used [[Zach Charbonnet]] as his example of a clear-cut handcuff worth spending aggressively on. In a hypothetical where Kenneth Walker III went down for the year, Charbonnet is a clean, unambiguous successor and going super strong early with FAAB is justified — as opposed to a muddy Rams-style scenario where a Kyren Williams injury would leave Blake Corum and Jarquez Hunter competing, with managers going all-in on one or the other and probably wasting budget. Harstad agreed that an injury with an obvious beneficiary is exactly the situation where a fast, aggressive reaction is merited even though the general lesson of the episode is not to overreact.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Charbonnet, whom Harris likes to call Sab Cav, played 29 snaps to 20 for [[Kenneth Walker III]], snaked away the goal-line touchdown, and still out-played him. Guest **Sam Gold** (not tracked) says what worries him most is that Seattle clearly seems to trust Charbonnet in the red zone and gave him those snaps, which means far more touchdown opportunity going forward. Gold's film note is that Charbonnet is at his best when he can build a full head of steam and hit top speed, which showed on a few early runs of six to eight extra yards where he made a man miss by lowering a shoulder or bouncing off a tackle, opportunities Walker simply did not get in this game.
- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Guest Dave Kluge (Football Guys, not a tracked expert) pairs Charbonnet and [[Kenneth Walker III]] as his number two Week 2 quandary — they are now back to back in his ranks at the RB2/RB3 bubble, having been a bench/start split in Week 1. Charbonnet looked like the better back, and Kluge offers three readings he cannot choose between: Walker is still limited by a preseason injury (he was run down from behind by Nick Bosa, which is not typical), the game plan wanted Charbonnet's thumping style against a physical San Francisco front, or it is an official changing of the guard under a new coaching staff and coordinator. Harris admits he copped out, ranking Charbonnet 27 and Walker 29 in standard but Walker 28 and Charbonnet 31 in PPR. Harris and Sam Gold noted on Monday's show that Walker kept getting met in the backfield on unblocked plays while Charbonnet ran when there was room — possibly bad luck rather than vision.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-15]]): Charbonnet is a real week-to-week threat to [[Kenneth Walker III]]'s workload but Waldman argues his Week 2 struggles were scheme, not talent — Seattle ran him mostly to the perimeter into even or minus-one box counts and he was stuffed repeatedly, while Walker's downhill runs came with a plus-one to plus-two advantage. Waldman noted a Field Gulls film breakdown crediting Charbonnet with doing the things Klint Kubiak's scheme asks (hitting the hole rather than hunting the cutback) and said he expects Seattle to eventually discover they should run Charbonnet downhill too, which would keep the split live. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) added that in Week 1 the pattern was reversed — Walker inside was not working and Charbonnet outside was.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): Charbonnet out-snapped [[Kenneth Walker III]] 35 to 23 but went 15 carries for 10 yards to Walker's 13 for 105 — the exact opposite of Week 1, when Charbonnet got the well-blocked plays and the lanes and Walker was simply being met by defenders. He was also the one thrown to the wolves on three impossible carries running out the clock. Harris calls him a strong, good player who can power in touchdowns and be good for fantasy when the breaks fall his way, but says Walker should be rated a little ahead of him in any given week for play-to-play explosiveness. Genuinely split job with real variance.
- 2025-09-19 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-19]]): Charbonnet is dealing with a foot problem and had not practiced as of Thursday of week three — one more missed practice on Friday and you can assume he is out. If he sits, Harris says [[Kenneth Walker III]] goes back to being the main man in Seattle at least for a week, and the post-week-one 'seismic shift' in that backfield looks 'more like a seismic blip.'
- 2025-09-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Charbonnet was maybe going to return for Thursday night but Harris said the messages were mixed as of recording, and he ranked Charbonnet well below [[Kenneth Walker III]] for Week 4 both because of the injury and because he thinks Walker is the better player, saying he is forgetting Week 1 a little. Guest Ben Gretch (Stealing Signals, not tracked) agreed in one word that he would rather use Walker. Harris also read a listener email noting 'charbonnet' is French slang for grinding or working hard like a coal miner, and adopted 'the coal miner' as Charbonnet's new nickname on the show.
- 2025-09-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-26]]): Charbonnet isn't going away — he scored on the drive before halftime while [[Kenneth Walker III]] was benched for the ball-throwing penalty, and scored again in the fourth quarter before it was called back on a shaky holding penalty on [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]]. Some of his plays were called into no space at all. But Harris is clear he doesn't offer the same pop as Walker, repeating his Almanac description: imagine Kenneth Walker, but slow. The Seattle backfield now looks pretty clearly illustrated with Walker leading.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Charbonnet out-snapped [[Kenneth Walker III]] and was the only touchdown scorer in the box score, but Harris stresses that came on a goal-line carry handed to him after Walker's electric 31-yard run left Walker gassed — and Walker also gets goal-line work. Harris's shorthand: 'what if Ken Walker but not fast? There's Zach Charbonnet.' He calls him a good player, just the lesser one. Guest **Sam Gold** (NOT a tracked expert) cites minus-1.1 rush yards over expected per attempt, a 17% missed tackle rate and a 16% breakaway rate against Walker's 4.1, 22% and 38%, and says the market 'pulled the gun a little too quickly' after Week 1 when Charbonnet had the better runs and everyone declared him the lead back — though he'll keep vulturing touchdowns and is typically a decent pass-catcher, if not used that way here.
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Chris Raybon (guest, not a tracked expert; [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-13]]): after a month where Kenneth Walker III looked like the more valuable back, Charbonnet reasserted himself as Seattle's clear red-zone and two-minute back, out-snapping Walker 33-18. Raybon said he's viewed Charbonnet as the more talented runner in terms of vision and the traits coaches value all season, even though Walker looks more explosive in space; he pointed to Seattle's weekly snap-share pattern (58/55/47/57/60 range) as evidence the coaching staff has consistently trusted Charbonnet in short-yardage and passing-down roles despite the eye test sometimes favoring Walker's highlight runs. Both hosts said they're re-ranking Charbonnet ahead of Walker after this game, while cautioning both are ultimately flex-only options behind Jaxon Smith-Njigba in a run-first, defense-driven Seattle game plan.
- 2025-10-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-21 (Week 8 Waivers & W7 Reviews)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Snap count was nearly even against Houston (34 for Charbonnet, 29 for [[Kenneth Walker III]]), but Charbonnet got both goal-line opportunities inside the five and converted both, plus a third-and-one look late in the third quarter (stuffed, since Harris says he needs a lane to get going but is a 'road grader' once he has one). Harris said the usage pattern now puts Charbonnet and Walker close together in the running back ranks heading into Seattle's Week 8 bye.
- 2025-10-24 — According to Cousin Josh (not a tracked expert, co-host) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: notes that as [[Kenneth Walker III]] has returned from injury, the Seahawks backfield has settled into a genuine committee -- 'maybe even in favor of Charbonnet' -- which wasn't the expected outcome. He cites Seahawks observer Sam Gold (not a tracked expert) describing Walker as the fast one on film with Charbonnet the more well-rounded, physical back. Chris Harris agreed with the framing of Charbonnet's emergence as a legitimate split rather than a temporary injury-fill role.
- 2025-10-31 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-31]]): Harris and Cousin Josh (co-host, not tracked) agree Charbonnet is a good power runner in the [[David Montgomery]] mold, but both are surprised Seattle has actually followed through on an even, unpredictable split with [[Kenneth Walker III]] all season rather than one back separating -- Josh still believes Walker is the more explosive player and expects Charbonnet's role to shrink if either signs elsewhere or gets hurt, but for now both are low-end flex plays only, ranked in the high teens this week.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): Charbonnet played 18 snaps to [[Kenneth Walker III]]'s 26 in the blowout of Washington and delivered a highlight block, but the in-close work went to Walker this time after Charbonnet had gotten it in Week 7. - 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): Charbonnet replaced [[Kenneth Walker III]] immediately after Walker's first-play reception and tackle, and scored from the six after a false start backed Seattle off the one. Harris's stance is that these two are used almost interchangeably and rankings that separate them by a wide margin every week are 'just generally incorrect', while acknowledging weekly variance.
- 2025-11-21 — According to Chris Harris on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-21]] [Dynasty]: Harris's number-two pick, built on the assumption [[Kenneth Walker III]] leaves Seattle in free agency for a bigger 'cornerstone' contract than the team would pay -- promoting George Holani to backup and Charbonnet to lead back. Harris has long compared Charbonnet to [[David Montgomery]]: not explosive or dynamic, but proven capable of handling a real workload (roughly a 50-50 split with Walker currently, with some signs that split is separating further after Walker's strong Week 12 comeback fourth quarter). Harris pegged him as a hypothetical RB19 if Walker leaves and Holani is just a clear-cut backup, versus his actual current ADP -- Josh noted expert-league consensus has him going around the fourth round (or eighth round in some 'expert' leagues), which both hosts called an inefficiency worth exploiting now.

Harmon frames this as a major offseason red flag for a run-built team whose new offensive coordinator has zero prior play-calling experience, and lists Tyler Allgeier and a cheaper Travis Etienne Jr. as fallback backfield options while Charbonnet recovers.

As of early May, Harmon says there's no clear recovery timeline despite the team's public optimism, and he personally would not bet on Charbonnet being available for the start of the 2026 season, projecting a return more likely in the back half of the year. That opens a real early-season opportunity in Seattle's backfield for rookie [[Jadarian Price]] and for [[George Holani]].

In the final year of his contract, Waldman floated that Charbonnet could even elect to rest and preserve himself for 2027 free agency rather than push back too soon, which would open the door further for [[Jadarian Price]] or [[Emmanuel Wilson]].

- 2026-08-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-31]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Charbonnet's knee injury actually occurred in January, with surgery not until the last week of February, so both hosts are skeptical of optimistic team reporting (credited mainly to Mike McDonald) suggesting he was 'way ahead of schedule' or could be active Week 1 -- Bob Harris says flatly 'the math doesn't work.'
