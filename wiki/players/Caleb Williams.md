# Caleb Williams

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — Guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Dynasty]: still his top overall 2024 quarterback prospect, but explicitly calls him "the most boom-bust top prospect at the position I've graded in a few years." Elite at buying time and off-schedule playmaking, with an aspirational ceiling comp of Aaron Rodgers — but the stated floor risk is real: a refusal to check the ball down or take a coached "small loss" rather than force a low-value big play, a pattern Harms also flags in [[Kyler Murray]]'s game. The downside comps if that habit doesn't get coached out are Jay Cutler or [[Drew Lock]] — talented but turnover/decision-making risks who never fully corrected the same instinct. Notes a real situational risk on top of the player evaluation: if Chicago drafts him into the same coaching/coordinator instability that derailed Justin Fields's development, the outcome could look similar regardless of Williams's superior talent — logged as context, not part of the player grade itself.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: his own independent view, corroborating guest Daniel Harms's 2024-01-15 "most boom-bust top prospect in years" framing — "the mentals are not always quite there on the field all the time with Caleb," though his ceiling remains higher than [[Anthony Richardson]]'s. Distinguishes the two explicitly as the class's top boom/bust-style prospects: Richardson is closer to "all boom" because Waldman rates his mental processing more settled than Williams's, even though Williams's ceiling grade is the higher of the two.

- 2024-02-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-05]]) [Dynasty]: a landing-spot-specific worry, prompted by Cliff Kingsbury's move to become Washington's OC and speculation the team could trade up from 1.02 to 1.01 for Williams. Waldman: "not that fanciful" a scenario, but worries it points at "the worst end of Caleb Williams to start his career" — more [[Kyler Murray]]-esque, "running around like a chicken with its head cut off" rather than playing structured football, echoing Kyler Murray's own bail-the-pocket-on-proximity tendency already logged on this wiki. Separately flags Kingsbury's receiver usage in Arizona (static alignments, no motion or shifts) as a scheme concern independent of the quarterback question.
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]) [Dynasty]: asked directly whether mock drafts uniformly slotting Williams/[[Drake Maye]]/[[Jayden Daniels]] 1-2-3 will all deliver on their draft capital — "hell no." Puts real odds on two of the three eventually hitting, but says "most likely only one" does, and that one is "most likely" Williams, ranking the trio Williams-Daniels-Maye without ruling out other quarterbacks sliding in between them in actual draft order. Restates that he doesn't dislike this quarterback class, but that the modern NFL's pattern of starting rookies too early without developing them means every passer in it — Williams included — carries real bust risk. His specific Williams bust comp: Jay Cutler or Jeff George, "body by Porsche, brain by Mattel" — an unbelievable physical/arm-talent package paired with mistakes that suggest the mental side isn't fully formed, especially if he lands with a staff that can't build the right infrastructure around him. Closing archetype read: "an aspiring Aaron Rodgers," but a more likely outcome, if he lands in a middling situation, is closer to a better version of [[Kyler Murray]].

- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]) [Dynasty]: reaffirms Chicago as a near-certainty and calls him "by far the best quarterback in this class," the most NFL-ready of the group, with a real bust risk attached mainly to organizational mismanagement — cites the Bears' history with Justin Fields and Jay Cutler as the pattern to worry about, not the player's talent. Pushes back explicitly on the recurring "next Patrick Mahomes" media comp (also previously hung on Zach Wilson and Drew Lock): argues it says more about analysts not wanting to admit they missed on Mahomes pre-draft than about real stylistic overlap, and that Williams's actual game — "the way that he moves and plays" — reads much closer to an "aspiring Aaron Rodgers."

- 2024-03-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-08]]) [Dynasty], solo cast covering the full QB class: reaffirms Williams as this class's only true dynasty priority pick — "if you're going to take Caleb Williams, he's the top priority pick... I don't think there's a one-two answer" — and the only 2024 quarterback he'd trust as a Day 1 starter without a redshirt runway: "safe enough to put on the field... begin to thrive over time." Acknowledges real careless-decision-making flashes (cites the Notre Dame game) and a tendency to lean on arm talent in college, but credits him for visibly learning from mistakes within and across games. Restates the Aaron Rodgers — not Patrick Mahomes — stylistic comp: both throw well off unconventional platforms, but Mahomes's is rooted in his baseball background, where Rodgers's (and Williams's) is not. Says Williams is more conceptually advanced than [[Justin Fields]] and should have an easier time in Chicago even under a "ham-handed" development approach — but separately volunteers he'd personally rather see the Bears keep Fields as their QB and trade the No. 1 pick for more draft capital, if that were on the table.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]), reacting to Chicago trading for [[Keenan Allen]] to pair with [[D.J. Moore]]: says he can't recall a recent rookie quarterback (Williams presumed, pre-draft at the time) getting paired with a receiver duo this good, calling it a strong situational bump for whoever Chicago ultimately starts at 1.01.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: reaffirms Williams and [[Michael Penix Jr.]] as "the two top quarterbacks in this class," restated while discussing Washington's plans at 1.02.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): his other "problem solver" pick alongside [[Blake Corum]] — praises his ability to "work on script even when it's off script," marrying his natural movement style with what works within structure. Contrasts 2023 tape (protection was inconsistent, forcing "Superman moments" on nearly every play) with a cleaner 2022 in a more stable offense, and says his biggest qualm is that he sometimes tries to be Superman when being "Clark Kent" would be fine — but calls him one of the most naturally gifted, flow-reading athletes in the class.

- 2024-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Feel It or F@#k It* ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-01]]), on whether Chicago can screw him up: "yes," probably, since it's true of "all these quarterbacks" — but says Williams is the one 2024 quarterback he's most confident could withstand mishandling, given how the Bears added [[Keenan Allen]] and Gerald Everett, hired Shane Waldron as OC, and moved on from Luke Getsy — reads it as the organization learning from its mistakes with [[Justin Fields]], though the real test will be in-game management and patience on the field.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]): a detailed pocket-movement contrast with [[Drake Maye]] responding to a fan question about whether the two are really that different — when Williams moves outside the pocket "it's with a purpose," reacting to real pressure rather than inviting it, and he stays efficient and can climb the pocket, keeping his eyes downfield, the same way Baker Mayfield climbs a pocket. Highlight-reel scrambling makes him look panicked, "but that's not the case" — the movement is a last resort used only when actually forced into it, unlike Maye who resorts to escaping unnecessarily more often.
- 2024-04-23 — According to guest QB trainer Will Hewlett ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-23]]): motion-capture testing showed Williams' velocity range (46-56 mph) is the largest variance Hewlett's team has ever measured while still maintaining spin rate at low velocity, and his T-spine rotation was the highest his biomechanist has measured in any athlete, comparable to reports of Patrick Mahomes; Williams grew up a swimmer, not a baseball player, and idolizes Aaron Rodgers, whose 'pop step' footwork he's incorporated in training; Hewlett called him 'as pro-ready as anyone I've ever worked with' and dismissed Johnny Manziel bust comparisons as lazy evaluation given Williams' work ethic and attention to detail.
- 2024-05-02 — According to Brandon Angelo ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): Williams is Angelo's example of a 'perfect' infrastructure landing spot — first overall pick to a Bears team that already invested in receivers, upgraded the offensive line, and has a GM committed to giving him whatever he needs, which Angelo said gives Williams all the tools to be a top-ten quarterback.
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman names Caleb Williams (not Jayden Daniels, contra co-host Bob Harris) as the rookie QB most likely to be a top-12 fantasy QB1 in 2024, and says he'd actively consider drafting Williams as a value play given how far he's fallen in best-ball ADP -- QB18 overall, first quarterback off the board among rookies, behind names like Jared Goff (QB14, off a QB7 finish) and Aaron Rodgers (QB19). Waldman frames that gap as pure positional-depth-driven value rather than a knock on Williams himself, noting Chicago's offensive-line upgrades support the pick.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Dynasty]: Caleb Williams is in the rare ~1% of rookie QBs Waldman is comfortable both starting in the NFL and rostering as a fantasy starter year one, with Matt Eberflus already naming him Week 1 starter over token competition (Tyson Bagent, Austin Reed). Waldman projects 3,500 passing yards as realistic and 4,000 possible if the offense stays healthy, comping his early-career trajectory to Peyton Manning — turnover-prone at first before 'pouring it on' later. He frames the offense as able to support three startable pass-catchers: [[DeAndre Swift]] as a low-end RB2, [[D.J. Moore]] as a true WR2, and [[Keenan Allen]] as a WR3, while noting [[Rome Odunze]] has a real shot to be a low-end WR3 or even push into WR1 range.
- 2024-05-14 — According to Adam Rank ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-14]]), Bears fan and NFL Network personality, not a tracked expert: Rank's realistic passing projection for [[Caleb Williams]]' rookie year is roughly 3,500 yards, expecting Chicago to lean on the run with [[DeAndre Swift]] behind a stacked receiver room (DJ Moore, Keenan Allen, Rome Odunze) rather than let Williams throw it 38-40 times a game. Pressed by host James Koh to commit to a number, Rank ultimately guaranteed Williams throws for 4,000 yards as a rookie, comparing his likely trajectory to C.J. Stroud's 2023 rookie season. Context offered for how big a deal that would be: no Bears quarterback has ever thrown for 4,000 yards in a season — Jay Cutler's career high, with Alshon Jeffery, Brandon Marshall and Matt Forte around him, was under 3,850.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]): Williams regressed noticeably at USC once Kliff Kingsbury arrived as OC after looking like a clear No. 1 pick prospect the year before, though both hosts are unsure how much of that falls on Kingsbury versus a weaker surrounding talent pool and outsized hype; separately, new Bears OC Shane Waldron ran an advanced, aggressive drop-back vertical passing scheme with Geno Smith in Seattle (distinct from typical Shanahan-tree offenses that lean on play-action), which Harmon thinks should be a good scheme fit for Williams.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): asked whether Williams finishes a top-10 fantasy quarterback as a rookie, Waldman said he is starting to feel it — not enough to make it a bet given the quarterbacks available around him, but a clear yes if Williams slips below ADP. He calls Williams a very good improvisational player who can also operate within structure, and dismissed the criticisms circulating as overblown talk from old-school voices or people manufacturing outrage for a paycheck, specifically the Amani Toomer segment attacking Williams over nail polish and over telling teammates to tidy the locker room. Waldman defended that as culture-setting from a player the Bears installed as director of operations, comparing it to a rookie Frank Gore screaming at the 49ers locker room after a loss. He likes the three receiving weapons around him. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) said he thinks Williams is straight up ten times better with a better receiving corps, while Waldman said a healthy [[Jayden Daniels]] is the likelier of the two to hit top 10.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: on 'top-12 fantasy QB this year' — 'I'm feeling it, but fuck where his ADP is.' Williams is QB15 in ADP and Waldman expects that to climb further after his preseason showing; the position is tight enough, and Chicago's offensive line is still not a strength, that paying up for a rookie facing regular pressure is not worth the price. Someone will take the risk, draft a strong team around him and brag about it, and in practice it will probably work — but in theory you can wait and get comparable production later. He would want shares at QB20-plus, not here. He expects [[D.J. Moore]] (WR20), [[Keenan Allen]] (~WR30) and [[Rome Odunze]] (WR38) to climb with him, and echoes Daniel Jeremiah's 'franchise-altering upside' line: Bears fans can start to see it.
- 2024-08-27 — According to host James Koh (not a tracked expert) and guest Derek Klassen (not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): Koh calls Chicago the best setup a rookie quarterback has ever had and projects Williams at 4,285 passing yards, with a low-side projection of 3,900 — he expects Chicago's first-ever 4,000-yard passer if Williams plays all 17. Klassen agrees it is not crazy, noting only about five rookies have done it (Andrew Luck, [[Justin Herbert]], Cam Newton, [[C.J. Stroud]], Jameis Winston) but that Williams is that caliber of prospect, with the 17th game and three 1,000-yard-capable receivers in [[D.J. Moore]], [[Keenan Allen]] and [[Rome Odunze]] plus a solid tight end in Cole Kmet. Klassen's core evaluation: people over-fixated on the backyard, off-platform arm-angle stuff; on true three- and five-step drops Williams plays on time far better than credited, and the loose plays were a function of a bad USC line and constant 20-point deficits. He expects a 'real put together NFL quarterback' who drives the ball over the middle and takes checkdowns, with maybe two silly plays a game.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman totally feels that Williams is the fantasy quarterback people will wish they had drafted more of. He sees flashes of '[[Aaron Rodgers]]-like' improvisational plays, and argues that with three receivers plus [[DeAndre Swift]] out of the backfield, Williams could be a top-five quarterback right now if the offensive line is even moderately better. Untracked co-host Bob Harris cautioned against pinning too much on exhibition play — there will be more pressure and defenses will adjust — but said Daniel Jeremiah's pre-draft 'franchise-altering upside' line, which he had thought hyperbolic, looked within the realm of possibility watching the preseason. Waldman agreed the top-five potential is there while wondering whether the offense will struggle early.
- 2024-09-05 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Film and Theory)]] [Dynasty]: he calls Williams objectively a phenomenal prospect who does very interesting and exciting things, but says of him personally, 'feel like we've seen that movie before.' He mapped it onto Andrew Luck versus Robert Griffin III — Luck was objectively the better prospect and one of the best of all time, but Harstad rooted for Griffin because Griffin was potentially revelatory. Luck was evolutionary; Griffin was revolutionary. He puts Williams on the Luck side of that line, which is a statement about his own interest rather than a bearish projection.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): a rocky, 'objectively horrible' debut — 93 yards on 29 attempts in a Bears win. Harmon's damning stat: on throws where he held the ball longer than 2.5 seconds he went 3-of-13 for 34 yards, a 23.1% completion rate, and he completed zero passes beyond 15 air yards. Harmon calls it 'old boomer analysis but it's the truth' — the raptor-testing-the-fence, get-away-with-it habits from college won't work in the league, and while the arm talent flashed, he needlessly showed it off from bad angles. He flags interior offensive line trouble, with Tennessee rattling him with pressure up the middle, and says bailing out of the pocket rather than evading within it is 'the thing that's going to crush him in the league.' Co-host James Koh (not tracked) argues Williams has arguably the best setup any No. 1 pick has had — [[Keenan Allen]], [[D.J. Moore]] and [[Rome Odunze]], a solid TE room and backfield — so the criticism lands on the QB, and notes the staff tried to script quick throws he wouldn't take.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): asked whether Williams can survive his offensive line, Waldman says yes long term — he will 'eventually develop into becoming a very good quarterback,' and his RSP scouting report specifically flagged that if any quarterback in the class could survive the onslaught of a bad offensive line and a dysfunctional front office like Chicago's, it is Williams. This year, though, 'is going to be rough,' and he separates that developmental confidence from fantasy usefulness: do not start him until the line shows on a consistent basis that it can protect him.
- 2024-09-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-17]]): deeply concerned but not giving up on Williams. Harmon quotes a 0.2 passer rating on attempts over 10 air yards — 3 of 17 for 52 yards, no touchdowns, two interceptions — and Chicago sitting at 2.6 net yards per pass attempt, dragged down by sacks. Per NextGenStats the Texans blitzed Williams on 42% of his dropbacks, their highest rate in two seasons under DeMeco Ryans, and Williams gained 15 yards while taking five sacks on those plays. Harmon puts the blame top-down rather than on the rookie: OC Shane Waldron has no answers to pressure or the blitz, and Harmon compares it directly to the 2023 Seahawks offense Waldron ran, arguing the proof of concept was there before Chicago hired him. Interior offensive line was 'yada-yada'd' and protections are not being set. Koh (not tracked) notes Williams looks excellent when he plants his feet and fires, but his feet get happy as games wear on.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-19]]): Waldman pushed back hard on the editorializing about Williams' postgame interaction with C.J. Stroud, saying observers do not know what they are talking about. He cited an on-record account from Williams' quarterback coach Will Hewlett, given on a podcast in May: after a long session, with no cameras and nobody around, Williams spent an hour helping Hewlett's son — a college quarterback — unprompted. Waldman's read is that a rookie who had just lost, been beaten up behind an offensive line that could not track stunts or twists, and still kept his team in the game may simply not feel like talking, and that competitiveness toward Stroud is a feature. He tied it to a scouting principle: character editorializing usually goes wrong, and the teams that do it well hand it to industrial psychologists or investigators. Co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) added that the only test that matters is whether teammates respond, citing Cam Newton, and said Williams' teammates appear to be buying in.
- 2024-09-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-20]]): the cleanest progress report of the young QBs. Williams is seeing plenty of pressure — much of it stunts the Bears line has not picked up — but he maneuvers the pocket, gets the ball out, scrambles, and works through one to two or three progressions to the right targets. Nothing about the Chicago scheme is hindering the evaluation, which Waldman treats as the key first-window question. Accuracy is not always there yet, but he is 'in the ballpark' and Waldman would only get concerned if accuracy stayed poor as pressure declined. Most important: he still looks like the college player he watched against Colorado and Notre Dame — same occasional confusion against ancillary coverage, but he does not make the same mistake twice, does not let one error spiral, and keeps the team in the game. Contrast with [[Will Levis]], who unravels after one mistake. See [[Rookie Quarterback Evaluation Windows]].
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): both see a steady upward progression from Week 1, which Koh calls horrid and says made Williams the worst quarterback in the NFL that week, worse than [[Bryce Young]], to a Week 3 with 52 attempts and real development. Harmon says the ball placement and accuracy have been good and that Chicago is asking a lot of him, real drop-back concepts and work at the line, which he is handling. The interceptions are classic rookie mistakes under pressure rather than arm-talent problems, and not as bad as [[Will Levis]] turnovers. Harmon has gone 180 degrees on the situation and now does not think it is a good one: he does not love Shane Waldron's scheme, the line has been bad in both phases, and the front office over-invested in weapons ([[D'Andre Swift]] at 1.8 yards per carry, Gerald Everett) instead of the offensive line. Koh floats that Williams may have had too many options early and looked more decisive in Week 3 locking onto a first read; Harmon likes the take.
- 2024-09-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-26 (Win-Now Trades and Jayden Daniels)]]) [Dynasty]: Williams is the counterexample to Washington's 'slow launch' handling of [[Jayden Daniels]] — Chicago is asking him to make the higher-end progression and manipulation decisions without an offensive line, and Waldman says we are seeing how that plays out. He pairs him with [[Anthony Richardson]], who is being asked to do a lot without polished, refined receivers, as evidence that the more demanding early script is the worse developmental environment. Co-host Adam Harstad (Football Guys, NOT tracked) adds the market context: he cites Kevin Cole that when two quarterbacks go back-to-back the earlier pick wins the career comparison only about 55% of the time, so a Williams-over-Daniels prior was never strong, and three good Daniels games are plausibly enough to flip it to roughly 55% Daniels.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): asked whether the Bears were wrong to take Williams over [[Jayden Daniels]], Waldman said 'fuck no' and got heated about the comparison, because the two are in completely different offenses. His analogy: Daniels was handed an electric car where you push a button, enter a destination and it drives you there accounting for traffic, while Williams was handed a pickup truck with the steering column on the wrong side and the manual shifter on the wheel. Williams is asked for pure downfield progression reads and faces far more pressure, so the mistakes show. Waldman argued Williams was the only quarterback in the class with the skills to be forced into 'this craptastic situation' and survive it, and that he is now starting to swim rather than tread water. He invoked Andrew Luck versus Robert Griffin III as the template — Griffin the toast of the town early, Luck the better career — while allowing Daniels may still have a great career.
- 2024-10-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-03]]): asked whether Chicago should have taken [[Jayden Daniels]] instead, Waldman answered emphatically no. His analogy: Williams is driving in Jamaica on cratered roads, up and down hills, column shifter, engine light flashing and a broken gas gauge — a bad offensive line, line-of-scrimmage checks and adjustments he must make himself, and progression reads where he has to manipulate defenses and decide where the ball goes, versus the far more predetermined menu Daniels gets. Waldman believes Williams was the only quarterback in the class capable of surviving that situation without having his career ruined, says his projections and what he has seen of Williams as a decision maker show him doing fine despite hand-wringing in Chicago, and expects both to end up good quarterbacks — with Williams currently overly deflated.
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]): asked whether NFL.com's claim that Williams's London game was one of the most special performances in Bears quarterback history is hyperbolic, Waldman answers 'super hyperbolic.' He has defended Williams all year and loves him, but says 'you haven't seen shit yet' beyond one great back-shoulder seam throw to [[Keenan Allen]] between the linebacker and safety; the rest looked like the same player he told everyone to relax about in September — fine, not special, and against a bad Jacksonville defense. Williams looks like a quarterback who will develop into a really good player but has not yet hit the special throws he made at USC. Waldman also cautions that Bears fans and long-time beat writers are a poor barometer, comparing them to Browns fans held hostage by franchise history.
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): the Bears' staff is not doing Williams any favors — Waldman 'feels it' on the premise that Matt Eberflus and the offensive staff are the problem. He was encouraged by the fourth-quarter adjustments only in the sense that Williams had to play Superman to make them work, because the average pressure rate and average time to throw were both rough. Some formations looked questionable, but Waldman stressed the offensive line is at the heart of it: if it protected Williams reasonably well, several scheme calls might have worked and the play-calling would draw less criticism. The goal-line handoff to a guard with no pro experience taking handoffs — with [[DeAndre Swift]] and [[Roschon Johnson]] available — was 'about as close to inexcusable' as he gets; getting cute in key moments is his bigger concern. He noted Williams still made some really nice throws.
- 2024-11-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-05]]): Harmon declines to make excuses for Williams, who he says has not been consistently accurate and has not played great, but argues most of the Chicago problem is around him. The Shane Waldron scheme is poorly designed — route combinations that do not fit together, pure progression reads regardless of coverage, and no answer for pressure, the same flaw Waldron had in Seattle. Arizona, 31st in pressure rate coming in, generated a season-high 46% pressure rate, six sacks and 16 different players with a pressure, tied for the most by any team in a game since 2018. Williams is also asked to make sight adjustments at the line. Harmon adds an ego-management burden: [[DJ Moore]] on a new extension, [[Keenan Allen]] expecting eight to ten looks, a top-ten rookie in [[Rome Odunze]], plus a tight end and a back on big money. Koh (not tracked) notes Williams has worked to dispel pre-draft diva talk and looked more decisive when Allen was off the field.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) noted Williams has been sacked 38 times, the second-most for a rookie quarterback in Weeks 1-10 since 1970, trailing only David Carr's 49 in 2002 — and asked whether Williams is the Stroud situation but worse. Waldman says there is genuine cause for fear, but that people are 'very short-term in their views about everything.' The Carr outcome is possible; so is the Troy Aikman outcome, where early punishment makes the player better, or the Andrew Luck, Cam Newton or Russell Wilson path — all of whom took heavy early hits. He flags it as a factor to be concerned about, not grounds to write Williams off. Waldman also entertains that the crotchety scouts who doubted Williams pre-draft may yet be right, without endorsing it. See [[Quarterback Pressure and Career Trajectory]].
- 2024-11-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]]): the Bears offense is 'for sure cooked' for 2024 and the live question is whether the remaining reps are more harmful than useful for Williams. Harmon frames it as a thought experiment — if a consulting firm asked how to ruin a genuinely good quarterback prospect, you would do exactly what Chicago has done: a bad offensive line, an offensive coordinator with a history of not fixing bad lines, and a defensive head coach with no handle on offense. He notes Williams is third in the NFL in sack rate behind [[Deshaun Watson]] and [[Will Levis]], both of whom missed starts, and that a sack is nearly as damaging as an interception. He is explicit that this is not a bust call. James Koh (not a tracked expert) adds the tape read: weeks 3-6 showed tangible progression (two 300-yard games, nine touchdowns to three interceptions), but post-bye Williams looks hesitant and scared to let it rip, nothing like the USC gunslinger, and Koh would rather see a couple of aggressive contested interceptions than this. Both blame the scheme — one of the widest formation splits in the NFL on third and fourth down, pure one-to-two-to-three-to-four progression reads rather than reads off alignment or matchup.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): Chicago's rookie-year handling of Williams is an organizational failure, not a talent verdict. Angelo says Ryan Poles erred by retaining Matt Eberflus, who then hired Shane Waldron — a coordinator Angelo calls not an outwardly loud leader who could not hold veterans accountable, an issue that followed him from Seattle. Williams was third in EPA per dropback two games in, then came out of the bye worse; Angelo blames absent in-game adjustments and failure to return to what worked (bunch formations that produced explosives in the nine-point Arizona game, boot action with a high-low to [[Rome Odunze]] against New England). He expects Thomas Brown to bring accountability and calls it fixable nine games into a rookie year. [[Matt Waldman]] adds that development here means scripting and game-planning to give clear reads and flow — the [[Brock Purdy]] setup — not mechanics, and that comparing him unfavorably to [[Jayden Daniels]] is unfair.
- 2024-11-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-18]]): Waldman walked back his jokes that Thomas Brown could not fix Williams — much of what was broken was low-hanging fruit. Relaying [[Brandon Angelo]]'s reporting from the prior week's show, Waldman said Shane Waldron's individual play designs were fine but were not sequenced so a rookie could learn from them, and, remarkably, Waldron was not giving Williams a prescribed number of steps on his drops — 'just get back there and wing it' — so he finished drops out of position to throw on timing, something Williams was used to having specified. Brown's fix was to shrink the operation: more RPOs, screens and short throws where the offense could not be blown up.
