# Caleb Williams

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — Guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Dynasty]: still his top overall 2024 quarterback prospect, but explicitly calls him "the most boom-bust top prospect at the position I've graded in a few years." Elite at buying time and off-schedule playmaking, with an aspirational ceiling comp of Aaron Rodgers — but the stated floor risk is real: a refusal to check the ball down or take a coached "small loss" rather than force a low-value big play, a pattern Harms also flags in [[Kyler Murray]]'s game. The downside comps if that habit doesn't get coached out are Jay Cutler or [[Drew Lock]] — talented but turnover/decision-making risks who never fully corrected the same instinct. Notes a real situational risk on top of the player evaluation: if Chicago drafts him into the same coaching/coordinator instability that derailed Justin Fields's development, the outcome could look similar regardless of Williams's superior talent — logged as context, not part of the player grade itself.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: his own independent view, corroborating guest Daniel Harms's 2024-01-15 "most boom-bust top prospect in years" framing — "the mentals are not always quite there on the field all the time with Caleb," though his ceiling remains higher than [[Anthony Richardson]]'s. Distinguishes the two explicitly as the class's top boom/bust-style prospects: Richardson is closer to "all boom" because Waldman rates his mental processing more settled than Williams's, even though Williams's ceiling grade is the higher of the two.

- 2024-02-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-05]]) [Dynasty]: a landing-spot-specific worry, prompted by Cliff Kingsbury's move to become Washington's OC and speculation the team could trade up from 1.02 to 1.01 for Williams. Waldman: "not that fanciful" a scenario, but worries it points at "the worst end of Caleb Williams to start his career" — more [[Kyler Murray]]-esque, "running around like a chicken with its head cut off" rather than playing structured football, echoing Kyler Murray's own bail-the-pocket-on-proximity tendency already logged on this wiki. Separately flags Kingsbury's receiver usage in Arizona (static alignments, no motion or shifts) as a scheme concern independent of the quarterback question.
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]) [Dynasty]: asked directly whether mock drafts uniformly slotting Williams/[[Drake Maye]]/[[Jayden Daniels]] 1-2-3 will all deliver on their draft capital — "hell no." Puts real odds on two of the three eventually hitting, but says "most likely only one" does, and that one is "most likely" Williams, ranking the trio Williams-Daniels-Maye without ruling out other quarterbacks sliding in between them in actual draft order. Restates that he doesn't dislike this quarterback class, but that the modern NFL's pattern of starting rookies too early without developing them means every passer in it — Williams included — carries real bust risk. His specific Williams bust comp: Jay Cutler or Jeff George, "body by Porsche, brain by Mattel" — an unbelievable physical/arm-talent package paired with mistakes that suggest the mental side isn't fully formed, especially if he lands with a staff that can't build the right infrastructure around him. Closing archetype read: "an aspiring Aaron Rodgers," but a more likely outcome, if he lands in a middling situation, is closer to a better version of [[Kyler Murray]].

- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]) [Dynasty]: reaffirms Chicago as a near-certainty and calls him "by far the best quarterback in this class," the most NFL-ready of the group, with a real bust risk attached mainly to organizational mismanagement — cites the Bears' history with Justin Fields and Jay Cutler as the pattern to worry about, not the player's talent. Pushes back explicitly on the recurring "next Patrick Mahomes" media comp (also previously hung on Zach Wilson and Drew Lock): argues it says more about analysts not wanting to admit they missed on Mahomes pre-draft than about real stylistic overlap, and that Williams's actual game — "the way that he moves and plays" — reads much closer to an "aspiring Aaron Rodgers."

- 2024-03-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-08]]) [Dynasty], solo cast covering the full QB class: reaffirms Williams as this class's only true dynasty priority pick — "if you're going to take Caleb Williams, he's the top priority pick... I don't think there's a one-two answer" — and the only 2024 quarterback he'd trust as a Day 1 starter without a redshirt runway: "safe enough to put on the field... begin to thrive over time." Acknowledges real careless-decision-making flashes (cites the Notre Dame game) and a tendency to lean on arm talent in college, but credits him for visibly learning from mistakes within and across games. Restates the Aaron Rodgers — not Patrick Mahomes — stylistic comp: both throw well off unconventional platforms, but Mahomes's is rooted in his baseball background, where Rodgers's (and Williams's) is not. Says Williams is more conceptually advanced than [[Justin Fields]] and should have an easier time in Chicago even under a "ham-handed" development approach — but separately volunteers he'd personally rather see the Bears keep Fields as their QB and trade the No. 1 pick for more draft capital, if that were on the table.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]), reacting to Chicago trading for [[Keenan Allen]] to pair with [[D.J. Moore]]: says he can't recall a recent rookie quarterback (Williams presumed, pre-draft at the time) getting paired with a receiver duo this good, calling it a strong situational bump for whoever Chicago ultimately starts at 1.01.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: reaffirms Williams and [[Michael Penix Jr.]] as "the two top quarterbacks in this class," restated while discussing Washington's plans at 1.02.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): his other "problem solver" pick alongside [[Blake Corum]] — praises his ability to "work on script even when it's off script," marrying his natural movement style with what works within structure. Contrasts 2023 tape (protection was inconsistent, forcing "Superman moments" on nearly every play) with a cleaner 2022 in a more stable offense, and says his biggest qualm is that he sometimes tries to be Superman when being "Clark Kent" would be fine — but calls him one of the most naturally gifted, flow-reading athletes in the class.

- 2024-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Feel It or F@#k It* ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-01]]), on whether Chicago can screw him up: "yes," probably, since it's true of "all these quarterbacks" — but says Williams is the one 2024 quarterback he's most confident could withstand mishandling, given how the Bears added [[Keenan Allen]] and Gerald Everett, hired Shane Waldron as OC, and moved on from Luke Getsy — reads it as the organization learning from its mistakes with [[Justin Fields]], though the real test will be in-game management and patience on the field.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]): a detailed pocket-movement contrast with [[Drake Maye]] responding to a fan question about whether the two are really that different — when Williams moves outside the pocket "it's with a purpose," reacting to real pressure rather than inviting it, and he stays efficient and can climb the pocket, keeping his eyes downfield, the same way Baker Mayfield climbs a pocket. Highlight-reel scrambling makes him look panicked, "but that's not the case" — the movement is a last resort used only when actually forced into it, unlike Maye who resorts to escaping unnecessarily more often.
- 2024-04-23 — According to guest QB trainer Will Hewlett ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-23]]): motion-capture testing showed Williams' velocity range (46-56 mph) is the largest variance Hewlett's team has ever measured while still maintaining spin rate at low velocity, and his T-spine rotation was the highest his biomechanist has measured in any athlete, comparable to reports of Patrick Mahomes; Williams grew up a swimmer, not a baseball player, and idolizes Aaron Rodgers, whose 'pop step' footwork he's incorporated in training; Hewlett called him 'as pro-ready as anyone I've ever worked with' and dismissed Johnny Manziel bust comparisons as lazy evaluation given Williams' work ethic and attention to detail.
- 2024-05-02 — According to Brandon Angelo ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): Williams is Angelo's example of a 'perfect' infrastructure landing spot — first overall pick to a Bears team that already invested in receivers, upgraded the offensive line, and has a GM committed to giving him whatever he needs, which Angelo said gives Williams all the tools to be a top-ten quarterback.
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman names Caleb Williams (not Jayden Daniels, contra co-host Bob Harris) as the rookie QB most likely to be a top-12 fantasy QB1 in 2024, and says he'd actively consider drafting Williams as a value play given how far he's fallen in best-ball ADP -- QB18 overall, first quarterback off the board among rookies, behind names like Jared Goff (QB14, off a QB7 finish) and Aaron Rodgers (QB19). Waldman frames that gap as pure positional-depth-driven value rather than a knock on Williams himself, noting Chicago's offensive-line upgrades support the pick.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Dynasty]: Caleb Williams is in the rare ~1% of rookie QBs Waldman is comfortable both starting in the NFL and rostering as a fantasy starter year one, with Matt Eberflus already naming him Week 1 starter over token competition (Tyson Bagent, Austin Reed). Waldman projects 3,500 passing yards as realistic and 4,000 possible if the offense stays healthy, comping his early-career trajectory to Peyton Manning — turnover-prone at first before 'pouring it on' later. He frames the offense as able to support three startable pass-catchers: [[DeAndre Swift]] as a low-end RB2, [[D.J. Moore]] as a true WR2, and [[Keenan Allen]] as a WR3, while noting [[Rome Odunze]] has a real shot to be a low-end WR3 or even push into WR1 range.
- 2024-05-14 — According to Adam Rank ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-14]]), Bears fan and NFL Network personality, not a tracked expert: Rank's realistic passing projection for [[Caleb Williams]]' rookie year is roughly 3,500 yards, expecting Chicago to lean on the run with [[DeAndre Swift]] behind a stacked receiver room (DJ Moore, Keenan Allen, Rome Odunze) rather than let Williams throw it 38-40 times a game. Pressed by host James Koh to commit to a number, Rank ultimately guaranteed Williams throws for 4,000 yards as a rookie, comparing his likely trajectory to C.J. Stroud's 2023 rookie season. Context offered for how big a deal that would be: no Bears quarterback has ever thrown for 4,000 yards in a season — Jay Cutler's career high, with Alshon Jeffery, Brandon Marshall and Matt Forte around him, was under 3,850.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]): Williams regressed noticeably at USC once Kliff Kingsbury arrived as OC after looking like a clear No. 1 pick prospect the year before, though both hosts are unsure how much of that falls on Kingsbury versus a weaker surrounding talent pool and outsized hype; separately, new Bears OC Shane Waldron ran an advanced, aggressive drop-back vertical passing scheme with Geno Smith in Seattle (distinct from typical Shanahan-tree offenses that lean on play-action), which Harmon thinks should be a good scheme fit for Williams.
