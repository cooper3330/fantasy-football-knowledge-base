# Caleb Williams

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — Guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Dynasty]: still his top overall 2024 quarterback prospect, but explicitly calls him "the most boom-bust top prospect at the position I've graded in a few years." Elite at buying time and off-schedule playmaking, with an aspirational ceiling comp of Aaron Rodgers — but the stated floor risk is real: a refusal to check the ball down or take a coached "small loss" rather than force a low-value big play, a pattern Harms also flags in [[Kyler Murray]]'s game. The downside comps if that habit doesn't get coached out are Jay Cutler or [[Drew Lock]] — talented but turnover/decision-making risks who never fully corrected the same instinct. Notes a real situational risk on top of the player evaluation: if Chicago drafts him into the same coaching/coordinator instability that derailed Justin Fields's development, the outcome could look similar regardless of Williams's superior talent — logged as context, not part of the player grade itself.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: his own independent view, corroborating guest Daniel Harms's 2024-01-15 "most boom-bust top prospect in years" framing — "the mentals are not always quite there on the field all the time with Caleb," though his ceiling remains higher than [[Anthony Richardson]]'s. Distinguishes the two explicitly as the class's top boom/bust-style prospects: Richardson is closer to "all boom" because Waldman rates his mental processing more settled than Williams's, even though Williams's ceiling grade is the higher of the two.

- 2024-02-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-05]]) [Dynasty]: a landing-spot-specific worry, prompted by Cliff Kingsbury's move to become Washington's OC and speculation the team could trade up from 1.02 to 1.01 for Williams. Waldman: "not that fanciful" a scenario, but worries it points at "the worst end of Caleb Williams to start his career" — more [[Kyler Murray]]-esque, "running around like a chicken with its head cut off" rather than playing structured football, echoing Kyler Murray's own bail-the-pocket-on-proximity tendency already logged on this wiki. Separately flags Kingsbury's receiver usage in Arizona (static alignments, no motion or shifts) as a scheme concern independent of the quarterback question.
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]) [Dynasty]: asked directly whether mock drafts uniformly slotting Williams/[[Drake Maye]]/[[Jayden Daniels]] 1-2-3 will all deliver on their draft capital — "hell no." Puts real odds on two of the three eventually hitting, but says "most likely only one" does, and that one is "most likely" Williams, ranking the trio Williams-Daniels-Maye without ruling out other quarterbacks sliding in between them in actual draft order. Restates that he doesn't dislike this quarterback class, but that the modern NFL's pattern of starting rookies too early without developing them means every passer in it — Williams included — carries real bust risk. His specific Williams bust comp: Jay Cutler or Jeff George, "body by Porsche, brain by Mattel" — an unbelievable physical/arm-talent package paired with mistakes that suggest the mental side isn't fully formed, especially if he lands with a staff that can't build the right infrastructure around him. Closing archetype read: "an aspiring Aaron Rodgers," but a more likely outcome, if he lands in a middling situation, is closer to a better version of [[Kyler Murray]].
