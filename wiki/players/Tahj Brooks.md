# Tahj Brooks

## Expert Takes

- 2024-09-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-11]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman's 'large' back in the small/medium/large game is Texas Tech's number 28, 5-10 and 230, whom he watched over four games (Oregon, Kansas State, Texas, Cal) and comps to a mix of Chris Brooks and Anthony Dixon. Strengths: nuance setting up lead blocks by changing stride length and drawing defenders into them, patience on power, cutbacks found only after gap reads are filled rather than automatically, identifying penetration during the exchange, 45-to-60-degree cuts at top speed and 90-degree hip openings behind the line, jump stops and jump cuts used discriminatingly, a stutter-head-fake-shoulder-dip into a stiff arm, high knees through low shots, contact balance that stalemates downhill tacklers, and real pass-protection feel including green-dog blitzes. He has 670 career touches with three fumbles — one per 223, which Waldman calls elite. Caveats: not a burner, will not test well, likely undrafted or a day-three pick who must win a camp spot, though 5-10/230 backs still have value ([[James Conner]], [[Joe Mixon]], [[Gus Edwards]], [[Audric Estime]]).
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: one of his most underrated backs in the class and a good receiving back, with a comp range from CJ Anderson and [[Christopher Brooks]] at the low end to Le'Veon Bell at the high end. Waldman says he is a better receiver and a more imaginative runner than the low-end comps and would not be surprised if he tests with genuinely good short-area quickness, which would push him toward the Bell end. At 5'10 and 230 he is quick, has excellent contact balance, can run through, over or around defenders, sets up and improves his blockers, and creates when there is no blocking at all — Waldman jokes his nickname should be 'Lemonade Stand' for turning lemon plays into lemonade at Texas Tech. As a receiver he attacks the ball with good hand-eye coordination, goes high or low, takes hard shots, and pairs that with pass protection prowess to stay on the field. Caveats: he is not beating NFL linebackers downfield, and he will need to test well at the combine to earn touches.
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman's headline call of the episode — asked to name a back as good as Le'Veon Bell in this class, he picks Brooks, whom McFarland failed to guess in three tries ([[Quinshon Judkins]], [[Cam Skattebo]], [[Devin Neal]]). Waldman's nickname for him is 'lemonade stand' because he turns would-be negative plays into positive ones; where a back like Anthony McFarland goes 10 for 5 with negative runs, Brooks would go 10 for 55. He calls Brooks a very good outlet receiver at 225-230 pounds who wins on wheel routes and tracks and catches through contact, and the Bell comp comes from short-area quickness — Waldman remembers Bell's elite three-cone. His concern is explosiveness, and he says Brooks might grade in his top five or top ten. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) counters that the model holds Brooks down on receiving — best season only 22nd percentile receiving yards per team pass attempt — though his 72nd percentile career PFF run grade and 0.27 elusiveness rate versus a 0.21 NCAA average grade well. Waldman jokes the episode alone will get Brooks drafted three rounds too early in his own dynasty league.
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]) [Dynasty]: Brooks is one of two players Waldman predicts will vastly outplay his draft position. Waldman expects a day-three pick or even a priority UDFA contract, and notes running back is the position with the most UDFA successes, so late-round dynasty and best-ball capital is warranted. The case is short-area agility rather than speed: a 6.9-second three-cone (against the roughly 6.75 Waldman remembers from Le'Veon Bell, whom he liked at Michigan State for the same reason) and an elite 20-yard shuttle, plus vision and contact balance that let him mitigate losses and turn bad plays into gains — Marshawn Lynch and Walter Payton behavior in Waldman's framing. His nickname for Brooks is 'lemonade stand' because he turned lemons into lemonade behind Texas Tech's blocking. Waldman expected anything under 4.65 to be a win and called the 4.52 a major victory. He warned his pre-draft ranking will be well above where Brooks is drafted.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: Brooks carries an above-78th-percentile grade and is a starting-caliber player, one of several backs he wants in the fifth or sixth round — but like [[Omarion Hampton]] he is very good in many areas without a great, surface-level noteworthy skill, which is what makes NFL teams shy away. He warned that the landing spot dictates early value: Brooks to Detroit means hoping for an injury, Brooks to Cleveland and 'suddenly we have something.' He anchored the fantasy cost of that fall with per-round scoring data — first-round backs have averaged roughly 15.5-16.5 points per game against 6.5-7.5 for fifth and sixth rounders, effectively RB1 versus RB45-50. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed and grouped him with [[Damien Martinez]] as a back to take in the fourth round of a rookie draft when nobody wants a sixth-round NFL running back.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: one of the better three-down backs in the class and available for nothing — Angelo has gotten him off waivers after rookie drafts and calls him 'nothing sexy' but able to start in a pinch. The path is low red zone work, an area where [[Chase Brown]] struggles (fine between the 20s, not the back you want on third-and-two), plus uncertainty about Brown's ankle after a strange late-season injury, with [[Zach Moss]] no real obstacle. Angelo also rates him among the class's better pass-protecting backs, so he could see third downs, and expects more between-the-20s work late in the season in a high-scoring Bengals offense. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees, praising his creativity inside and outside the structure as a fit for short-yardage work, and had Brooks and [[Jordan James]] as his two best pre-draft pass protectors at the position by a wide margin. Waldman took him as a luxury pick at the equivalent of 313.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Brooks has had a good camp and is getting decent reps, and Angelo expects him to take a growing share of the low red zone and some third-down work as Cincinnati hits November, December and January weather, on the reasoning that [[Chase Brown]] is not a 65-70% touch-share back. He rates Brooks one of the two best pass protectors in the rookie class. The role Angelo describes is the tertiary option thriving while defenses focus on [[Ja'Marr Chase]], [[Tee Higgins]] and [[Joe Burrow]]. [[Matt Waldman]] noted Brooks needed either an injury or a cut ahead of him and that [[Samaje Perine]] is still there — a 'tractor' whose power is inevitable but slow enough that you can climb aboard and take the key out [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]): Waldman says the preseason box score badly misrepresented Brooks — 2.5 yards per carry behind a second-team offensive line that was 'throwing lemons at him', with not a single properly blocked play, while Brooks made lemonade by mitigating losses, slipping underneath defenders, breaking tackles and churning for two or three extra yards, exactly what he did at Texas Tech. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked) agrees and notes people got more excited about [[Braelon Allen]] running 11 untouched yards. On role, Kluge reports [[Samaje Perine]] took the third-down snaps with the first team, projecting [[Chase Brown]] on early downs and near the goal line, Perine on third down, and Brooks as the direct plug-in if Brown misses time. Waldman adds Brooks could be eased in later if he earns pass-protection trust. Both call him a roughly 20th-round dart who will likely be dropped and re-added through the season.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]): when observers judged Brooks by the box score and were unimpressed, Waldman's read of the tape was that he was doing exactly what he did at Texas Tech — making lemonade out of lemons in a lot of bad situations. He groups it with his [[Nick Chubb]] theory that a back's early tape has to be studied contextually rather than by yardage totals.
- 2025-08-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Brooks was his first super-deep sleeper, which he called a [[Matt Waldman]] special — Waldman had him behind [[Ashton Jeanty]] and roughly tied for his second-favourite back in the class, yet he lasted to the sixth round. Buried on the depth chart, he got unburied when [[Zach Moss]] suffered a possibly career-ending neck injury, leaving only [[Samaje Perine]] as a blocking and third-down back. Harris said Brooks has no path to anything while [[Chase Brown]] is healthy, but if anything happens to Brown it sounds like it will be Brooks. Ciely agreed, seeing style similarities to Brown that make him the natural answer. Both said he is not a must-handcuff in a 12-team, 16-round league — and Harris noted the waiver edge is limited because every waiver column will list him first.
