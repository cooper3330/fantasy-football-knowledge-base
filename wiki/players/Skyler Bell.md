# Skyler Bell

## Expert Takes

- 2026-01-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-26]]) [Dynasty]: could become a starter, but hands technique is Waldman's biggest concern. He acknowledges Bell's highlight-reel acrobatic catches are real, but says his catch technique is sloppy enough that he also drops catchable targets, comparing him to Kyle Williams -- a player whose highlights created hype that didn't fully match how much he 'fought the ball' in-game. Waldman likes Bell as a good open-field runner with route-running upside and sees him projecting more as a slot/flanker, meaning his fantasy upside will vary heavily with landing spot; he expects Bell to be a media/draft-pundit favorite for understandable reasons, but is not convinced the underlying tape fully supports that hype.
- 2026-02-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-02]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said Bell is 'a little bit' overhyped, arguing evaluators are reacting to his athletic ability and highlight-reel catches while overlooking that he 'claps at' and fights the ball too often on contested targets. He compared the hype cycle to Kyle Williams a year earlier -- a fast player who drew big buzz around this time last season before the shine wore off by fall camp. Waldman said Bell is genuinely very fast and could grow into a good player, but he doesn't project him as an instant-impact rookie.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-05]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo named [[Skyler Bell]] of UConn his 'sure thing' Day 2-plus prospect, praising his tempo and route variation and his ability to win both as a boots-on-the-ground runner and going up for the ball in the air; he noted Bell had only one game under six receptions all season, and that 'worst' outing was still a four-catch, 135-yard, two-touchdown game. Angelo flagged Syracuse as Bell's bad tape -- he got bullied tracking the ball in that matchup -- but otherwise called him intelligent and abrupt on double moves, someone who can play 11 personnel almost immediately and possibly work into a 12-personnel role later. He pegged Bell as a likely Day 3 pick, probably in the fourth-to-fifth-round range, though he wouldn't be shocked by a third-round selection. Waldman corroborated the evaluation, noting Bell is his WR10 on the board heading into final Combine-week rankings updates.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Bell is a serial 'clap-attacker' -- ugly catch technique in contested spots, not due to being physically overpowered but from mispositioning his hands entering through the defender, which costs him 50-50 balls. Waldman graded him among his WR4/WR5 range in the class and is betting he's the exception to the rule -- like Golden Tate, Marvin Harrison Sr. and Terry McLaurin, players who caught the ball 'ugly' but still produced -- rather than a Quentin Johnston-type who has to fix the flaw first to succeed. If he cleans up his hand placement in contested catches, Waldman believes he's a long-term starter; he called it a real gamble either way.
- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Bell is a 'serial clap attacker' -- catching with his palms/flats instead of his fingertips, which creates a violent recoil and drop risk -- but Waldman is betting he's the exception to the rule rather than another cautionary tale like Quentin Johnston or [[Courtland Sutton]]. His distinction: exception receivers (his historical comps are Golden Tate, [[Terry McLaurin]] and Marvin Harrison Sr.) clap-attacked as their base technique on almost every catch, while cautionary-tale receivers alternated between clap-attacking and trapping the ball as a fallback, which signals confidence and skill deficits rather than a consistent, if unorthodox, technique. Bell fits the first group -- when he uses a different attack it's proper technique, not a fallback -- so Waldman is optimistic he can start and thrive early with only minor issues to iron out, though the drop risk if it's not corrected is real.
- 2026-04-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-06]]) [Dynasty]: on NFL.com comping Skyler Bell to [[Ja'Marr Chase]], Waldman says he's feeling it in that Chase tops his comp spectrum for Bell, but calls it not a true one-to-one -- Chase can play inside/outside, has power/quickness/speed after the catch, wins the ball vertically, and is a fantastic route runner. Waldman instead slots Bell a tier below Chase, comparable to former Jets/Commanders WR Laveranues Coles, saying if Bell's game comes together he could exceed Coles within a few years but likely won't reach Chase's level. He likes the build and movement similarities and says Bell wins at the catch point like [[Terry McLaurin]] -- good, but with hand technique that is currently 'awful.' Waldman lists only four modern-era (mid-90s+) receivers he'd similarly praise for contested-catch ability -- Marvin Harrison Sr., Early Doucet, Golden Tate, and McLaurin -- with Bell potentially the fifth if he proves he can catch consistently against tighter NFL coverage windows.
- 2026-04-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-27]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman strongly approves of Buffalo's 4th-round pick of Bell, calling him a potential steal of the draft and revealing he has him ranked WR5 on the pre-draft RSP board. He frames the pick alongside [[Keon Coleman]]'s situation -- teammates reportedly like Coleman's talent but pushed him to 'figure some things out' on craft, and the team reportedly turned down trade offers for him this offseason -- and thinks Bell gives Buffalo enough outside speed to eventually let Coleman kick inside as a power slot. With [[Khalil Shakir]] and [[DJ Moore]] already drawing attention, Waldman projects favorable, matchup-heavy usage for Bell even on a limited route diet, comping him to a 'Gabe Davis with better hands' and forecasting a 900-1,100 yard season, with room to grow into and eventually supplant Moore as the top target.
