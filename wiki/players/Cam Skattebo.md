# Cam Skattebo

## Expert Takes

- 2024-12-12 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-12]]): Skattebo is his second technician of the 2025 class and reminds him of [[David Montgomery]] coming out of Iowa State — the cognitive skill set through contact, taking different types of contact and marrying it with efficient footwork, and not a burner. He cites 22 total touchdowns, 2,000 total yards and six yards a carry, and says 'you know it's coming but no one has seemed to be able to slow him down', while acknowledging [[Ashton Jeanty]] is the Heisman-caliber back of the class. Angelo doesn't know where he lands in a deep RB class (Montgomery was a third-rounder) but frames the archetype as valuable: the guy whose 35-yard gain is four broken tackles and a chug, an energy-giver and identity piece — 'those are dinosaurs nowadays.'
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: the back in the class who least needs a specific landing spot, in the Jamaal Williams / [[David Montgomery]] archetype — an energy giver down to down, a cold-weather player nobody wants to tackle, and the kind of complement a [[De'Von Achane]] or [[James Cook]] type offense needs to be at its best. Angelo credits good work with his feet, reliable hands as a pass catcher and strong balance post-contact across contact types. He is explicit about the ceiling: probably not a high one, but a long career like Montgomery's or Williams'. The one thing to clean up is pass protection technique — a lack of technical acumen rather than effort, fixable in a pro RB room. Angelo hopes he does not fall to the fourth round, where opportunity dries up.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): Waldman feels it that Skattebo goes as one of the first three running backs drafted — the market is enamored with his size, contact balance, quickness, receiving ability, production and highlight plays, and if the flaw is correctable that love is deserved. The single thing keeping him outside the RSP's pre-draft top three to five backs is pass protection: he 'likes to headbutt people' as his mode of operation, tips off everything and is simply bad at it right now, though it is learnable if he leads with his hands instead of his head. Waldman calls the fix a projection but not a massive one, since Skattebo can contribute immediately even with the pass-pro issues. Bob Harris (an Arizona alum) needles the Sun Devil throughout.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21]]): Waldman expects to be higher on Skattebo after the draft than before it, and would not be surprised to have him among his top five running backs on the post-draft board. Two reasons: he thinks Skattebo will be drafted early enough to have a role, and although the pass protection is currently bad, Waldman regards pass protection as one of the easier things for a back to improve — so he is comfortable banking on improvement. He notes the NFL buzz on Skattebo is contradictory depending on who you ask, and reminds listeners it is the season when 'everyone is lying until they start spending the draft capital'. He groups Skattebo with [[Jaylin Noel]] as players with fixable flaws whose board position will move with landing spot and capital.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): he would 'much rather have' Skattebo than [[Omarion Hampton]] because Skattebo runs harder and actually has a plan for when he hits defenders. Angelo, a Bears fan, calls him '[[David Montgomery]] plus' — a very similar athlete but better through direct contact at the same career stage and a notably better processor, noting Montgomery came out of Iowa State as a poor processor with a wide veer mechanism who bounced everything outside early in Chicago. He sees him as a natural complement to [[DeAndre Swift]] in Chicago provided he cleans up his pass protection quickly. [[Matt Waldman]] also floats him as the kind of short-yardage banger a team like Jacksonville should add in the fourth or fifth round rather than spending premium capital on a back behind a bad line.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]): asked whether Skattebo will be the Giants' lead back by year's end, Waldman says he feels it, but on the fence and with hedging. He believes [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] has a stranglehold on the starting job to open the year despite Skattebo looking good, because pass protection has been Skattebo's persistent issue and he has to stop leading with his head and improve as a blocker. If he figures that out, Waldman thinks that by November he may start stealing touches. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) frames it as an evolution rather than a takeover — he spoke with Chris Pflum of Giants Insider, who is enthusiastic but acknowledges it is a process — and notes the coaching staff keeps talking Skattebo up as a receiver, which is not Tracy's strength since Tracy does not always catch the ball cleanly. Harris also points out Skattebo ran behind poor blocking at Arizona State and will not have great blocking in New York either, which may be part of why they drafted him.
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: comps Skattebo to [[David Montgomery]] on skill set, saying he has enough ability to eventually reach 100-120 touches in this offense but must be much better as a blocker to get there. He frames the Giants backfield as [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] the hare and Skattebo the tortoise, with [[Devin Singletary]] the monkey wrench in a three-headed committee — Singletary being short, slow, not strong, but possessing unbelievable vision and quickness and, crucially, being assignment-sound, which coaches value (Waldman relays Jim McCormick's line that Singletary is 'coach's catnip'). If Skattebo gets and stays healthy, Waldman expects a more even split in the second half of the season. [[Chris Harris]] notes the hamstring has cost him most of training camp so any chance of beating out Tracy before Week 1 is gone, and raises ball-security concerns; Harris also worries Skattebo's contact-seeking style leads to concussions, while conceding Waldman's RSP view that double-digit rushing touchdown seasons are plausible later in his career.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-15]]): Waldman predicts Skattebo will be the Giants' starter by mid-October and essentially uncontested by that point — a three-way committee will persist on paper, but he will be the clear leader. He praised the power running and physicality, citing Skattebo running through Donovan Wilson in the crease, plus balanced touch and clear receiving ability, and noted a sharp usage increase from Week 1 to Week 2 including more pivotal situations. His caveat was self-inflicted: Skattebo needs to stop leading with his head into contact and concussing himself. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) said this was one of his few offseason narratives — decent draft capital, a style that fits an offense that does not block well — delayed by an August hamstring injury, and that it now looks to be coming to fruition.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the real lesson of the five-quarter Giants-Cowboys game was the backfield. [[Devin Singletary]] got literally one carry — the first offensive snap — and was then sat down, and Skattebo sure seemed to become the more valuable of the two Giants backs. The raw snap count was 33 Skattebo to 27 [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]], but that undersells it: from halftime forward it was 18-11 in Skattebo's favour and he took more and more of the work as the game went on. He had a big inside-zone run up the gut in the fourth quarter, pile-driving down to the one, then finished the drive with the score. Harris's comparison: Tracy can be too cute by half and patters his feet rather than hitting it, whereas if you know one thing about Skattebo it is that he hits it hard every single time. Open question whether this becomes a pure takeover or a split; Harris also wonders if late-game fatigue in a track meet inflated the shift.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris ranks Skattebo RB32 for week 3 against Kansas City, ahead of [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] at RB38 — flex territory 'you don't feel awesome about'. Skattebo got more run in week 2 against Dallas with 11 rushing attempts, possibly because Tracy is battling a calf issue or because Tracy's dancing running style has him on the outs. Harris described Skattebo as doing 'a pretty interesting Peyton Hillis impersonation' — not very elusive, resembling an angry rhinoceros, and running 'like he's got a magnet in his forehead and every defensive player is made of metal'. On that basis Harris said if he were Brian Daboll he knows which back he would use near the goal line, and it would be Skattebo. He still advised against starting any Giants or Chiefs back if avoidable. Harris pushed back on a listener mixtape claiming Skattebo is already RB22, saying he has him a little lower.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-22]]): 'feel it' on Skattebo being the lead back for the Giants going forward. Waldman said the only real weakness is pass protection — and even that is arguable, since he plays all out and will ram his head into a defender's chest. He praised the contact balance, movement skills, burst around the corner and receiving ability, all of which he had charted at Arizona State. The Giants made him earn the job (he did not start in Week 2) but the carry share keeps climbing, and [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] left with a shoulder injury that required x-rays. Waldman noted he wrote at the draft that the Giants saw him as ideally suited to their roster because their offensive line does not block well, and his PFF college metrics show he remained okay behind poor blocking — though he is unsure how long that translates in the NFL. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed the ship had already left the station.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'kind of felt like a folk hero was born.' [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] started and took the first five touches, but after Tracy's shoulder stinger Skattebo was basically the only thing the Giants had going — a leaping, stumbling rushing touchdown at the end of the first half after Kansas City roughed Russell Wilson, and 45 yards by himself on a fourth-quarter drive inside the ten with the score 16-6. Harris's scouting note: unbelievably physical, catches it pretty well, not much speed from a dead stop but do not let him get a head of steam. Caveats: [[Devin Singletary]] worked in, Tracy's shoulder is unknown, and Wilson kept the ball on a zone read rather than give it to him.
- 2025-09-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] hurt, guest Ben Gretch (Stealing Signals, not tracked) called Skattebo his fifth-hardest Week 4 call and said his college profile is already translating — he breaks tackles, bounced off a tackler and spun outside on his first Sunday night play with Cris Collinsworth raving, and is a fan and locker-room favorite for his physicality. Gretch stressed the combination of goal-line work and a genuinely good receiving profile, allowing three-down usage, while conceding the long speed is not on [[TreVeyon Henderson]]'s level; the risks are a tough Chargers matchup, a rookie quarterback in [[Jackson Dart]], and [[Devin Singletary]] playing more than you want because coaches trust him in pass protection. Harris agreed, calling Singletary 'coach's catnip' but a known limited quantity, which makes Skattebo's workload feel safe; Harris ranked him RB24 standard and RB23 PPR, said he could argue for much higher, and concluded that anyone who drafted or waiver-claimed Skattebo is now looking at a starter in almost every league — he shops in a better department store than Henderson this week.
- 2025-09-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-26]]): Harris would trade Skattebo for [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] in a heartbeat — not because he dislikes him, he likes him fine, but because Skattebo is the kind of found-money running back the market overpays for. Skattebo also headlines Harris's suggested [[Ashton Jeanty]] package (Skattebo plus a WR3 like [[Jaylen Waddle]] or [[Chris Olave]]), and Josh reports Andy Behrens (not tracked) wanted to trade Skattebo plus [[Chris Olave]] just to get [[Emeka Egbuka]].
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): with [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] out with a shoulder problem, Skattebo started, took the first carry, and played 52 offensive snaps to [[Devin Singletary]]'s 17 — a clear workload win. The efficiency was ugly for a defensible reason: the Chargers were not remotely afraid of [[Jackson Dart]] beating them deep, so in run situations they loaded roughly nine in the box, and Harris will not blame him much for being asked to throw his body against the rocks. He is more power back than not and will not make many big plays unless the field opens up. The frustration is scoring — four carries inside the five and none converted, including a goal-line run where he got in the clear with a head of steam and Derwin James simply trucked him. Harris notes this was exactly the game script that should have produced a touchdown.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-02]]): Skattebo is going to get a huge number of touches because the Giants have to keep drives going — Angelo called him effectively the best offensive player on the team right now, which he framed as an indictment of the roster rather than a compliment. Both hosts loved the Skattebo–[[Jackson Dart]] energy (lead blocking, running through faces) while warning it enables the worst in a rookie quarterback; [[Matt Waldman]] compared them to the Wolf of Wall Street pairing, with Skattebo as the McConaughey character. Waldman also compared their collision-seeking styles and invoked David Montgomery hitting the slopes one too many times as the durability worry.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'Skattebo keeps looking like a usable player' — a beastly grinder with over 100 scrimmage yards. The damaging exception was a red-zone fumble returned 85 yards for a Saints touchdown, the 'fumble six' that Harris calls the true crusher of the game, though the Giants went right back to him on the very next series, so there was no punishment. He was also stuffed on a third down early. Harris prefers him going forward to [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]], who could be back as soon as this week: 'I think Skattebo is more desirable.'
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-09]]): with [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] expected back for Thursday night and the Giants issuing morning assurances that the job still belongs to Skattebo, the right way to play it for one week is to keep starting Skattebo -- Harris ranks him ahead of Tracy and as the usable Giants back -- while acknowledging a surprise split is possible after this week. He added that Skattebo plus mostly Eagles is how he would build Thursday-night lineups, since it is hard to be enthused about any Giants pass catchers with [[Malik Nabers]] out. [[Matt Waldman]] did not dissent.
- 2025-10-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-10]]): Skattebo took over the Thursday-night win outright — three goal-line touchdowns and 112 scrimmage yards on 21 touches, with Harris calling him 'the particle accelerator.' The workload picture is now clear: Harris says we need not have worried about [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]], who didn't get his first touch until the Giants' third possession, finished with four touches, and on his only red-zone carry immediately tried to avoid contact, bounced outside and got tackled; [[Devin Singletary]]'s box-score line came in garbage time. Harris frames the broader Giants story cautiously — a 1-4 team that had just given a game away in New Orleans, a home touchdown underdog to the champs, so 'mostly what it is is a good story' — and flags Denver on the road in Week 7 as a hard matchup, while conceding Dart and Skattebo handled this supposedly hard matchup fine.
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-13]]): asked whether Skattebo is a league winner, Waldman said 'fuck it' — while adding he is enormously fun to watch, right up until he pass protects, which worries him. He called Skattebo a smart runner and said that if he can become as smart a pass protector he will be something; he already is a good running back who will deliver some high-end RB1 weeks because of the touchdown component. The condition on that is whether [[Jackson Dart]] can keep the Giants offense competitive. Waldman also pushed back on the celebration of Skattebo head-butting a wall, saying not to encourage him and to let him play a while.
