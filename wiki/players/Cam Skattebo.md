- 2024-12-12 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-12]]): Skattebo is his second technician of the 2025 class and reminds him of [[David Montgomery]] coming out of Iowa State — the cognitive skill set through contact, taking different types of contact and marrying it with efficient footwork, and not a burner. He cites 22 total touchdowns, 2,000 total yards and six yards a carry, and says 'you know it's coming but no one has seemed to be able to slow him down', while acknowledging [[Ashton Jeanty]] is the Heisman-caliber back of the class. Angelo doesn't know where he lands in a deep RB class (Montgomery was a third-rounder) but frames the archetype as valuable: the guy whose 35-yard gain is four broken tackles and a chug, an energy-giver and identity piece — 'those are dinosaurs nowadays.'
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: the back in the class who least needs a specific landing spot, in the Jamaal Williams / [[David Montgomery]] archetype — an energy giver down to down, a cold-weather player nobody wants to tackle, and the kind of complement a [[De'Von Achane]] or [[James Cook]] type offense needs to be at its best. Angelo credits good work with his feet, reliable hands as a pass catcher and strong balance post-contact across contact types. He is explicit about the ceiling: probably not a high one, but a long career like Montgomery's or Williams'. The one thing to clean up is pass protection technique — a lack of technical acumen rather than effort, fixable in a pro RB room. Angelo hopes he does not fall to the fourth round, where opportunity dries up.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): Waldman feels it that Skattebo goes as one of the first three running backs drafted — the market is enamored with his size, contact balance, quickness, receiving ability, production and highlight plays, and if the flaw is correctable that love is deserved. The single thing keeping him outside the RSP's pre-draft top three to five backs is pass protection: he 'likes to headbutt people' as his mode of operation, tips off everything and is simply bad at it right now, though it is learnable if he leads with his hands instead of his head. Waldman calls the fix a projection but not a massive one, since Skattebo can contribute immediately even with the pass-pro issues. Bob Harris (an Arizona alum) needles the Sun Devil throughout.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21]]): Waldman expects to be higher on Skattebo after the draft than before it, and would not be surprised to have him among his top five running backs on the post-draft board. Two reasons: he thinks Skattebo will be drafted early enough to have a role, and although the pass protection is currently bad, Waldman regards pass protection as one of the easier things for a back to improve — so he is comfortable banking on improvement. He notes the NFL buzz on Skattebo is contradictory depending on who you ask, and reminds listeners it is the season when 'everyone is lying until they start spending the draft capital'. He groups Skattebo with [[Jaylin Noel]] as players with fixable flaws whose board position will move with landing spot and capital.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): he would 'much rather have' Skattebo than [[Omarion Hampton]] because Skattebo runs harder and actually has a plan for when he hits defenders. Angelo, a Bears fan, calls him '[[David Montgomery]] plus' — a very similar athlete but better through direct contact at the same career stage and a notably better processor, noting Montgomery came out of Iowa State as a poor processor with a wide veer mechanism who bounced everything outside early in Chicago. He sees him as a natural complement to [[DeAndre Swift]] in Chicago provided he cleans up his pass protection quickly. [[Matt Waldman]] also floats him as the kind of short-yardage banger a team like Jacksonville should add in the fourth or fifth round rather than spending premium capital on a back behind a bad line.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]): asked whether Skattebo will be the Giants' lead back by year's end, Waldman says he feels it, but on the fence and with hedging. He believes [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] has a stranglehold on the starting job to open the year despite Skattebo looking good, because pass protection has been Skattebo's persistent issue and he has to stop leading with his head and improve as a blocker. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) frames it as an evolution rather than a takeover — he spoke with Chris Pflum of Giants Insider, who is enthusiastic but acknowledges it is a process — and notes the coaching staff keeps talking Skattebo up as a receiver, which is not Tracy's strength since Tracy does not always catch the ball cleanly. - 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: comps Skattebo to [[David Montgomery]] on skill set, saying he has enough ability to eventually reach 100-120 touches in this offense but must be much better as a blocker to get there. If Skattebo gets and stays healthy, Waldman expects a more even split in the second half of the season.
