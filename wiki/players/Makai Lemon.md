# Makai Lemon

- 2025-11-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-24]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman rates Lemon's hands as good as [[Carnell Tate]]'s -- calling him 'automatic' with spotless technique and tough at the catch point against physical coverage -- but sees him as a merely decent route runner limited by below-elite dynamic athleticism, which caps his ceiling relative to Tate and [[Jordyn Tyson]]. His outlook is landing-spot dependent: in a plain offense his floor looks like a contributing [[Parker Washington]]-type WR3, but Waldman said if he lands in a scheme like Ben Johnson's system that wants an [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] type, there's a real path to that level of ceiling.
- 2025-12-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-22]]): Waldman calls Lemon 'very good' but a tier below Carnell Tate at the top of the 2026 receiver class -- not a [[Ja'Marr Chase]]-level outside/inside alpha, but a 'heck of a football player' whose outcome depends heavily on landing spot and scheme usage.
- 2026-01-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-22]]): Among the receivers Washington could target at pick 7 (alongside Carnell Tate and Jordyn Tyson, all seen as likely top-15 picks), Angelo would take Lemon first because he can be an immediate down-to-down option who elevates Jayden Daniels' short-to-intermediate game, which is where Daniels flourishes and creates post-catch opportunity.
- 2026-02-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-09]]) [Dynasty]: in a debate over whether [[Carnell Tate]] is overrated relative to Lemon because Lemon has played far more slot reps, Waldman calls the two 'splitting hairs' -- both are pro-ready and he'd be happy with either, though he gives a slight nod to Tate. He notes the prevailing industry model favors receivers with heavy slot reps, citing recent successful WR draft classes, which is why Lemon's profile draws praise -- but argues that model overweights raw alignment percentage relative to translatable route-running skill.
- 2026-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: in the same slot-snap-percentage discussion, Lemon 'passes the test' of a fantasy-community model favoring receivers with heavy college slot usage, spending a large share of his snaps in the slot -- in contrast to draft-class rival [[Carnell Tate]], who barely played there. Waldman said he'd lean toward favoring Lemon specifically if he lands with a team planning to use him slot-heavy, but cautioned the model isn't determinative on its own -- what actually matters is whether a receiver can read a defense and tell a story with his route running from any alignment, a trait he credits to both Lemon and Tate at an elite level ('as clean at tracking and attacking a football as I've seen in a while from some guys').
- 2026-02-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-17]]): Harmon grades Makai Lemon alongside Carnell Tate as the top two receivers in his early 2026 class look -- 'I know what those guys are,' and that both would produce in any NFL offense right now. His one lingering question is measurables: Harmon jokes he hopes Lemon isn't 'secretly 5-foot-9, 180 pounds,' which he says would change his evaluation.
- 2026-02-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-24]]): grouped with Carnell Tate in Harmon's early top tier of the 2026 WR draft class, both projected as top-10 caliber picks ahead of a clear drop-off to the rest of the group.
- 2026-02-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-27]]): Harmon says Makai Lemon 'would be a great fit' in New Orleans' motion-heavy offense given how much free-release advantage it creates for smaller receivers, but flagged real uncertainty about his size/measurables as a question mark for a Saints fit given the team's recent preference for bigger receivers ([[Devaughn Vele]], [[Ja'Lynn Polk]], and its earlier interest in Isaac TeSlaa).
- 2026-03-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-02]]) [Dynasty]: not concerned that Lemon skipped the 40-yard dash at the combine, saying 'we all knew that he's not the fastest dude.' Waldman referenced his own earlier scouting report (written for Football Guys in November/December) comparing Lemon to Amon-Ra St. Brown, but expects Lemon to go earlier in the draft than St. Brown's mid-round slot because NFL teams already know his profile well. He also flagged unverified social-media chatter -- which he did not personally witness -- claiming Lemon 'bombed' three separate team interviews, and that his podium demeanor seemed different than in past appearances; Waldman declined to weigh in further since he wasn't in the room, and was skeptical generally of both teams' and reporters' ability to correctly parse interview performance.
- 2026-03-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-03]]): Makai Lemon did not run the 40 at the combine but measured a solid 5'11, 192 lbs, easing concern he'd check in in the 180s. Harmon views him as in contention with Carnell Tate for the class's top receiver spot, rating him among roughly five receivers he'd be comfortable taking in round one.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Lemon is 'dummy-proof' -- a physical, tactically sound receiver at all three levels who wins after the catch and finds soft spots in zones regardless of quarterback fit, in contrast to concerns raised about [[Jordan Tyson]] and [[Carnell Tate]]'s counterpart risk. Waldman gave him a mid-to-high first-round grade from nearly every NFL team and praised his toughness, saying he 'took harder hits than any receiver in the cycle, got back up, same motor all four quarters.' Both hosts named Lemon the one receiver in the class who is truly landing-spot-proof for dynasty purposes -- Angelo said you'd have to be 'an idiot' to draft him without a quarterback plan given his ceiling. Waldman has him a hair ahead of Tate for safety at 1.02 in superflex startups and rookie drafts, though both said either pick is defensible.
- 2026-03-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-24]]) [Dynasty]: Lemon (USC, 5'11', 192 lbs) is the consensus WR2 in the 2026 class and, per Harmon, could have a higher NFL production ceiling than [[Carnell Tate]], but does not fit all 32 offenses the way Tate does because he was primarily a slot receiver in college (68.5% of sampled snaps from the slot). Harmon compared his likely NFL slot-rate bucket against first-round WRs charted since 2021 -- Emeka Egbuka (~74%), Jaxon Smith-Njigba (~83%), Treylon Burks (~68%) and Kadarius Toney (~77%) -- and placed Lemon firmly in the Egbuka/JSN bucket rather than the Burks/Toney bucket, calling Toney 'an abysmal pick' who never developed a real route tree. Lemon's zone success was 82.9%, ranking between Chris Olave and Jalen Waddle historically, and he beat the prospect average at every route except the nine. Man coverage success was 74.2% -- just south of JSN's 75%, ahead of Egbuka's 68%, comparable to Puka Nacua, and close to Malik Nabers' 74.4% (though Harmon notes Nabers, who ran only 50% slot as a prospect, is a tier above Lemon). Press-beating checked in around 70%. Contested catch: one drop (1.1% of sample), an 19% contested target rate, and an 88.2% catch rate on those looks, highlighted by a heavily praised Iowa-game reel. Co-host James Koh noted Lemon's 2025 resume -- Biletnikoff winner, 1,100 yards, 11 TDs, 14.6 YPR -- shows he is not just a dump-off slot player, and pointed to his athletic family pedigree (father and uncle played college football, a cousin and uncle in MLB) as a factor in a smooth NFL transition. Both hosts noted Lemon's combine stare/intensity drew Amon-Ra St. Brown comparisons -- Koh called it a St. Brown 'cosplay' that came off a little awkward -- but agreed it is a non-issue for his evaluation as a player.
- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Lemon is one of the best pure pass catchers Waldman has scouted in recent years -- tough and clutch at the catch point, with advanced hand and body positioning, and he tracks/attacks the ball like a veteran, rarely leaving his feet unnecessarily. On contested and back-shoulder targets he goes straight up rather than leaning back into his frame the way [[George Pickens]] did at Georgia and with the Steelers, which Waldman says makes it easier for defenders to disrupt the catch. Waldman rates Lemon and [[Carnell Tate]] as having the best hands he's scouted in recent years.
- 2026-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-30]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman pushes back on the 'wide receiver one truther' consensus that ranks Makai Lemon ahead of [[Carnell Tate]] because Lemon's slot/flanker versatility reads as the 'safer' profile. Waldman still has Lemon as his WR2 in the class behind Tate, arguing the league trend toward two- and three-tight-end personnel (responding to nickel/umbrella coverages) is squeezing the slot-receiver role, so pure X/split-end value (Tate's) may age better than flanker/slot value (Lemon's) if Lemon lands on a personnel-heavy tight-end offense. He stresses this isn't a knock on Lemon as a flanker, just a structural argument for valuing Tate's X-receiver profile higher -- a trend co-host Bob Harris notes untracked analyst JJ Zachariason (Late Round Fantasy) has also written about, with the Rams as the current 13-personnel leader.
- 2026-04-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman ranked USC's Makai Lemon his WR2, calling him close to [[Carnell Tate]] on his board but more scheme-dependent, projecting him best as a slot receiver comparable in size and usage to Amon-Ra St. Brown. He praised Lemon's zone route running, contested-catch ability, run-after-catch skill and physicality, and dismissed concerns about his 4.58 pro-day 40 (he didn't run at the Combine) as an overreaction given a slot receiver needs acceleration and change of direction more than long speed, both of which Lemon has. Waldman said if a team surrounds Lemon with one or two veteran receivers to buffer him, he could be the leading fantasy scorer among 2026 rookie WRs even though Waldman still expects Tate to be the more skilled player over a full career; a bad landing spot for Tate and a great one for Lemon could flip his post-draft rankings.
- 2026-04-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-20]]) [Dynasty]: Asked whether [[KC Concepcion]]'s yards-after-catch ability is rated above Makai Lemon's, Waldman agreed it's a real gap, not a hot take -- Lemon is a tough runner who does damage after the catch, but Waldman does not have him at Concepcion's level in that specific skill.
- 2026-04-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-28]]): Eagles traded up ahead of the Steelers to take Lemon at 20 to pair with [[DeVonta Smith]], continuing the post-[[A.J. Brown]]-trade rebuild toward a versatile, movable receiver room (Dontayvion Wicks and Marquise Brown also added). Harmon pushes back on the narrative that Lemon is 'slot mostly': he's a good enough route runner at 5'11'/192 to also play outside as a Z, comparing him to Jaxon Smith-Njigba's 75% success rate vs. man (Lemon just behind), ahead of Emeka Egbuka (68% outside as a rookie) and in the same outside-viable bucket as Puka Nacua (74.2%) and Malik Nabers (74.4% despite 50% slot snaps). Ranked him a first-round, tier-one receiver prospect. His concern isn't Lemon individually but roster construction: Philadelphia's top three receivers (Smith, Lemon, Wicks at 6'1'/206) are all undersized, a potential issue in contested-catch, compressed red-zone situations -- paired with a Sean Mannion-led scheme shift (Eagles ranked 25th in Harmon's 'static score' metric last year and are expected to add more motion and under-center work). James Koh (untracked co-host) called it a good fit if Philadelphia pulls off that scheme transition, with Devontae Smith as the proven alpha number one.
- 2026-05-11 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-11]]) [Dynasty]: pushed on the bear case for Makai Lemon -- that Philadelphia's coordinator situation is shaky, the offense could trend down, and Lemon will be asked to do 'A.J. Brown-esque things' he isn't built for -- Waldman said he doesn't buy the argument. He expects that even if the Eagles' staff changes, they'll hire coordinators who lean into what Lemon does well rather than force a poor scheme fit, and he'd still take Lemon over the field as the number-two rookie receiver off the board.
- 2026-07-14 — According to Jake Seeley (guest, The Athletic, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: after A.J. Brown was traded to the Patriots, Seeley says he actually moved rookie Makai Lemon down this offseason despite having a higher public opinion of him than most, because an early camp injury, a likely heavy-slot role behind new OC Sean Mannion (comparing it to Omar Cooper's slot-heavy usage with the Jets), and doubt about whether Mannion's scheme gets the ball over the middle to Jalen Hurts all cap the ceiling; he has Lemon at WR31 overall and says he's already near max. Chris Harris independently lands close at WR34 PPR / WR40 standard, roughly a 7th-8th round pick, under the theory Lemon could be an Amon-Ra St. Brown-lite if the middle of the field opens up, though he stresses that's unproven until games are played. Both agree camp/preseason hype (uniform, drills, an exhibition flash) shouldn't move either rank higher since each already views him near ceiling, but a genuinely bad slow start - limited two-WR usage, no rapport with Hurts over the middle - could push both down.
- 2026-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-16]]) [Dynasty]: in a self-described 'take shopping' segment (a loosely-held opinion, not a real prediction), Harmon floated that rookie Makai Lemon could out-produce [[DeVonta Smith]] in 2026 if Philadelphia's offense trends more 12-personnel/Kevin O'Connell-style, since that would push Smith outside as the on-ball X receiver and let Lemon -- who Harmon compares in prospect profile to Puka Nacua, Amon-Ra St. Brown and Jaxon Smith-Njigba -- soak up the easy slot 'gimmick' work, especially if Jalen Hurts is rushed and dumping the ball off quickly. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) rated the scenario a 1-in-10 given Hurts' track record of locking onto Smith and their built-up chemistry, and Lemon still needing to build that rapport. Harmon noted Lemon battled a hamstring injury and had a quiet OTA period, dealt with limited slot-vs-outside clarity given [[Dontayvion Wicks]]-style questions elsewhere, but flagged strong RP success rates for Lemon as a prospect: 89% on out routes, 88% on comebacks, 66.7% on corners (small sample) -- he was especially strong on downfield outbreakers on his Iowa tape, though he doesn't run many 9-routes. Harmon personally still expects Smith to be the better fit if Philadelphia leans back into its established Jalen Hurts-friendly, straight-line vertical scheme, but sees a path to more Lemon usage ('Rashee Rice treatment,' extension of the run game) if the run game and Hurts both take a step back under new OC Sean Mannion.
- 2026-07-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-28]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Lemon profiles as a high-volume slot option in Philadelphia, fitting the archetype of slot receivers 'crushing it' in the modern NFL, and Harmon expects him to compete with Dontavian Wicks for slot snaps. He also projects Lemon as the Eagles receiver most likely to win with the ball in his hands, making him the offense's likelier 'layup' receiver relative to [[DeVonta Smith]].
