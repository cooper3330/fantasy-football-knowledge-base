# Trey McBride

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-08-27 — According to host James Koh (not a tracked expert) and guest Derek Klassen (not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): Koh calls McBride an absolute lights-out playmaker at tight end and one of the two stars, with [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], anchoring what he considers a badly underrated Arizona pass-catching group. Klassen cites the balls [[Kyler Murray]] was throwing to McBride late in 2023 as evidence Murray was already playing at a high level despite the counting stats, and lists 'another good year out of Trey McBride' as one of the explicit conditions for Murray taking a step forward in 2024. Both frame him as a genuine every-down weapon rather than a complementary piece, in an offense also adding [[Tip Reiman]] and a converted Elijah Higgins at the position.
- 2024-09-05 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Film and Theory)]] [Dynasty]: McBride is his cleanest example of updating slower than your leaguemates. Harstad was high on him coming out, McBride had a terrible rookie year, and by four or five weeks Harstad had written off the redraft season — rookie tight ends rarely produce, as he noted [[Sam LaPorta]] and [[Dalton Kincaid]] later showed is not impossible. He saw teams actually cut McBride late in that rookie season and called that indefensible: if you are not going to give a rookie tight end a full year, do not draft him. Holding through the bad rookie data, on the theory that dynasty asks 'is this guy good' rather than 'is he producing right now', was handsomely rewarded.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: McBride managers should expect hit-or-miss weeks, because [[Kyler Murray]] cannot even spot him in the red zone off pre-snap looks with a cushion he should recognize. Waldman sees the same pattern Murray had with [[DeAndre Hopkins]] — publicly blaming receivers for route errors while carrying his own significant shortcomings — and expects only one of McBride or [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] to be supported in a given week rather than both. He warned the weapons around Murray will get frustrated by season's end if this continues.
- 2024-12-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-30]]): McBride would be the best fantasy tight end in football with a good NFL quarterback — Waldman calls it low-hanging fruit that a player this good scored no touchdowns until the final week, arguing [[Kyler Murray]] is a skilled quarterback but not a very good one, a distinction he insists matters, and questioning whether Murray is a touchdown maker as a thrower. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert), who suspects Waldman of Murray-hating, counters with the production: McBride hit 1,000 yards, one of only two tight ends to do so this season alongside [[Brock Bowers]], and ranked top three in receptions (a league-leading 104), targets, receiving yards, yards per game, yards per route run, targets per route run, team target share, yards after catch and receiving first downs. Waldman's concession is that the ceiling difference is real — McBride, Bowers and [[Malik Nabers]] would all be closer to career years with high-quality quarterbacks feeding them.
- 2025-01-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-06]]): McBride should be setting the kind of records [[Brock Bowers]] set as a rookie and is close to that production level, but in Waldman's view he is still being saddled with his quarterback situation — raised while resisting the idea that Las Vegas' staff deserves much credit for Bowers. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) countered in the moment that [[Kyler Murray]] deserves at least a little credit after a four-touchdown game. The exchange is a talent-versus-environment argument about elite tight end production rather than a ranking change.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman chose [[Brock Bowers]] over McBride to start a franchise with but called it close, saying McBride 'should probably be one of the top two to three producers every week' at tight end because he has almost everything Bowers has apart from the very top-end explosion — and his explosion still grades better than average among that tier of starters. The distinguishing knock is on the Arizona passing game rather than the player: Waldman characterised [[Kyler Murray]]'s play as mediocre-to-bad with a hold-the-ball, 'it's my ball' mentality, which produced fewer highs and more mid-range outcomes than the Raiders' deliberate effort to feed Bowers.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with Arizona interested in an extension, Waldman is absolutely comfortable with McBride going as the TE2 in drafts as early as round three, and says on ability he would be comfortable even in round two. The hesitation is quarterback, not player: it is either McBride or [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] who thrives with [[Kyler Murray]], and Waldman thinks McBride has the better chance of sticking as the main guy. He also lists McBride second among tight ends he would take over [[Travis Kelce]] today, behind [[Brock Bowers]].
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates McBride a 2 out of 10 — [[Kyler Murray]] throws every single pass to him. Harris rates him a 5, purely as a scaled version of his [[Brock Bowers]] objection to early tight ends: he is still probably not taking a tight end in the third round, but it gets less offensive to him there, so McBride does not get an 8. On the player Harris is emphatic — the hands and body control at that size are ridiculous, and the notion that McBride cannot score touchdowns is 'foolishness beyond foolishness'.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon has nothing negative to say about McBride — a wonderful player, one of the true needle movers at tight end and one of the rare guys you can actually run an offense through — but predicted [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] outproduces him in 2025. His reasoning is a general pattern: when an offense was run through one player and everyone finished the year complaining about the offense, the target hierarchy is more up for debate than the market assumes. Harmon is not calling for McBride's role to be minimized, only for Harrison to be maximized. James Koh (not tracked) noted McBride's 147 targets and 111 receptions in 2024, essentially matching [[Brock Bowers]]'s 112 catches.
