# Trey McBride

## Expert Takes

- 2024-08-27 — According to host James Koh (not a tracked expert) and guest Derek Klassen (not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): Koh calls McBride an absolute lights-out playmaker at tight end and one of the two stars, with [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], anchoring what he considers a badly underrated Arizona pass-catching group. Klassen cites the balls [[Kyler Murray]] was throwing to McBride late in 2023 as evidence Murray was already playing at a high level despite the counting stats, and lists 'another good year out of Trey McBride' as one of the explicit conditions for Murray taking a step forward in 2024. Both frame him as a genuine every-down weapon rather than a complementary piece, in an offense also adding [[Tip Reiman]] and a converted Elijah Higgins at the position.
- 2024-09-05 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Film and Theory)]] [Dynasty]: McBride is his cleanest example of updating slower than your leaguemates. Harstad was high on him coming out, McBride had a terrible rookie year, and by four or five weeks Harstad had written off the redraft season — rookie tight ends rarely produce, as he noted [[Sam LaPorta]] and [[Dalton Kincaid]] later showed is not impossible. He saw teams actually cut McBride late in that rookie season and called that indefensible: if you are not going to give a rookie tight end a full year, do not draft him. Holding through the bad rookie data, on the theory that dynasty asks 'is this guy good' rather than 'is he producing right now', was handsomely rewarded.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: McBride managers should expect hit-or-miss weeks, because [[Kyler Murray]] cannot even spot him in the red zone off pre-snap looks with a cushion he should recognize. Waldman sees the same pattern Murray had with [[DeAndre Hopkins]] — publicly blaming receivers for route errors while carrying his own significant shortcomings — and expects only one of McBride or [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] to be supported in a given week rather than both. He warned the weapons around Murray will get frustrated by season's end if this continues.
- 2024-12-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-30]]): McBride would be the best fantasy tight end in football with a good NFL quarterback — Waldman calls it low-hanging fruit that a player this good scored no touchdowns until the final week, arguing [[Kyler Murray]] is a skilled quarterback but not a very good one, a distinction he insists matters, and questioning whether Murray is a touchdown maker as a thrower. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert), who suspects Waldman of Murray-hating, counters with the production: McBride hit 1,000 yards, one of only two tight ends to do so this season alongside [[Brock Bowers]], and ranked top three in receptions (a league-leading 104), targets, receiving yards, yards per game, yards per route run, targets per route run, team target share, yards after catch and receiving first downs. Waldman's concession is that the ceiling difference is real — McBride, Bowers and [[Malik Nabers]] would all be closer to career years with high-quality quarterbacks feeding them.
- 2025-01-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-06]]): McBride should be setting the kind of records [[Brock Bowers]] set as a rookie and is close to that production level, but in Waldman's view he is still being saddled with his quarterback situation — raised while resisting the idea that Las Vegas' staff deserves much credit for Bowers. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) countered in the moment that [[Kyler Murray]] deserves at least a little credit after a four-touchdown game. The exchange is a talent-versus-environment argument about elite tight end production rather than a ranking change.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman chose [[Brock Bowers]] over McBride to start a franchise with but called it close, saying McBride 'should probably be one of the top two to three producers every week' at tight end because he has almost everything Bowers has apart from the very top-end explosion — and his explosion still grades better than average among that tier of starters. The distinguishing knock is on the Arizona passing game rather than the player: Waldman characterised [[Kyler Murray]]'s play as mediocre-to-bad with a hold-the-ball, 'it's my ball' mentality, which produced fewer highs and more mid-range outcomes than the Raiders' deliberate effort to feed Bowers.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with Arizona interested in an extension, Waldman is absolutely comfortable with McBride going as the TE2 in drafts as early as round three, and says on ability he would be comfortable even in round two. The hesitation is quarterback, not player: it is either McBride or [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] who thrives with [[Kyler Murray]], and Waldman thinks McBride has the better chance of sticking as the main guy. He also lists McBride second among tight ends he would take over [[Travis Kelce]] today, behind [[Brock Bowers]].
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates McBride a 2 out of 10 — [[Kyler Murray]] throws every single pass to him. Harris rates him a 5, purely as a scaled version of his [[Brock Bowers]] objection to early tight ends: he is still probably not taking a tight end in the third round, but it gets less offensive to him there, so McBride does not get an 8. On the player Harris is emphatic — the hands and body control at that size are ridiculous, and the notion that McBride cannot score touchdowns is 'foolishness beyond foolishness'.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon has nothing negative to say about McBride — a wonderful player, one of the true needle movers at tight end and one of the rare guys you can actually run an offense through — but predicted [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] outproduces him in 2025. His reasoning is a general pattern: when an offense was run through one player and everyone finished the year complaining about the offense, the target hierarchy is more up for debate than the market assumes. Harmon is not calling for McBride's role to be minimized, only for Harrison to be maximized. James Koh (not tracked) noted McBride's 147 targets and 111 receptions in 2024, essentially matching [[Brock Bowers]]'s 112 catches.
- 2025-10-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-03]]): McBride is a sell for Harris on valuation, not on player quality — FantasyPros has him and [[Brock Bowers]] 19th and 21st overall, ahead of [[Ashton Jeanty]], and Harris says he would not spend an end-of-second-round pick on either today. He likes having these tight ends and calls the playoff-week advantage 'so fun' when they score 16 and the opponent gets five at the position, but argues other positions help you more relative to the replacement-level tight ends most leagues start. Concretely, he endorses Andy Behrens's suggestion (not a tracked expert) that [[Davante Adams]] for McBride is a deal to jump on — 'oh my god, please do it' — and would consider trading McBride for [[J.K. Dobbins]] or [[Kenneth Walker III]], describing both tight ends as round-four value now rather than free.
- 2025-10-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-14 (Week 7 Waiver Wire & More W6 Film)]]): McBride was even better than his box score against Indy -- on a third-and-17 he beat coverage deep to convert, caught a 1-yard TD once [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]'s replacement package was in, and felt strongly he was interfered with on a critical fourth-down target at the end of the game. Harris says he and Warren are absolutely locked-in weekly starters.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06 (Players We Think We Know)]]) [Dynasty]: another split with co-host Adam Harstad (NOT tracked), who placed McBride on the [[Sam LaPorta]] end of the tight end spectrum. Waldman puts him a little higher — better than [[Tucker Kraft]], possibly in his own tier between [[George Kittle]]/[[Brock Bowers]] and everyone after them. His stated reason McBride hasn't shown it fully is quarterback play: Waldman says he has known who [[Kyler Murray]] is for a long time and the Cardinals have now figured it out too. He argues McBride could sustain his production with a quarterback who is a somewhat higher-end version of [[Jacoby Brissett]] — someone who spreads the ball around, can do some of what Murray does with McBride as a first read, but can also get to him as a second and third read.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: McBride is the clearest beneficiary of Arizona's quarterback change and receiver injuries. Harmon notes he has six touchdowns with [[Jacoby Brissett]] versus six touchdowns in his entire career before this year, and that he is targeted on 38% of his routes when Brissett is blitzed. Because San Francisco blitzes heavily with no pass rushers left, and Brissett has held up against the blitz at a 55% success rate per Next Gen Stats, Harmon says to look for McBride to see maybe 20 targets in the game — Koh's version is 40, in jest — and regrets not putting an over on his receptions in the picks segment. The framing is that the quarterback change, not the tight end, changed.
- 2025-11-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-18]]): McBride had another double-digit catch game against San Francisco, and Harmon noted he has now caught more touchdowns this season than he had in his entire prior NFL and college career combined, another beneficiary of the passing volume [[Jacoby Brissett]] has generated for Arizona.
- 2025-12-01 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-01]]): Trey McBride scored down the seam late to cut Tampa's lead, extending a streak Harris said stood at 14 straight games with at least five catches; he now has eight touchdowns on the season. Harris used the game to needle the old narrative that 'there was something weird about Trey McBride that meant he couldn't score touchdowns' -- calling that take 'always stupid' since touchdowns are largely random. He also flagged rookie Bam Knight flashing on a couple of long, self-created runs before fumbling, with the backfield mostly waiting on [[Trey Benson]]'s return.
- 2025-12-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-16]]): [[Trey McBride]] scored from the two-yard line against Houston and drew the bulk of Jacoby Brissett's early passing volume, underscoring that he remains the offense's clear top target regardless of who plays quarterback.
- 2025-12-19 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: His number four bust for 2026 despite calling McBride's current season 'incredible' and 'historic.' Harris draws the parallel to drafting [[Sam LaPorta]] early in 2023 and [[Brock Bowers]] early this year -- both busted value relative to draft cost -- and argues tight end is too volatile a position to pay a top-12-to-17 price for, partly because of how much physical abuse the position takes. He flags 2026 offensive uncertainty in Arizona: a possible new OC and head coach, [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] returning, and how differently McBride produced with [[Jacoby Brissett]] under center versus [[Kyler Murray]]. His core stat: even in this historic season, McBride is currently only the 25th most valuable player in fantasy by value-based drafting -- 'that's the best case' outcome for a pick made in the first two rounds. Harris says he'll rank McBride one or two at TE and got him himself in the middle of the fourth round for great value, but refuses to draft any tight end that early.
- 2025-12-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-23]]): McBride got an end-zone target he failed to bring in (Harris says it's 'not a drop' since he got hit on the play), and Harris found it galling watching so many dump-offs go to [[Elijah Higgins]] instead in a game the Cardinals could barely sustain drives in. Even so, Harris says of course you're still using McBride, though the Cardinals face a tougher matchup in week 17 than Atlanta's soft one.
- 2026-01-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: raised as a legitimate alternate 2025 fantasy MVP pick when discussing the award with Cousin Josh -- Harris says McBride's production would have ranked as roughly a WR5-caliber season among pass catchers, with only [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]], [[Puka Nacua]], [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and [[George Pickens]] producing more value. Harris says he'd have been fine with someone naming McBride MVP instead of [[Christian McCaffrey]] or [[Jonathan Taylor]].
- 2026-01-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: McBride's raw fantasy point total would have ranked as the WR5 in standard and WR4 in PPR, fueled late in the year by [[Jacoby Brissett]] targeting him constantly. Harris says he still won't spend a first-round or early-second-round pick on a tight end regardless, framing it as a value-based-drafting problem: because a TE11/12 is available in the 11th round, the point gap between a historic TE1 season and replacement level is far smaller than the gap at RB or WR, so even McBride's outlier year (25th in standard VBD terms, 14th in PPR) doesn't clear the bar. He points to [[Brock Bowers]] and [[Sam LaPorta]] as recent tight-end hype cycles that disappointed, plus the 'many Kelce years' pattern of pundits chasing a repeat of an outlier season, as reasons the market will overpay for McBride in 2026 even though his talent and role (a 'wide receiver playing tight end') are legitimate.
- 2026-01-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: floated McBride as the Cardinals player he's most worried about for 2026 -- not on talent, but because he's being priced as a potential first-round fantasy tight end off a target-funnel offense that carries heavy regression risk. Harmon argued Drew Petzing's scheme uniquely made the tight end a true first read, and that if [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] and [[Michael Wilson]] are used more interchangeably next year under a new play-caller, some of McBride's target share could migrate outward even though no incoming staff would deliberately reduce his role.
- 2026-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-11]]): McBride is a strong route runner and ball-winner who plays more like 'a really good wide receiver two who gets targeted like a wide receiver one' within Kyler Murray's offense, cited alongside [[Brock Bowers]] as a move tight end good enough to supplant a team's WR2/WR3 in the target pecking order -- one of the archetypes Waldman believes could still thrive even if the league shifts toward blocking-first, condensed-personnel tight end usage. - 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris has [[Trey McBride]] and [[Brock Bowers]] as a clearly separated top tier at tight end, calling them interchangeable and possibly flipping their order, but said neither he nor co-host Josh (untracked) will actually draft either one -- they typically go end of round two/early round three, too rich for Harris's deliberately value-based approach to the position. Harris noted he owned McBride himself last year at a round-five discount, which is what made the pick a 'no-brainer' in retrospect; that price is gone now.
- 2026-06-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-01]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman cited Trey McBride's success under OC [[Drew Petzing]] in Arizona as a preferred alternative to paying up for an injury-risk tight end like [[Sam LaPorta]], grouping McBride in his top tight-end tier alongside [[Brock Bowers]].
- 2026-06-25 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: McBride is one of the two 'safe bets' the Cardinals passing offense revolves around, calling him 'the best pass-catching tight end in the league.' [[Matt Waldman]] projects McBride still leading the team in targets -- close to 160 -- and around 1,200 yards, ahead of [[Michael Wilson]] and well clear of [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]].
- 2026-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Cousin Josh (untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-14]]): both gave McBride a 6, echoing the [[Brock Bowers]] discussion -- the concern is largely quarterback play (Kyler Murray) rather than McBride's talent, layered on top of both hosts' general reluctance to draft any tight end this early regardless of who it is.
- 2026-09-01 — According to guest Ben Gretsch (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-09-01]]): raised as context for why he's drafting early tight ends less aggressively -- Trey McBride 'just broke the position' last year with roughly 50 more targets than any other tight end and about 100 more PPR points than anyone else at the position, making him (with [[Brock Bowers]]) one of the two elite options Gretsch lets fall to others while betting on [[Colston Loveland]] and [[Tyler Warren]] instead.
