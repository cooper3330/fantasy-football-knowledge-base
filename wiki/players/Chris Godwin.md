# Chris Godwin

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]): the strongest praise given to a Tampa Bay receiver in this wiki — a "complete skill-set guy" comparable to prime Rob Gronkowski in versatility: can play X, slot, or all three receiver alignments at a Pro Bowl level, which makes him much harder to replace than a receiver room can compensate for by piecing together multiple lesser pieces (unlike, in Waldman's view, Houston's [[Nico Collins]]/[[Tank Dell]] room). The only live question is health and age (getting older, injury risk) rather than role or scheme fit.
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): a retrospective critique that complicates the "role is settled" read above — Harmon calls Tampa Bay's 2023 decision to move Godwin from his natural slot role to primarily an outside flanker role "a gigantic mistake," not corrected until roughly Week 14-15. Now relevant again because OC Dave Canales, who eventually got the alignment right, is **leaving for the Carolina head-coaching job** — reopening the question of who calls Godwin's role in 2024 and whether the late-season fix sticks.
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): a downstream note rather than a new grade — Waldman flags that [[Baker Mayfield]]'s own 2024 redraft outlook would take a real hit if Godwin leaves Tampa Bay in free agency on top of a possible [[Mike Evans]] departure, underscoring how load-bearing Godwin's all-alignment role is to the offense as a whole.

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): described as "a really good two, but not a one" — cited as the reason Tampa Bay's receiver room would have collapsed into critical need had [[Mike Evans]] left in free agency, with unproven rookie Trey Palmer (not a tracked page) as the only other option.
- 2024-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-25]]): Godwin was misused outside in 2023 — a 39% slot rate after 72% in 2022 and 70% in 2021 — and Koh flags the resulting 64% catch rate as the worst in four years against a 74.5% average from 2019-2022, despite 83 catches for 1,000 yards. Harmon notes he still cleared 72% success vs man every year of his first four seasons with press marks of 80.4%, 78% and 76% from 2019-2021, then fell to 66.9% vs man and 61.5% vs press in 2022, his first year off the ACL; he has not charted 2023 yet. He calls Godwin a man-beating slot who wins on big-boy routes and a good bounce-back candidate now that Cohen and Todd Bowles have said he is moving back inside — with the caveat, drawn from [[Michael Gallup]] and [[Rashad Bateman]], that some players never recover their confidence. Koh, citing an age-28 contract year, would not be surprised by one of the best Godwins yet.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Godwin can approach — though probably not reach, and not a triple crown — peak [[Cooper Kupp]] fantasy production, and could be one of the top five options in receptions this year. The reasons: [[Baker Mayfield]] now has freedom to make calls and change plays at the line that he wasn't allowed last year, Godwin is healthy and looked good, and he is playing the slot in a Rams-style scheme. Waldman expects him to return to being one of the most underrated fantasy receivers in the NFL.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Harmon is buying Godwin's role emphatically — he is balling, playing great, and Tampa Bay has done exactly what Harmon predicted, lining him up in the slot but designing out-breaking routes for him from there, which matches where he wins on the field. Baker Mayfield is throwing to his first read at a high rate and Godwin is that first read on those plays. Asked to buy or sell Godwin outproducing [[Mike Evans]] this season, Harmon initially wanted a push, then sold — he still expects Evans to be the most productive receiver on the team on the back of touchdowns.
- 2024-10-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-15]]): Godwin is the lead example of veterans outproducing young breakouts — he is third in the NFL in first downs per route run behind [[Rashee Rice]] and [[Nico Collins]] (both banged up), with an 81% catch rate that would be a career high, 7.5 yards after catch per reception, 63% of his yards coming after the catch and 56.6% of targets producing a first down, on pace for 122 receptions, 1,448 yards and 14 TDs. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) argued it is simply the move back to the slot; Harmon says the slot is a big part but not the whole story — Godwin is again winning big-boy routes over the middle against cover two and beating man coverage, elements that had gone missing post-ACL and that his Penn State RP profile always showed. Harmon calls him a 'souped-up power slot' and the perfect archetype for today's NFL, with stock up on Liam Cohen and [[Baker Mayfield]] too.
- 2024-10-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-24]]): Godwin dislocated his ankle and is out for the year, which Harmon called a particularly brutal outcome — a contract year, playing at an elite level, and only just now rounding back into form after the long recovery from his multi-ligament knee injury. Harmon reiterated his long-standing affection for Godwin, a player Reception Perception loved as a prospect and through his early career. He declined to blame Todd Bowles heavily given the game situation, and said playing a visibly hobbled [[Mike Evans]] in the same game was arguably worse. Harmon drew the broader lesson alongside the [[Brandon Aiyuk]] knee injury: get paid when you can, because you never know what a player looks like coming back. Godwin's season-long alignment split was 65% outside / 35% inside.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon is cautiously positive on Godwin's outlook — the season-ending ankle injury is not a multi-ligament knee problem likely to bleed into next season, and he just wants good reports on the recovery. Godwin is 28 now and 29 at the start of next season, which he called not terrible for this archetype, comparing him to [[Cooper Kupp]]: no longer the player he was but still useful. He was already producing in exactly that role for Liam Coen. Tampa should try to retain him and has the cap room to do it; if not, Harmon named Arizona as a fit needing an interchangeable inside receiver alongside [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]. On price, he pointed to [[Calvin Ridley]]'s much-mocked 23-million deal now looking like nothing, and to Godwin's own 20-million average, but warned this looks like a weak receiver free agent market and that paying third-contract money to players coming off injury or uneven seasons — Godwin, [[Amari Cooper]], [[Stefon Diggs]], [[DeAndre Hopkins]] — is hard to justify.
- 2025-01-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-09]]): Harmon says that if he lowered the 250-route qualifying threshold, the actual answer for Best Underneath Wide Receiver would have been Godwin rather than [[Khalil Shakir]]. Before his season-ending injury, 17.5% of Godwin's routes on throws under 10 air yards produced a first down — leading the NFL by a lot — alongside a 74.5% success rate on those targets. Koh underlines the stakes: it was a spectacular season in a contract year, and at his age this is his last big payday, so the injury hurts on multiple levels. Both hosts flag Godwin's free agency as something they will watch closely this offseason.
- 2025-01-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-13]]) [Dynasty]: Godwin is one of the most underrated receivers of this era and could have played any role, including an Amari Cooper role, which [[Matt Waldman]] proposes and Harmon endorses. Harmon says Godwin got pigeonholed as a slot yards-after-catch player and was one of the NFL's best YAC receivers this season, and argues that archetype matters more than ever because almost nobody will drop back and rip it between two-high safeties — [[Bo Nix]] being the exception, in Harmon's telling because he is too inexperienced to know better — so everyone else matriculates the ball down the field. But at his best Godwin could also line up against press man corners and win downfield. Harmon calls him, after [[Tee Higgins]], the next-ranked free agent receiver, complicated by injury; he says Tampa should not let him out the door, but if they do he would be a perfect fit with [[Jayden Daniels]] in Washington on in-breakers and as a run-game asset, with the cap room available. Waldman calls him arguably the most versatile receiver of his generation and better than three dozen future Hall of Famers with better stats.
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): Godwin is the archetype Washington should buy — an inside-out complementary receiver who is a menace in the slot and a real contributor as a run-game blocker, exactly the orbit piece to pair with a pure perimeter X in [[Terry McLaurin]] rather than signing another man-beating X. Harmon has been on this idea for a while and expects a decently big market: Godwin is 28, turning 29 in late February, coming off a great season and materially younger than the [[Amari Cooper]] / [[Davante Adams]] / [[DeAndre Hopkins]] free agent tier, with Tampa Bay and a Liam Coen-led Jacksonville (roughly 35 million in cap space) both plausible bidders. The caveat is the season-ending knee injury and a second such injury on his record. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) adds the culture-fit argument with McLaurin and [[Jayden Daniels]] and would overpay.
