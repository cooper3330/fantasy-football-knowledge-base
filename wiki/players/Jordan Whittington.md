# Jordan Whittington

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]) [Dynasty]: not stashing the Rams rookie even in deep dynasty leagues — 'no, not at all. I want proof.' He grants Whittington was a former running back prospect at Texas but says so was Lil'Jordan Humphrey, whom Waldman rated as the better Texas player and who has bounced around the league; he does not think Whittington is much of a route runner. Waldman reads the Charles Robinson [[Deebo Samuel]] comparison as driven by size and physicality and, mostly, by clicks — his running 'molly dispenser' metaphor for camp content.
- 2024-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-06]]): the Texas rookie has no Reception Perception profile yet, but James Koh (not tracked) says the tape shows a Sean McVay archetype — 6'1, 205, almost exclusively a slot player at Texas alongside [[Adonai Mitchell]] and [[Xavier Worthy]], with 42 catches for roughly 500 yards and one touchdown. Koh calls him crazy tough after the catch in the mold of [[Cooper Kupp]], without the speed to wow anyone. Harmon agrees the Rams have a type and says camp hype matters here because [[Puka Nacua]] and Kupp both carry injury histories, while [[DeMarcus Robinson]] is a career journeyman outside receiver who would not fill the inside role if Nacua misses time — making Whittington the likelier contingency.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]): despite leading the Rams in preseason receiving yardage, 'fuck that' on believing in him now. Waldman's objection is the vanilla-defense discount — Whittington is doing a nice job with what he is asked to do, but the question is how he performs with [[Matthew Stafford]] making adjustments against defenses that are not vanilla. He also notes Whittington is a converted running back who was never used at a level that built real confidence in him at Texas, so what you are seeing is him doing what he already does well. Maybe a future, but 'his future isn't in the present.' Waldman separately reaffirms he does believe in [[DeMarcus Robinson]].
- 2024-09-03 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): his hot take is that the Rams hit another home run and Whittington outgains [[Adonai Mitchell]] and [[Xavier Worthy]] this year. Koh calls him 'slot Puka' — a lunch-pail player who was the third option at Texas behind Worthy and Mitchell, played almost exclusively in the slot, is tough after the catch and does not shy from contact, though he is a fine rather than good athlete, which is why Texas never moved him outside. [[Matt Harmon]] says he has nothing on Whittington and is more skeptical: [[Cooper Kupp]] is now almost exclusively an inside player and will block his snaps, with [[DeMarcus Robinson]] at flanker and [[Puka Nacua]] outside, so realistically none of this happens unless there is an injury — though Harmon did notice a Puka-like catch-and-turn on in-breaking routes in the preseason.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): the Texas rookie played only one snap and ran one route in Week 1 (a touchdown that was called back), which Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) both attribute to the staff not having him up to speed rather than to a verdict on him. Koh says Whittington was the guy he'd have picked all offseason to emerge as the Rams' WR3, and expects him to factor into the rotation once the coaching staff gets him ready — they weren't anticipating a [[Puka Nacua]] injury. Harmon frames the opening for him specifically: with Nacua out, the Rams need a 'tough player' to take on the rugged, physical outside role, and he doesn't think [[Tyler Johnson]] is that guy while [[DeMarcus Robinson]] is a pure vertical receiver.
