# Jordan Whittington

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]) [Dynasty]: not stashing the Rams rookie even in deep dynasty leagues — 'no, not at all. I want proof.' He grants Whittington was a former running back prospect at Texas but says so was Lil'Jordan Humphrey, whom Waldman rated as the better Texas player and who has bounced around the league; he does not think Whittington is much of a route runner. Waldman reads the Charles Robinson [[Deebo Samuel]] comparison as driven by size and physicality and, mostly, by clicks — his running 'molly dispenser' metaphor for camp content.
- 2024-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-06]]): the Texas rookie has no Reception Perception profile yet, but James Koh (not tracked) says the tape shows a Sean McVay archetype — 6'1, 205, almost exclusively a slot player at Texas alongside [[Adonai Mitchell]] and [[Xavier Worthy]], with 42 catches for roughly 500 yards and one touchdown. Koh calls him crazy tough after the catch in the mold of [[Cooper Kupp]], without the speed to wow anyone. Harmon agrees the Rams have a type and says camp hype matters here because [[Puka Nacua]] and Kupp both carry injury histories, while [[DeMarcus Robinson]] is a career journeyman outside receiver who would not fill the inside role if Nacua misses time — making Whittington the likelier contingency.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]): despite leading the Rams in preseason receiving yardage, 'fuck that' on believing in him now. Waldman's objection is the vanilla-defense discount — Whittington is doing a nice job with what he is asked to do, but the question is how he performs with [[Matthew Stafford]] making adjustments against defenses that are not vanilla. He also notes Whittington is a converted running back who was never used at a level that built real confidence in him at Texas, so what you are seeing is him doing what he already does well. Maybe a future, but 'his future isn't in the present.' Waldman separately reaffirms he does believe in [[DeMarcus Robinson]].
- 2024-09-03 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): his hot take is that the Rams hit another home run and Whittington outgains [[Adonai Mitchell]] and [[Xavier Worthy]] this year. Koh calls him 'slot Puka' — a lunch-pail player who was the third option at Texas behind Worthy and Mitchell, played almost exclusively in the slot, is tough after the catch and does not shy from contact, though he is a fine rather than good athlete, which is why Texas never moved him outside. [[Matt Harmon]] says he has nothing on Whittington and is more skeptical: [[Cooper Kupp]] is now almost exclusively an inside player and will block his snaps, with [[DeMarcus Robinson]] at flanker and [[Puka Nacua]] outside, so realistically none of this happens unless there is an injury — though Harmon did notice a Puka-like catch-and-turn on in-breaking routes in the preseason.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): the Texas rookie played only one snap and ran one route in Week 1 (a touchdown that was called back), which Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) both attribute to the staff not having him up to speed rather than to a verdict on him. Koh says Whittington was the guy he'd have picked all offseason to emerge as the Rams' WR3, and expects him to factor into the rotation once the coaching staff gets him ready — they weren't anticipating a [[Puka Nacua]] injury. Harmon frames the opening for him specifically: with Nacua out, the Rams need a 'tough player' to take on the rugged, physical outside role, and he doesn't think [[Tyler Johnson]] is that guy while [[DeMarcus Robinson]] is a pure vertical receiver.
- 2024-10-01 — According to James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-01]], with [[Matt Harmon]] broadly agreeing: Whittington is a get-in-early name. He had played 48 total snaps across his first three games, then played 56 offensive snaps in Week 4 against Chicago — the most of any non-offensive-lineman skill player in the game — leading the Rams in routes, targets (8), receptions (6) and yards (62). Koh's read is that when Sean McVay hands an unknown player that volume he is signalling trust, and with [[Puka Nacua]] out for extended time and [[Cooper Kupp]] injured there is real opportunity. Harmon was more measured, saying he has little charting on him yet but may put some up after this game; he likes that Whittington fits a Rams offense prioritizing physicality, run-after-catch and receiver blocking. Alignment: about 41% out wide, roughly 60% inside — nearly all inside at Texas only because [[Xavier Worthy]] and [[Adonai Mitchell]] played outside.
- 2024-10-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-17]]): Whittington is the Rams receiver Harmon is most curious about. He leads the NFL in team offensive success rate when targeted at 73.9% — a down-and-distance measure, not a Reception Perception number — and the only other player above 70% is [[Ja'Marr Chase]], with [[Chris Godwin]], [[Chris Olave]] and [[Tutu Atwell]] rounding out the top five. Whittington has played about 56% out wide and 40% in the slot, which Harmon flags as unusual given he was almost exclusively a slot player at Texas alongside [[Xavier Worthy]] and [[Adonai Mitchell]]; Harmon does not think he has played well outside this year. The open question is what his role becomes when [[Cooper Kupp]] returns and reclaims the slot. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) was the more bullish of the two — 'he can play football,' a tough dude who fits the Rams mold, and if anyone unlocks him it is Sean McVay.
- 2025-02-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-26]]): Whittington is the second-most interesting player in the Rams receiver room behind [[Puka Nacua]] now that [[Cooper Kupp]] is being moved on and [[Tutu Atwell]] and [[DeMarcus Robinson]] hit free agency — Harmon said 'I love Jordan Whittington' and confirmed he will get a full Reception Perception charting profile this offseason, likely in the second-year player drop in May. The stated caveat is that he remains an unproven commodity: some flashes, but injuries in 2024 meant he was never fully unleashed. Harmon's read is that Whittington is the internal option but not enough on his own, which is why he expects the Rams to be active in the receiver market.
- 2025-05-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-30]]) [Dynasty]: Koh (not tracked) puts Whittington in tier seven as one of his 'sleeper king' names, arguing that with [[Cooper Kupp]] gone Sean McVay could slide him into the slot he played at Texas and unlock something. Harmon agrees the OTA reports — Whittington got a bigger role while [[Puka Nacua]] had an excused absence and reportedly performed well — are a start on building a drumbeat, while cautioning hard against grand OTA proclamations. His structural argument for Whittington is that condensed formations blur slot and outside roles, so even with Nacua and [[Davante Adams]] taking slot work there is still room on the depth chart for another slot player. The counter he raises himself: McVay's obsession with tight ends — the failed [[Brock Bowers]] trade-up, the [[Tyler Higbee]] target surge, and the second-round pick spent on [[Terrance Ferguson]] — may mean 12 personnel blocks that path. He notes Whittington is a known hard worker with strong character reports going back to Texas.
- 2025-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]): Whittington profiles as a big slot — he was a full-time slot at Texas, lining up consistently between Adonai Mitchell and [[Xavier Worthy]] — and Harmon says he could be a down-the-line [[Cooper Kupp]] type of player for the Rams, though he is unsure he will get to chart him in the preseason unless there is real camp pop. James Koh (not a tracked expert) likes the player a lot in real-life terms but is explicitly not interested in fantasy: [[Kyren Williams]] takes a huge share, [[Matthew Stafford]] will feed [[Davante Adams]] and [[Puka Nacua]], and Whittington is not a full-time player because Adams and Nacua both slide into the slot themselves. Heavy 12 personnel with Tyler Higbee and [[Terrance Ferguson]] further suppresses the Rams' slot snaps.
