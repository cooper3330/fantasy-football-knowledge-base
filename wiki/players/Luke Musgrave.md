# Luke Musgrave

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): had Musgrave ranked 35th overall pre-draft (7th tight end) — higher than most of the industry, on athletic ability — but in hindsight calls him "slightly overrated" relative to teammate [[Tucker Kraft]], whom he had in the same tier 15 spots lower on his board. Grades Musgrave's speed as an asset but sees him as less refined than Kraft in the areas where tight ends get most of their targets.

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Dynasty]: reiterates that his athletic ability and speed are real, well-liked traits, but treats the comparison to [[Tucker Kraft]] as "more of a price thing than anything else" — Kraft offers more in the middle of the field and the shallow areas at a lower cost.
- 2024-07-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]): his prospect model liked Musgrave more than Michael Mayer coming out of college, and he expects Musgrave to reclaim the '1A' receiving tight end role in Green Bay this year over Tucker Kraft. He pushed back on the narrative that Kraft passed Musgrave down the stretch last season, noting Musgrave was playing at less than 100% off injury during that late-season stretch, and that Musgrave's per-route and target-share numbers pointed to him as the lead tight end when healthy. Waldman also likes Musgrave's 'climbable depth chart': Green Bay's WR room (Dontayvion Wicks, Romeo Doubs, Jayden Reed, Christian Watson) is talented but no one is a locked-in 20%+ target-share player, leaving room for a tight end to exceed projection -- he put a 15% target share, which he said would make Musgrave a TE1, well within Musgrave's range.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-15]]): asked whether Musgrave could do what [[Tucker Kraft]] is doing, Waldman said he could do about 80% of it, plus roughly 10% Kraft cannot — winning deep one-on-one, since Musgrave has more speed. He is a good receiver and a big, agile athlete, but not as rugged after the catch as Kraft. The verdict is opportunity, not talent: given how Green Bay uses the offense, Musgrave will only get his occasionally, and Waldman thinks it stays mostly the Tucker Kraft show even with [[Jayden Reed]] out six to eight weeks with a collarbone injury. He does not expect a Green Bay two-tight-end set that works like [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] and [[David Njoku]] in Cleveland, and instead expects the vacated snaps to go to [[Savion Williams]], Malik Heath or [[Matthew Golden]].
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): 'feel it' on Musgrave mattering now that [[Tucker Kraft]] has torn his ACL, largely because of the volume of free, uncontested flat and gimme throws Green Bay's tight end has been getting. Musgrave is not as physical as Kraft but is quicker, a little more elusive and a very good contested-catch receiver; used the same way, Waldman estimated he gives you about 80 to 90 percent of what Kraft produced. Bob Harris (not tracked) recalled that Musgrave was the tight end Green Bay drafted to be Kraft before Kraft, and that beat writers steered him to Kraft as the one to watch — now Musgrave finally gets the opportunity.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-04]]): Musgrave is his number four waiver add of Week 10 at just 1% rostered, so available in effectively every league, and the direct replacement for [[Tucker Kraft]]'s torn ACL. Harris notes the two were drafted the same year and that he has always remembered how enthusiastic the Packers front office was about Musgrave. The caveat is that Musgrave almost certainly cannot block like Kraft, and blocking was exactly why Kraft never left the field, so Harris expects rotation rather than a clean inheritance of the role — if you were riding Kraft, taking a one-week chance on Musgrave is fine, but do not assume the same snap share. Musgrave made a few catches on Green Bay's late tying drive. Jeff Erickson of RotoWire (not tracked) also had him on his list.
