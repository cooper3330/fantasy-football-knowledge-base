# Roschon Johnson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his name as "Roshon
> Johnson"; normalized here to the real name, Roschon Johnson.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]): at his current Underdog best ball ADP of RB35, Waldman thinks that price is probably close to Johnson's peak-production ceiling for 2024. Credits his tackle-breaking ability and receiving chops, but downgrades the practical receiving impact — expects the pass-catching flashes to show up "once a week or once every other week" rather than at the highlight-reel rate fans assume from seeing the same few plays looped on social media. Prefers backfield-mate [[Khalil Herbert]] as the better value at the position given Herbert's superior explosiveness and instincts, and expects both backs to be underpriced as a tandem because the position group's efficiency projection is muddied by Chicago's still-unresolved rookie-quarterback situation. [Best Ball]

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]] [Best Ball]: notes he's "a little less dialed in" on Johnson relative to [[Khalil Herbert]] following Chicago's addition of [[DeAndre Swift]], but still views him as the cheaper of the two backs (RB37 vs. RB41 on Underdog prior to the move) and floats Herbert, not Johnson, as the more likely trade chip if the team consolidates the backfield.
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): in breaking down Chicago's crowded backfield, described Johnson as the piece who 'can catch, can block, and can run with power' alongside [[DeAndre Swift]] and [[Khalil Herbert]], part of a trio Waldman projects combining for roughly 1,600 rushing yards and 10 touchdowns.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: asked to choose between Johnson and [[Kyle Monangai]], Angelo takes Johnson early in the season and Monangai late. The reasoning is that the physical B-back role in a Ben Johnson system — if it resembles Detroit's — matters, and Roschon Johnson's pass protection was his calling card at Texas, which is what earns the low-red-zone and high-leverage work that will not go to [[DeAndre Swift]]. [[Matt Waldman]] endorses the take, adding that trust in a pass protector takes real game action to establish because camp cannot simulate it. If Johnson misses time or underperforms, Angelo expects Monangai waiting.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Johnson is a year-three player who has fallen into a bigger role than anticipated — he expects Johnson to open the season playing the Jamal Williams role for Ben Johnson, a lucrative one Angelo noted produced around 21 touchdowns a few years back. The logic is subtractive: Ben Johnson does not want [[DeAndre Swift]] near the goal line because Swift is poor there, so Johnson gets it by default, and this is not a [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]/[[David Montgomery]] split where both are good in the low red zone. Angelo called him a very late-round pick with real weekly value, especially in weaker Bears matchups — some weeks will look like eight carries for 28 yards and two touchdowns, which is fine at that cost. Both hosts stressed the risk: Angelo and [[Matt Waldman]] agreed Johnson has the most to lose if [[Kyle Monangai]] pushes [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]): Johnson is getting the goal-line touches in Chicago, but Waldman's read is that the staff is partly keeping him involved so he stays sharp and does not feel devalued, rather than because the job is locked. He speculates that if [[Kyle Monangai]] proves himself early — [[Ben Johnson]] has already said they trust Monangai in pass protection, and Waldman calls him a hard, smart runner — the staff could shift goal-line reps that way and Roschon Johnson may find himself on the way out. Waldman flags this as a theory he is rationalizing from his own priors, not a report.
