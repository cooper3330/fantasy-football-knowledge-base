# Brandon Aiyuk

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]) [Best Ball]: asked whether he'd bet on Aiyuk as a true WR1 — "oh hell yeah, I'm feeling that." His case is route running: Aiyuk is the best route runner on the 49ers and has developed into one of the top route runners in the league. Context at the time: past 1,000 yards and WR14 on the season. Waldman expects the Brock Purdy connection to keep improving and says "his career is only moving up" — and adds the market angle, that he's getting Aiyuk at a bargain price anyway, so the bet costs little.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]) [Best Ball]: a strong Divisional Round matchup case against Green Bay — averages 3.7 yards per route run against single-high coverage and 3.54 against Cover 3, both looks the Packers are likely to return to after playing heavier Cover 4/two-high against Dallas. Notes Green Bay allowed the 4th-worst completion percentage and 5th-most yards per game to outside receivers between Weeks 10-17, and that CB Jaire Alexander was banged up late in the Wild Card game.
- 2024-01-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-25]]): ranked the single best pure receiver remaining in the playoffs, ahead of [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[Deebo Samuel]], [[Zay Flowers]] and [[Rashee Rice]] — "one of the best separators in the NFL, one of the most efficient receivers in the NFL," over 1,300 yards on 100+ targets, "right on the cusp" of the complete elite tier alongside St. Brown. Heading into the NFC Championship at Detroit, expects a huge game against the league's worst secondary by raw numbers — "if Ayuk doesn't go for 100 yards, I would be absolutely stunned," the only real caveat being negative game-flow if San Francisco's run game and defense make the passing game unnecessary. Also revisits the prior Divisional Round loss to Green Bay: [[Brock Purdy]] "probably had more of those throws available to him" than he took, tying back to the earlier grip/weather-driven criticism of Purdy's performance.
- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]): a quiet Super Bowl LVIII box score (6 targets, 3 catches, 49 yards) attributed to the Chiefs defense choosing to live with him beating them on a couple of in-breakers against a safety rather than let [[Deebo Samuel]] work against linebackers in space. In the offseason-outlook segment, calls him "woefully underpaid" on a fifth-year option paying $14 million, floats Kansas City as a hypothetical trade suitor ("I'd call San Francisco, see what's up with Brandon Aiyuk") while doubting he's realistically available given San Francisco's contender window, and flags him as one leg of an approaching roster crunch — not all three of Aiyuk, [[Deebo Samuel]] and George Kittle survive on the roster past 2024 given the cap picture.

- 2024-04-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-15]]: with Aiyuk's camp pushing for a new contract (not formally requesting a trade) and Aiyuk unfollowing the 49ers on social media, names Buffalo as his top non-49ers fit — a team needing a full route-tree runner with after-catch skills to complement [[Stefon Diggs]]'s departure — followed by Carolina (lead receiver alongside [[Diontae Johnson]] as the complement) and New England (bare receiver room behind Kendrick Bourne, not a tracked page).
- 2024-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: ranks [[Brandon Aiyuk]] as his dynasty WR8, calling him a top-8-to-10 receiver overall who comps as part-[[Keenan Allen]]/part-[[DeAndre Hopkins]] based on 2022 RP success-rate data; amid trade rumors (his agent denied a formal demand), argues San Francisco must prioritize re-signing him as the offense's premier X-receiver press-coverage beater rather than doubling down on [[Deebo Samuel]] and [[George Kittle]].
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman pegs it roughly 80/20 likely that Brandon Aiyuk, entering the final year of his contract and unlikely to be extended by San Francisco in Waldman's view, is playing elsewhere by 2025 -- with [[Ricky Pearsall]] projected to be the beneficiary of that departure.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said he'd take Aiyuk over [[Jaylen Waddle]] on talent, calling him 'a very good route runner' and 'an all-around stud' coming off nearly 18 yards per catch on 75 catches last year. He tied Aiyuk's fit directly to Brock Purdy, whom he described as a great anticipation/timing passer who needs strong route runners — a role Aiyuk fills and [[Deebo Samuel]] does not. He has Aiyuk ranked around WR20, calling that his median outcome rather than his ceiling, and noted the trade rumors around Aiyuk fell apart because 'the league would much rather have Brandon Aiyuk' than the 49ers were willing to give up; San Francisco drafted [[Ricky Pearsall]] as a lighter Deebo-type complement instead. Waldman also referenced Aiyuk's rocky rookie start under Kyle Shanahan (drafted 25th overall) as now resolved.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: On the Steelers-Aiyuk trade rumor sparked by Daniel Jeremiah's unsourced comment, both hosts say DJ's league connections mean the report shouldn't be dismissed outright, but they read Aiyuk's social-media hints as classic contract-leverage theater -- comparable to [[Deebo Samuel]]'s similar 2022 public trade-request/unfollow routine, which ended with Samuel staying in San Francisco and signing an extension. Harmon's read is that Aiyuk ultimately stays a 49er: rival teams know San Francisco's cap situation means the 49ers won't accept anything less than [[A.J. Brown]]-level compensation (a first- and third-round pick), and no team is offering that for a receiver they know is negotiating rather than truly forcing an exit.
- 2024-06-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-27]]): In a 'choose your fighter' comp against [[Nico Collins]]' 2023 season, Harmon noted Aiyuk's 2022 Reception Perception numbers (77.1% vs. man, 79.4% vs. zone, 79.3% vs. press) are almost identical to Collins', placing both in his 'tier-two superstar' bucket just below elite. He called Aiyuk a yards-after-catch-based receiver by reputation but argued his route running is actually the stronger, more defining part of his game now, and that he has the ceiling to become a true elite receiver if he lands in a more heavily-featured role. Harmon gave Collins' specific 2023 profile a slight statistical edge on after-catch explosiveness, but said if forced to pick the better overall player between the two right now, he'd still take Aiyuk. Note: Aiyuk's 2023 charting was not yet complete at the time of this episode (expected mid-to-late July), so this comparison used his 2022 data only.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman is not panicking on [[Brandon Aiyuk]]'s 49ers contract situation, expecting it to get ironed out like most star-player standoffs, but flagged that Aiyuk is being drafted at his ceiling as roughly WR12 even though his career-best finish was WR13. He said other options in that same price range -- naming [[Mike Evans]] specifically -- may be more appealing than paying full price for Aiyuk's best-case outcome, since every first-round-ish pick is drafted at a ceiling but Aiyuk is going 'a little later than that' relative to his own history.
- 2024-07-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]): the best outcome for Aiyuk's fantasy value is staying in San Francisco, because he is a timing route runner and the rumored destinations do not have timing quarterbacks — [[Jayden Daniels]] in Washington and [[Drake Maye]] in New England are 'definitely not' there yet, and Waldman says a trade there would 'ruin a perfectly good player' who would wait roughly two years for QB play. He notes the 49ers' negotiating pattern (Deebo in 2022 to July 31, Nick Bosa into September), Aiyuk's $14.1M final year, his ask of ~$30M against an offer under the $28M [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] got, and that Dallas would be the one appealing landing spot as a co-No. 1 with [[CeeDee Lamb]]. Aiyuk's ADP on Underdog has slid from 12 to 16; Waldman says if it drops much further he becomes a value and he will buy discount shares. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) notes Aiyuk has never topped 114 targets and wants that volume bumped.
- 2024-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-06]]): Aiyuk's 2023 charting is one of the best seasons in Reception Perception history — 96th percentile success rate vs man, 91st vs zone, 97th vs press, the 13th-best press score ever charted, and only the ninth time any receiver has cleared the 90th percentile in all three in a season (joining [[Davante Adams]], [[Justin Jefferson]], [[Stefon Diggs]], Antonio Brown, [[Michael Thomas]]). He did it as the clear-cut X, 80% outside and 84% on the line. Harmon rejects the system-receiver label: 93.6% on the dig (20% route rate), ~90% on the slant (24%), 60% on the nine, 91.7% on comebacks. He calls Aiyuk a top-six or seventh receiver in football and says a trade to Pittsburgh's [[Scheme vs Talent]] mismatch under Arthur Smith would be the inverse of the Diggs-to-Buffalo trade; anything less than the [[A.J. Brown]] package of a first and a third would be a cataclysmic failure by San Francisco. James Koh (not tracked) agrees the floor is a first-rounder and argues SF should not trade him at all.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): Waldman is lower on Aiyuk than he was a year ago. Beyond the unresolved contract, his concern is environmental — San Francisco's style of play gets a lot of players hurt, and their receivers in particular seem unhappy with the coach or the organization, so he sees more risk here than in taking [[DK Metcalf]] or [[Nico Collins]] later. Bob Harris (not tracked) noted the market had been drafting Aiyuk at his ceiling (WR12-13 finishes the last two years) and that the current discount, while real, is not large enough to be exciting; if a trade happens, a new city, team and offense could mean an 800-900 yard season rather than a top-12 one. Waldman expects the 49ers ultimately get a deal done — this is their pattern, citing Nick Bosa signing around September 6th after holding out all summer.
