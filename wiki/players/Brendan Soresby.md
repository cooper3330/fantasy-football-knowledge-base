# Brendan Soresby

- 2026-05-11 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-11]]) [Dynasty]: after charting five games of Brendan Soresby (6'3', 235 lbs, started at Indiana then Cincinnati before transferring to Texas Tech, now facing a gambling investigation tied to DraftKings bets on his own team that could push him into the supplemental draft), Waldman called him 'one of the best if not the best' quarterbacks in his accuracy charting this cycle. He reads cover 1/2/3 well for a college passer, spots pre-snap pressure, throws with anticipation to protect receivers, and layers touch on scramble-drill shots rather than forcing pinpoint throws -- Waldman compared his ball placement instincts and arm-angle variety to 'how promising Jake Cutler once was' early in his career. Concerns: he plays almost exclusively out of pistol/spread and needs real work on 3-5-7 step drops from center (Waldman watched him trip over the line), can be too aggressive in the red zone, and needs better disguise recognition (cover 4) versus current-level pros. Pending more film, Waldman slots him right behind [[Fernando Mendoza]] -- roughly even with or just below Ty Simpson pre-draft, in the range he had Michael Penix Jr. a couple years back -- and says he's worth stashing FAAB for in dynasty. Landing spot matters: he floated the Steelers (citing reporter Mark Kaboly) given Aaron Rodgers' presence, or a long-term sit-and-learn spot like Cleveland behind Dillon Gabriel.
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-22 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Dynasty]: says Soresby is worth bidding on as a long-term dynasty build prospect -- worth a future second-round rookie pick, or a significant chunk of FAAB if you're rebuilding, reasoning that a non-competitive team isn't sacrificing anything real by spending bidding dollars rather than draft picks. Waldman rates him as one of the top three or five quarterbacks in this range and comps him to a young Jay Cutler for his arm touch and mobility in the pocket, while flagging the Cutler comp's cautionary side: Cutler's Denver-to-Chicago transition was damaged less by his own play than by a toxic first call with incoming coach Josh McDaniels (per Ted Sundquist, on record with Waldman previously), which soured the relationship before it began -- a reminder that coaching fit and staff turnover can derail even a talented young passer. Waldman previewed a full scouting report on Soresby, including where he'd have ranked him as a 2026 prospect, in his RSP dynasty newsletter this month.
