# Kyle Monangai

## Expert Takes

- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]) [Dynasty]: the Rutgers back does not strike him as extremely explosive, but has a decent enough initial burst, good contact balance, is a powerful runner and a pretty good blocker, and plays with high effort. Waldman sees potential to develop along those lines and enjoyed watching him — a developmental profile note from his summer college viewing rather than a ranking.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Going Deep)]]): comps him to Devonta Freeman — about 5-9, 210, versatile enough to run zone, gap and duo, sets up blocks well, and a determined runner who gets his pads down and works through multiple points of contact. The headline number is ball security: 410 career touches in three years with zero fumbles. Waldman ties that to his RSP grading work showing only about 3% of backs who reached elite fantasy production graded below an adequate ball-security tier, and only about 10% of RB1/RB2 finishers did. He also credits toughness — doing it on a Rutgers team that is not good, against solid Big Ten defenses. The caveat: not the fastest, and Waldman files him with the players he expects to have a longer NFL career than a big fantasy career.
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: the back he most wants to be more than his grade says. Waldman wants him to be a Ray Rice or Devonta Freeman in both phases and thinks he can get closer to the Freeman end, but the receiving game simply is not there yet. He is a smart, creative runner who runs through contact extraordinarily well, tough as nails, a creator and the anchor of the Rutgers offense — but he is listed 5'9 210 at best, and Waldman would not be surprised if he measures 5'8 200. The stated conclusion is that Waldman has not graded him better than who he is, and that this is a 'just a little bit more, please' profile rather than a projection of untapped upside.
- 2025-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is 'feeling' the comparison to [[Ray Davis]] and [[Jaylen Warren]] — a day-three back who emerges as a contributor and could grow into a lead role — and says Monangai is closer to Davis in how he breaks tackles and runs between the tackles. Contact balance, vision, footwork and ability to set up creases are all really strong, and he is one of the most determined runners in the class: with a low center of gravity, if two or three defenders do not wrap him up, third-level tacklers have to start over, so runs you expect to die at 15 yards become 35. The speed is not great; the quickness and short-area change of direction are. Receiving is the clear gap — Rutgers barely used him there — but Waldman thinks he can develop into a check-down and screen option.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]): the Bears' seventh-rounder absolutely can carve out a role, because he runs with better vision than either [[Roschon Johnson]] or [[DeAndre Swift]]. At 5-8, 211 he is a bowling-ball load carrier who can pass protect, accelerates through contact and finishes — a doubles hitter who wins on volume rather than a big-play back. The caveat is receiving: Rutgers' scheme gave few chances to see him catch, and what Waldman did see was not enough to say he is good at it. If he shows that ability, Waldman says he would comfortably be a top-15 back in this class and a potential starter. Waldman thought he was underrated as a prospect.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]) [Dynasty]: the seventh-round Rutgers back is the type who could be the surprise of the Chicago backfield. Waldman rates him as having better vision than [[Roschon Johnson]], being more versatile as a runner and possibly the best decision-maker of the Bears backs; if he shows he can catch the ball a bit better, he could take the job. The stated limitation is that he is not very explosive, and some teams weight explosion heavily at the position — but Waldman argues Ben Johnson's history with Jamal Williams and [[David Montgomery]] shows there is a defined role for the slower, savvier runner in this scheme. Waldman expects a split in which Johnson gets short-yardage work because the Bears 'know what Johnson is,' with Monangai having to prove himself first.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): Waldman has the rookie at RB32 in his Footballguys rankings — an aggressive placement Bob Harris (not tracked) teases him for as 'edgy.' The framing is explicitly two-sided: Monangai and [[DeAndre Swift]] could move in opposite directions, with Swift continuing to rise and Monangai falling, and Roschon Johnson also in the mix, so this is a stated position rather than a confident one. Waldman's supporting logic is Ben Johnson's Detroit track record of carrying multiple fantasy-relevant backs — multiple top-24 finishers in each of the last three years, sometimes top 12 — which leaves room for a second Chicago back to matter.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Monangai is an interesting blend of Chicago's two incumbents — not as obviously fast or explosive as [[DeAndre Swift]], but tougher between the tackles, able to make defenders miss in space, good in pass protection and good in the low red zone. That means he can eat off both [[Roschon Johnson]]'s and Swift's plates. Angelo's split verdict: Roschon Johnson early in the year, with Monangai becoming meaningfully more impactful as the season goes deeper. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees the take is the most reasonable read, cautioning that it usually takes time for a staff to trust a rookie in pass protection because live game action, not practice, is where that is proven.
- 2025-07-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-28]]) [Dynasty]: 'fuck it' on Monangai being a lock for the Chicago RB2 job, but he is closer to a lock than [[Roschon Johnson]] supporters think and is someone to watch closely. From the Rutgers film, Waldman says Monangai runs just as hard as Johnson, has better vision, more quickness and change of direction, and more ceiling of growth. His stylistic comp is Ray Rice — explicitly on the field only, Waldman separating the player from what Rice did to himself and his wife — and while he does not think Monangai gets as good as Rice at his peak, he could be good enough to be a fantasy starter in the top-20 to top-24 RB range if he adapts fast. Bob Harris (untracked) says Zach Pearson of Bears Report frames it the same way, with D'Andre Swift the primary and [[Roschon Johnson]] the short-yardage back; both agree nobody should be drafting Monangai in redraft, but he belongs on deeper dynasty radars.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]) [Best Ball]: Monangai is his single biggest outlier in the Footballguys rankings — he has him as a low-end starter in a three-RB league and expects him, not [[Roschon Johnson]], to be Chicago's clear-cut No. 2. The trigger is Ben Johnson praising his pass protection ('we can rely on Kyle Monangai this year… I feel like we can go through the year with this guy'), which Waldman calls huge given [[Caleb Williams]] was sacked 68 times. His scouting shorthand: NFL-caliber backs out of Rutgers arrive used to muddied creases and linemen who do not reach their blocks, so the NFL looks easier — he cites [[Isiah Pacheco]] — and that adversity made Monangai a better on-field processor and a tough, physical runner. Waldman adds that if [[DeAndre Swift]] gets banged up, Monangai could be an every-week starter, while noting Chicago's failure to add a front-line back signals real satisfaction with him.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Monangai is his rookie riser and the Bear with the most to gain. He does not see a long-term NFL starter but does see a surprisingly good secondary back, and weighted heavily that the coaching staff says it is confident going into the season with him and can count on him in pass protection — the part of his game they were least sure about. The trait Waldman keeps returning to is relentless physical toughness, which he says is why [[David Montgomery]] and Jamal Williams got so much run. He also argued Rutgers backs have played behind poor lines, so they are practiced at mitigating losses and are less burdened in interior decision-making. The scenario: if Monangai comes on, Chicago can bump [[Roschon Johnson]] out of the red-zone role; [[Brandon Angelo]] agreed completely and said if Johnson does not show, it becomes the Monangai show near the goal line [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]): Waldman says Monangai is one of only two massive outliers in his Footballguys rankings (the other being [[Rashee Rice]] on the hamstring-LCL combination injury, where he defers to [[Brandon Angelo]]'s take) — he has had Monangai as roughly a top-25 to top-30 runner since June and would rather go down with the ship, praising a runner who takes two tacklers to bring down and then drags them another 15 yards. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked), who covers the Bears camp reports, has had Monangai ahead of [[Roschon Johnson]] since mid-June and calls him one of the most mispriced players in fantasy. Kluge highlights a split between ESPN's Courtney Cronin reporting Roschon Johnson locked down RB2 with passing-down and goal-line work, and the coaching staff never once saying Johnson's name — while Eric Bieniemy raves about Monangai, Declan Doyle praises him, and Ben Johnson called him a guy they can lean on this fall. Kluge cites Anthony Amico's comp of Monangai's height, weight, 40 and 10-yard split to Jamaal Williams, who produced in Ben Johnson's offense.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]): Waldman notes [[Ben Johnson]] has already said publicly that Chicago can trust Monangai as a pass protector going into the season, which is the specific competency that unlocks rookie playing time. He describes Monangai as a hard runner and a smart runner, and speculates that if he proves himself early the staff could start feeding him goal-line reps currently going to [[Roschon Johnson]] — with the caveat that the coaching staff also does not want to signal to Johnson that they do not value him. Waldman frames it as the same dynamic he sees with [[Luther Burden III]] and [[Olamide Zaccheaus]]: the veteran holds the role while the younger, cheaper option earns trust. Guest Dan Hatman (Scouting Academy, NOT tracked) reinforced that pass protection is exactly the isolated skill coaches script practice periods to test.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Monangai, 5% rostered, is the one waiver name both hosts independently ranked — Harris's number four and the number five of guest BRANDON FUNSTON of The Athletic (NOT a tracked expert). Funston liked him in college watching him against Washington, calling him a bowling ball and a yards-after-contact machine, and bets that if Ben Johnson imports the dual backfield he ran in Detroit, the guy he drafted fills the [[David Montgomery]] short-yardage role; Funston says flatly he has never been a [[Roschon Johnson]] fan and thinks Johnson runs 'like football Frankenstein.' Harris agrees on the players — he does not think [[DeAndre Swift]] is good and thinks Roschon Johnson is really not good — but pushes back hard on the framing, saying the certainty with which people assert that a 'David Montgomery role' exists in Chicago is dumb, since it may have been circumstance rather than scheme, and nobody will know until Monday night.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Going Deep Listener Q&A)]]): a watch-list name rather than an actionable one. Angelo says Monangai has gotten very little work outside garbage time in the Detroit game and that the entire non-[[DeAndre Swift]] Chicago backfield has under five carries on the season. Waldman says he would keep an eye on Monangai and [[Roschon Johnson]] because Swift's inefficiency behind the league's best yards-before-contact line means a change is coming, but stresses that neither back has done anything yet to allow a judgment. Both expect Chicago to either settle on one or two backs or bring in a veteran to toughen the run game.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): 'fuck it' on Monangai having earned the lead role regardless of [[DeAndre Swift]], despite 198 total yards on 29 touches — Waldman's caution is that Cincinnati is an awful run defense. He said Monangai played very well and showed everything he had described from his Rutgers tape, when Waldman had him as one of his highest-rated sleepers and ranked him sky-high at Football Guys expecting Swift to fail; Swift instead figured it out and it is his job. He believes Monangai can handle volume, runs hard and is physical, and may have earned more touches from a cautious Ben Johnson. The revisit condition he set: if Swift misses another couple of weeks and Monangai posts even half to 60 percent of this yardage against tougher run defenses, starting with the Giants.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): Monangai made his first start with [[DeAndre Swift]] injured and ran for 176 yards, reaching 100 by the first drive of the third quarter. Harris credits it as 'great stuff' but adds an explicit context caveat — against this Bengals defense 'it's also hard to know where the bad tackling ends and Monangai begins', and he offers Britain Brown's touchdown run through 'the widest lane you've ever seen' as proof of how bad the defensive effort was.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06]]): Ben Johnson has seen enough to give Monangai a real role. Waldman describes a consistent ball carrier who finds the crease he is supposed to find, hits it hard, and needs two defenders to bring him down — November-December football. He had roughly 44-46% of snaps in the Ravens game before the Bengals breakout, and the Bears said publicly he has earned more opportunities. Angelo projects him as the [[David Montgomery]] of this offense: the on-schedule, low-red-zone, touchdown-scoring complement who takes pressure off [[Caleb Williams]], with [[DeAndre Swift]] closer to an even split when healthy, and doubts Swift is back in Chicago next year. Waldman's fantasy framing is explicit — use him as an RB3 or RB4 with RB1/RB2 upside, and do not pay RB1/RB2 prices anticipating the ceiling. Both float a [[Jaylen Warren]] / [[Kenneth Gainwell]] style split that could break either way.
