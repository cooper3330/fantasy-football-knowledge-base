# Luke McCaffrey

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. Son of Ed McCaffrey and brother of
> [[Christian McCaffrey]]; played receiver at Rice after starting his college
> career at quarterback.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast on the 2024 WR class A-Z ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): has room to be a savvier route runner with his stems and setups, but has a good feel for pursuit in the open field and times his movements well to avoid contact. Patient on perimeter runs from the backfield behind pulling linemen and receivers, and — "surprise, surprise" — knows how to finish as a runner and uses his frame to work through contact. Waldman: he "looks like a player who spent a lot of time on executing the fundamentals of handling a football" with Christian and their father Ed.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): a high-floor pick — expects him to be in the league a long time even without a single elite-level trait, playing efficient, technically sound football for someone who hasn't played receiver long. Projects him drafted in the fifth or sixth round range as a player who can help an NFL receiver room.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo picks McCaffrey as a player who could sizzle quietly through camp and then produce in games, projecting he may be the most reliable target in Washington's offense outside of [[Terry McLaurin]]. He praises McCaffrey's work at the catch point and through the middle of the field, arguing Washington badly needs a safety-blanket target next to McLaurin's more vertical, level-three skill set.
- 2024-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-16]]): Harmon pushed back on hype around McCaffrey (which he attributes partly to name recognition), noting he's a Rookie Roundup-graded developmental prospect on a thin sample -- his best charted routes were slants and flats, which fits if new OC Kliff Kingsbury installs an Air Raid-style horizontal passing scheme. Harmon liked his hands in tight coverage but said he's still figuring out route running at a high level, and projects him at best as a short-area slot receiver -- a fit behind departed slot man [[Curtis Samuel]] -- with real bust risk if he needs more development time than a team craving instant impact can afford. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) was openly skeptical that McCaffrey deserves the buzz he's getting. [Dynasty]
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): Washington will hope McCaffrey grows into the WR3 role while [[Olamide Zaccheaus]] gets the initial opportunity. Asked directly whether McCaffrey is a solid dynasty prospect a year or two from production, Waldman said 'without a doubt' — he can be a WR2 in this league, with the ability to eventually become a good route runner, good athletic ability, and a smart player who reads coverages at a more advanced level because he was the starting quarterback at Nebraska before transferring to Rice and switching to receiver. He also noted McCaffrey can run. The frame Waldman offers is the family one: his father slowly worked his way up in a Zaccheaus-style path into becoming a good NFL starter — not everyone is [[Christian McCaffrey]], sometimes you are an Ed or a Luke. [Dynasty]: a patient stash rather than a 2024 contributor.
- 2024-08-27 — According to guest Derek Klassen (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): a report has McCaffrey failing to crack Washington's top four receivers even after the [[Jahan Dotson]] trade. Klassen is blunt that name recognition is the only reason he was a third-round pick — 'if his last name wasn't McCaffrey, he's not going in the third round' — arguing the tape, the athleticism and the profile do not match the capital. He also notes McCaffrey has only played wide receiver for two years, so nobody should be surprised he is not yet a top-four option. Host James Koh (not tracked) treats it as further evidence the Dotson trade was nonsensical, since Washington's fallback plan behind [[Terry McLaurin]] is McCaffrey, Dyami Brown and an older Jamison Crowder.
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): reports say McCaffrey is not among Washington's top four receivers — on a depth chart both hosts say does not contain four real receivers. Harmon calls him a developmental rookie taken too early at the bottom of the third round, a strange pick chiefly because Washington needed immediate help; he names [[Javon Baker]], [[Troy Franklin]] and [[Malik Washington]] as receivers he would have preferred at or after that point. He is careful not to write off the career: on a team planning to develop him into a high-volume slot the pick would have been fine. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) is harsher — McCaffrey has played wide receiver for only two years, was not especially productive, is a short-area player at best, and 'if he did not have the name McCaffrey you're not taking this guy in the third round.' Koh stresses how hard it is to learn the position on the job in the NFL.
