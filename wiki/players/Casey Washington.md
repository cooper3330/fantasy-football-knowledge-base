# Casey Washington

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2024-07-04 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-04]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo said he's heard good things about Washington (Illinois) in Atlanta camp despite a weak college offense, flagging him as a name to watch behind [[Drake London]] and [[Darnell Mooney]] -- if he earns snaps down the stretch, or if Mooney is injured, he could be a serviceable depth option given the Falcons have little proven beyond their top two receivers.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-19 (NFL QB Lunacy)]]): a conditional deep-league note only — if Atlanta loses the first two of its closing games and falls out of contention, Waldman expects more snaps for Washington and KhaDarel Hodge if healthy, because [[Michael Penix Jr.]] worked with them on the second team and has more familiarity with those receivers than with the starters.
- 2025-07-30 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-30]]): Washington, Atlanta's 2024 sixth-rounder out of Illinois, has surprising athletic traits that Koh says he did not see on the college tape — a 4.46 forty at 6-1, 200, a 39.5-inch vertical and a 10-7 broad jump, indicating legitimate short-area burst. With [[Darnell Mooney]] out several weeks and no other clear second option, Koh flags him as a name to keep in mind, while stressing the open question is whether the coaching staff can draw that athleticism onto the field. [[Matt Harmon]] separately laments that Atlanta never added the speed vertical X type it needed, comparing the situation to Arizona's [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] deployment problem.
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-13]]): multiple beat reports out of the Falcons-Titans joint practice praised Washington, a 2024 sixth-rounder with exactly one rookie catch, who scored a deep touchdown in 11-on-11 and looked like [[Michael Penix Jr.]]'s long-ball threat with [[Darnell Mooney]] out. Harris tempers it — he does not think this puts Washington anywhere near Mooney's role in the draft sense, mainly because Mooney is expected back early in the season after his shoulder injury — and frames it as simply his first real exposure to the name. Dave Kluge of Footballguys (guest, not a tracked expert) confirms strong Atlanta buzz and jokes about the dynasty-trade overreaction it invites.
- 2025-08-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Washington is the final name on the 2025 super-deep sleeper list — a 2024 sixth-round pick out of Illinois with one career catch, now an absolute favorite of Atlanta beat reporters in camp. He has run with the starters since the [[Darnell Mooney]] shoulder injury and has taken Mooney's X-receiver job, which Harris notes matters because [[Drake London]] is often not the true X — he plays flanker and slot with motion, whereas the X has to beat press off the line. Getting off the line was considered Washington's weakness as a prospect, but his releases apparently look better, his hands are reported as great, and coaches talked him up through joint practices, where he twice ran the Mooney-style deep route to cash in on [[Michael Penix Jr.]]'s arm for big plays. Harris's caveats: he cannot even say for sure Washington makes the 53, though it seems likely given the first-team reps, and Atlanta's receiver depth behind the top is thin. Explicitly not a draft recommendation — 'we're not drafting him, but stay tuned' on whether the coaches' pretty words become playing time.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): worth continuing to monitor, though he expects [[Darnell Mooney]]'s return to cut into the role. Washington was Atlanta's most reliable receiver on the field for most of Week 1 and played big on third-down targets, and nearly had a game-winning long play that Antoine Winfield Jr. broke up with a great defensive effort — Waldman notes [[Michael Penix Jr.]] needed only slightly better placement for it to be a touchdown. Monitor-list status, not a start.
