# Blake Corum

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Going Deep - Draft Prospects)]]) [Dynasty]: both hosts have him in their top five running backs in the class, and Angelo calls him possibly "the safest prospect in this class" at running back. The case is built entirely on [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]] rather than measurables — feel for closing space in tight and open areas, not size, speed, or explosiveness. Comped to [[Kyren Williams]] and [[Devin Singletary]] as the modern template for a back who wins on processing rather than traits, with a more grounded aspirational comp to Ray Rice (small, unspectacular long speed, but excellent between the tackles and in the open field) rather than JK Dobbins (raised as a more explosive but probably unrealistic ceiling comp). Waldman notes he personally wasn't as high on [[Kyren Williams]]'s talent level as the market ended up being (treating [[Devin Singletary]] as more the "exception to the rule" back then), which is part of why he's willing to rate Corum — whom he sees as more explosive than Williams — this highly. Both expect a Day 2 pick, not the first running back off the board.
- 2024-02-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]) [Dynasty]: reaffirms him as one of the class's clear early-round talents, calling his between-the-ears skill set "very high level" — "chess master-like" understanding of leverage and timing, mistake-free as a runner, a [[Kyren Williams]]-esque player who is "the best back in the room" purely because he understands the position better than anyone else in it, not because of physical gifts. Explicitly floats Dallas as a dream landing spot — would take pressure off [[Tony Pollard]] and add a reliable, low-mistake option to the backfield. Same episode, according to [[Matt Waldman]]: places him on an explicit talent spectrum between Kyren Williams (the low-athleticism floor of the archetype) and Ray Rice (a "more souped-up, athletic version" at the top), closer to one end than the other but not naming which. Groups him with [[Jonathan Brooks]] near the top of his early-round-talent list, with [[Dylan Laube]] named explicitly as Waldman's third of that group — the precise 1-2 order between Corum and Brooks isn't stated on air.
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: reaffirms him as a versatile gap-and-outside-zone runner, fast enough to satisfy all but the pickiest speed-obsessed coaches — "Corum is no robot." Praises his understanding that initiating contact lets him dictate terms with his quickness, forcing defenders to react a step behind him.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): names him, alongside [[Caleb Williams]], as one of two "problem solvers" of the class — dictates, orients, understands and reacts to the moving picture in front of him rather than relying on athletic traits, and is prepared for whatever situation the play presents. Calls him a better, more layered problem solver than [[Trey Benson]] ("I see managers run" — Benson's own description of his running style), and says that layered game is why Corum can fit and succeed in any environment/room, even if the ceiling of that success will vary with opportunity.

- 2024-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Feel It or F@#k It* ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-01]]), with co-host **Bob Harris** (not a tracked expert): reaffirms him as "the safest running back prospect available for sure" and likely Waldman's RB2 — close to an instant NFL starter, high enough as a "rotational starter" that a couple of minor improvements in camp could have him starting immediately. Floor comp: Kyren Williams/[[Devin Singletary]]-type "land"; ceiling comp: a healthy-career Ray Rice, another 5'8"/205 back who added ~15 pounds and became a Pro Bowler — Waldman thinks Corum is probably a better runner than either Williams or Singletary. On scheme fit, calls him one of the most versatile backs in the class: works in a Chargers zone/read-option scheme (despite Waldman not wanting to see incumbent [[Gus Edwards]] pushed out), a Carolina heir-apparent role next to Bryce Young, Mike McDaniel's Miami zone scheme (with the caveat [[Trey Benson]] may fit [[Raheem Mostert]]'s role better), or a multiple-tight-end Vikings/Cleveland-style offense.
- 2024-04-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-23 (Evaluation vs. Valuation)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls Corum the safest running back in the class — floor is an immediate role contributor, ceiling is Pro Bowl caliber if opportunity and fit break right. He grades nearly every trait 7.5-8/10 with nothing elite, but highlights his vision and patience finding cutback lanes behind Michigan's massive line, his red-zone instincts (a 'red zone monster' who finds the smallest creases and follows blockers rather than bouncing outside), and his receiving ability out of the backfield. He downplays Corum's lack of elite 40 speed, arguing he 'finds the hole faster than guys who ran faster 40s.' Corum was still shaking off a 2023 knee injury and wasn't as explosive as the year prior, but Waldman expects more juice to return. On ceiling, with guest Chad Reuter (not tracked), he floated size/build comps ranging from Giovanni Bernard or Brian Westbrook (if Corum doesn't add weight) up to Ray Rice on the high end if he adds 10-15 pounds while staying quick, also noting [[Austin Ekeler]] as the kind of late-developing outcome a similar-profile back like Bernard might have had in a different situation.
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: compared Corum's path behind Kyren Williams to Chester Taylor's backup role behind Adrian Peterson in Minnesota — Taylor posted 1,500 total yards the year before Peterson arrived, then still added 800 yards during Peterson's 1,300-yard rookie season. Waldman said Corum 'is no Adrian Peterson' but could be a Ray Rice-type back, projecting a 2024 time-share with Corum overtaking Williams as lead back as soon as this year if he stays healthy, no later than 2025. He also flagged Sean McVay's history of being fickle with running back workloads as a complicating factor for projecting either player's role with confidence.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman doesn't expect Blake Corum to overtake Kyren Williams in 2024 without a Williams injury, but expects the changeover by 2025 given Sean McVay's history of cycling feature backs (Todd Gurley, Cam Akers, Williams); McVay has already praised Corum publicly, which Waldman reads as a real signal given how rarely McVay compliments unproven backs. Waldman's model is a 2006-07-style Chester Taylor/Adrian Peterson split - not identical, but roughly 800 yards for Corum in a complementary 2024 role while Williams gets around 1,200, with Corum taking over as the lead back in 2025. Waldman calls a late-round pick on Corum this year reasonable insurance.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris (not tracked) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Best Ball]: The hosts called [[Blake Corum]] (RB35, round 9 on Underdog) 'wildly overdrafted' given the Rams' history under Sean McVay of feeding one lead back (see [[Kyren Williams]]) and last year's minimal early-down usage for backups like Royce Freeman (~77 total touches) and a talented Zach Evans, who barely played. Waldman acknowledged the counterargument -- Corum's contingent value is real given last year's 4-game Williams injury cratered the offense, and Corum could see 80% snap share if Williams goes down -- but noted McVay 'doesn't like rookies usually' and Corum was reportedly viewed as somewhat of an off-field-concern prospect coming out. If Corum earns significant early playing time anyway, Waldman said that would signal McVay himself, not just the front office, is driving it.
- 2024-07-12 — Discussing the Rams backfield, JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]) called Corum 'probably' the closest size/style handcuff comp to Kyren Williams in this class -- a Day 2 pick the Rams specifically made after their lack of depth cost them in 2023, which he treats mainly as a risk factor for Williams' workload security rather than a standalone endorsement of Corum's weekly value. Waldman added that on film Corum is actually the more talented player between the tackles, with better decision-making and second/third-level creation ability than Williams, though that upside is moot as long as Williams stays entrenched as Los Angeles' clear starter.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): Waldman likes Corum and what he can do, but tells [[Kyren Williams]] managers not to panic — the Green Bay usage bump (11 snaps, five carries for 25 yards, one catch for eight yards, against roughly eight snaps in the first four games combined) reads as Corum absorbing the Ronnie Rivers role rather than the start of a committee. The gating factor is pass protection, where Williams is trusted and demonstrably reliable. Waldman expects Corum to eventually cut into Williams' time, but the timeline is not now, and he flags Sean McVay's backfield deployment as unpredictable enough that he has spent six years asking 'what the hell'.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman says he loves Corum and that if any back in this class can Wally Pip an incumbent it is Corum over [[Kyren Williams]] — the Rams are a dinged-up game or two away from Corum getting the job. But he is explicit that he is not holding his breath for that to happen in 2025, and he still expects to draft Williams as an RB1. The read is therefore a handcuff/contingency valuation rather than a standalone-value call.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Best Ball]: asked whether Corum could be on his way out of Los Angeles, Waldman said it is possible with Sean McVay but that 'Corum is a better running back than people realize,' and his extra year in the outside zone scheme is a real edge over rookie [[Jarquez Hunter]], who came from a gap-heavy Auburn system. He would not treat the RB2 job behind [[Kyren Williams]] as decided until week three or four. ADP has them nearly tied in the 16th round — Corum RB63, Hunter RB64 — which Waldman read as the market being genuinely split. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) said at that price you are not paying a handcuff premium and he would still take the chance on Corum, while preferring other late backs discussed on the episode.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): the overall split was still lopsided — 41 snaps and 19 touches for [[Kyren Williams]] to 18 snaps and five for Corum — and Harris says he is not changing any recommendations, 'except to say the snap count in the red zone was 10-8 in favor of Williams.' Corum was on the field for an entire red-zone series (the one where they force-fed [[Davante Adams]]), took a good red-zone carry on a third series, came out for Williams, then came straight back in and powered in the touchdown. Each back got three carries inside the 20; only Corum got one inside the five. Harris reminds listeners he liked Corum's limited rookie tape over the summer and says this 'at least bears continued watching' — the two even run alike, 22 and 23.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-22]]): Corum's 8 carries for 53 yards against Philadelphia (a better per-carry figure than [[Kyren Williams]]' 20 for 94) has him earning begrudging respect from Sean McVay, and Waldman expects his workload to keep growing — though not to an even split. He framed Corum as the contingency if Williams cannot stay healthy, in which case the Rams would have to do gymnastics to sort out the lead role. Bob Harris (not tracked) added red-zone work as the near-term path to value.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): guest Jeff Erickson (RotoWire, not tracked) makes Corum his number-three Week 5 add at 27% rostered, noting he gets first- and second-quarter carries, looks good when he is out there, and could get hot in a game the Rams keep riding — not because [[Kyren Williams]] is going away, since Williams has a long-term deal. Harris agrees more strongly than usual: Corum is a top-five elite handcuff right now, has looked fantastic, and if you roster Kyren you have to find a way to add him. He stresses this is not a blowout artifact — Corum works in on the second series every game, because the Rams are a throwback offense that wants to pound between the guards with both backs. Erickson counters that he would not add Corum without Kyren, since he did not make his own top five.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Corum is 'definitely working in' — 11 touches last week and eight the week before, and crucially not garbage-time touches, since both were close games, which Harris said is why people's ears have perked up relative to previous years. But it has not reached the point where you can use him 'with both barrels' like [[David Montgomery]]. Harris compared him to the proverbial TE7 problem: to be startable you need the touchdown, and if the touchdown does not come you are asking why you started a back who touched it six times. Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) said his DFS mindset says never play a backup needing everything to break right for a top-20 week — while conceding bye weeks ([[Josh Jacobs]], Brian Robinson Jr.) may force it, and that double-digit touches are easy to imagine here.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: a 17%-rostered deeper-league name in the same mode as [[Kendre Miller]] and [[Tyler Allgeier]] — backs who are actually kind of usable on their own and can get a bunch of work. Corum 'kind of left us holding the bag' on Thursday night in what Harris read as a doghouse game after a fumble and a bad drop on a pass in the flat, as though he were being sent a message — but [[Kyren Williams]] fumbled later too, so Harris isn't sure. He is now part of the offense.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-10]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether Corum is a buy-low, Waldman is 'kind of feeling it' — Corum deserves a roster spot, is a good cutback runner with nice burst, and if [[Kyren Williams]] goes down Waldman believes Corum is the guy this year and probably next year too, rather than [[Jarquez Hunter]], whom a segment of the market has been screaming for to no effect. The caveat is pass protection: Corum overextended in the same way Waldman criticized [[Jonathan Taylor]] for and gave up pressure. Waldman also noted Corum was not his own RB1 in the class.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): Corum was Harris's number three and guest **Brandon Funston**'s (The Athletic, not tracked) number four Week 11 add. Funston likes three straight games of 12-plus carries but notes the Rams do not use him at the goal line - two goal-line carries all year, one in Week 1 and one this week - or in the passing game, where [[Kyren Williams]] handles it; Harris jokes that on film 'was that Kyren Williams?', they are hard to tell apart. Both frame him primarily as insurance: worth grabbing in a deeper league regardless, but 'if you have Kyren Williams you got to get yourself Blake Corum'. Harris's caveat: 'I don't think you want to use Blake Corum or [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] in a lineup, that's the bottom line.'
