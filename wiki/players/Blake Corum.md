# Blake Corum

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Going Deep - Draft Prospects)]]) [Dynasty]: both hosts have him in their top five running backs in the class, and Angelo calls him possibly "the safest prospect in this class" at running back. The case is built entirely on [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]] rather than measurables — feel for closing space in tight and open areas, not size, speed, or explosiveness. Comped to [[Kyren Williams]] and [[Devin Singletary]] as the modern template for a back who wins on processing rather than traits, with a more grounded aspirational comp to Ray Rice (small, unspectacular long speed, but excellent between the tackles and in the open field) rather than JK Dobbins (raised as a more explosive but probably unrealistic ceiling comp). Waldman notes he personally wasn't as high on [[Kyren Williams]]'s talent level as the market ended up being (treating [[Devin Singletary]] as more the "exception to the rule" back then), which is part of why he's willing to rate Corum — whom he sees as more explosive than Williams — this highly. Both expect a Day 2 pick, not the first running back off the board.
- 2024-02-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]) [Dynasty]: reaffirms him as one of the class's clear early-round talents, calling his between-the-ears skill set "very high level" — "chess master-like" understanding of leverage and timing, mistake-free as a runner, a [[Kyren Williams]]-esque player who is "the best back in the room" purely because he understands the position better than anyone else in it, not because of physical gifts. Explicitly floats Dallas as a dream landing spot — would take pressure off [[Tony Pollard]] and add a reliable, low-mistake option to the backfield. Same episode, according to [[Matt Waldman]]: places him on an explicit talent spectrum between Kyren Williams (the low-athleticism floor of the archetype) and Ray Rice (a "more souped-up, athletic version" at the top), closer to one end than the other but not naming which. Groups him with [[Jonathan Brooks]] near the top of his early-round-talent list, with [[Dylan Laube]] named explicitly as Waldman's third of that group — the precise 1-2 order between Corum and Brooks isn't stated on air.
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: reaffirms him as a versatile gap-and-outside-zone runner, fast enough to satisfy all but the pickiest speed-obsessed coaches — "Corum is no robot." Praises his understanding that initiating contact lets him dictate terms with his quickness, forcing defenders to react a step behind him.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): names him, alongside [[Caleb Williams]], as one of two "problem solvers" of the class — dictates, orients, understands and reacts to the moving picture in front of him rather than relying on athletic traits, and is prepared for whatever situation the play presents. Calls him a better, more layered problem solver than [[Trey Benson]] ("I see managers run" — Benson's own description of his running style), and says that layered game is why Corum can fit and succeed in any environment/room, even if the ceiling of that success will vary with opportunity.

- 2024-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Feel It or F@#k It* ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-01]]), with co-host **Bob Harris** (not a tracked expert): reaffirms him as "the safest running back prospect available for sure" and likely Waldman's RB2 — close to an instant NFL starter, high enough as a "rotational starter" that a couple of minor improvements in camp could have him starting immediately. Floor comp: Kyren Williams/[[Devin Singletary]]-type "land"; ceiling comp: a healthy-career Ray Rice, another 5'8"/205 back who added ~15 pounds and became a Pro Bowler — Waldman thinks Corum is probably a better runner than either Williams or Singletary. On scheme fit, calls him one of the most versatile backs in the class: works in a Chargers zone/read-option scheme (despite Waldman not wanting to see incumbent [[Gus Edwards]] pushed out), a Carolina heir-apparent role next to Bryce Young, Mike McDaniel's Miami zone scheme (with the caveat [[Trey Benson]] may fit [[Raheem Mostert]]'s role better), or a multiple-tight-end Vikings/Cleveland-style offense.
- 2024-04-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-23 (Evaluation vs. Valuation)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls Corum the safest running back in the class — floor is an immediate role contributor, ceiling is Pro Bowl caliber if opportunity and fit break right. He grades nearly every trait 7.5-8/10 with nothing elite, but highlights his vision and patience finding cutback lanes behind Michigan's massive line, his red-zone instincts (a 'red zone monster' who finds the smallest creases and follows blockers rather than bouncing outside), and his receiving ability out of the backfield. He downplays Corum's lack of elite 40 speed, arguing he 'finds the hole faster than guys who ran faster 40s.' Corum was still shaking off a 2023 knee injury and wasn't as explosive as the year prior, but Waldman expects more juice to return. On ceiling, with guest Chad Reuter (not tracked), he floated size/build comps ranging from Giovanni Bernard or Brian Westbrook (if Corum doesn't add weight) up to Ray Rice on the high end if he adds 10-15 pounds while staying quick, also noting [[Austin Ekeler]] as the kind of late-developing outcome a similar-profile back like Bernard might have had in a different situation.
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: compared Corum's path behind Kyren Williams to Chester Taylor's backup role behind Adrian Peterson in Minnesota — Taylor posted 1,500 total yards the year before Peterson arrived, then still added 800 yards during Peterson's 1,300-yard rookie season. Waldman said Corum 'is no Adrian Peterson' but could be a Ray Rice-type back, projecting a 2024 time-share with Corum overtaking Williams as lead back as soon as this year if he stays healthy, no later than 2025. He also flagged Sean McVay's history of being fickle with running back workloads as a complicating factor for projecting either player's role with confidence.
