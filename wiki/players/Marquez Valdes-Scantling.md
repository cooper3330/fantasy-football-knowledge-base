# Marquez Valdes-Scantling

> ⚠️ Takes below are from April 2024, while he was an unsigned free agent —
> treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show* ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-11]]), buy/sell on a reported link to the Chargers: both sell as a Chargers fit specifically, calling the pairing with [[Quentin Johnston]] in an already-thin receiving room "hysterical" (Koh flags it as genuinely funny/bad, not a real endorsement). Harmon separately says he does think there are situational fits elsewhere for MVS as a deep-thread field-stretcher, provided he isn't leaned on as a top-three target earner.
- 2024-07-09 — According to James Coe (co-host, not tracked) and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: MVS, at 6'4', is one of several size-based 'cast-offs' Buffalo brought in this offseason, alongside Chase Claypool and a player referred to on air as 'Matt/Mack Collins.' Harmon thinks the archetype maps well to what the Bills actually need -- a big-bodied X who can run clear-out routes and stretch the field vertically -- freeing [[Keon Coleman]] to work the short-to-intermediate slot game. Coe was skeptical of offseason buzz around this group and generally unimpressed with Buffalo's approach at the position outside of the [[Curtis Samuel]] signing, calling the WR room a real concern for [[Josh Allen]] entering what Coe called his 'absolute peak form.'
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): after MVS became the first player since [[Brandon Cooks]] in 2016 to score two touchdowns and gain 100+ receiving yards in a half, and only the second receiver to reach 100 yards against Atlanta this season, Waldman calls him 'catnip for people chasing fantasy points' on the waiver wire — with [[Chris Olave]] and [[Rashid Shaheed]] on IR, the target vacuum is real. But his hands are worse than [[Courtland Sutton]]'s or [[Gabriel Davis]]'s: expect roughly one of three deep balls to connect in a game, so three or four targets buys a puncher's chance at bye-week production and nothing more. He explicitly tells listeners to discount last week's Atlanta line by 50-55 percent as the realistic expectation, and to manage FAAB accordingly.
- 2024-11-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]]): a sell on the three-target, three-catch, 109-yard, two-touchdown Week 10 line, even though both hosts agree the tape looked genuinely good rather than lucky — Koh (not a tracked expert) was struck by how secure his hands looked and how cleanly he got open downfield, saying he had never seen that from MVS. Harmon's sell is contextual: New Orleans had literally nobody left at receiver, with [[Chris Olave]] possibly out for the year, [[Rashid Shaheed]] already done, [[Bub Means]] hurt and Cedric Wilson banged up, so MVS was the default recipient of the play-action shots that underpin the Saints offense. He also frames this as the classic fired-head-coach interim bounce under Darren Rizzi and expects it to be a one-game bump. MVS could not get on the field in Buffalo earlier this season before being cut; Harmon notes he is finally playing with a good quarterback in [[Derek Carr]].
- 2024-11-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-29]]): MVS leads the entire NFL in yards per route run since Week 8, ahead of [[Puka Nacua]], [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] and [[A.J. Brown]] — 201 yards on 61 routes and only six catches, a spike Harmon presents as a trivia answer neither host could have guessed. James Koh says the New Orleans tape looks like a different player entirely, with visibly higher confidence and a more aggressive attacking style at the catch point, and speculates it is a last-chance-in-the-league mentality after being cut, with a nod to whatever Klint Kubiak is doing with him. The caveat is baked into the numbers: this is an extremely low-volume, boom-oriented profile built on a handful of deep receptions rather than a target-share change.
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]) on Valdes-Scantling's one-year deal with Seattle: he 'really likes that signing for what they need'. Seattle needed an outside body after trading [[DK Metcalf]], and Harmon's case is functional rather than talent-based — they just need somebody to stretch the field and keep the offense from becoming condensed, opening space for [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]]. He also flags it as another used-to-play-for-that-coach signing, noting MVS had a productive cup of coffee in New Orleans under Klint Kubiak. Koh frames all three of MVS, Hollins and [[Nick Westbrook-Ikhine]] as 'sacrificial X receiver alley'.
- 2025-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-19]]): Harmon casts the newly signed MVS as Seattle's sacrificial X in three-receiver sets — a lid lifter whose job is to clear space underneath for [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] and [[Cooper Kupp]] rather than to produce. Koh independently praises MVS's late-2024 stretch in New Orleans under the same coordinator, Klint Kubiak, calling it low-key very good and under-reported, and says the tape looks like a completely different player from the one who could not get on the field in Buffalo — attacking the football and getting after it. Both hosts caution it is not certain that form travels to Seattle, and Koh argues the roster still lacks a stronger outside presence to create genuine matchup problems for JSN.
- 2025-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-07]]): Koh (not a tracked expert) noted that Seattle's actual needle mover at X receiver is Valdes-Scantling, which he framed as the reason the team is floating [[Elijah Arroyo]] in that role at all. He conceded he loves the move conceptually because Klint Kubiak brought out the best of MVS in New Orleans, but said there is a hard ceiling on what MVS can be, with an ancient [[Cooper Kupp]] and [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] also in the room and no one who reliably wins outside. Harmon's stated preference is for rookie fifth-rounder [[Tory Horton]] to grow into the X job instead.
- 2025-06-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-30]]): teams keep coveting Valdes-Scantling as the journeyman X who stretches the field so everyone else works underneath, but Waldman's objection is that enforcing that role requires actually catching the ball — he jokes MVS's approach is buy two, get one free. He notes MVS momentarily looked great as a Saint and much less so as a Bill, and questions how much the Packers won because of him. Waldman says he is not a hater, only disappointed because he liked MVS coming out of school, and still projects roughly 60 targets, 30 catches and a bit over 500 yards, meaning the production comes in spurts. He sees MVS in a tight race for third in Seattle's pecking order with Noah Fant. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) called him a waiver-wire watch-list player, not a draft pick.
- 2025-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]): MVS is a sensible sacrificial X signing for Seattle because the offense needs someone to stretch the field while [[Cooper Kupp]] works underneath and [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] lives in the intermediate area. From roughly week 10 on in New Orleans he ran 41 routes against man coverage at 1.8 yards per route run — not spectacular but passable — with four touchdowns, three drops and a 23.8 yards-per-reception average, which Harmon summarizes as a diet-Coke version of [[Alec Pierce]]: you let it rip when he is open. James Koh (not a tracked expert), who says he is at the top of the list of people who have mocked MVS, was struck by how different he looked in New Orleans — aggressive, attacking the football, getting open downfield, visibly rejuvenated — and credits Clint Kubiak with taking the handcuffs off, while conceding he does not know whether the separation numbers back up the eye test.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-27]]): Harmon calls the Seattle cut very surprising. He liked what MVS did after joining the Saints, saying he walked in cold off the street, picked up the Kubiak system pretty well and made real impact plays, which is why Harmon thought Seattle bringing him into the same scheme was notable and smart. His release is the trigger for Harmon's [[Tory Horton]] enthusiasm, since it clears the perimeter X job in that room.
