# Patrick Mahomes

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: a specific 2024 redraft value call — expects Mahomes to be pushed down draft boards by [[Lamar Jackson]] (and possibly Jalen Hurts) at the very top of quarterback rankings, but not to fall out of the top five, "probably not far beyond four." Frames the value case purely as a cost-vs-rank gap rather than a talent downgrade — going in the third or fourth round instead of the first or second, at a point in drafts where he'd otherwise be a natural value pick if the board falls that way. Grouped with [[Travis Kelce]] and [[Rashee Rice]] as the core of a Chiefs offense with "just enough pieces" to compete with anyone, backed by a defense Waldman singles out (L'Jarius Sneed, Nick Bolton, Chris Jones) as a strong IDP core in its own right.
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): an explicit defense of an AFC Championship win with modest counting stats — 30/39, 241 yards, but a 77% completion rate and 100 passer rating against a top-tier Baltimore defense with a receiver room in question, called out as the real signal. Fourth Super Bowl in six seasons — "insane," "unprecedented" — and unlike early-career Brady, "this is Mahomes' team." The caution attached: Harmon is skeptical Kansas City can run this formula back in 2024 if the receiver room isn't addressed beyond [[Rashee Rice]] and a healthy [[Travis Kelce]] — "how much more can you ask Superman to do."

- 2024-02-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-12]]), reacting to the Super Bowl LVIII win: his fullest statement yet on what separates Mahomes as a player, not a value target. Names him "a wiser Brett Favre" — the same instinct to see through what a defensive scheme is trying to disguise rather than fully diagram it first, "but a much more artistic and creative" processor who makes far fewer of the era-defining mistakes that came with Favre's improvisation. Credits Chris Jones with the same defensive-side skill (adapting mid-series once he reads what the 49ers were actually doing rather than sticking to the called scheme), and frames the Chiefs' championship window as built on a small group of elite improvisers (Mahomes, Jones, Kelce, plus secondary pieces like L'Jarius Sneed) compensating for a receiver room that "has not been strong from a fantasy perspective."
- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]), Harmon's most unqualified praise of the season, delivered independently of Waldman's "wiser Brett Favre" framing the day before but landing on a near-identical mechanism: 399 total yards (9 rushes for 66) in Super Bowl LVIII against a defense-first opponent that left him with no healthy outside receivers, and Harmon says there's essentially nothing to nitpick — he "barely ever makes any mistakes" on pre-snap coverage diagnosis, has the league's best pressure-to-sack ratio because he escapes rushers at an elite rate, creates plays with his legs, and (unlike prior scrambling-improviser comps) is not "a reckless gunslinger" at this stage of his career. Harmon's explicit framing: Mahomes doesn't win now primarily because he's more physically gifted than the field — he is — but because his mental processing has caught up with and now exceeds his physical tools, the same growth path Harmon says [[Brock Purdy]] still has ahead of him. See [[Quarterback Processing and Confidence]].
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]): Waldman says Mahomes remains a top-two-or-three safest fantasy QB, and while he could hit 5,000 yards, Waldman is skeptical his average depth of target rises dramatically just because Xavier Worthy (4.21 40) and Marquise Brown (roughly 4.27-4.28 40) add speed. He cites Mahomes' own quote, surfaced by Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) from a Football Guys article, that he needs to get the ball out quickly and check down against cover-two rather than force the deep shot. Waldman expects Kansas City to attack cover-two more by threatening with route depth to hold safeties honest while still funneling the ball to yards-after-catch options underneath, projecting distributed, YAC-driven production rather than a true vertical-passing shift.
- 2024-09-05 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Film and Theory)]] [Dynasty]: Mahomes is his archetype for a dynasty anchor and for the 'free production' idea. His startup cost in one-QB leagues has sat at the late second or into the third round for years; you can draft him today, get four years of high-end QB1 production, and still trade him for what you paid — production only costs you something if the player is losing value over time, and young stars do not. Harstad said he does not think about anchors by position at all but by player, and Mahomes is one of the very few he can look at and be confident will still be a high-end producer three to five years out. Same logic, he noted, whether the player is at a premium position like [[Justin Jefferson]] or not.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-19]]): responding to Fran Tarkenton's claim that Mahomes does nothing Tarkenton could not do, both hosts said Tarkenton has earned the right to be crusty — but that the distinguishing trait is structure, not improvisation. Co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) argued Tarkenton could physically do everything Mahomes does, yet basically only played off-script and holds the all-time record for sacks taken and sack yardage lost (roughly 10 yards per sack), whereas Mahomes has one of the NFL's lowest sack rates and the lowest average yards lost per sack, picks his scramble spots, and gets what he needs on maybe 80% of scrambles — Harstad calls him the most dangerous rushing QB in the league even though Lamar Jackson runs more often. Waldman agreed after rewatching Tarkenton film that Mahomes is clearly better in structure. Harstad's view: Mahomes' historical uniqueness is having the improvisational ability without depending on it, unlike Tarkenton or early-career Elway.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): nothing is wrong with Mahomes's game — he is still a great NFL quarterback and that will not change; he is simply not an ideal fantasy quarterback right now, and Waldman insists those are two different things. Bob Harris (untracked) offered the analogy that Mahomes has gone back in time and put on a Donovan McNabb jersey under Andy Reid: a lot of short passing. Waldman argued the scheme is working for Kansas City even if it is not working for the ratings, the excitement level or fantasy managers — the defense is playing well, they are running the ball reasonably, and they are simply not taking shots downfield, which he is not sure they are trying hard to do. The main fantasy beneficiary is [[Rashee Rice]], running five-yard routes and turning them into production for people who drafted him at a discount.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: recounting a stir he caused on NFL Radio, Waldman held that Mahomes, 'while clearly the best quarterback in the NFL, is not a good fantasy quarterback because he hasn't been' — his last top-ten regular-season week at the time of recording was Week 12 of the prior season, while [[Daniel Jones]] already had three top-ten weeks in 2024. His framing: 'two things can be true — you can be the best at something and not good at another thing.' The corollary he draws is that he hunts fantasy quarterbacks on lousy teams playing from behind who must throw, or quarterbacks without protection who are forced to run.
- 2024-11-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-07]]): Mahomes is the cleanest example of athletic transfer in football evaluation — Waldman said that watching his tape he remarked Mahomes was 'a shortstop who's playing quarterback', and that the arm angles and body movements everyone said would not translate were in fact technically sound movements borrowed from turning double plays and fielding a baseball. Waldman credits a trainer wise enough to build strength, flexibility and maneuverability so those movements survive contact and injury, and to rehab him so the trait set does not diminish when he plays hurt. He grouped Mahomes with Joe Montana (a point guard operating Bill Walsh's distribution offense) and Tom Brady (a Michigan boxer whose pocket slipping and infighting footwork came from the ring) as evidence for the generalist-development thesis co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) drew from David Epstein's 'Range'.
- 2024-12-05 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-05]]): Mahomes is one of the most efficient rushing quarterbacks in NFL history in large part because he weaponizes the protection rules — the fake slide the defender let up on should be a 15-yard penalty 'period, full stop', and he habitually runs toward the sideline as if going out of bounds before pulling up late, knowing a defender who touches him risks 15 yards and an ejection. Harstad was careful to say this is smart, not dishonorable ('like Nikola Jokic kicking the basketball'), and that the fault lies with rule-makers, not Mahomes. His efficiency profile is noted as scrambling almost exclusively on third-and-long — running for 13 on third-and-12 — with a very high conversion rate. Waldman compared the rule-bending to [[DeAndre Hopkins]] playing like a pro wrestler in his routes: an old broom that knows all the corners.
- 2025-01-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-27]]): on the 'NFL script writers' conspiracy after the AFC Championship, Waldman grants the spot of the ball was a mistake but rejects the framing — the same stories attached to Michael Jordan's Bulls and Tom Brady's Patriots, and much of it is psychological pattern-matching to a winning record. His substantive point is developmental: Mahomes spent his early years 'playing circus football', the league answered with two-high shells that removed the deep game for everyone, and Mahomes responded by learning to play low-mistake, game-managing football — 'he's Tom Brady-ing it' — despite pre-draft reports pegging him as a reckless [[Jameis Winston]] Jr. with more mobility. He cites a Shulkopedia note that Mahomes's snap success rate was 99th percentile in that game, a mark reached by only about four quarterbacks. Bob Harris (not tracked) adds the fantasy split he defends against colleague Jeff Mans: Mahomes can simultaneously be a poor fantasy quarterback and the best quarterback in the NFL.
- 2025-02-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: a late QB1 at this point in the offseason. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) flagged that Mahomes ran 98 run-pass-option plays in 2024, tied for second-most in the NFL, and threw on 97 of them, wondering whether he is morphing back into a pure passer and whether ranking him QB5 in a mock was too low. Waldman's price depends on the receivers: whether [[Rashee Rice]] returns healthy, whether [[Xavier Worthy]] takes a step and whether the offense demands it, and what a full offseason with [[DeAndre Hopkins]] and his 'old man game' yields. He also allowed that 2024's short passing game may have been a substitute for an ineffective run game after [[Marquise Brown]] and Rice got hurt. Harris, long a Mahomes skeptic in fantasy, said he will likely draft him more in 2025 because the price is right.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'feel' Mahomes over [[Bo Nix]] at the first Underdog QB6 price — he has not played to the level people expected, but he is proven, the offense should continue, and Waldman thinks Kansas City is finally getting closer at wide receiver. Untracked co-host Bob Harris disagreed on price, calling Mahomes overpriced at QB6 and noting he has not been a weekly QB1 for a couple of years — QB1 weeks yes, and impressive ones, but not a QB1 season. Harris has him QB12 in his own initial Footballguys rankings and called even that 'overly aggressive', moving him up only because a scheme shift is possible. Harris said he will have far more shares of Nix than Mahomes.
- 2025-06-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-30]]): reports of Mahomes' demise are exaggerated and the decline narrative is easy June clicks built on selectively chosen stats. Waldman lists the legitimate causes of the dip in bottom-line production: offensive line problems, defenses playing cover three against the scheme and the time it took to adjust, and years of failing to develop a drafted receiver — Mecole Hardman, [[DeMarcus Robinson]], Chris Conley, [[Skyy Moore]], Justin Ross, with [[Marquise Brown]] and [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]] unable to stay healthy — before [[Rashee Rice]]. He pushes back at Ben Roethlisberger's age-25 workout critique via trainer Bobby Stroupe's rebuttal, and notes that quarterbacks pushed out of teams often show plenty left elsewhere, though he does not think Kansas City is headed that way. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) says Mahomes is locked into a tier of his own in the middle of round one or the middle of the QB1s and expects a possible return to a pass-heavier attack given a more robust receiving corps and uncertainty around [[Isiah Pacheco]].
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]): 'totally buying' the offseason talk that Kansas City goes back downfield after Mahomes' average depth of target sank to around seven yards in the post-[[Tyreek Hill]] years, with Mahomes going as QB6. His reasoning: other teams have shown you can attack deep even in the umbrella-coverage era, and he thinks Andy Reid and Mahomes got too cautious because they could still win dinking-and-dunking while assuming their receivers would develop — but the [[Skyy Moore]] and [[Kadarius Toney]] group never acclimated to the system. [[Xavier Worthy]] has shown he can, and Waldman expects more of what Worthy did down the stretch. The other enabler is the offensive line: more confidence in the line and the run game means more willingness to take shots and live with the risk. Bob Harris (not tracked) notes Reid has publicly talked up Worthy and that this predates the [[Rashee Rice]] legal news.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): he is 'feeling' that Kansas City goes vertical on the league this year, on the strength of Mahomes, Andy Reid and [[Xavier Worthy]], plus a visible preseason effort to push the ball downfield. Waldman believes the Chiefs think they have found an antidote to the umbrella coverages they faced — either to prevent them or to foil them — and calls Mahomes the bargain of the top five to seven quarterbacks. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) agreed, noting Mahomes's average depth of target has fallen every year since [[Tyreek Hill]] left, that both coach and quarterback have said the same thing since OTAs, and that a more vertical passing game should also open things up for [[Isiah Pacheco]], who he says has looked great in camp.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): Harris notes you are getting the best quarterback in football at plus 900 to lead the league in passing while he is only QB6 in season-long fantasy, and said he took Mahomes around the sixth round in a recent mock — Mahomes sits inside the top six quarterbacks Harris will draft. Guest Chris Raybon (Action Network, not tracked) made Mahomes his favorite leading-passer bet at 9-1: a full season of [[Marquise Brown]] and [[Xavier Worthy]], [[Noah Gray]] as insurance if [[Travis Kelce]] is washed, [[Brashard Smith]] as a receiving back, underneath options that free deeper routes, and — 'most importantly' — a real left tackle, with the caveat of a possible [[Rashee Rice]] suspension.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): 'I'm very concerned about this Chiefs offense.' He says the personnel around Mahomes 'flat out sucks' with the top two receivers out, that Andy Reid has leaned into a Mickey Mouse short-route design antithetical to how the rest of the league attacks, and that the run game is as poor as the pass game — an 11-yard [[Kareem Hunt]] run 'feels like a miracle.' The fantasy silver lining is that Mahomes leads all quarterbacks in scramble yards: 'he's like, I just got to keep breaking glass, because ain't nothing else working.' [[Chris Harris]] flags a blown coverage where [[Tyquan Thornton]] was 10 yards behind the defense and Mahomes overthrew him by 10, plus [[Travis Kelce]]'s goal-line drop that became an interception, and says something feels broken. Harmon caveats that Chiefs panic in Week 2 has aged poorly three years running.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): a clear disagreement between host and guest on degree. Guest Jamie Eisenberg of CBS (NOT a tracked expert) says the [[Xavier Worthy]] deep usage plus protection plus [[Rashee Rice]] returning from suspension and [[Travis Kelce]] over the middle is what they have been waiting for, that he had been saying buy low on Mahomes in Weeks 2 and 3, and that with Joe Burrow out and [[Lamar Jackson]] hurt he is a top-five and maybe top-three quarterback rest of season — you have to be thrilled to own him. Harris agrees Mahomes should be a fantasy starter again but will not say top three after one game. Eisenberg adds that Mahomes is currently the Chiefs' best rusher and is running more by necessity than by choice, which is part of why he likes him.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: it would be 'super dumb' to call the 2-3 Chiefs cooked — all three losses were one-score games of the kind they won all of last year, so some of this is luck leveling out, and [[Rashee Rice]] returns Week 7. Harris thought Mahomes was still wonderful and liked that Kansas City has kept its intent not to be a dink-and-dunk offense, even with Jacksonville sitting far back; Mahomes had the keeper run for the early 14-0 lead and a clinical seven-minute fourth-quarter drive, and the Chiefs have averaged 32 points over the past couple of weeks. The caveats: Mahomes remains the team's leading rusher, and the 99-yard pick six on an inside slant to [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]] was a bad decision when [[Travis Kelce]] was there.
- 2025-10-10 — According to guest Cousin Josh (Detective Fish, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-10]]): Mahomes is his number one hot start not to trade. He is QB2 as they record, has been elite in four of five games from a scoring perspective, and the rushing production is 'out of control' and not going away — though Josh dislikes that Mahomes seeks contact instead of sliding. Josh says Mahomes leads the NFL in air yards, ahead of [[Baker Mayfield]], which floored him, and [[Rashee Rice]] is back soon; he projects a top-three quarterback finish with a lot of 25-30 point games in a second half where the Chiefs 'do jerk stuff,' and would not flip him even to get tricky and land a [[Trey McBride]]. Harris agrees, noting he had Mahomes as a top-five quarterback in the almanac and rosters him widely because he went QB7 or QB8 in many drafts while people argued he was no longer a top-12 fantasy quarterback; his standing principle is that it is very difficult to be the best quarterback in the world and not be good for fantasy, and the Chiefs tried hard for a couple of years to make that so with one-yard passes and underdeveloped personnel.
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-13]]): Mahomes looked 'like a psycho' in the Sunday-night win over the Lions, playing aggressively and working the full field instead of settling for short check-downs, the opposite of the 'constipated' Chiefs passing game from the past year-plus. He remains right at the edge of being Kansas City's leading rusher and scored the game's decisive touchdown himself on a faked pitch near the goal line, effectively stealing a red-zone score that had been earmarked for [[Isaiah Pacheco]]. Harris's lingering frustration is that production isn't consolidating around one receiver week to week -- [[Xavier Worthy]] caught a fourth-and-three touchdown, but Marquise 'Hollywood' Brown stole the show with two touchdowns on just four catches, including a jet-motion pop-pass score. Harris expects the distribution to keep bouncing between Worthy, Brown and JuJu Smith-Schuster, especially with Rashee Rice's return looming, which could push the offense back toward shorter routes.
- 2025-10-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-16]]): [[Patrick Mahomes]] and the Chiefs offense are being underrated because of the team's 3-3 record, but Harmon called it 'a good, potentially great' offense that ranked 8th in EPA per dropback over the prior four weeks even with [[Xavier Worthy]] and Hollywood Brown banged up. Mahomes lacks the intermediate-area (10+ air yards, between the numbers) target volume that rivals like Sam Darnold (JSN), Baker Mayfield (Emeka Egbuka), Jared Goff (Amon-Ra St. Brown) and Matthew Stafford (Puka Nacua) get from a true big-slot receiver -- only 12 completions on 21 attempts (57%) in that area this year -- and Harmon argued Rashee Rice's return is what could unlock that volume given Mahomes is already efficient throwing there. He also noted the run game looked notably better with Kansas City getting under center and pounding the ball, which should further open the middle of the field.
- 2025-10-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-21 (Week 8 Waivers & W7 Reviews)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Mahomes looked 'back to his aggressive ways' in a blowout of the Raiders, and Harris said the return of [[Rashee Rice]] didn't send the offense back to conservative three-yard outs for at least one week, which makes Mahomes 'very valuable indeed.' The caveat is a crowded receiver room -- Harris said to keep starting the top Chiefs pass-catchers with the understanding that target distribution could accidentally minimize any one guy in a given week; Chiefs route shares that game were 26 [[Xavier Worthy]], 18 JuJu Smith-Schuster, 17 [[Marquise Brown]], 16 Rashee Rice, 11 Tyquan Thornton, plus [[Travis Kelce]].
- 2025-10-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-23]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman says he loves drafting quarterbacks like [[Patrick Mahomes]] in the second or third round of dynasty startups because it is 'basically free production' -- Mahomes will produce whatever he is going to produce over the next four or five years, and because he is a Hall-of-Fame-caliber passer with a Hall-of-Fame coach on a Hall-of-Fame trajectory, his real-world trade value at the end of that stretch should land close to what you paid for him today, meaning the years of production in between effectively cost nothing. He frames this with co-host Adam Harstad's distinction between an unreachable 'capital-V' ideal value and the practical 'lowercase-v' market value that actually matters for trades: Mahomes' ADP dipped earlier this season while 'everybody was kind of out on' him, then spiked back up once he caught fire, but Waldman treats those swings as buying-window noise around a settled long-run value rather than a real change in who Mahomes is.
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-10-27]]) [Redraft, Superflex]: three more 20-plus-air-yard attempts brought his season total to 29, versus just 13 through seven weeks last season. Harris cites the stat as small confirmation of what's obvious on tape -- Kansas City's offense is being schemed more aggressively downfield this year, which raises the fantasy ceiling of everyone in the passing game.
- 2025-10-31 — According to Adam Harstad (untracked Film and Theory co-host, [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-31]]) [Dynasty]: in a 'platonic ideal' value model a player's value should decline after every big game, because each big performance is one fewer big game remaining in a finite career -- but real-world value stays flat for a fully-scouted star because a huge game teaches the market nothing new about who he already is. Harstad said he flagged this exact inefficiency back in 2020, when Mahomes was going in the third round of dynasty startup drafts despite already being an MVP-caliber quarterback with a decade of elite production ahead -- calling that price 'insane'. The exploitable edge: buy Mahomes at a third-round startup price, start him for five years of top-tier QB production, then sell him at the end for roughly that same third-round price, since his value never depreciates the way the model predicts it should -- meaning the five years of production were effectively free. Matt Waldman agreed and named [[Justin Jefferson]] and, increasingly, [[Brock Bowers]] as skill-position players entering the same 'flat value' territory.
- 2025-11-03 — According to guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert) on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): still a Chiefs believer, but 'it's like you're looking at a copy of a copy — still recognizably the Mahomes Chiefs', better than last year, yet for the first time he lacked the faith Mahomes would pull it out, and it felt 'more like a one-man band than it had all season', which is strange given the healthier supporting cast. Harris notes no passing touchdowns, an interception, what the broadcast said would be his career-low single-game completion percentage, and sudden Buffalo pass rush with Bosa added. Doherty's charitable read: they need the bye, the left tackle back, a plan for three running backs, and a reassessment of the offense. Harris takes hope from the deep shots — the Marquise Brown bomb to the one, a well-defended deep ball to [[Xavier Worthy]], a third-and-long conversion to [[Travis Kelce]] — things they were not doing successfully last year.
