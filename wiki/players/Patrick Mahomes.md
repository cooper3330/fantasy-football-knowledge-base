# Patrick Mahomes

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: a specific 2024 redraft value call — expects Mahomes to be pushed down draft boards by [[Lamar Jackson]] (and possibly Jalen Hurts) at the very top of quarterback rankings, but not to fall out of the top five, "probably not far beyond four." Frames the value case purely as a cost-vs-rank gap rather than a talent downgrade — going in the third or fourth round instead of the first or second, at a point in drafts where he'd otherwise be a natural value pick if the board falls that way. Grouped with [[Travis Kelce]] and [[Rashee Rice]] as the core of a Chiefs offense with "just enough pieces" to compete with anyone, backed by a defense Waldman singles out (L'Jarius Sneed, Nick Bolton, Chris Jones) as a strong IDP core in its own right.
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): an explicit defense of an AFC Championship win with modest counting stats — 30/39, 241 yards, but a 77% completion rate and 100 passer rating against a top-tier Baltimore defense with a receiver room in question, called out as the real signal. Fourth Super Bowl in six seasons — "insane," "unprecedented" — and unlike early-career Brady, "this is Mahomes' team." The caution attached: Harmon is skeptical Kansas City can run this formula back in 2024 if the receiver room isn't addressed beyond [[Rashee Rice]] and a healthy [[Travis Kelce]] — "how much more can you ask Superman to do."

- 2024-02-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-12]]), reacting to the Super Bowl LVIII win: his fullest statement yet on what separates Mahomes as a player, not a value target. Names him "a wiser Brett Favre" — the same instinct to see through what a defensive scheme is trying to disguise rather than fully diagram it first, "but a much more artistic and creative" processor who makes far fewer of the era-defining mistakes that came with Favre's improvisation. Credits Chris Jones with the same defensive-side skill (adapting mid-series once he reads what the 49ers were actually doing rather than sticking to the called scheme), and frames the Chiefs' championship window as built on a small group of elite improvisers (Mahomes, Jones, Kelce, plus secondary pieces like L'Jarius Sneed) compensating for a receiver room that "has not been strong from a fantasy perspective."
