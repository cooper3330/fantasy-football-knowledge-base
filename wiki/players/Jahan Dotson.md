# Jahan Dotson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his name as "Jahan
> Dawson"; normalized here to the real name, Jahan Dotson.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]), asked to evaluate Dotson after a confounding down 2023 (Dotson himself cited multiple explanations without full clarity, per Waldman): grades him "a very skilled wide receiver three who can give you wide receiver two numbers in the right offense," but is unsure incoming OC Kliff Kingsbury's scheme (not a tracked entity — coach) will be that offense — output will hinge heavily on whichever quarterback situation Washington settles into. Credits him as a good route runner capable of functioning as a matchup WR2. Notes Kingsbury has a track record of running his favored receiver out of the same spot on nearly every snap — a dig at his Arizona usage of Rondale Moore and Andy Isabella (neither tracked) — and while Waldman thinks Dotson is a better player than either of those two, he's not convinced that guarantees the scheme actually unlocks him. Discussed in the same breath as [[Terry McLaurin]], whose forced X-only usage is partly attributed to Washington lacking another true X-caliber receiver — Dotson included.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Dynasty]: Dotson is more of an unknown than [[Terry McLaurin]] — Waldman wants to see him build on the flashes shown two seasons ago, stay healthy, and find usage in Washington's offense under [[Jayden Daniels]]. Despite the uncertainty, his explicit recommendation for anyone who traded for or drafted Dotson is to hold, not sell, calling it too early to have full information and joking he'll still be futilely holding Dotson well into the 2030s if it doesn't pan out.
- 2024-06-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-04]]): Dotson's 2023 season (49-83-518-4TD, 59% catch rate) was a statistical step back from his rookie year, and the Reception Perception profile agrees -- man/zone/press success rates held roughly steady (70.6% vs. man, 79.3% vs. zone, 72.2% vs. press, down from 69.9/80.1/78.9 as a rookie, with press falling from the 87th to 68th percentile) but nearly every individual route score declined: curl dropped from 86.7% to 73.8% (below NFL average), along with the corner, nine, post, out and comeback routes, while the dig route stayed elite (82%, essentially matching his rookie mark). Harmon attributes much of the decline to a usage shift rather than pure regression: his deep-route rate fell from 42% of routes as a rookie to 28% in 2023, while his slot rate rose from ~30% to 40% -- and separately faults Washington's offense under Eric Bieniemy as poorly designed (disconnected route concepts, no run-game identity), speculating some players 'lost faith' in the scheme. Harmon's headline take, unchanged from his 2022 profile, is that Dotson projects as a 'rock solid number two receiver' -- not a bust, but also not the [[Terry McLaurin]]-mantle successor some fans want, noting McLaurin's success rate vs. man (75%) still clears Dotson's (70%) comfortably. With [[Curtis Samuel]] gone, Harmon wants Dotson used more aggressively in the slot (targeting 45-50% of snaps there vs. 40% last year) to absorb vacated underneath volume alongside McLaurin. Co-host James Koh (untracked) pushed back harder on the framing, noting Dotson was down on almost every individual route year-over-year and shouldn't be let off easy purely on offensive context.
- 2024-06-25 — According to James Koh (untracked co-host) and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Dotson is considered 'fine' as Washington's clear secondary target but not in [[Terry McLaurin]]'s class; the hosts expect new OC Kliff Kingsbury's offense to concentrate volume much more than last year's spread-it-thin Eric Bieniemi scheme did, which may lift Dotson's target quality somewhat even if McLaurin remains the clear first read.
- 2024-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-16]]): Harmon acknowledged Dotson's per-route/per-target metrics were dreadful in 2023 -- just 0.13 targets per route run, worse than several bit-part Washington pass catchers -- but said he's staying in on Dotson anyway because 'he has good film,' calling him a functional-to-more-than-functional starting receiver whose profile got needlessly buried in a broken Eric Bieniemy offense. James Koh (not tracked) argued Dotson stands to benefit the most from the coordinator change to Kliff Kingsbury and expects a real bounce back in 2024; Harmon agreed. [Redraft (Standard)] [Dynasty]
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon still has 'the candle lit' for Dotson as a potential quality starting receiver, but that projection is contingent on Washington's offensive line and run game coming together. Guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn (not tracked) rated the Commanders' line bottom-five in the NFL, with only free agent LT Tyler Beach and RG Sam Cosmi projected as reliable starters and a rookie (Brandon Coleman) likely at left tackle. Both agreed that if new OC Kliff Kingsbury leans toward heavier 12/21 personnel with play-action and RPOs rather than a spread, true drop-back scheme, it would funnel more targets to Dotson and [[Terry McLaurin]] as the top two receivers -- but Kingsbury's pass-heavy track record raises doubt that it happens.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): after the trade to Philadelphia, Dotson is a 'fuck it' barring injury ahead of him. Waldman notes Washington moved him despite having no big names at receiver, meaning they preferred other options. His scouting read: Dotson's quickness and separation over the middle are real and he can make plays after the catch, but his biggest issue is catching the football on exactly the route types that would benefit him, he lacks size and power, and he is not a good contested-catch option — not high-end at any single thing besides quickness. At best he is a hedge for [[DeVonta Smith]] or a beneficiary if [[Dallas Goedert]] gets hurt; Bob Harris (not tracked) called him fifth in the pecking order behind [[Saquon Barkley]] and cited Jason Wood's finding that Kellen Moore's WR3 has drawn about a 13% target share, which cannot sustain fantasy value. [Dynasty]: Brown and Smith are both signed long-term, so the bandwidth may never appear.
- 2024-08-27 — According to host James Koh (not a tracked expert) and guest Derek Klassen (not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): Koh calls Washington trading Dotson — a first-round pick from only two years ago — for a third-round pick 'horrific' and 'absolutely unnecessary', on the grounds that a team building around a rookie quarterback should be adding talent around [[Jayden Daniels]], not subtracting it. Klassen says three months earlier he would have called the trade insane: Dotson looked like the clear WR2, and as a rookie showed he could go up for the ball and play in traffic better than his size suggests, is a pretty good route runner, and is tough. But through the offseason and July it seemed Dotson was falling quickly out of favor, so Klassen will not condemn the move — while allowing the fault may be Kingsbury's for not knowing how to use him.
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): Washington traded Dotson within the division to Philadelphia after reportedly trying to include him in [[Brandon Aiyuk]] packages that San Francisco declined. Harmon does not think Dotson is a superstar — as much as he liked his Reception Perception prospect and rookie profiles, nothing about them screamed a top-tier receiver, only a solid NFL one, and he still believes he can be that. He thinks Dotson fills a real need for an Eagles depth chart thin beyond its top three, and expects him to play some slot, where Harmon considers him better than outside; his deployment in Washington is 'a whole other conversation.' Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) hated the trade for Washington, particularly its August timing — no plan was in place, and a first-round pick from two years ago was moved for a third-round return without using the offseason or one of the deepest receiver drafts ever to replace him.
