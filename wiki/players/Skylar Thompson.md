# Skylar Thompson

## Expert Takes

- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): asked whether Thompson can 'save' the Dolphins, Waldman says probably 'f**k it' — but he likes Thompson's game more than the verdict suggests. The main structural problem is handedness: Miami practices and schemes for a left-handed quarterback, and Waldman noticed a lot of dropped passes from the starters when Thompson played earlier in his career, which he attributes partly to the lefty-to-righty switch being a bigger adjustment than people realize. What he likes relative to [[Tua Tagovailoa]]: a better arm, comparable pocket mobility and pocket management when he is on, and some daring if his confidence grows. He puts maybe a 25% chance on Thompson nailing the job down and becoming the best quarterback of his draft class, noting he beat out Mike White and that the staff and teammates trust his command and behind-the-scenes growth. Roster advice: only add him in deep dynasty or if you roster Tua and have no alternative. Miami also signed Tyler Huntley off the Ravens' practice squad.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-19]]) [Dynasty]: with Tua Tagovailoa injured, Thompson is the Dolphins' starter 'for the foreseeable future' and is the swing Waldman wants over a mediocre alternative. Waldman had Thompson as his top-graded QB in the 2022 class — a class he calls dreadful — and says other scouts (he names Dave Rozano, and reports unnamed league scouts with second- to third-round grades) liked him more than consensus, while NFL.com's Chad Reuter was skeptical. He grades him high-ceiling/low-floor and very volatile: better arm than Tua, as mobile, and a better pocket player when he understands the offense, with a path to being one of the late-round anomalies like Brady, Warner or [[Brock Purdy]] — or a complete flop. He excuses the 2022 rookie tape (compressed playbook, many dropped passes, righty-for-lefty offensive adjustments like the 49ers' Montana-to-Young switch) and calls the Tyler Huntley signing insurance behind Thompson, not a threat. Co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) is more sober — 'I don't think Skylar Thompson is going to be anything' — but claimed him on waivers in two leagues, taking him over Andy Dalton in Superflex and dropping [[Bryce Young]] for him, because Dalton is a known QB20-24 while Thompson could be QB12 with those weapons and that head coach: 'I'd rather have the guy who I think sucks over the guy I know sucks.'
- 2024-09-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-20]]): a genuinely interesting monitor case. His rookie sample was tiny and Miami significantly limited the offense and played ultra-conservative with him; when he did get passing chances, Waldman charted numerous drops from [[Tyreek Hill]], [[Jaylen Waddle]] and Braxton Berrios on plays that could have been big — possibly a righty-versus-lefty adjustment issue after Tua Tagovailoa. He layered on a point made by Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) on that morning's *Film and Theory* episode: when a late-round quarterback sticks on a roster for three seasons it is a signal, because those players tend to become at least journeyman starters and sometimes have notable stretches — Tony Romo being the best-known example. Thompson is now in year three with good weapons around him, and Waldman still counts him as inside that first six-to-eight-game evaluation window. See [[Roster Longevity as Talent Signal]].
- 2024-11-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is letting Thompson go, saying we have seen enough to know the Dolphins have no confidence in letting him throw the ball downfield and that it is well past that point. He left one door open: if Thompson lands somewhere he is allowed to wing it and be a little more reckless, he could still be a thing.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): a name worth dusting off. Waldman had Thompson as his highest-scored quarterback in the 2022 class — a class where he notes Mr. Irrelevant [[Brock Purdy]] proved the best of a group the market favored [[Kenny Pickett]], [[Malik Willis]] and [[Desmond Ridder]] from — with an asterisk that he knew Thompson would not be drafted highly. His central argument is that Miami suppressed the evidence: when Thompson started, Mike McDaniel effectively chopped the offense in half and would not let him throw downfield, which Waldman now believes was about staying close and playoff-viable rather than an indictment of the quarterback, and reflects McDaniel's high-control West Coast mindset even into Thompson's third year. In Pittsburgh he expects the governor to come off. Waldman thinks Thompson is at worst QB3 there and might beat out Mason Rudolph for the backup job, citing Steelers-watching friend Steve Voke on the tenor shifting from 'may not make the roster'. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) said 'fuck all that' but holds shares in [[Dynasty]] and superflex.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Film & Theory Sequences, Signal, Steelers)]]): [[Skylar Thompson]]'s good Pittsburgh preseason came with the caveat that he was likely facing third-teamers, but Waldman found the framing revealing — Thompson said he had learned from being around [[Aaron Rodgers]] and had learned over the years to just be himself and go down swinging. Waldman's read from the Miami tape is that Mike McDaniel put a governor on him: at Kansas State he was far more aggressive, more willing to throw downfield and buy time, but Miami in a playoff push wanted a quarterback who would not make mistakes, so the play calls kept him running the ball and getting off the field. When they did let him go, he connected and receivers dropped it. Waldman offers this as a coaching lesson rather than a fantasy recommendation.
- 2026-05-11 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-11]]) [Dynasty]: on Skylar Thompson landing in Baltimore behind [[Lamar Jackson]] in a crowded QB room (Diego Pavia, Austin Reed, Joe Fagnano), Waldman revisited his long-standing take that Thompson was mismanaged by Mike McDaniel in Miami -- McDaniel kept a tight leash on backups like Thompson and Malik Washington and got 'conservative and constipated' in play-calling once forced to expand their role. In Pittsburgh, Thompson beat expectations in the QB3 battle behind Mason Rudolph/Aaron Rodgers/Will Howard, reportedly telling the team he finally decided to 'play his game' instead of conforming, which showed up on tape in the preseason; Waldman thinks Pittsburgh's staff noticed enough to prefer Thompson's traits over Joe Fagnano's still-developing feel and Tyler Huntley's known 'won't embarrass us but won't win big games' ceiling. He wouldn't be surprised if Thompson ends up QB3 in Baltimore too.
