# Braelon Allen

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name as "Braylon Allen"; normalized here to his real name, Braelon Allen.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — Guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Dynasty]: names him his most overrated 2024 running back prospect, pushing directly back on public "RB1 of the class" chatter. His case: heavy college volume was largely a product of Wisconsin having no other real receiving/rushing options after an offensive overhaul, not evidence of an elite skill set; he grades as a poor pass protector who doesn't yet get his hands or feet in the right spots, a limited lateral athlete without the change-of-direction to make defenders miss in tight spaces, and lacking a true third-gear breakaway burst. Ranks him behind [[Bucky Irving]], [[Trey Benson]], and [[Blake Corum]] in the class — all backs he credits with real lateral suddenness that Allen lacks — and would not have him in his personal top five at the position.
- 2024-02-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]) [Dynasty]: independently lands on the same skepticism as Harms, on competitiveness rather than pure talent — for a player near 250 lbs, "the lack of competitiveness... down to down just isn't there," in explicit contrast to Audric Estime (not tracked, ASR: "Audrick Estime"), whose tape shows a big man consistently playing through contact rather than around it. Explicitly complicates the read by his age: Allen will play his rookie season at only 19 years old. Grades his ceiling, if he becomes more consistent, in the same range as [[Jaylen Wright]] and [[Dylan Laube]] — a notch below Trey Benson — but sees real downside risk if the inconsistency doesn't resolve, unlike Wright, whose trajectory Angelo reads as more likely to rise than fall.
- 2024-02-22 — **Waldman**, same episode: situates him inside what he calls "the Wisconsin curse" — both Jonathan Taylor and Melvin Gordon (neither tracked) struggled and were briefly labeled busts early in their NFL careers before becoming Pro Bowlers, a pattern Waldman attributes to Wisconsin's volume-heavy, schematically narrow rushing offense not preparing backs for a pro run-scheme diet. His comp for Allen if he does *not* make the same adjustment those two eventually made: [[Najee Harris]] — a player who had to learn to drop his pads and stop trying to run through/over/around everybody the way Alabama let him, rather than a naturally translatable NFL runner, without Harris's receiving-game profile to fall back on. Explicit dynasty framing via a price analogy: Allen's cost of acquisition will run "Lamborghini," but a cheaper "Lexus"-tier alternative exists in the same range — floats Miami's Christopher Brooks (not tracked) as the example — without needing the "Mercedes money" bet on a boom/bust unproven rookie. Names Baltimore as the explicit dream fit — a low-volume-rushing complementary role behind [[Lamar Jackson]] rather than as the offense's engine — and, more broadly, wants him to sit behind an established, similar-archetype veteran (an explicit "please be the Derrick Henry understudy for a couple years" comp) and inherit the role only once that veteran ages out, rather than being handed a workhorse role as a rookie. Expects a fast (mid-4.4s) 40 time at the combine to push him into Round 2 regardless of these concerns.
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast: identifies a mechanical cause behind the competitiveness question Angelo raised on 2024-02-22 — Allen turns his hips away from the defender just before contact (the same flaw Waldman says Isaiah Spiller, not tracked, showed two years earlier), which "neuters" his power reservoir because hips and pads end up misaligned. Does this more often against defenders his own size or larger than against smaller ones he'll bully. Also flags heel-clicking on hip-flip direction changes that costs him his balance. Concludes the talent is "obviously there" but leaves open whether he'll fix it.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): his own "do not draft" pick, framed as an era problem rather than a talent one — volume-based, rhythm-based runners are hard to find NFL fits for now that the league has largely moved away from running-back-centric offenses, and the modern shift favors dynamic backs like [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], [[Tyjae Spears]] and [[James Cook]] over a 245-lb back who needs touches to get going. Floats a hypothetical exception: friend-of-the-program Chad Reuter's suggestion that Jim Harbaugh's Chargers offense (with [[Justin Herbert]], post-[[Austin Ekeler]]) could make Allen a 12-15-touch complementary piece even without being a true feature back. [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, adds a mechanical diagnosis for the "fragmented game" critique: Allen turns his hips away from the defender just before contact (the same flaw Isaiah Spiller, not tracked, showed), which neuters his power reservoir when his hips and pads misalign — happens mainly against defenders his own size or larger, rarely against smaller ones he can bully. Calls him a "fragmented" player whose reps flip between good and bad within the same game, without the from-17-to-19 maturation Waldman would want to see; would rather have a running back with fewer questions, grading him a round two/early round three prospect.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]): uses Allen as the worked example of grading a range of outcomes rather than a single right/wrong verdict — "I don't like a lot about Braelon Allen's game, but I can understand how he could be one of the three most productive backs in his class." [[Brandon Angelo]], same episode, extends the framework explicitly: contrasts Allen's wide range of outcomes with [[Caleb Williams]]'s much narrower one — Allen "could be out of the league in two years" or, in another outcome, "a Pro Bowl caliber back in two years."
