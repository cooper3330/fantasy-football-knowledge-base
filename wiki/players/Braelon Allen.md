# Braelon Allen

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name as "Braylon Allen"; normalized here to his real name, Braelon Allen.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — Guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Dynasty]: names him his most overrated 2024 running back prospect, pushing directly back on public "RB1 of the class" chatter. His case: heavy college volume was largely a product of Wisconsin having no other real receiving/rushing options after an offensive overhaul, not evidence of an elite skill set; he grades as a poor pass protector who doesn't yet get his hands or feet in the right spots, a limited lateral athlete without the change-of-direction to make defenders miss in tight spaces, and lacking a true third-gear breakaway burst. Ranks him behind [[Bucky Irving]], [[Trey Benson]], and [[Blake Corum]] in the class — all backs he credits with real lateral suddenness that Allen lacks — and would not have him in his personal top five at the position.
- 2024-02-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]) [Dynasty]: independently lands on the same skepticism as Harms, on competitiveness rather than pure talent — for a player near 250 lbs, "the lack of competitiveness... down to down just isn't there," in explicit contrast to Audric Estime (not tracked, ASR: "Audrick Estime"), whose tape shows a big man consistently playing through contact rather than around it. Explicitly complicates the read by his age: Allen will play his rookie season at only 19 years old. Grades his ceiling, if he becomes more consistent, in the same range as [[Jaylen Wright]] and [[Dylan Laube]] — a notch below Trey Benson — but sees real downside risk if the inconsistency doesn't resolve, unlike Wright, whose trajectory Angelo reads as more likely to rise than fall.
- 2024-02-22 — **Waldman**, same episode: situates him inside what he calls "the Wisconsin curse" — both Jonathan Taylor and Melvin Gordon (neither tracked) struggled and were briefly labeled busts early in their NFL careers before becoming Pro Bowlers, a pattern Waldman attributes to Wisconsin's volume-heavy, schematically narrow rushing offense not preparing backs for a pro run-scheme diet. His comp for Allen if he does *not* make the same adjustment those two eventually made: [[Najee Harris]] — a player who had to learn to drop his pads and stop trying to run through/over/around everybody the way Alabama let him, rather than a naturally translatable NFL runner, without Harris's receiving-game profile to fall back on. Explicit dynasty framing via a price analogy: Allen's cost of acquisition will run "Lamborghini," but a cheaper "Lexus"-tier alternative exists in the same range — floats Miami's Christopher Brooks (not tracked) as the example — without needing the "Mercedes money" bet on a boom/bust unproven rookie. Names Baltimore as the explicit dream fit — a low-volume-rushing complementary role behind [[Lamar Jackson]] rather than as the offense's engine — and, more broadly, wants him to sit behind an established, similar-archetype veteran (an explicit "please be the Derrick Henry understudy for a couple years" comp) and inherit the role only once that veteran ages out, rather than being handed a workhorse role as a rookie. Expects a fast (mid-4.4s) 40 time at the combine to push him into Round 2 regardless of these concerns.
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast: identifies a mechanical cause behind the competitiveness question Angelo raised on 2024-02-22 — Allen turns his hips away from the defender just before contact (the same flaw Waldman says Isaiah Spiller, not tracked, showed two years earlier), which "neuters" his power reservoir because hips and pads end up misaligned. Does this more often against defenders his own size or larger than against smaller ones he'll bully. Also flags heel-clicking on hip-flip direction changes that costs him his balance. Concludes the talent is "obviously there" but leaves open whether he'll fix it.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): his own "do not draft" pick, framed as an era problem rather than a talent one — volume-based, rhythm-based runners are hard to find NFL fits for now that the league has largely moved away from running-back-centric offenses, and the modern shift favors dynamic backs like [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], [[Tyjae Spears]] and [[James Cook]] over a 245-lb back who needs touches to get going. Floats a hypothetical exception: friend-of-the-program Chad Reuter's suggestion that Jim Harbaugh's Chargers offense (with [[Justin Herbert]], post-[[Austin Ekeler]]) could make Allen a 12-15-touch complementary piece even without being a true feature back. [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, adds a mechanical diagnosis for the "fragmented game" critique: Allen turns his hips away from the defender just before contact (the same flaw Isaiah Spiller, not tracked, showed), which neuters his power reservoir when his hips and pads misalign — happens mainly against defenders his own size or larger, rarely against smaller ones he can bully. Calls him a "fragmented" player whose reps flip between good and bad within the same game, without the from-17-to-19 maturation Waldman would want to see; would rather have a running back with fewer questions, grading him a round two/early round three prospect.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]): uses Allen as the worked example of grading a range of outcomes rather than a single right/wrong verdict — "I don't like a lot about Braelon Allen's game, but I can understand how he could be one of the three most productive backs in his class." [[Brandon Angelo]], same episode, extends the framework explicitly: contrasts Allen's wide range of outcomes with [[Caleb Williams]]'s much narrower one — Allen "could be out of the league in two years" or, in another outcome, "a Pro Bowl caliber back in two years." See [[Prospect Pro-Readiness vs Ceiling]].
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: rated Allen's long-term outlook behind Breece Hall as worse than Isaac Guerendo's behind Christian McCaffrey, noting the Jets loaded the running back room further behind Hall, which he reads as the team 'throwing things at the wall.' He said Allen falling as far as he did in the draft reflects concern he rested on his laurels after a strong freshman year and never fully put his game together, even accounting for a coaching change. Waldman also credited Hall's return from injury — still producing despite a rough Buffalo game while regaining confidence — and said a healthier offensive line (pending Tyron Smith's health) leaves 'too many ifs' stacked against Allen relative to Guerendo's clearer outlook.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman is 'not in love' with [[Braelon Allen]] as a standalone prospect -- calling his skills fragmented and not yet fully integrated -- but said the Jets' offensive line (if healthy) plus Aaron Rodgers' ability to dictate to defenses could let Allen deliver 8-10 rushing TDs and 1,000-1,200 total yards as a rookie if [[Breece Hall]] gets hurt early and he plays out a full season, since Allen is a good gap runner in a scheme suited to that skill. He called Allen a 'must-grab' at his current cost (last two rounds of best ball drafts, some after pick 20), naming him the clear-cut handcuff to Hall ahead of Israel Abanikanda.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): 'feel it' on Allen as a bye-week fill-in and rotating roster piece, not a hold-forever asset if you do not roster [[Breece Hall]]. Watching his touchdown plays, Waldman found them 'pretty gimme' — well-executed and schemed up front rather than impressive on Allen's end, though he finished one well. He explicitly warns against treating him as 'instant Breece Hall territory' if Hall misses time. The positive case: a competent back with fixable flaws who can be powerful, has good acceleration and can catch the football — good enough on that roster. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) agreed with the streaming framing.
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-25 (Campus2Canton with Felix Sharpe)]]): the Jets are deploying Allen in a bully-ball role — 13 personnel with a fullback leading the way — and Waldman said he looks good doing what he does well and is certainly an asset for New York right now. Earlier in the episode Waldman also argued Allen had no business being a day-three pick, attributing the slide to factors about him rather than to his ability.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): Waldman rejected the framing that Allen is the more explosive, dangerous Jets back over [[Breece Hall]], while being careful not to run Allen down — he has played well, and Waldman had RSP-subscriber film analysis of Allen's game coming. His distinction is scheme and role: there is 'a little bit more schemed-up components' to what Allen is doing, the Jets are giving him a booster seat, 'but he's not a baby either.' Far more is demanded of Hall than of Allen right now, and Waldman said that if the Jets asked Allen to replace Hall he would perform well — the roles, not the talent gap, explain the surface numbers.
- 2025-06-05 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-05]]): with reports the Jets want to use Allen more, he projects as the [[David Montgomery]] half of Tanner Engstrand's imported Detroit two-back system, with [[Breece Hall]] in the [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] role and [[Isaiah Davis]] as the third back. The caveat Angelo attaches is that the split only creates two useful fantasy backs when the offensive line is strong, as it is in Detroit; behind the Jets line both backs land in mediocre no-man's land, and [[Justin Fields]] taking a good chunk of carries inside the 10 further limits the touchdown equity Allen would normally inherit in that role.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]): responding to Zack Rosenblatt's report that the ex-Detroit staff in New York will use Allen in a [[David Montgomery]]-like role, Waldman says yes, Allen will be a thing — but not a Montgomery-level thing, because the Jets do not have Detroit's offensive line, and Bob Harris (not tracked) adds nor the quarterback, who runs a lot himself. Waldman's landing spot is RB3/RB4 value: solid as a bye-week option or a desperation start, and it does bring down [[Breece Hall]]'s outlook. Bob Harris notes Allen had been RB54, that the news will lift him, and that he would be more interested if Hall falls further; Waldman would take Allen around RB44 and no higher than about RB40.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): Harris says it is 'crazy to me how much Braelon Allen talk we get' — Allen is good and will play, because that is what happens in backfields now, but if [[Breece Hall]] gets two-thirds of the work that is all Harris needs; Allen can steal some touchdowns and that is the shape of it. Ben Gretch (guest, NOT tracked) notes the Lions staff appears to view Hall as their [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and Allen as their [[David Montgomery]], with carries split and a heavy run volume in a low-play-count offense. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]) — 'fuck it' on Allen heading for a breakout, with the blunt reason being that it is the Jets. He allows Allen will do well enough to be an RB3 with some better games, but says as long as [[Breece Hall]] is healthy enough Hall is the better back; Allen is a bruiser, a different type, whom the team may want to hang its hat on and might do so politically if the perception forms that Hall does not play hurt. Waldman rates him a bit better than [[Roschon Johnson]], while flagging [[Justin Fields]] taking red-zone carries as a cap. Bob Harris (not tracked) countered with the Aaron Glenn and Tanner Engstrand Detroit template — Hall in the [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] role, Allen in the [[David Montgomery]] role — noting Gibbs was RB5 and Montgomery RB9 when on the field together, and argued that dialling that back 75% still yields usable RB2/RB3 play. Waldman called that a fair point but stressed the Jets' offensive line is nowhere near Detroit's.
- 2026-01-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-19]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman says he likes [[Braelon Allen]] but is worried about a serious knee injury suffered this year. His outlook is entirely tied to [[Breece Hall]]'s future in New York -- if Hall is traded or gone, Allen gets a real shot and is worth keeping on 40-plus-man rosters, or the back of a bench in rosters between 25 and 40. Waldman's ceiling case, if Allen reaches full maturity, is a 1,300-1,400-yard, 10-touchdown back, but he says he doesn't trust the Jets organization to actually let that happen. Bob Harris shared the same 'like the player, worried about the injury' read.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris and co-host Josh (untracked) flagged [[Braelon Allen]] as a direct beneficiary if [[Breece Hall]]'s holdout drags into camp, expecting Allen to climb into the [[Rhamondre Stevenson]]/[[J.K. Dobbins]] range if that happens, though not into the top-24. Harris used last year's Allen coverage as a cautionary tale about trusting beat-reporter narratives -- widespread reports had Allen in an even split with Hall entering last season, which turned out to be false; Allen instead missed most of the year hurt. Harris said the lesson is he won't alter rankings off beat-reporter chatter going forward, though he conceded Allen's actual role plans were never really tested given the injury.
- 2026-07-17 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-17]]): Allen's 2025 injury is cited as the likely reason last year's Aaron Glenn 'three-headed monster' rhetoric about the Jets backfield never actually cost [[Breece Hall]] snaps; Harris expects Glenn to repeat similar committee talk again this camp and says it should be ignored the same way.
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-20]]): Harmon calls Braelon Allen one of the more talented backup running backs in the league and a good complement to Breece Hall, whose recent groin injury raises his short-term relevance.
