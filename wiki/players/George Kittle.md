# George Kittle

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]): a quiet Super Bowl LVIII box score with no major statistical impact beyond a fourth-down reception, and a scare — he left for the locker room at one point in the game before returning, with the hosts noting they had no way to gauge how limited he actually was playing through it. In the offseason-outlook segment, they flag 2023 as a career-best efficiency year for Kittle (most yards per target of his career) even as San Francisco is read as facing a coming, unavoidable roster crunch — not all three of Kittle, [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and [[Deebo Samuel]] survive on the roster past 2024 given the cap picture.
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): with [[Brandon Aiyuk]] injured, Harmon calls Kittle San Francisco's best man-coverage beater from a downfield perspective and probably their most explosive player — 41 of [[Brock Purdy]]'s 80 passing yards against man versus Kansas City came on one deep shot to him, and team success rate when throwing to Kittle is 61.9%, best on the roster. Harmon frames this as a problem rather than a compliment: your best coverage-beater being a tight end is not where a contending offense wants to be.
- 2025-05-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Kittle at TE3, pick 43 overall behind [[Brock Bowers]] and [[Trey McBride]], is the value of the elite tight end tier — he was third in total tight end points and first in points per game in 2024. Waldman's argument is positional aging: tight ends hold value later than other positions because route-running acumen and zone-reading survive athletic decline, citing Tony Gonzalez, Rob Gronkowski in Tampa, Antonio Gates post-foot-surgery and Travis Kelce; Kittle also still looks as quick as ever. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) added that Kittle has been a top-five fantasy tight end in six of seven seasons since 2018 when playing at least 14 games, had seven 20-point games in 2024 including a 30-point Week 17, and that the blocking-usage knock costs some floor but the spike weeks are unavailable elsewhere. Stated risk: whether he fades down the stretch.
- 2025-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]): Harmon is cooler than the market on the 'just take George Kittle' consensus — he is a 31-year-old tight end turning 32 in October who will miss time now and again, and the enthusiasm is largely a function of how thin the 49ers' receiver room is behind [[Brandon Aiyuk]]'s injury. James Koh (not a tracked expert) raised the point that San Francisco may be overly reliant on Kittle and [[Christian McCaffrey]] out of the backfield, and describes Kittle's style as all gas and no brakes — which is why they love him and also why he ends up broken and missing time. Both treat him as a real talent with a durability tax rather than a safe floor pick.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-27]]): even with San Francisco's receiver room decimated, Harmon cautions against assuming a Kittle target explosion. He will not say a bad word about him as a player, but notes the NFL and fantasy world perpetually want Kittle to get more targets and he simply never does — 94, 86, 90 and 94 targets over the last four seasons while playing 14-plus games in each. Harmon's reasoning is that teams do not normally run an offense through a tight end unless they are a specific kind of team, and he allows the Niners might become one this year after signing Kittle to a new contract, but he expects the volume to flow to [[Ricky Pearsall]] and [[Christian McCaffrey]] instead.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates Kittle a 3 out of 10 and frames it as a pricing problem — we knew Kittle was good when he cost a sixth-round pick last year, and now you are paying a third, with open questions about [[Brock Purdy]]'s supporting receivers. Harris rates him a 2 and takes the other side of the pricing argument: he was right when he complained ADP was dumb about Kittle in rounds five and six, and this year ADP is simply on the mark. Harris's only reservation is a slightly above-average injury history over Kittle's career, though not last season, which he admits he is whistling past.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Kittle is going to be a big story and the hamstring is the thing to find out about. He had about four catches on the opening drive including a touchdown and was then instantly out with the hamstring, with the broadcast crew not noticing until they showed him on the sideline in a hat. Harris separately notes [[Jauan Jennings]] hurt his shoulder, not his calf, and was out.
