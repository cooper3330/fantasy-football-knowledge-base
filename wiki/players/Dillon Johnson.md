# Dillon Johnson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name as "Dylan Johnson"; normalized here to the real name of the Washington
> running back — a Mississippi State transfer — Dillon Johnson.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman's fourth name in the class's early-round-talent-caliber group (after [[Blake Corum]], [[Dylan Laube]], and [[Jonathan Brooks]]). Played through a foot fracture and, per Waldman, a quad strain and a knee injury sustained during the CFP semifinal, then played through it into the national championship game. Waldman treats that as directly contradicting an unverified rumor — a leaked/disputed video reportedly of former Mississippi State coach Mike Leach questioning his toughness — since what Waldman actually saw on tape was a hard-nosed, tight-crease runner with some of the best contact balance in the class on both direct and indirect shots. His real reservation is explosiveness: good initial acceleration but not enough top-end speed to consistently turn a good run into a 40-plus-yard gain, meaning his path onto an NFL field depends on out-competing faster backs who lack his instincts and physicality rather than on any big-play trait of his own. Both hosts group him with [[Trey Benson]] as the "need a little nasty" archetype NFL running back rooms value, and Angelo notes how easy it is to overlook Johnson's per-play impact on Washington tape dominated by [[Rome Odunze]]'s much higher profile.
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: directly re-litigates the Mike Leach toughness rumor, now citing an actual recorded phone call (circulating on YouTube) of the late Leach saying he was glad Johnson left Mississippi State because he "wasn't very tough." Waldman pushes back on the record: in the five games he studied, Johnson made effective pre-snap box reads with only one bad decision, and he played through a broken bone in his foot plus other leg injuries for most of 2023, including through a playoff run and into the national championship game. Comps the mismatch to historical cases of tough runners wrongly labeled soft (Terrell Davis, Arian Foster — neither tracked), while noting Leach's broad popularity in media and public perception.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): his high-floor pick if he's a GM — one of the top-five backs in the class regardless of a likely fifth/sixth/seventh-round slide, and explicitly doesn't care about his lack of top-end speed or Mike Leach's toughness comments, pointing to Johnson playing through a broken foot bone, a bad knee and an ankle issue last season while still playing physically and catching the ball well. Comp: Roschon Johnson — not a top-end talent, but a reliable every-down player on the goal line, in pass pro, catching and running inside zone or gap. Says he'd rather draft a proven, efficient back like Johnson in that range than gamble on unproven upside names like Anthony McFarland or [[Isaac Guerendo]] (not tracked/tracked respectively), comparing his own scouting instincts here to past Spencer Ware/Alex Collins/Arian Foster/Chester Taylor-type grinder-back hits.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'fuck them' as draft picks — Johnson and [[Kimani Vidal]] are waiver-wire watch-list guys, not draftable, and are not even locks to make rosters. He grants Johnson has looked great, but so do a lot of players in preseason, and the people covering the team daily still have him behind [[Chuba Hubbard]] (whose knee is not an issue), behind [[Jonathan Brooks]] once healthy, behind [[Miles Sanders]] and behind Raheem Blackshear. Bob Harris (not tracked) agrees on the draft verdict but stresses the other half: put him high on the watch list, because Terrell Davis nearly did not make a roster, [[Nick Chubb]] was covering punts as a rookie, and Isaiah Crowell got his shot only when two backs ahead of him got hurt. With new kickoff rules likely pushing teams to keep more special-teamers, many of them backs, attrition creates the opportunity.
