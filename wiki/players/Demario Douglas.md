# Demario Douglas

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: scores almost exactly average (97.5) on Adam Harstad's [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] — a solid, if unspectacular, raw yards-per-route-run number (106.1) dragged all the way to league-average by zero receiving touchdowns on the year. Both hosts read him as more valuable than the score implies: on a barren New England offense, a throw to Douglas "wasn't just the best option, it was pretty much the only option to make something positive happen" for the Patriots — Waldman felt he should have scored higher given the degree to which he outclassed his own supporting cast. Verdict: a pause on paying a third-round rookie-draft price, but a clear buy in the fourth or fifth round.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): named
  alongside [[Michael Wilson]] as the most promising ascending player in a
  group of the league's worst receiver rooms, based on 2023 film rather than
  pre-draft evaluation — rated ahead of [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] as a slot option
  specifically because he can beat man coverage in the intermediate area,
  which Robinson can't do consistently.
- 2024-07-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-05]]) [Dynasty]: Douglas graded as a 'bizarre' rookie profile. He was a pure slot/motion piece (50.1% of snaps from the slot, 81.5% off the line, another 9.1% out of the backfield primarily as a pre-snap motion option) with an extremely constricted route tree -- 48.4% of his routes were screens, slants and flats, a tree Harmon compares directly to Rashee Rice's. Counter to the typical pattern for that route profile, Douglas actually graded better vs. man (69.1% success, 53rd percentile, a good mark for a rookie) than vs. zone (75.6% raw success but closer to the bottom quartile among his peers), which Harmon calls 'weird' and hard to project -- both a plus (more than a typical slot player) and a red flag (unclear ceiling). His catch rate was concerning at only 62% given how many easy underneath targets he ran, though he had strong hands otherwise (only 1 drop charted, 62.5% contested catch rate) and flashed after the catch (69.4% of his catches came in space, broke a tackle on 48 of those). Harmon's best statistical comp is Darnell Mooney's 2020 rookie season (69.8% man / 74.7% zone / 53.1% press), while noting Mooney didn't play as much pure slot as Douglas did. Heading into 2024, both hosts see Douglas's role as murky under a new coaching regime that drafted [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] and Javon Baker and could still lean on veterans Kendrick Bourne (if healthy) and JuJu Smith-Schuster, calling his path to playing time 'shaky at best' even though they believe he's shown he can be a useful NFL slot piece.
- 2024-07-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon called Demario Douglas (referred to on air by an ASR-garbled nickname) a 'hard to decipher' but genuinely useful player -- an unusual man-coverage-beating slot receiver (69% success rate vs. man) rather than the more typical zone-beating slot type -- calling it 'a nice showing for a sixth-round pick out of Liberty.' He expects Douglas to remain a solid complementary role player in New England's receiver room alongside [[Kendrick Bourne]].
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Douglas is New England's second-leading route runner (46, behind Hunter Henry's 53) but Harmon calls him an odd fit for what this offense needs. From his Reception Perception work, Douglas is a man-beating slot receiver who is definitively not a downfield threat and not really a threat in the intermediate area either — a slant-flat merchant, reflected in a 3.0 average depth of target this year. Harmon's point is scheme mismatch rather than a talent knock: if the plan is under-center play action off a good run game, you cannot have Douglas as your leading receiver.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-10]]): Harmon reiterated his offseason read that Douglas is 'a bit of an oddball slot receiver' who is a better man-coverage beater than a zone beater, and said he still generally feels that way about him as a player. Co-host James Koh (not tracked), who had flagged Douglas on his own podcast, argued that only one of Douglas and [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] gets a real bump from [[Drake Maye]] starting, and said he is more intrigued by Douglas for the immediate Week 6 window — expecting a fine game — while thinking Polk plus Maye is the better long-term answer.
- 2024-10-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-15]]): Douglas was always going to be the first beneficiary of [[Drake Maye]] taking over, because he is the layup target in this offense — someone Maye could rely on in the first start, though not a player who works much downfield. Harmon admits Douglas is hard for him to get a handle on: he is one of these man-beating slot receivers, clearly a good player, but Harmon says outright he does not know what the ceiling is. He would like to see Douglas and [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] alternate roles, because their strengths are different and neither is being deployed to his strengths right now. The broader frame is that New England's is a room of WR3s until it finds a true X.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls it an unpopular opinion but says Douglas is only going to give you so much — he would start [[Kayshon Boutte]] ahead of him, keep [[Ja'Lynn Polk]], and try to trade Douglas to another team for more value than he was acquired for. The broader point is that New England's only competence at the position is drafting slot receivers, and Douglas is one of about four primary slot types on the roster, which caps his ceiling regardless of his own ability.
- 2025-02-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-28]]): Douglas tied for the Patriots team lead in receptions (66) and was second in yards at 621 on a team where nobody cracked 675 receiving yards. Harmon calls him 'a good receiver three, a slot guy — kind of a weird slot guy because he's more of a man coverage beater than a zone beater,' but somebody you happily play as your slot receiver. His ceiling is capped by role rather than ability: Harmon wants New England to add a genuine outside dog so Douglas can stay in the slot and [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] can work from the flanker spot. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) called him perhaps the most reliable receiver on the 2024 roster.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): with reporting (Mike Reiss, ESPN) that [[Stefon Diggs]] could see heavy slot usage, Waldman called Douglas strictly a late-round hedge on Diggs and warned he might be relegated to punt returns. He joked that the Patriots only ever see slot receivers and might field three or four at once, but the framing is clear — Douglas is a contingency, not a target.
- 2025-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-30]]): Harmon thinks Douglas is a pretty nice player and had his profile queued to publish within a couple of weeks, but he pushes back on the section of fantasy Twitter that is jacked up about him purely because he projects as Josh McDaniels' slot receiver. Harmon's counterexample is [[Hunter Renfrow]], the archetypal McDaniels slot who fell out of favor in Las Vegas immediately after signing an extension — so the assumption that the role itself confers value is shaky.
- 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]): Harmon names Douglas one of the two favorites for Patriots playing time alongside [[Stefon Diggs]] and is curious how the slot snaps split between them, since Diggs projects more outside. Citing Taylor Kyles' Patriots coverage, Harmon is interested in Douglas being more than a slot-only player, and his 2023 Reception Perception profile supports it: a better man beater than zone beater at a 69.1 percent success rate versus man, someone who could hack it outside in the short areas though not downfield. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) calls Douglas a set-it-and-forget-it type but caps his ceiling, saying it is not great and he is mostly a slot guy.
- 2025-08-13 — According to Dave Kluge of Footballguys (guest, not a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-13]]: Douglas is his number one ADP surprise — WR70, going in round 16, effectively free, when Kluge projects him over 100 targets. He liked last year's tape, notes Douglas took five times as many slot snaps as any other Patriot, and that Josh McDaniels historically funnels the offense through the slot; Foxborough buzz has Douglas 'unguardable' with [[Drake Maye]] praising him, while the shiny new toy [[Kyle Williams]] is struggling to get up to speed and [[Kayshon Boutte]] survives on old draft-profile faith. Kluge thinks the targets go to [[Stefon Diggs]] and Douglas. [[Chris Harris]] has him WR60 standard / WR57 PPR, called him a post-hype sleeper the previous day, says he will 'definitely wind up with him', but caps expectations at a flex piece rather than a top-50 receiver.
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]): Waldman is 'feeling' Tom Curran's (NBC Sports) projection of 120 targets for Douglas — and says he believes that more readily than the claim that [[Hunter Henry]] is locked in as New England's number one target. His view is that Henry is the safety blanket and a viable option, but the staff seems very intent on getting Douglas the ball, which makes him a reasonable healthy WR3 in fantasy. Both hosts noted the market has been early on Douglas before, with Bob Harris (not tracked) observing that fantasy analysts are sometimes a year or two ahead of a breakout that may never arrive.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Douglas, 16% rostered, is guest BRANDON FUNSTON's number two waiver add and Harris's number five. Funston's case is Josh McDaniels's history with slot receivers and pass-catching backs plus hoped-for second-level improvement from [[Drake Maye]] — opportunity exists, so the history is worth gambling on. Harris puts him on the list but is the explicit contrarian: he cautions against role-based reasoning ('cool, so James White was worth owning in fantasy for all eight years of his career — obviously not') and says the paint-by-numbers analysis is almost never right, adding that if Douglas were going to be a good NFL player it feels like it would have happened by now.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest Andy Behrens (not tracked) said he almost ranked Douglas ahead of [[Stefon Diggs]] for Week 1 and only did not because his ranking heuristic leans toward the player with the better best case — sometimes one player is likelier to reach 10 fantasy points while another is likelier to reach 20, and he feels that way about Diggs versus Douglas. Both hosts stressed the practical conclusion is to start neither: Harris is skeptical New England has fixed the offense and says 'you are under no obligation to start one player from every team.'
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-13]]): 'fuck it' on Douglas as a legitimate weekly play despite three catches for 71 yards, though it is closer to a wait-and-see. Waldman noted the day would have been bigger if not for an inexplicable pass interference call on [[Stefon Diggs]] that looked more like defensive back Kool-Aid McKinstry's doing, which cost roughly another 50-yard touchdown. The reason to monitor him is usage: Josh McDaniels (not tracked) deployed Douglas and [[Kayshon Boutte]] against their tendencies, and if that continues there will be good weeks. But Waldman judged Douglas's upside capped relative to Diggs and Boutte. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) added the target math: on only 26 [[Drake Maye]] attempts, Boutte saw five and Douglas four while everyone else including Diggs and [[Hunter Henry]] saw three, and that distribution will not repeat weekly. Verdict from both: emergency bye-week fill-in yes, consistent play no.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): with [[Kayshon Boutte]] dealing with a hamstring injury, Waldman said 'Pop Douglas Month' is possible if Boutte misses the month. New England featured Douglas inside against a slot matchup, ran him across the field with throwback action and worked double moves to the outside; he won a pretty contested catch on an out-and-up-and-out and was targeted on three downfield throws from [[Drake Maye]], winning two. The caveat is real: Waldman said [[Stefon Diggs]] is the one to lean on and Douglas is the pray-and-hope option, still probably third behind [[Hunter Henry]] and Diggs, with Douglas and [[Mack Hollins]] fighting over what is left. Bob Harris (not tracked) disclosed he had just traded for Douglas.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): Douglas posted the first 100-yard game of his career after [[Kayshon Boutte]] left with a hamstring injury, scoring on a wheel route out of the backfield on a designed gimmick play from [[Drake Maye]] and adding a 58-yard deep over on third-and-eight where he was not the first read. Harris cautions against overreading it — 58 of the yards came on 'a little bit of a luck play' and he would not call Douglas a downfield monster or a one-to-one replacement for what Boutte had been doing. He does allow the new coaching staff has used Douglas as more than the [[Wan'Dale Robinson]]-style one-yard-pass type, and being the second receiver in a good offense 'is not the worst thing I've ever heard of'.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-04]]): Pop Douglas posted his first career 100-yard game — 6 targets, 4 catches, exactly 100 yards with 30 after the catch, and a touchdown — on just 13 routes. James Koh (not a tracked expert) stresses how far he has fallen: under a 26% snap share over his last seven games versus a 62% average in 2024. Harmon's explanation is role, not talent: a poor start (around 13 targets for 13 yards at one point) cost him snaps to a rising [[Stefon Diggs]] as a slot flanker and to Mack Hollins, whose run blocking New England wants while trying to fix the ground game, plus heavy multiple-tight-end usage with Austin Hooper, [[Hunter Henry]] and [[Jack Westover]]. Harmon calls him a low-usage, high-yield player when they do move him around, including a backfield alignment for his touchdown. He also flags that Douglas played after his uncle was shot and killed on Saturday night.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): an explicit self-correction — Harris had floated Douglas as the emergency play behind [[Stefon Diggs]] and says 'I led you absolutely astray there.' The Patriots played [[Kyle Williams]] far more (roughly 17 routes to Douglas's 10), and Harris did not want Williams either outside of his touchdown; [[Mack Hollins]] was the real beneficiary.
- 2026-01-22 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-22]]): Harmon (using Douglas's 'Pop' nickname) listed him alongside [[Kayshon Boutte]] and Stefon Diggs as one of three underrated Patriots receivers who made the big plays New England needed to beat Houston, noting Douglas moves around the formation and produced a notable big play in that game as a complementary role player.
- 2026-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Normalized from the transcript's garbled 'Pop Douglas.' Harmon flagged Douglas as a potential explosive-play change-of-pace option behind [[Stefon Diggs]] against Seattle, noting that earlier in the season the Patriots ran scissor and switch-release slot concepts alternating Diggs and Douglas, and that at one point this year Douglas was one of the NFL's most productive receivers on a per-route basis against man coverage. Harmon was clear this has been a part-time role rather than a full-time one for Douglas all season.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Douglas's days as an outside/split-end receiver in New England are over, in Waldman's view -- based on his college tape, Waldman thinks the smart move is to use him as a big slot or move him around as a flanker/big-slot hybrid, especially with Stefon Diggs no longer part of the offense. That role change, alongside the possible addition of [[AJ Brown]] and continued investment in [[Kyle Williams]], is part of a broader reshaping of the Patriots' receiver room Waldman and [[Brandon Angelo]] expect this offseason.
- 2026-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-30]]) [Dynasty]: Despite being 'pretty buried' last year, playing only 25.9% of Patriots snaps, Demario Douglas averaged 3.2 yards per route run versus man coverage -- sixth-most among 200-plus-route receivers behind only George Pickens and Nico Collins -- and Harmon's charting shows he's graded better versus man than versus zone, unusual for a player often typecast as a pure slot. A Boston Herald report now has Douglas rotating through every alignment combination with [[A.J. Brown]] and Romeo Doubs rather than being locked to the slot. Harmon floats Douglas as a potential [[Stefon Diggs]]-role replacement (a roughly 50/50 inside-outside role, matching Diggs' 52.3% slot rate last year), while cautioning it's 'never a one-for-one' comp and he doesn't expect Douglas's route share to rise much given the crowded room.
- 2026-08-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: With [[Kayshawn Boutte]] traded away, Harris expects Douglas to again be the third receiver in three-wide sets out of the slot for New England, but is openly skeptical of the camp hype around him: 'everybody said he had a great camp -- where have I heard that before? Oh right, every single season of his career.' He's not buying Douglas as a fantasy factor this year and reiterates he'd rather take [[Romeo Doubs]] as the Patriots deep-round receiver bet.
