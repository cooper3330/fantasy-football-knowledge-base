# Demario Douglas

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: scores almost exactly average (97.5) on Adam Harstad's [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] — a solid, if unspectacular, raw yards-per-route-run number (106.1) dragged all the way to league-average by zero receiving touchdowns on the year. Both hosts read him as more valuable than the score implies: on a barren New England offense, a throw to Douglas "wasn't just the best option, it was pretty much the only option to make something positive happen" for the Patriots — Waldman felt he should have scored higher given the degree to which he outclassed his own supporting cast. Verdict: a pause on paying a third-round rookie-draft price, but a clear buy in the fourth or fifth round.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): named
  alongside [[Michael Wilson]] as the most promising ascending player in a
  group of the league's worst receiver rooms, based on 2023 film rather than
  pre-draft evaluation — rated ahead of [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] as a slot option
  specifically because he can beat man coverage in the intermediate area,
  which Robinson can't do consistently.
- 2024-07-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-05]]) [Dynasty]: Douglas graded as a 'bizarre' rookie profile. He was a pure slot/motion piece (50.1% of snaps from the slot, 81.5% off the line, another 9.1% out of the backfield primarily as a pre-snap motion option) with an extremely constricted route tree -- 48.4% of his routes were screens, slants and flats, a tree Harmon compares directly to Rashee Rice's. Counter to the typical pattern for that route profile, Douglas actually graded better vs. man (69.1% success, 53rd percentile, a good mark for a rookie) than vs. zone (75.6% raw success but closer to the bottom quartile among his peers), which Harmon calls 'weird' and hard to project -- both a plus (more than a typical slot player) and a red flag (unclear ceiling). His catch rate was concerning at only 62% given how many easy underneath targets he ran, though he had strong hands otherwise (only 1 drop charted, 62.5% contested catch rate) and flashed after the catch (69.4% of his catches came in space, broke a tackle on 48 of those). Harmon's best statistical comp is Darnell Mooney's 2020 rookie season (69.8% man / 74.7% zone / 53.1% press), while noting Mooney didn't play as much pure slot as Douglas did. Heading into 2024, both hosts see Douglas's role as murky under a new coaching regime that drafted [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] and Javon Baker and could still lean on veterans Kendrick Bourne (if healthy) and JuJu Smith-Schuster, calling his path to playing time 'shaky at best' even though they believe he's shown he can be a useful NFL slot piece.
