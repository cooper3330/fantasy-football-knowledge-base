# Jalen Milroe

## Expert Takes

- 2024-09-25 — According to guest Felix Sharpe of Campus2Canton (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-25 (Campus2Canton with Felix Sharpe)]]) [Dynasty]: Milroe is an interesting case study — probably underdeveloped as a passer, but undeniable as a rushing threat. Sharpe framed the upside through [[Jalen Hurts]], who left Alabama for Oklahoma and developed into a passer, and argued Milroe has an advantage Hurts lacked in that he is already working under Kalen DeBoer, one of the best offensive play-callers in the country.
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman likes Milroe and defends him against the market's two main knocks. He calls the release compact — a javelin thrower's motion — and says Milroe is more accurate and a much better pocket player than people realize, provided you remind yourself repeatedly that this is not Nick Saban's Alabama offensive line and that Saban would have had a conniption over the protection Milroe played behind. On hand size (eight and three quarters, a quarter inch under [[Joe Burrow]]) Waldman does not worry 'a f**king bit', and he attributes Milroe's fumbles to blindside hits and pressure while cocking the ball rather than to hand size — the sort of hits only a Ben Roethlisberger or Steve McNair would have survived.
- 2025-02-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-27]]) [Dynasty]: [[Jalen Milroe]] is the only prospect who could close the gap on [[Cam Ward]] for his QB1 spot. Waldman calls his release the best in the class — he went back and compared it to Dan Marino's, 'comes back and out like he's throwing a javelin' — and says Milroe manipulates the middle-of-field defender the way Peyton Manning did, citing a mesh concept against Georgia where he held the linebacker with his eyes with a guard being bull-rushed into his lap and threw a pinpoint 15-yard out. He frames Milroe as a better pocket player than [[Jalen Hurts]] with higher accuracy upside, and the best pure runner at quarterback since [[Lamar Jackson]] at 225 pounds with 'curvy linear speed like Matt Forte'; possible 4.38-4.39 at the combine. Caveat: production dipped in 2024 behind a far worse Alabama offensive line, with blindside strip-fumbles. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees the range is wide — [[Jalen Hurts]] at the top, [[Trey Lance]] at the bottom if the infrastructure misfits — but likes the day-two cost; both name Detroit as the ideal landing spot and say Milroe is what people wanted [[Hendon Hooker]] to be.
- 2025-03-28 — According to Cory Kinnan, Reception Perception's quarterback charter (not a tracked expert), on [[Matt Harmon]]'s show ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-28]]): Milroe is his QB3 behind [[Cam Ward]] and [[Shedeur Sanders]], though he concedes that is 'mainly because I just don't like [[Jackson Dart]], [[Will Howard]], [[Tyler Shough]].' The arm is powerful but stiff and linear — no arc, touch or layering, few arm slots — yet he saw the lowest man-coverage rate of anyone charted (under 30% over eight games) because defenses will not turn their backs on his legs, and his man success rate charts higher than Dart's. Kinnan calls his floor dangerously low because operating quickly and anticipating pressure is the floor-raising skill and it is his worst, citing 11 interceptions and games against South Carolina and Oklahoma where he looked 'truly undraftable,' against highs like the Georgia game where he beat nickel and safety blitzes. He is a pure upside shot he would take at picks 33-50 but not fourth overall, praising the work-ethic signal from a hideous Senior Bowl into a more accurate combine and pro day with Jordan Palmer. Harmon rejects the Jalen Hurts comp on throwing style; Kinnan accepts it only on the work-ethic axis and both raise Anthony Richardson and Malik Willis as the downside.
- 2025-03-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-31]]) [Dynasty]: Milroe is the best pocket player in the class. Waldman credits him with [[Lamar Jackson]]-style 'quiet feet' — subtle moves that keep his eyes downfield and his body in position to throw — as opposed to the 'loud footwork' of early-career Michael Vick or [[Kyler Murray]], which is flashy but drops the eyes, forces resets, and often creates pressure by working outside the blockers' leverage. Milroe has just as much mobility as those two while climbing, spinning, sidestepping and flushing without wrecking his linemen's protection or his receivers' routes. He is also among the class's best manipulators, and not with the basic hold-the-safety-during-the-drop trick every prospect can do: he moves linebackers and nickels in the middle of the field within 10 to 15 yards of the line late in the play, against the blitz, with a tackler about to land in his lap. Waldman calls his release the most compact in the class and perhaps several classes, going back to Dan Marino's tape for comparison. The single missing tool is touch — he does not get enough air under the ball for corner fades, the honey hole between safety and corner in cover two, or throwing receivers open at the boundary, and he is prone to overshooting those targets.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: Milroe is the player Waldman most suspects he is too bearish on. He is QB2 on Waldman's board, but no quarterback in the class processes the field the way Milroe does in the situations that matter; Waldman graded him conservatively on two accuracy tiers because of sample size, and said that if those had gone the other way Milroe would have been his QB1. He would not consider [[Cam Ward]] at all and would instead wait to take Milroe in the third, fourth or fifth round of a rookie draft for the value, accepting that he will be seen as a project. [[Brandon Angelo]] agreed he is not as far behind as people think, stressed his maturity and preparation — the thing that separates him from an [[Anthony Richardson]] profile — and used [[Malik Willis]] as the cautionary and hopeful comp: thrown into the fire in the worst situation, then capable under LaFleur in Green Bay. Angelo's preferred landing spot is Pittsburgh, calling Milroe a better processor than [[Justin Fields]] was last year and pointing to a year behind [[Aaron Rodgers]] before taking over; both value the extra defender his run game forces teams to account for.
- 2025-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: Milroe is one of Waldman's top three quarterbacks in the class alongside [[Cam Ward]] and [[Shedeur Sanders]], and he holds the HIGHEST upside of the three — the best chance for upside in the draft, in Waldman's words. Asked by Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) whether the Tommy Rees connection in Cleveland points to a surprise at pick two, Waldman notes Milroe was invited to Green Bay for the draft, which may signal feelers, and says he is 'feeling' the possibility while joking it would be fitting for the team he roots against. He also floats Milroe as a good landing spot for the Giants if he slid to the second round and could sit behind [[Russell Wilson]].
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): Milroe is his pick to go HIGHER than the market expects, and he read the wave of anonymous-scout leaks (an AFC scout claiming Alabama's skill players would have staged a 'mass exodus' had he stayed) as deliberate pre-draft misinformation from teams hoping he falls. Waldman answered it by reading nearly identical anonymous quotes once written about [[Patrick Mahomes]] ('reckless', 'plays crazy'), [[Jalen Hurts]] ('entitled attitude', 'game manager') and [[Lamar Jackson]] ('no shot at playing quarterback in the NFL'), calling the exodus story 'bullshit'. He expects a team to trade up in the late first or a dramatized mid-second slide the media will frame as a fall when Milroe was projected round three or four only a month or two earlier. [[Brandon Angelo]] independently wrote down the same answer, calls Milroe a first-round talent and the perfect long-term swing behind a veteran, and would be shocked if he got past 25-26 — naming the Giants, Browns and Steelers, though he wants no quarterback near Arthur Smith. Waldman would love him banked behind [[Jared Goff]] in Detroit.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]) [Dynasty]: one of only two quarterbacks in this class he would spend a pick on as a pretend NFL GM (the other being [[Kurtis Rourke]]), specifically because you get him at a discount with everyone underestimating him. His reasoning is the Washington template — a franchise turned around entirely by pairing a head coach with a super-athletic running quarterback — and he says watching Milroe he thought that if you could teach this guy to throw, that alone is worth the investment. He was surprised Milroe did not go earlier. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) says he targeted Milroe in a pre-NFL-draft dynasty rookie draft, missed out, and ended up with [[Tyler Shough]] instead.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Dynasty]: he hopes Milroe does not start in Seattle this year, but wants to see it eventually — Milroe may be the best running quarterback drafted since [[Lamar Jackson]] and, in Waldman's view, offers more as a runner than Michael Vick did in his prime. He is also an underrated pocket player who manipulates the field well when he understands what he is seeing; the NFL question is coverage disguises, though Waldman calls him a massively good student who will grow fast. He compares the path to Jordan Love's and thinks Milroe could be considerably better than Love. ESPN reportedly has him second on the depth chart ahead of [[Drew Lock]] with situational red zone usage planned; Waldman likes situational use continuing even if [[Sam Darnold]] falters, and says he drafted Milroe in dynasty and would rather wait a year or two than spend a third or fourth rounder now.
- 2025-07-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-28]]): 'fuck it' on Milroe starting in Seattle by year's end absent a [[Sam Darnold]] injury — though Waldman says everyone wants to feel it. He expects Seattle to build red zone packages for Milroe and to let him close out games when the Seahawks are up more than 14–17 points, producing exciting moments and real promise that will fuel the narrative. But he does not think Darnold plays badly enough to lose the job: Waldman credits Minnesota's offensive line and receiver trio for part of 2024 but insists Darnold genuinely played well against pressure and against the blitz, and he gives Seattle's upgraded-on-paper line the benefit of the doubt. Bob Harris (untracked) is more ambivalent, saying Darnold could lose the job at any time.
