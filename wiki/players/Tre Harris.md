# Tre Harris

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2025-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-19]]): from early Reception Perception charting of the 2025 class, Harmon flags the 6-foot-3, 210-pound Ole Miss receiver as a man-coverage-beating perimeter option and says his charting profile looks 'a little [[Alec Pierce]]-ish' so far. He does not see him as a necessary first-round pick — more a day-two guy on the early data — and names Kansas City and Detroit as teams needing exactly that perimeter type. Harmon told James Koh (not tracked) to put his eyeballs on Harris at the Combine the following week and wondered aloud whether he can rip a sub-4.4 forty; Koh admitted he was not well versed in the film and would go look.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): Harris has a variety of route setups but does not apply them with expertise convincing to NFL defenders — Waldman says right now they are not even convincing to college defenders. The fix he prescribes is attacking his steps with more ferocity and executing with precision and snap, which is currently missing. That urgency is the whole gap between Harris being a contested-catch and zone option and being a contested-catch and zone option who is also a strong man-to-man route runner.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: Harris is the player he fears he is too bullish on. He carries an 80-85th percentile grade and Angelo comped him to Allen Robinson II, calling him a standout on traits and metrics — but two season-ending injuries are hard to stomach for a receiver still developing skill, and the biggest predictor of future injury is past injury. His stated mechanism is motor learning: a college star has everything flow through him, then in the NFL he must learn a narrow role among equal or better athletes, which is jarring on its own; add a camp or preseason injury and someone else plays that role better while he watches. He cited Jalen Reagor — lighting up Eagles camp, a one-handed catch in joint practice, then hurt and never the same — and Corey Coleman as players who never recovered mentally or from a confidence standpoint.
- 2025-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-11]]): the Ole Miss product (6'2', 205, 4.54 forty, 38.5-inch vertical, 10'5' broad) is a true straight-line X — 54.9% of snaps on the line, 78.8% outside, and unlike [[Jayden Higgins]] or [[Tetairoa McMillan]] he will not move inside. Charting: 73.2% success vs man, 72.2% vs press, but only 75.2% vs zone, and the tree is stripped down — 56% of routes were go, curl or screen, with under 8% of all routes breaking to the outside (corner, out, comeback, flat). 61.5% on the nine, 85.3% on the slant, and Harmon says teams fear his vertical speed enough to open the curl. Harmon likes the play speed better than the timed speed (he was injured late in the season) and calls the contested-catch work above average and improving, but flags almost no sideline work and calls the profile volatile — no floor, high ceiling. Comps span [[DK Metcalf]] at the top, [[Mike Williams]] upper-middle, [[Alec Pierce]] as the hoped-for landing spot, and — Harmon's 'scary' comp — Terrace Marshall, whose LSU numbers (74% vs man, 75.7% vs zone) are nearly identical. Grades him a second-rounder and a developmental year-one player. James Koh (not a tracked expert) is less enthused, hung up on the sideline work.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21 (Film & Data with Dwain McFarland)]]): Harris is a very good ball winner in a [[Tetairoa McMillan]]-type outside role who runs well after the catch, but he decelerates poorly on hard-breaking routes — curls and comebacks come out at half to three-quarter speed. Waldman's image is a chess player who isn't yet a master, still studying the board and keeping a finger on the piece as he moves it; he attributes it to Harris being a former quarterback still learning the route game. Waldman thinks he can get there because the bend is visible on tape, but contrasts him directly with [[Isaiah Bond]], who 'runs everything at full speed' and has no such issues. Waldman mentioned watching Harris' tape alongside Chad Johnson and [[Davante Adams]] clips with Dave Kluge to illustrate the difference between having a technique and executing it with NFL-level craft.
- 2025-04-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-29]]): Harris to the Chargers is 'exactly the guy that they needed' — a true X so [[Ladd McConkey]] can play flanker in two-receiver sets and slide inside, and so [[Quentin Johnston]] can work horizontally off the line rather than at X. Profile: 6-2, 205, 4.54 with a 38.5-inch vertical, 78.8% of snaps outside, 54.9% on the line, 78th-percentile success rate vs press, 85% on slants and 88% on curls because he threatens vertical then whips back, 21.5% contested target rate with a 75% contested catch rate. Harmon and Lance Zierlein both comp him to [[Alec Pierce]], with the archetype range running from Terrace Marshall at the floor through Pierce to [[DK Metcalf]] at the ceiling; Harmon expects him to start as a sacrificial X and hopes he develops past it. James Koh (not tracked) pushes back that Harris's sideline work — corner routes, nine routes, hand-eye at the boundary — looked poor, which matters for a full-time X; Harmon agrees it is a wide-range-of-outcomes profile. Lane Kiffin's everything-breaks-inside offense and the presence of [[Mike Williams]] could mean a partial redshirt, though Harmon doubts they drafted him this high to sit.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]) [Dynasty]: Harris is of greater interest than [[Quentin Johnston]], but Waldman cautioned that does not mean you pull the trigger. Harris is more promising and a little better at catching the ball, but he is not the route runner Johnston is, and Waldman put getting open ahead of catching the ball at this stage even though he rates the two nearly equal in importance. With Mike Williams back to man the X, he expects Harris and Johnston to split flanker snaps short term, and he would still give Johnston the short-term edge; dynasty-wise the answer flips to Harris. He also flagged the opposite risk — by the end of the season Johnston may be making plays and Harris buried.
- 2025-05-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-16]]): the Chargers drafting Harris is less a bet on Harris popping off than a move that 'unlocks more [[Ladd McConkey]]' by giving the offense a straight-line X. Harmon places Harris on that archetype's ladder — Terrace Marshall Jr. at the bottom (Harris is better, in his view), [[Alec Pierce]] as the startable midline, [[DK Metcalf]] as the peak — and jokes that after being rumored to trade for Metcalf, the Chargers instead bought the cheap version, with the upgrade from [[Russell Wilson]] to [[Justin Herbert]] as the added variable.
- 2025-06-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-05]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is 'not as bullish on Tre Harris year one as some people seem to be.' Angelo likes what Harris can be but calls the Chargers a weird offense to project — Harris's best fit is a high-volume X role, and Angelo's thought experiment is flipping him with [[Jayden Higgins]] in Houston so [[Nico Collins]] could play more Z. In Los Angeles the primary read is [[Ladd McConkey]], because he is that good and because it worked last year. Waldman's optimistic case requires stacking two ifs — [[Quentin Johnston]] leaving and [[Mike Williams]] being beaten out or unhealthy — and even then lands at maybe 1,000 yards and eight touchdowns on 80–90 catches; he suspects the Harris hype is media wish-casting. Asked to choose in rookie drafts, both prefer [[Kyle Williams]] on situation, since Harris has no near-term path to WR1 while McConkey is on the field.
- 2025-06-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-27]]): Harris shows some release nuance but lacks the interplay of patience and suddenness — the footwork emphasis and hand counters — on enough routes; Waldman showed Dave Kluge [[Davante Adams]] and Chad Johnson tape beside Harris's and said the techniques are nominally the same but look worlds apart, invoking Adams's own line that it took him three years to learn to separate against press. He also clap attacks and fights the ball like [[Courtland Sutton]] — drops with big plays — but without [[Quentin Johnston]]'s tracking problem, which is why Waldman thinks Harris is the better X of the two. [[Matt Harmon]] charts him at a 78th-percentile success rate versus press, but on a very small handful of routes, and frames him as a developmental player who can still play early in a sacrificial X role: stretch the field, do two things well, and take on route-tree depth later. If [[Mike Williams]] goes down, Harmon hopes for [[Alec Pierce]]-style production.
- 2025-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-16]]): Harris is an official Chargers camp holdout as part of the leaguewide second-round fight over fully guaranteed contracts. Koh argues this matters more for Harris than for most rookies because he is a straight-line player who needs downfield chemistry with [[Justin Herbert]] — exactly the sort of player who has to be on the field. He is relaxed about a few days but says three or four weeks would worry him. Harmon adds that with [[Mike Williams]] opening camp on PUP this was the ideal window for Harris to take heavy reps, potentially claiming the X-receiver job and freeing [[Quentin Johnston]] from it; he thinks the odds of that decrease by the day. Harmon's general rule is that he hates rookie receivers missing offseason time, citing [[Rome Odunze]] and [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] as players who looked behind after camp absences. Both see Harris as behind [[Ladd McConkey]] regardless.
- 2025-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-18]]): Harris is still unsigned amid the wave of second-round rookie contract standoffs (Harmon notes the Bears signed Ozzy Trapilo and the 49ers signed their second-rounder the same day) and Harmon wants the holdout ended immediately, because with [[Mike Williams]] retired the Chargers will need Harris starting at X sooner than planned rather than as a mid-season promotion behind a placeholder.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]): Harris' outlook is obviously better with Mike Williams retiring — Waldman is 'feeling' the value bump and grants he could win the starting job — but he is not enthused even so, capping his likely production at roughly 600-800 yards this year. He has the ability to become a 1,000-yard receiver but it will take more work, and Waldman details why rather than leaving it vague: Harris is not patient and sudden off the line of scrimmage. Compared with [[Davante Adams]], who performs the same moves but lulls the defender to sleep and then pulls the trigger suddenly — at the line, at the top of the stem, before the break, with sharp breaks and real pacing — Harris' move is 'a dance move that doesn't have the nuance and rhythm,' robotic and not fluid, and it does not cover enough surface area to work at NFL pace. At Ole Miss performing the move was enough; in the NFL it is how you perform it, and Waldman notes Adams himself said beating press took him two-to-three years. Waldman also flags that with top corners not shadowing [[Ladd McConkey]], Harris will draw the best cover men. He wants beat-writer reporting specifically on Harris' press releases.
- 2025-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-25]]): Harmon sees Harris as a low-volume but useful X receiver, and the whole Chargers room being 'good enough' is contingent on him being ready to play early. He is 6-2, 205 out of Ole Miss with a 4.54 forty and a 38.5-inch vertical that shows real short-area burst; both Harmon and Lance Zierlein comped him to [[Alec Pierce]]. His prospect charting is strong on the routes that matter for the role — 85 percent success on slants and 88 percent on curls — and he threatens vertical then comes back to the football well. The stated deficiency is sideline work along the boundary, which Harmon and Koh both flag as a problem for an X in a Greg Roman offense that will need someone to win 15-20 yards downfield on a corner against cover two. Harmon disagrees with Roman's reported plan to slot him into [[Josh Palmer]]'s old Z role, preferring [[Quentin Johnston]] at Z and Harris at X.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]): Waldman describes Harris as an up-and-down player who makes the spectacular plays but has to get better at the mundane details — details he believes Harris will eventually master, but not necessarily now — and says it is a real possibility that [[KeAndre Lambert-Smith]] passes him on the depth chart if Lambert-Smith opens well and sees the field more than expected in September. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) notes Harris was extremely productive in college but within an injury-narrowed band of games, expects a slow start, and cautions that Greg Roman's offenses have historically supported only one clearly fantasy-relevant receiver — which he still expects to be [[Ladd McConkey]].
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Dynasty]: an interesting player but more for dynasty than for 2025 [[Redraft (Standard)]]. Waldman rates [[KeAndre Lambert-Smith]] the better route runner of the two at this point, while allowing Harris is probably the better pass catcher, and expects the two to split roughly evenly as the fourth option in the receiving room. The specific limitation he names is press release — Harris does not have much nuance getting off press yet. [[Chris Harris]] frames the summer context: during the brief window when [[Mike Williams]] retired and [[Keenan Allen]] had not re-signed, people were sending Tre Harris to Canton, and the market has since calmed to something more realistic — a vertical-stretch player who makes an occasional big play rather than a week-to-week volume role.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-29]]): the [[Keenan Allen]] signing puts a damper on the Chargers rookies. Before that, Harmon's preferred alignment was Harris as the year-one X with [[Ladd McConkey]] at flanker and slot; the Chargers instead appear to view Harris as a [[Josh Palmer]]-type flanker, which Harmon does not like — he sees Harris as more of an [[Alec Pierce]] type, a straight-line X receiver who may in time develop into more, and playing him at flanker puts him behind Allen and McConkey for those reps. He also flagged concern for the Chargers' right side of the offensive line (Mekhi Becton injured, Trey Pipkins) affecting the passing game, while allowing that Jim Harbaugh and Greg Roman may fix it.
