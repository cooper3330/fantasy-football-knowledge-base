# Dylan Laube

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name as "Dylan Lobby"; normalized here to the real name of the New
> Hampshire running back, Dylan Laube.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-01]]): the episode's headline small-school riser — a small-school (New Hampshire) player who, in Senior Bowl one-on-ones, "was running with the receivers and torching defensive backs" as a pass catcher and also beat linebackers. Angelo's read: this is exactly the kind of confirmation Senior Bowl week exists to provide — "maybe the NFL might be higher on this kid than we might have thought" — since receiving work rarely shows up on his college tape at the volume needed to grade it confidently otherwise.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-01]]), relaying secondhand Senior Bowl reports rather than his own tape: corroborates Laube as a name he's specifically hearing about and needs to revisit.

- 2024-02-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-12]]) [Dynasty]: his own tape-based follow-up, no longer just relaying secondhand Senior Bowl buzz. Confirms the small-school-riser framing directly — All-Star Games exist precisely to test whether a small-school player (5'10", ~210 lbs, faced FCS-level competition) "can really hold up with big-time football," and Laube's Senior Bowl week was "validation that he holds up pretty well": quick, efficient, a skillful pass catcher who understands how to use his pads, not powerful but capable enough to handle between-the-tackles work. Names him a genuine [[Austin Ekeler]] archetype candidate — "that doesn't mean he's exactly Austin Ekeler, it just means he has some traits that could put him in a role similar to that." Explicitly the best 2024-fantasy-relevance bet of a trio of underrated Waldman-liked backs this episode (with Georgia's Daijun Edwards and Boise State's George Holani) — "Dylan Laube is probably your best shot" — while cautioning this is not a strong running back class at the top and none of the three should be top-12-rookie-round picks.
- 2024-02-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]) [Dynasty]: goes further — grades him a top-five back in the class outright (public boards have him at RB15-20), calling him "kind of that diamond in the rough player at the position." The receiving case is now the centerpiece: a legitimately good outside/boundary receiver, not just a checkdown option — presses the boundary well, adjusts cleanly to under- and over-thrown balls, does traditional-receiver things "you don't see as often" from a back. Also a genuinely good accelerator on tape (cites a long touchdown against Towson's defense). Floor comp: a spot-starter Kenneth Gainwell (not tracked) — "a smart runner who's kind of gained more of those receiving traits" — but explicitly a better receiver and a more abrupt runner than Gainwell was. Ceiling comp, caliber-wise: somewhere between [[James Cook]] and Kenneth Gainwell. [[Matt Waldman]], same conversation, corroborates and ranks him ahead of [[Jonathan Brooks]] overall — "there's not much difference in score for me with him and [[Blake Corum]]," praising his feel for integrating pad level, leverage, and footwork into contact, including the ability to spin away from what looks like a certain multi-defender tackle. Named alongside Brooks, Corum, and [[Jaylen Wright]] as one of the class's clear early-round talents regardless of actual draft slot.
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: his strongest receiving-specific praise yet — sharp route breaks off a good deep weight drop, strong pacing command on route setups, and disciplined hand/arm technique (frames rather than pushes off) to legally create separation. Flatly calls him "the best route runner of the backs in this class."

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): still a high-floor pick — the class's best receiving back in his tier, a "poor man's [[James Cook]]": a little less explosive at max velocity but potentially more impactful as a receiver than Cook, less so as a runner. Projects him a fifth-round pick capable of impacting an NFL backfield quickly, ahead of [[Blake Watson]] in that receiving-back tier. [[Matt Waldman]], same episode: agrees, calling him the best of the class's backs at positioning himself at the catch point on vertical routes — "plays it like a wide receiver" — and praises his processing on screens, blocking setups, and reading the field in and out of the box.
- 2024-05-02 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): Dylan Laube (referred to on air by a garbled name) lands with real opportunity in Las Vegas after the Raiders whiffed on trading up for a quarterback (Atlanta took theirs first) and settled into an Aidan O'Connell/Gardner Minshew competition that Waldman expects to produce a lot of checkdowns. Waldman said incumbent passing-down back Ameer Abdullah has proven to be a special-teams/third-down piece and nothing more, giving Laube 'a really good chance' to be the complement to starter [[Zamir White]] — both hosts noted the over/under on White's receptions is probably around five for the season — and called Laube a player with a unique enough skill set to stick around the league.
