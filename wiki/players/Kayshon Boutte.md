# Kayshon Boutte

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]): calls Boutte one of the more intriguing speculative stash adds on expanded offseason rosters given New England's thin receiver room behind an ACL-recovering [[Kendrick Bourne]], comparing the upside path to a Rhamondre Stevenson-style late breakout if Boutte proves to be a worker — though Waldman isn't yet sure that he is.
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]): Waldman has 'always liked' Boutte and 'feels it' on him as a future contributor after three catches for 59 yards and a touchdown with [[Drake Maye]], with whom he has been building chemistry away from the starters. He comps him to a mix of Robert Woods and Jarvis Landry, and points to the staff giving him more snaps over the past three weeks — including beating Derion Stingley one-on-one on a deep shot, which Waldman thought looked legitimate rather than a coverage bust at the end of the half. The caveat is New England's history of throwing receivers at the wall to see what sticks: he notes he could list about 15 non-slot receivers there who never did anything, and that plenty of people liked [[Ja'Lynn Polk]], a healthy scratch in this game.
- 2024-10-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-15]]): Boutte's involvement is interesting — only three targets but a 19-yard average depth of target, and stylistically he is the vertical X type New England lacks amid all the flanker/slot bodies. Harmon did not like the LSU film in Boutte's final season, calling him undisciplined and not technically sound, with a strange college arc where his freshman year was his best and he got worse from there, then nothing as a rookie. Koh (not tracked) walks through the athletic testing that dropped him to the sixth round in 2023: expected to run in the 4.4s at 5-11, 195, he ran a flat 4.5 with a sub-30-inch vertical and a sub-10-foot broad jump, numbers both hosts call bad for his size and inconsistent with how athletic he looks on film. Verdict: monitor whether the role holds; still a long shot alongside [[Javon Baker]].
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: asked whether Boutte is New England's best big-play threat, Waldman said 'feel it' — partly by default, because [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] 'can't hold onto the ball right now.' On Boutte's own merits: two big plays in the past two games, and while 'he's never been known as a huge speedster, he does have enough acceleration to get past one-on-one coverage and win the ball and track it down.' The caveat is snap count — 'it may not be as quick of an emergence as we'd like to see, but it is a steady emergence as a factor in that passing game,' and Waldman expects that role to keep growing.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31]]): the pair disagree on him. Angelo says Jerod Mayo is doing a poor job managing the receiver room and that New England should not be starting [[K.J. Osborne]] and Boutte — he would rather they start [[Javon Baker]]. Waldman defends Boutte partially: he has been the Patriots' best receiver, which is more an indictment of the room than praise, and Waldman thinks Boutte would make a good number three now and could have a long career in that role; he also credits Boutte with two big plays late to set up the winning touchdown. Angelo concedes the number-three projection. Both are amazed [[Tyquan Thornton]] is still getting snaps, and both say [[Drake Maye]] needs far more help than he is getting.
- 2024-11-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-20]]): Boutte has run a route on 96.3% of New England's dropbacks over the last four weeks, by far the most on the team, and has become their X and vertical guy — remarkable for a player who was a roster-bubble candidate in camp, as Koh pointed out. Harmon is unmoved: 'I was never that big on Kayshon Boutte. Maybe I end up being wrong, but I don't really think there's anything there.' He notes Boutte is not being targeted at a rate matching the routes and reads the whole thing as evidence the Patriots are in discovery mode, likely to scrap the room and start over around [[Drake Maye]] next offseason.
- 2025-01-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-13]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon gives Boutte credit for real improvement — he was a genuine roster-bubble player who began the year behind Tyquan Thornton, who is no longer on the roster at all, and he built a little something with [[Drake Maye]] running vertical routes as the team's X. Harmon still calls him an inconsistent player, as he was as a prospect, and says he would hope Boutte does not enter next season as New England's starting X receiver. The realistic outcome he sees is a future hanging onto the roster as a depth piece. [[Matt Waldman]] frames the question as whether the production was desperation or evidence of something more, noting the character concerns and the slot-flanker profile.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]) [Dynasty]: Boutte is the New England receiver Waldman would install as the starter, saying he thinks Boutte gives you more than [[Demario Douglas]] if he has caught on to what is going on — and noting Boutte is nominally a slot who can play flanker, yet was still beating people when the Patriots lined him up at X. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees Boutte looks better and better and can become a secondary option in an offense: four years ago he was a top-three dynasty devy asset before multiple surgeries and legal issues pushed him down, and he has fought through it and is turning into a pro. The caveat both give is that New England has roughly four primary slot receivers on the roster and keeps miscasting them.
- 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]): Harmon considers Boutte locked into a roster spot and, if he had to bet, would have him open the season as the Patriots' X receiver on the back of a reportedly good camp — ahead of [[Kyle Williams]] and well ahead of [[Ja'Lynn Polk]]. He frames this as an open competition at X that [[Terry McLaurin]] would win instantly if traded to New England, so Boutte holds the job by default rather than by conviction.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): New England is committing malpractice with Boutte — a player they nearly ran out of town after year one who then played X and played well enough there, but whom Angelo wants on another team as a slot or flanker. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed and put a number on it: freed from his current role, Boutte is a player who could give you 800 to 1,100 yards, and he compared him to Jarvis Landry with a little more speed. Waldman's extended joke — 'a turtle in a rabbit costume with an outboard motor' — was about Boutte finding ways to win without top-end speed. Both floated Buffalo and Carolina as better fits, Carolina as a successor to [[Adam Thielen]] [Dynasty].
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]): Waldman is 'absolutely feeling' Tom Curran's (NBC Sports) prediction that Boutte outproduces [[Stefon Diggs]]. He liked Boutte coming out of school as a very good flanker/slot type who wins in the middle of the field after the catch, adjusts well to the ball and runs decent routes — a gambling issue, not the tape, submarined his draft stock into the late rounds. Waldman notes that after a quiet rookie year New England then played him at split end, the worst of the three roles for him given he is not unbelievably fast, and he still played well. Curran reports Boutte looks better than Diggs, is winning contested catches, has improved hands and works well with [[Drake Maye]] in the red zone and two-minute drill. Waldman has moved him up his rankings and added him in late drafts; Bob Harris (not tracked) cited beat writer Tom King placing Boutte neck-and-neck with Diggs for target share behind [[Hunter Henry]].
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]) [Dynasty]: 'fuck yes' he remains a weekly start — Waldman traded [[Stefon Diggs]] and [[Tank Bigsby]] for Boutte and a second-round pick in the Footballguys Dynasty IDP League and does not regret it, though he suspects he could have gotten more back. Boutte is playing exactly as Waldman charted him at LSU: a [[Robert Woods]] / Jarvis Landry-type who wins back-shoulder throws, runs good routes against zone, is rugged, wins contested balls and takes hits — all on display against the Raiders. Beat writers say the two receivers [[Drake Maye]] is most comfortable with are Hunter Henry and Boutte. Waldman adds he may still be lined up at X, his worst position, and could still be New England's best receiver. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) cautions Diggs and [[Demario Douglas]] are not going away.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: this is a clean disagreement. Guest John Daigle (Establish The Run, not tracked) has Boutte as his number-one waiver add for the second straight week — the Patriots still have a wide-open No. 1 receiver job, Boutte leads the team in receiving yards, matches Hunter Henry for the team target lead, has been the guy [[Drake Maye]] peppers downfield across Maye's 12 full starts, and Daigle thinks he is growing into more than a deep-shot specialist in year three; he would bid 4-6 FAAB. Harris is skeptical: the 16-yard touchdown against Miami was a genuinely good play, reaching around a defender in the left corner of the end zone and showing good deep-ball skills, but he never likes his deep-ball receivers slow and short — Boutte is not that slow but is kind of short, no six-foot-four guy — and watching with fan's eyes, the fact that Boutte was invisible for much of a shootout where New England needed to move up and down the field scares him a little. Maye played really well and did not look for him.
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-13]]): 'feeling it' on Boutte, and a provisional add if you have the roster room. Boutte scored twice and made the game-sealing catch on a difficult back-shoulder throw. Waldman, a self-described Boutte devotee, had noted a week earlier against the Bills that [[Drake Maye]] climbed the pocket and missed Boutte working free up the left boundary on what would have been a huge play, so the encouraging development is that Maye now appears to be coming around to featuring and trusting him, which is what Tom Curran (not tracked) had argued earlier. Waldman emphasized how physical Boutte is relative to the other Patriots receivers and said he 'looks alpha' when given the football. His scheme complaint: New England plays him at X when he profiles as a big slot flanker, which Waldman compared to asking Eddie Van Halen to play the ukulele, but conceded the Patriots have nobody else for the X.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Boutte caught an end-of-half bomb from [[Drake May]], adjusting to reel in a pass thrown a bit too far. Harris said Boutte has been in his top-40 receivers for a few weeks now and is staying there off this game, though he noted he doesn't have to lock that in for the rest of the year.
- 2025-10-23 — According to Scott Pianowski (untracked Yahoo analyst) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-23]]): Boutte has three touchdowns in his last two games but only seven total targets on the season, making him touchdown-dependent (Pianowski's high target count for him in any game is five) against a Browns defense built to be a 'rock fight.' Pianowski was nervous relying on 'touchdown deodorant' and floated the safer, lower-ceiling [[Stefon Diggs]] as an alternative target earner. Harris was more bullish, saying he has started Boutte every week in one league and would keep doing so in desperate lineups, citing how the Patriots' passing offense under [[Drake Maye]] (with Josh McDaniels back as OC and Mike Vrabel as head coach) has become a genuinely usable environment for ancillary pass-catchers. An untracked source (Jake Seely) was cited comparing Boutte's touchdown-heavy, low-target profile to [[Nick Westbrook-Ikhine]].
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said to add [[Kayshon Boutte]] now, projecting him as New England's WR1 of the future. He's shown physicality at the line, has consistently won third-down targets the past few weeks, flashed enough deep speed to win vertically, and beat a strong cover corner (Denzel Ward) on a double-move comeback route. Waldman expects the Patriots to keep expanding his role; Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed based purely on the eyeball test -- size, physicality and playmaking that 'leap out' on tape -- and noted quarterback Drake May's quality elevates the profile further.
- 2025-10-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-30]]): Boutte has been 'insane' this year -- Harmon says he's among the top 35-36 fantasy WRs and, remarkably, dead last in that group in targets per route run, meaning his production is being driven almost entirely by an unsustainable 16.7% touchdown rate on a 13.8% target-per-route rate (touchdown rate higher than target rate is a statistical red flag Harmon calls out directly). Harmon guesses Boutte's career touchdown rate is under 2%, reinforcing that this pace won't hold. He still credits Boutte for playing his current role well and floats that moving him off X receiver could unlock more volume, which would help sustain value even as the touchdown rate normalizes.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-04]]): Boutte left after six routes in the second quarter with a hamstring injury; Mike Vrabel called him day-to-day and unlikely to practice early in the week, and Boutte posted on his own Discord that an MRI showed a grade one sprain with a possible Sunday return in Tampa or the Thursday night Jets game. Harmon says Boutte has genuinely been good and should not be kicked to the curb, but flags his profile as unstable: when Harmon charted him he was lining up almost exclusively at X running vertical routes, and his touchdown rate per target (16.1%) still exceeds his targets per route run (13.5%), which is a volatile way to produce. Harmon argues a [[Kyle Williams]] step forward would actually help Boutte by letting him take flanker and slot snaps and level out that production.
- 2025-11-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-11-18]]): Boutte (33% rostered, questionable to return from a hamstring injury) is Harris's No. 3 waiver add -- and he'd have ranked him ahead of [[Andrei Iosivas]] and [[Darnell Mooney]] outright if he were certain Boutte were healthy. Harris compares the Patriots' pass-spread tendencies to how the Bills used Keon Coleman in 2024: touches get distributed around rather than concentrated, so Boutte's appeal is volatility -- a chance at a long play and a touchdown rather than a guaranteed target floor -- against a Bengals defense in a game Harris likes. Guest Jake Seeley (The Athletic, not tracked) agrees Boutte would leapfrog the other two receivers on the list if his hamstring checks out, noting he was told earlier in the season Boutte 'can't keep doing it' on low volume, and that the volume concern is overstated if he's producing on limited touches.
- 2025-11-21 — According to Chris Harris on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-21]] [Redraft (Standard)]: Boutte practiced in full for New England and should be back in the lineup against the Bengals; Harris ranked him in his top 50 receivers. He didn't rank [[Mack Hollins]] in the top 50 despite Hollins having held a role for much of the year, and admitted that call 'could absolutely be wrong' since Boutte's return doesn't automatically make Hollins unusable.
- 2025-11-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-27]]): Boutte returned from a hamstring injury and was immediately reinserted as a starter, playing 80% of snaps, though he didn't produce much in his first game back. Harmon likes his over on 37.5 receiving yards (-114) against the Giants, who rank fifth in single-high coverage rate; Drake May has been one of the NFL's most efficient QBs throwing downfield against single-high looks, and Harmon says that coverage typically funnels targets to the X receiver outside -- which is Boutte's limited, volatile role. He projects at least 40 yards.
- 2025-12-09 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris's #2 Week 15 waiver add -- Boutte lacks a consistently large role but is an integral part of a genuinely good Patriots offense behind Drake Maye. Harris says he'd rather add a player tied to a strong offense like this than a similarly-uncertain-target receiver stuck with a third-string or rookie quarterback elsewhere, calling him a good second-flex or deep-league WR3 option.
- 2026-01-22 — According to Matt Harmon and James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-22]]) [Redraft]: the hosts highlighted that Boutte beat Texans corner Derrick Stingley for a touchdown on essentially the same route/coverage beat he hit Stingley with in last year's playoffs -- the two were LSU teammates -- and noted this year's version came against tighter coverage, a higher degree of difficulty. Harmon expects Boutte, Stefon Diggs and [[Demario Douglas]] to again be the Patriots' most productive receivers against Denver, with alignment likely putting top Denver corner Kamari Lassiter on Kyle Williams rather than Boutte, similar to how New England picked its spots away from Lassiter last week.
- 2026-02-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-17]]): Boutte was miscast as New England's X receiver last season only because nobody else on the roster plays that spot (Stefon Diggs doesn't play X either). Harmon thinks Boutte could be a more consistent player moved around the formation into more of a Z/slot role, and would like to see that if the Patriots bring in a true outside X-type receiver this offseason.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Boutte is a dynasty buy-low target Waldman still believes in. Angelo added a specific mechanism -- he thinks Boutte is part of a package the Patriots would use to acquire [[AJ Brown]] from Philadelphia, tied to the post-June 1st cap situation making that trade more workable. If that trade happens, Boutte's landing spot would shift as part of the deal; separately, both hosts see the Patriots' broader receiver-room shakeup (Boutte, [[Kyle Williams]], [[Demario Douglas]], [[Romeo Doubs]]) as fluid this offseason.
- 2026-07-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman strongly 'feels' Boutte's fantasy relevance and thinks he's being wasted -- if the Patriots properly deployed their receiving corps (Boutte in the slot, [[AJ Brown]] at flanker, Romeo Doubs at X), Boutte could be their second- or even first-leading receiver. He believes any team that trades for Boutte 'is stealing him,' name-checking Atlanta and even Kansas City as plausible destinations, and calls New England's current usage of him an underuse of a legitimate talent.
- 2026-08-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-25]]): Boutte was traded from the Patriots to the Texans for Jalen Reed and a 2028 seventh-round pick, minimal compensation Harmon called instructive about expectations -- he views Boutte as a solid but unspectacular number-three receiver, not overrated. Boutte took just 8.9% of his snaps from the slot with New England last year, sixth-lowest among all WRs and one of only seven receivers under a 10% slot rate (company: Ty Felton, Tay Martin, George Pickens, Kyle Williams, KeAndre Lambert-Smith, Rashad Bateman) -- a group Harmon calls classic 'sacrificial X receivers.' He was targeted on just 12% of his routes, showing he isn't a high-volume player as a pure boundary X. Harmon expects Houston to play him more as a Z/flanker, which he graded as a better college fit (a Day 3 prospect Harmon 'wasn't incredibly high on'), and thinks the role bump could raise his target rate. His full RP profile isn't finished yet, but so far Harmon calls him good-handed and strong at the catch point, not a consistent separator.
