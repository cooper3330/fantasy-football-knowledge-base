- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-05]]) [Dynasty]: naming [[Michael Trigg]] his riskiest overall prospect in the 2026 class, Waldman described a 'Jekyll and Hyde' evaluation -- in good reps Trigg flashes elite catch-point technique and acrobatic, contested-catch ability in traffic, but on the very next rep looks like he does not know correct hand technique at all. At roughly 235-245 lbs he plays more like a running back in open space, with good balance and tackle-breaking ability, than a traditional tight end. Waldman compared him to Orson Charles, a similarly built, fast, good-handed tight end out of Tampa who never found a lasting NFL role, and raised the risk that Trigg becomes a player 'good at a lot of little things but nothing that fits today's NFL.' He weighed grading Trigg conservatively (12th-15th among tight ends, contributor tier) against betting on the flashed technique the way scouts once bet on inconsistent-handed but explosive veteran receivers, and ultimately landed on the generous grade, making Trigg his TE2 as a receiving-value prospect. Because of the tight end personnel-usage debate, Waldman plans to publish two separate grades for Trigg depending on whether his NFL team runs heavy 12/13 personnel (where his blocking, which both hosts agree is currently poor, would matter more) or uses him purely as a receiving weapon.
- 2026-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-11]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls Indiana's Trigg probably the most volatile top-end move tight end prospect in the 2026 class because his hands and catch technique are elite on one rep and subpar the next -- he flashes elite jump-ball, high-point and back-shoulder technique with perfect hand position, then clap-attacks the ball or uses the wrong hand position on the very next target. Waldman frames three legitimate scouting conclusions: grade him high on flashed technical upside, grade him as talented-but-inconsistent and live with the drops, or withhold a high grade until he proves consistency -- and expects NFL scouts, execs and coaches to genuinely split across all three. He's excellent after the catch, running 'more like a big running back.' Waldman says if Trigg locks in consistent catch-point habits regardless of the pre-draft scouting-report consensus, he should become a productive NFL passing-game weapon.
- 2026-04-16 — According to Dalton Wasserman (guest draft analyst, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: [[Michael Trigg]] out of Baylor is 'a tight end in name only' -- essentially a receiver who isn't a good blocker -- and Wasserman's day-four/five comp for him is Oronde Gadsden II, a similarly receiving-only tight end who caught on late last year with an injury-thinned depth chart.
- 2026-04-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-20]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman took Michael Trigg with pick 3.08 in a tight-end-premium superflex startup and called him 'probably the most boom-bust of the early-round tight ends available,' saying Trigg would not have passed Bob Harris's 'Mason-Taylor test' in the RSP gauntlet drill -- capable of making a spectacular catch on an inaccurate throw and then dropping an easy one. Even so, Waldman said there is real upside and he was comfortable with that risk near the fourth round given the format's tight-end premium. Bob Harris (untracked co-host) pushed back slightly, saying Trigg 'might actually make' the gauntlet cut on a good day but agreed he's inconsistent.
- 2026-04-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman would take a late-round dynasty flier on Trigg, who went undrafted and signed with Dallas. He notes an anonymous NFL scout said publicly that everyone in the league knew Trigg could play but that the reason he went undrafted was makeup concerns -- reportedly widely regarded as difficult to work with -- not talent. Waldman says if Trigg can address that perception he has real upside, contrasting him with Dallas incumbent [[Jake Ferguson]], a well-regarded, versatile player who likely isn't getting displaced. Trigg is a stash-only, high-upside dynasty add if nothing better is available late.
- 2026-06-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-29]]) [Dynasty]: Calls Michael Trigg a better athlete than starter [[Jake Ferguson]] and says he's someone people 'should be drafting late' in dynasty leagues; co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) adds he's worth monitoring off the waiver wire in redraft if Ferguson gets hurt or Trigg has a strong camp.
- 2026-08-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-06]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman's long-term camp buy. Trigg has had good camp moments and, notably, publicly thanked the Baylor coaching staff for sticking with him while he matured, after a former scout said everyone knew he was an early-round talent who fell in the draft because he was 'an asshole.' Waldman treats that public self-awareness as a real signal -- people who are that way rarely admit it -- and views it as a genuine maturity marker rather than PR. Dallas doesn't have another player with Trigg's blocker/receiver/all-purpose combo ([[Jake Ferguson]] still plays high-level ball but isn't that archetype), and Waldman calls Trigg a late-dynasty-draft target who could eventually deliver top-20 WR-equivalent value in 1.5-PPR/PPR formats and overtake Ferguson for the lead receiving role at tight end.
