# T.J. Hockenson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: **feeling it** with Nick Mullens at quarterback. The skill basis: Hockenson is "great in zone coverage" and positions himself well against man — the kind of dependable route runner who lets a fill-in quarterback throw with anticipation. Ruling: drop him one tier from his TE1-overall ceiling, but he remains startable.
- 2023-12-21 — Both hosts on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]], in passing: [[Matt Waldman]] and **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) both state they like Hockenson, and Harstad uses him as the reference point for Vernon Davis — "Vernon Davis was like T.J. Hockenson, the next level" — while naming Davis, Rob Gronkowski and George Kittle as the last truly complete all-phase tight ends. An archetype endorsement, not a change to the fantasy outlook.
- 2024-12-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-10]]): Hockenson's return is the underrated reason the Vikings offense is peaking. Even on a modest four-catch, 45-yard line with a couple of critical third downs, he has become Minnesota's man-coverage answer, averaging 6.67 yards per route run against man over the last four weeks — a number Harmon calls berserk. Because he can be moved around the formation, the Vikings now have [[Justin Jefferson]] drawing bracket attention, [[Jordan Addison]] eating against zone and off man, and Hockenson beating man looks, which Harmon says adds a dimension that makes them much harder to defend. He was not on the field for the early-season stretch, so the offense had not shown its full deck until now.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman initially 'felt' Hockenson hard at Underdog TE5 — healthy, and whatever new quarterback Minnesota brings in should lean on him — then reversed himself live on air once he remembered [[Sam Darnold]] might be gone and [[J.J. McCarthy]] is in there: 'Fuck T.J. Hockenson.' He ended on [[Mark Andrews]] instead. Worth recording as a reversal driven entirely by quarterback uncertainty rather than by Hockenson's own play or health.
- 2025-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-18]]): with [[Jordan Addison]] likely suspended to open 2025 and Minnesota expected to play more two-tight-end sets (they brought back Josh Oliver), Harmon flags Hockenson's early-season reception props as a spot to look at the over — he is a short-area compiler and rookie [[J.J. McCarthy]] under pressure will look to dump into the flat quickly. Caveat: Hockenson was not healthy for most of 2024.
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]): Waldman thinks [[J.J. McCarthy]] makes Hockenson a fantasy roller coaster, and the mechanism is the red zone — Hockenson can win there and is a good second or third option, but McCarthy was a poor red-zone decision-maker at Michigan. Early in the year, with [[Justin Jefferson]]'s hamstring and [[Jordan Addison]]'s suspension, Hockenson may be the first or second read; once he slides to third, Waldman expects McCarthy to skip him, dump off, run, or throw it up for grabs. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked) had hoped Hockenson would stay the underneath safety valve and target magnet, but reports he drew zero targets with the first team while McCarthy took shots downfield and checked to running backs before him. Since Hockenson's fantasy value is volume-based rather than YAC and touchdowns like [[George Kittle]] or [[Brock Bowers]], missing volume is a real concern; Kluge puts him in the tight end dead zone, drafted in the middle rounds on name recognition.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: With [[Jordan Addison]] suspended three games and the receiver room otherwise thin behind [[Justin Jefferson]], Harmon expects Hockenson to be pounded with targets and calls him a PPR scam in fantasy — catching a lot of passes to supply the singles and doubles a 2024 Minnesota offense of home runs and triples was missing. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) raises him as an early-season surprise candidate while noting he is getting a little older. The caveat attached to the whole Vikings section is quarterback change: [[J.J. McCarthy]] is unproven and Harmon expects some level-setting after last year's overachievement.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]]: Hockenson is the likeliest beneficiary of Minnesota's shift toward a bootleg-heavy, [[J.J. McCarthy]]-friendly design. Nguyen expects an uptick in Hockenson's targets specifically on the little crossers and flat routes that come off boot concepts — the easy completions the Vikings will lean on with a first-year starter instead of deep play-action shots. The corollary of the same argument is fewer quality downfield targets for the receivers.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-15]]): asked to say goodbye to Hockenson's fantasy value, Waldman said 'totally feeling that.' The reason is entirely the quarterback and the protection — [[J.J. McCarthy]] does not currently have the offensive line to make multiple reads and throw accurately from the pocket, and even with Christian Darrisaw back and a full complement of linemen, Waldman says McCarthy is still not good in the red zone, plays too much hero ball, locks onto one read and will not throw the ball away. In that structure Hockenson is the second or third read on most looks, so Waldman projects one to three targets a game where he is the first read, plus three to four bad targets or missed opportunities where he should have been thrown to and is not.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): the [[J.J. McCarthy]] problem does not change what you do with [[Justin Jefferson]], but 'you cannot play Hockenson. I don't think you should play Hockenson right now.' He notes there was a lot of [[Jalen Nailor]] early in the game and you are not using him either, and that [[Jordan Addison]] returning around Week 4 may help the offense but does not fix the quarterback play.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): asked in his Yacht Club chat whether Josh Oliver's touchdown means Hockenson is 'cooked or useless,' Harris says that's probably an exaggeration — but he also doesn't want to be 'over fooled' by six catches for 38 yards built on 'so much hopeless check-down stuff.' His formulation: Hockenson probably does keep getting ranked inside the top 12 tight ends, 'but it's obviously a disgusting position,' and he 'certainly does not belong in the conversation right now as one of the must-use, so-thrilled-to-have-him guys.' He would not object to dropping Hockenson to chase a red-hot streaming tight end.
- 2026-01-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Ben Gretsch (untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-15]]): Hockenson turns 29 in July with two years and a $15M base left, none of it guaranteed -- a cut saves $15M against a $5M cap hit. Both note he was effectively marginalized within the offense during the 2025 season already and wouldn't be surprised to see him hit the market. Harris says it's fair to note he signed his big extension in 2023 and immediately tore his ACL that same season rather than underperforming the deal on merit. Gretsch still considers him 'a competent tight end' if he becomes available, but with the Vikings carrying heavy cash obligations elsewhere (Justin Jefferson, Jordan Addison) and treating Jefferson/Addison as their primary receiving punch, both think Minnesota could let him go.
- 2026-06-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-08]]): Waldman floated that if Kyler Murray doesn't click with Justin Jefferson the way he ideally should, T.J. Hockenson could see a target bump similar to Trey McBride's role in Arizona, where Murray skipped uncertain first reads and funneled the ball to his tight end instead.
- 2026-08-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Jim McCormick (untracked co-host) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-10]]) [Film Room Zeros]: Both flag T.J. Hockenson as a decline candidate for 2026. McCormick says the tape shows him 'plotting' more, sitting in his areas rather than creating separation, and argues target-earning is a skill that's fading with his athletic decline -- he thinks [[Juwan Jennings]] can replicate the role better right away. Harris notes Hockenson finished 29th among tight ends last year (15 games, 20 yards per game, 27th in per-game scoring) despite being further removed from his severe knee injury, and graded his 2025 speed a 'C' among tight ends. Harris ranks him 24th at the position and says the poor 2025 tape, not just Minnesota's disastrous surrounding context, is the real concern -- calling him no longer separated from the rest of an already weak position, 'that infinite sadness of tight ends.'
