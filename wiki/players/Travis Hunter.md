# Travis Hunter

## Expert Takes

- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10]]): a top-five pick in his opinion and genuinely a first-round talent at both receiver and cornerback, which is rare enough that you should not take either away from him. Angelo expects him to play offense in the NFL — you cannot claim two better playmakers already exist on your offense — with situational defensive snaps, maybe 30 a game. From a movement-signature standpoint he compares Hunter to [[Justin Jefferson]] for length, how he plays the ball above the rim and his timing in the air, while conceding Hunter is far rawer in route acumen. He is on pace for roughly 1,200 yards and double-digit touchdowns. [[Matt Waldman]] raises the process risk: a team may tell him pre-draft it wants him at corner, and a top-five pick told to switch positions may not be happy — the question is whether Hunter is decisive and self-aware enough to answer honestly rather than people-please his way into a camp he does not want.
- 2024-12-12 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-12]]): Hunter is his headline technician of the 2025 class, and the surprise is how much he has developed as a receiver — last year he leaned on athletic traits, now there's a technical timing element, boots-on-the-ground route running plus in-air work playing the defender. Angelo calls the in-air technical proficiency 'really rare': going up with timing and understanding exactly how much room he has, plus good hands. On defense he breaks down routes so well he sometimes knows what's coming before the receiver does. His unique edge is holding the knowledge and inventory for both sides at once. Angelo argues he should keep playing both ways in the NFL and would be worse if you took one side away — routine and snap-count physiology are built around two-way volume — and would give him significant snaps on both sides as a decision-maker.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]): Hunter is 'a pretty f**king awesome cornerback who happens to also play wide receiver at a high level,' and Waldman likens the Heisman to a little Charles Woodson revenge, noting how few two-way players are genuinely great at both spots. As a receiver prospect, if he is not used solely at corner, Waldman expects him to grade as one of the top five receivers in the RSP and says he might even be top three, while allowing that could still change. He is sympathetic to the argument that [[Ashton Jeanty]] deserved the Heisman as a defense-focal main weapon with incredible numbers, and separately 'feels the argument' that San Jose State's Nick Nash should have won the Biletnikoff over Hunter.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]): Hunter is the best wide receiver prospect in the 2025 class and it is not particularly close, on the holistic nature of what he brings, and he is the only player Angelo would call a day-one primary starter — but only if he plays full-time receiver. That is the whole risk: nobody knows how many offensive snaps he takes, because he also has the potential to be an All-Pro defensive back, which some circles value more. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees Hunter belongs in the top tier alongside [[Luther Burden III]] and [[Matthew Golden]] in no particular order, and has the same reservation about snap share being the unknown that governs his fantasy value.
- 2025-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-30]]) [Dynasty]: he has not charted Hunter yet and holds no firm opinion, but from talking to people around the league his read is that Hunter probably does not play wide receiver in the NFL — most evaluators he has spoken to want him at cornerback, moonlighting as a fourth receiver on roughly 12 designed snaps a game so defenses have to account for him. Harmon's framing is a value argument: 'he's probably not Ja'Marr Chase as a wide receiver,' and a shutdown corner is far rarer than a good solid receiver. He notes Daniel Jeremiah wrote the opposite — receiver who moonlights on defense — and calls DJ the best in the business, so 'there's something to that.' Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) agrees with the consensus: elite contested-catch hands and play strength from his defensive background, but he cannot picture a high-level NFL receiver. Harmon will chart him regardless and expects the RP data to be illuminating.
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman feels the statement that Hunter is an early-round draft killer for fantasy GMs — he thinks Hunter is 'too good as a cornerback' and expects defense to be the priority, calling him a great playmaker who will have big weeks as a part-time receiver, with the danger being that you have to gamble before you know the landing spot and the team's intent. He plans to include Hunter as a normal receiver in the RSP while warning readers he is a better cornerback, and notes [[Matt Harmon]] told him the receiver question is probably moot but that Hunter is an incredible receiver. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) says Hunter would be the top-graded receiver in his class model and a top-five prospect all time in the model back to 2018 if he played receiver full time — elite with the ball in his hands — but the two-way usage makes him risky. Despite the risk Waldman would still take him ahead of [[Tetairoa McMillan]] in a rookie draft.
- 2025-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-07]]): Harmon repeats a line he has pushed since the combine — if Tennessee drafts Hunter and says 'we see him as a corner', that will not survive contact with their receiver depth chart: 'when you see him running routes side by side with [[Treylon Burks]] you tell me you're not going to play that guy wide receiver.' The argument is need-driven rather than an evaluation of Hunter's corner play: the Titans' room is thin enough that the offensive usage becomes unavoidable.
- 2025-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-19]]): Harmon charted three Hunter games as a receiver and is deliberately holding the profile back, teasing a comp he thinks listeners will find interesting and calling him an incredible player. He argues the tide is turning toward Hunter being a full-time wide receiver because 'you can't tell — he's a really, really good wide receiver,' and frames the pre-draft debate as three or four inflection points: current media speculation (the corner-first framing is popular because a ten-play offensive package is easier to conceptualize than a defensive one), the drafting team's own view (Tennessee told him at the combine corner-first, Cleveland receiver-first), and then the quarterback's — his hypothetical is New England at four, where [[Drake Maye]] runs five plays with him in OTAs and demands him on the field every snap. On dynasty rookie draft questions Harmon's answer is that no one knows yet, and it depends heavily on where he lands.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): a legitimate playmaker who blends good technique and conceptual knowledge of the position with excellent athletic ability and creativity. He has a variety of release moves that work because he varies pacing and baits opponents, but he needs that detail on all of his releases rather than some, and needs to be more selective about which moves he uses against off coverage outside — poor selections have gotten him pinned to the boundary. Waldman's framing caveat matters for the whole class: Hunter may open his career as a part-time gadget receiver playing most snaps at cornerback, which would make the 2025 class look below-average after year one if judged hastily. Even so, if he does not begin as a full-time receiver, Waldman thinks there is still a chance he ends his career as one. [Dynasty]
- 2025-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-28]]): Hunter is 'the best receiver in this draft class by a lot' in Harmon's opinion, and he says his dream outcome is New England taking him fourth overall and making him a full-time wide receiver to give the room a real number one. He adds an important charting caveat from watching him at Colorado: Hunter runs fake hitches on snaps not going his way because he is never in offensive meetings, part of what Harmon calls the brain-breaking Colorado evaluation environment shared with [[Shedeur Sanders]].
- 2025-04-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: this is one of the best prospect profiles Harmon has ever charted — 'far and away' the best receiver in the class with a tear-and-a-half drop to [[Tetairoa McMillan]] and [[Emeka Egbuka]]. Hunter posted 92nd percentile success rate vs man, 97th vs zone (87.9%, trailing only [[Calvin Ridley]] among Power 5 prospects) and 97th vs press (84.4%, the best Harmon has charted for a prospect facing press on 22%+ of routes), on an all-green route chart where every route but the nine and corner cleared 80%. He did it as a true on-the-line X (outside 94% of sampled snaps, on the line ~90%), splitting 42.6% left / 51.6% right rather than living on one side, and while playing 100+ total snaps in 11 of 13 games and barely attending offensive meetings. Ball skills: 1.2% drop rate, 81.8% contested catch rate on 13% of targets, in space on 67% of catches, down on first contact only 36.6%. Harmon's comp is prime [[Odell Beckham Jr.]] — similar release, stop-on-a-dime curls/comebacks, ball skills and size; he flagged 'performative hitches' on backside plays that exist to save Hunter's body. Caveats: Big 12 competition (Harmon notes BYU was the best defense he faced and one of his best charted games), route details still to tighten like [[Malik Nabers]] and [[Garrett Wilson]], and total uncertainty about whether he is even a full-time NFL receiver. Koh (not tracked) added the Shohei Ohtani framing and questioned whether a sub-180-to-188 pound frame holds up playing both ways.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: Hunter is his highest-graded receiver prospect since 2019 — an A grade on build, play speed, ball skills, in-and-out of routes and releases, with an uncanny knack for finding and playing the ball (he described a tracked ball over the shoulder, head down, re-find, basket catch with a defender draped on him) and a defender's-eye sense of how he will be covered. He called him a possible gold-jacket, once-in-a-lifetime talent, and a [[Justin Jefferson]]-level receiver — but asked what Jefferson would be at 70% of the snaps. Because Cleveland, the Giants or New England will not reveal his corner/receiver split until deep into summer, Angelo would still take [[Ashton Jeanty]] at 1.01 and calls Hunter a big risk ticket there. [[Matt Waldman]] has him as his WR2 in the class with a franchise-caliber, start-immediately grade — top 10-15 of any receiver he has graded in five or six years — docking him only for getting pinned to the boundary more than expected; he relayed Sigmund Bloom's points that receivers are paid roughly $10M/yr more than high-end corners and that Mike Vrabel's background might make him more open to a genuine two-way role. Both said he is far more draftable after the draft, when depth chart and coach-speak are known.
- 2025-04-18 — According to guest Cory Kinnan (not a tracked expert, a Browns fan) on [[Matt Harmon]]'s show ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-18]]): he is '98% sure' Cleveland takes Hunter, pointing to Andrew Berry's long-standing affection — a friend at the Colorado pro day sent him photos of Berry in a deep squat watching Hunter — and to Berry publicly comparing him to Shohei Ohtani, which you do not do and then pass on the player. On fit, Kinnan likes that [[Jerry Jeudy]] is best as a flanker or in the slot while Hunter is best at X or in the slot, giving alignment versatility, with [[Cedric Tillman]] as the sacrificial X and [[David Njoku]] living underneath and ripping seams — three pieces that create a lot of green grass. The stated caveat is the one that always haunts Cleveland: nobody knows who is throwing the football, with [[Kenny Pickett]], [[Joe Flacco]] and a possible [[Jalen Milroe]] pick at 33 in play. Harmon calls himself a Hunter wide receiver enthusiast.
- 2025-04-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-22]]): Hunter is the class's clear WR1 and tops the Reception Perception 2025 board in success rate versus man, versus zone and versus press — but Harmon draws a distinction: Hunter is the best separator in the class, not yet the best route runner, and the truly elite players are both. He compared the criticism to [[Jerry Jeudy]], a 'TikTok route runner' full of frenetic breaks rather than getting to depth within the concept, and noted Cleveland fixed that for Jeudy with runway routes off play action — which Harmon thinks would suit Hunter too. On a projected Cleveland landing spot he called a 950-to-1,000-yard rookie season within the range of outcomes, citing [[Malik Nabers]] being force-fed on a bad 2024 Giants team, while conceding he cannot say how likely it is: the quarterback room is [[Joe Flacco]] and [[Kenny Pickett]], the offensive line is 'a ticking time bomb,' and target competition includes [[Jerry Jeudy]], [[Cedric Tillman]] and [[David Njoku]]. Harmon also flagged the mystery-box element — how much he actually plays receiver and dedicates himself to the craft — and noted usage proof of concept: 4.8% slot snaps in his 2024 charted games but 61.7% slot the prior year.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): Cleveland is the team most likely to take a quarterback it is not ready to start, and the right move instead is to make Hunter — 'the most unique player of this generation' — the centerpiece of the franchise and build around him. Angelo fears they trade the pick away and take [[Jalen Milroe]], ruining his career, and says the Browns need another year removed from the Deshaun Watson debacle before drafting a rookie passer; if they trade out of two 'they have lost their marbles'. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees, noting Andrew Berry seems head over heels for Hunter. Separately Angelo calls Hunter the only receiver in the class with a higher ceiling than [[Luther Burden III]].
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-04-28]]) [Dynasty]: Jacksonville trading up for Hunter was, in Harmon's view, entirely unsurprising — all 32 teams should have been 'head over heels' for him, and Cleveland moving out was a genuine haul, not a smokescreen. Harmon's core distinction: Hunter is an elite separator but not yet an elite route runner, and he was not in offensive meetings at Colorado, so he was 'primarily a cornerback that just went out there and did stuff at wide receiver.' Jacksonville says they view him as an offensive player first, though Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) both expect him to still play defense as a two-way player. In Harmon's charted sample he took more snaps outside than any player he charted and was primarily on the line of scrimmage, after being much more of a slot player in 2023 — so he can play all three receiver spots, just not as a run-fitting power slot like [[Chris Godwin]]. Harmon calls the ceiling unlimited but says he still puts a premium on having seen it done in the NFL, so for year-one production and dynasty rankings he stays with [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] ahead of Hunter.
- 2025-05-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-30]]) [Dynasty]: Hunter is in tier three, which Harmon concedes is unusually aggressive for him — he is normally conservative with rookies and prefers to see what a player is in the NFL before granting tier-two status. He says Hunter and [[Tetairoa McMillan]] are both this high because of situation, and that a strong in-season Reception Perception rookie report could push Hunter into tier two 'and it could even be earlier than that, because I do think he's a great player'. Sharing a field with [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] does not bother him: 'both of those guys can eat without one having to sacrifice for the other'. The caveat is ramp-up time — this is the first time Hunter has focused solely on the receiver position after playing two ways.
- 2025-06-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: asked whether WR29 in round five is too high given two-way uncertainty, Waldman said to ignore the uncertainty and buy the playmaking — he projects close to 900 yards, 60-65 catches and five touchdowns, which supports a WR3 price. His strategic caveat is roster construction: if you take Hunter you should come back for a steadier workload player afterward to mitigate the risk, naming [[Calvin Ridley]] as purely a 'path to workload' guy, with [[Jaylen Waddle]] a bargain on last year's down season and [[Jerry Jeudy]] also interesting. He would take Hunter over Ridley, while conceding [[Zay Flowers]] at WR28 is the more even-work option. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) said he is a [[Chris Godwin]] fan until he dies and would take Godwin instead, and separately noted GM James Gladstone's framing that some weeks Hunter will be more defense and some more offense — which Harris said he needs to know in advance unless he is getting IDP points.
- 2025-07-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-28]]): 'feel it' on Hunter being at risk of a gadget/scheme role on offense in year one. Waldman's distinction: a matchup player wins one-on-one against tight man press on timing routes when everyone knows the ball is coming to him on third-and-15, while a scheme player is an elevated gadget player — his example is Gabriel Davis — who gets open by sneaking behind a defense that has flowed elsewhere. On Colorado tape Hunter won at times but got pinned to the boundary a lot by physical corners, which Waldman finds telling for a player who is himself a press corner: knowing how to do one thing does not mean you are good at the other. Given the two-way snap splits being discussed, he does not expect a 900–1,100 yard receiving season unless something changes in camp, and says he is lowering his own expectation. Bob Harris (untracked) points to GM James Gladstone saying some weeks will be more defense and some more offense, with no advance notice, and refuses to spend WR3 draft capital on that uncertainty.
- 2025-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-01]]): Hunter charted above the 92nd percentile against man, zone and press at Colorado — Harmon's answer to the level-of-competition objection is that he smoked that competition, which is the point. He calls Hunter an elite separator and great ball winner who still needs to work on the craft of route running, and offers a prime [[Odell Beckham Jr.]] comparison. Hunter was also unbelievable on screens and almost never goes down on first contact, which matters because Harmon says Liam Coben's Buccaneers offense was the best screen team in the NFL last year. Harmon wants Hunter taking reps at all three receiver positions alongside [[Brian Thomas Jr.]], which is why [[Dyami Brown]] as a sacrificial X and [[Parker Washington]] in the slot matter. The stated complication is that Jacksonville and Hunter both intend for him to play both ways, so a competent third and fourth receiver is a prerequisite.
- 2025-08-13 — According to Dave Kluge of Footballguys (guest, not a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-13]]: WR31 at pick 64 is too cheap for a Heisman winner taken second overall — in preseason Hunter played 11 of 12 first-team offensive snaps, mostly in the slot, and zero first-team defensive snaps, listed as WR1 and third-string defensive back. Kluge sees Jacksonville (James Gladstone, Liam Coen) rebuilding the 2024 Buccaneers, with Hunter in the [[Chris Godwin]] slot role and [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] as the X like [[Mike Evans]], while conceding Godwin at this stage is the better player. [[Chris Harris]] is lower and pokes holes — Gladstone also drafted [[Tutu Atwell]], and copied schemes do not reproduce roles — but calls the underlying point fantastic: the market suppressed a rookie it never suppresses, out of defensive-snap fear, and a sixth-round pick on that upside is not where drafts get lost.
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: projects Hunter as the number two in the Jacksonville passing game — a [[DeVonta Smith]] or [[Jordan Addison]] type role — at roughly 110 targets, 60-70 catches, 900-950 yards and five to seven touchdowns, and says he is banking on the lower end of every one of those ranges. His concern is organizational: he considers the Jaguars' decision makers young and a little naive for wanting Hunter to play both ways simply because he could in college, and thinks that may only last two to three years before injuries force a choice, since he will not be effective on either side while playing that volume. Waldman is fine with taking him at his sixth-round cost but agrees with [[Chris Harris]] that there are players with better ceilings there. Harris notes the encouraging signal that camp has treated Hunter mostly as a receiver, with defense looking like an occasional extra-DB role, and finds it striking that a Heisman winner and number two overall pick lasts to the sixth round.
- 2025-09-04 — According to guest Jake Trowbridge (not a tracked expert) on the DFS segment ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-04]]): Hunter goes in his DraftKings flex at an even $5,000 — deliberately, because 'his own team is basically putting him in the flex spot'. Trowbridge says he will not get caught holding the bag until Hunter and Jacksonville figure out the two-way split, and sets an explicit condition: if Hunter doesn't pop off in Week 1, the only way he appears in future lineups is as part of the Jacksonville defense. [[Chris Harris]] pressed on whether placing the higher-drafted, more expensive rookie in the flex was a message that Hunter needs to stop concentrating on cornerback and play receiver.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Going Deep Rookie Receivers and RB Depth Charts)]]): Angelo, reading Liam Coen's press conferences, said the message out of Jacksonville is that Hunter is everything they hoped and then some, that he will major in offense and minor on defense, and that his single preseason series showed him in the Chris Godwin role — option routes, timing-based work, screens, the short-to-intermediate weapon in Coen's system — which is why they traded up to No. 2. Waldman called that a huge endorsement of Hunter being closer to his year-one ceiling than expected, and named him alongside [[Emeka Egbuka]] and [[Matthew Golden]] as the rookie receivers he feels safest with for the first six to eight weeks, on the logic that the passing game is the last thing to die when a team goes bad. Caveats: Coen is an inexperienced head coach and the team may be streaky, and Angelo tied volume to whether the run game ([[Travis Etienne Jr.]], [[Tank Bigsby]], [[Bhayshul Tuten]]) works — if it fails, more Trevor Lawrence dropbacks. Angelo expects Etienne or Bigsby traded by the deadline.
