# R.J. Harvey

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-04]]): Harvey — the six-foot invisible rabbit — scored Denver's first touchdown against Houston on a wheel route out of the backfield where the linebacker tried to hold him, could not, and left him by himself. Harris notes [[J.K. Dobbins]] was working well earlier and was stuck on seven carries into the early fourth quarter, and puts the Broncos running back snap count at 28 to 19, with a third back getting 14.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-13]]): asked to choose between Harvey and [[Woody Marks]] for Week 11, Waldman would still take Marks, largely because Denver's usage is unreliable — Sean Payton wants to 'cog-ify' everything, treating every position as an interchangeable part in the machine, so even given the touches [[J.K. Dobbins]] has gotten, Waldman is not confident Harvey gets that volume. [[Chris Harris]] has the two side by side in his ranks and says there is no definite right answer.
- 2025-12-01 — According to Andy Behrens (untracked, Yacht Club/The Deep Shot co-host, [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-01]]): R.J. Harvey scored two short touchdowns against Washington, including a clear goal-line look, which Behrens said makes him 'fine as a fantasy play.' But when Denver wanted to run out the clock late, it was Jaleel McLaughlin on the field instead of Harvey -- 'that is not good,' per Behrens. He also flagged that Harvey gets 'ragdoll tackled' when wrapped up, calling it 'no path to a long career,' and said he doesn't think Harvey has been very good yet as a receiver, though sometimes backs improve there.
- 2025-12-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-23]]): Harvey made essentially one play all game between drops and unimpressive runs, but it was a big one -- he broke a tackle in the third quarter and took it to the house to momentarily tie the game at 17. Harris calls him 'clearly in your lineup' against the Chiefs in week 17.
- 2026-01-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-05]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman does not see Harvey as a true feature back. He liked flashes in Harvey's game and thinks he could improve, but flags real deficiencies in decision-making and tackle-breaking that make him better suited as a complementary piece. He argues Sean Payton is deeply committed to a specific way of using backs and won't hand the offense to Harvey as lead option unless he's genuinely special -- so Waldman expects Denver to restock the cupboard via waivers, free agency or the draft (floating a free agent target as a speculative example) and possibly add a third rotational piece who runs routes well.
- 2026-01-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-15]]): Harvey has been a good player for Denver's run game, but Harmon stopped short of calling him a superstar or a back that scares opposing defenses, part of why he views the Broncos' skill-position group as not particularly explosive heading into the divisional round against Buffalo.
- 2026-01-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and untracked co-host James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-27]]) [Dynasty]: with [[J.K. Dobbins]] out, Harvey has 'clearly shown he's a nice player' and could take a year-two leap, but he has not yet emerged as someone who can be a 'sustaining rusher' on his own -- part of why Denver's offense couldn't overcome a depleted receiver room in the AFC Championship loss. Koh floated that Denver may still look to add a complementary back this offseason, something more reliable in the Dobbins mold rather than a full bell-cow replacement, suggesting the staff currently views Harvey as part of a committee rather than a settled lead back.
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Dave Kluge (Footballguys.com, untracked guest) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-12]]): Denver bringing back [[J.K. Dobbins]] rather than committing to Harvey reads as the Broncos deciding Harvey isn't 'the best thing since sliced bread.' Kluge, watching from Colorado, agrees Dobbins was the better player on tape in 2025 despite Harvey being the flashier year-two story with decent fantasy production. The wrinkle: Denver was clearly funneling goal-line and red-zone work to Harvey late last season even before Dobbins's injury, so both hosts expect a genuine, messy split rather than Dobbins simply reclaiming the job outright.
- 2026-04-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-23]]) [Dynasty]: Harris ranked Harvey #6 in the rookie redraft, well above Andy Behrens's (untracked) #12 -- Behrens flatly said 'he stinks,' arguing the Broncos will never build the run game around a back his size, that J.K. Dobbins (who outrushed Harvey in only 10 games) is back and forms a more effective tandem, and that on 15 touches '12 of those are going to be a little icky.' Harris countered that Harvey's highlight plays are great and cited play-calling evidence of organizational commitment: Sean Payton threw him a goal-line screen 'approximately 730 times' last year, showing Payton 'super wanted' Harvey scoring touchdowns, and that Harvey catches the ball well. Harris still called Harvey's overall running 'a freaking disaster' on a moment-to-moment basis, framing this as a scheme-fit bet rather than a talent endorsement.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris ranks [[R.J. Harvey]] RB32, just behind [[J.K. Dobbins]], noting he wasn't a huge fan of Harvey last year and remains skeptical of the emerging 'Jonah Coleman' third-down hype narrative out of Denver, calling it premature speculation. He expects Harvey and Dobbins to stay closely bunched in rankings until real practice/camp information arrives.
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-22 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Dynasty]: unimpressed with Harvey's late-season work as a runner after J.K. Dobbins went down, framing him now mainly as insurance against a Dobbins re-injury and a return specialist/situational piece rather than a lead-back threat, in a backfield also featuring [[Jonah Coleman]].
- 2026-07-24 — According to Chris Harris and Andy Behrens (untracked co-host) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: both hosts agree the fantasy market is overestimating Harvey relative to [[J.K. Dobbins]]. Harris says Harvey 'has a known role' as a rotational piece who won't be a full-workload back, and notes even bullish dynasty-minded 'futurist' analysts are reportedly souring and shifting attention to [[Jonah Coleman]] instead. Both hosts recall watching Harvey in November and December games and thinking he looked 'really kind of terrible at football' even while scoring touchdowns -- Behrens argues the touchdowns were more a function of Sean Payton scheming him into red-zone work (even when Dobbins was healthy) than of Harvey's own play, calling it 'hook or crook' production, possibly Payton chasing his own fantasy outcomes. Harris says nothing short of an injury to Dobbins is likely to change their ranks, and calls chasing Broncos backfield camp storylines 'a Rorschach test' not worth spending brain cells on. Both note Harvey is going notably earlier than Dobbins in current best ball drafts despite the value clearly sitting with Dobbins.
- 2026-08-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-06]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman flags Harvey at RB31 in ADP but says he wants no part of Denver's backfield committee if [[J.K. Dobbins]] gets hurt, calling a Dobbins-Harvey-plus-others timeshare a situation that 'makes no sense' to draft into given how split the touches would be.
- 2026-08-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harvey finished as the rest-of-season RB17 after [[J.K. Dobbins]]' Week 10 injury, 'on the shoulders of some touchdowns' but 'looked pretty bad as a runner' per Harris, who noted Harvey got a 'distracting amount of red zone receiving work' while starting even though Dobbins' underlying workload had been more repeatable and valuable. Harris said if everyone is healthy he prefers Dobbins, but acknowledged Harvey is only a second-year player who can still improve, and expects Denver's backfield -- also including rookie [[Jonah Coleman]] -- to be a three-way committee 'because that's the NFL now.'
