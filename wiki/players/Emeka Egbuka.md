# Emeka Egbuka

## Expert Takes

- 2025-02-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-04]]): Egbuka is a player he has his eye on and intends to chart early in the pre-draft process, so he is explicit that he has not formed his own evaluation yet. What he relays is the industry pitch — a dirty-work, power-slot player — and notes that at Ohio State it was Egbuka doing the grimy underneath work while Chip Kelly's design freed [[Jeremiah Smith]] on the boundary. Harmon floats him as a possible fit for Houston if the Texans lean into more 11 personnel, while cautioning that what Houston actually needs most is a true outside receiver.
- 2025-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-19]]): from his early prospect charting, Harmon calls the Ohio State receiver a perfect fit for the gritty run-game-willing slot role several teams need in the back half of round one, naming Minnesota, Pittsburgh, the Chargers and Houston as candidates. His read is that Egbuka may be slot-only or at least slot-mostly, but that he gives a lot of effort in the run game. Harmon's caveat on Minnesota specifically is that spending roughly the 24th overall pick on a receiver is a luxury when you already have [[Justin Jefferson]] and [[Jordan Addison]] and need defensive help.
- 2025-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-07]]): Egbuka is 'a perfect fit for this Texans team' and the sort of prospect Houston should draft and develop rather than chasing a veteran — a dirty-work, lay-up-target receiver who can play slot or flanker, the missing piece next to three perimeter man-coverage winners. Harmon notes Daniel Jeremiah (not a tracked expert) reported Egbuka is expected to run a 4.3 at his pro day, and is openly skeptical: 'I don't see 4.3 on the tape for Emeka Egbuka' — while conceding he did not see 4.29 on [[Matthew Golden]]'s tape either. Harmon's broader point is that Houston's 2024 problem was three players who all commanded a lion's share of targets with one football, similar to the Bears, and that Egbuka's archetype solves it rather than compounding it.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): Egbuka has starter skills but a game limited to specific roles — best as a flanker or future slot working linear routes across the field at varying depths, where his open-space running after the catch produces. Waldman explicitly records disagreement with colleague Chad Reuter (not a tracked expert), who believes Egbuka can be more than what showed the past two years. Waldman's worry: asked to run timing routes against tight man, win at the boundary and earn contested catches, there is real disappointment risk. He opts for the wrong attack on targets, lets the ball into his frame unnecessarily, is unprepared for catchable-but-imperfect throws, lacks sharp detail at the break point and drifts out of breaks, giving good corners the angle. On the [[Puka Nacua]] comparison: Egbuka is 'in the same city, but not the same neighborhood.' He also suspects some logo scouting attaches to Ohio State prospects. See [[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]].
- 2025-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-26]]) [Dynasty]: Egbuka (6'1″, 202) is a 'slam dunk number two receiver' worth a first-round pick in a league starved for WR2s, and Harmon rejects the low-upside label attached to the archetype. Charted: 73.6% of snaps in the slot (fewer than [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] took), 5% in the backfield, 81% behind the line; 68% vs man, 83.6% vs zone — which would have led the entire 2024 class including [[Malik Nabers]], [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] and [[Rome Odunze]]; 92nd-percentile press success on only 7.6% of routes; 14% contested target rate with 75% conversion and one drop in the sample. 87.5% on slants at 19% slant usage, but 47.4% on nines and at-or-below average on posts, digs and curls. Harmon calls him a power slot, slot-leaning not slot-only, with an [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] ceiling comp that requires the right offense; Koh (not tracked) is more bullish, projecting outside snaps and offering a Steve Smith Sr. comp Harmon partly resists.
- 2025-04-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: grouped with [[Tetairoa McMillan]] as the next tier after [[Travis Hunter]] but a tear-and-a-half down, and not a true day-one WR1 archetype. Harmon explicitly contrasted him with [[Luther Burden III]] — Egbuka is 'much cleaner' as an evaluation with the ceiling and floor closer together, a lower ceiling but a much higher floor, whereas Burden's outcomes are far wider.
- 2025-04-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-08]]) [Dynasty]: both hosts are big fans and push back hard on the common objection that Egbuka is 'just a number two receiver'. Harmon's counter is a salary argument — look at what number twos, threes and fours are paid on the open market ($10M for [[Tutu Atwell]], [[Dyami Brown]], [[Josh Palmer]]) against first-round rookie deals: [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] at $8.8M, [[Rome Odunze]] at $5.6M, [[Zay Flowers]], [[Jordan Addison]] and [[Xavier Worthy]] all under about $3.5-4M, i.e. Greg Dortch and Noah Brown money. If a good receiver fills out your room cheaply on a rookie contract, the 'only a two' label is not a reason to pass. Harmon expects Egbuka to go in roughly the back half of round one, the Xavier Worthy range, and says that is where he should go.
- 2025-04-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-22]]): Egbuka wins the best slot-only superlative by default — Harmon says he will be slot-mostly rather than slot-only, as will [[Jaylin Noel]], because the class has no true slot-only prospect besides [[Xavier Restrepo]]. On the substance, Egbuka is Harmon's WR3 in the class if [[Travis Hunter]] is counted as a receiver, and his success rate versus zone coverage would have led all prospects in last year's class as well as scoring well in this one. He is not an explosive downfield player, but he gets open, and Harmon thinks continued growth as an after-catch player would make him one of the better slots in the league. James Koh (not tracked) agreed he is a slot early but expects him to work himself into an outside role, calling him 'a dog.'
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-04-28]]) [Dynasty]: Egbuka at 19 was Harmon's WR3 in the class behind [[Travis Hunter]] and [[Tetairoa McMillan]], with a clear drop to everyone after him, so he calls 19 the perfect spot for the player. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) could not make the pick work given Tampa just gave [[Chris Godwin]] three years and $66M with $44M guaranteed — either you trust the injury recovery or you don't. Harmon grants it is 'a little naughty' from an alignment standpoint since Egbuka's best role is Godwin's, but makes it work three ways: best player available on their board; [[Baker Mayfield]] needs a deep pool of quality options and the room is aging ([[Mike Evans]] 32, Godwin turning 30); and the route data fits Tampa's quarterback. Egbuka took 73.6% of his sample snaps from the slot and 81% off the line of scrimmage, and his best routes break outside — 78.6% on outs, 82.6% on flats — while Mayfield loves quick and deep out routes and anticipates them better than throws over the middle. Harmon calls him slot-mostly, not slot-only. Fantasy caveat: year one 'stinks,' but he likes him in dynasty and Koh floats a 12th-round redraft dart.
- 2025-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-20]]) [Dynasty]: guest JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) filed Egbuka under landing spots that hurt his feelings while still drafting him ahead of the market. His worry is archetype: Tampa Bay does not seem to care about receiver archetype at the draft — they played the slot-profiled [[Jalen McMillan]] on the perimeter as a rookie — and with [[Chris Godwin]] returning to the slot, Egbuka likely plays outside. He expects general production struggles in 2025 with [[Mike Evans]] and Godwin present, but sees a possible through-the-roof dynasty value next year if Evans moves on and Godwin's injury lingers; he comped Egbuka to [[Robert Woods]] as an inside-out player, and named him as his acceptable worst case when trading [[Rome Odunze]] for the 1.08. Waldman's film comp is 'an aspiring [[Puka Nacua]]' — a flanker or slot who does real damage after the catch as a physical runner — and he thinks Egbuka is a better flanker than McMillan, making him a lower-volume Nacua for [[Baker Mayfield]] right away.
- 2025-05-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-30]]) [Dynasty]: Egbuka is in tier four with [[Chris Olave]], [[Zay Flowers]] and [[Josh Downs]], which Koh (not tracked) calls high given [[Chris Godwin]]'s extension and a rookie role that is not set up to produce. Harmon agrees the rookie year is likely a soft one — Koh floats a [[Rome Odunze]]-style 700-800 yards, Harmon calls that aggressive and would set the range at 550-600 — but likes the multi-year picture: a good offensive ecosystem that survived the [[Liam Cohen]] departure by promoting passing game coordinator Josh Grizzard from within, and [[Baker Mayfield]], whom Harmon says played at a top-ten quarterback level last year. Egbuka's out-breaking routes and toughness at the catch point map onto Mayfield's game. With [[Mike Evans]] in a contract year and Godwin aging and injured, Harmon says it would be 'zero percent surprising' if Egbuka were the most important receiver on the Bucs by 2026, and that he blows past [[Jalen McMillan]], whom he calls a number three at best.
- 2025-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-30]]): Egbuka was a clear-cut second-tier prospect for him, not a fringe first-rounder — an 82nd-percentile success rate versus zone, 68% versus man (only 42nd percentile) and a 92nd-percentile mark versus limited press looks, with the best hands in the class and a 75% contested catch rate. Harmon says he could be the most productive rookie receiver in the league this year given the offense and quarterback, and notes the last paragraph of his charting profile compares his data to [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]'s early NFL numbers; the shared trait is being grounded through the catch, which generates YAC despite Egbuka going down on first contact on 70% of in-space attempts. His out-breaker chart is all green, which suits [[Baker Mayfield]]. The caveat Harmon states plainly: if [[Mike Evans]] stays healthy and [[Chris Godwin]] returns fully, Egbuka could be blocked exactly like [[Rome Odunze]] or [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] were. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) initially disliked the landing spot for Egbuka's runway but now sees it as great team-building, and independently reached the St. Brown comp.
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: a measured skeptic — a good prospect but not a great one, and he expects Egbuka to finish third in Tampa receiving behind [[Mike Evans]] and [[Chris Godwin]], with a 900-1,100 yard season requiring Godwin not coming back or Evans getting hurt. Waldman thinks Egbuka can play flanker in something like the [[Puka Nacua]] role for the Rams, though not at that production level. On the camp buzz, he points out [[Jalen McMillan]] generated the same praise last year for being smart and prepared; he does rate Egbuka above McMillan and calls him more rugged. The core argument is that Godwin — whom Waldman calls one of the most underrated receivers of the past 12-15 years, able to play every position and having seen far more defensive disguises — is simply the better slot player with a more sophisticated game, however fast a learner Egbuka is. Waldman is fine with the eighth-round price and would like him a little cheaper. [[Chris Harris]] agrees, calling him a bench player to start in a crowded room, and notes Godwin was second to [[Ja'Marr Chase]] in fantasy points per game before his injury.
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-20]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon is 'full on buying' Egbuka and is bumping him up both dynasty and redraft rankings as expectations climb — he hammers the over on a FanDuel 825.5 receiving-yard line even after conceding the closing line value is gone. The case: he was in the same prospect tier as [[Tetairoa McMillan]], who went eighth overall; an 82nd-percentile success rate versus zone at 83.6%, second in the class behind only [[Travis Hunter]] and a mark that would have led the entire 2024 charted class; 87.5% success on slants, making him the underneath layup target. With [[Jalen McMillan]] on IR and [[Chris Godwin]] not yet jogging, Egbuka opens as the number two opposite [[Mike Evans]] and should benefit from the veteran deference defenses pay Evans. Todd Bowles called him a number one receiver on any other team who can play all three spots; Harmon and James Koh agree he can handle X but say full-time X would waste his versatility.
- 2025-08-28 — On the same mid-round breakout question, DENNY CARTER of Rotoworld (guest, NOT a tracked expert) said on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]] [Redraft (Standard)] that he would go Egbuka over [[Ricky Pearsall]], because the avenue looks wide open for him in Tampa Bay. According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]]): he leans the other way and has Pearsall a little higher, but he does not think people are down on Egbuka — he calls Carter a rarity for preferring him and says it is good to hear another voice, since the industry consensus he sees skews to Pearsall. Both hosts agree both rookies have a real path to production.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: bold prediction that Egbuka catches 100 passes and leads the Buccaneers in receptions as a rookie. Harmon framed it as his most 'boring' prediction — chalky now versus where the market was in April — and stressed it is not a bet against [[Mike Evans]], who can still have a nice year. The case rests on how concentrated the Tampa passing game is, much like early last season when [[Chris Godwin]] was the second most productive receiver in the league behind [[Ja'Marr Chase]], plus Egbuka playing what Harmon called a 'layup' role in a well-oiled offense. James Koh (not a tracked expert) called it genuinely bold, noting Evans himself saw only 110 targets and 74 receptions in 2024.
- 2025-09-04 — According to guest Jake Trowbridge (not a tracked expert) on the DFS segment ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-04]]): Egbuka is his Week 1 receiver at $4,600 against Atlanta, on the reasoning that with [[Chris Godwin]] out and [[Jalen McMillan]] out, [[Mike Evans]] and Egbuka should eat — and that this is probably as cheap as Egbuka will ever be. He framed the pick partly as pre-empting Week 2 pricing: the question isn't only who pops off, but who becomes so overpriced afterward that you have to get him now. [[Chris Harris]] independently landed on Egbuka too, one of three overlaps between their lineups.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): 'absolutely he is for real' — Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) called this the most unnecessary question of the day given how much pre-season evidence there already was on a polished player. Waldman sees him in a [[Puka Nacua]] / [[Robert Woods]]-style role in this offense. His caveat is a scheduling one drawn from the [[Chase Claypool]] precedent (a usage comparison, explicitly not an ability comparison): over the next three to four weeks defenses will game-plan to clamp him while also handling [[Mike Evans]], so by roughly Weeks 8-9 expect the clamps and for other Tampa receivers to benefit instead. Still quality, just no longer a surprise.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): two touchdowns and Harris calls it big league. The first came on the route combination Tampa has always run with [[Chris Godwin]] and [[Mike Evans]], the dagger, with Evans running the clear-out and Egbuka catching the underneath ball and taking it 30 yards. The winning score was a deep post that Harris says was a really good throw by [[Baker Mayfield]] and a baller route by Egbuka. Context: Tampa managed only 260 total yards and no individual topped 67, so this was efficiency rather than volume; Evans went five for 51 but was plenty involved and Harris says he should be fine.
- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-11]]): Egbuka may be the biggest riser of the rookie class — Harmon had him in the same prospect tier as [[Tetairoa McMillan]] (his second tier, below the Malik Nabers/Drake London/Marvin Harrison Jr./Ja'Marr Chase group) despite McMillan going eighth and Egbuka being seen as a late first, and now thinks he might exceed the 'best slot receiver in the league' ceiling he originally gave him — potentially the engine of the Tampa Bay passing offense once Mike Evans, 32 and in his contract year, moves on. Week 1: three catches, 58 yards, both touchdowns lined up outside, roughly 52% out wide and 48% slot per Next Gen Stats, with 87% of receiving yards coming from outside alignments. He notes a 92nd percentile success rate versus press as a prospect on a small sample, and draws the [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] parallel of a collegiate slot who was never slot-only. He credits Josh Grizzard for not boxing him into the slot.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-22]]): with [[Mike Evans]] dealing with a hamstring, Tampa Bay looks set to use Egbuka as its number one receiver. Waldman's evaluation: Egbuka tracks the ball really well and is getting open downfield a lot, though many of those wins come against zone — but he also shows skill against tight coverage at the catch point, which Waldman called a nice sign. He expects [[Chris Godwin]]'s return to complement rather than displace him, with Godwin taking the flanker or slot role as needed.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-23]]): Egbuka is the direct beneficiary of Tampa Bay's receiver attrition and Harmon reiterated that being in on him was never only about injuries — he thinks Egbuka is 'a really good player' in a good ecosystem, and he made 'some real big plays down the football field' in Week 3. With [[Mike Evans]] out on a hamstring, [[Chris Godwin]] not yet back and [[Jalen McMillan]] on IR, what looked in April like one of the deepest receiver rooms in the league is now Egbuka, [[Sterling Shepard]] and [[Tez Johnson]], meaning Tampa has to put more and more on Egbuka. Harmon also credited [[Baker Mayfield]] for continuing to produce through the injuries, saying he was a longtime Baker hater who has been won over.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): 'I am officially a very big Emeka Egbuka fan — I will be delighted to use him every week even when the other guys are in there, because he has been nothing but a baller.' Egbuka was questionable going in, like [[Tucker Kraft]] was, and still produced: an insane one-handed grab over Sauce Gardner early in the second quarter, and then the game-winning deep out late after the Jets' blocked-field-goal touchdown, which Harris credited as both a special [[Baker Mayfield]] throw and a great catch. Guest SCOTT FISH (Fantasy Cares, NOT tracked) used the same point against [[Chris Godwin]]: 'Egbuka's got a clearly defined role now,' making Godwin's eventual role and alignment ambiguous.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): Harris is candid that in Tampa's comeback Egbuka partly lucked out — he and [[Bucky Irving]] both broke crazy runaround plays for 70-plus-yard touchdowns where things simply happened — and that [[Baker Mayfield]]'s numbers looked great without him playing very well. Guest Jamie Eisenberg (CBS, not a tracked expert) is far more bullish: he thinks Egbuka has a chance to be great, cites the play against Sauce Gardner two weeks earlier plus this performance, and says there is no way you could take this kid off the field. Both hosts said they would not trade Egbuka for [[A.J. Brown]] even while agreeing Brown is the better fantasy asset on paper — Eisenberg's reason being room to grow four games into a rookie season, weighed against target competition returning with [[Mike Evans]] and [[Chris Godwin]].
- 2025-10-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-03]]): Egbuka is guest Josh Van Blank's number one trade-away (Van Blank is NOT a tracked expert), and he credits the Tampa beat writers for being right in the preseason about how much the Buccaneers adored him. The sell logic is target competition, not talent: [[Chris Godwin]] played 'a lot more than I expected' in his first game back rather than being ramped up slowly, and [[Mike Evans]] is still to come — 'if the other two guys are super accomplished very good players, it matters.' Van Blank also flags that this week's 77-yard touchdown came off a ball deflected between two Eagles defenders. Both hosts have him in essentially the same spot, around 28-29 overall (FantasyPros 29). Targets: [[Breece Hall]], who both agree is on a different level and worth doing, or [[Quinshon Judkins]], which Van Blank calls fair and daring because it's the Browns. Van Blank expects Egbuka to go late round two or early round three next year.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Egbuka hit a bomb shot and added a red-zone touchdown in the Buccaneers-Seahawks shootout. Guest **Sam Gold** (NOT a tracked expert) is unambiguous: Egbuka 'is the only one in this offense that I know for a fact I'm going to start no matter what,' with [[Chris Godwin]] a toss-up. Harris notes the offense looks capable of supporting three receivers on occasion, but that [[Mike Evans]]'s return will potentially affect Egbuka too.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-07]]): through five games Egbuka is fourth in the NFL in receiving yards and is 'so far and away the most important player' in Tampa Bay's passing offense even with [[Mike Evans]] out, and would be Harmon's current Offensive Rookie of the Year favorite. Across three charted games (Jets, Falcons, Seahawks) he has been in the slot on only 31.4% of snaps and on the line of scrimmage 60.1% of the time — 37 on-line to 15 off-line snaps in Week 5 — a big reversal from a prospect profile Harmon expected to be a short/intermediate dirty-work slot. He has run posts on 13% of routes at an 83.3% success rate (versus sub-9% usage and sub-69% success as a prospect) and digs on 20.7% at an 89.5% success rate, with just under 82% success versus zone and roughly 73% versus man. Harmon graded him a clear first-round, tier-two prospect alongside [[Tetairoa McMillan]] and says he now looks better; he dismisses fantasy-Twitter separation-score concerns outright. Caveat: how the role changes when Evans returns.
- 2025-10-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-10]]): Harris had asked on Wednesday's paywall show whether this was the moment to sell Egbuka, on the theory that [[Chris Godwin]] and [[Mike Evans]] getting healthy would turn Tampa into a three-man show; Andy Behrens (Yahoo, NOT a tracked expert) said he would simply hold, and Harris now thinks Behrens will be proven correct. The change is Godwin's fibula injury, which has essentially ruled him out for Week 6 — the same broken bone that cost him the second half of last season, which Harris finds alarming and reads as a potentially long-term thing. That, he says, would really solidify Egbuka's role, so anyone who held off on trading him should feel good about it.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): Egbuka opened with a deep dig for a big gain on the second play, then paid it off against a blown coverage in the flat, and hit another deep dig mid-second quarter — 'the [[Jared Goff]] throw' — to reach 3 for 76 and a touchdown early. He was squaring off with Christian Gonzalez all day and Harris calls the matchup terrific, with both winning at times; Egbuka dropped what could have been another touchdown late in the third, which led to [[Tez Johnson]] scoring instead.
