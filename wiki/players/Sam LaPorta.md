# Sam LaPorta

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]): a self-graded miss, and an unusually specific one. Waldman "wasn't extremely high on him because I thought he needed the perfect fit — and he found the perfect fit." He raises it because a former scout and biomechanics/analytics consultant who has worked with roughly 30 teams (a longtime RSP subscriber, unnamed on air) wrote to tell him he had "really nailed the LaPorta evaluation" — meaning the *floor* scenario rather than the ranking.
- 2023-12-21 — **Waldman**, same episode, what that floor scenario actually said: LaPorta "winds up on the bench getting fattened up and put on special teams because the coach and the GM are in a pissing match over whether he should have been drafted" — the coach wanting an inline tight end who can block, the GM having drafted a weapon, the coach telling him to gain 20 pounds and block, and then telling the GM the pick was wrong. The consultant's reply, per Waldman: "that's literally the RIP for so many NFL players." Recorded as an evaluation-method point — a tight end's floor is frequently organizational rather than physical. See [[Player Development and Coachability]].
- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]], same episode, on the fit that materialized: names LaPorta's Detroit as the league's best current example of an organization that recruits for cultural fit and gets players to accept coaching, alongside [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[David Montgomery]], Jared Goff and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]].
- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): notes plainly that LaPorta's rookie-year results have outproduced [[Dalton Kincaid]]'s, and calls LaPorta "underrated" in his own pre/post-draft tiers (11th overall, in his top tight-end tier alongside Kincaid at 5th) — but says he remains a "Kincaid over LaPorta guy" long-term regardless, on the belief that a tight end's rookie season is usually not his final form. Not a downgrade of LaPorta, who Waldman still rates a top-5 dynasty tight end; recorded as a case where Waldman's stated ranking preference and the season's actual production diverge.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): the strongest single-line praise yet — "one of the best young tight ends, if not the best young tight end in the game" — cited among the reasons Detroit's core is set up for continued success beyond its 2023 coaching staff.
- 2024-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-30]]: LaPorta's rookie season (235.3 fantasy points, 86 catches, 889 yards, 10 TDs in 17 games) "threatened" Mike Ditka's 1961 all-time rookie-TE record (235.6 points in 14 games) — off by just 0.3 points, the closest anyone has come. Waldman notes he's "perversely pleased" Ditka's mark still stands, but suspects it will fall soon given how close both LaPorta and [[Kyle Pitts]] have come. Waldman: "Laporta's fit made sense to me from the moment the Lions took him," the basis of his post-draft prediction that LaPorta could be this class's most productive tight end.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): LaPorta is ascending into a top-three NFL tight end. What impressed Angelo most as a rookie was not the raw production but how steady he was — tight end is a hard position to play early in the NFL, and he did it about as well as anyone historically has as a rookie. Angelo expects another step up in year two, and uses that ascension as part of his argument against [[Jameson Williams]] commanding enough target share in Detroit. [[Matt Waldman]] separately named LaPorta among the tight ends he treats as system-safe, alongside [[George Kittle]], [[Travis Kelce]] and Mark Andrews, because Detroit's condensed, multi-tight-end looks under Ben Johnson generate favorable matchups (see [[Tight End Value in Condensed Formations]]).
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): LaPorta's first-read target share has collapsed from 19.9 percent in 2023 to 8.6 percent through two weeks of 2024, a drop of more than 50 percent, with [[Jameson Williams]] absorbing the volume at a team-high 34.5 percent. Harmon calls that a big, big drop-off and wonders whether minimizing LaPorta has actually been bad for the Lions offense, which he thinks has been topsy-turvy and inconsistent through two weeks. He expects Ben Johnson and [[Jared Goff]] to go back to the film, see all the layups LaPorta provides — the seven-to-eight yard catch plus ten after — and return to their bread and butter rather than staying JMo-obsessed.
- 2024-10-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-24]]) [Dynasty]: used as the direct film comparison for [[Brock Bowers]]. Waldman rates LaPorta the better one-on-one route runner of the two, but says Bowers has better blocking technique in certain regards, more strength, and is more dynamic as a receiver in separation, at the catch point going up for the ball, and after the catch — enough that the route-running edge does not close the gap. Co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, not tracked) puts LaPorta's rookie season, along with [[Kyle Pitts]], below Bowers over the first half of their respective rookie years.
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): LaPorta is his worked example that a tight end rarely holds the No. 1 read — once Detroit got [[Jameson Williams]] going this season, LaPorta 'for the most part falls behind' him, because teams want to hit big plays to their wide outs rather than nickel-and-dime to tight ends. Harmon's rule is that the talent gap between tight end and receivers has to be enormous for a tight end to ascend a whole playbook and become the first read, which is why he reserves it for a [[Travis Kelce]] or [[George Kittle]] type — and even Kittle is not always first in the progression.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18]]): Waldman uses LaPorta as the calibration point for [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] and, in doing so, revises the popular story about him. LaPorta was RSP's 11th-ranked player on the 2023 post-draft cheat sheet as a dark horse to break Mike Ditka's rookie record, and Waldman notes his 4.59 forty sits a hundredth of a second inside the elite tier with a 21.1 mph career max GPS speed. But he argues the success of LaPorta's game does not hinge on speed: most of his big vertical rookie wins came on long-developing play-action routes against zone and broken zone, not on pulling away from coverage. He warns that the memory of those games will stay 'emblazoned in the minds of fantasy GMs for years' and distort how comparable prospects are judged.
- 2025-05-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-26]]): Waldman is 'totally feeling it' that the second-half [[Sam LaPorta]] is the real one — Bob Harris framed 2024 as a tale of two seasons, with a slow start and 13-plus fantasy points per game down the stretch. Waldman's reasoning is fit: LaPorta is a high-end route runner and after-the-catch option, reliable, and Detroit likes to work the middle of the field, which is exactly where they use him; when they do go to the sideline it is the short-to-shallow-intermediate range where LaPorta's out route does its best work. He expects the second half to be closer to the 2025 baseline than the first. He also turns the slow start into a general lesson: LaPorta had a hamstring issue in camp, and camp injuries often linger longer than expected, so keep that in mind for lineup decisions even when nobody would bench a player of that profile outright.
- 2025-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-11]]): Harmon wonders whether LaPorta is deployed more heavily in 2025, saying it felt like Detroit went away from him specifically to get [[Jameson Williams]] more involved under Ben Johnson. With John Morton taking over the play calling and no guarantee that the same emphasis on Williams carries over, Harmon's read is that Williams takes a statistical step back while LaPorta takes a step forward — though he caveats that the overall health of the Detroit offense, including an offensive line that lost Frank Ragnow, is the bigger unknown.
- 2025-11-06 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT tracked) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06 (Players We Think We Know)]]) [Dynasty]: LaPorta is the cautionary tale making managers gun-shy about [[Brock Bowers]] — the consensus number one dynasty tight end after his rookie year, he hasn't matched that production and is now more like the number four or five dynasty tight end. Harstad stresses he isn't playing badly, but that LaPorta and Bowers were simply different as rookies: LaPorta's year was surprising and good, Bowers looked like a superstar. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed he is a good player and typed him as a [[Mark Andrews]]-style tight end — does certain things really well, and in the right system with a real target priority he can be a high-volume catch option, but that is a different species from Kittle and Bowers.
- 2025-11-11 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) with agreement from [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-11]]): LaPorta is catching everything, sitting at an 82% catch rate and 12.2 yards per reception, which Koh calls really impressive production for a tight end. He was Detroit's third-most-targeted pass catcher in the Week 10 win over Washington with 5 targets, 5 catches and 53 yards, behind [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] (8) and [[Jameson Williams]] (7) — usage the hosts describe as sensible given Detroit's talent hierarchy. Harmon adds that 2025 has been LaPorta's best work as a blocker in the NFL so far, part of a broader read that this Lions roster is a group of players all hitting their prime years at once.
