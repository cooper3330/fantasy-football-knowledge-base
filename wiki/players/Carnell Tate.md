- 2025-11-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-24]]) [Dynasty]: after watching seven Ohio State games in depth, Waldman called Tate one of the best wide receivers he's scouted in the past five to six years, comparing his route running, ball-winning ability and blocking (against linebackers and safeties, and in the open field) to [[Justin Jefferson]]. He said Tate would fix what ails the Bills as the alpha receiver they lack -- a coverage-beater who forces defenses out of man coverage and lets every other Buffalo weapon benefit. He measures roughly 6'3', 195 lbs, and Waldman rates him ahead of [[Jordyn Tyson]] and [[Makai Lemon]], comparable to [[Luther Burden III]], just below [[Malik Nabers]], and ahead of [[Rome Odunze]]'s class tier. In a live dynasty rookie draft that day, Waldman said he'd take Tate first overall, ahead of hyped running backs like Jeremiah Love.
- 2025-12-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-22]]): in an early look at the 2026 receiver class, Waldman ranks Tate among the best receivers he's scouted in the past five years, placing him in the middle-to-bottom of a very brief high-end tier at the top of the class.
- 2026-02-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-09]]) [Dynasty]: pushing back on a social-media narrative that Tate is an overrated X receiver because of his low slot-rep percentage compared to [[Makai Lemon]], Waldman uses [[Drake London]] as a counter-example -- London's best college season (2021: 88 catches, 1,084 yards) came while he played the slot only about 9% of the time, yet he's since thrived as an NFL slot player. Waldman's takeaway: slot-rep percentage is a poor predictor because it conflates volume with role, and what matters is whether a receiver's route-running technique translates across alignments -- which he believes Tate's does, even though Ohio State simply didn't deploy him there. He calls Tate vs. Lemon 'splitting hairs' -- both projected to be phenomenal pros -- while giving Tate a slight edge.
- 2026-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: discussing a fantasy-community model that flags low college slot-snap percentage as a red flag for translating to today's slot-heavy NFL passing game, the hosts noted Tate charted around 14% of his snaps in the slot at Ohio State -- among the lowest of any hyped 2026 receiver prospect. Waldman said he wouldn't 'bash' Tate for that: he views Tate as a [[Justin Jefferson]] type who is more fluid than his outside-only reputation suggests, calls him fundamentally sound and clean as a route runner and ball-tracker, and believes that skill translates to the slot if a team needs it -- Ohio State simply had no reason to move him inside with fellow alpha [[Jeremiah Smith]] also on the roster. Angelo agreed the slot-percentage model is 'honestly kind of overblown' now that NFL offenses increasingly build the passing game around one true alpha receiver's ability to win from any spot rather than forcing him into a designed 12/13-personnel slot role.
- 2026-02-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-17]]): in an early 2026 draft class preview, Harmon names Carnell Tate as one of the two receivers he's most locked in on so far (alongside Makai Lemon), saying 'I know what those guys are' and that both would produce dropped into any NFL offense today. He grades this year's five-receiver top tier (Tate, Makai Lemon, Jordyn Tyson, Denzel Boston, KC Concepcion) as better in sum than the 2023 class (JSN, Jordan Addison, Zay Flowers, Quentin Johnston) but not quite up to the 2024 class (Malik Nabers, Marvin Harrison Jr., Rome Odunze, Brian Thomas Jr.) or the Drake London/Chris Olave/Garrett Wilson class.
- 2026-02-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-24]]): in his still-preliminary 2026 WR prospect rankings, Harmon has Tate in his top tier alongside Makai Lemon, projecting both as top-10 caliber picks with a clear drop-off to the rest of the class.
- 2026-03-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-02]]) [Dynasty]: not concerned about Tate's combine 4.53 40 (scouts hand-timed him anywhere from 4.45 to 4.54) -- called it 'feel it' overall because 'the most important thing that matters is skills': excellent route running, plays fast, excels at the catch point. Cited Marvin Jones and Larry Fitzgerald as examples of pass catchers who led the league in yards per catch despite running similarly modest times. Directly compared him to Michael Pittman Jr., who ran a similar 40 time but, per Waldman, 'is not the route runner that Tate is or even the same level of pass catcher.' Waldman's overall view: Tate will be 'probably one of the safer if not the safest picks' at wide receiver in this draft class.
- 2026-03-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-03]]) [Dynasty]: Carnell Tate's 4.53 40 at the combine was 'disappointing but not bad' -- the eighth-slowest among the receiver class -- but Harmon says the panic around it misread what makes a vertical receiver. He researched the top-21 receivers with 250+ yards on 20-plus-air-yard passes last season and found only 3 of 20 ran sub-4.40, with outside-alignment guys averaging a 4.49 40; stars like Puka Nacua (4.57), CeeDee Lamb (4.5), Tee Higgins (4.54) and Courtland Sutton (4.54) were similarly 'slow' as prospects. Tate measured a lighter-than-expected 192 lbs (Harmon expected roughly 200), and both hosts agree the combination is a mildly disappointing measurement weekend, though co-host James Koh (untracked) called the time itself fine on its own. Harmon's player comp for Tate is Calvin Ridley as a route runner with some George Pickens-ish traits at the catch point, and he considers Tate a top-five receiver in a deep class, though not on the same top-10-pick tier as Malik Nabers or Drake London, partly due to reported injury concerns that could push him off the WR1 spot.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Tate and [[Makai Lemon]] form a clear top tier at receiver, with disagreement on order -- Waldman has Lemon a 'smidge higher' for safety, Angelo prefers Tate. Angelo argued Tate 'can do anything in the tree,' unlike Lemon, comparing a bad QB-fit risk for Lemon to the Brian Thomas Jr./Trevor Lawrence mismatch, and separately said that if he had [[Cam Ward]]'s Titans he would rather draft Tate than Love because Tate 'can really win deep,' which fits Ward's extend-the-play style. Waldman called Tate a viable, high-floor pick for builders prioritizing safety and a long production track record. Both hosts listed Tate among the 'landing-spot-proof' top-four receivers for dynasty rookie drafts, alongside Lemon.
- 2026-03-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-24]]) [Dynasty]: Tate (Ohio State, 6'2', 192 lbs, ran a 4.53 40 -- Harmon says not a concern since top 2025 deep threats averaged 4.49) grades as the top WR in the 2026 class and fits all 32 NFL offenses because of his ability to win outside as a boundary/X receiver. His three most-run routes -- nine, dig, curl -- were dominant: 68.6% success on the nine, 82% on the dig, 84.1% on the curl. Man coverage success was elite at 77.3% (well above the 75th percentile) and press-beating checked in at 75%. Contested-catch numbers were described as some of the best Harmon has ever charted: 91.7% contested catch rate on a 26.1% contested target rate, zero drops in the RP sample and zero drops all season at Ohio State. Co-host James Koh floated CeeDee Lamb and Chris Olave as comps; Harmon pushed back, saying he sees Tate more as an early-career Calvin Ridley blended with George Pickens at the catch point, with a long-term developmental comp to DeAndre Hopkins -- a bigger-handed contested-catch X receiver who got better and better as a separator as he matured as a pro. The caveat: 76.5% success versus zone (32nd percentile) and a 70% slant success rate, both depressed by an almost entirely vertical route tree with no screens or slants, which Harmon flags as a real red flag versus zone rather than a red-line concern given how little he was schemed for easy completions. Koh floated Tate as an ideal fit opposite Malik Nabers with the Giants (their T.J. Higgins to Nabers' Ja'Marr Chase), replacing Darius Slayton and giving Nabers a true number-two after Wan'Dale Robinson's departure.
- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Tate is Waldman's WR1 in the 2026 class, 'not even close.' Compared to Jordan Tyson, Tate is further along in the nuanced timing and pacing of his route setups -- the 'patience and suddenness' contrast that separates prospects from true NFL technicians. Tate already delivers moves with the timing and gravity to make defenders register and react, rather than executing them so fast they have zero effect. Waldman calls him more than a big-play threat, praising his growing feel for intermediate timing routes (curls, digs) run against a defense's best cover man in critical drive situations -- the routes that separate stars from field-stretchers. He's been saying this on other podcasts already; he considers it settled, not a hot take.
- 2026-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-30]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman ranks Carnell Tate as his WR1 in this class ahead of [[Makai Lemon]], preferring Tate's pure X/split-end profile over Lemon's slot/flanker versatility. His reasoning is structural, not a knock on Lemon: the NFL trend toward more two- and three-tight-end personnel (a response to smaller, faster nickel/umbrella coverages) is squeezing slot-receiver snaps league-wide, so Tate's split-end skill set should hold value better than a flanker's as that trend continues -- a pattern Waldman says the Rams' heavy use of 13 personnel already illustrates and that untracked analyst JJ Zachariason has also charted.
- 2026-04-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman named Ohio State's Carnell Tate his clear WR1 in the 2026 class, giving him a player-comparison spectrum topping out at Randy Moss with Justin Jefferson as a more likely outcome -- a score he said is in the same range he gave Malik Nabers, i.e. 'instant star for that team' unless he lands next to an elite receiver. He praised Tate's route technique, releases, above-average after-the-catch ability and elite catch-point hands (grouping him with Makai Lemon as the class's two best hand-technique receivers), and projected him to split end given verified speed -- scouts privately clocked him roughly 0.2-0.25 seconds faster than his 4.53 Combine 40. Comparing him directly to Lemon, Waldman said Tate is the better route runner against man coverage and would take him outside over Lemon, though he cautioned post-draft team fit and landing spot could still flip his final one-two order.
- 2026-04-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-06]]) [Dynasty]: on whether Ohio State's Carnell Tate is a reincarnation of [[Justin Jefferson]] (Bob Harris invoking analyst Frank Coyle's 'Hindu theory' comp framework), Waldman says he's feeling it -- Tate may never quite reach Jefferson's heights but should get close. He praises Tate as a smooth, disciplined route runner who sells pacing, footwork, body position and stem angles within route timing. He pushes back on after-the-catch criticism, arguing Tate is often catching in traffic rather than getting deep-threat or YAC-friendly targets, and that comparisons to Steve Smith-type playmaking are unfair; Tate is not Brandon Marshall, Terrell Owens, A.J. Brown or Torry Holt, but Waldman thinks he's better than a catch-and-slide receiver. He calls Tate a near-lock Year One starter almost anywhere -- even on a run-funnel Vikings roster he'd expect Tate outside with Jordan Addison in the slot -- and projects him as likely the best receiver on what could be a bad team.
- 2026-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: Tate grades below only [[Malik Nabers]] (~95 on Waldman's 100-point scale) among recent WR1 prospects, and above Luther Burden's ~92. His route-setup ability, breaks and catch-point hands (kept together as well as Makai Lemon's on difficult high-point grabs) make him 'almost peerless' at the catch, though he's not a big-play tackle-breaker like Chase or Nabers -- more a long-striding vertical X in the George Pickens mold (a comp Waldman said isn't his favorite) who takes what's blocked. His man-coverage success rate in Matt Harmon's Reception Perception charting is the 7th-best mark since 2021, ahead of many top-10 picks; Waldman said he'd take Tate over [[Tetairoa McMillan]] '14 times out of seven,' citing Tate's ability to beat true press-man like AJ Terrell, something he doubts McMillan has shown. Harmon agreed the gap is closer, roughly 4-to-3. Both call Tate the unambiguous WR1 of the 2026 class.

- 2026-04-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-28]]): Tate was his WR1 in this class despite not being a 'tier one' prospect like Marvin Harrison Jr. or Malik Nabers -- but the best available receiver in a weak class, taken 4th overall by Tennessee. Charted him at a 77.3% success rate vs. man coverage (89th percentile, 7th-best mark charted since 2021), second in the class only to KC Concepcion, with cleaner hands and better contested-catch ability (91.7% contested catch rate, 26.1% contested target rate, zero drops charted all season). Comps him to a blend of George Pickens at the catch point and early-career Calvin Ridley as a route runner -- reliable and always in the right spot, but also a big-time playmaker, not just a safe player. Projects him as the X in new OC Brian Dabol's expected spread-and-shred 11 personnel offense alongside [[Calvin Ridley]] and [[Wan'Dale Robinson]], with Elic Ayomanor more likely the movement Z. Strong route marks: 84% success on curls, 80% on posts, 83% on intermediate digs -- a good fit for Cam Ward's downfield, outside-the-numbers style. Weakness: lower success rate vs. zone (a product of his vertical route tree, not held against him) and limited YAC as a build-up-speed player. Calls him the odds-on favorite to lead all rookie receivers in yardage in 2026 if Ward takes a year-two step. James Koh (untracked co-host) agreed it was 'a great pick' addressing a Titans offense that was one of the league's worst at drops last year.
- 2026-05-11 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-11]]) [Dynasty]: asked to feel-it-or-fuck-it the claim that Carnell Tate leads all rookie receivers in production this year, Waldman feels it -- Tate projects as the primary receiver in [[Cam Ward]]'s downfield-oriented Titans passing game, winning targets on routes that break back to the quarterback, and Waldman called him 'no worse than a low-end wide receiver two in fantasy this year' at a draft price he doesn't expect to be steep. - 2026-05-25 — On over-the-top minicamp praise for Carnell Tate (a reported three-touchdown practice day, beating defenders deep, high-point catches), [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-25]]) [Best Ball/Dynasty] pushed back that this is mostly noise for beat writers who 'didn't know who the fuck Carnell Tate was' before camp -- Tate isn't doing anything on the practice field he didn't already show on film, which had more nuance than a shirts-and-shorts session can capture. - 2026-07-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Dave Kluge (footballguys.com, untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-10]]): Harris notes tracked expert [[Matt Waldman]] has compared Carnell Tate to [[Justin Jefferson]]. Kluge agrees the ceiling is immense given Tate's draft capital (No. 4 overall) and production/athleticism profile, but argues the best-case outcome is actually a 'quiet camp' with no viral highlight clips, since Titans social/beat coverage is incentivized to hype every contested catch and would inflate his cost above fair value. Both frame this as part of a broader market correction after fantasy drafters got 'rookie drunk' overpaying for [[Malik Nabers]], [[Rome Odunze]] and [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] following [[Justin Jefferson]] and [[Ja'Marr Chase]] hitting as sixth-round rookies.
- 2026-07-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-14]]) [Dynasty]: Carnell Tate 'maps really really well' with [[Cam Ward]]'s tendencies. Tate's prospect profile posted an 84% curl-route success rate, an 89th-percentile 77.3% success rate vs. man coverage (the 7th-best mark by any prospect RP has charted since the 2021 draft class), an 83% dig-route success rate, and a near-69% success rate on nine routes despite 40-time concerns. Harmon comped Tate's body type to 'a taller, skinnier version of peak [[Calvin Ridley]]' and said Tennessee's pick made sense beyond raw need -- it is a scheme/skill match, since Ward's strongest routes (per QB charting) are the same in-breaking curl/dig/slant combinations Tate wins on. Harmon rates the Ward-Tate chemistry as the swing factor for how good the 2026 Titans passing offense can be.

- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is happy paying up for Tate as his rookie WR2 (around a 6th-round ADP) because it lets him also get [[Michael Wilson]] and [[Parker Washington]] afterward, in the 7th-9th and 4th-5th rounds respectively. Tate runs the full route tree from the Z, projects for solid run-after-catch ability, and Waldman jokes 'Justin Jefferson was moonlighting as Carnell Tate at Ohio State.' Waldman's floor projection is 900-1,100 yards out of the gate with a real ceiling near 1,700 yards -- Harris notes that ceiling, even if unlikely, is why he'll pay slightly more for Tate than for Washington, who probably has a lower ceiling even though he may be the safer bet to hit his numbers.
