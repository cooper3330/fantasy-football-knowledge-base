# Wan'Dale Robinson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Dynasty]: cautions that New York's total lack of a passing game inflates how good any Giants receiver can look, but still credits Robinson with real after-the-catch value, solid contested-catch ability, and adequate speed. Projects him as a likely second- or third-most-productive option in the offense if the Giants land a quarterback who can support three or four weapons — his outlook is entirely a quarterback-situation question, not a talent question.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): "might be
  the best player" on paper of the Giants' disaster-level receiver room, and
  Harmon says he has "a pretty good understanding" of what he offers — but
  caps the outlook there: "I don't think Wan'Dale Robinson can [beat man
  coverage in the intermediate area] on a consistent route-by-route basis,"
  rating [[Demario Douglas]] ahead of him as a slot option for that reason.
- 2024-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-16]]): Harmon said he's 'not that big of a fan' of Wan'Dale Robinson, calling him 'not a needle mover' but 'all right in the slot' -- a complementary short-area piece behind [[Malik Nabers]] rather than a difference-maker. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2024-10-10 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert), co-host with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): Robinson's target rate is not to be relied on. Harstad calls the usage close to unprecedented and jokes it is its own Disney character. He notes that his usual rule is that usage is sticky and efficiency is noise, which would argue for sustainability, and that defenses are perfectly happy to concede these low-upside dump-offs rather than let the Giants take shots at [[Malik Nabers]]. But this is empty calories, and at some point the Giants eat more vegetables and fewer Cheetos. He adds a specific catalyst: [[Darius Slayton]] showing up last week in Nabers' absence gives the Giants reason to work both outside receivers, which would cut into Robinson's volume.
- 2024-10-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-17]]): Harmon calls himself 'the world's number one Wan'Dale Robinson hater' — Robinson was fifth in the NFL in receptions but 42nd-45th in receiving yards, a ratio he calls disgusting and gross. His diagnosis of the mechanics: Robinson 'turns, catches and falls,' so he is a layup and gadget target rather than a producer. Harmon does not want him carrying that target volume, but grants he is a useful slot receiver with some athleticism if the Giants get him moving — simple drag routes, or designed downfield shots off a play-action high-low where [[Malik Nabers]] runs an over route and Robinson sneaks in behind. He is explicit about the ceiling: Robinson will never win a big post against man coverage or split safeties like [[Rashid Shaheed]]. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) called him a fake receiver but part of a functional trio.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): with [[Drew Lock]] replacing [[Daniel Jones]], Waldman expects Robinson to be accessed a little more on off-script plays, since Lock extends and creates outside structure more readily than Jones does. A secondary beneficiary note rather than a full evaluation — the bulk of the Giants passing-game upside in Waldman's view goes to [[Malik Nabers]] on downfield and contested targets.
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-13]]): [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] is 'acceptable' as a third receiver and nothing more — Harmon flags that there is a big teardrop in quality between [[Malik Nabers]], Robinson and [[Jalen Hyatt]], notes people know Robinson is not one of his favorite receivers, and says he was being deliberately generous by even calling him an acceptable three. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) argued [[Jalen Tolbert]] is the more versatile of the two because he can play outside and run a fuller route tree, while conceding Robinson is the better after-catch player. Harmon adds that the Giants finished tenth-lowest in slot yards from receivers despite Robinson being on a 100-catch, roughly 600-yard pace at one point, and that because the team seems committed to Robinson in the slot, any addition should be an outside or downfield player instead.
- 2025-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-08]]): Harmon calls Robinson a replacement-level 'pop-gun' slot receiver and expects his volume to evaporate now that [[Daniel Jones]] is gone — [[Russell Wilson]] is not throwing the Robinson hitch route. Despite 90-plus catches on under 700 receiving yards, he ranked 75th of 76 qualifying receivers in EPA per target at -0.23 on 7.4 yards per reception, with only [[Elijah Moore]] (76th) below him; [[Dontayvion Wicks]] was 74th for drop-related reasons. Robinson caught 66% of his targets and has the highest catch rate among receivers with negative EPA per target, which Harmon flags as abnormal. Both hosts expect those targets to disperse elsewhere.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-16]]): not buying it, but owing Robinson 'a personal apology' after 10 targets, 8 catches, 142 yards and a touchdown against Dallas. Three of his 20+ yard receptions came on go routes, including the score, and at least two were up the seam on deep hash in-breakers — the first time since Kentucky that Harmon has seen him run and win deeper routes rather than the short stuff. He credits [[Russell Wilson]] for the throws and notes there were no blown coverages. The reason he still will not buy: this is the profile of a quarterback-driven spike week rather than a role change, and Robinson still took several short targets in the same game. Harmon acknowledges he has said almost nothing nice about Robinson in his NFL career and that the performance was genuinely awesome.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest John Daigle (Establish The Run, not tracked) made Robinson his number-five Week 3 waiver add, arguing the route tree has genuinely changed under [[Russell Wilson]] — a career of strictly underneath usage replaced by running nine routes literally the whole game against Dallas — and that he has scored double-digit fantasy points in both games of this Giants iteration. Harris is far more sceptical, ranking him tenth and calling him only an honorable mention: the production came against blown Dallas zone coverages that left him completely by himself down the seam, with Matt Eberflus's defense not understanding the assignment, and Greg Olsen said on the broadcast it was insane. Harris trusts it will not happen going forward and expects the Chiefs to have the deep seam covered. Both agree it is a bench-stash week rather than a confident start.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest Scott Pianowski of Yahoo Fantasy (not a tracked expert) nominated Robinson as his one player with upward ranking pressure after a 8-142-1 week 2, but still has him only in the WR40s. He called Robinson a 'PPR scam' — 93 catches last season without reaching 700 yards, only six career touchdowns, an 8.7 yards-per-catch average and a role that is generally not downfield; the big week came because Dallas escorted him anywhere he wanted to go in zone. Pianowski conceded the counterargument: 18 targets and 14 receptions already, a floor of roughly five to eight targets and four to six catches every week, and a narrow target concentration on a team that should trail often. He would prefer [[Russell Wilson]] keep starting over rookie [[Jackson Dart]] for Robinson's sake, since Wilson is 'a bad 20 minutes away from being benched at any time'. Harris noted Robinson's downfield plays came only because Wilson found him wide open.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-23]]): Robinson had just one catch for 26 yards, but Harmon singled him out approvingly — he went over the middle and took a hit to make it, which Harmon called 'playing big boy football' and contrasted with receivers who make excuses about contact over the middle (a callback to his prior comments on [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]). He also acknowledged Robinson 'dunked on me' after an earlier disagreement. The take is about toughness and route willingness rather than a projection of volume in an offense Harmon considers unable to go anywhere with [[Russell Wilson]].
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Robinson is a low-end WR3 or flex — Harris WR34, Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) WR32 — and the case is opportunity by elimination with [[Malik Nabers]] gone. The problem both identified is that the Giants may not want to throw 20 times: Bell described a staff that trusts its defensive line, studied early [[Josh Allen]] film, and beat the Chargers by running [[Cam Skattebo]] 25 times and [[Jackson Dart]] 10 designed times, wanting no risk with Dart. If a team throws under 20 times, the top receiver may not see five targets. The bull case is negative game script: a turnover, a 17-point deficit, and the Giants can no longer smash Dart into the line. Harris said they are ranking him without enthusiasm but he is usable.
- 2025-10-14 — According to guest Jake Seeley (The Athletic, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-14 (Week 7 Waiver Wire & More W6 Film)]]): Robinson (under 60% rostered) is Seeley's top-ranked available wide receiver this week -- if he's on your waiver wire, he's the priority target, ranked above [[Kendrick Bourne]] and [[Elic Ayomanor]] on Seeley's list.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with [[Malik Nabers]] out, Robinson converted a tough tight-window throw from [[Jackson Dart]] against defensive pressure and was heavily targeted throughout, including the near-miss tight-window throw that deflected into a Theo Johnson touchdown. Harris said he 'will be a regular in the top 36' wide receivers for at least the near term while Nabers remains out.
- 2025-10-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Jake Trowbridge (untracked, DFS lineup-reveal co-host, [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-23]]): Trowbridge paired Robinson ($5,200) with [[Jackson Dart]] in a Giants stack, floating -- while calling it 'dumb and gross' but 'kind of true for fantasy purposes' -- that Robinson is filling the target volume vacated by an injured [[Malik Nabers]]. Harris pushed back that Robinson 'essentially plays a different position' than Nabers: rather than one game-breaking deep shot, he racks up a steady stream of short/intermediate targets (e.g. seven-yard outs) that add up over a game, giving him a solid floor at a cheap price rather than Nabers' explosive ceiling.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-06]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris stacks Robinson with [[Jackson Dart]] at $5,300 on DraftKings and notes he ranks Robinson roughly five to ten spots lower than Andy Behrens (not a tracked expert) did on the Wednesday Yacht Club show, depending on format. His stated reasoning is format-dependent rather than enthusiastic: in full PPR he is perfectly happy to bank nine catches for 41 yards, and the price is cheap enough that the low-aDOT profile does not have to hurt.
- 2025-11-24 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: In the Giants-Lions overtime game, Harris said Robinson looked 'every bit' like Amon-Ra St. Brown, hauling in a deep touchdown behind two defenders, a deep out and a deep over where he threw his body at the ball to make the catch, and absorbing a helmet-to-helmet hit on another route but hanging onto it. It's the kind of alpha-receiver usage and performance combo Harris hasn't previously used to describe Robinson.
- 2026-02-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-12]]): Robinson, a longtime Harmon skeptic target, led the Giants in targets (140), receptions (92) and yards (1,014) in 2025 -- 13th among all NFL wide receivers in yards, just behind [[George Pickens]] and ahead of [[Zay Flowers]]. Harmon says the tape backs up the leap: his air yards per target jumped from 4.7 to 8.5, and his slot-alignment rate dropped from 70-78% over his first three seasons to 56% in 2025, meaning for the first time in his four-year career he was 'playing real wide receiver' running actual downfield routes rather than functioning as a checkdown option (he posted a literal negative EPA/target in 2024). Harmon calls it a full reversal of his prior 'fake wide receiver' read on him. The complication: Robinson is a pending unrestricted free agent hitting the market March 2nd, and with the Giants ranked 20th in cap space, Harmon doubts New York keeps him -- 'death, taxes, and Darius Slayton hanging around the Giants roster,' but not necessarily Robinson.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Andy Behrens (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-05]]): Robinson is expected to leave the Giants. Harris says he doesn't think Robinson is actually that good -- 'a good blood and guts little slot type dude' -- but that Giants receivers historically get their market value inflated (he compares this to years of overrating Darius Slayton), and Robinson only looked as productive as he did because Slayton was unreliable, tight ends couldn't catch, and Malik Nabers went down, funneling him targets by default. Behrens agrees this was 'an imperfect storm' rather than true talent, and both say Robinson should chase the biggest offer he can get since neither expects him to sustain that role-driven production for a new team.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-05]]): Robinson's Reception Perception profile went up as a pending free agent, and Harmon highlights how the Giants changed his route tree -- in 2024 he ran a hitch-heavy tree that produced a literal negative EPA per target despite 93 catches for 699 yards, but in 2025 they mixed in deep posts and deep overs. Harmon calls the resulting profile 'interesting' and considers Robinson a solid, perfectly capable route runner and separator even though the Giants' offense overall wasn't fun to watch.
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Dave Kluge (Footballguys.com, untracked guest) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-12]]): Robinson signs with Tennessee, reuniting with head coach Brian Daboll, whom players have reportedly followed to multiple stops. Kluge doesn't want Robinson miscast as a true WR1 (a role he was forced into last year) and prefers him as a clear No. 2 -- comparing it to his prior complementary role behind a top receiver -- while acknowledging real target volume in every Daboll-Robinson season together historically. Kluge also flags [[Cam Ward]] playing better than his results suggested late last season. Both call Robinson the poster child of the 'infinite sadness' WR3 tier: his fantasy value is likely capped unless none of Tennessee's younger receivers (Iuke, [[DK Metcalf]], possibly [[Calvin Ridley]]) step up, in which case a dysfunction-driven high-target role could emerge -- which Harris says isn't a basis he wants to draft on. Kluge notes Robinson began running more downfield routes for the first time last year (after being used purely as a gimmicky, short-area extension of the run game previously), which is a source of some untapped optimism.
- 2026-03-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-17]]) [Dynasty]: [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] signed a 4-year, $78M deal with Tennessee ($38M guaranteed, ~$19.5M/year) after a career-high 1,048-yard 2025 season with the Giants. Harmon's charting showed New York finally mixed deep posts, corners, and digs into his previously hitch-heavy 'Mickey Mouse' route tree, spiking his air yards per target from 4.9 (2024) to 8.7 (2025) -- though nearly 30% of his 2025 yardage came in just two games (8 catches/142 yards vs. Dallas, 9 catches in an OT win over Detroit). His Reception Perception grades were middling: 67% success rate vs. man (44th percentile) and 77% vs. zone (40th percentile), both below Harmon's usual '70/80' bar for a good slot receiver, which Koh flagged as concerning since he's almost exclusively a slot player who should be beating zone more. Harmon called him a solid-but-replaceable average NFL receiver, and worried paying him $20M/year could block a cheaper option like [[Chimere Dike]] from getting the same route diet on a rookie contract.
- 2026-04-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-28]]): signed to a four-year, $70M deal to be Tennessee's slot answer, freeing new first-round pick [[Carnell Tate]] and [[Calvin Ridley]] to play the boundary. Harmon views the Titans' 2026 receiver room (Robinson, Tate, Ridley, plus Elic Ayomanor and Chimere Dike) as a clear upgrade over last year's drop-plagued group, even if not yet a top-10 unit league-wide.
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-22 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: rejects the idea Robinson leads the Titans in receiving/passing yards, but does think he could lead the team in targets and possibly receptions, working as [[Cam Ward]]'s underneath outlet on off-script/scramble plays the way he did for Jackson Dart last year. Waldman's structural read: [[Carnell Tate]] wins early with intermediate/deep-sideline shots as Ward's aggressive downfield target, then as defenses adjust to Tate, Ward checks the ball down more often in the second half, shifting volume to Robinson. Currently going as WR46 (9th round), Bob Harris (untracked co-host) calls Robinson the 'safest' of the Robinson/[[Jayden Reed]]/[[Josh Downs]] trio going in that range, citing his back-to-back 90-catch seasons.
- 2026-07-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: In the 2026 Almanac profile, Wan'Dale Robinson's film grades are Speed B, Elusiveness B+, End Zone C (carryovers), with Hands upgraded to B after a much-improved 2025 season; Situation grade B-. Harris calls him 'a hard-working wiggly worm' and cites tape: breaking free of rookie safety Andrew Mabuka for a TD vs the Eagles in Week 6, hauling in a contested throw amid traffic vs the Chiefs in Week 3, and grinding out a 1-yard screen for a first down vs the Broncos in Week 7. Robinson's 1,014 receiving yards in 2025 is the highest total this millennium for any receiver his height/weight, surpassing Cole Beasley's 967 (which needed a [[Malik Nabers]] injury and a bad Giants team). New Titans OC Brian Dable, who coached Robinson in New York, reportedly pushed to sign him for $38M guaranteed, but Tennessee then drafted [[Carnell Tate]] No. 4 overall and still has [[Elic Ayomanor]], [[Chimere Dike]] and [[Calvin Ridley]], muting volume-WR hype. Harris projects only ~700 yards/5 TDs but calls him 'a baller' worth a bench spot, ranking him WR48 in standard leagues. Untracked Almanac contributor 'Cousin Josh' adds he isn't sure Robinson is meaningfully different from [[Parker Washington]] or [[Ladd McConkey]], calling the WR position 'super flat' with value findable late.
- 2026-07-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (untracked co-host) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-14]]) [Dynasty]: Koh named Wan'Dale Robinson the favorite for Titans WR2 over [[Calvin Ridley]] after Tennessee paid him to reunite with OC Brian Dable; Harmon pushed back, calling Robinson merely 'fine' and not a man-coverage-beating perimeter threat -- 64% curl-route success rate, under 67% on out routes -- while crediting him for a 2025 step forward as a real route-runner on digs (66.7% success) and posts (50%, small sample) rather than a purely gadget 'Mickey Mouse' option, projecting him as Ward's underneath safety-blanket rather than a big-play perimeter piece.
- 2026-08-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-18]]): guest Jeff Bell (FantasyPros' top-accuracy WR ranker, not a tracked expert) is 32 spots above Yahoo ADP (WR62) on Robinson in his new Titans role, seeing two paths to value -- either a heavy short/intermediate 'easy button' role for Cam Ward in Brian Daboll's offense (Cole Beasley comp), since Robinson knows Daboll's scheme better than anyone else on the roster, or a downfield role building on flashes shown last year. Bell calls him the 'last rest stop' before ADP value falls off a cliff at the position. Harmon (himself in the mid-50s, still above ADP) is more skeptical: he thinks it's live that Robinson leads the Titans in catches, but is generally fading the whole Titans passing offense given an overwhelmed Cam Ward and a rotating WR corps (Alec Pierce, Calvin Ridley, Chimere Dike) in a preseason he called horrific for the first-team offense.
