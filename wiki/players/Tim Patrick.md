# Tim Patrick

## Expert Takes

- 2024-08-27 — According to host James Koh (not a tracked expert) and guest Derek Klassen (not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): Patrick, who has not played a football game in two years and was on the trade block at the time of recording, is functioning as Denver's second wide receiver ahead of [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] — a fact Koh calls incredible and says he cannot understand, noting Patrick is no [[Calvin Ridley]]-caliber talent. Klassen reads it two ways: genuine resilience by Patrick, and Sean Payton being 'so smart and so full of himself' that he is determined to make a particular player archetype work. Klassen thinks it honestly can work, with health the obvious caveat. As a big-bodied jump-ball receiver he is also part of the odd fit Klassen flags with risk-averse passer [[Bo Nix]].
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): Patrick was cut by Denver days after insider reports said he was having a strong camp and would be the WR2 opposite [[Courtland Sutton]], then landed on Detroit's practice squad. Harmon is openly skeptical of the reporting rather than the player — 'I just don't know if I buy the idea' that a receiver dominating camp gets released — and calls this a case of insiders treating their audience as stupid. On the player himself: he is 6-4, has been a good contested-catch receiver, and pre-injury was a fine rotational third-down possession type, similar to what [[Josh Reynolds]] has been for Detroit, with the two essentially swapping places. The caveat is enormous — back-to-back training camp tears (ACL, then Achilles) and no real football since 2021. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) argues that even at 70% Patrick would be a usable real-football piece Detroit leans on situationally, while stressing he is not fantasy relevant. Harmon expects Detroit to try Patrick, Allen Robinson and Donovan Peoples-Jones and elevate only one.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: on Patrick landing in Detroit on the practice squad, Waldman thinks he will have value by year's end but framed it as a waiver-wire hold rather than a draft asset — if you need a receiver three, four or six weeks from now because of injury, Patrick can play the [[Josh Reynolds]] type of third-receiver role Detroit needs. Waldman noted Denver had shopped him and could not find a trade because of his age and injury history, but still believes he can play. Untracked co-host Bob Harris supplied the roster context: Detroit had only four receivers on the active roster, they do not want Kalif Raymond as the long-term WR3 because they value him as a returner, and Donovan Peoples-Jones did little last year — so promotions were coming quickly.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Going Deep)]]): Patrick is his top post-waiver addition. He fills the outside X role Detroit thought Donovan Peoples-Jones would fill — a big-bodied red-zone target with a bigger frame and catch radius than [[Josh Reynolds]] and, Angelo argues, comparable at this stage of their careers to a healthy [[Courtland Sutton]]. The obvious caveat is health after back-to-back Achilles and ACL injuries. Angelo's larger point is knock-on flexibility: a trustworthy X lets Detroit move [[Jameson Williams]] around as a zone beater, which he calls the offense's X-factor given Williams' maturation, and it pairs with [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]. He notes Detroit has not had a trusted go-up-and-get-it outside receiver since Calvin Johnson and that Dan Campbell has been hunting one.
