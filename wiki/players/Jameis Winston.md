# Jameis Winston

## Expert Takes

- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]): Waldman thinks Jameis Winston could be this year's 'Joe Flacco' - a cheap, safe insurance option who fits Kevin Stefanski's downfield, play-action, run-heavy scheme better than Deshaun Watson currently does while Watson recovers from shoulder surgery. Waldman reads Cleveland's decision to bring in both Winston and Tyler Huntley, rather than hand the backup job to Dorian Thompson-Robinson, as a sign the organization wants a real contingency plan in case Watson struggles again, calling Winston 'not great, but cheap and safe enough.'
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman is 'totally feeling' that Winston starts for Cleveland by midseason. His reasoning is financial and behavioural rather than schematic — the Browns have already restructured to free up as much cap space as they could off [[Deshaun Watson]], leaving them with as much room as anyone in the NFL, which he reads as preparation for moving on. If Watson does not come around, Waldman says Winston 'is more than serviceable.' Untracked co-host Bob Harris agreed, called it 'a very Browns thing to happen,' and added that Winston is the ultimate rah-rah guy who will fire a locker room up.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman expects Winston to start with [[Deshaun Watson]] out, and says yes, he has enough weapons to deliver fantasy value — 'he'll do a good job with [[David Njoku]], I think [[Cedric Tillman]]'s intriguing,' and [[Jerry Jeudy]] if 'he gets sent to Steve Smith boot camp.' Winston's traits: 'he's a good downfield thrower, he's aggressive where he needs to be,' and 'he can make do with what's there.' Waldman set the bar carefully — 'he may not be [[Joe Flacco]] and what Flacco did down the stretch, but there's a chance that that can happen' — and expects Winston to find one or two receivers who become startable. He also blasted the club for listing Winston as the emergency third quarterback in a home game, reading it as executives avoiding a stadium chanting Winston's name as a reminder of their own failure, with Dorian Thompson-Robinson injuring a finger.
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]) — a Browns fan — Winston's 334-yard game against Baltimore is not a thin line over [[Deshaun Watson]] but a 'big thick line,' and it indicts the Browns' front office for sunk-cost ego. On the play itself: still a bit reckless at times, but Waldman would now call him 'evolved to daring,' with footwork that continues to look better as it did in New Orleans, more drops from center allowing the offense to expand, a wealth of targets spread successfully, and strong ball placement. Caveat he volunteered: Baltimore allows the most passing yards in the NFL and had multiple secondary injuries, so do not get carried away — but the idea that this is not better is 'fuck that, it's better.'
- 2024-10-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-29]]): Winston is the fit Cleveland's offense actually needed — Harmon and Koh have argued for months that [[Deshaun Watson]], an almost exclusively shotgun passer, was oil and water with Kevin Stefanski's under-center play-action scheme. Winston is 'not special' and a worse decision-maker than [[Joe Flacco]], but he can stand in the pocket, go under center and rip downfield throws, which is the same skill set that made Flacco work here last year, minus the locker-room baggage. Harmon notes Ken Dorsey formally took over play-calling in the Baltimore win and that the offense went under center far more than Winston's season split. Stefanski has named him the starter for the rest of the season. Caveat: a schedule of Chargers, Saints, Steelers twice, Broncos and Chiefs is a real test.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31]]): Winston is giving Cleveland life after Deshaun Watson's ruptured Achilles, and the fit is structural, not just energy. Waldman notes Watson is a pistol quarterback who hamstrung Kevin Stefanski's under-center scheme, while Winston is a natural under-center dropback passer — which restores the play-action game, the downhill run game and the cover-one/cover-three one-on-one looks off two-tight-end sets. Waldman also argues Winston has genuinely improved since Tampa: his footwork no longer lags his body on drops, he climbs pockets under better control, so the throws are less erratic. Angelo, a Browns fan, thinks Winston is the starter for the foreseeable future and has earned the right to have a 2025 rookie sit behind him — a Geno Smith-style trajectory, a capable risk-taking starter who wins you big games, though he stops short of saying Winston will be as good as Geno. Both note the Ravens win came on one week of first-team practice.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]): the full Winston experience against Denver — 497 passing yards plus 171 interception return yards against him, and per Optus Stats the 668 combined figure is the most by any quarterback in a single game in NFL history, alongside two pick sixes. James Koh (co-host, not tracked) called him 'the Peyton Manning of Blake Bortleses', which Harmon endorsed. Harmon noted via Scott Barrett that Cleveland is first in the NFL in catchable air yards with Winston and dead last with [[Deshaun Watson]], and that over the prior three weeks Winston ranked third in under-center dropbacks and second in under-center passing yards — the aggressive, downfield, play-action-and-bootleg profile [[Kevin Stefanski]]'s scheme is built for. His caution for fantasy: it is a 'pump and dump', inflating receiver production without winning football, so the usage numbers may not be real. Both hosts would give him a Geno Smith / Baker Mayfield style three-year deal with a two-year out, and floated the Jets as a suitor if Cleveland passes.
- 2024-12-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-09]]): asked whether Winston could be an even better starter in Cleveland if retained for 2025, Waldman says 'fuck it' — 'he's broken, but he's broken in a fun way,' and he does not believe that wiring changes. He describes one game in which Winston overthrew a stand-in-the-pocket seam route for an interception he should have fit in, then earlier flushed left, turned back to his right while still moving left, and hit [[Jerry Jeudy]] in stride on one of the more difficult throws Waldman saw that week. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) noted the attempt volume under Kevin Stefanski and Ken Dorsey — 27, 41, 46, 46, 58 — and said two Cleveland beat writers told him the team may have realized its error in moving on from [[Joe Flacco]], so Winston sticking around next year is entirely possible.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-19]]): benched in Cleveland for Dorian Thompson-Robinson, and Harmon's line is that the only surprise is anyone being surprised. Winston averages over six air yards per attempt, third highest in the NFL, versus DTR's minus 1.03 across 34 attempts — behind the line of scrimmage on average, which Koh said he had never seen from a quarterback. Harmon puts Winston in the [[Joe Flacco]] mold but even more aggressive and more prone to mistakes, with more mental errors reading coverage; he notes Winston owns the record for most combined passing yards and interception-return yards in a game, and that a pure backup on a one-year deal does not get to keep piling up more mistakes than big plays. He expects Winston to keep finding starting jobs and to 'burn bright and burn out fast', and says he'd be curious to see him with [[Malik Nabers]], [[Garrett Wilson]] or [[Jakobi Meyers]].
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]): Winston holding a Giants starting job for at least half the season is a 'feel it' under the right circumstances — the caveat being whether New York adds another veteran (Russell Wilson and Aaron Rodgers were both still free agents) or drafts a quarterback. Waldman frames his profile as high variance: highs better than [[Daniel Jones]]'s highs, lows as bad or worse, but with an aggression he values because it force-feeds a true alpha the way it did [[Michael Thomas]] in New Orleans. He points to the volume evidence from Cleveland, where [[Jerry Jeudy]] averaged nearly nine targets a game and posted a WR5 finish over his eight starts. Bob Harris (not tracked) jokes that the Winston drafters are the YOLO best ball crowd.
- 2025-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-26]]): Winston signing with the Giants for two years and $8M is, in Harmon's words, a contract that means there is 'zero percent chance' he starts 17 games — he notes [[Jacoby Brissett]] got $6.3M from Arizona as a pure backup and [[Gardner Minshew]] signed a two-year, $25M deal, so $4M a year signals a bridge/backup role and leaves the Giants free to add [[Russell Wilson]] or draft [[Shedeur Sanders]] third overall. Harmon calls the Winston effect a 'pump and dump scheme' — big touchdown and air-yard spikes followed by a flattening, as happened with [[Jerry Jeudy]] in 2024. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) agrees you get touchdowns, air yards and a lot of interceptions, but says it will at least be more fun than 2024.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-13]]): Winston was named the Giants' starter with [[Jackson Dart]] in concussion protocol, and Harmon is unusually cold on it. He calls the Winston effect the 'pump and dump scam' and says the problem is who he would be pumping — [[Darius Slayton]] and [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] do not move the needle the way [[Jerry Jeudy]] and [[Cedric Tillman]] did in Cleveland last year. Harmon is blunter still on the player: not a good starting-level NFL quarterback, a fantasy scam who got benched for Dorian Thompson-Robinson, and people only get excited because he is goofy and produces receiver stats. Koh disagrees on the fantasy angle, calling it exciting and a must-add in two-quarterback formats, and expecting a big Slayton bump.
- 2025-11-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-11-18]]): with Jaxson Dart out on concussion protocol, Winston injected far more life into the Giants offense than Russell Wilson would have, per Harris, including a rushing touchdown and several big completions to Isaiah Hodgins. But Harris says Winston was 'supremely lucky' not to have thrown four interceptions -- he counts five throws that landed in Packers defenders' hands, two dropped on the same drive plus a game-ending interception where the receiver (Jalen Hyatt) stopped his route rather than Winston misfiring. Harris's bottom line: if Dart can't return, Winston gives the offense a better floor than Wilson ever would, but the tape was far shakier than the box score suggests.
- 2025-11-21 — According to Chris Harris on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-21]]: With [[Jackson Dart]] expected to clear concussion protocol, Harris moved Dart back into his quarterback ranks, sending Winston -- whom Harris jokingly nicknamed 'the wacky, waving, inflatable, arm-flailing tube man' -- back to the bench.
- 2025-11-24 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Winston 'played awesome throughout' the Giants' overtime loss to Detroit - over 200 passing yards in the first half, only one truly bad moment (a fourth-quarter pick that didn't cost them), and repeated big-arm throws to Wan'Dale Robinson and Theo Johnson. Harris sided with broadcaster Jonathan Vilma in criticizing interim coach Mike Kafka for kicking a field goal on 4th-and-long late instead of going for it, and for calling a draw play on 3rd-and-7 in overtime that got stuffed before Winston was sacked on 4th down to end the game - laying the loss on coaching, not on Winston's play.
