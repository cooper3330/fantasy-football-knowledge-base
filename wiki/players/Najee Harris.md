# Najee Harris

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): both hosts say they're "intrigued" by what Harris and [[Jaylen Warren]] could look like under new OC Arthur Smith, whose Tennessee offenses fed one back overwhelmingly (Derrick Henry: 300+ carries in 2019 and 2020, with no other Titans running back topping 54 carries in either season). Harmon separately credits Mike Tomlin's track record as a "kingmaker" for running backs across very different runner archetypes (the specific comp: Le'Veon Bell). No explicit split projected between Harris and Warren beyond the general expectation that the offense funnels volume hard to its best backfield piece.
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]) [Dynasty]: Despite Pittsburgh declining Najee Harris's fifth-year option, Waldman is 'totally feeling' Harris as a likely long-term piece -- reading the option decline as a money/leverage move so the team can negotiate year-to-year rather than a signal they're moving on. Citing a direct conversation with Steelers.com writer (and former 4for4 analyst) Dale Lolley, Waldman notes the roughly $14M option cost more than a franchise tag would, that Pittsburgh has tag flexibility if it's needed, and that the team will keep working toward a longer-term deal -- Waldman's read is Harris's future in Pittsburgh remains very likely.
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): with the ADP gap between Harris (RB22) and [[Jaylen Warren]] (RB25) narrowing, Waldman still takes Harris, projecting him for roughly 1,200-1,300 total yards and 11 touchdowns versus Warren's projected ~1,000 total yards and 6-7 touchdowns. He argued Harris still draws the most predictable, defense-friendly touch volume but fits Arthur Smith's downhill run-game style better than Warren, and expects the usage gap to widen as Smith leans on his history of establishing a run game. Bob Harris agreed, calling the current price gap too small to matter and siding with Harris.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31]]): 'this is the best I've seen Najee Harris look his whole career' — three straight 100-yard games and about 140 total yards on Monday night. Waldman credits the [[Russell Wilson]] moon balls for stretching the field and the move to heavy 12 personnel, which he calls a genuinely good Steelers grouping because [[Pat Freiermuth]] is a very good blocker and Darnell Washington can make do on size. The caveat for fantasy is a three-back rotation: Waldman says Harris, [[Jaylen Warren]] and [[Cordarrelle Patterson]] can each take a real workload and the team can rotate all three to wear a defense out — he jokes Patterson would give you nine carries at 6.8 a pop and a touchdown before his body gave out.
- 2024-11-14 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14 (Age Cliffs and Dented Cans)]] [Dynasty]: Harris was one of the 2023 dented cans the show named and he has cashed — he and [[Joe Mixon]] are the only backs with three straight 100-yard rushing games this season, and Harris has among the most 100-yard games in the league. Harstad was the most willing of the group to buy the original 2021-22 hype because of what Harris showed as a rookie, but still thought the top-five dynasty ranking was premature. He calls Harris 'a good player' and uses the annual cycle of people being out on him as the clearest illustration of the emotional discount that creates dented cans in the first place.
- 2024-12-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-23]]): Harris was out-carried 12-9 and out-produced 48-42 rushing yards by [[Jaylen Warren]], and Waldman noted Pittsburgh did not exercise his fifth-year option, so 'we'll see where his next contract comes from.' The framing is of a back whose team has declined to commit, in a backfield where the change-of-pace back is trending up.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): Harris is not simply a locked-in RB3 — Waldman still thinks the right landing spot could make him a lot more, whether via an injury ahead of him or a team pairing him with a bad drafted back. The caveat is that you should not acquire him as a fantasy GM expecting that immediately, because it is unlikely to be available on arrival; it is a watch-list situation. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) emphasized the durability and consistency — a thousand yards every year, stays healthy — and wondered aloud whether the offense and circumstances in Pittsburgh, rather than Harris, capped the production. Waldman floated Las Vegas as a plausible fit alongside [[Geno Smith]].
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman is feeling Harris for a career year with the Chargers, who have moved on from [[J.K. Dobbins]] and released [[Gus Edwards]]. His case: Harris is the only back in this era with four straight 1,000-yard rushing seasons, he is a genuinely good receiving back, and he will finally be used to his capacity in a Harbaugh offense with consistent skill talent around him — he stresses Pittsburgh had talent but not consistent talent. He expects the Chargers to keep building the offensive line, thinks it is already better than Pittsburgh's, and says the quarterback is better too. Bob Harris (untracked) notes [[Ladd McConkey]] and the returning [[Mike Williams]] round out the supporting cast; both agree Harris and Warren can be winners so long as you don't overpay.
- 2025-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-26]]): Harris was a poor fit in Arthur Smith's Pittsburgh run game because he is a gap runner, and Smith's preference is not simply big backs — Harmon argues what Smith actually wants is a back who can hit outside-zone lanes, which is why Atlanta loved [[Bijan Robinson]] and [[Tyler Allgeier]], and why [[Derrick Henry]] worked for him (Henry being an outside-run stiff-arm back, closer to Chris Johnson than Jerome Bettis or Earl Campbell). Harmon says [[Jaylen Warren]] can hit those lanes but is not a full-time player, so Pittsburgh should draft a back with one of its third- or fourth-round picks.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): asked to compare and contrast Harris with [[Omarion Hampton]], Waldman takes 'Najee Harris every day of the week,' acknowledging it is an unpopular take because people treat Hampton as Jim Brown, which he is not. Waldman argues Harris got a raw deal in Pittsburgh — a trash offensive line for years and unsettled quarterback play — while still posting four straight 1,000-yard seasons, catching the ball very well, showing decent burst and breaking tackles; he just needs a bit more of a lane. He expects the Chargers to feel they lucked into him, and predicts he will laugh if Harris runs for 1,200-1,300 yards in Los Angeles while Hampton looks like Harris in Pittsburgh. He cites the Steelers' history of misjudging backs — poo-pooing [[James Conner]] for Benny Snell.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]): the [[Omarion Hampton]] pick at 22 does not bode well for Harris's one-year deal, but Waldman pushes back on the damaged-goods theory. He credits Matt Bitonti of Football Guys (not tracked) with the useful analytical habit of asking whether a running back pick signals the incumbent is hurt, and says there are awkward reasons it could apply here — but from what we have seen, Harris has had no major injury; he has simply been a grinder behind a weak offensive line with lousy quarterback play. His conclusion is a split, not a replacement.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]): Harris is getting underrated while [[Omarion Hampton]] gets rocketed up boards on surface-level reasoning. On the mystery fireworks eye injury that has him on the non-football injury list, Waldman — who says he has three eye doctors in his family — argues a superficial injury heals quickly but must be given time, so a patch or a shutdown is expected precaution against re-injury, inflammation or infection rather than a long-term sight concern; the fact that he is on such a list does not by itself imply severity. His football case for Harris is pass-protection chops and between-the-tackles work that he thinks exceed Hampton's, and he pushes back on the idea that Harris was the problem in Pittsburgh. His scenario: Hampton is named the bell cow in Week 1 and Harris is taking 60% of the touches by Week 3. Waldman does hold open the possibility that the eye issue turns out to be more serious than reported.
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]): Waldman thinks the market has overcorrected and written Harris off for dead. He argues Harris has already learned multiple NFL offenses so the missed install matters far less than it would for rookie [[Omarion Hampton]], and that Harris has spent years being blamed as the bad guy behind a crappy offensive line the way [[James Conner]] was before his turnaround. Waldman is holding a 50-50 split projection: if Hampton stumbles on pass protection, hot reads or coverage recognition, Harris takes half the work; only if Hampton adjusts cleanly does Harris fall to injury-insurance touches. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked) agrees Harris is undervalued because he lacks the eye-popping advanced metrics people want, calling him a technically sound back who doesn't miss games and always makes the right read — Kluge got him in round 11 of a staff league and feels good about that even at 50% of snaps in a good offense, while still expecting Hampton to lead. Both note the [[Rashawn Slater]] injury lowers the whole backfield's rushing projection.
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the fireworks eye injury is being overblown and Harris should be fine. Drawing on family members with roughly a hundred years of experience as optometrists, Waldman describes the likely diagnosis as a hyphema — disruption in the anterior chamber of the eye with blood entering it — which sounds alarming but heals straightforwardly provided the player avoids head contact, heavy lifting that creates ocular pressure and vigorous exercise for about five to six weeks. That makes the Chargers' stated timeline credible and the 'he might lose his eye' conspiracy theories unfounded. On the field, Waldman argues Harris is better than what Pittsburgh showed behind a bad line with no real quarterback, and expects a roughly even split with [[Omarion Hampton]], with Harris keeping snaps through blocking and receiving.
- 2025-08-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: a sanctioned media leak confirmed Najee is unlikely to start the year playing, will not play Week 1, and could land on the non-football injury list, which would cost him at least the first month. Harris called it the first real peek at the truth amid the meandering assurances from Jim Harbaugh, and dropped him way outside the top 100 to roughly an 11th-round speculative stash. He still expects Najee to play at some point this season and to take part of the workload back from [[Omarion Hampton]]. Asked whether the Hampton drafter must handcuff him, Harris said it is not mandatory, but the price is cheap enough that owning both could produce a league winner if one gets hurt.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): Harris is 'actually a pretty good back' and the market underrates him, largely because Waldman does not trust Pittsburgh media or Pittsburgh fans' judgment on running backs, which shaped Harris's reputation. With reports that Harris will play Week 1 despite his offseason eye injury, Waldman expects an even split with rookie [[Omarion Hampton]] rather than a Hampton takeover, and says that is exactly why Hampton is being drafted too high. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) concurred that the Chargers have signalled a committee, pointing to Greg Roman saying they will run Hampton with somebody else no matter what.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): Harris avoided the PUP list and the Chargers say he has a chance to be ready for week one. Chris Harris still puts [[Omarion Hampton]] 'way out in front' of an active Najee in his week-one ranks purely on the readiness quotient, but warns that gap could narrow quickly once games start.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris calls him the most significant unknown of Week 1 following his eye injury. The Chargers likely know whether he can play Friday against the Chiefs but have said nothing definitive, and while Najee has been practicing, Chris Harris stresses that practicing in September means much less than it does in camp because nobody is getting tackled. He plans to rank Najee among the running backs to start the week but not very highly, given the uncertainty over how much he will play, and advises that where you drafted him you can afford to be conservative with lineups even if he is active. He adds that rookie [[Omarion Hampton]] going in the third round means the market stayed relatively conservative on him all August, which now looks like the right call given Najee may actually play.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): Harris's absent Week 1 role was conditioning, not depth chart — he simply had not been able to get in shape coming off the eye injury. Los Angeles still needs a back who can be a jitterbug in tighter creases against more zone looks, and who catches and pass-protects out of the backfield, and Waldman says Harris does all of that very well. He expects closer to an even split than the market anticipates. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) framed it as The Mechanic: [[Omarion Hampton]] is Jan-Michael Vincent, the hotshot, and Najee is Charles Bronson, the savvy professional with four straight 1,000-yard seasons and solid if unflashy fantasy production — and he is not going anywhere.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Najee closed the Monday night win — the final seven snaps of the game were his, three of them carries — immediately after [[Omarion Hampton]]'s fumble on a clock-killing handoff. Harris wonders whether Najee, coming back from the offseason eye injury, simply needed time to build up cardiovascularly. At 37% rostered he sits in an awkward middle ground: already owned in deep leagues, not yet worth adding in shallow ones. Guest John Daigle (Establish The Run, not tracked) does not mind him at all, citing touches growing from two in Week 1 to ten in Week 2 (out-touching Hampton 10-9) and a career-high 4.8 yards per touch — not exciting in the abstract, but exciting for Najee — plus the value of exposure to what is currently one of the league's best offenses with [[Justin Herbert]]. Harris pushes back that the efficiency comes on a statistically insignificant sample and his real point is that Hampton has not looked good.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris ruptured his Achilles against Denver and will assuredly miss the rest of the season — Chris Harris said he saw 'the telltale explosion inside his calf' live on a play-action fake with five minutes left in the first half and knew immediately. Up to that point Najee had been the better of the two Chargers backs on Sunday, though Harris credited that mostly to running lanes rather than to anything [[Omarion Hampton]] was doing wrong. From the injury forward Hampton took 43 of the remaining 48 offensive snaps, with Hassan Haskins in only as a blocker on five, so the season-ending outcome hands the backfield to Hampton outright.
