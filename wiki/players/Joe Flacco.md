# Joe Flacco

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-01 — Both hosts on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]] [Redraft (Standard)]: credit the Browns' late-2023 offensive turnaround under Flacco less to Flacco or head coach Kevin Stefanski individually than to offensive line coach Tom Cable, who built a good-enough run-blocking unit (without [[Nick Chubb]] for most of the year) to let Flacco play the pass-first, full-field-read offense Stefanski reportedly wanted all along but hadn't had the quarterback to run — contrasted with Deshaun Watson's struggles in the same building. Waldman had recommended starting Flacco in a Week 17 matchup against the Jets even without Amari Cooper available. See [[Scheme vs Talent]].
- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode: whether Flacco is retained as Cleveland's 2024 starter over Deshaun Watson is framed as a financial/political question rather than a performance one, given Watson's contract — "it should" happen on merit, in his view, but he's skeptical the front office will actually make that call.
- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): after one Flacco start, credits him with spreading the field and accessing secondary receivers better than any Cleveland quarterback in years — a specific, film-based point rather than just veteran-presence praise. Separately praises Flacco as an "adult in the locker room" and steadying professional influence on a team that needed one, distinct from his on-field grade ("is Flacco as good as people thought after the 2012 playoffs? No"). Says he's glad the Jets didn't sign Flacco instead of riding out Zach Wilson, purely because a Flacco-led Cleveland Super Bowl run is the more fun story — floats it as a Kurt Warner-level Cinderella comparison if it happens.
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: frames the Browns' Wild Card loss to Houston (a rough Flacco game, multiple turnovers) as the end of "a great summertime romance," not a reason to doubt what he showed in the regular season — "he ran that offense with those talented players better than any quarterback by a lot who's played for the Browns the last two seasons," Deshaun Watson included. Expects Flacco has earned a "chunky" veteran backup contract (speculates 2-year/$40M range) somewhere, and is skeptical Cleveland can financially justify keeping him over Watson's contract. Floats — as a "who on planet Earth" hypothetical, not a real prediction — that Watson's contract is technically tradable if a team were willing to take it on and later restructure it.

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: names him, along with [[Ryan Tannehill]], [[Gardner Minshew]] and Jameis Winston (not tracked), as a possible Minnesota bridge-QB option after [[Kirk Cousins]]'s departure to Atlanta — likes the idea of Flacco throwing play-action vertical shots the way he did under Kevin Stefanski in Cleveland, since that maps well to Kevin O'Connell's scheme — but ranks [[Jacoby Brissett]] above all of them as the better bet.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): the idea that Flacco would fix the Colts offense did not survive contact with Minnesota — 16-of-27 for 179 yards and an interception, with Indianapolis putting up a season-low 227 yards in a 21-13 loss despite the defense forcing three turnovers. Waldman grants that Flacco is a better decision-maker than [[Anthony Richardson]] and has far more experience reading disguises, but points to a third-and-two where [[Trey Sermon]] was wide open in the flat and Flacco instead forced the ball into double coverage 15 yards downfield to effectively end the game, plus an underthrown corner route to [[Josh Downs]] that looked like an Anthony Richardson throw. His framing: a rookie who might grow out of those errors versus a veteran who lapses back into them once comfortable, with a known and lower ceiling. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) added they should have expected less against a Brian Flores blitz-heavy defense.
- 2024-11-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]]): Flacco has been worse than [[Anthony Richardson]] from an efficiency and EPA-per-drop-back standpoint, and the Colts offense has objectively gotten worse with him behind center — he threw three interceptions in Week 10, one of eleven quarterbacks under 200 passing yards. Harmon and James Koh (not a tracked expert) both treat Flacco as a placeholder whose only useful function is to play himself out of the job so that a Richardson return reads as a plan rather than a second benching; Koh allows that the second half of the most recent game was 'pretty decent'. Harmon is pointed that Steichen's public leadership-and-veteran justification is what makes the situation look cooked.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): if [[Kenny Pickett]] cannot handle pressure, Flacco can step in 'right away and be at least salvageable for that offense' — a known quantity who gets too aggressive and misreads some coverages, but who, when hot and schemed into favorable situations, is 'even more than serviceable.' Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) is 'all in on Joe Flacco' as the Browns starter, which is why he is buying late [[Jerry Jeudy]] shares at reasonable [[Best Ball]] prices and feels better about [[David Njoku]] with Flacco throwing. Waldman does not endorse the fantasy angle himself, only the football floor Flacco provides.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): Waldman is 'feeling' Flacco as Cleveland's Week 1 starter and dismissed the play-the-rookies chorus — Andrew Berry and Kevin Stefanski are not trying to lose, Flacco is comfortable in the system, and what he does well fits Cleveland's power-run and play-action identity. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed on Flacco but flagged ball security, noting roughly six turnovers in Flacco's last four Colts games and that people covering the team say protecting the football is the requirement; he also thought [[Kenny Pickett]] might be close enough to threaten. Waldman disagreed on the Indianapolis evidence: those receivers could not hold the ball, [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] was badly banged up and the line was poor. Cleveland's line and receivers are better and it has two legitimate tight ends, and Flacco has always been good at targeting the tight end. He also argued Flacco's late dip was seasonal ebb and flow from starting mid-year.
- 2025-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-16]]): Flacco is the outcome Harmon quietly wants for [[Cedric Tillman]]'s fantasy value, because the deeper digs and in-breaking throws Tillman wins on are exactly what Flacco was ripping during Cleveland's 2023 run. He is honest about the caveat: Flacco was terrible with Indianapolis last year and is 40 years old, and Koh (not tracked) compares the situation to Matt Ryan going to the Colts. Harmon says the Browns' four-way competition between Flacco, [[Kenny Pickett]], [[Dillon Gabriel]] and [[Shedeur Sanders]] is the most genuinely equitable quarterback battle he can remember, and that his heart says Flacco wins it.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-05]]): Harmon is selling the widely held view that the Browns offense is fantasy-viable simply because Flacco is starting. He cited a 0.9 adjusted yards per attempt under pressure in 2024 — dead last among 36 quarterbacks with 240-plus attempts — and paired it with a Browns offensive line he does not trust at tackle. He allowed that Cleveland's under-center, boot and play-action system fits Flacco better than Indianapolis's spread shotgun attack, and said in hindsight the Colts signing him made no sense at all: not a mentor type, not a system fit, and a player coaches will feel pressure to play. Harmon expects him to struggle against Minnesota, who he says beat Flacco up last year, though he conceded Flacco throwing bombs could produce the occasional deep shot to [[Cedric Tillman]].
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): feel it — Flacco starts the whole season. Kevin Stefanski drafted to a scheme Flacco fits, and while he will not light the world on fire he should be good enough to play a full year while Cleveland decides whether to clear the decks at quarterback and use Flacco as a 2026 bridge. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) argues trading [[Kenny Pickett]] burnt the boats — it is Flacco or bust — and that the odds of Flacco recapturing 75% of his 2023 five-week stretch are far better than [[Dillon Gabriel]] or [[Shedeur Sanders]] emerging this season. Waldman's caveat: turnovers are the risk, and there is a universe where Flacco gets benched.
