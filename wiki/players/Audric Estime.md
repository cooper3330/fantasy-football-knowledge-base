# Audric Estime

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name as "Audrick Estime"; normalized here to the real name of the Notre
> Dame running back, Audric Estime. He is also referenced, not tracked as a
> new take, by [[Brandon Angelo]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]
> as a positive contrast case for [[Braelon Allen]]'s competitiveness.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: ran a 4.71 forty at the NFL combine, then a 4.58 at his pro day; Waldman reads his true speed as somewhere between the two — below average for an NFL starter, and it shows on tape, but "more than good enough to play in the league and contribute." His breakaway runs at Notre Dame depended on defenders being poorly leveraged or slow to stop-turn-and-reaccelerate (a five-to-seven-step process); expects that to happen less often in the NFL, but says his other traits and skills should compensate enough for production given a good fit and continued craft development.
- 2024-05-02 — According to Brandon Angelo ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): Estime lands well in Denver behind Sean Payton, who in year two of his scheme has historically deployed two backs — a pass-game/zone weapon (Alvin Kamara-type) plus a two-down 'banger.' Angelo argued Javonte Williams hasn't filled the banger role because he can't control the pace of his runs the way Payton's system calls for, and with Williams entering a contract year and set to hit free agency, the two-down role could open up for Estime, whose pacing and tempo Angelo specifically likes. He called it a sizable role and a day-three player with a real path to a significant touch share as soon as next year. Waldman noted he still likes UDFA Jaleel McLaughlin from last year's class in the same backfield.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo has Estime one spot behind [[Blake Watson]] on his board and currently going around pick 36 in rookie drafts (versus pick 59 for Watson), making him the less value-efficient of Denver's two rookie backs given how similarly he grades them.
- 2024-07-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]): Estime should be more assured of a Denver roster spot than [[Javonte Williams]], and fits Sean Payton's system as a powerful runner with pretty good footwork who can take [[Samaje Perine]]'s change-of-pace, closeout-carry and goal-line role. The concern is speed — he will have to be more timely about setting up blocks 'mainly because he just isn't very fast.' Waldman's specific worry is archetype risk: he thinks A.J. Dillon is much faster than Estime, and if Dillon and backs like him are not doing well in the NFL, that is a bad signal. He frames Estime as the player people watch on Notre Dame tape and over-project, because it does not translate as cleanly to the pro level.
