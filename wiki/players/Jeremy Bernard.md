# Jeremy Bernard

## Expert Takes

- 2026-06-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-29]]) [Dynasty]: Feels Aaron Rodgers individually working with rookie Jeremy Bernard will matter long-term but downplays the short-term significance, comparing it to a past example of a scout overselling individualized veteran-QB reps with an undrafted running back as more important than it actually was. Projects Bernard to clearly beat out [[Roman Wilson]] for the third-receiver role -- 'not going to be even close' -- forecasting close to 75 targets, about 600 yards and 4 touchdowns. Compares the opportunity to what people hoped [[Calvin Austin III]] would be in this offense, a hope that ultimately disappointed, but notes Bernard now has more established weapons drawing attention elsewhere on the roster.
- 2026-10-01 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-10-01]]) [Dynasty]: Jeremy Bernard is Angelo's top breakout pick among rookies -- he out-snapped Roman Wilson 15-to-5 after a Wilson drop turned into an interception, and offers run-game value Pittsburgh's other receivers can't match, including a 40-50-yard lead block on a Darnell Washington run and clean interior blocks walling off a linebacker for Jalen Warren. Angelo calls him one of the best post-catch players in his draft class dating back to his Washington tape, and says he's positioned to take over volume because DK Metcalf and Michael Pittman Jr. 'don't look good' and appear to be playing hurt, and Wilson is making too many mistakes, while none of those three offer anything after the catch the way Bernard does.
- 2026-10-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-10-13]]), his #3 overall waiver add: with Roman Wilson out, Michael Pittman on IR and Ben Skowronek also out, Bernard has 'gained Aaron Rodgers' trust,' running 32 of 34 routes last week and drawing nine targets (with a drop, including one earlier in the season that led to an interception). Harris expects a decent workload against Tampa Bay despite a tough road matchup for Pittsburgh, pushing back when guest Jeff Erickson (untracked RotoWire co-host) jokingly warns him off relying on a 'road' sample-size take. Harris says if Bernard weren't a rookie he'd expect Rodgers to simply cycle to someone else next, and when asked to rank Bernard versus [[DK Metcalf]] he still takes Metcalf -- but calls Bernard's performance good enough to take a flier on.
- 2026-10-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (untracked co-host) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-10-20]]): since Week 5, [[Jeremy Bernard]] leads the Steelers in target share (28%), catch rate (81%, 17-of-21), yards per route run (1.92), and yards after catch (102), despite a short 3.57-yard average depth of target built on a lot of quick screens. Harmon likes him as [[Aaron Rodgers]]'s go-to 'check down or touchdown' option and projects him as a [[Jakobi Meyers]]-type possession piece -- probably more physical after the catch than Meyers but a slightly less effective downfield route runner. Rodgers himself said postgame that Bernard's confidence is starting to catch up with his ability despite some missed assignments, which both hosts read as a strong signal given Rodgers' history of being hard on young receivers. Koh pushed back on the 'Steelers can't develop a receiver' narrative, calling Bernard a 'high character, high effort' player who should be a reliable role piece even without the superstar ceiling some past Steelers WR hits flashed.
- 2026-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: The rookie wasn't targeted deep (only one throw beyond 10 air yards) but was trusted repeatedly on third downs -- including a mistake where he caught one past the sticks on fourth down but then ran backwards into a tackle -- before scoring the game-winning touchdown in overtime. Harris says it 'probably takes a certain kind of Steelers game flow' like this one to make Bernard fantasy-usable given how limited his route tree is. Guest Scott Fish (untracked) admits he had Bernard ranked below Kendrick Bourne last week and calls that the wrong call in hindsight, while cautioning that replicating 49 Rodgers pass attempts again isn't something to bank on weekly. In the Week 7 waiver countdown Fish rates Bernard around 51% rostered -- appealing bench depth in 10-team leagues with 8+ bench spots, but a tough sell as a must-add given the offense's run-first bent most weeks.
- 2026-10-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-10-22 (Going Deep Eagles & 2027 QB Class)]]) [Dynasty/Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman frames Bernard's early usage as an extension of Pittsburgh's run game -- inside breaking slants, over and crossing routes, screens, RPOs, swing passes and fly sweeps, with only occasional outside fades -- and says Bernard has a real catch-technique issue, not an overblown narrative: he's an underhand catcher who doesn't bring the ball to his frame correctly, the same flaw Garrett Wilson has jokingly called 'catch shaming' and that it showed up at Alabama and still shows up occasionally now. Waldman compares Bernard's current role to a pre-breakout [[Rashee Rice]] and ultimately to a Randall Cobb-style timing/underneath operator alongside [[Aaron Rodgers]], extrapolating roughly 107 targets a year for a true slot-primary role in that offense -- translating to six-to-eight catchable, low-volatility looks a game and strong PPR value if the usage holds. He credits trust from McCarthy, Rodgers, [[DK Metcalf]] and OC Todd Haley (all cited as saying Bernard is bright, hard-working and asks great questions) for the expanding role, and separately praises his decision-making as a ball carrier -- including a backfield arrow run and a game-winning bubble screen -- as what extends his usefulness beyond a typical rookie ceiling. The next step for a true breakout, per both hosts, is graduating to intermediate, timing-based route work rather than staying strictly a run-game extension.
- 2026-10-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-10-26]]): Bernard managed just 3 catches for 27 yards a week after a huge game, and Harris said it's 'really hard to go all in' on him given how run-heavy and checkdown-reliant Pittsburgh's [[Aaron Rodgers]]-led offense is.
- 2026-10-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-10-26 (Feel It or Fk It)]]): After a breakout week, Jeremy Bernard was quiet in Paris as the Saints defense adjusted. Waldman says opposing defenses identified his usage as an extension of the run game on screens and swarmed with two defensive backs to shut it down early. On a missed deep opportunity, Waldman notes Aaron Rodgers was upset about an apparently overthrown corner route, but on film Bernard's release was disrupted by a defender in the stem of a two-man route and he corrected his break angle a step late, costing roughly three steps total -- 'a little bit that's on Bernard.' In the red zone the Saints instead targeted Darnell Washington, whom Waldman calls arguably the team's best fade-ball catcher next to Pat Freiermuth, over DK Metcalf, whose fade-route positioning lapses the hosts joke require 'two fades in a row' before he remembers the technique.
- 2026-10-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-10-29]]): Harmon credits Jeremy Bernard's emergence as a real driver behind Pittsburgh's offensive turnaround -- the Steelers have been a top-10 offense by Harmon's efficiency metrics since Week 3, leaning into a run-heavy 'bully ball' identity, with Bernard giving the passing game a dimension it previously lacked alongside [[Darnell Washington]] and [[DK Metcalf]] ('Metcalf is Metcalf,' per Harmon). Harmon noted Bernard can also play some Z receiver. James Koh (co-host, not tracked) was skeptical of Pittsburgh overall, calling the team 'a huge fraud' given a brutal closing schedule (Browns, Bengals, at Philadelphia, Denver, Houston, Jacksonville, Baltimore, Carolina), while Harmon pushed back that he's at least enjoyed watching this offense the last few weeks for the first time in the Aaron Rodgers era.
