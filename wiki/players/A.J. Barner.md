# A.J. Barner

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-30]] [Dynasty]: "an NFL caliber athlete" but not much of a fantasy option — 6'5"/~250 lbs, projects as an in-line blocker/short-area receiver who'll probably need to add 10-15 lbs of muscle. Can occasionally win the intermediate game off play-action. Best-case NFL role comped to Brock Wright — a two-and-three-tight-end-set contributor who can start if called upon and be an occasional weekly matchup play.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]): Barner should retain the underneath role in Seattle's two-tight-end sets under Klint Kubiak even with [[Elijah Arroyo]] arriving — he is good in tight windows, finds open creases against zone, and is 'the most believable inline blocker' on the roster at this point. Waldman frames the Arroyo hype as running into Barner, not past him, and expects Barner to keep meaningful red-zone and short-area work while Arroyo takes the vertical play-action shots [[Noah Fant]] used to.
- 2025-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]): Barner was quietly really good as an inline tight end last year, which is part of why Harmon expects Seattle to play a lot of 12 personnel even after cutting [[Noah Fant]]. James Koh (not a tracked expert) likes him as a prospect but stresses he is still unproven, and finds the Fant release odd for a team whose receiver arrangement points toward heavier personnel. The pairing with rookie [[Elijah Arroyo]], who both hosts expect to need development time, leaves Barner as the more reliable inline piece of the two.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris (untracked co-host) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-20]]): Jaguars safety Andrew Wingard bit hard on a play fake and gave up a 63-yard catch-and-run touchdown to Barner, part of a pattern of Wingard being a liability in coverage (he also got beat deep by [[Jake Ferguson]] the following week). The takeaway: start any streaming tight end likely to see even modest snaps and targets against Jacksonville while Wingard is on the field.
- 2026-01-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-19]]) [Dynasty]: with [[Elijah Arroyo]] battling his own availability issues, Waldman still likes holding [[A.J. Barner]] as a cheap goal-line producer -- comparing the potential Seattle split to older TE tandems where one player handled the goal-line/short-yardage role (Wesley Walls, or Taysom Hill's usage in New Orleans) while another handled more downfield work (Keith Jackson-type, or the Penn State-to-Oregon comp made in the episode). He'd hold Barner as a cheap points source while Arroyo keeps learning.
- 2026-02-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-09]]): Barner caught an early tight-window throw from Sam Darnold and scored a touchdown in the Super Bowl, part of Seattle's plan to exploit a slot-size mismatch while JSN drew Christian Gonzalez's coverage.
- 2026-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-11]]), relaying analysis from Reception Perception writer Max Toscano (untracked): the Seahawks drafted Barner out of Indiana/Michigan primarily as a blocker -- a great in-line player with excellent hands but limited explosiveness and a route tree that doesn't extend much beyond the shallow area of the field. His value comes from walling off/posting up linebackers and safeties in the first 10-15 yards and from misdirection or play-action runs into space, where his power and contact balance let him 'run through people,' functioning as an extension of the ground game rather than a man-coverage-beating route runner. Waldman notes Barner finished as fantasy TE14 as a rookie despite nobody projecting him for fantasy value, calling him a potential 'canary in the coal mine' for a broader shift toward valuing in-line blocking tight ends over spread-style move tight ends if the league trends toward heavier personnel groupings.
