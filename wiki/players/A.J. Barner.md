# A.J. Barner

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-30]] [Dynasty]: "an NFL caliber athlete" but not much of a fantasy option — 6'5"/~250 lbs, projects as an in-line blocker/short-area receiver who'll probably need to add 10-15 lbs of muscle. Can occasionally win the intermediate game off play-action. Best-case NFL role comped to Brock Wright — a two-and-three-tight-end-set contributor who can start if called upon and be an occasional weekly matchup play.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]): Barner should retain the underneath role in Seattle's two-tight-end sets under Klint Kubiak even with [[Elijah Arroyo]] arriving — he is good in tight windows, finds open creases against zone, and is 'the most believable inline blocker' on the roster at this point. Waldman frames the Arroyo hype as running into Barner, not past him, and expects Barner to keep meaningful red-zone and short-area work while Arroyo takes the vertical play-action shots [[Noah Fant]] used to.
- 2025-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]): Barner was quietly really good as an inline tight end last year, which is part of why Harmon expects Seattle to play a lot of 12 personnel even after cutting [[Noah Fant]]. James Koh (not a tracked expert) likes him as a prospect but stresses he is still unproven, and finds the Fant release odd for a team whose receiver arrangement points toward heavier personnel. The pairing with rookie [[Elijah Arroyo]], who both hosts expect to need development time, leaves Barner as the more reliable inline piece of the two.
