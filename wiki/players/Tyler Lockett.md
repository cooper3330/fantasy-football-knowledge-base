# Tyler Lockett

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): still credited as part of "one of the best three-person wide receiver trios in the NFL" alongside DK Metcalf and [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]], but his roster spot is flagged as a real 2024 question under new HC Mike McDonald — a notable cap hit makes him a plausible cap-casualty/trade candidate, floated specifically as a fit to reunite with outgoing Seattle OC Shane Waldron in Chicago. Explicit career-stage read: "on the back nine of his career" but "probably can still play a little bit."
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman doesn't think Lockett, entering his age-31 season, has lost much and expects at least one more strong year, maybe two, even as Jaxon Smith-Njigba threatens to pass him as Seattle's top receiver. Waldman has them virtually even in his own rankings (Lockett WR35, JSN WR36) even though current Underdog ADP has JSN going notably ahead (WR44 vs WR51 for Lockett).
- 2024-06-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-20]]): calls himself 'the biggest Lockett guy in the world' but says plainly Lockett is 'definitely a declining player.' Notes the team restructured his contract to keep him under new coach Mike Macdonald's staff, calling that a positive signal for his role, but expects Jaxon Smith-Njigba to pass him on the depth chart this year as Lockett's heavy flat/screen-route usage (12.4% flat routes last year, above NFL average) doesn't fit new OC Ryan Grubb's vertical scheme as well as JSN's intermediate/downfield profile.
- 2024-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-06]]): Lockett — a receiver Harmon says he has pounded the table for his whole career — is still a quality NFL receiver but no longer in the superstar tier he occupied through 2022. Harmon argues 2022 may have been Lockett's best season ever, with his career-best man success rate, second-best zone and roughly third-best press score, which makes the 2023 step back the harder lesson: not enormous, but enough to raise an eyebrow, and with older receivers the trend rarely zigzags back up. It is either a steady decline or the cliff. Harmon reads the Seahawks' restructure as loyalty to a franchise lifer and a new staff signalling they reward their guys, and suspects Seattle is internally prepared for him not to be the same player. James Koh (not tracked) frames [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] as the likeliest beneficiary of the target share.
- 2024-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): Lockett is a declined version of himself but nowhere near washed — as Seattle's third receiver he is better than most third receivers in the NFL, and Harmon thinks the new staff can deploy him creatively, particularly with more slot reps if [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] moves around. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) agrees, saying he went back and watched a lot of Lockett tape: not as explosive downfield as he was, but he can still play.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-12]]): Lockett's box score (seven targets, six catches, 77 yards, team lead in yardage) hides a scaled-back role — third on the team in routes and only 52% of offensive snaps, routinely off the field in two-receiver sets in favor of [[DK Metcalf]] and [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]]. Harmon draws a distinction he says the fantasy industry collapses: Lockett is a declining player, not a washed one, and analyzing aging receivers is a sliding scale rather than a binary. Evidence he is still trusted: all six of his catchable looks were first-read targets and he was targeted on 33% of routes run. Notably he was not kicked inside — 70% of snaps outside, and six of seven targets came from an outside alignment — which Harmon attributes to Smith-Njigba owning the slot. He compares Lockett's current value to [[Jalen McMillan]]: reliable quick, underneath separation and a man-beater. Both hosts wonder if the snap cap is preservation; Harmon expects a critical role but not a big fantasy one.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Lockett is not going away. Harmon calls himself Lockett's number one fan and a declining player is still too good to be a zero in this offense, pointing to the Week 1 big plays on third down when Geno Smith went into chaos mode and looked for him. The limitation Harmon notes is that Lockett is not an over-the-middle player, which is exactly the area of the field Ryan Grubb's offense is now attacking with [[DK Metcalf]] and [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]].
- 2024-12-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-10]]): Harmon says it is the end of the line for Lockett. He is still on the field a great deal but has been targeted on only 9% of his routes over the last three games and posted a zero-catch game, leaving him a distant third in the Seattle receiver room behind [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] and [[DK Metcalf]]. Harmon still salutes him as an RP hall of famer. Koh agrees on the trend but thinks the old man has something left in the tank that is just no longer accessible snap to snap, and would not be surprised by a clutch play if Seattle reaches the postseason.
- 2025-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-30]]): Lockett's time in Seattle is over for all intents and purposes — he calls the ceremonial Week 18 social-media sendoff a goodbye and describes him as a declining player, joking that he has not personally reckoned with it yet ('we haven't filed for divorce, we're still living in the same house'). Koh (not a tracked expert) agrees, and both use Lockett's decline as the reason Seattle cannot also trade [[DK Metcalf]] — without DK the room would go from one of the strongest in the league to one of the weirdest, leaving [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] with Jake Bobo.
- 2025-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-19]]): Lockett is a likely cut given his cap charge, and Harmon reads the Seahawks posting a 12-minute video of him running routes — which Lockett quote-tweeted saying he watched all 12 minutes — as a goodbye. Harmon calls him a genuine retirement candidate rather than a free agent, since he already has a thriving Seattle real estate business and may not want the Allen Robinson path of hanging on at the bottom of playoff rosters. He has been in decline for two years and turns 33, after six straight seasons of 105+ targets fell to 74 targets, 49 catches, 600 yards and 2 TDs in 2024, though he still averaged 12.2 yards per reception. Both hosts stress he can still play — he was ahead of [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] as a rookie in 2023. James Koh (not tracked) floated the Rams as a versatile inside/outside fit with speed left; Harmon countered with the Raiders, given cap room, Pete Carroll and nothing at receiver beyond [[Jakobi Meyers]], while Koh said that would be 'too Raidery' and does nothing for them.
- 2025-02-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-26]]): Lockett being cut is 'all but a certainty' — co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) noted a $31 million cap hit and $15 million average per year on a Seattle receiver group carrying the most cap in the NFL at nearly $69 million, money that comes off once he goes. Harmon still likes the theory of Lockett landing with the Raiders. Both hosts treated his departure as the mechanism that frees [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] to move outside from a 77% slot rate.
- 2025-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-13]]): Lockett is Harmon's template for the underrated technician — the previous holder of the 'most underrated receiver in the NFL' title now passed to [[Josh Downs]], and a close comp for him because of the contested-catch, over-the-shoulder, win-in-tight-spaces skill set. Harmon and Koh note that early-career Lockett was a much better tackle breaker, and that over roughly his last four or five seasons he chose to go down immediately after the catch and avoid contact — which kept him on the field and available.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Best Ball]: he is 'kind of feeling it' on Lockett leading Tennessee in receptions or yardage, calling him the better pure route runner of himself and [[Calvin Ridley]] even though Ridley has more downfield upside. Waldman argues the two sub-1,000-yard seasons are context, not decline: per [[DK Metcalf]], Ryan Grubb's staff ignored the veteran scouting reports on coverage that Pete Carroll used to act on, and Lockett said he accepted a back seat so Seattle could develop [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] and keep Metcalf happy. Lockett is in his early 30s but had four or five straight 1,000-yard seasons before that. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) disagreed on the WR1 label — he still expects Ridley — but conceded Lockett after the 15th round is the better value buy.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: posed as either/or against [[Calvin Ridley]] — a question he also put to Bob Harris (co-host of Feel It or F**k It, not a tracked expert), who chose Ridley — Waldman concedes Ridley is easily the top guy on paper but builds the Lockett case around football IQ. He cites [[DK Metcalf]]'s post-trade comments that he and Lockett spent years emailing Seattle's staff scouting packets on opposing corner and safety alignments, that Pete Carroll's regime implemented the solutions, and that Ryan Grubb's staff thanked them and did nothing after about three games — implying more meat on the bone than Lockett's own 'I took a back seat for the team' narrative admits. If Lockett can be the coach on the field for [[Cam Ward]], Waldman sees an 800-1,000 yard season that could threaten Ridley in receptions and yardage. He explicitly flags that a player supplying his own camp narrative is itself something to watch out for. [[Brandon Angelo]] is cooler, saying it depends whether Lockett plays the slot where he was overproductive or is bumped outside, and that he has lost a step.
- 2025-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-01]]): Harmon grants that Lockett is obviously better than [[Van Jefferson]] has ever been at any point and probably has more left, but states plainly that he is a declining player, and now profiles as the slot move-around guy rather than a downfield-alignment starter, still leaning on in-breakers. James Koh questions what is left of Lockett's athletic profile at this stage of his career and says he does not see him as an every-down player anymore. Both agree Tennessee added five receivers this offseason and that every one of them outside [[Calvin Ridley]] carries a serious deficiency, with Harmon comparing the likely shape of the room to Denver in 2024 — [[Courtland Sutton]] on the field for about 90.5% of dropbacks and nobody else over 50% — while noting the Titans lack a Sean Payton to scheme it.
