# Spencer Rattler

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]), discussed alongside [[Xavier Legette]] as his South Carolina target: liked what he saw at South Carolina better than his earlier Oklahoma tape, and now considers him draftable — possibly even developable — moving better in the pocket than expected. A real split noted in his coverage-beating ability: strong against one of man/zone coverage and poor against the other (Waldman was not certain on air which was which), which he summarizes as a prospect who currently has "half a game" and needs to develop the other half.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-01]]): a maturity/coachability storyline rather than a film update — singled out as the Senior Bowl week's clearest example of a prospect visibly growing up on camera. Rattler himself acknowledged being "a really cocky 17-year-old kid... I wasn't as good as I thought I was," which Angelo reads as real evidence he can now take on-field criticism from evaluators during Senior Bowl week and use it constructively rather than being above it. On the field: "did okay," but "spinning a little bit" — a mixed, not a clean, week.

- 2024-03-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-08]]), solo cast on the full QB class, resolving the man/zone split flagged but not specified on 2024-01-29: baseline is similar to Baker Mayfield, but the strengths/weaknesses against coverage run more opposite. Mayfield struggled more with tight man-to-man coverage, especially under pressure forcing him to climb the pocket; Rattler has more trouble reading defender position in zone coverage when his offense isn't scheming a single clear throw. He's much better identifying open options against man-to-man, and his best zone-coverage success comes when the offense simply leverages his mobility and accuracy as a quick-read distributor. Grade: potential to become a good backup and maybe a journeyman starter — teams are intrigued, but how fast he improves reading zone coverage is the thing to track.
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman framed the Saints' backup QB battle as a fantasy non-event for Spencer Rattler either way -- he favors Jake Haener to win the job, and said that if Rattler wins it instead, [[Derrick Carr]] simply holds the starting role all year, making the competition not worth paying attention to from a fantasy standpoint.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]) [Dynasty]: Rattler was a pre-draft favorite of many analysts, but Waldman does not think he is a savvy field reader at this stage — he is good against certain zone looks and much weaker against man coverage, which Waldman notes is backwards from the usual profile of a quarterback entering the NFL. He expects New Orleans to keep all three quarterbacks because the backup competition behind [[Derek Carr]] was close enough, with [[Jake Haener]] having had a cancer scare mid-preseason after being expected to push for the No. 2 job. Waldman's advice is that you do not need Rattler right now, while cautioning listeners to ignore the 'yuk yuks' about it being a dull competition and stay open to him still growing.
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]): Rattler was 'serviceable for a half' in his start before Tampa Bay adjusted and Todd Bowles dialed up pressure that changed the game. Waldman 'feels it' on the Saints' decision to start him over Jake Haener only in the narrow sense that they wanted a more mobile quarterback who could get outside and create off-structure when the play breaks down — but he says that framing is a bit of a choose-your-own-evidence exercise, and that Haener is 'probably still the better quarterback.' He thinks Rattler can keep growing in the offense but needs a lot of work, and would not be surprised to see Haener at some point if Rattler cannot pull it together; he rates New Orleans as having two underrated developmental quarterbacks on the roster.
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): Rattler's development will not be linear, like most quarterbacks, so up-and-down weeks are expected — and this was not a great week in a lot of respects. Waldman said [[Jake Haener]] is the better quarterback right now, and singled out a specific flaw: Rattler puts [[Chris Olave]] in bad situations, where Haener targets him hard without exposing him to hits. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) framed Rattler's struggles as part of why the Saints offense has cratered alongside [[Derek Carr]]'s absence.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): in the post-[[Derek Carr]] Saints competition Waldman placed Rattler as the combination of Jake Haener's conceptual quarterbacking and [[Tyler Shough]]'s physical tools, purely on the strength of the starting experience he accumulated in 2024 in this same offense. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) noted Kellen Moore publicly called it a three-way battle between Shough, Haener and Rattler with the door left open to adding a veteran, and that the 2024 Rattler tape 'wasn't fantastic' — though he questioned what the expectations even were. Waldman's overall read was that Shough is the organization's hope, which leaves Rattler as the experienced fallback rather than the favorite.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): Rattler held off second-round rookie [[Tyler Shough]] to win the Saints' starting job. Harris re-watched Rattler's rookie tape to write an Almanac profile and called the experience 'not my favorite' — he was 'truly god-awful' across six starts as a rookie — but concedes it was only six starts and that Kellen Moore may have gotten to him and made him 'vaguely competent'.
