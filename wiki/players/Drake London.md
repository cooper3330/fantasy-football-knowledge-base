# Drake London

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: "feeling that possibility" on top-15 wide receiver value for 2024, contingent on Atlanta hiring a real head coach and solving its quarterback situation after moving on from Arthur Smith. Attributes Smith's firing mainly to poor quarterback play and media friction rather than misuse of his skill talent, and expects the new staff to make sure London (along with [[Bijan Robinson]] and Kyle Pitts) actually gets used at the level of his talent once a competent passer is in place. Flags that London's ADP will likely still carry a premium, since the industry consensus already expects an Atlanta breakout.
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: closes out a running "please don't trade London for [[Zay Flowers]]" debate from earlier in the season — "no question to me that London had the more impressive rookie year," with London also roughly six months younger than Flowers. Says the market on the two receivers is close, maybe even slightly favoring Flowers, and calls that a mispricing now that the full season is in.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): one of only five wide receivers to ever earn a tier-one grade ("worth picking in the top 10... a ready-made star") on Harmon's cross-class Reception Perception "stacked board," alongside [[Ja'Marr Chase]], Chris Olave, DeVonta Smith and Garrett Wilson (Olave/Smith/Wilson not tracked) — a durable pedigree marker distinct from Waldman's more recent redraft-value read on him. See [[Reception Perception Methodology]].
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): direct scheme-fit optimism tied to Atlanta's new OC hire (Zach Robinson, from the Rams) — Harmon says he'd "love to see" London used on the same big in-breaking routes the Rams ran for Puka Nacua and Cooper Kupp, and calls the incoming offense "well set up to be in line with the league's meta right now" for a talented X-type receiver like London.

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: reacting to the finalized [[Kirk Cousins]] signing — expects an uptick in both volume and target quality. Says London's talent profile has no real limitations ("there's nothing where I see... there's a limitation on this guy") and groups him with [[Terry McLaurin]], [[D.J. Moore]] and Allen Robinson (not tracked) as talented X-receivers stuck on bad-quarterback carousels — arguing London is in that same star caliber and projects as a true No. 1 once paired with functional QB play. Calls Cousins especially well-suited to unlock him: Cousins trusts receivers on contested, tight-window throws and loves working the middle of the field on the exact route types (digs, crossers, slants, big posts) where London wins most. Cites Atlanta's WR-starved 2023 offense (209 pass attempts to wide receivers, fewest in the NFL) as the depressed baseline Cousins should lift, and separately flags the receiver depth behind London (Austin Mack, Chris Blair, Josh Ali — none tracked, no meaningful stats) as a real need Atlanta should still address. See [[Weak Quarterback Play and Receiver Value]].


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: "all in" on London benefiting from the finalized [[Kirk Cousins]] signing, alongside [[Kyle Pitts]] and the Falcons' run game.

- 2024-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-21]]): building on the YouTube route-tree breakdown, notes London's 2023 route tree skewed less over-the-middle than it should have in [[Scheme vs Talent|Arthur Smith's]] offense, with his curl-route rate jumping sharply from year one to year two because he was used almost exclusively as an X receiver. Calls him one of the best young pure X-receivers in the league but also someone who can move to slot/flanker in a [[Michael Pittman Jr.]]-type off-ball role. Because of that X-heavy usage, argues Atlanta still needs another true outside receiver even after adding [[Darnell Mooney]] and [[Rondale Moore]], since neither profiles as a real X — Mooney is "a 3" and Moore is "a pure gadget player." See [[Scheme vs Talent]].

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): named the
  best current player in Atlanta's "fragile" receiver group. In a hypothetical
  long-term comparison against [[Rome Odunze]] as a rookie, the hosts say "we
  love Drake London" and he's been exactly the player his prospect profile
  promised — but still give Odunze the edge as a prospect and, projecting
  forward, as the better long-term player, largely on downfield explosive-play
  ability. Also floated in an "all in" Falcons scenario if Atlanta used a
  premium pick on Odunze to pair with London and [[Kirk Cousins]].
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: Atlanta waiting until pick 187 to add a receiver (Casey Washington) tells Harmon the team has 'tremendous internal faith' in London as a volume-hog X receiver, alongside heavy reliance on [[Kyle Pitts]] and complementary roles for [[Darnell Mooney]] and [[Rondale Moore]]. He floated -- without fully endorsing it -- a 'simplify the picture' theory where London and a hypothetical Rome Odunze could each command roughly 130 targets apiece behind Kirk Cousins, questioning whether Cousins can really feed that many mouths given his track record with a thin Atlanta/Minnesota supporting cast. Co-host James Koh (untracked) pushed back that London and Odunze would be too redundant as similar X-receiver archetypes to both thrive, while Harmon countered the team could motion them around to create matchup problems. Both agreed Atlanta passing on Odunze at 8th overall for a QB doesn't change London's outlook.
- 2024-05-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-29]]), on a tangent about inflated breakout expectations: London is being drafted as a top-20 overall pick and the WR11 off the board this offseason, yet last year's WR14-16 (Brandon Aiyuk, Michael Pittman, DK Metcalf) averaged roughly 83 catches, 1,200 yards and 6 touchdowns combined -- a genuinely good season that would nonetheless be branded a disappointment for London given his current ADP. [Redraft (Standard)] [Best Ball]
- 2024-06-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-20]]): new OC Zach Robinson (ex-Rams assistant under Sean McVay) inherits an offense that was dead last in the NFL in targets to wide receivers last year (209, ~50 fewer than the next-lowest team, Chicago at 259) under Arthur Smith, so the volume bar is trivially easy to clear. London ran an outside-alignment 80%+ of his snaps in 2023, among the most 'static' deployments Harmon has charted (alongside Quentin Johnston, Nico Collins, Marquise Brown, Calvin Ridley, Tee Higgins); Harmon expects a McVay-tree offense to run far more 11 personnel than Arthur Smith did and to move London into more condensed/inside alignments to unlock play-action dagger concepts (comped to Puka Nacua's favorite dig-on-dagger route) with Darnell Mooney stretching the field vertically. London's route-success data is elite on out (88%), corner (63.2%) and near-elite on dig/curl/slant. Harmon projects London for 1,300 yards on his medium projection (aggressive vs. his 905-yard 2023), even with Atlanta's overall WR target share still projected below league average, and flags the shallow WR depth chart (Mooney, Rondale Moore, Ray-Ray McCloud) as the risk to hitting the 11-personnel usage he's betting on. Discussion also touched Kirk Cousins fitting play-action well under Zimmer and rookie Michael Penix Jr. sitting behind him.
