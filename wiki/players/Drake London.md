## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: "feeling that possibility" on top-15 wide receiver value for 2024, contingent on Atlanta hiring a real head coach and solving its quarterback situation after moving on from Arthur Smith. Attributes Smith's firing mainly to poor quarterback play and media friction rather than misuse of his skill talent, and expects the new staff to make sure London (along with [[Bijan Robinson]] and Kyle Pitts) actually gets used at the level of his talent once a competent passer is in place. Flags that London's ADP will likely still carry a premium, since the industry consensus already expects an Atlanta breakout.
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: closes out a running "please don't trade London for [[Zay Flowers]]" debate from earlier in the season — "no question to me that London had the more impressive rookie year," with London also roughly six months younger than Flowers. Says the market on the two receivers is close, maybe even slightly favoring Flowers, and calls that a mispricing now that the full season is in.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): one of only five wide receivers to ever earn a tier-one grade ("worth picking in the top 10... a ready-made star") on Harmon's cross-class Reception Perception "stacked board," alongside [[Ja'Marr Chase]], Chris Olave, DeVonta Smith and Garrett Wilson (Olave/Smith/Wilson not tracked) — a durable pedigree marker distinct from Waldman's more recent redraft-value read on him. - 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): direct scheme-fit optimism tied to Atlanta's new OC hire (Zach Robinson, from the Rams) — Harmon says he'd "love to see" London used on the same big in-breaking routes the Rams ran for Puka Nacua and Cooper Kupp, and calls the incoming offense "well set up to be in line with the league's meta right now" for a talented X-type receiver like London.

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: reacting to the finalized [[Kirk Cousins]] signing — expects an uptick in both volume and target quality. Says London's talent profile has no real limitations ("there's nothing where I see... there's a limitation on this guy") and groups him with [[Terry McLaurin]], [[D.J. Moore]] and Allen Robinson (not tracked) as talented X-receivers stuck on bad-quarterback carousels — arguing London is in that same star caliber and projects as a true No. 1 once paired with functional QB play. Calls Cousins especially well-suited to unlock him: Cousins trusts receivers on contested, tight-window throws and loves working the middle of the field on the exact route types (digs, crossers, slants, big posts) where London wins most. Cites Atlanta's WR-starved 2023 offense (209 pass attempts to wide receivers, fewest in the NFL) as the depressed baseline Cousins should lift, and separately flags the receiver depth behind London (Austin Mack, Chris Blair, Josh Ali — none tracked, no meaningful stats) as a real need Atlanta should still address. See [[Weak Quarterback Play and Receiver Value]].

- 2024-06-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-20]]): new OC Zach Robinson (ex-Rams assistant under Sean McVay) inherits an offense that was dead last in the NFL in targets to wide receivers last year (209, ~50 fewer than the next-lowest team, Chicago at 259) under Arthur Smith, so the volume bar is trivially easy to clear. London ran an outside-alignment 80%+ of his snaps in 2023, among the most 'static' deployments Harmon has charted (alongside Quentin Johnston, Nico Collins, Marquise Brown, Calvin Ridley, Tee Higgins); Harmon expects a McVay-tree offense to run far more 11 personnel than Arthur Smith did and to move London into more condensed/inside alignments to unlock play-action dagger concepts (comped to Puka Nacua's favorite dig-on-dagger route) with Darnell Mooney stretching the field vertically. London's route-success data is elite on out (88%), corner (63.2%) and near-elite on dig/curl/slant. Harmon projects London for 1,300 yards on his medium projection (aggressive vs. his 905-yard 2023), even with Atlanta's overall WR target share still projected below league average, and flags the shallow WR depth chart (Mooney, Rondale Moore, Ray-Ray McCloud) as the risk to hitting the 11-personnel usage he's betting on. Discussion also touched Kirk Cousins fitting play-action well under Zimmer and rookie Michael Penix Jr. sitting behind him.
- 2024-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-25]]): London was used as a pure boundary X in Arthur Smith's offense with almost no positional versatility, because Atlanta had nobody else who could play outside — a problem both hosts say still is not solved. Harmon's bull case is a scheme fit: per Fantasy Points data, [[Kirk Cousins]] led all quarterbacks with a 150.6 passer rating on in-routes and deep and mid-level crossers in 2023, and had the most attempts and completions on those routes in 2022 — exactly what should become London's bread and butter. He sketches alignments where [[Darnell Mooney]] runs vertically outside and [[Kyle Pitts]] works off or on the line so London can in-cut. Koh expects a great statistical year and loves the quarterback-receiver fit, while conceding the surrounding room is one of the ten worst in the NFL.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-12]]): after a 3-target, 2-catch, 15-yard Week 1, Harmon separates deployment from outcome and says he is 'not worried about Drake London' — he loved the usage and calls him a great player. Charting: over 60% of snaps off the line of scrimmage with frequent motion and slot reps, versus 80% outside alignment in 2023 down to 70% in Week 1, and 38% of routes were posts, digs or slants rather than the 2023 curl diet. Harmon says he got open consistently against Pittsburgh's zone but windows close fast against a good secondary when [[Kirk Cousins]] will not plant on his back foot and rip it. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is more agitated, calling London 'a certified stud' and demanding [[Zac Robinson]] scheme him targets even into contested coverage.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): London and [[Kyle Pitts]] are in for a long season, and Waldman framed it as a [[Kirk Cousins]] problem rather than a talent problem. Atlanta's offense is operating almost entirely in the middle of the field, and Cousins is not throwing the back-shoulder boundary balls that are London's and Pitts's best trait — Waldman called both two of the better back-shoulder boundary players to come out of college football in their respective draft classes, and said Atlanta has never really liked to feature them that way or had a quarterback to do it. He watched the last two games and says Cousins still is not delivering with the velocity required. On longer-developing concepts Cousins goes happily to [[Darnell Mooney]] and Ray-Ray McCloud, with whom he has better rapport. Defenses are taking London and Pitts away and making Cousins go elsewhere. Waldman said this is really a [[Michael Penix Jr.]] discussion — until Penix plays, he does not expect the two to be featured. He was careful to add they are not bad players; they are misfit and unfeatured. The scheme itself, he conceded, is working fine.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): asked whether fantasy players put too much stock into London and [[Kyle Pitts]] or whether circumstances hold them back, Waldman said it can be both, and pinned it on the quarterback. Kirk Cousins is 'the anti-Josh Allen' — no wheels, will not prolong plays, thinks he has Allen's arm but does not, and crucially does not trust-throw. London and Pitts are both trust-throw, contested-catch targets, so Cousins instead feeds the speedsters and plays winning football within a scheme built to protect him from what he cannot do. Waldman's conditional: if Michael Penix Jr. ever finds himself in a game, targets for these two would suddenly elevate.

- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-04]]): London's 14-target, 9-catch, 118-yard, 3-touchdown game versus New England was one of the best receiver games of the season, and Harmon says he should never leave a game without 10 targets. His supporting stat: in the five of [[Michael Penix Jr.]]'s ten career starts with an adjusted yards per attempt over 7.0, London's target totals were 18, 16, 15, 10 and 14; the low-target games are the bad ones. Harmon says a fourth-year London is at peak form, dominant against every coverage — 'screw separation, that guy's open' — dunking on Christian Gonzalez downfield and feasting on slot mismatches against Marcus Jones. All three of Atlanta's touchdowns came against man coverage, which Reception Perception charting says London beats. Atlanta still lost on a missed extra point, and Harmon keeps his broader critiques of the coaching staff.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06]]): the New England game may have unlocked the obvious. Waldman notes [[Michael Penix Jr.]]'s great strength is fades and jump balls at every depth, London's great strength is going up and taking the ball, and against the Patriots all three London touchdowns were fade-type routes, plus a fourth fade where London worked back inside to cut off an off-platform throw. Angelo says London could be a top-five NFL receiver, does off-target plucking and rebounding better than almost anyone, beats you at all three levels with body control and route-stem positioning, and has a frame that is domineering to defensive backs in the low red zone. Angelo's line is that Atlanta must make him option one — he should never see fewer than 10 targets a game — and wants him moved around and used from the slot for mismatches, the way he thinks Houston should be using [[Nico Collins]]. Waldman's caveat: whether it repeats over the next two games, or was a one-off.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-06]]): London is 'on a complete heater right now' and Harmon takes him over 70.5 receiving yards (-114) against Indianapolis, reasoning that the Colts have been very beatable by wide receivers all season and that Indianapolis is likely to put up a big number, forcing Atlanta to chase. The stated risk is the newly acquired Sauce Gardner — Harmon is unsure how much Gardner plays immediately or whether he travels with London. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) expects Gardner on the field for man coverage right away and thinks he probably does track London quite a bit, calling it a slightly risky bet but one he likes overall since zone coverage requires more feel and practice reps to absorb.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): 'we're starting Drake London without fail' — six catches, 104 yards and a touchdown against the Colts, with the feared Sauce Gardner effect never materialising. Harris disputes Kurt Warner's on-air explanation (a broadcaster, not tracked) that Indianapolis's zone usage was what protected London; Harris says he likes Warner but thinks he is simply wrong, because Atlanta made it easy by motioning London off the outside where Gardner exclusively plays. He counted it happening easily more than a dozen times: London aligns outside, Gardner walks over tight to him, London motions away pre-snap and becomes someone else's problem. London's touchdown was a wheel route on a linebacker, which Harris asks how anyone can call good coverage scheming.
