- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: his most overrated back of the 2025 class — though he still ranks Henderson RB2 in the class, so the objection is miscasting rather than talent. The public sees a [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] / [[De'Von Achane]] blue-chip play-speed type; Angelo says he is really a plus-athlete version of [[Kyren Williams]] coming out of Notre Dame: three-down reliability, good pass protection, a genuine receiver, able to hit the home run, but without an elite through-contact or movement toolbox. He notes Todd McShay chatter about a first-round selection and thinks GMs will pay for the three-down profile. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed and framed the archetype as a pyramid — [[Tony Pollard]] at the top, Kyren Williams in the middle, [[DeAndre Swift]] at the bottom — placing Henderson above Swift but short of Pollard, because he can stay on schedule but, in Angelo's phrase, has no escape hatch when a play breaks down.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'fuck it' on Henderson at Underdog RB22, taking [[James Conner]] at RB23 instead — 'James Conner all the way'. Waldman grants Henderson is a good young back and fast, but says the market treating him as '[[Jahmyr Gibbs]] part two' is ridiculous and that he will be 'frought-ly overvalued' because people assume he will be unleashed for a zillion opportunities. His own comp is closer to what people hoped [[DeAndre Swift]] could become, and he specified that the football-analysis crowd, not the fantasy crowd, is the group carrying that view — with the caveat that he thinks Swift has a lot of holes in his game and Henderson can do schematically what teams want only in a limited way. Untracked co-host Bob Harris reported most people he hears consider Henderson the class's RB2 and 'it's not even close', over the [[Omarion Hampton]] diehards.
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman likes but does not love Henderson — competent between the tackles when the play is straightforward, but he loses balance and struggles to pick the right tool when he has to react to a defender on interior cuts. In space and as a receiver he is much better, and Waldman places him at the top of a [[DeAndre Swift]] archetype (with Jerick McKinnon at the bottom), with the risk being that an NFL team mistakes him for an every-down back the way teams have with Swift. He accepts a partial Tevin Coleman comp on the gap-versus-zone issue but thinks Henderson is better in space. His board range is wide: as high as fourth or fifth among running backs, theoretically as low as 18th. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) calls him middle-of-the-road on traits but the number-three prospect in his model — 62nd percentile career adjusted production, 66th percentile PFF, 54th percentile Zierlein at 6.38 — second without draft capital, third behind [[Omarion Hampton]] with it, and he could climb to number two with a sub-4.45 forty.

- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]): Waldman says he is coming around on Henderson after being cooler on him in an earlier conversation, and the place he sees for him is 'the deluxe [[DeAndre Swift]] package'. - 2025-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-30]]): from the first-ever Reception Perception running back charting of the top 12 incoming backs, Henderson was very strong in man/gap and literally the worst runner Harmon charted in zone. - 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Henderson fits the weapon-back role in a receiver room full of slot types, but Angelo was careful to bound the comp — asked repeatedly during his rookie series whether Henderson is [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] or [[De'Von Achane]], his answer is no: not as creative a runner as those two, and not their equal as a route runner.
