# TreVeyon Henderson

- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: his most overrated back of the 2025 class — though he still ranks Henderson RB2 in the class, so the objection is miscasting rather than talent. The public sees a [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] / [[De'Von Achane]] blue-chip play-speed type; Angelo says he is really a plus-athlete version of [[Kyren Williams]] coming out of Notre Dame: three-down reliability, good pass protection, a genuine receiver, able to hit the home run, but without an elite through-contact or movement toolbox. He notes Todd McShay chatter about a first-round selection and thinks GMs will pay for the three-down profile. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed and framed the archetype as a pyramid — [[Tony Pollard]] at the top, Kyren Williams in the middle, [[DeAndre Swift]] at the bottom — placing Henderson above Swift but short of Pollard, because he can stay on schedule but, in Angelo's phrase, has no escape hatch when a play breaks down.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'fuck it' on Henderson at Underdog RB22, taking [[James Conner]] at RB23 instead — 'James Conner all the way'. Waldman grants Henderson is a good young back and fast, but says the market treating him as '[[Jahmyr Gibbs]] part two' is ridiculous and that he will be 'frought-ly overvalued' because people assume he will be unleashed for a zillion opportunities. His own comp is closer to what people hoped [[DeAndre Swift]] could become, and he specified that the football-analysis crowd, not the fantasy crowd, is the group carrying that view — with the caveat that he thinks Swift has a lot of holes in his game and Henderson can do schematically what teams want only in a limited way. Untracked co-host Bob Harris reported most people he hears consider Henderson the class's RB2 and 'it's not even close', over the [[Omarion Hampton]] diehards.
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman likes but does not love Henderson — competent between the tackles when the play is straightforward, but he loses balance and struggles to pick the right tool when he has to react to a defender on interior cuts. In space and as a receiver he is much better, and Waldman places him at the top of a [[DeAndre Swift]] archetype (with Jerick McKinnon at the bottom), with the risk being that an NFL team mistakes him for an every-down back the way teams have with Swift. He accepts a partial Tevin Coleman comp on the gap-versus-zone issue but thinks Henderson is better in space. His board range is wide: as high as fourth or fifth among running backs, theoretically as low as 18th. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) calls him middle-of-the-road on traits but the number-three prospect in his model — 62nd percentile career adjusted production, 66th percentile PFF, 54th percentile Zierlein at 6.38 — second without draft capital, third behind [[Omarion Hampton]] with it, and he could climb to number two with a sub-4.45 forty.
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman does not qualify Henderson as the top of this running back class even though most people do. His projection is a [[DeAndre Swift]] / [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] type role — the 'A back' who gets put in space and wins matchups when the quarterback pulls defenders elsewhere, rather than a runner you trust between the tackles unless the play is perfectly blocked. Waldman allowed he could be a little better or a little worse than those comps depending entirely on where he lands.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]): Waldman says he is coming around on Henderson after being cooler on him in an earlier conversation, and the place he sees for him is 'the deluxe [[DeAndre Swift]] package'. Measured against [[Ashton Jeanty]] as the standard for knowing how to run the football, he grades Henderson about two-thirds of the way there — but argues that does not matter much because teams will know exactly what they have and limit him to the things he does well. Specifically: very fast, very good movement, will deliver the yardage between the tackles when the play is blocked up, and catches the ball reasonably well, with some things still to work on. Waldman is comfortable with a defined role of that shape.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: Henderson is his pick at 1.02 over [[Quinshon Judkins]] and [[Omarion Hampton]] — he profiles as the one who catches the most balls, so he has the highest range of outcomes from a fantasy standpoint, his role should be clear from day one, and as a good pass protector he can be on the field for all three downs in the right offense. Angelo gave Hampton the lowest floor of the three if the second-level processing is not fixed. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed Henderson is the instant-impact choice while preferring Judkins as a talent, and Angelo added the caveat that a high touch count for Hampton or Judkins is hard to cash in if their offense rarely reaches the red zone, where Henderson's reception volume is more format-proof.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): Denver is his favorite running back landing spot and Henderson is the name that comes to mind at Sean Payton's middle-first pick, because Payton has been itching for his [[Alvin Kamara]] — the 'joker' who wins on angle and option routes, pass protects, and creates explosives. Angelo notes [[Audric Estime]] already fills the secondary Mark Ingram role, so what Payton lacks is the playmaker who stresses a defense in a different dimension the way Payton's best Saints offenses did with Drew Brees and Kamara. He adds that day-two and day-three backs in this class ([[Brashard Smith]] among them) could grow into the role, so Denver need not spend the first-rounder to fill it.
- 2025-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-30]]): from the first-ever Reception Perception running back charting of the top 12 incoming backs, Henderson was very strong in man/gap and literally the worst runner Harmon charted in zone. Harmon presents that as a strong schematic fit rather than a red flag, because Josh McDaniels' New England offense should run a heavy dose of man/gap. He offers this as the clearest example of his takeaway from the project — that you cannot know which schemes a back is actually comfortable in without charting it, and that the charting only becomes useful when paired with what the landing-spot offense actually runs.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Henderson fits the weapon-back role in a receiver room full of slot types, but Angelo was careful to bound the comp — asked repeatedly during his rookie series whether Henderson is [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] or [[De'Von Achane]], his answer is no: not as creative a runner as those two, and not their equal as a route runner. What he does have is what Angelo calls the running back trifecta: high-end top-end play speed, good acceleration, and great hands, plus quality pass protection — three things he says keep a back relevant regardless of size. The value is that Henderson can stress a backer or safety on the third level from a detached alignment, and New England is devoid of explosive playmakers. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed while noting [[Rhamondre Stevenson]] remains a very good interior runner, angle-route runner and screen player [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-15]]): Henderson earned a ranks bump on joint-practice reports — the speed everyone hoped would translate looks like it will, with repeated reports of him breaking long runs. Harris does not think Henderson immediately, or maybe ever, dominates the work share, but moved him to RB28, 77th overall in standard and 75th in PPR, while already ranking him ahead of [[Rhamondre Stevenson]]. He notes his rank is now closing on the overall ADP of 59, and that he has much less of a problem with someone reaching a little beyond his rank. Cousin Josh (untracked co-host) separately said Henderson is a rookie back you can sell him on, unlike [[Kaleb Johnson]].
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: taking Henderson in the third round would be a bad idea — the fifth or sixth is the right price. Waldman expects a timeshare with [[Rhamondre Stevenson]], who is not the explosive speedster Henderson is but is a good receiver, a smart runner with good footwork and power, and better at mitigating losses behind a line that is not yet good. Henderson will blow through a tight crease if given one, but is not good at avoiding multiple penetration points to get back to the line of scrimmage. His projection: 900-1,000 total yards and six to eight touchdowns, with Stevenson near 900 rushing yards and still the goal-line/red-zone factor. [[Chris Harris]] notes Henderson's preseason hype has not yet moved his ADP off early fifth round (ADP 51) and that the Patriots have faced no defensive starters; Waldman dryly dismisses judging him off that tape.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): Henderson is Harris's number three 'might be wrong about'. He raised Henderson from where he had him entering August because the rookie has obviously been awesome throughout, and he says friends close to the organization love him — a fantastic player with a speed element who is scary to tackle. [[Rhamondre Stevenson]] is not going anywhere, but Harris notes a 60-40 split still leaves a good back fine. His hesitation is entirely the surroundings: he thinks the New England roster 'stinks', the offensive line probably still stinks despite trying a bunch of guys, and while [[Drake Maye]] is good, the backup situation is dire. So Harris will not pay a third-round ADP for him and will happily miss the boat. Ben Gretch (guest, NOT tracked) is definitely in: explosiveness pops off the charts on film and in the college data, plus a 100-yard preseason kick return, a touchdown run and receiving work — his bull case is that Henderson may already be the best skill player on the offense by a lot, an [[Alvin Kamara]]-type rookie. Both would rather he stopped returning kicks. [Redraft (Standard)]

Chris Harris framed it as a pure long shot rather than a prediction, and both agreed New England's roster is otherwise thin.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Henderson had arguably the best camp and exhibition season of any running back, and this was the July storyline that most obviously resolved and moved the market. - 2025-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest Andy Behrens (not a tracked expert) has Henderson high in his Week 1 ranks, consistent with an all-aboard offseason trend — he took him in the second round of a 16-team keeper league (functionally a third-round price) after already keeping [[Malik Nabers]] and [[Ricky Pearsall]], leaving him only shopping running backs with his first pick. Behrens says Henderson is a player he lands 'almost everywhere' against normal drafters but never gets against fantasy analysts, where he will not fall outside roughly the top 28 picks. - 2025-09-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-15]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman splits his verdict — Henderson is overrated in redraft this year but is someone you want to invest in for dynasty, with a strong chance to develop into a lead back he compared to [[Tony Pollard]].
