# Isaac TeSlaa

## Expert Takes

- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): plug the Arkansas receiver into an offense with two or three veteran receivers and a good tight end and he can earn intermediate and vertical mismatches against linebackers, safeties and nickelbacks who cannot handle his size and explosion. Waldman names this the 'Claypool-Galladay production privilege' — the same setup that briefly worked for [[Chase Claypool]] alongside [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]] and Diontae Johnson, and for Kenny Golladay alongside Marvin Jones Jr. and Golden Tate — and warns it is temporary, because defenses eventually make the athletic receiver the focus and production dips unless he has added more than playing the NFL's version of catch in the park. To even earn that privilege TeSlaa must tighten his positioning against defenders on vertical boundary routes and his attack of the ball; to become a star he needs much better man-to-man route running, with more precision and snap in his breaks. See [[Claypool-Galladay Production Privilege]].
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): asked whether TeSlaa could be the next Kenny Golladay or [[Chase Claypool]], Waldman says yes — and means it as a warning, not a compliment. If he lands on a team with two good receivers and gets played inside, he is 'a big boy with a good catch radius' who runs well after the catch with excellent athletic ability, and will win matchups against linebackers and safeties. Waldman's predicted arc: he blows up for the first five to seven games, fantasy players call him an undiscovered gem, then defenses roll coverage his way and you never hear from him again. The escape hatch is self-awareness — TeSlaa can get better if he recognizes the early success was 'propped up' and that he has not made it.
- 2025-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-16]]): a curiosity case from the day-three pile — almost 6'4 and 214 pounds but a slot-only player, unproductive at Arkansas (under 1,000 combined yards over his last two seasons) after dominating Division II Hillsdale College before transferring. Harmon calls the athletic comparables 'the most insane you've ever seen' — Javon Walker, Allen Robinson, [[Rome Odunze]], Andre Johnson — and says he likes some of what will appear on the Reception Perception profile, while hedging that he is sure TeSlaa 'will do nothing in the NFL'.
- 2025-04-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-22]]): TeSlaa is a 'freak show athlete' who is still very raw at the position — a former wing-T quarterback in high school who came to receiver late, which shows on film. In his Reception Perception sample he took roughly 70% of snaps in the slot, but at 6-4, 214 with an Andre Johnson comparison Harmon thinks he can play some outside too, so he declined to name him the class's slot-only receiver. Harmon also likes him as a deep sleeper alongside Arkansas teammate [[Andrew Armstrong]], and argued both were underutilized and under-discussed because Arkansas's offensive line under Bobby Petrino was poor enough that the quarterbacks were constantly under duress and receivers were forced to redirect routes and scramble.
- 2025-04-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-29]]): Detroit trading two third-rounders (102 and 226) to take TeSlaa 70th overall is 'a huge bet' — Harmon liked him as a day-three dart throw but calls this throwing the whole dart board. The profile is bizarre: a wing-T high school quarterback who dominated Division II Hillsdale, then produced almost nothing at Arkansas (under 900 yards across two seasons; 28 catches, 546 yards, 3 TDs in 10 games last year, 2.8 receptions per game), yet at 6-4, 214 with a 4.43 and a 39.5-inch vertical he has athletic comparables to Allen Robinson, [[Rome Odunze]], Javon Walker and Andre Johnson. Charting: 70.1% slot, 70% off the line, only 3.7% of routes against press, 66% vs man, 81.3% vs zone, and strong deep work — 66% on nine routes, 73.9% on posts, 78.6% on outs, plus a 19.2% contested target rate with an 80% catch rate and zero drops in the entire sample, and a broken tackle on 50% of in-space attempts. Harmon reports uniformly excellent work-ethic and football-character reviews. Neither host can figure out where he plays; they half-seriously float him as Detroit's [[Taysom Hill]] given the wing-T background, and note the [[Jameson Williams]] fifth-year option and trade rumors as context. James Koh (not tracked) is warier, saying Detroit's proof of concept for developing projects left with Ben Johnson and Antoine Randle El.
- 2025-05-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-26]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman says [[Isaac TeSlaa]] is not a value at a WR16-among-rookies ranking — he calls that a little high and says there are probably at least 20, maybe 25 receivers he would rather have, naming [[Chimere Dike]], [[Konata Mumpfield]], [[Elic Ayomanor]], [[Tai Felton]] and [[Isaiah Neyor]] as preferences. He also calls Detroit a bad fit. He tested and rejected the [[Chase Claypool]] / Kenny Golladay comparison: both of those breakouts came on offenses that barely used the tight end and had two good receivers drawing coverage so the third option could be matched on a linebacker or safety, and Detroit has [[Sam LaPorta]], [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and [[Jameson Williams]] already, with Williams the more refined player. He puts TeSlaa at best fourth or fifth in the target pecking order absent injury, in a third tier of rookie receivers. Long term, he likes the dynasty play; he doubts a rookie-year Golladay-style breakout.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Harmon wonders whether Detroit views TeSlaa as an X receiver, since there is no opening in the slot behind Amon-Ra St. Brown and [[Jameson Williams]]. He notes the Lions have repeatedly wanted a big body — they brought in [[Tim Patrick]] late last season and Allen Robinson late in the prior preseason — and that TeSlaa's athletic comps run to Robinson and similar larger receivers. The caveat is a big one: going from an Arkansas slot role to being Detroit's X as a rookie would be a substantial ask, and Patrick can hold the big-body job while TeSlaa develops. Koh (not tracked) calls him a crazy good athlete and an interesting sleeper in a target vacuum.
- 2025-06-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-06]]): with [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] possibly limited in camp after knee surgery, TeSlaa is one of the two Lions storylines Harmon is most interested in tracking — specifically whether Detroit views him as St. Brown's backup in the slot or as a perimeter wideout, since he had, in Harmon's words, 'a very interesting collegiate reception perception profile, to say the least.' No projection or ranking attached; framed purely as a role question worth watching through training camp.
- 2025-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-11]]): Harmon floats TeSlaa as a candidate for the sacrificial X role in Detroit — the on-the-line, farthest-guy-on-the-boundary job that would free [[Jameson Williams]] to play more flanker — on the basis of his size and athleticism, which Harmon compares to a big-bodied receiver like Allen Robinson, the archetype Detroit has repeatedly chased (Robinson himself, then [[Tim Patrick]]). The caveat is that TeSlaa has never actually played that position, having been almost a pure slot receiver at Arkansas, so Harmon thinks he too may profile best as a flanker-slot type. Koh calls the third-round pick, acquired by trading up, a big-time reach on a developmental player he expected to go in the fifth round; Harmon says the same and admits he had barely thought about TeSlaa since the draft, while conceding the draft capital means Detroit must have a plan.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): TeSlaa is a rookie riser — running around with the ones and filling the third receiver role in the Morton offense. Angelo was candid that this is probably not a lucrative fantasy role in 2025, but framed the medium-term case around Detroit's cap: with [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] paid and [[Jameson Williams]], Sam LaPorta and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] all due — Gibbs potentially at the richest running back contract in NFL history — someone gets squeezed, and TeSlaa playing well makes Detroit likelier to let Williams walk. He praised TeSlaa's size, blocking and athletic ability. [[Matt Waldman]] added that dropped between two or three good players, TeSlaa could produce a [[Chase Claypool]] or Kenny Golladay-style big season in a limited role, with the caveat that he must keep growing and not let production convince him he has arrived [Dynasty].
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: TeSlaa is a project worth tracking. Measurables are elite — 6-4, 215, 4.43 with a 39.5-inch vertical and 17 bench reps, comparable to Allen Robinson, [[Rome Odunze]] and Javon Walker — but production was minimal, under 600 receiving yards in his final Arkansas season after dominating at Hillsdale, and he was a high school wing-T quarterback. His charted alignment was odd for the athletic profile: 28% outside, 70.1% slot, 1.8% backfield, ~70% off the line of scrimmage, yet his best routes were posts, nines, corners and outs. Charting positives: 81.3% success rate vs zone (62nd percentile, small sample), an 80% contested catch rate, no drops at all at Arkansas, and good vision after the catch. Camp and preseason reports have him pushing [[Tim Patrick]] for the X role and blocking hard; Harmon shifted from expecting a pure redshirt to thinking he plays some. He caps it by noting TeSlaa would be roughly the fifth target behind [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[Jameson Williams]], [[Sam LaPorta]] and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) raised the Matt Jones bust comparison — athlete, not yet a receiver — which Harmon partly accepts on the route side while citing strong character reports.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): he is 'fucking feeling' that TeSlaa is overhyped this year. Waldman classifies him as a [[Claypool-Galladay Production Privilege]] type — a receiver who gets looks because of the surrounding talent when he is on the field — and the open question is whether he will be on the field at all, since he has not shown he beats one-on-one coverage other than in a straight line against backup corners. He allows that TeSlaa has made the plays a good college player should make and could become a better pro than Chase Claypool or Kenny Golladay, but says the path runs through injury to [[Jameson Williams]] or [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] forcing Detroit to plug him in, which he does not expect in 2025. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) agreed, holds [[Best Ball]] flyers purely for portfolio diversification, and said in redraft he is more likely to take [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]] — while conceding Waldman had just talked him down on Thornton too.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-29]]): TeSlaa becomes Detroit's WR3 after Tim Patrick was traded to Jacksonville for a sixth-rounder, and Harmon said he has 'really mattered a lot more than I expected' — he charted him purely off the athletic measurables despite no college production, and Detroit has converted him from a collegiate slot to a perimeter vertical X, which his prospect profile hinted at through his route-tree success signals. Charting: 62nd percentile success rate vs zone, 66.2% vs man (not a weakness, but below the prospect charting average), with a small man sample; the preseason man-coverage tape has been impressive. Harmon doubts he matters much for fantasy given target volume, though Dan Campbell's staff love his run-game physicality. James Koh (not tracked) countered that TeSlaa's real fantasy impact may be negative — siphoning designed touches from [[Jameson Williams]].
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-06]]): 'fuck it' on TeSlaa becoming a weekly fantasy play — not this year, though Waldman said next year, sure. He credited a nice catch and a touchdown where TeSlaa got open on a route over the middle, but classified him as a garbage-time play right now who will get occasional work on an unpredictable basis. Both hosts agreed he is only startable if forced there by byes and injuries. [Dynasty] The 'next year, sure' framing is the dynasty-relevant part of the take.
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-10-09]]): TeSlaa jumped to a 34% snap share and 54% of dropbacks with Kalif Raymond injured, drawing no first-half targets but staying on the field for 53% of routes. Harmon reads Detroit's intent as a big-bodied X — 81% of his snaps came out wide, versus 78% for [[Jameson Williams]] and 69% for [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] — after a pre-draft Reception Perception profile that showed heavy slot alignment but X-receiver route indications and better zone than man results. He thinks TeSlaa is the piece that lets Detroit move Jameson Williams around more, and that they should get him more involved. James Koh (not tracked) added that TeSlaa appears to need no scheming — Detroit runs its base offense and throws to him if he wins — which he contrasted sharply with the Ben Johnson era of designing early looks for Jamo. Harmon noted the RP data on him is mixed.
