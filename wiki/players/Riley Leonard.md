# Riley Leonard

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2025-04-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not tracked) with guest Cory Kinnan (not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-18]]): Koh argues Leonard has the most upside of any mid-round quarterback and says he feels like 'one of one' in believing the 6'4", 220 former higher-rated basketball recruit can actually throw — he just was never asked to at Notre Dame. Kinnan is torn: there are no big throws on the tape, Duke spammed mesh and Notre Dame turned him into a battering ram, and he wonders how much can be installed with him week to week, though he notes a beautiful slot fade for a touchdown against Ohio State. Kinnan's comp is Josh McCown — a freak athlete (99th percentile shuttle, 97th percentile vertical) who hangs around as a journeyman and finds spot starts; Harmon's best case is what people expected [[Bo Nix]] to be. Three-game charting: 86% curls, 82% slants, encouraging even if pigeonholed as a short-area option. Both float Denver behind Nix as an ideal landing spot.
- 2025-06-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-06]]): the sixth-round rookie is a live dark horse to get into the mix if [[Anthony Richardson]]'s shoulder lingers and [[Daniel Jones]] falters — 'Riley Leonard could get in the mix here too, why not?' Harmon calls him toolsy and super athletic, cut from the same cloth as the other two, and says that while Notre Dame did not ask him to make many throws, he was pretty good when asked. He cites a Reception Perception mini-sample charted by Corey Kinnan: 82% success rate on slants, nearly 86% on curls, 50% on the nine (collegiate average) and a 50% success rate on the dig, which Harmon calls 'horrible, not what you want to see.' Koh (not tracked) noted the slant/curl/nine profile lines up with what an outside X receiver runs, so Leonard's success would be north-south rather than on in-breakers.
- 2025-12-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-08]]): In his first career start replacing the injured [[Daniel Jones]], Harmon graded Leonard 'fine-ish for the expectations of a sixth-round rookie' - solid in the quick game, with the mobility (including a rushing touchdown) that made evaluators like Yahoo's Nate Tice tag him pre-draft as a 'dollar store Daniel Jones' sleeper. Harmon cautioned against overreading the tape either way since Jacksonville played a soft, two-high shell for most of the fourth quarter, but his overall takeaway was that the debut 'could have been worse.'
- 2025-12-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (untracked co-host) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-12-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Leonard's first career start, replacing the injured Daniel Jones vs Jacksonville, was 'passable' -- Harmon called it a 'dollar store Daniel Jones impression.' He was capable getting the ball out quickly in the short/quick game and scored on a designed run, but both hosts questioned whether he has the arm to push the ball downfield and outside the numbers to Alec Pierce. Harmon projected Michael Pittman Jr. to be 'peppered' with underneath zone-coverage targets in two-high looks while Pierce sees more single-high, deep-shot opportunities Leonard may struggle to hit. From having watched him at Notre Dame, Harmon said Leonard has 'zip' on the ball and interesting tools but is far from a complete passer, having run a scheme that leaned heavily on designed runs; whether Steichen's staff can develop him further is untested. Leonard's next start is on the road at a Seahawks defense that plays a lot of two-high, a tougher matchup than Week 14.
- 2026-01-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-26]]) [Dynasty]: fuck it -- Waldman does not think Leonard emerges as a quarterback of note for Indianapolis, dismissing the 'Golden Domer' pedigree bump some analysts give Notre Dame quarterbacks. He adds that Skylar Thompson remains in the Pittsburgh quarterback conversation as a lower-tier name worth tracking only in very deep leagues.
