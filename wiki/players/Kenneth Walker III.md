# Kenneth Walker III

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: "a great player" he plans to draft a little higher than market price in 2024. His mechanism is about the coach, not the depth chart: Pete Carroll's pattern is to keep "coveting" one lead back until he can't anymore, and right now that back is Walker, with Zach Charbonnet standing by. "That happens due to injury almost exclusively" — but Waldman is explicit he isn't scared off by that risk, wanting Walker's "explosive threat" upside as a running back two.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]), with Pete Carroll now out and new HC Mike McDonald in: Walker and Zach Charbonnet reaffirmed as "two pretty good backs," part of a Seattle offense Harmon calls talent-rich "top to bottom" — with the specific Pete-Carroll-driven usage pattern from the January take now an open question under new coaching.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman thinks [[Kenneth Walker III]] (RB16, round 7 on Underdog) and [[Zach Charbonnet]] (RB41, rounds 11-12) are both starting to slide in ADP and expects that to continue as new OC Ryan Grubb's pass-heavy, tempo-driven scheme -- imported from a Washington offense with one of the highest drop-back rates in the country -- gets more camp attention. He called Walker the more compelling player in space and the better receiving back of the two, but said his ceiling is capped by Charbonnet's presence and the new pass-heavy scheme, comparing his profile unfavorably at that price to Tank Dell, Alvin Kamara, Dalton Kincaid, Anthony Richardson, George Pickens and Keenan Allen, all of whom he thinks carry higher ceilings relative to draft cost.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-12]]): he is becoming an even bigger Kenneth Walker III fan and simply loves watching him play, while flagging the caveat that Walker has to fight through a lot to get what he gets — so how the Seahawks team performs around him matters to the output. Waldman offered no ranking or projection, framing it purely as an appreciation of the individual game. Harstad (NOT tracked) joked that he could guess Walker was a Waldman favorite without knowing anything, the way you can guess who a sub-4.4 runner appeals to.
- 2025-01-30 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-30]]: Klint Kubiak's arrival in Seattle should mean much more outside zone, which he considers a better fit for Walker, and he expects Kubiak to get the offensive line into far better shape than it was under Ryan Grubb. [[Matt Harmon]] adds a wrinkle that cuts the other way for the offense as a whole — he sees Walker as an under-center back, while [[Geno Smith]] is more of a shotgun quarterback, and he thought there was 'a little bit of an oil water effect' between those two preferences last season. Harmon expects Kubiak's under-center play-action and heavier personnel to suit Walker's profile, and reads the hire primarily as a run-game and protection fix.
- 2025-02-03 — According to co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-03]], echoed by [[Matt Waldman]]: Klint Kubiak's arrival as Seattle's offensive coordinator should transfer the significant early-season role he gave [[Alvin Kamara]] in New Orleans to Walker, whom the staff still seems to prefer over [[Zach Charbonnet]] — Harris called it a great one-two punch while cautioning not to overstate the case. Waldman agreed and twice stressed the qualifier: Seattle has to fix the offensive line, and that needs to be a priority this offseason. Framed alongside Waldman's view that the Seahawks now want to run the ball more to complement their defense.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: Walker is 'criminally underpriced' at Underdog RB18 and Waldman jumped in unprompted to feel him over [[Omarion Hampton]] at RB19 — the whole thesis is the Klint Kubiak offense, with Waldman pointing to what that scheme did for [[Alvin Kamara]] last year and telling listeners to 'pay attention to these coaching moves'. This is a scheme-fit call rather than a usage or talent re-rating, and the contrast he drew is with rookie backs whose fits are unknown before the draft.
- 2025-02-26 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-26]], relayed alongside [[Matt Harmon]]: Walker was not utilized the right way in 2024 and should thrive under new coordinator Clint Kubiak because Walker 'wants to bounce' and Kubiak's system will run far more outside zone — Koh noted Kubiak's father essentially founded outside zone. Harmon agreed with the fit and said reviving a run game that was terrible in 2024 was probably the biggest reason Kubiak was hired, adding that Seattle expects offensive line improvement from the system change alone. Harmon's framing: encourage the bounce rather than fight it.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): 'feel it in theory, fuck it in reality.' Walker is an excellent fit for Klint Kubiak's scheme — a very patient runner and one of the best in the league working the perimeter, which is exactly what Kubiak wants to run. The reality check is Seattle's offensive line: Walker tends to feel he has to create for the paucity of blocking and swings for the home run, whereas with [[Zach Charbonnet]] you know exactly what you're getting. With Charbonnet nipping at his heels, Waldman said it is very possible Charbonnet ends up the better fantasy asset of the two. He pointed listeners to his weekend fantasy notebook for the full Seattle backfield breakdown.
- 2025-03-18 — According to Bob Harris (untracked co-host) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]): Seattle hiring Clint Kubiak is a positive for Walker specifically, because of Kubiak's stated love of Walker's speed — an enthusiasm Bob Harris shares and has raised before. This comes inside a segment where [[Matt Waldman]] is otherwise negative on Seattle's free agency, arguing the team is overpaying for assets because its offensive-line-building philosophy has not worked since the Pete Carroll era.
- 2025-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) named Walker one of his favorite 2025 running back values while admitting a love-hate relationship with him — rush-to-rush he is inconsistent and gets stuffed frequently, but he is a home-run hitter, and last season he roughly doubled his previous best target share as a pass catcher. Zachariason likes the Klint Kubiak arrival in Seattle plus some offensive line investment, expects a more run-heavy attack (fine for Walker specifically, unlike a receiving back where you would want pass volume), and argues the target pie has to go somewhere: with [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]] projected as a clear-out perimeter runner, an aging [[Cooper Kupp]] and [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]], he still sees a 10-11% target share for Walker. Waldman did not push back on the profile.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]): the hosts openly disagree here. Waldman calls Seattle the most confusing backfield of his projection update and lands on a committee, citing reporting that the team may lean more on a split because of concerns about Walker's ability to stay healthy and how well [[Zach Charbonnet]] played — while conceding that a healthy, right Walker gives 'junior [[Christian McCaffrey]]-esque' vibes and that he is one of the league's best perimeter runners, a fit for Klint Kubiak's outside zone. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) says 'fuck that, ain't no split': Greg Bell of the Tacoma News Tribune told him this is a Walker featured role with Charbonnet spelling him, not a 1A/1B, and Mike Clay's numbers from 11 games together had Walker at 68% of carries and 14% of targets to Charbonnet's 19% and 8%, with Charbonnet averaging 6.5 fantasy points in those weeks. Harris stresses how consistently Kubiak praises Walker ('we're going to get the ball to our best runner and that's Walker'), notes only mild foot soreness, and says he is drafting and ranking Walker aggressively.
- 2025-08-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Walker remains a top-20 running back option for him despite the August noise around [[Zach Charbonnet]], though he flagged that Walker has missed a lot of August with a foot injury while Charbonnet has had a good camp. Harris said nobody outside the building knows the split, he is not taking that particular cheese off the plate yet, and he is listening for a real leak that names a starter — at which point he would absolutely have to change the ranks.
- 2025-09-03 — According to James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]] [Redraft (Standard)]: bold prediction that Walker finishes top five in scrimmage yards among running backs. Koh said people do not realize how hard Walker plays and runs, that his tackle-breaking was some of the best Koh charted in 2024, and that the style fits Klint Kubiak's outside zone scheme — health is the stated condition. [[Matt Harmon]] went further, saying a top-two or top-three fantasy finish is in the range of outcomes and that nobody is considering Walker in that conversation; he called Walker one of his favorite fourth-round picks and is bullish on the whole Seattle rushing ecosystem. Harmon's hesitation: across both the Pete Carroll and current staffs it has always been Walker until he gets hurt, with [[Zach Charbonnet]] shelved rather than mixed in to preserve him.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: both Harris and guest Andy Behrens (not a tracked expert) ranked Walker well ahead of [[Zach Charbonnet]] for Week 1 despite camp beat reports that Charbonnet took reps ahead of him, and both are comfortable being called rubes for it — Behrens: 'I'm just giving the edge to the guy that I think is the starter and the superior runner,' noting Walker practiced in full through the final preseason week. Harris compared the beat-report panic to noticing that [[Breece Hall]] is atop the Jets depth chart: analysts learn seven bullet points and recite them fast. Harris also pushed back on the assumption that a Kubiak/Shanahan-branded Seattle offense will be 75% run and produce a 2,000-yard rushing attack with [[Jalen Milroe]] goal-line scores — they signed a quarterback coming off 35 passing touchdowns and have a 100-catch receiver plus another with an all-time season on his resume, so 'they might throw it a little bit. It's not 1974.'
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): 'fuck it' on Walker returning ADP value, despite loving his game. Reasons: enough [[Zach Charbonnet]] mixed in (Waldman was 'super disappointed' by how much work Charbonnet got, and unsurprised by how good he looked), doubt about whether [[Sam Darnold]] is for real, and a team he expects to play from behind. He likes Klint Kubiak's scheme in theory but is not sure Seattle has the quarterback or offensive line experience yet. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) noted beat writers said Kubiak was a Walker guy from day one, but by mid-August Mike Macdonald was saying Walker might be the guy 'if you get on the damn field.' Waldman's practical read: owning both backs gives you a weekly RB1, but neither being the clear workhorse is the problem.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): this did not work out the way Harris expected to begin the year. Walker played 20 snaps to 29 for [[Zach Charbonnet]] and lost the goal-line touchdown to him. On a rewatch Harris thinks Walker was a little unlucky, since plays called for him got stuffed through no fault of his own and the same play then found an alley for Charbonnet. Guest **Sam Gold** (not tracked) agrees on the run film, describing classic outside zone with occasional fullback where Walker kept getting hit in the backfield in the first half, plus a screen game the Seahawks have been unable to block for a decade. Gold's one positive is that Seattle likes to split Walker into the slot and move him around, and with only [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] and an old [[Cooper Kupp]] on the roster he will vacuum up some short PPR catches that lift his floor. Harris's verdict: all we can do is be an eyeball, and it did not look good.
- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris says he was alarmed enough by Week 1 to do something he normally never does after one game — move [[Zach Charbonnet]] ahead of Walker in standard (27 to 29), though he kept Walker ahead in PPR (28 to 31). He and Sam Gold thought some of it was bad luck: Walker was repeatedly met in the backfield on plays that simply were not blocked, while any room that existed happened to fall to Charbonnet. Guest Dave Kluge (Football Guys, not a tracked expert) moved Walker down quite a bit and would start [[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]] or [[Jordan Mason]] over him; Harris would start [[Jordan Mason]] or [[Javonte Williams]] over Walker. Both say it is completely fine to rest Walker a week if you have a quality flex alternative, and both flag that the Steelers run defense may not be the intimidating matchup it is assumed to be after Week 1, while noting Seattle will not bring a Justin Fields-style rushing threat.
- 2025-09-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-12]]): Walker was deliberately excluded from the Overreaction Theater countdown, which the hosts jokingly named the Kenneth Walker memorial list, because the Seattle backfield had already been discussed at length all week on the show. Both Harris and guest cousin Josh (untracked) said Walker would otherwise have been high on both lists. Josh, who wrote Walker's Almanac blurb and was a huge fan, says he is still kind of in but that claiming it feels as great as it did when he wrote that blurb a month and a half ago would be a complete lie; Harris notes his own Week 2 rank of Walker drew an 'oh boy' reaction from Josh.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman leans Walker over [[Zach Charbonnet]] long term but calls the backfield volatile and week to week until it normalizes. His film read: against a Steelers defense missing four defensive starters, nearly all of Walker's carries were designed downhill and run to a side with a plus-one to plus-two box advantage, which let him make cutbacks and bounces at the first and second level, while Charbonnet was run mostly to the perimeter into even or minus-one boxes and was stuffed repeatedly. He noted the long-standing knock that Walker hunts the home run instead of hitting the hole, plus recurring health concerns, and cited a Field Gulls film piece making the Charbonnet-fit-in-Klint-Kubiak's-scheme case. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) relayed Seattle Times beat writer Bob Condotta saying Seattle may not have a plan beyond 'get a running attack going, however it happens' — no answer yet.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): a total reversal from Week 1. The snap count still favored [[Zach Charbonnet]] 35 to 23, but Walker went 13 carries for 105 yards to Charbonnet's 15 carries for 10, including a lightning-strike run around the left edge in the fourth quarter to put the game away. Harris's read is that this is genuinely a split job and not a run-heavy enough attack for both to see 20 touches, so there will be variance — but the game proved Walker 'should still in any given week be rated a little bit ahead of Charbonnet just for his play-to-play explosiveness.' Charbonnet is the better bet when blocking breaks well and near the goal line; Walker is the home-run hitter who wins you a week.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): New Orleans came in trying to stack the line against Walker, with the broadcast pointing out multiple snaps where six defenders stood right at the line of scrimmage — which is exactly why [[Sam Darnold]] threw early and often. Walker still scored twice: on the first, he got no blocking at all and 'somehow muscled through like three guys' from short range; on the second, after a Darnold deep post to [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] set it up, he banged it in again at the goal line. The Seattle starters sat the entire fourth quarter of a blowout.
- 2025-09-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: ahead of the Thursday night Seattle game Harris said he split Walker quite a bit higher than [[Zach Charbonnet]] for Week 4, partly because of Charbonnet's injury and uncertain availability (mixed messages at recording time), but also because he simply thinks Walker is the better player and is discounting Week 1. Asked for a one-word answer on whether he would rather use Walker, guest Ben Gretch (Stealing Signals, not tracked) said yes. Harris had few other Seahawks questions for the game and noted DraftKings had Walker at plus-110 for an anytime touchdown.
- 2025-09-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-26]]): Walker has maintained a clear lead in the Seattle backfield, but Thursday was not a great night — too much bouncing and too many runs losing yardage, including a spin move that went backwards five yards. When Seattle actually blocked for him he looked very fast and powerful. He threw the ball at a tackler for an unsportsmanlike penalty that took Seattle out of field goal range, which is why he was effectively benched until halftime and [[Zach Charbonnet]] was in for the first touchdown. Harris also flags that Walker flat-out dropped a dump-off, contrasting him unfavourably with [[Trey Benson]]'s reliable hands.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Harris and guest **Sam Gold** (NOT a tracked expert) both prefer Walker to [[Zach Charbonnet]] and both say so explicitly. Gold's numbers: Walker 4.1 rush yards over expected per attempt versus minus-1.1 for Charbonnet, 22% missed tackle rate versus 17%, and a 38% breakaway rate versus 16% — 'a clear difference between these guys.' He'd much rather have Walker long term but concedes Charbonnet will keep stealing touchdowns because Walker runs so hard he gets gassed and comes off, which is exactly how Charbonnet's goal-line score happened, right after an electric 31-yard Walker run. Harris says he doesn't really believe in advanced metrics but likes it when they match his eyeballs — 'it's confirmation bias, that's all I want.' Gold also thinks Walker has better vision, while faulting him for trying to bounce too hard.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): Walker had good early success including an arrow route out of the backfield for a big third-and-five gain, but ran into 'buzzard's luck' — stopped at the one from the three and again from the one, after which Seattle put [[A.J. Barner]] under center for a tush push. Snap count was 26 Walker to 18 [[Zach Charbonnet]], with both sitting late in the blowout. Harris calls it 'a real split', one of his personal biggest surprises of the season, noting Charbonnet got the in-close work in Week 7 and Walker got it here without succeeding: 'feels like you can use either one, but neither guarantees you much'.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): Walker started against Arizona, the first play was a throw to him, he was tackled and immediately came out for [[Zach Charbonnet]]. Harris will not draw conclusions from a blowout, 'but I'm using my eyeballs - there just isn't a lot of deference paid to Walker, which is why I really do think the people who are continuing to rank them every week with a big distance between them are just generally incorrect'. Variance can strike in any given week, but he treats the two as used almost interchangeably.
