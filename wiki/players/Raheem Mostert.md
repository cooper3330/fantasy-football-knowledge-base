# Raheem Mostert

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-01 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]] [Best Ball]: names his own strategy after Mostert's 2023 season (18 total touchdowns at age 31 as Miami's lead back) — draft the cheaper half of a backfield committee rather than the presumed lead. Was buying Mostert shares a round or two behind De'Von Achane in 2023 best-ball drafts (roughly 10th–11th round) and plans to keep doing it in 2024 if the price stays cheap.
- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode [Redraft (Standard)]: corroborates as a value running back two for 2024, "maybe even lower" — expects Mostert to cede more early-down work to Achane and settle into a complementary role similar to what Jeff Wilson Jr. held in 2023.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: Miami's $9M extension through 2025 confirms Mostert will play a central role in 2024, complicating [[De'Von Achane]]'s touch outlook. Says his week-to-week role "is up and down for him and it's gonna remain up and down for him to an extent" — but volume is a big deal for RBs, and Waldman has personally won leagues sitting on cheap Mostert shares (round 8, RB27 ADP this cycle) until the volume opportunity showed up.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo projects Mostert, who turns 32 by the end of next season, is playing his last usable year as a lead option in Miami, with the team wanting to keep [[De'Von Achane]] fresh and [[Jaylen Wright]] positioned to become the backfield's touch leader by next season.
- 2024-08-05 — According to Bob Harris (co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]: Mostert in the seventh or eighth round is far more appealing than [[De'Von Achane]] in the second, because he does not think Mostert goes away — while conceding that if at all possible the younger player gets moved in front of him. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed on role: he expects Mostert or another back to take the north-south work while Achane handles east-west open-space touches, though he thinks the workload split will be closer than it was the previous season.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): Mostert is slept on. He remains a capable runner with a defined role that is not only goal-line work — Waldman expects closeout opportunities, and thinks Miami will ride the hot hand with him early in games because he is still fast, which suits their offense. Comparing the two backs, he calls Mostert less creative than [[De'Von Achane]] but savvier, and says that relative to what each cost, Mostert has the better chance of returning his investment from this point in the season while Achane would need an incredible run to justify second-round capital.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): on Pete Carroll's minicamp comment that Las Vegas is not a one-man backfield, the 'Mostert is the lightning to Jeanty's thunder' framing is right in theory and is what the staff wants — but the practical objection is availability: when did Mostert last play more than 14 games? Waldman projects roughly 80 carries plus about 25 receptions, 600–700 total yards at most, and sees him as a plausible red-zone specialist good for five or six touchdowns without capping [[Ashton Jeanty]]'s double-digit scoring. He also flags Sincere McCormick and, situationally, Zamir White as reasons the split could become two- or three-headed rather than a clean 1A/1B.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Best Ball]: he is feeling Mostert at RB69 (223rd overall) because he believes Pete Carroll 'just enough' that there will be some kind of split — not 50-50, but enough that he expects [[Ashton Jeanty]] nearer 1,000-1,100 rushing yards than 1,300-1,400. And if wrong, Mostert is by far the best back in the room after Jeanty: old but still very fast, can catch, and two years removed from an 18-touchdown top-five running back season. Bob Harris (not tracked) explicitly disagreed on the split — Carroll picks his guy and rides him everywhere he has been, keeping a good backup for when the guy is down, and Harris cited Vinny Bonsignore expecting a 275-to-300-touch desired plan plus Chip Kelly's history of heavy back workloads (Carlos Hyde, LeSean McCoy). Both agreed the price is right regardless.
