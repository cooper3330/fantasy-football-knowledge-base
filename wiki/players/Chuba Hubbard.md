# Chuba Hubbard

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]) [Best Ball]: feeling Hubbard as Carolina's de facto starter at his RB39 ADP, noting he's improved as a decision-maker every year and was clearly the better performer than [[Miles Sanders]] from game one, with the Panthers' upgraded guard play helping both blocking phases.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): Waldman says Hubbard has improved every year from Mike Gundy's Oklahoma State offense through Carolina and got notably better as last season went on — a more mature player with real upside right off the bat. He projects the Carolina backfield splitting the better part of 1,200-1,300 rushing yards, with Hubbard knocking on 700 and [[Jonathan Brooks]] taking roughly 640 mostly later in the year. The caveat is the offensive line, which Waldman thinks has the potential to provide the support both backs need. Guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) likes what Hubbard has done the last two seasons but considers Brooks clearly the better player.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): Waldman said Hubbard holds the Carolina job this season — he is 'playing too well' and knows the system, so [[Jonathan Brooks]] gets eased in rather than handed the role while recovering from an ACL tear. Waldman's stated caveat is owner David Tepper, an unpredictable wild card who could force a change independent of performance.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): Hubbard ran 28 times against the Giants — the highest carry total of his career including college — looked fantastic, and signed a four-year extension Waldman pegs around $30-plus million. Bob Harris (not tracked) cited Kyle Brandt's 'angry runs' segment; Waldman agrees this was an enraged runner. On the contract, Waldman argues it looks worse than it is: Carolina carries roughly $165 million of dead cap by the time it matures, there is an out after two years, and the deal is a drop in the bucket. The optics are bad for [[Jonathan Brooks]] investors but the structure is friendly and leaves room for two good backs.
- 2024-11-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]]): Hubbard has been one of the best and most efficient backs in the league this season, full stop — Harmon says if you scoff at the extension that is a separate team-building conversation, one he is actually willing to defend, but the on-field production is not in question. He lists Hubbard alongside Carolina's rookie pass catchers and a legitimately good offensive line as evidence the Panthers now have real players around [[Bryce Young]], which he treats as part of why Young looks better post-benching.
- 2024-12-12 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-12]]): raised as a comp for [[Chase Brown]] — a genuine NFL starting running back who has earned the role, with Angelo noting Dave Canales's staff doesn't want to take him off the field. With the [[Jonathan Brooks]] injury, Hubbard is 'going to be the guy in that offense', and Waldman adds that his extension 'looks a little smarter in injury hindsight now' — Angelo: '100% does.'
- 2024-12-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-23]]): he 'feels it' on Hubbard's steady, substantial improvement being a genuine rarity. At Oklahoma State Hubbard was a gap/draw/screen runner who 'needed a compass' if the crease wasn't there; he has since become a far more nuanced runner, improving visibly every season, to the point that by last year it was clear he was better than free-agent import [[Miles Sanders]] — and he has now earned the big contract. Waldman's key point is that most improvement in the NFL is singular (he cites [[Josh Allen]]'s accuracy, or backs learning to pass block); Hubbard improved multiple facets, going from good athlete to genuinely good technician at a high-processing position where many backs lean on instinct to their own detriment. Getting learned technique down to the speed of instinct earns Waldman's kudos. He also noted [[Jonathan Brooks]]'s second ACL tear clears Hubbard's path.
- 2025-01-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-27]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: emphatically 'fuck that' on Hubbard being a one-year wonder — the guy is good and has gotten better every year, can do it all at this stage, and will be the main guy going forward barring injury. He expects [[Bryce Young]]'s improved second half to bring balance that makes life easier for Hubbard, and does not expect [[Jonathan Brooks]] back. Bob Harris (not tracked) added reporting: Mike Kay of the Charlotte Observer indicated the staff loves Hubbard's violent running style as a fit for the offense, and the running game is central to how Dave Canales turns quarterbacks around — in Tampa Bay they ran it a ton and poorly, [[Rachaad White]] getting 270-plus carries. The catch both agree on: Hubbard will not be good value in 2025 drafts, you will pay a premium, and they expect a fifth- or sixth-round rookie back to be worth handcuffing to him.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-15]]): Hubbard is flag player number 4 — waiting until late in the fourth for him is nearly two full rounds of value. Harris says he was not complimentary about Hubbard early and explains why: the surrounding years gave him no space (post-[[Christian McCaffrey]] rookie work, the 2-15 [[Bryce Young]] season, bad offensive lines). Under Dave Canales in 2024 he got room and delivered an RB13 season, sitting out the last two games with calf and knee injuries after a 165-scrimmage-yard game he capped with an overtime touchdown, which Harris reads as the Panthers keeping their powder dry rather than a real fade. He calls Hubbard big, strong, nasty, a better mover than expected with decent speed and strong lateral agility who does not slow down to cut, and thinks the [[Rico Dowdle]] signing was about [[Jonathan Brooks]] tearing another ACL, not about replacing Hubbard. He has him RB14 versus an RB18 ADP, and argues the deeper cause is the market systematically pushing RB2s too late.
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Hubbard is his true round-four answer — he has him in the early third in his own ranks but says you never need to pay that, so grab him anytime in the fourth. Harris calls him the John Wick of the NFL in his Almanac because the Panthers kept throwing bodies at him ([[Miles Sanders]], [[Jonathan Brooks]]) and none of them stuck. Guest Scott Pianowski (Yahoo, not tracked) makes him his round-four safe pick: 2024 workload was very consistent even through a horrible start while the Canales offense and [[Bryce Young]] settled in, and he thinks the market misunderstands the [[Rico Dowdle]] addition — Carolina will not run Hubbard into the ground but he keeps a consistent touch and target share and is their goal-line back, on a team ready to be a competitive 6-11 to 8-9 outfit rather than a laughingstock. Harris separately named him one of his 'flag players' for 2025.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Hubbard is a flag player and one of his three most-drafted backs, appearing on three teams. He paid a second-round pick in Large, a 16-team keeper league where enough running backs are kept to create real scarcity, but in normal drafts got Hubbard in the late third and early fourth. Harris argues the discount is less about anyone thinking Hubbard is a fraud and more about the market 'perpetually misreading the importance of the positions you draft' — his rule is that you draft the scarce position first, not last, and running back is the slimmest and scariest market there is. He was 'very happy' with those late-third and early-fourth prices.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): Hubbard — whom Harris had flagged as a risky pick and calls the far more important of the two 'flag players' in this game because of draft cost — was the main Panther who played well early, with chunk gains that combined with a couple of well-timed [[Bryce Young]] scrambles to sustain drives. Then in the third quarter he inexplicably disappeared for back-to-back series in favour of [[Rico Dowdle]], and Harris wondered whether he had missed an injury. Hubbard returned down 20 with seven minutes left and it apparently was not anything, then scored a garbage-time screen touchdown where he made a man miss and took it to the house.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): Harris flagged an 'uh-oh kind of moment' for the first time. He had been arguing Hubbard is a flag player and still wants that to be right — 'I'm still happy to have Chuba Hubbard' and 'I'm not looking for trouble where there might not be any' — but watching the film, [[Rico Dowdle]] is who got the offense going in the second quarter, and in the fourth quarter, with the game not yet salted away, Carolina rode Dowdle for a string of carries down to the five. Hubbard was solid on the second drive and caught a pass down to the one, but it was Dowdle who came back in to score the icing touchdown.
- 2025-10-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-03]]): the leaks have already arrived on Hubbard's calf injury and he will not play in Week 5. Harris connects it backwards — [[Rico Dowdle]] had already been taking a suspiciously bigger share of the workload the previous couple of weeks, so it is now easier to guess Hubbard was being babied, which 'hopefully explains only so-so results.' Harris prefaces the whole injury segment by saying this is the least value he or anyone can add, because everyone is transcribing the same sanctioned NFL leaks and nobody can legislate usage in advance.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-06]]): Waldman keeps Hubbard as the job holder when healthy, and if forced to choose he leans Hubbard over [[Rico Dowdle]] as the better pro because of the extra juice — more speed than Dowdle, better in the open field, and he would have turned some of the 50-plus-yard creases Dowdle hit into house calls. He is also a tough inside runner who has really improved his game and gotten smart about how to hit creases; Waldman said that in college he would have taken Dowdle hands down, so this is a reversal driven by pro development. Bob Harris (not tracked) noted Dave Canales prizes violent runners and has used that word about Hubbard, per Mike Kaye of the Charlotte Observer. Both expect a split with Dowdle rather than a clean displacement either way.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-04]]): Hubbard is now clearly behind [[Rico Dowdle]] in Carolina, and Harris grades his own ranking miss as leading listeners astray less by starting Hubbard than by failing to elevate Dowdle. On the Green Bay film it was frankly lucky that Hubbard played as much as he did — his snaps came because Dowdle kept leaving with a groin issue. Hubbard did convert a big third-down carry and carried down to the four-yard line on the scoring drive, only for Dowdle's groin to stage what Harris called a miracle recovery so he could vulture the touchdown. Dowdle was absolutely the main plan.
