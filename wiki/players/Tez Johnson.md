# Tez Johnson

## Expert Takes

- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Going Deep)]]): the Oregon slot receiver he kept circling on 2023 tape ('who is 15?') ahead of [[Bo Nix]], [[Troy Franklin]] and [[Bucky Irving]], and whom he calls the best receiver on that offense — roughly 86 catches, about 1,100 yards and 10 touchdowns, with 93%-plus of snaps in the slot. He is listed around 5-10 and 160-170 pounds. Angelo pushes back on the reflexive [[Tank Dell]] comp: Dell can play inside-out and separates vertically, which Johnson does not, but the one part of Dell's Houston tape he does share is elite low-red-zone separation and being a quarterback-friendly option in high-leverage situations, where Angelo rates him among the best in the nation. Chain-mover with after-catch creation and some downfield speed. Projects him as a day-two pick.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]): Johnson is one of his favorite route runners in the 2025 class and someone he expects to have a long NFL career, with a [[Tyler Lockett]] comparison including the punt-return ability and toughness for his frame. Angelo notes less mass makes changing direction easier, but the thing he values most is that Johnson's route running shows up in high-leverage situations — when Angelo watched Oregon last year, Johnson was the go-to guy, and where the ball goes when it matters is a specific thing he charts. [[Matt Waldman]] groups Johnson with Jaylin Noel as players likely to be good pros but probably not high-end fantasy producers — the twos and threes that define a thin class.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): Waldman pushes back on the size panic around the 154-pound Oregon receiver, which he expects to depress Johnson's draft capital. His historical counter is Gerald 'Ice Cube' McNeil, listed at 5'7 and 145 pounds, who made the 1987 Pro Bowl as a return specialist with 728 return yards and two touchdowns, later posted a 1,366-yard season on 87 total returns, averaged 897 return yards per season across five years with four 700-plus-yard seasons, and missed only four games in his career. Waldman's argument: returning kicks in the 1980s and 90s, with coverage units getting a 15-to-20-yard longer runway into hits, was far more dangerous than working the middle of the field in 2024's spread-out, lighter, safety-emphasized NFL. He is therefore open to the idea that Johnson's weight will not be the problem the media makes it, and that defenders will struggle to land a debilitating hit on him.
- 2025-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether Johnson at 5-foot-9, 154 pounds with a 4.51 forty — the slowest time of any receiver under 170 pounds, per Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) — is too small for the NFL, Waldman says 'fuck it.' His precedent is Gerald McNeil, 'Ice Cube,' who played for Cleveland at 146 pounds and made a Pro Bowl as a returner, and Waldman argues Johnson is the better receiver of the two. Johnson is not asked to run past corners vertically; he works across the field, wins targets and uses genuine short-area explosion for YAC and to beat man coverage underneath. He also invokes Anquan Boldin (4.71) and Le'Veon Bell as proof quickness matters more than speed — and there is far less of Johnson to hit. Bob Harris remained unconvinced, repeating that 4.51 simply is not very fast.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21]]): asked whether the fantasy world is overstating the 154-pound combine weigh-in, Waldman says yes, it is overstated. He does not see a high-end fantasy player, but he does see a nice slot contributor with a long career across two or three teams giving roughly 500–700 yards at his best, and if he builds a rapport with a really good quarterback, a 700–900-yard fixture who becomes a fantasy option in a lot of leagues. Waldman pushes back hard on the speed criticism — Johnson ran about a 4.5 but is 'blindingly quick', and he argues small surface area plus acceleration is exactly what the middle of the field rewards; he would rather have that than a 230-pound receiver absorbing more contact there. He also notes Johnson takes contact well, and invokes the small, durable Ice Cube McNeill archetype. Practical advice: nothing to do now, but add him off waivers if he makes an active roster and a role emerges.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]) [Dynasty]: worth monitoring in Tampa Bay. Waldman calls him very tough over the middle for a little guy, unbelievably quick, and a candidate for return work, RPO usage and middle-of-the-field slot snaps — he only fell in the draft because of his size. His historical comp is Gerald 'Ice Cube' McNeil, the roughly 150-155 pound Browns returner who made two Pro Bowls in an early-to-mid-1980s era with effectively no player-safety rules. Waldman's projection is a potential bye-week flex value down the line, likely not in 2025 unless attrition opens the door. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) noted that with [[Chris Godwin]] and Emeka Egbuka's fellow rookie [[Tetairoa McMillan]] unavailable early, Tampa needs bodies, and framed it as good news for dynasty investors: the organization appears to be building a passing attack for after [[Mike Evans]] and Godwin.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): Johnson was the number two Week 11 waiver add for both Harris and guest **Brandon Funston** (The Athletic, not tracked), who picked him up early, admits bias because Johnson has been useful for him, and cites four touchdowns in the last five games plus the opportunity created by no [[Chris Godwin]] and no [[Mike Evans]]. Harris flags the production as partly lucky - in the Patriots game Johnson scored only because [[TreVeyon Henderson]] looked to the sideline to see whether he was allowed to score at the one, and then Johnson's big game came on a long, meaningless trailing drive. Funston counters that he looks fine catching the ball across the middle and 'production is sort of the bottom line'. Harris is candid that at 33 percent rostered Johnson only qualifies by a whisker and that he would prefer [[Alec Pierce]] and [[Troy Franklin]], ranking both ahead of Johnson.
