# Rashee Rice

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): wants to watch more before drawing a firm conclusion, but notes his rookie-year role is more of a "Skyy Moore"-style underneath/slot job than the outside role scouts projected for him coming out. Explicitly does not put him in the same tier as the season's top rookie receivers, applying the same "do the harder thing" caution as [[Tank Dell]] — playing with Patrick Mahomes elevates good receivers but doesn't by itself prove one is good. See [[Role Difficulty and Replaceability]].
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: a real reversal, and Waldman flags it as such. Adam Harstad's [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] places Rice in the same top tier as [[Jayden Reed]] and [[Zay Flowers]] — top 10 in the class on touchdown-adjusted efficiency, in the range of Christian Watson's 2022 season (the downside comp: manufactured/scheme-inflated touches that don't reflect his real role) but also of Tyreek Hill, Percy Harvin and Chris Godwin (the upside comp: legitimate hyper-efficient talent that just wasn't schemed the way scouts expected at SMU). Waldman: "if you told me one of these top five guys was going to turn out to be a bust, I would bet on Rice" — but also, if one becomes the league's next 1,500-yard receiver, he'd bet on Rice ahead of Reed or Flowers to be that guy. [[Matt Harmon]] independently echoed the same "different receiver in Kansas City than the SMU projection" read (per Waldman, citing Harmon's own separate commentary — Harmon was not a guest on this episode). Waldman calls this his single biggest pre-draft miss of the class — he graded Rice as overrated by 19 draft spots and had him at pick 44 overall (a late-fourth-round dynasty rookie value, alongside players like Parker Washington and Andrei Iosivas); he now says he needs to revisit the tape to see whether the role is sustainable rather than a product of Kansas City's scheme.
- 2024-01-15 — Guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Dynasty]: "100% feeling this," and gives the most detailed positive case yet on the role question. Explains the season arc directly: roughly six of his first nine weeks were played predominantly in the slot; after the Miami/Eagles stretch he became a true 50/50 inside-outside player as Andy Reid's scheme (which asks every receiver to learn all three alignments and manufactures touches rather than assigning a fixed role, unlike how the offense is built around Travis Kelce) opened up. Ties the current role directly back to his SMU tape — explosiveness and acceleration out of routes/stems that had been masked in his final college season by a foot injury — and expects the "training wheels" to come off in year two with more vertical-route usage as trust with Patrick Mahomes deepens. Also notes the rest of Kansas City's receiver room (Kadarius Toney, Marquez Valdes-Scantling, Justin Watson) all regressed under a first-year position coach, making Rice's growth look even more like an individual signal rather than a scheme-wide bump.
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]): "perfectly nailed" role read continues — praises how completely Rice has embraced Kansas City's underneath role relative to his SMU tape, to the point of pushing back on "you greedy dynasty folks" wanting more of an outside/vertical (aDOT) role right away: "let's just keep him in this role." Notes he's currently the only proven weapon in a shorthanded receiver room after a quiet Wild Card win over Miami (drops from Travis Kelce and Justin Watson, no other breakout performer).
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): the clearest matchup edge on the board for the Chiefs-Bills Divisional Round game — Buffalo has given up the 5th-most receptions and 9th-highest completion rate to slot receivers, tied for the 10th-most slot touchdowns allowed. Both hosts still flag the obvious counter-risk: Sean McDermott may simply take Rice away entirely and dare Kansas City's unproven other receivers to beat them, since KC currently has no clear second option.
- 2024-01-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-25]]): ranked the clear #5 (last) of the five receivers remaining in the playoffs — "the most limited of these five receivers" — while still calling him "a very good player," notably better than any other Kansas City non-alpha weapon (explicitly rated above Kadarius Toney/Justin Watson-tier options). Sets his effective archetype ceiling explicitly at Cooper Kupp — "the highest-level player of that archetype" (a short/intermediate, zone-beating, YAC-driven number-one target rather than a true X receiver) — with the acknowledgment that his rookie-year yards-per-route-run is right in Puka Nacua's range even as the archetype itself caps his ranking below the other four.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): continued optimism after the AFC Championship win, calling him "the notable exception" to GM Brett Veach's otherwise weak wide-receiver drafting history. Hopes Kansas City adds another proven weapon around him via free agency — floats [[Tee Higgins]], [[Mike Evans]] and Michael Pittman Jr. (not a tracked page) as the top-tier names available, while noting Cincinnati is expected to franchise-tag Higgins.

- 2024-02-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-12]]) [Best Ball]: a real downgrade in ADP terms, delivered as an explicit "Gabe Davis effect" comp — valuable production, but not a role Waldman trusts to repeat in one-on-one, game-on-the-line moments. "He's not the Stefon Diggs of this offense, he was the Gabe Davis of this offense" — schemed up rather than a true matchup receiver, "valuable for football and it can be valuable for fantasy, but it's not reliably valuable for top-five-round draft" picks. Concrete market data he cites on himself: went at the tail end of round 3 in his first 2024 mock draft; in his current mock (picking 6-10), Rice is still on the board undrafted. States he's "more on that side" — i.e., happy to let him fall past even a round-3 price.
- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]): in Super Bowl LVIII, combined with [[Travis Kelce]] for 18 of Kansas City's targets (Pacheco and Justin Watson next-closest with 6 and 5), a touch under their ~20-plus combined postseason norm. Separately, in the offseason-outlook segment, calls Rice "a fascinating player to discuss and debate" this offseason and points to his own (non-podcast) YouTube mailbag for a fuller comparison of Rice's rookie-year Reception Perception profile to [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]'s — the model case for a slot-heavy rookie role expanding into a true alpha in year two. See [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]].

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): Kansas City still sorted into the "critical need" wide receiver bucket despite Rice's play — "Rashee Rice is a great player, but they need somebody" who can play X and beat man-press coverage on the outside, which Harmon says isn't Rice's game based on his rookie-year film. Frames him as a good complementary starter who needs a true outside receiver added alongside him, not a replacement for him.

- 2024-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-04]]), in a historical study of poor college-separation prospects who became NFL hits (offered as context for [[Keon Coleman]]'s profile): names Rice one of only five "inarguable hits" — alongside [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[Cooper Kupp]], Tyler Boyd and Juju Smith-Schuster — out of 32 charted prospects who finished below the 35th percentile in two of three main Reception Perception coverage metrics (success rate vs. man/zone/press) across the last several draft classes. Notes Rice's SMU tape looked "completely different" from a vertical X receiver to an off-the-ball "layup" slot receiver once Kansas City moved him inside, and relays that Rice told Harmon at Super Bowl media night the slot plan was laid out by Andy Reid's staff as soon as he was drafted. See [[Reception Perception Methodology]].

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on *Reception Perception: The Show* ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-11]]): pushes back on the volume of fantasy takes about Rice's legal situation (a marijuana-possession issue tied to his hit-and-run case) before any suspension is known, arguing there's no fantasy-relevant read to make yet. On role: given his collegiate profile, wonders whether Kansas City's coaching staff might build out more of a package for him outside next season, since so much of his college and 2023 rookie work came in the slot (48% inside / 50% outside snaps in 2023) — draws a alignment (not usage) parallel to how [[Tyreek Hill]]'s snaps were more 50/50 outside/inside than perceived. Co-host James Koh is more cautious, not wanting to load him up with more outside/press-man responsibility than his rookie tape actually showed.
- 2024-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (untracked co-host) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-16]]): [[Rashee Rice]]'s felony charges from a six-car crash (doing offseason work over Zoom while the case proceeds) make the Chiefs question his availability and reliability, and combined with an unsettled WR depth chart (Marquise Brown on a one-year deal, an aging Travis Kelce) is pushing Kansas City toward taking a receiver early in a deep 2024 class.
- 2024-05-07 — According to James Koh (untracked co-host) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-07]]), in discussion with [[Matt Harmon]]: Koh cautions that Rashee Rice's breakout production should be scrutinized closely, saying a lot of it was 'Mickey Mouse' screens and YAC-driven touches rather than true separation, and grades him a 'high-quality WR2' -- below [[Marquise Brown]] in pure talent, though he says he's still finishing charting the back half of Rice's sample and could revise that view. Both hosts note a real availability risk, floating a scenario where Rice is suspended for six games, which factors into their bearish read on teammate [[Xavier Worthy]]'s target ceiling even in that scenario.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman flags Rice's legal situation as a real fantasy risk, estimating he could miss anywhere from four to eight games or potentially a full season, which is pushing his best ball ADP down and opening opportunity for [[Xavier Worthy]] and Marquise Brown in Kansas City's receiver room.
- 2024-07-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-02]]) [Dynasty]: Rice's SMU prospect profile was poor -- 1st percentile success rate versus man, 7th percentile versus zone -- while playing 83.6% of snaps outside as an X, and drew online criticism of Harmon's process at the time. Andy Reid instead deployed him almost opposite as a rookie: 47.6% of snaps in the slot/backfield, and 50.9% of routes were screens, slants or flats (38.3% slants alone, the highest Harmon has ever charted for one player). Results: 63% success versus man (29th percentile), roughly 80% versus zone (60th percentile), and 68.2% versus press despite facing press on only 10.3% of routes -- Harmon stresses the numbers 'aren't that different' from college, just deployed to hide the man-coverage weakness. He compared Rice's rookie stat line (78 catches, 77.5% catch rate, 1,355-yard-pace efficiency) as almost identical to Juju Smith-Schuster's 2022 rookie year, and also drew a Jarvis Landry comparison given similarly slot-heavy, high-slant route trees -- calling both plausible outcomes (900-1,100-yard slot seasons), with [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] as the growth-and-expansion ceiling comp if Rice adds more zone-beating routes. Rice excelled in space (62% of targets, breaking first contact on over half), which Harmon says is his real value; he flagged a 36% success rate on nine routes as a real weakness, and noted mild concern about Rice's offseason conduct versus a 'working maniacally hard' profile like Juju's.
- 2024-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-01]]): Rice charted 60th percentile against zone and a sneaky-passable 53rd against press as a rookie, but struggled in ISO man situations with very low post, nine and corner success rates; he functioned well on outs, digs and intermediate work and is very good with the ball in his hands. Harmon flags a 38.3% slant-route rate as a 'route shrub' that has to expand. With [[Xavier Worthy]] and [[Marquise Brown]] clearing space, Harmon sees Rice as the chain-mover and says there is a real chance he catches around 105 balls; he notes Rice's year-one RP data is similar to [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]'s and that the hosts have comped him to Jarvis Landry (Koh) and JuJu Smith-Schuster (Harmon). Caveats: 2023 was 79 catches on 102 targets for 938 yards and seven scores, he may be the No. 3 in the room, and Harmon suspects the legal case leads to a 2025 rather than 2024 suspension. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) has him behind Brown in yards but ahead in receptions.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the anticipated suspension may not arrive in time to matter. Harris relays reporting from Football Guys colleague Drew Davenport that the criminal case has not been located as filed and the civil case ended in December, and notes the NFL has recently been waiting for players to move through the legal system before disciplining them — so drafting Rice as a WR4 could return real value. Waldman separately names Rice as a beneficiary of the two-high safety shells defenses use against [[Xavier Worthy]]'s vertical speed, which opens the middle of the field.
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): pressed by co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on whether Rice could take on a full-time outside role — especially with [[Marquise Brown]] hurt — Harmon softens a previously flat no. He would still be 'a little surprised' at full-time X work: Rice's rookie Reception Perception numbers against man coverage are sub-50th percentile, though not disastrous against press, and the specific unresolved skill is winning downfield against press-man, since the short and intermediate work is established. Betting on it means betting on an [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]-style year-over-year development curve — but because Rice exceeded his expectations as a rookie, Harmon will not totally rule it out. Koh's angle is suspicion around the [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]] re-signing, wondering whether it hints at a Rice suspension or a plan to play both together; Harmon says he would be shocked if a healthy, active Rice lost snaps to Smith-Schuster. Harmon also notes he had expected Rice and [[Skyy Moore]] to swap roles, but Rice is clearly the man you want with the ball in space.
