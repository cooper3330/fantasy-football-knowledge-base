# Isaiah Likely

## Expert Takes

- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: Discussing Baltimore's receiving room, Harmon said the Ravens 'gotta try to find some way to get Isaiah Likely on the field because he's just too good' to keep stashed behind [[Mark Andrews]], flagging his 16.1 yards-per-reception average last year (boosted by several big plays) on only 64 targets as evidence of untapped efficiency. He suggested Baltimore, with two traditional in-line tight ends who both offer real target-earning ability when split out wide, is one of the few teams that could credibly lean on two-tight-end sets more heavily this year, even though most teams that talk up 12-personnel don't actually follow through on usage.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman is skeptical of [[Isaiah Likely]] having real standalone value at his current TE23 price, since the Ravens ran 12-personnel only 11% of the time last year -- far below the Todd Monken-led Georgia offenses that led the nation in two-TE sets -- though he noted that could shift with [[Derrick Henry]] now in the backfield, whose extra gap-blocking presence could open more 12-personnel looks. He cited Likely's 40 targets/5 TDs last year and expanded reps in the slot and backfield as reasons for some optimism, and said the price is fair as a [[Mark Andrews]] handcuff/insurance play without paying a 'handcuff premium' on top of it. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) floated speculation that heavy Likely chatter could be setup for a trade of Likely, Charlie Kolar, or even Andrews, which Waldman said he wasn't necessarily buying but found worth raising.
- 2024-07-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]): Likely is a good value at a TE21 price because of two distinct paths, which Waldman puts explicit odds on. If Mark Andrews (who says he is 100% after last year's injury) gets hurt, Likely taking over as a fantasy starter is an 85–90% proposition. The second path — Likely moving around the formation into receiver roles and earning real volume — Waldman puts at only about 15%, because [[Rashad Bateman]] and [[Zay Flowers]] are better matchup options on the outside, so Likely gets scheme plays rather than sustained targets. Bob Harris (not tracked) is more bullish on that second path, citing Todd Monken's long history of heavy two-tight-end usage including at Georgia. Both hosts note they are heavily invested in Andrews continuing to dominate.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Likely is a must-add. Kansas City devoted anywhere from two to four defenders to [[Mark Andrews]], which vacated zones for Likely, and he still got hurt reaching for a difficult pass, returned, and nearly won the game. The usage change is the signal: Baltimore ran 53 percent two-tight-end sets in this game, up from roughly 10 percent last year, after an offseason of talk about getting Likely involved that never showed up in preseason — no reason to reveal the plan early. Waldman had flagged him in his fantasy notebook about two weeks before the season, and says if anything happens to Andrews he goes to the moon. He is explicit, though, that Likely's emergence does not mean Andrews is finished.
- 2025-01-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-21]]) [Dynasty]: Likely had a great divisional-round game with [[Mark Andrews]] struggling, and Harmon says to write it down in pen — if Andrews is not on the roster next season he will be drafting Likely in fantasy 'probably too high.' He groups Likely with [[Zay Flowers]] and [[Rashad Bateman]] as Baltimore's likely top three pass catchers next year and argues all three are on the upswing, in the prime-age core of a team he expects to run it back rather than rebuild.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: asked to pick two of [[DeAndre Hopkins]], Likely and [[Rashad Bateman]], Angelo takes Bateman and Likely. He expects Likely to take a step forward because he offers genuinely higher-end capabilities than [[Mark Andrews]] at this stage, even though the passing game will probably revolve around [[Zay Flowers]]. [[Matt Waldman]] complicates it by asking what a bigger Hopkins slot role would do to Likely's and Andrews' snaps, and both note the open question of how much 11 personnel Baltimore runs — whether the best set is two tight ends or Hopkins with Likely.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): Likely avoided the PUP list despite missing all of camp with a broken foot requiring surgery. Harris expects him to need time to fully ramp up, a window in which [[Mark Andrews]] should get a safer work share — though he thinks this is Andrews' last season in Baltimore and there is eventually a chance at a baton passing, 'not guaranteed, definitely possible'.
- 2025-08-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the Ravens say Likely will return in the early part of the season, which Harris calls appropriately vague. His read is skeptical — Week 1 is technically early, but the phrasing sounds like the team preparing everyone for missed games rather than a Week 1 return.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Likely needed foot surgery at the start of training camp and had not practiced as of this recording, but he avoided the PUP list, which suggests the Ravens expect him back in September. Harris notes Likely is not unhappy about his contract, so if he could play he would — and that means he is pretty clearly unusable in Week 1 lineups. He adds that this gives [[Mark Andrews]] a small amount of extra safety.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): 'fuck it' on Likely becoming a fantasy thing despite being the second-most-targeted receiving asset for Baltimore on Thursday night and doing a nice job with big plays. Waldman expects him to tease people from time to time with the opportunities he gets because [[Lamar Jackson]] spreads the ball around, but said it remains [[Mark Andrews]]'s show unless Andrews is traded — and he does not think Baltimore trades him during this playoff contention window.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-10]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman expects Likely to leave Baltimore. The Ravens want a better blocker who wins against zone coverage — which is [[Charlie Kolar]] — and are not as concerned about the explosive element Likely brings, while Likely should command bigger money from a team convinced he can be their version of [[Oronde Gadsden II]]. Waldman's read is that Ravens fans will be disappointed if he is not re-signed and will watch him do his thing elsewhere, but that the split is the likely outcome.
- 2025-12-09 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-09]]): Likely faces a Bengals defense that's been poor against tight ends and has had solid recent games; Harris notes he'd have had two more touchdowns already if not for a drop and a blown move at the goal line, making him a streaming consideration for Week 15.
- 2025-12-12 — According to Jake Trowbridge (untracked co-host) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-12]]): Trowbridge chose Likely over teammate Mark Andrews as his cheap DFS tight end against Cincinnati at $3,400, saying he'd prefer Likely even at equal pricing because Likely doesn't require a touchdown to pay off -- pointing to their earlier matchup against the Bengals where Likely, not Andrews, led the tight end in receiving yardage. [Best Ball]
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Dave Kluge (Footballguys.com, untracked guest) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-12]]): Likely signs with the Giants for essentially the same cash as [[Mark Andrews]]'s new Ravens deal, which both hosts find a strange allocation given Andrews's decline. Kluge argues 'the idea of Isaiah Likely is much cooler than what he's actually done' -- outside one huge Week 1 game (~160 yards, a touchdown that was ruled just short) he's finished tight end 20 or worse every year and never averaged 30 receiving yards a game. Harris still says he'd rather spend real money on Likely than on Andrews given Andrews's three straight years of declining production. Both connect the Likely (and Pat Ricard) signings to new coach Harbaugh building a Detroit-style, multi-tight-end run-heavy scheme, which Kluge thinks boosts [[Jackson Dart]]'s rushing outlook more than it boosts Likely himself, with [[Theo Johnson]] projected to handle more of the in-line blocking work.
- 2026-04-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-06]]) [Best Ball]: with Isaiah Likely now with the Giants, Waldman finds it fascinating given Theo Johnson is still on the roster, and notes reports of the team expecting big numbers from Likely, though he cautions team-speak 'big numbers' may not match fantasy expectations. Waldman sees a path to an 800-1,100 yard, 8-10 touchdown season if Likely is used as a slot/move receiver replacing [[Wan'Dale Robinson]], especially with [[Malik Nabers]] still working back to full health and the Giants likely to add another receiver in the draft. But he's skeptical of a clean Year One TE1 role: he expects a run-heavy 12/13-personnel offense behind [[Cam Skattebo]] and [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]], with Theo Johnson and Thomas Fidone as interior tight ends and Likely deployed more as a detached, matchup-based piece. He flags the free-agency praise as a possible hedge if the Giants miss on a top receiver in the draft, and notes Likely is currently priced as a value in best ball drafts before hype likely pushes the cost up.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris ranks [[Isaiah Likely]] TE19 and said he has 'some unknown upside' since he's never actually been treated like a full-time starter, but was skeptical of anyone projecting a 1,200-yard breakout, calling that outcome outside his realistic range on the Ravens' offense. He directly compared the current Likely hype to last year's identical hype cycle around [[Theo Johnson]], which fizzled -- and noted Theo Johnson is still on the Giants' roster as competition in a similar spot this year, a red flag for parallel hype elsewhere.
- 2026-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-28]]): Likely is a rare downfield-vertical-threat tight end, which Harmon thinks could let the Giants run functional 3-tight-end heavy personnel (with Theo Johnson and blocking specialist Chris Manhertz) similar to the Rams' 2025 model. Harmon cites Puka Nacua averaging an 'obnoxiously high' 6.19 yards per route run on 114 routes (18th-most among WRs) out of 12/13 personnel in the regular season plus playoffs, far outpacing the next-closest qualifiers (Luther Burden 5.13 on only 63 routes, JSN 4.91 on 150). He cautions that most teams chasing the heavy-personnel trend this offseason will fail because it requires both a true difference-making receiver and a genuinely 'sacrificial' blocking tight end (the Rams had Terrance Ferguson) -- but he thinks the Giants may actually have the pieces to pull it off.
- 2026-06-25 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Likely is the safest fantasy asset in the Giants' passing game outside of [[Cam Skattebo]] -- 'they went out and got him, they think very highly of him' -- and he'll play 'a bushel of snaps' regardless of personnel grouping (11/12/13), giving him a real shot at a top-five fantasy tight end season. [[Matt Waldman]] projects Likely to lead the team in targets at 90, and expects him to be the true slot receiver in an offense that leans heavily into 12 personnel around John Harbaugh's deep tight end room (with [[Colston Loveland|Johnson]], Thomas Fidone and Chris Manhurts).
- 2026-07-14 — According to Jake Seeley (guest, The Athletic, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with new Giants HC Harbaugh bringing Likely over, Seeley is aggressive on him, projecting a Matt Nagy-style lead-TE role similar to what Cole Kmet got in Chicago, even though Likely never fully broke out as a target hog previously; he has Likely inside his top 12 TEs. Chris Harris is more conservative, ranking Likely 14th among TEs and deferring for now to [[Theo Johnson]]'s proven blocking role, projecting Likely's ceiling as a back-end TE1 near the Sam LaPorta/Harold Fannin/George Kittle range - as high as he'll go barring proof Likely is the true 1-2 target option in a two-TE-heavy scheme. Both flag camp/preseason personnel usage (how much 2-TE) as the real signal to watch, since a predictable, single-role usage pattern (like Evan Engram's 'passing tell' role) would hurt Likely's value.
- 2026-07-31 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-31]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris has Isaiah Likely one ranking spot ahead of [[Mark Andrews]] (TE14 to Andrews's TE15) despite not wanting either as a fantasy starter. Both hosts believe last year's camp extension conversations initially favored Likely before an injury intervened, after which the deal reportedly went to Andrews instead -- a moment Harris calls potentially significant in hindsight ('a sliding doors moment'). Harmon says Likely remains the more interesting name to him of the two Ravens tight ends.
- 2026-08-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)] [Dynasty]: Harris's top post-hype sleeper is Isaiah Likely, now with the New York Giants after Baltimore chose to keep Mark Andrews instead -- for what Harris called 'almost an exact dollar' swap versus what re-signing both would have cost. Both hosts said they still can't fully explain the Ravens' decision, speculating it could reflect a locker-room or quarterback-relationship dynamic (Lamar Jackson possibly closer with Andrews) rather than a talent judgment, since Likely still flashed playmaking ability whenever he got chances. By all camp accounts he's been a go-to target for Jackson Dart, and Harris cited a real track record: in the nine career games where he's drawn at least six targets, he's produced PPR TE1-caliber points in eight of them. Dart leaned heavily on Theo Johnson last year, which Harris expects to carry over into heavy tight-end usage generally. Harris ranks Likely in the tier just below the top group (Brock Bowers, Trey McBride, Colston Loveland, Tyler Warren) and calls him one of the best values on the board given he was essentially a free signing, sitting outside the top-12 TE ADP.
