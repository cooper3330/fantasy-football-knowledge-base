# Drake May

## Expert Takes

- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris said May 'simply made himself an every-week fantasy starter,' throwing into tight intermediate windows with excellent ball placement and adding scrambles, though he cautioned not to get carried away given Tennessee is the league's worst team. The concerning moment: May took an unprotected hit, got his head slammed into the turf on a scramble, and was pulled for Josh Dobbs before passing the concussion test and re-entering. Harris framed the season-long question as whether May will start taking better care of himself, since the offense clearly needs him upright to keep spreading production to ancillary pieces like [[Stefon Diggs]], [[Kayshon Boutte]] and [[Rhamondre Stevenson]].
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-10-27]]) [Redraft]: 'even human for a little while' with an ill-advised interception thrown right to a Browns linebacker, but the dam burst in the second half -- a bootleg touchdown to [[Hunter Henry]], a two-yard slant touchdown to [[Stefon Diggs]], and an 'effortless' deep flick to Kayshawn Boutte, plus 50 rushing yards of his own. This came despite a brutal Cleveland pass rush (Myles Garrett alone had five sacks). Harris says May is 'certainly locked into your lineup' regardless of matchup.
- 2025-10-31 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-31]]), matched independently by Cousin Josh (co-host, not tracked): both hosts had May in a preseason tier with [[Justin Fields]], [[Trevor Lawrence]], [[C.J. Stroud]], [[Jordan Love]] and [[Caleb Williams]] around QB15, hoping one would pop but not expecting a takeover. Instead May has 'completely lifted' New England's offense to 6-2 and one of the league's higher-scoring units, despite what Harris calls a supporting cast without a single 'supremely above average' piece -- a rookie left tackle repeatedly beaten badly, shaky interior line spots, an aging [[Stefon Diggs]], and journeyman contributions from a receiver like Kayshon Boutte and from [[Hunter Henry]]. Harris compares May to [[Jayden Daniels]] and [[C.J. Stroud]]: once he's shown that level of true quarterback play on film, he considers it a skill the player owns, and won't be shocked -- but also isn't worried -- if the numbers dip in the second half as the cast's limits show through.
- 2025-11-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-14]]): coming off New England's eighth straight win, Harris says May 'was awesome' against the Jets even though the fantasy stat line undersold it because two Patriots touchdowns were runs. He describes May settling into a locked-in mode where he sees the defense, buys time with his legs, and then delivers with slowed-down, precise mechanics -- comparing the feel (not the resume) to Mahomes on May's best reps. Highlights: a surgical seam throw to Hunter Henry, back-to-back improvisational completions to [[Stefon Diggs]], and a calm third-and-ten conversion to Demario Douglas under a blitz. His worst throw was a floated deep ball intended for Kyle Williams that should have been intercepted. Harris says May 'really needs to understand how the MVP race works' but that the tape itself is legitimately great, not homer talk -- 'these are not footy pajamas talking.'
- 2025-12-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-15]]): May had two rushing touchdowns (a zone-read keeper and a QB draw) in an opened-up first half against Buffalo where New England 'cracked open the playbook' with a reverse and a throwback pass, building a 24-7 lead. But once the Patriots blew that lead in the fourth quarter, Harris says May 'didn't look like the MVP needing that drive' -- he just held the ball, unable to find anyone open, which Harris attributes to New England lacking 'a true difference maker separator' among its receivers. New England is now flexed to Sunday Night Football at Baltimore in Week 16.
- 2025-12-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-22]]): Waldman is feeling New England as a potential Super Bowl dark horse behind May and Mike Vrabel, crediting Vrabel's prior head-coaching experience for instilling a mindset that lets the team 'win ugly and win big.' He notes the skill-position veteran presence -- [[Stefon Diggs]] and [[Hunter Henry]] -- gives May something to lean on, and says while it wouldn't surprise him if the Patriots win their first playoff game, winning a second and third would be more surprising, which is exactly the profile of a dark-horse run.
- 2026-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Heading into Super Bowl LX, Maye is dealing with a shoulder injury Harmon believes is more serious than Sam Darnold's oblique issue, despite both camps saying they are '100 percent' healthy. Harmon called Maye's second-year leap remarkable given he inherited pass-catchers and an offensive line both ranked 32nd in the NFL in 2024, and said his legs have been as important as his arm in the playoffs -- 65 rushing yards on 6.5 yards per attempt against Denver (vs. just 4.1 yards per passing attempt that game), and 10 carries for 66 yards in a 16-3 win over the Chargers. Maye enters the Super Bowl in MVP-tier conversation but faces a Seattle defense ranked number one in the NFL by EPA per play that plays heavy zone and will keep eyes on his scrambles. Harmon projects a close game (20-13 or 24-20 Seattle, not a blowout), crediting Maye and an underrated, well-coached Patriots defense.
- 2026-02-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: discussing a clip of a Seahawks defensive back saying May 'plays like a young quarterback' who has to confirm a route is open before throwing rather than trusting leverage and anticipation like Matthew Stafford, Waldman broke down the difference as 'processing confidence' -- May sees the same reads elite processors see but isn't confident enough to throw before separation shows, which is fatal against elite defenses (referencing the Super Bowl loss). Waldman noted May's strong rookie season came against a schedule light on top-tier defenses, and that his supporting cast (Kayshawn Boutte, [[Demario Douglas]], an older [[Stefon Diggs]]) isn't a strong man-coverage-beating group, forcing May to take more risk than his current confidence level supports. Both hosts see closing that anticipation-versus-confirmation gap, not physical tools, as the next step in his development, contrasting him with [[Caleb Williams]], who they said already takes those risks with better confidence.
- 2026-07-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-21]]): Alfredo's charting showed Drake May threw very few screens or flat routes as a rookie, instead leaning on a heavy in-breaking pass diet -- 17% curls (his best route, 84% success rate) and 13% slants -- while also pushing downfield, with 10% route participation on go/nine routes at a strong 58.5% success rate. Harmon said he currently views May as a better quarterback than [[Jalen Hurts]], even acknowledging Hurts also grades well on go balls. May's lowest-graded route last year was the out route at 60.5% success, an area Harmon wants to see improve, along with more dig-route usage (just 6.7% last year, versus an above-average post-route rate).
