# Jared Wiley

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-30]] [Dynasty]: one of the better examples of a recurring NFL-draft archetype — the tall, skinny wing/backside tight end matched against a linebacker or slow safety on sail/seam routes and backside screens, typically overdrafted for his size and mobility alone (the "Luke Stocker type"). Wiley stands out from that archetype because he has real ability to work underneath routes and gain yardage after the catch rather than just "plodding." A fifth-or-sixth-round NFL draft pick who Waldman thinks "might actually merit something on the field" and is worth watching more closely.
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]): Waldman is 'totally feeling' Jared Wiley as an immediate contributor for the Chiefs, but clarifies that mostly means special-teams snaps early, plus some schemed-up offensive touches likely to generate outsized hype relative to actual target volume. He frames Wiley as the next step in Kansas City's long search for a complementary/eventual Travis Kelce-succession piece -- after journeyman TE Jody Fortson got hurt and Blake Bell didn't stick as the answer -- and expects GM Brett Veach to keep prioritizing the position. Wiley is described as more acrobatic than incumbent Noah Gray, with the two not viewed as far apart overall for 2024.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo flags Wiley as interesting because a significant tight end vacancy is coming in Kansas City once [[Travis Kelce]] retires, and Wiley has real athletic upside and vertical/seam ability. Waldman says Wiley is a player where he's more willing to root for an outcome above his pre-draft report than usual — comparing the downside range to fifth/sixth-round tight ends like Will Mallory and Luke Stocker who got miscast into a diminished 'Kenny Golladay wide receiver' role — because Wiley shows the bend and after-catch ability to avoid that trap, and lands in a good room to develop as a timing route runner behind Kelce and Noah Gray before he's needed.
