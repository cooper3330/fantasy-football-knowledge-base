# Jalen Hurts

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): introduced only as the comparison point in a [[Lamar Jackson]] value discussion — Waldman expects Jackson to supplant [[Patrick Mahomes]] as the 2024 QB2 in redraft rankings, with Hurts as the only real competition for that spot. Flags Philadelphia's hiring of Kellen Moore as offensive coordinator as a live, ungraded variable on Hurts's own 2024 outlook — "people are going to have feelings about that without seeing any evidence of outcomes or possible outcomes" — rather than offering his own read on the scheme change.
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): the first actual grade on the Kellen Moore hire Waldman flagged as ungraded a day earlier — mixed/tepid. Harmon calls it "definitely an upgrade" over outgoing OC Brian Johnson but still expects "something left to be desired." The specific concern is structural: Hurts is "such a hyper-specific quarterback," almost never taken under center, and Harmon isn't sure whether that's an offensive-design choice or a Hurts limitation — flags Moore's history of strong under-center play-action work in Dallas as a potential fix, but says the bigger issue is a lack of complementary weapons beyond A.J. Brown and DeVonta Smith (not tracked pages), a much steeper drop-off than the offense had in 2022.

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: revisits the still-open Kellen Moore question in light of the [[Saquon Barkley]] signing — cautions that "we're not really giving enough credence to just how different this offense is going to look" under new OC Kellen Moore, calling it the bigger swing factor on both Hurts's own 2024 outlook and how large a role Barkley actually plays in the passing game.
- 2024-05-14 — According to Chris Long ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-14]]), not a tracked expert [Dynasty]: [[Jalen Hurts]]' finger injury, plus a knee issue, contributed to Philadelphia's late-season collapse last year, and top receiver [[A.J. Brown]] was also out for the playoff loss. Long views the [[Saquon Barkley]] signing partly as long-term insurance for Hurts, reducing Philadelphia's reliance on Hurts' own rushing after years of him absorbing a heavy hit-and-carry workload. Long also flagged the Eagles' offensive line as increasingly fragile around Hurts given the retirement of their longtime center and the advancing age of their starting left tackle, even while noting the unit still has the best O-line coach in football in Jeff Stoutland.
- 2024-08-27 — According to guest Derek Klassen (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): Hurts is his pick for the quarterback most likely to disappoint, despite [[A.J. Brown]], [[DeVonta Smith]], [[Dallas Goedert]] and a still-solid line. The concern is transition: Philadelphia is moving from a pared-down Shane Steichen / Brian Johnson offense of RPOs, simple verticals and quarterback run into Kellen Moore's true NFL drop-back passing with pure one-two-three progressions across the field, something Hurts has rarely been asked to do successfully. On top of that, protection-setting responsibility now falls to Hurts with Jason Kelce retired — Klassen recalls Kelce waving Hurts off and overruling his call. He compares it to [[Lamar Jackson]] last year but notes he rated Lamar a better player, and points out Moore's last two quarterbacks, [[Justin Herbert]] and [[Dak Prescott]], are elite pre-snap processors. Klassen says the bottom cannot fall out given the talent, but doubts top-six production in year one, while allowing Hurts could get there in two years. Host James Koh (not tracked) counters that Hurts's exponential year-over-year development — 'the NFL's Goku' — is unlike any quarterback growth he can recall.
- 2024-10-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-03]]): Hurts is the case study for his rushing-share framework — 20% of total yardage on the ground in 2021, 17% in 2022 and 14% in 2023, and the seasons where he looked like a running quarterback are the ones where he supported fewer fantasy receivers. Co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) pushed back and called the yardage cut circular: Hurts had essentially the same rate of pass and rush attempts in both years (432 pass / 139 rush in 2021, 460 / 165 in 2022), but yards per attempt rose from 7.3 to 8.0 and passing yards from 3,144 to 3,701 — the change was the arrival of [[A.J. Brown]], not any change in Hurts' willingness to run. Harstad's fix: judge rush attempts, not rush yards.
- 2024-12-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-09]]): Hurts, QB3 on the season at 22.3 points per game, is the 'good version' of a fantasy black hole quarterback — you get so much production you cannot complain, but he demonstrably suppresses his teammates. Waldman points to [[A.J. Brown]] being schemed wide open as a free-access receiver against off coverage and simply not getting the ball (he argues Brown should be the T.O. of this generation for what he does on the field), and to Hurts taking touchdowns off [[A.J. Brown]] and [[DeVonta Smith]] once the offense gets inside the five. See [[Fantasy Black Hole Quarterbacks]] — the tiering exercise was credited on air to Alex Hanowitz.
- 2024-12-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-12]]): Hurts is a very good but hyper-specific quarterback, and every offense he plays in ends up bending to him the way Seattle's did around Russell Wilson. He is excellent throwing outside the numbers down the field, which is why he and [[A.J. Brown]] have made 'beautiful music together', but he turns down throws, reacts poorly to pressure at times, and blitz recognition remains a big-time issue — so leaning into the run game partly makes sense. Harmon still wants Philadelphia off 31st in neutral pass rate, and thinks a system that elevates nobody beyond Brown and [[DeVonta Smith]] is a concern. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) went further, calling the offense a vanilla, cookie-cutter, out-of-the-box scheme with no plays that stress a defense and too little pre-snap motion; Harmon countered that Philadelphia is roughly 19th in dropbacks with motion, more than last year.
- 2024-12-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-17]]): Hurts will go down as a Russell Wilson type — a quarterback whose win-loss record will always outstrip where he ranks league-wide, and around whom every successive coordinator eventually converges on the same offense because you cater to what he does well. Harmon rates Hurts as more self-aware about his own game than Wilson was. His critique is about declined opportunities rather than mistakes: the throws a quarterback *doesn't* take matter more than the ones he does, and Hurts bails on plays, scrambles instead of resetting the pocket, and rarely works the middle of the field, which is corrosive for receivers on a run-heavy team. He contrasts [[Jayden Daniels]], who he says gives his receivers chances — especially [[Terry McLaurin]] — and is beating the pre-draft 'souped-up Jalen Hurts' comparison. James Koh (not tracked) agreed Hurts makes winning plays more than stat-sheet plays, citing the tush push.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Harmon took Hurts under 25.5 pass attempts at -105, because throwing is not how Philadelphia wants to win right now — they were very conservative in the wild-card round and the Rams defense is one you may want to be even more conservative against. On coverage, he drew a clear split: the Rams jumped to about 26% cover-1 against [[Sam Darnold]] despite ranking 19th in cover-1 usage on the season, and that is exactly what Hurts shreds, because man coverage on the outside just means going to [[A.J. Brown]] over and over. Two-high, more cover-2 or cover-3 and mixed coverages are where Hurts can struggle. James Koh (not a tracked expert) added that if Philadelphia's offense repeats last week's performance they lose this game.
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): Hurts was extremely efficient against Washington — 246 passing yards, four total touchdowns, a 71 percent completion rate, and just two incompletions combined when targeting [[A.J. Brown]] (96 yards), [[Dallas Goedert]] (85) and [[DeVonta Smith]] (45). Harmon pushes back hard on the broadcast consensus that defenses should simply 'let Jalen Hurts keep it' on read options: while Hurts is not the 60-yard breakaway threat [[Saquon Barkley]] is, he has been much better in the designed quarterback run game, and Barkley blocks on those plays, so the two-pronged run threat is real. That, plus Brown outside, is what he thinks makes the attack unique in the league.
- 2025-01-30 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-30]]: Kellen Moore's best work in Philadelphia was personnel-driven rather than innovative — he stripped out the timing-based concepts that defined his Dallas offense because Hurts is a 'gotta see it and then rip it' quarterback who either throws it, moves on, or runs. [[Matt Harmon]] agrees Moore pushed the right buttons around Hurts, but frames Hurts as being 'definitely more in that like [[Russell Wilson]] mode where every offense just kind of becomes the same offense,' unlike [[Dak Prescott]] and [[Justin Herbert]], whom he calls supercomputers and big-time processors. Both treat this as a warning sign for what a Moore offense would look like in New Orleans with [[Derek Carr]] — Koh fears an overload of underneath throws.
- 2025-02-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'feel it' that 100 fewer completions than the prior season — with [[Saquon Barkley]] still around — will drag on Hurts's draft value, and Waldman thinks the gap between Hurts and the field will be smaller than people expect, making him a centerpiece of the late-round-quarterback argument. On the league-high 2.7 seconds per pass attempt in the pocket, Waldman said that is simply his game and always has been: going back to Alabama and Oklahoma, when Hurts got disrupted in the pocket he held the ball too long, and the more invested he got in a play the more mistakes followed — questionable throws leading to picks, dropped or missed throws. He rates Hurts a studious, hard-working, good starting quarterback who may not belong in the set-and-forget top two or three.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): asked to grade Hurts good / very good / great after Super Bowl LIX, Waldman lands on 'very good and verging on great' — his scale being that a good quarterback needs a great team to be top-five, a very good one does not need everything working perfectly, and a great one drags a holed roster to top-five production. Hurts still has apparent flaws, but Waldman says defenses cannot exploit them regularly with this line, skill talent and defense around him. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) notes the 17-of-22, 221 yards, 2 TD, 1 INT line plus 11 carries for 72 yards and a score, and argues Hurts's greatest asset is emotional control. Waldman adds Hurts is going QB4-QB5 in early mocks behind [[Jayden Daniels]], [[Josh Allen]], [[Lamar Jackson]] and [[Joe Burrow]] and may be slightly overlooked, though ~100 fewer completions than the prior year is a volume consequence of [[Saquon Barkley]].
- 2025-02-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-11]]): Hurts' Super Bowl LIX game — 17 of 22, two passing touchdowns (to [[DeVonta Smith]] and [[A.J. Brown]]), one interception on a Cover-0 blitz, 11 carries for 72 yards and a rushing score — was 'classic Jalen Hurts,' decisive as a scrambler and attacking deep outside the numbers, with the pre-snap work to use [[Saquon Barkley]] as an outlet against zone-behind-blitz looks that had troubled him all season. The hosts disagree on tier: James Koh (not tracked) argues the legs, the tush push and the deep-ball accuracy make him elite and unquestionably top five; Harmon puts him around top 10-12, preferring Lamar Jackson, Josh Allen, Patrick Mahomes and Joe Burrow, citing limitations throwing over the middle that make him dependent on Brown and Smith winning outside, and noting he is in the 'Russell Wilson zone' where every offense becomes the Jalen Hurts offense — which just won a Super Bowl. Both agree he is more than good enough for this roster and locked up long-term.
- 2025-05-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-26]]): with the tush push surviving the rules vote, Waldman would take [[Jalen Hurts]] over [[Jayden Daniels]] at the current price, where Daniels is going QB3 and Hurts one spot behind. Co-host Bob Harris (Footballguys, not a tracked expert) supplied the case: Hurts has double-digit rushing touchdowns in each of the last four seasons, 52 total, 12 more than [[Josh Allen]]'s 40, with no other quarterback above 20 — Harris calls that a huge separator if you are investing in rushing equity. Hurts leads all quarterbacks in attempts inside the five over the three years the tush push has been tracked, at 40.7% of Philadelphia's carries from there. Harris attributes the softer perception to roughly 100 fewer pass attempts last year, crediting [[Saquon Barkley]]. Waldman adds Hurts is past his own rookie-hype and second-year adjustment period while Daniels is about to enter his.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Hurts' two rushing touchdowns in the opener were both red-zone scrambles on called pass plays, not Tush Push carries — Harris' point being the rushing production is real but arrives through the same mechanism as the passing game's limitations. His stated tell: 'you can tell when an unsuccessful pass play is about to happen' because Hurts hits his back foot, looks at his first read, pulls it down, and if there's no room to run he drifts sideways and throws on the move with poor results. He credits Hurts for making no big mistakes and never putting the ball in jeopardy the way [[Dak Prescott]] did, and for sealing the game on a scramble when Dallas played man without a spy. Guest JEFF ERICKSON (RotoWire, NOT a tracked expert) agreed the outing showed Hurts has 'real limitations' that Philadelphia no longer tries to coach out of him after trying two years ago — 'you'd rather have a better thrower as your quarterback for your second-round receiver.'
