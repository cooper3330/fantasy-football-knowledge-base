# Jalen Hurts

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): introduced only as the comparison point in a [[Lamar Jackson]] value discussion — Waldman expects Jackson to supplant [[Patrick Mahomes]] as the 2024 QB2 in redraft rankings, with Hurts as the only real competition for that spot. Flags Philadelphia's hiring of Kellen Moore as offensive coordinator as a live, ungraded variable on Hurts's own 2024 outlook — "people are going to have feelings about that without seeing any evidence of outcomes or possible outcomes" — rather than offering his own read on the scheme change.
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): the first actual grade on the Kellen Moore hire Waldman flagged as ungraded a day earlier — mixed/tepid. Harmon calls it "definitely an upgrade" over outgoing OC Brian Johnson but still expects "something left to be desired." The specific concern is structural: Hurts is "such a hyper-specific quarterback," almost never taken under center, and Harmon isn't sure whether that's an offensive-design choice or a Hurts limitation — flags Moore's history of strong under-center play-action work in Dallas as a potential fix, but says the bigger issue is a lack of complementary weapons beyond A.J. Brown and DeVonta Smith (not tracked pages), a much steeper drop-off than the offense had in 2022.

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: revisits the still-open Kellen Moore question in light of the [[Saquon Barkley]] signing — cautions that "we're not really giving enough credence to just how different this offense is going to look" under new OC Kellen Moore, calling it the bigger swing factor on both Hurts's own 2024 outlook and how large a role Barkley actually plays in the passing game.
- 2024-05-14 — According to Chris Long ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-14]]), not a tracked expert [Dynasty]: [[Jalen Hurts]]' finger injury, plus a knee issue, contributed to Philadelphia's late-season collapse last year, and top receiver [[A.J. Brown]] was also out for the playoff loss. Long views the [[Saquon Barkley]] signing partly as long-term insurance for Hurts, reducing Philadelphia's reliance on Hurts' own rushing after years of him absorbing a heavy hit-and-carry workload. Long also flagged the Eagles' offensive line as increasingly fragile around Hurts given the retirement of their longtime center and the advancing age of their starting left tackle, even while noting the unit still has the best O-line coach in football in Jeff Stoutland.
- 2024-08-27 — According to guest Derek Klassen (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): Hurts is his pick for the quarterback most likely to disappoint, despite [[A.J. Brown]], [[DeVonta Smith]], [[Dallas Goedert]] and a still-solid line. The concern is transition: Philadelphia is moving from a pared-down Shane Steichen / Brian Johnson offense of RPOs, simple verticals and quarterback run into Kellen Moore's true NFL drop-back passing with pure one-two-three progressions across the field, something Hurts has rarely been asked to do successfully. On top of that, protection-setting responsibility now falls to Hurts with Jason Kelce retired — Klassen recalls Kelce waving Hurts off and overruling his call. He compares it to [[Lamar Jackson]] last year but notes he rated Lamar a better player, and points out Moore's last two quarterbacks, [[Justin Herbert]] and [[Dak Prescott]], are elite pre-snap processors. Klassen says the bottom cannot fall out given the talent, but doubts top-six production in year one, while allowing Hurts could get there in two years. Host James Koh (not tracked) counters that Hurts's exponential year-over-year development — 'the NFL's Goku' — is unlike any quarterback growth he can recall.
- 2024-10-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-03]]): Hurts is the case study for his rushing-share framework — 20% of total yardage on the ground in 2021, 17% in 2022 and 14% in 2023, and the seasons where he looked like a running quarterback are the ones where he supported fewer fantasy receivers. Co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) pushed back and called the yardage cut circular: Hurts had essentially the same rate of pass and rush attempts in both years (432 pass / 139 rush in 2021, 460 / 165 in 2022), but yards per attempt rose from 7.3 to 8.0 and passing yards from 3,144 to 3,701 — the change was the arrival of [[A.J. Brown]], not any change in Hurts' willingness to run. Harstad's fix: judge rush attempts, not rush yards.
- 2024-12-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-09]]): Hurts, QB3 on the season at 22.3 points per game, is the 'good version' of a fantasy black hole quarterback — you get so much production you cannot complain, but he demonstrably suppresses his teammates. Waldman points to [[A.J. Brown]] being schemed wide open as a free-access receiver against off coverage and simply not getting the ball (he argues Brown should be the T.O. of this generation for what he does on the field), and to Hurts taking touchdowns off [[A.J. Brown]] and [[DeVonta Smith]] once the offense gets inside the five. See [[Fantasy Black Hole Quarterbacks]] — the tiering exercise was credited on air to Alex Hanowitz.
- 2024-12-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-12]]): Hurts is a very good but hyper-specific quarterback, and every offense he plays in ends up bending to him the way Seattle's did around Russell Wilson. He is excellent throwing outside the numbers down the field, which is why he and [[A.J. Brown]] have made 'beautiful music together', but he turns down throws, reacts poorly to pressure at times, and blitz recognition remains a big-time issue — so leaning into the run game partly makes sense. Harmon still wants Philadelphia off 31st in neutral pass rate, and thinks a system that elevates nobody beyond Brown and [[DeVonta Smith]] is a concern. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) went further, calling the offense a vanilla, cookie-cutter, out-of-the-box scheme with no plays that stress a defense and too little pre-snap motion; Harmon countered that Philadelphia is roughly 19th in dropbacks with motion, more than last year.
- 2024-12-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-17]]): Hurts will go down as a Russell Wilson type — a quarterback whose win-loss record will always outstrip where he ranks league-wide, and around whom every successive coordinator eventually converges on the same offense because you cater to what he does well. Harmon rates Hurts as more self-aware about his own game than Wilson was. His critique is about declined opportunities rather than mistakes: the throws a quarterback *doesn't* take matter more than the ones he does, and Hurts bails on plays, scrambles instead of resetting the pocket, and rarely works the middle of the field, which is corrosive for receivers on a run-heavy team. He contrasts [[Jayden Daniels]], who he says gives his receivers chances — especially [[Terry McLaurin]] — and is beating the pre-draft 'souped-up Jalen Hurts' comparison. James Koh (not tracked) agreed Hurts makes winning plays more than stat-sheet plays, citing the tush push.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Harmon took Hurts under 25.5 pass attempts at -105, because throwing is not how Philadelphia wants to win right now — they were very conservative in the wild-card round and the Rams defense is one you may want to be even more conservative against. On coverage, he drew a clear split: the Rams jumped to about 26% cover-1 against [[Sam Darnold]] despite ranking 19th in cover-1 usage on the season, and that is exactly what Hurts shreds, because man coverage on the outside just means going to [[A.J. Brown]] over and over. Two-high, more cover-2 or cover-3 and mixed coverages are where Hurts can struggle. James Koh (not a tracked expert) added that if Philadelphia's offense repeats last week's performance they lose this game.
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): Hurts was extremely efficient against Washington — 246 passing yards, four total touchdowns, a 71 percent completion rate, and just two incompletions combined when targeting [[A.J. Brown]] (96 yards), [[Dallas Goedert]] (85) and [[DeVonta Smith]] (45). Harmon pushes back hard on the broadcast consensus that defenses should simply 'let Jalen Hurts keep it' on read options: while Hurts is not the 60-yard breakaway threat [[Saquon Barkley]] is, he has been much better in the designed quarterback run game, and Barkley blocks on those plays, so the two-pronged run threat is real. That, plus Brown outside, is what he thinks makes the attack unique in the league.
- 2025-01-30 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-30]]: Kellen Moore's best work in Philadelphia was personnel-driven rather than innovative — he stripped out the timing-based concepts that defined his Dallas offense because Hurts is a 'gotta see it and then rip it' quarterback who either throws it, moves on, or runs. [[Matt Harmon]] agrees Moore pushed the right buttons around Hurts, but frames Hurts as being 'definitely more in that like [[Russell Wilson]] mode where every offense just kind of becomes the same offense,' unlike [[Dak Prescott]] and [[Justin Herbert]], whom he calls supercomputers and big-time processors. Both treat this as a warning sign for what a Moore offense would look like in New Orleans with [[Derek Carr]] — Koh fears an overload of underneath throws.
- 2025-02-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'feel it' that 100 fewer completions than the prior season — with [[Saquon Barkley]] still around — will drag on Hurts's draft value, and Waldman thinks the gap between Hurts and the field will be smaller than people expect, making him a centerpiece of the late-round-quarterback argument. On the league-high 2.7 seconds per pass attempt in the pocket, Waldman said that is simply his game and always has been: going back to Alabama and Oklahoma, when Hurts got disrupted in the pocket he held the ball too long, and the more invested he got in a play the more mistakes followed — questionable throws leading to picks, dropped or missed throws. He rates Hurts a studious, hard-working, good starting quarterback who may not belong in the set-and-forget top two or three.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): asked to grade Hurts good / very good / great after Super Bowl LIX, Waldman lands on 'very good and verging on great' — his scale being that a good quarterback needs a great team to be top-five, a very good one does not need everything working perfectly, and a great one drags a holed roster to top-five production. Hurts still has apparent flaws, but Waldman says defenses cannot exploit them regularly with this line, skill talent and defense around him. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) notes the 17-of-22, 221 yards, 2 TD, 1 INT line plus 11 carries for 72 yards and a score, and argues Hurts's greatest asset is emotional control. Waldman adds Hurts is going QB4-QB5 in early mocks behind [[Jayden Daniels]], [[Josh Allen]], [[Lamar Jackson]] and [[Joe Burrow]] and may be slightly overlooked, though ~100 fewer completions than the prior year is a volume consequence of [[Saquon Barkley]].
- 2025-02-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-11]]): Hurts' Super Bowl LIX game — 17 of 22, two passing touchdowns (to [[DeVonta Smith]] and [[A.J. Brown]]), one interception on a Cover-0 blitz, 11 carries for 72 yards and a rushing score — was 'classic Jalen Hurts,' decisive as a scrambler and attacking deep outside the numbers, with the pre-snap work to use [[Saquon Barkley]] as an outlet against zone-behind-blitz looks that had troubled him all season. The hosts disagree on tier: James Koh (not tracked) argues the legs, the tush push and the deep-ball accuracy make him elite and unquestionably top five; Harmon puts him around top 10-12, preferring Lamar Jackson, Josh Allen, Patrick Mahomes and Joe Burrow, citing limitations throwing over the middle that make him dependent on Brown and Smith winning outside, and noting he is in the 'Russell Wilson zone' where every offense becomes the Jalen Hurts offense — which just won a Super Bowl. Both agree he is more than good enough for this roster and locked up long-term.
- 2025-05-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-26]]): with the tush push surviving the rules vote, Waldman would take [[Jalen Hurts]] over [[Jayden Daniels]] at the current price, where Daniels is going QB3 and Hurts one spot behind. Co-host Bob Harris (Footballguys, not a tracked expert) supplied the case: Hurts has double-digit rushing touchdowns in each of the last four seasons, 52 total, 12 more than [[Josh Allen]]'s 40, with no other quarterback above 20 — Harris calls that a huge separator if you are investing in rushing equity. Hurts leads all quarterbacks in attempts inside the five over the three years the tush push has been tracked, at 40.7% of Philadelphia's carries from there. Harris attributes the softer perception to roughly 100 fewer pass attempts last year, crediting [[Saquon Barkley]]. Waldman adds Hurts is past his own rookie-hype and second-year adjustment period while Daniels is about to enter his.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Hurts' two rushing touchdowns in the opener were both red-zone scrambles on called pass plays, not Tush Push carries — Harris' point being the rushing production is real but arrives through the same mechanism as the passing game's limitations. His stated tell: 'you can tell when an unsuccessful pass play is about to happen' because Hurts hits his back foot, looks at his first read, pulls it down, and if there's no room to run he drifts sideways and throws on the move with poor results. He credits Hurts for making no big mistakes and never putting the ball in jeopardy the way [[Dak Prescott]] did, and for sealing the game on a scramble when Dallas played man without a spy. Guest JEFF ERICKSON (RotoWire, NOT a tracked expert) agreed the outing showed Hurts has 'real limitations' that Philadelphia no longer tries to coach out of him after trying two years ago — 'you'd rather have a better thrower as your quarterback for your second-round receiver.'
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): Harris calls this 'the worst of Jalen Hurts' — he stares down the rush, throws with no rhythm, and nearly got [[DeVonta Smith]] killed on a throw that landed him on his back. Harris says no quarterback in the league has a bigger swing between comfortable and uncomfortable, and Hurts was obviously uncomfortable here; he wants one game where it is tight against a good defense and Hurts is not staring at the rush. Harmon agrees Hurts 'leaves a lot of meat on the bone and just doesn't operate over the middle of the field,' while crediting him as really good at placing the ball into tight windows outside the numbers — a strength Kansas City's coverage denied him. Both note Philadelphia did not need him to be aggressive to win.
- 2025-10-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-03]]): Hurts is FantasyPros' 23rd overall player, which Harris calls crazy — you would effectively be taking him with the second-to-last pick of the second round, when passing is down and 'quarterbacks are all kind of the same.' His frame: the difference between QB3 and QB13 right now is about four fantasy points per game, which matters, but if you can upgrade elsewhere and make up those points the trade is good. Harris concedes he could probably get more for Hurts than for [[Jayden Daniels]], but says even for Hurts he'd say yes to [[Breece Hall]], [[Javonte Williams]], [[Rashee Rice]], [[DK Metcalf]], [[Cam Skattebo]] or [[Quinshon Judkins]]. He extends the same logic to [[Caleb Williams]], [[Drake Maye]], [[Baker Mayfield]], [[Patrick Mahomes]] and [[Justin Herbert]]: find the team that thinks a quarterback is a huge advantage and weasel scarce-position players out of them.
- 2025-10-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-10]]): Hurts 'continues not to suck' but is 'pretty far shy of a great player.' In the loss to the Giants he had one chance to take the game over — [[DeVonta Smith]] behind a blown coverage — and threw it five yards too far out in front of him; later he threw his first interception in 305 attempts into the flat looking for [[Jahan Dotson]], nearly returned for a pick six, which ended the competitive portion of the game. Harris says defenses are now playing zone, sitting back, keying the line toward [[Saquon Barkley]] and assuming Hurts will roll one way so they can crash that side, and that Hurts's second half was a lot of rolling out, cutting the field in half, holding the ball and then running for one yard or throwing it away. His verdict: if anything Hurts is not aggressive enough as a thrower. Hurts did get a rushing touchdown on four consecutive tush pushes but only 13 rushing yards on the night.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Hurts posted a monster fantasy day beating the Vikings on the road with zero rushing scores, instead throwing deep touchdowns to [[A.J. Brown]] and a career-long 79-yard bomb to [[DeVonta Smith]], plus a scramble-drill throw on 4th-and-4 to Brown. Harris flagged a structural red flag underneath the box score: Philadelphia is 'the most predictable running game in the NFL' -- every single snap with Hurts under center is a run, and the offense never runs play-action from under center, reportedly because Hurts is uncomfortable with his back to the defense under the new OC. Harris said defenses (Minnesota specifically) are clearly diagnosing this pre-snap, loading the box and meeting [[Saquon Barkley]] in the backfield on early downs, and that the Eagles' skill talent is bailing out a scheme playing 'with one hand tied behind their back.' He wants to see whether Philly breaks the tendency; until then, passing production is happening despite the scheme, not because of it.
- 2025-10-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-21]]): Jalen Hurts looked 'freaking awesome' in the Eagles' 28-22 win over Minnesota, throwing accurate deep balls and reading the Brian Flores defense well despite the run game struggling (Saquon Barkley finished sub-3 yards per carry). Harmon noted the Eagles broke out unusual under-center play-action -- rare for Hurts, one of the most shotgun-heavy quarterbacks in the league -- as a new wrinkle. He credited the strong game partly to [[DeVonta Smith]] calling his own touchdown play at halftime and to the passing offense finally cashing in on outside criticism, but cautioned the underlying route-concept diversity for [[A.J. Brown]] (mostly stop/go routes) hadn't fundamentally changed, leaving it unclear whether this level of play repeats.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): the same hesitance he described in [[Jordan Love]] 'has been a problem for Jalen Hurts a lot of the time throughout this entire good Eagles run'. Philadelphia's opening drive was methodical - three third-down conversions including a great third-and-10 throw to [[Will Shipley]] and a stop route to [[DeVonta Smith]] - but Hurts got peanut-punched on a quarterback draw near the goal line and lost a fumble, and Philly managed 125 first-half yards with all but 48 coming on that drive. He also blew a wide-open would-be touchdown throw to Grant Calcaterra to open the second half. Harris thinks the Eagles, off back-to-back Super Bowls, are in a kind of chill-out mode with Hurts 'a very chilled out individual', and that what they actually are offensively will only show up in the playoffs.
- 2025-11-17 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-17 (Week 11 Game Film Review)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: for the second straight week Philadelphia's passing game struggled badly, and Hurts finished 135 pass yards and 31 rushing yards, salvaged only by a tush-push rushing touchdown -- the game's only score. His one clean throw was a rollout dime to Jahan Dotson; otherwise he badly missed [[DeVonta Smith]] on a double-covered deep ball and overthrew Dallas Goedert on what should have been a big gainer. Harris compared the offense's malaise to how Philadelphia looked in the second half of 2023, worsened by right tackle Lane Johnson getting bull-rushed out of the game early by Aidan Hutchinson and never returning. He's still starting Hurts, [[A.J. Brown]] and Smith given the tush-push floor and an upcoming plus Cowboys matchup, but said he's 'on alert.'
- 2025-12-01 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-01]]): After Philadelphia's blowout loss to Chicago, Harris said the Eagles' offensive environment has soured badly over four straight messy games -- 'the quarterback feels a little bit exposed,' the offensive line isn't good, and the play-calling isn't good. He noted this same offense scored 33 on the Rams, had a big passing game against a good Vikings defense, and topped 300 yards against Denver earlier this season, yet now 'looks helpless as a kitten' and 'badly coached.' Harris said Jalen Hurts, [[Saquon Barkley]], [[A.J. Brown]] and [[DeVonta Smith]] remain talented but the offense has them 'in a bind' -- there's no fantasy team where you can bench any of them, even though the current ceiling is low.
- 2025-12-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-04]]): Hurts 'is not a bad quarterback' and flashed real explosiveness with a QB draw of over 20 yards from empty formation against the Bears, but the hosts blame Kevin Patullo's predictable scheme (mostly hitch/curl-flat concepts, bottom-five in motion and play-action rate) rather than Hurts for Philadelphia's offensive stagnation, noting he's never had a consecutive offensive coordinator at any level, college or pro.
- 2025-12-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-08]]): Previewing Monday night against the Chargers, Harris described Philadelphia's offense as in an 'existential crisis' due to line injuries and underperformance - without Lane Johnson and without adequately replacing last year's Mekhi Becton, plus a center who has missed time with a concussion. Harris said Hurts' style requires everything around him functioning well, so the shaky line has become magnified, but concluded fantasy managers are still 'pretty much locked into' starting Hurts and the rest of Philadelphia's skill players regardless.
- 2025-12-09 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Hurts threw two first-half interceptions (one sailed over Dotson's head and was nearly picked, one where he 'just stared down' DeVonta Smith), added a first-half fumble, then threw again in the second half and in overtime (though the OT pick was called a legitimately great defensive play). Harris says Hurts crystallizes as a player who 'doesn't throw well in rhythm,' is slow through his progressions, and no longer makes plays on the move -- when the Chargers flushed him from the pocket he seemed destined to throw it away or run out of bounds. Harris spreads blame across a freelancing receiver corps, a beat-up/assignment-blowing offensive line, and a head coach he calls 'always five seconds away from a nervous breakdown' and not temperamentally suited to be an NFL coach, while admitting he could be wrong about that read. Despite the ugly tape, with a home date vs. the Raiders in Week 15, Harris says he can't get Hurts out of his top-10 quarterbacks for the week.
- 2025-12-15 — According to Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (untracked guest on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-15]]), discussing with [[Chris Harris]]: Philadelphia finally called actual designed run plays for Hurts again in a 31-0 blowout of the Raiders, which Doherty says gives him a bit more faith since 'his viability is not geared around the pass game... it's geared around other stuff.' He's careful to frame it as not a full buy-in -- 'I'm not necessarily looking at it like a positive... like Jalen Hurts has won me back over' -- but says it's 'just enough' to keep him from dropping Hurts below other shaky quarterbacks (Joe Burrow, Lamar Jackson, Sam Darnold) who have equally concerning questions right now. He notes it's 'not theoretical anymore' -- the run designs are visibly back on tape.
- 2026-01-13 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-13]]): after Philadelphia's wild-card loss to San Francisco, Harmon pushed back on 'Hurts is mid' takes while also declining to defend him as more than that -- 'he is what he is.' Per Next Gen Stats, Hurts ranked 24th in the NFL in passer rating (88.6) on intermediate throws (10-19 air yards), just ahead of J.J. McCarthy and Tua Tagovailoa, with 3 TD and 2 INT there on the season, but he was 4th-best in the league on throws of 20-plus air yards (9 TD, 1 INT), trailing only Lamar Jackson, Drake Maye and Matthew Stafford. Harmon called him a guy whose limitations 'you have to work around' -- not a tier-one or tier-two QB, but capable of top-five-level play in the biggest moments (he has won a Super Bowl). He floated Cliff Kingsbury as a scheme fit given Philadelphia's skill talent, though he's skeptical of Kingsbury generally unless surrounded by star talent. He expects Hurts remains the starter, but wonders if Philadelphia adds QB competition if backup Tanner McKee gets traded given a thin 2026 QB market.
- 2026-01-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-22]]): Hurts' long-standing flaw -- holding the ball too long the more invested he gets in a play, leading to forced throws and fumbles -- has never been coached out because he's never had back-to-back seasons with the same offensive coordinator, going back to college. Waldman ties this to a mastery framework: repetition of the same system, not raw talent, is what lets a quarterback play fast, comparing it to Philip Rivers still starting at 44 off pure system mastery. Both hosts argue Philadelphia's offense needs to stay run-heavy and under-center to protect Hurts -- a credible run game and believable play-action, including his own boot-and-keep ability, does 'a massive favor' for the whole unit. A Matt Nagy hire would be disastrous because Nagy refuses to run under-center or 12/13 personnel; both instead pushed for Jim Bob Cooter, whose Shane Steichen-lineage offense (under-center friendly, already proven with [[Saquon Barkley]] and [[DeVonta Smith]]) would preserve continuity.
- 2026-02-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-12]]) [Dynasty]: with Philadelphia moving on from Kevin Petulo to new OC Sean Mannion and passing-game coordinator Josh Grizzard (previously paired with Baker Mayfield in Tampa Bay), Harmon expects a shift toward more shotgun, downfield passing and attacking the intermediate middle -- an area the old Eagles scheme, built around static, stop-route-heavy concepts, rarely targeted. Harmon notes Hurts, who turns 28 in August, has a track record of answering doubters with an offseason leap and 'wouldn't be surprised' if he adapts to a more varied passing structure, though he acknowledges Hurts has looked comparatively static over the last couple of seasons, particularly this past year.
- 2026-04-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-14]]): pushing back on a recent article framing Hurts as a near net-negative for the Eagles' offense, Harmon says Hurts is a good player and squarely in the top-10 QB conversation, though not top five. He argues the Eagles' offensive structure has long catered to Hurts's stylistic preferences the way it did for Carson Wentz late in his Eagles tenure -- not inherently bad, but it creates real schematic limitations as the league trends elsewhere. Co-host James Koh (untracked) adds that shaping an offense around a starting QB's preferences is true leaguewide (citing Joe Burrow's shotgun-only tendencies and Kyler Murray avoiding the intermediate middle of the field), and notes that good playcallers like Kellen Moore and Shane Steichen got Hurts to 'meet in the middle' during the 2023 Super Bowl run; both hosts flag that the Eagles' new playcaller wants a fuller schematic pivot this year, and whether Hurts can evolve to fit it is an open question.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-05-07]]) [Superflex]: ranked Hurts noticeably below market again this year, which untracked co-host Josh calls 'a Chris Harris staple' -- a recurring, multi-year pattern of Harris fading Hurts relative to consensus. Harris concedes the strategy 'serves me pretty well,' i.e. he stands by the track record of being lower than the field on Hurts without walking back the position.
- 2026-07-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host, not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-28]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: both hosts flag a fit concern between Hurts and Philadelphia's undersized 2026 receiver room ([[DeVonta Smith]], Makai Lemon, Dontavian Wicks are all under 205 lbs, only Wicks over 6'0'). Harmon frames it as a likely 'trial year' adjustment for Hurts within a more McVay/Packers-style scheme new play-caller Sean Mannion is expected to run, while crediting Hurts as unfairly criticized and capable of adjusting. Koh is more pointed, arguing that unlike every other team building its roster around its franchise quarterback, Philadelphia's receiver room 'is not built for Jalen Hurts' -- calling it either a misread of what he likes or a signal the front office doesn't view him as a true franchise quarterback, contrasting it with how even Carolina built around Bryce Young. Both agree Hurts activated AJ Brown's go-route game beyond what Tennessee got from him, crediting that as an underrated Hurts strength.
