# Jauan Jennings

## Expert Takes

- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): Jennings is for real, and Waldman publicly apologized for steering someone off him. Career highs across the board — three touchdowns, 175 yards, 11 catches — against a Rams defense whose coverage he called soft and mistake-ridden, so some of it was schedule. But Waldman said Jennings also made three or four spectacular catches late, spectacular either for toughness or for going over the back of a defender to win the ball. His scouting frame: Jennings looked like 'Brandon Marshall's little brother,' the same way he did at Tennessee, where Waldman had him pegged as an easy 225-pound receiver — and the media reaction when he weighed in at the Senior Bowl around 206 to 208 pounds was disbelief, because he plays like 225 to 230. The caveat is availability of others: Jennings is a real asset as long as [[Deebo Samuel]] and [[George Kittle]] are out, and with only one of them out he still gives you a puncher's chance as a flex. Waldman also noted [[Brandon Aiyuk]] does not look fully back, is making lapses, and came up short on the deep opportunities this soft coverage should have handed him.
- 2024-09-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-26]]): after a three-touchdown career game versus the Rams, Harmon says the 49ers are content to lean on him and reiterates a long-standing view — 'Jauan Jennings is a good player,' who blocks his ass off and is clutch on third downs, and whom San Francisco extended even after drafting [[Ricky Pearsall]] in the first round. Koh adds the NextGenStats context: no receiver has been more successful against press coverage, with Jennings catching all nine of his press targets for 147 yards and two touchdowns — a direct inversion of teammate [[Brandon Aiyuk]]'s press numbers. Neither host projects the touchdown rate forward; the framing is that Jennings is a legitimately good role player being rewarded while Aiyuk works back into football shape.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): asked whether Jennings is the best receiver in San Francisco, Waldman said 'right now I'm feeling it,' with an explicit expiry date. He recalled thinking Jennings was the next Brandon Marshall coming out of Tennessee until the weigh-in came back at 206 pounds despite playing like 228 — a beast at times, but with hands issues in how he attacked the football and without a full route tree. What has changed is development in San Francisco: a real trust level, especially against zone coverage and in contested-catch situations, with Brock Purdy unafraid to fit the ball into him downfield. Waldman qualified it — Brandon Aiyuk will step up and Deebo Samuel is a factor when healthier, so Jennings is the best receiver playing right now but that will change as Aiyuk rounds into form. He added that George Kittle is the best overall pass catcher on the field for San Francisco.
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Jennings has gone from steady third-down contributor to a genuine difference-maker — top 10 in EPA per target and first downs per route run, and one of the most efficient receivers in the NFL this year, with 58.3% team success rate when targeted. Alignment-wise Harmon has him at 48.9% slot and 50.7% out wide, which makes him a slot-flanker, not the X the 49ers now need after [[Brandon Aiyuk]]'s injury; Harmon says he isn't consistently a man-coverage beater except on very short routes near the line. James Koh (not tracked) is more bullish for fantasy and calls him a must-add, one of the top waiver-wire claims of the week, expecting him to walk into a big target share.
- 2024-11-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]]) — an extended screen-share breakdown, the longest segment of the show — a buy on Jennings in his new X receiver role. Kyle Shanahan confirmed after the game it was Jennings's first time at X and that he will play there the rest of the year; Jennings then led the team in routes and in percentage of snaps outside, at 75 percent lined up wide against Tampa Bay versus a roughly 50-50 inside/outside split before. Harmon admits he never thought of Jennings, a power slot, dirty-work run blocker and late-down player, as a [[Brandon Aiyuk]] replacement, but says the NGS route chart makes it inspired: Jennings's chart is dominated by out-breakers from both sides, and 10 of his 19 catches last regular season came on third down, when man coverage rates spike. Since X is where you have to win isolation reps, man-coverage production is the relevant signal — the reason Reception Perception weights man over zone for X receivers. His caveat: Jennings will not take the top off and will not be elite in isolation like Aiyuk, but he can handle the role.
- 2024-11-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-20]]): Jennings passes the eyeball test and is San Francisco's most effective receiver right now 'by a pretty decent margin.' Fantasy Points data had him at a 45% share of first-read targets in the Seattle game, and his yards per route run sits just behind [[Nico Collins]], [[A.J. Brown]] and [[Justin Jefferson]]; his success rate and EPA per target are both high. Harmon has long had a fascination with him: he gets open against man coverage, settles well, runs routes to the right depth, which is why he is a third-down chain-mover and why he has translated to the X spot with [[Brandon Aiyuk]] out. The caveat — he is a possession threat, not a frightening separator over the top, so an offense with Jennings as its No. 1 is not scary. Harmon buys him as a No. 2/3 long term alongside a healthy Aiyuk and [[Ricky Pearsall]], and flags a possible contract squeeze since Jennings signed only a two-year extension.
- 2024-12-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-10]]): asked true or false on whether Jennings is San Francisco's number one receiver over [[Deebo Samuel]], Harmon answers true — but frames it as a statement about Samuel rather than a promotion for Jennings. He likes Jennings, calls his league-wide efficiency metrics really good, especially against zone coverage, and says he should be part of the 49ers' future at the position, but he is not dangerous enough down the field to be a true number one. Harmon's preferred structure: a healthy [[Brandon Aiyuk]] as the one, Jennings as the two, and [[Ricky Pearsall]] developing in the slot, which he calls a pretty good group. Koh agrees without reservation.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-19]]): 'no question' Jennings has been the better real-life player than [[Deebo Samuel]] over recent weeks, and Harmon gives him the edge for Week 16 even though he expects Jalen Ramsey to shadow Deebo as the bigger name. Against cover two — Miami is fifth in the NFL in cover-two rate — Jennings sits right behind [[George Kittle]] among San Francisco pass catchers, with Kittle, Jennings, [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and Samuel all above two yards per route run versus cover two and Samuel the lowest of the four. Harmon's caveat is protection: he rates Miami's pass rush and run defense as much improved, singling out Calais Campbell and rookie Chop Robinson, so [[Brock Purdy]]'s ability to deliver under pressure is the swing factor. Jennings also had two drops in the Rams game.
- 2025-01-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-09]]): Jennings earns honorable mentions for both ball winner and most improved. Harmon says he jumped from role player to high-volume player and stayed very efficient, and that he has always been good on third downs, against man coverage and on out-breaking routes — this season he took that role to another level. The praise is deliberately unglamorous: not highlight-reel in the way [[Tee Higgins]] or [[Mike Evans]] are, but consistently moving the chains with defenders draped all over him. Koh backs it with Next Gen Stats — third-most receiving yards in contested-catch situations at a 56% contested catch rate, which he calls really good.
- 2025-01-28 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]], relayed alongside [[Matt Harmon]]: Jennings is the specific archetype Buffalo should chase — a receiver you can rely on to 'clutch up' on third and fourth down, decent against man coverage, someone who simply gets open in a big spot. Harmon does not endorse the name but agrees on the profile, adding that the version he wants is a man-beater who can break inside into the intermediate middle of the field, naming [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and [[Nico Collins]] as the type and noting both were mid-round picks who took a couple of seasons to arrive — the skill set is scoutable, not a top-five-pick requirement.
- 2025-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-05]]): Harmon is on record as a big fan of Jennings as a dirty-work grinder and blocker, and says he was told before the 2024 draft that the Vikings and Kevin O'Connell were obsessed with finding their own version of him — which Harmon found funny given how productive Jennings became once San Francisco's injuries hit. The caveat: Harmon thinks Jennings was somewhat overstretched as the 49ers' X receiver in 2024, i.e. good player, wrong job description. He uses Jennings as the archetype comp for what [[Cooper Kupp]] can still be at this stage of his career.
- 2025-02-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-26]]): Jennings is the proof-of-concept for role expansion in San Francisco — Harmon had been asking for years what Jennings would do with a bigger role, and 2024 answered it, showing the role can in fact be expanded for a tough, reliable player who was fourth in pedigree in that room behind [[Brandon Aiyuk]], [[Deebo Samuel]] and [[Ricky Pearsall]]. Harmon's concern for 2025 is that there is 'no Jauan Jennings on this roster other than the actual Jauan Jennings' — nobody else is due for that kind of breakout. He profiles Jennings as a flanker/slot who also took some X snaps in 2024, a fit the Shanahan system rewards. The caveat, raised by co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) and not contested: Jennings is not a burner, so he cannot replace the vertical element if Aiyuk is out.
- 2025-05-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-16]]): Jennings is an interesting piece who has played extra-receiver and big-slot roles, and Harmon admits a long-standing affinity for him and notes many fantasy players got him off waivers in 2024 on the back of a strong yards-per-route-run season. He sees real design upside in pairing him with [[Ricky Pearsall]] — one in the slot, one at flanker, motioning at the snap, condensed formations and switch releases — but at a consensus range of WR39 to WR45 alongside Pearsall and [[Brandon Aiyuk]], Pearsall is the one he wants because he goes latest.
- 2025-05-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Jennings at WR38 (about the sixth round) is a fine price and he is 'getting a ton' of him in drafts, though he would rather the price fall two or three rounds — he prefers taking Jennings and [[Ricky Pearsall]] in the eighth to tenth, since a WR3 season likely needs [[Brandon Aiyuk]], [[George Kittle]] and [[Christian McCaffrey]] hurt again. He finished WR24 in 2024 with career highs of 113 targets, 77 catches, 975 yards and six touchdowns, including a 175-yard, 11-catch Week 3 and five games over 88 yards; Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) noted he likely clears 1,000 yards without a mid-season hip injury and a Week 18 ejection. Waldman's structural case: [[George Kittle]] is a man-beater and Pearsall's speed stretches coverage, so Jennings gets worked open in zones. Waldman expects Jennings to be the more valuable of the two early, with Pearsall overtaking him as the season goes.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Jennings is San Francisco's fallback answer at X for the first stretch of the season. Harmon notes Jennings already played some X for the 49ers last year, so if [[Brandon Aiyuk]] is unavailable to start the year and [[DeMarcus Robinson]] serves a likely three-game suspension and no veteran is added, the cleanest alignment is Jennings at X with [[Ricky Pearsall]] at flanker or slot and depth bodies rotating behind them. Harmon calls the 49ers' need a temporary asterisk rather than a real hole — roughly the first six weeks — with Isaiah Hodgins, Russell Gage, fourth-rounder Jordan Watkins (5-foot-11, 200, Ole Miss, uncharted) and Trent Taylor as camp options, and [[Jacob Cowing]] a possible second-year step after making no impact as a rookie.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): Jennings is a steal at WR37 ADP — on projection he belongs in the top 30, and Waldman has him at 25 to 26. With Deebo Samuel gone and [[Brandon Aiyuk]] not expected back for the start of the season by Kyle Shanahan's own account, Jennings is one of only two proven receivers San Francisco has alongside DeMarcus Robinson, which is also part of why Waldman is so bullish on [[Christian McCaffrey]] carrying the offense. Bob Harris (not tracked) underlines the point that Jennings goes four picks ahead of [[Ricky Pearsall]] at WR41 and calls him the one to draft.
- 2025-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]), who has just charted Jennings for the first time with the profile due on the site: Jennings is a solid number two you can roll out and feel fine about, but not a number one. He is not an exceptional separator — a 67% success rate versus man and 77.6% versus zone, both right around average — and where he genuinely excels is at the catch point, barely dropping the ball with an 81.8% contested catch rate. He uncovers well in the intermediate area (dig 75%, curl 80%) and plays all three receiver positions, which is valuable to San Francisco, but he does not bring explosive playability at X. Harmon flags that a lot of his 900-plus-yard season came on contested catches, which always worries him a little. On the contract standoff, Harmon sides with the player — $7.8 million is light when [[Tutu Atwell]] makes $10 million — while conceding the 49ers have no cash and that Jennings's separation profile does not obviously justify a big raise. James Koh (not tracked) says the Niners will neither pay nor trade him.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]] (ASR: 'Juwan/DeJuan Jennings'): Nguyen likes Jennings a lot, maybe more than most, for his inside-outside versatility and because he is one of the better run-blocking receivers and a great fit in the 49ers offense. But he has not seen Jennings at all in camp — a calf injury, with whispers that it is contract-related, which Nguyen declined to speculate on beyond noting it is now typical for San Francisco receivers with contract situations to sit out camp and start slow. Even if Jennings plays week one, Nguyen thinks flying out of the gate is a hard ask with no practice behind him.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]]): Jennings is holding in for more money in San Francisco and Harris flatly does not believe the reported calf injury — he calls the whole thing a pissing contest between the two parties, including the threat that the 49ers might put Jennings on IR to start the year. He lowered Jennings a few spots in the receiver ranks and about a round overall, from a seventh-round pick to roughly an eighth. His expectation is that Jennings gets a salary bump and the calf 'will miraculously feel amazing,' but as with [[Terry McLaurin]], going this deep into practices with no work increases the chance of a slow beginning even if he plays Week 1. JJ Zachariason (guest, not a tracked expert) said Jennings could be solid but rated [[Ricky Pearsall]] as the likelier breakout of that group.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-27]]): Jennings could open the season on IR with a calf issue, which Harmon and Koh both note sarcastically is surely unrelated to his contract dispute. Harmon calls him a good player with a really good Reception Perception profile — good hands, good after the catch, good in the intermediate area — but explicitly not a full-field separator. On the money, he lists players paid more per year than Jennings including [[Kendrick Bourne]] and [[Brandin Cooks]], and notes he sits behind rookie-contract players like [[Rome Odunze]] and [[Drake London]], calling that insane and saying San Francisco is now more motivated to get a deal done given the injuries around him.
- 2025-08-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the 49ers put out a single statement saying both that they are not trading Jennings and that he is questionable with a calf — which Harris finds funny and reads as confirmation Jennings must have asked for a trade over his contract. He says he does not know what Jennings' deal is going to wind up being. On the depth behind him, San Francisco has [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling] re-signed after cutting Russell Gage, [[Skyy Moore]] acquired by trade and rookie [[Jordan Watkins]]; Harris says the most interesting name of that group would actually be [[Kendrick Bourne]], just cut by New England, as a former 49er who already knows the offense.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris suspects Jennings's absence is contract-related rather than calf-related — or possibly both — while noting the 49ers have no incentive to tell anyone which. Jennings is not a sure Week 1 participant. Because the rest of the 49ers receiver group you drafted around him is also active, Harris says you have no reason to push the envelope with Jennings in Week 1 lineups.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-04]]): the 'incredible healing power' of Jennings' calf arrived the moment the 49ers agreed to pay him, per Adam Schefter adding $3M to his 2025 contract after Jennings turned down a longer-term extension — Harris treats team and agent injury statements as PR he is under no obligation to believe, saying 'the agents lie, the teams lie'. Jennings will be in the Week 1 lineup against Seattle, but Harris flags a different worry: after weeks of holding in and missing practice, what shape is he in? He cites the 49ers doing this exact thing with [[Brandon Aiyuk]] the prior summer, when Aiyuk was active but barely used through September. Harris ranked Jennings for Week 1 but deliberately not high enough that you feel obligated to start him — waiting a week to see how it looks 'makes some good sense'.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-05]]): Harmon is buying Jennings as a useful, productive player after a three-million-dollar incentive adjustment, while capping expectations — he sees him as the fourth option behind [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[George Kittle]] and [[Ricky Pearsall]], albeit the on-ball guy until [[Brandon Aiyuk]] returns. He read the contract outcome as Jennings losing the leverage fight: one strong year as a depth receiver who stepped up, and the incentive-only bump signals the Niners see Aiyuk and Pearsall as the room's future. On the charting: a solid Reception Perception profile, 81.8 percent contested catch rate, average-but-not-bad separation against man and zone, excellent intermediate separation on digs, outs, curls and comebacks, but in space on only 26 percent of sampled catches and 62nd in yards after catch per reception despite going down on first contact on just 38.5 percent of charted in-space attempts — he breaks tackles without ripping off big gains, so a reliable chain-mover rather than an explosive player. Koh (not tracked) countered that his big games were real, listing a 12-target, 11-catch, 175-yard, three-touchdown Week 3 plus four other double-digit-target games, and noted they all came from injury-driven opportunity that the staff did not otherwise give him.
