# Malachi Corley

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]), on Shrine Game prospects: "this kid's gonna be one of those sleepers, he already is." Another running-back-build receiver who is excellent after the catch and adjusts well to the ball in zone coverage — "runs over people or through people." Explicit DJ Moore comp, qualified as a "mid-grade version with a chance at some upgrades available" rather than the finished article. Expects an early-career role even if not full-featured right away, with real fantasy relevance if the role expands — "we will like that as fantasy managers."
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-01]]): a reinforcing update with more technical specificity — "there's so much more in him than meets the eye" beyond the size-driven [[Deebo Samuel]] (5'10", 215 lbs) comp everyone reaches for on sight. Flags real, already-visible route-running nuance and catch-point work "away from frame," better than the early pre-Senior-Bowl scouting consensus had credited him for.

- 2024-02-29 — According to **James Koh** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]): flags him among the draft's "freak athletes" — a 5'10"/215 "fire hydrant" who consistently breaks through arm tackles, consistent with the after-catch power comps already logged above.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): his speed is probably a better mismatch for safeties than for cornerbacks, which shifts his best projection to the slot. Two-way goes from the slot also mean he isn't as reliant on a vast array of stem work and route setups — which is good news, since he currently lacks that repertoire and would need it to become a strong outside starter. Can still contribute early from the slot without mastering it.
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: called the Jets' pick 'a historical Jets pick' in the negative sense unless the situation breaks perfectly — Aaron Rodgers healthy all year, an already-loaded offense, and Corley used as a schemed gadget player. He praised Corley as a terrific runner after the catch who does good work over the middle, comparing his best-case usage to how Green Bay deployed Allen Lazard in his best season, winning via scheme rather than man coverage. His core concern is that 'gadget players tend to get lost in terms of volume' — a couple of big games with little else — and he doubts Corley projects as a true man-to-man winner at the NFL level based on his tape, calling it 'a singular path' to success.
- 2024-05-02 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): Waldman flagged Corley as his overrated/risk pick, worried the Jets will use him as a gadget piece in space rather than develop him as a true man-to-man slot winner — a skill Waldman says Corley wasn't asked to show at Western Kentucky, where he got the ball immediately because he was simply better than everyone on the field. Waldman fears the Aaron Rodgers-is-here-one-more-year timeline won't allow that development, drawing a parallel to how Rodgers publicly praised then abandoned young Green Bay receivers like Romeo Doubs and [[Christian Watson]] once the mental/pre-snap demands ramped up in camp. Brandon Angelo disagreed, calling the draft room's excitement about Corley real — they wanted a Randall Cobb-type piece for Rodgers — and arguing Corley can be underrated if used as a centerpiece the way [[Deebo Samuel]] was in his best 49ers years; Angelo's bigger concern is regime stability, since a coaching change could redefine his usage entirely, as happened to Laviska Shenault under Urban Meyer.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman is 'feeling' Corley as an under-the-radar asset only if his ADP stays outside round 14; he projects Corley into an Allen Lazard-style role — a limited route runner who struggles to win man coverage but thrives in zone and offers plus yards-after-catch, a job Lazard himself no longer seems invested in. He noted Aaron Rodgers could exploit Corley for occasional big games, and framed him as a better [[Best Ball]] dart-throw than a redraft target; if Corley's price climbs into rounds 8-12, Waldman is out entirely.
- 2024-06-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-11]]) rookie profile: Corley (5'11"/215) was an extreme 'create-a-touch' gadget receiver at Western Kentucky -- 99.3% of snaps off the line of scrimmage, 89% in the slot/backfield, and saw press coverage on just 1% of routes, with 52% of his charted routes being screens or slants. Harmon called the [[Deebo Samuel]] comp (via Lance Zierlein) an 'instant red flag' role-based comp, not a play-style match, though he noted real value: only broken down on first contact 44% of in-space touches, elite after-catch power ('one step above a running back'), and 85% success vs zone coverage (well above the 80% benchmark, though inflated by short-route volume) versus a below-benchmark 66% vs man coverage. Harmon rated him a clearly better overall prospect than fellow gadget comps [[Rondale Moore]] and [[Wan'Dale Robinson]], projecting a No. 3/slot-gadget role for the Jets rather than a true receiver ceiling, and flagged that the touch-design responsibility falls to the coaching staff (not GM/OC-in-practice [[Aaron Rodgers]], who called Corley his favorite receiver in the draft and invited him to stay in his guest house) -- a dependency that makes Harmon 'not super confident.' Corley's arrival is also seen as capping [[Xavier Gipson]]'s snaps.
- 2024-07-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-09]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon's charting has Malachi Corley as a slot-only (99.3% of snaps off the line, saw press coverage on just 1% of routes) gadget-type receiver heavy on slants (36.4%) and screens (16.2%), with a solid-not-great 66% success rate vs. man but a strong 84.8% vs. zone; he had a number of charted drops and isn't a contested-catch threat, so Harmon wants him schemed underneath touches to use his after-the-catch ability rather than as a high-volume target earner. James Coe (not tracked) was lower on Corley's contested-catch ability than some evaluators but agreed he's a fun after-the-catch watch, noting YAC production is notoriously hard to sustain at the NFL level. Both see him blocking [[Xavier Gipson]]'s path to slot snaps.
