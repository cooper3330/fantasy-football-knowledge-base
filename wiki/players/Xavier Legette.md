# Xavier Legette

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]), on Senior Bowl prospects: draws a DK Metcalf comp from most of the industry, which Waldman thinks is defensible — big, physical, fast enough to make plays. His own range puts the outcome "somewhere between Alshon Jeffery and DK Metcalf," which he calls plenty of athletic goods to play in the league barring off-field issues. Names him specifically as part of why he considers this a genuinely strong receiver draft class, independent of what analytics-model-based evaluators might say about him.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-01]]): a real tempering after live Senior Bowl reps, not a reversal — Angelo: "I was hoping to see more... on tape sometimes it's a great play if you're a guy of that stature, but just everything looked a little too sloppy, a little too forced," and expected more dominance given his size than he actually saw this week. Both hosts keep the DK Metcalf/Alshon Jeffery archetype framing from the prior episode, but now flag real questions about whether he lands at the Metcalf end of that range or slides toward the middle/lower tier.

- 2024-02-29 — According to guest **Eric Froton** (NBC Sports college football analyst — not a tracked expert) and **James Koh** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, hosted solo by Koh with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]) [Dynasty]: a mixed combine-week read building on the Senior Bowl tempering already logged above. Froton: elite deep-ball production — 7 of 10 contested deep-shot catches, 20 yards/catch and a perfect 158.3 passer rating when targeted against man coverage in the SEC, 25% of targets downfield — but not a true separator given a bigger, "clunkier" frame that doesn't snap off routes cleanly at his listed size (~6'1", 220-225); floats him as the group's likeliest bust if that limitation doesn't translate. Koh, independently: still "torn" on him — strong athletic traits and the ability to stack corners deep, but saw far fewer contested catches made at the Senior Bowl than expected and still has real questions about his overall route-running polish.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): gets DK Metcalf comps, which Waldman doesn't like — he prefers a comp to Metcalf's former Seattle teammate instead (implying [[Tyler Lockett]]), without getting carried away with it. The reasoning: Legette has the hip mobility to drop his weight in forced pursuit and overrun angles, and if he applies that to his route running, he becomes a much better man-to-man receiver rather than just a boundary/contested-catch player — at that point the comparison worth discussing is guys like David Boston and [[A.J. Brown]].
- 2024-04-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-18]]) [Dynasty]: Legette's 2023 RP profile is a genuinely difficult evaluation — a plus athlete (6'1, 221 lbs, 4.39 40, 40" vertical) with elite contested-catch ability (81.8% catch rate on a 28.9% target rate) and strong zone-coverage success (81.4%), but raw man/press coverage success (19th percentile) reflecting his unusual 5th-year-only breakout after four near-empty college seasons. Harmon comps his best-case outside role to Alshon Jeffrey but says he'd rather deploy him in tight splits and motion running slants/digs, Deebo Samuel-style, to hide the man-coverage limitations; he calls Legette an easy Round 2 pick with the higher ceiling but lower floor of the two prospects discussed, and notes he is more landing-spot dependent than Troy Franklin, with LaViska Shenault-type misuse as a real risk. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) largely agreed, emphasizing Legette's play strength and subtle hand-fighting as NFL-translatable traits.
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]) [Dynasty]: called Legette the class's likeliest player to disappoint his next team, worried he gets typecast into a full-time X role despite better zone-coverage tape -- a path he compares to [[Treylon Burks]] or [[Jonathan Mingo]] being scheme-mismanaged in Carolina. He noted Legette has said the Panthers told him they'll pick him at 33 if he's on the board, which Harmon dismisses as standard pre-draft flattery that means nothing. Separately, Harmon gave Legette the top contested-catch grade among the class's non-top-three ball winners (Legette, Coleman, Baker) at an 81.8% contested-catch rate, praising subtle, strong hand-fighting that creates late separation rather than an obvious push-off. Measurables: 6'1"/221, sub-4.4 forty, 40" vertical -- "top of the class" athletically. Net: freak-athlete upside and elite ball skills, but real scheme/role risk if he lands with a staff without a plan for him.
- 2024-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-30]]): Harmon is mixed on Legette -- a 19th-percentile success rate vs. man press ('sucks') but a solid 81.4% vs. zone, paired with elite measurables (6'1"/221, 4.39 forty, 40-inch vertical) and excellent contested-catch hands. He compares Legette's projected usage (crossers, deep posts, jet sweeps, per new OC Dave Canales' own post-draft description) to either a vertical-X Alshon Jeffery type or a non-gimmicky [[Deebo Samuel]] role, and says he needs 30-40% slot reps to be efficient. Harmon's conclusion: a useful player, maybe a high-end two, but the lack of downfield man-coverage separation caps his ceiling short of a true No. 1 -- 'go find me a below-average separator who's been a legit number one; that guy doesn't exist.' Harmon expects Legette to pair with Diontae Johnson and Adam Thielen in a meaningfully upgraded Panthers trio versus 2023.
- 2024-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (untracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-07]]) [Best Ball]: both take the under on Vegas's 700.5-yard line, with Koh's median projection at 49 receptions, 632 yards, 5.2 TDs. Koh says he 'absolutely loves' Legette as a prospect and real-life player but not from a rookie fantasy angle, citing Bryce Young's uncertain year-two trajectory, a run-heavy Dave Canales scheme, and a crowded backfield (Chuba Hubbard, Miles Sanders, Jonathan Brooks, Rashad Penny). Harmon adds that Canales has already signaled [[Diontae Johnson]] as the offense's X receiver and primary read/'target magnet,' with [[Adam Thielen]] the clear #2 (outside in 2-WR sets, slot in 3-WR), leaving Legette projected third on the team in targets -- likely starting slow and getting more involved as the season progresses. Both flag upside for a single splash touchdown despite a modest floor, with Koh estimating something closer to 500 receiving yards is plausible if the passing volume doesn't materialize.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman confirms as '1,000% true' the pre-draft reports that Legette needs to refine his route running, saying Carolina likely drafted him as high as they did partly because owner David Tepper wanted a local product, but also because his ability to drop his weight, stop quickly and flip his hips flashes real timing-route upside — he just isn't there yet. Waldman explains NFL separation comes from the top of the route stem (the sudden stop-and-drive), not the first five college-style yards, which is exactly where Legette needs development. Comping him to fellow Panther [[Jonathan Mingo]] and, historically, Terrace Marshall, Waldman sees Legette as a bit shorter, stouter and faster than Mingo, with comparable catch radius and YAC ability — but calls Carolina's overall receiver plan under-organized.
- 2024-06-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-20]]): despite the DK Metcalf-style big-body-X comps, Harmon doesn't expect Carolina to line Legette up as an outside X receiver. Citing how the Panthers coaching staff has talked about him publicly — getting him on the move, crossing routes, backfield work, some rushing plays — Harmon compares the usage plan to a Deebo Samuel-lite role (not full running-back usage, but off-the-line and in motion often), most likely at flanker, rotating slot reps with Adam Thielen. Notes his RP profile's strengths (crossing routes, zone coverage success) support that plan, alongside real weaknesses elsewhere in the profile. Expects him to start the year as the nominal third receiver behind Diontae Johnson (X) and Thielen (Z in two-WR sets, kicking inside in 11 personnel, opening the Z spot for Legette). Harmon: trusts Canales/Idzik with his development but doesn't expect heavy volume immediately. [Dynasty]
- 2024-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-25]]): Legette is a raw rather than bad prospect — 19th percentile success rate vs man and press, 62nd percentile vs zone, winning on in-breaking routes (digs, slants, flats) and roughly average on the out. Harmon's stated skepticism is the archetype: a fifth-year breakout, older, winning mostly on athleticism, who will need role catering and is not ready to play outside day one, much like [[Jonathan Mingo]]. His best case is a [[Christian Watson]] rookie year — 611 yards, seven touchdowns, nearly 15 yards a catch, low usage and high yield — while noting Legette said publicly that day he had a rough start to camp. Koh is openly higher, calling it a man crush: 6-foot-1, 221 pounds, a 4.39 with a 40-inch vertical, big games of 9-178 vs North Carolina and 9-217 vs Jacksonville State, with contested-catch strength and ball tracking he considers hard to teach.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): Waldman is the low man. He didn't think Legette was a first-round talent (more a round-two player), calls him a faster Alshon Jeffery who needs route work, and says his ceiling rises sharply only if he proves he can run timing routes — which Waldman doubts. He expects Carolina to shoehorn him into screens, RPOs and motion as a Kenny Golladay-type without the surrounding talent to generate the safety and linebacker mismatches Golladay got in Detroit. Waldman projects about 60 targets, 36 receptions, 10-11 yards per catch and a score, and says he'd rather just use Deebo-style motion touches on [[Deebo Samuel]]-like cheaper option Ameer Marcet-Smith already on the roster. Guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) is a little higher at 79 targets, and shares the concern that a late-first pick shouldn't be limited to screens.
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon is 'really nervous' about Legette's rookie season. A foot injury (not broken) has him running with the second team, and his RP profile flags him as a developmental player — lost offseason time hurts any rookie receiver but hurts a developmental one far more, unlike [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]], who at least already knew how to run routes and be a pro. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) goes further and declares the rookie season over for production purposes, stacking the reasons: WR3 behind two legitimate target earners in [[Diontae Johnson]] and [[Adam Thielen]], a run-heavy staff with [[Chuba Hubbard]] and [[Jonathan Brooks]], and now [[Jonathan Mingo]] eating snaps. Both stress they are not writing off the career — Koh says he loves the tape — only the 2024 week-to-week production.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: asked what he would tell Legette investors given his enthusiasm for [[Jonathan Mingo]], Waldman said 'wait till next year.' He expects Carolina to use Legette on specialty plays — RPOs, screen passes, occasional fade routes — the kind of manufactured touches he compared to [[Gabriel Davis]] type usage, rather than a full route-tree role. The hope is that he develops into a full-fledged route runner and becomes a more reliable fantasy option next year or the year after. Waldman also joked that David Tepper should stop writing on napkins and that Legette should tune out what is said about him.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-12]]): both hosts were surprised that Legette led Carolina in Week 1 targets with seven, ahead of [[Diontae Johnson]]'s six, turning them into four catches for 35 yards. Harmon frames the rookie as part of the athleticism injection Carolina deliberately added around [[Bryce Young]] alongside Johnson and [[Adam Thielen]], and says he liked the pieces they brought in. The caveat is that Carolina started pulling starters in the fourth quarter of a lopsided loss, so some of the target edge is garbage-time distribution rather than a settled pecking order.
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): with [[Adam Thielen]] on IR, Koh expects Legette to get a real runway. Harmon calls him a big-time project receiver who is not ready to be a true number one, but says Carolina does not need him to be, since [[Diontae Johnson]] can take the X snaps. Harmon's core warning is deployment: running Legette out as a big boundary X is the worst thing they can do, and he compares it directly to [[Treylon Burks]], whose career the Titans damaged by doing exactly that. He wants Legette off the ball or in the slot on horizontal routes where he can break open, describing him as a non-gadgety [[Deebo Samuel]] type rather than a curls, comebacks, outs and go-routes receiver; he got 40 percent slot routes in Week 3 with Thielen at 76.5 percent, and Harmon expects a Legette-versus-[[Jonathan Mingo]] competition for slot reps. Koh adds the Reception Perception prospect profile: his best routes were the dig and the slant, both after-catch routes for a 6-foot-1, 220-pound athlete who ran a 4.39 with a 40-inch vertical. Koh notes the 44 percent success rate on the nine is well below the roughly 50 percent benchmark, and Harmon reframes it — he is a ball winner downfield, not a separator — with Koh wanting him hunted on the seam against nickel corners. Both expect slow development. Legette had two catches, two targets and 26 yards after the catch in Week 3.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): asked if Legette is for real, Waldman said he is for real 'in this scheme right now, that's for sure' — Carolina is working him over the middle and he is producing yards after the catch. The qualifier is explicit: he is not a complete receiver right now, but he does not have to be given what the offense asks of him. Waldman made the whole thing conditional on Andy Dalton continuing to play well, joking about the 'red-headed rifle' still being the red rifle rather than the red-headed stepchild, and said the same dependency applies to [[Chuba Hubbard]]'s production.
- 2024-10-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-01]]): the usage is the story — [[Dave Canales]] split Legette roughly 50/50 inside and outside, moved him around the formation and gave him jet sweeps and horizontal breaking routes, which Harmon says is exactly right because Legette is not a player you can drop in as a full-time X or a full-time slot. He mocked the pre-season idea that Legette was ticketed for 'the [[Mike Evans]] role' as if that were a role an offense simply has rather than a Hall-of-Fame-caliber player. Both hosts flagged the caveat that Legette dropped about two passes in a game full of Panthers drops, but Harmon said he could not be more pleased with the usage regardless of production: it looks like a coach who knows what he has. Koh called him a big, strong, athletic playmaker rather than a defined-position receiver.
- 2024-10-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-29]]): Harmon is 'pretty positive to some degree' on Legette — he likes the routes Carolina has assigned him and thinks the coaching staff has shown the right ways to use him, so he expects them to hit on him to some degree, while doubting he becomes a superstar. If [[Diontae Johnson]] is traded, Harmon expects veteran David Moore — the team's route leader and a largely sacrificial X — to take the X snaps rather than Coker or Legette.

His usage so far has been off the line of scrimmage: some slot reps, runway routes, crossers, slants and in-breaking routes, which Harmon said is where Legette belongs. His worry is that Carolina now force-feeds him the X role at roughly 75/25 if [[Adam Thielen]] sticks and slots into the flanker/slot rotation with [[Jalen Coker]]. Both Harmon and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) said flatly they do not love that outcome. Harmon rated the film on Legette and Coker as roughly equal, framing it as praise for Coker rather than criticism of Legette. - 2024-11-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-26]]): Legette told media Carolina was moving him to X and Harmon saw a lot more X reps against Kansas City out of the bye — a change he does not love. In his rookie report Harmon wrote that Legette's best usage was off the line of scrimmage and moving around, and while he has done better against man coverage than Harmon expected, he would rather long-term see a true X on the roster who is more of a vertical threat, because that is not where Legette is at his best. The move also cascades through the room, pushing [[David Moore]] to flanker and clouding where [[Jalen Coker]] fits when healthy.
- 2024-12-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-03]]): Xavier Legette 'has actually outperformed my expectations this year' and is a rookie you can start, ranking behind [[Keon Coleman]] in Harmon's second-tier re-rank but by a hair. On upside Harmon puts him last of the group because he is not sure Legette has the instincts, and applies his upside-versus-theoretical-upside distinction — with Legette you are imagining a plus-plus route runner who separates downfield, which nobody has seen from him as a rookie or as a prospect. He also dislikes the usage: Carolina has been asking Legette to play more of an X role, which Harmon says is not the best version of him. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) countered that the sample is the point in Legette's favour — he has been deployed in the slot, at flanker and at X, so there is far more evidence on him than on Coleman.

Harmon says the Panthers started his rookie year deploying him exactly right: off-the-ball alignments, runway routes, deep outs, even touches out of the backfield, and his man-coverage score was good doing that. He acknowledges Legette is built like an X but says he does not play like one, and would rather see him as the flanker with [[Jalen Coker]] as the power slot and a genuine vertical X — a comebacks-and-curls man-coverage beater, the role [[Diontae Johnson]] was supposed to fill — added next offseason. - 2024-12-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-31]]): Harmon admits he is 'a little nervous' about Legette lately. He thought the rookie played reasonably well early when Carolina moved him around the formation — backfield, slot, flanker — but says the move to a traditional X receiver role has gone poorly, which is 'exactly what I would have expected.' His reasoning: Legette is not that kind of separator, so playing X would require being an 80/20 contested-catch [[Alec Pierce]]-style Alshon Jeffery type, and Harmon does not like relying on a young player for that. He now sees really bad work at the catch point, despite catch-point mentality being what he liked in the prospect profile, plus injuries and an apparent loss of confidence — Koh says he is not planting his foot and going. Legette has the team's highest air yards per target over the last three games. Both would rather see him in the slot, on bubble screens and downfield seams — Koh says he would crush the [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] route tree — and Harmon says Carolina still needs a real lid-lifting X, because even if Legette works out, that is not his role.

The issues are refinement at the top of routes and uncovering consistently; the physical ability is all there but the pre-draft risk that the refinement never comes is what appears to be happening, leaving a splash-play tertiary type rather than someone you trust down seven in the fourth quarter. - 2025-02-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-21]]): Legette inverted the normal rookie curve — better early, worse late. Pre-bye, with [[Diontae Johnson]] still handling X, Carolina used him the way Harmon and James Koh had prescribed during the prospect cycle: off the line of scrimmage, in motion, on in-breakers, a [[Deebo Samuel]]-style movement role. When they moved him to full-time X post-bye the results cratered, including four drops from week 11 on, which Harmon attributes largely to eroded confidence rather than lost hands — he draws the parallel Steve Smith made about [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] being role-shuffled as a rookie. He says Legette should be a move-around perimeter player who gets roughly 30% of his reps in the slot, not an X — Carolina used him that way early, then after the bye, once [[Diontae Johnson]] was traded away, pushed him into X, and he had his worst games of the season down the stretch. Harmon sees the Panthers' room as an old big slot in [[Adam Thielen]], a young big slot in [[Jalen Coker]] and Legette as the movable piece, with nobody who takes the top off or beats man coverage outside; he would be okay walking into the season with those three but says they should not hesitate if a true man-beating X falls to them.

- 2025-05-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-16]]): a disappointing rookie year — 84 targets, 49 catches, 497 yards, four touchdowns, a 58% catch rate, and Reception Perception marks of 65.5% vs man, 55.1% vs press (17th percentile) and 75.4% vs zone, all below what Harmon wants. Koh adds a Next Gen split of a 74% catch rate from the slot against 50.8% outside. Harmon's explanation is deployment: before the week 10 bye Legette was off the line 61.8% with an 11.6% backfield rate, running the crossers, posts, in-breakers and backfield snaps Dave Canales described at the draft; after the [[Diontae Johnson]] trade he finished the season on the line 63.5% with a 5.9% backfield rate, essentially locked at X, and the man and press numbers fell as the role hardened. His NFL route chart is nearly identical to his college one — bad on nines, curls and comebacks, good on posts, digs, slants, flats and outs — which Harmon likens to [[Deebo Samuel]]'s chart, arguing the fix is scheming him away from press rather than hoping a coach teaches a 19th-percentile-vs-press college prospect to beat it. Koh pushes back harder on the coaching, saying he is too big and fast to be that bad at press. Both are shaken by the catch point: 81.8% contested-catch rate and one drop as a prospect became a 60% contested rate and a 13.5% drop rate, with five drops from week 10 on, which Harmon hopes is confidence tied to the midseason position change. He was in space on just 32% of catches, too few for a player who should create after the catch. Koh still holds out hope for the Alshon Jeffery outcome Harmon floated in the college profile; Harmon would simply rather never see him at X, comparing the misuse to [[Treylon Burks]] in Tennessee.

He separates the narrative from the player: he rejects the theory outright, but he is 'feeling the potential' that Legette simply improves his hands on his own — while not going off the wall to buy that either.

[[Matt Harmon]] largely agrees on the deployment — he wants Legette mostly as a flanker/slot, comparable to how he views [[Jayden Higgins]] — but pushes back on the ball-winning point, saying Legette was not a ball winner on perimeter plays last year and that McMillan profiles better there. Harmon adds that Legette's low success rates against coverage will likely stay low, while cautioning that a 10th-percentile success rate means open less often than average, not never open. Because Legette is a big body, Harmon would use him for [[Sacrificial X Receiver]] snaps so McMillan can move around the formation.

Harris noted everyone said last year that he was very raw, a great athlete who would take time, so a year later you are not allowed to declare him a bust — but he raised the possibility Legette goes the way of [[Jonathan Mingo]] and Terrace Marshall Jr., in which case, he warned, do not buy any green bananas on [[Tetairoa McMillan]] either. Ciely was unwilling to bury him or to say he is not a bust, but said he definitely has not looked like a first-rounder. [[Jalen Coker]]'s renewed camp visibility may simply be Legette being hurt.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-29]]): the [[Adam Thielen]] trade opens slot snaps for Legette as well, and Harmon reiterated the pre-draft view that he is a player who needs role catering — his rookie year was 'verifiable proof', with a 17th percentile success rate versus press coverage after he was pushed into the X role when [[Diontae Johnson]] was traded. Harmon and James Koh agreed Legette is the Carolina receiver who most needs to move around the formation: X, flanker or slot, hunt favorable matchups, and treat him almost like [[Deebo Samuel]], getting him moving horizontally and laterally away from defenders rather than asking him to win vertically off the line. Harmon still expects him to end up better than N'Keal Harry, whom he invoked as the cautionary comp.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Legette is guest BRANDON FUNSTON's number four and triggers the episode's central disagreement. Funston says he likes the talent, concedes Legette is raw, and is really buying opportunity: [[Jalen Coker]] is on IR for four to six weeks and [[Adam Thielen]] was traded to Minnesota, leaving [[Tetairoa McMillan]] and Legette — he wants dice rolls on players with a clear path to playing time. Harris says he cannot tell if there is any talent there, would need to see much more, and argues that if the player stinks the volume does not matter — otherwise you would just add [[Hunter Renfrow]] for the path to volume. The two argue openly over whether volume is 'the whole game': Harris's position is that coaches decide who is good and then feed them, not that volume discovers who is good. He does allow that the Panthers coaching staff may genuinely think their 33rd pick is good, in which case volume will find him.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-05]]): Harmon is buying Legette for the first month — 'not crazy', but he expects Carolina to get the role right. With [[Adam Thielen]] traded and [[Jalen Coker]] on IR, the flanker-plus-occasional-slot job Thielen held opens up, and Harmon argued first-round capital matters here less as a talent guarantee than as evidence the team wants him to work: he was the first draft pick Dan Morgan and Dave Canales made together, a consensus choice. He rejected the idea that drafting [[Tetairoa McMillan]] hurts Legette, arguing the opposite — McMillan is a classic throwback X along the boundary, which frees Legette from that job, a dynamic Harmon compared to [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and [[Deebo Samuel]] in San Francisco where the two added to rather than subtracted from each other. Koh (not tracked) agreed and praised Canales as one of the better offensive designers in the league; Harmon added that Legette's best routes — deep in-breakers — line up with what [[Bryce Young]] does well. Carolina also brought [[Hunter Renfrow]] back from being cut to start in the slot.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-09]]): Harmon said flatly that Legette should never run a route that breaks outside and should only ever be run over the middle of the field, after another sideline mishap continued a Panthers pattern he traced back to Jonathan Mingo's rookie year — catching the ball but not finding a way to stay in bounds. He does not know what is going on with Legette or with the team, which he thought looked unprepared in a Week 1 loss to Jacksonville. Tetairoa McMillan, by contrast, made a couple of sweet sideline grabs in the same game.
- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-11]]): Koh was surprised Legette was not put in better spots in Week 1 and wants to see more deep overs and in-breaking routes moving horizontally rather than toward the sideline, while conceding Dave Canales did give him some. Harmon's counter is blunter — 'he might just not be good' — pointing back to a boom-bust profile Carolina made an aggressive first-round bet on: 35th percentile success rate versus man, 26th percentile versus zone, 17th versus press. He allows there are things to hold onto, notably that with [[Tetairoa McMillan]] taking the X role Legette will not have to play there, and that it is only Week 1, but says if a player with those charting marks does not work out it would not be the most surprising thing in the world.

He does not, for one moment, really look like he knows what the hell he's doing.' Harris says he is cuttable, and that if [[Hunter Renfrow]] — now Carolina's third receiver, with a two-touchdown day — is the level of receiver available on waivers, he would be fine making that swap. A 26.7% catch rate, dead last in yards per route run among second-year receivers with 20+ routes at 0.1, behind even [[Roman Wilson]] and Jamari Thrash who barely play, and a 0% first-downs-per-route-run. Harmon attributes part of it to the Carolina context — short target depth because the offense cannot protect long enough to push downfield, and the situation worsening with Robert Hunt and Austin Corbett to IR — but the profile is brutal, and he says the team needs [[Jalen Coker]] back badly.

His image: there are too many loose threads dangling from Legette's game, and defenders can pull on any one of them and the whole thing falls apart while he is running around the field.
