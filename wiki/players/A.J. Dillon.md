# A.J. Dillon

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]): he is holding firm on Dillon and calls him underrated. Dillon arrived in great shape; fantasy players dislike him because he is not super fast or explosive, but Waldman's case is that he is a powerful runner who can catch and who runs with good vision and decision making — precisely the traits he doubts in rookie [[Marshawn Lloyd]]. The implication he draws is that Lloyd may not simply take the job.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): the Dillon signing reads as Philadelphia looking for a bigger back to take some volume off [[Saquon Barkley]] — not to displace him, but to avoid running him into the ground after a huge carry season. Waldman sees Dillon as the type who can close out games when the outcome is decided, or take key touches in specific areas of the field where he can be effective if not downright good. He acknowledges this framing will seem strange to some, and it is the basis for his mild preference for [[Bijan Robinson]] over Barkley at the top of drafts.
- 2025-06-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-30]]): the market is sleeping on Dillon in Philadelphia because everyone defaults to [[Will Shipley]] on draft capital and year-two status. Waldman notes Dillon reportedly had a stellar camp in Green Bay before a neck injury and would likely have been the RB2 behind [[Josh Jacobs]] and taken load off him. He rejects the plodder label, comparing Dillon to [[Rhamondre Stevenson]], and sketches paths to value: if the Eagles take early leads Dillon could get the majority of closeout touches or a red zone specialist role (though he concedes the tush push limits the goal-line piece), and if [[Saquon Barkley]] were hurt he expects a Shipley-Dillon split in which Dillon gets as many or slightly more carries as the runner. Third in line he would watch rookie [[Montrell Johnson]], a straight-line runner with speed who behind this line could give [[Isaac Guerendo]]-type production. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) admitted he is 100% sleeping on Dillon and has too many Shipley shares.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Best Ball]: Dillon is being written off as the slow, plodding Green Bay guy when the record says otherwise — he was one of the best blockers in his draft class, was squeezed into a three-headed committee with Jamaal Williams in the power/blocking role and [[Aaron Jones]], and still produced decent yardage and touchdowns through the air and on the ground when healthy. Waldman's argument is that Dillon, not the 212-pound [[Will Shipley]], is the between-the-tackles and closeout back if [[Saquon Barkley]] misses time, and that Dillon has chances to cut Shipley's role toward a 50-50 split even with Barkley healthy. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) said Waldman had just made him start worrying about Dillon as a threat to his Shipley shares.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Dillon took the rotational carries in the third quarter once [[Will Shipley]] left with a rib injury, finishing with three carries to Shipley's three. Harris' eyeball evaluation was unflattering — Dillon 'still pretty much looked like a slow as molasses power back,' maybe a little smaller than before, 'but didn't look very fast.' He doesn't see a rosterable handcuff behind [[Saquon Barkley]] at this point, only a scramble waiting to happen if Barkley gets hurt.
