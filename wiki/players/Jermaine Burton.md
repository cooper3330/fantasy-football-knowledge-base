# Jermaine Burton

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast on the 2024 WR class A-Z ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): has all the tools of an effective NFL route runner, and Waldman thinks his best outcome is at flanker or in the slot, where he can get defenders on their back hip and excels as a pass catcher — attacks the ball early, has the focus to win with a safety coming downhill, and wins contested targets while defenders use him "as a ping-pong ball." A tough, consistent, smart player.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): a high-floor pick — candidate for "better NFL career than college career" since his college box score undersold his upside, buried behind Georgia/Alabama depth. Comps him to Robert Woods as a versatile X/flanker/slot piece, though Angelo thinks he's best deployed as a flanker who can also work the slot (a Rashid Shaheed-style deep/win-me role) rather than forced outside as a pure X. [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, agrees, adding that he was graded just a few points behind [[Ja'Marr Chase]] as a Georgia freshman before being stuck in wide-receiver-purgatory buried on the depth chart; calls him a day-one starter in a role, with real after-the-catch value thanks to short-area quickness, patience and suddenness. Both hosts peg him as a high-floor guy who in a weaker class would go round two/three but may fall to round three or four here.
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]) [Dynasty]: named Jermaine Burton most likely to exceed his draft slot, ranking him fourth overall in the class on pure talent, ahead of [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]. Cites a 75% success rate vs. man, 77.3% vs. press, and a 96.6% success rate on dig routes (14.6% of his sample). 32.6% of his targets were contested and he won 78.6% of those, showing he finishes at the catch point despite big-play speed (4.45 forty, 38.5" vertical, 11'1" broad jump at 6'1"/196). Compares his style to an "angry, aggressive Chris Olave" or a Brandon Aiyuk/Stefon Diggs X-flanker hybrid, and thinks his route running could let him win over the middle outside like Brandon Ayuk. Caveats hard, though: league sources flagged serious character red flags stemming from the incident striking a fan on the field at Alabama, and Harmon says that risk -- not talent -- is why Burton could fall to day three despite RB4-grade film.
- 2024-05-02 — According to Brandon Angelo ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): Burton is his top underrated pick and a top-12 player on his board who he thinks will be a steal in the third round. Angelo argues Burton is a better scheme fit for the Bengals offense than [[Tee Higgins]] in some ways — Burton can run the full route tree and win at all three receiver spots, while Higgins wins primarily at the catch point on boundary routes and can't threaten defenses vertically the way [[Ja'Marr Chase]] does. If Higgins is traded, Angelo projects Burton sliding into the flanker role opposite Chase; if Higgins stays, he sees Burton learning quickly as a versatile depth/rotational piece. He compared Burton's competitive, YAC-heavy profile to Robert Woods, prime Emmanuel Sanders and a 'bigger Ty Hilton,' and noted the third-round draft slide (earlier than expected given underwhelming college production) is actually a positive signal that teams valued traits over box-score stats.
- 2024-05-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-02]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon was surprised Cincinnati took Burton with the 16th pick of round three, saying his film graded as the wide-receiver-four of the class and 'round one worthy' — a real fall attributable to off-field character concerns, continuing what Harmon frames as a Bengals pattern of taking chances on flagged prospects. Burton ranked 3rd in the class in man-coverage success rate and 4th versus press, with a 96.6% success rate on dig routes and strength across downfield and intermediate route trees (not just press-beating outside stuff); he also saw heavy contested-target volume at Alabama (32.6% of his sampled targets contested) and caught 78.6% of those. Koh questioned Burton's actual role given [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Tee Higgins]] both lock down outside spots, calling it a possible 'redshirt year.' Harmon countered that Chase could see a career-high slot rate (up from ~35% toward 40%+) to create room outside, and floated Burton as [[Joe Burrow]]'s long-term Higgins replacement if Higgins leaves in free agency after 2024, calling him 'essentially a round-one player at a huge discount.'
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Burton is both hosts' top reach-for pick and their pick for biggest receiver riser by next year. Angelo has been most consistent on Burton, drafting him a round and a half early because he expects Burton to be the heir apparent once [[Tee Higgins]] departs Cincinnati; Waldman traded a future first-round pick to move up and take Burton at pick 114, arguing there's no equivalent talent likely available at the end of next year's first round. Both argue Burton could ultimately outplay Higgins — offering after-the-catch movement intellect and competitiveness Higgins lacks — and could pair with [[Ja'Marr Chase]] the way [[A.J. Brown]] and [[DeVonta Smith]] complement each other, since in Waldman's view Higgins is more a true '2' than a '1B' receiver.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]): Waldman compares Jermaine Burton's potential role in Cincinnati's offense to Will Fuller playing off DeAndre Hopkins, with Ja'Marr Chase in the Hopkins role - a field-stretching complementary piece who could take over one-on-one boundary snaps if he beats out Tee Higgins for the outside role, which in turn would make Higgins expendable via trade.
- 2024-07-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-04]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said Burton is a player he is drafting in every single dynasty draft, arguing Burton is actually a better long-term version of what [[Tee Higgins]] brings -- more multi-dimensional, better after the catch, and more durable, where Higgins' health for the playoffs is a real question in some years. Both hosts agreed Burton is the 'in crowd's' favorite Bengals sleeper and deservedly so, and that if Higgins is traded mid-season there will be real target volume beyond [[Ja'Marr Chase]] to split between Burton, [[Andrei Iosivas]], and [[Charlie Jones]].
- 2024-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-23]]): Burton has first-round talent even if he did not put it together in college, and the charting is emphatic — an 86th-percentile success rate versus both man and press coverage, which Harmon says marks 'a receiver that's just good at the game.' He calls him an awesome separator who works well downfield with a primary home outside. Two caveats frame the take: the serious character and maturity concerns that pushed him to the third round, and the fact that Alabama's grip-it-and-rip-it deep passing, run-first offense with poor quarterback play suppressed his production. Harmon loves the Cincinnati landing spot precisely because [[Joe Burrow]] is a credible authority figure who can demand Burton stay dialed in, and reports Burton has been engaged and taking feedback so far. Structurally, Burton matters beyond his own box score: he is the pure outside player who lets [[Ja'Marr Chase]] move inside more often, so Harmon needs him to be an immediate contributor for that plan to work. James Koh (not tracked), 6'0', 195 lbs, agreed he can play and expected no wasted-pick complaints even if the snaps are slow to come.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]): asked to pick between Burton and [[Andrei Iosivas]] in a deep 2024 free draft, Waldman takes Burton — the better all-around player and already a little more refined as a rookie — while noting Iosivas can typically be had off waivers instead. If [[Tee Higgins]] is traded, he sees Burton inheriting more of the [[Tyler Boyd]] slot role and switching off with [[Ja'Marr Chase]], with Iosivas taking the outside job.
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]) [Dynasty]: [[Andrei Iosivas]]' camp buzz does not mean Burton is toast — Harmon points out Burton is a rookie while Iosivas has had a full year in the building, and expects an in-season battle where whichever player takes to whichever role wins the snaps. He also frames the alignment question: if Cincinnati wants [[Ja'Marr Chase]] working more from the slot, Burton outside is the enabler; if not, Iosivas plays slot. Harmon notes Burton is a great athlete too, and stresses the 2025 stakes — with [[Tee Higgins]] likely leaving because the Bengals do not pay two receivers plus a quarterback, the answer may end up being both Burton and Iosivas.
- 2024-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): Burton has not had a clean preseason and has not worked much with the first team, but Harmon thinks he should play a big role in the future of Cincinnati's receiver room, especially with [[Tee Higgins]] likely gone after the year. Per [[Reception Perception Methodology]] charting at Alabama he lined up outside on 82.6% of sampled snaps and on the line for 70.2% — a near-pure X/flanker with essentially no slot history — and he separated at all three levels. Harmon and James Koh (not tracked) agree the one silver lining of a [[Ja'Marr Chase]] absence would be getting Burton meaningful outside snaps sooner rather than later.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon has kept Burton relatively high in his updated dynasty rankings and says it would be crazy to dock a rookie for preseason usage when he played deep into fourth quarters; a third-round pick doing nothing as a rookie is unsurprising on the surface. The concerning part is the professionalism reporting — Paul Dehner Jr. of The Athletic wrote the Bengals will not trust Burton in the lineup until they can trust him, echoing Dane Brugler's pre-draft note that Burton got himself on the shit list with both the Georgia and Alabama staffs. Harmon says the ceiling clearly exists but a stacked room with Chase, Higgins, Iosivas, Trenton Irwin and [[Charlie Jones]] has no incentive to absorb his ups and downs; there is a very good chance he is a Week 1 healthy scratch. He calls a red-shirt rookie year survivable but not over. Koh (not tracked) stresses the perfect talent-and-role fit if the off-field stuff clicks, and Harmon adds that Burrow reaching out in OTAs without it taking is itself a bad sign.
