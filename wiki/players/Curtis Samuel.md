# Curtis Samuel

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]): reacting to Samuel's free-agent signing with Buffalo (3 years, $24M, $15M guaranteed) — Harmon calls it "an A-plus signing," rating it a bigger deal for him personally than the general NFL public seemed to (a Buffalo beat writer, Ryan Talbot, quoted him as having "gushed" about it). Grounds the excitement in four charted seasons of success rate vs. man coverage: 76.6% (2018), 75.4% (2019, outside/flanker in Carolina with Kyle Allen at QB), 75.8% (2020, under current Bills OC Joe Brady in Carolina), 75.4% (2023, Washington) — all clear the ~75% mark Harmon frames as roughly the 80th percentile for the league. Also strong on specific routes (83% success on the curl, 77.6% on slants) and able to win vertically (nine routes, posts, corners), which Harmon says makes him capable of the things [[Gabe Davis]] (not a tracked page) wasn't — both a downfield threat and an intermediate separator. Explicitly not calling him a fantasy superstar ("do I think he's going to be a fantasy league winner? No, and I don't care") — the case is that he's a good, useful player whose box-score output (never a 900-yard season, only one 700-yard season) has been dragged down by poor surrounding offenses and quarterback play (Kyle Allen in 2019; a crowded, disorganized Washington offense under Eric Bieniemi in 2023), not by his own talent. His best statistical season (2020, 1,000 total yards) came in Joe Brady's Carolina offense, which produced four different 1,000-yard players that year. Reunited with Brady in Buffalo — now installing his own scheme with a full offseason rather than inheriting Ken Dorsey's playbook — Harmon floats him as a personal breakout/hype-list name ("my march to 1100") entering his age-28 season, while cautioning that not every player he charts and likes becomes a fantasy difference-maker. Projected as a clear No. 2 (behind [[Stefon Diggs]]) or complementary third option to [[Khalil Shakir]] in Buffalo's receiver room, expected to line up primarily outside to let Shakir keep working the slot, and to take over the pre-snap-motion/free-release role the Bills previously gave non-receivers like Isaiah McKenzie and Deonte Harty. Harmon pushes back on the idea that Samuel and Shakir are redundant — different archetypes (Shakir more of a get-the-ball-in-space YAC slot piece; Samuel a digs/outs/corners route runner who can also beat press). Signing is read as reducing, but not eliminating, Buffalo's need to draft a receiver early in 2024 — a rookie no longer has to start Day 1 and can be brought along slowly.
- 2024-07-09 — According to James Coe (co-host, not tracked) and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Curtis Samuel is the best overall addition to Buffalo's revamped WR room on his 3-year, $24M deal (cheaper than the 3-year, $39M Gabe Davis got from Jacksonville), and the most well-rounded option -- Coe called him 'the most consistent man beater they've got on this team, not even close,' citing a long track record of high success rate versus man coverage in Reception Perception charting. Both hosts agreed the best version of Samuel comes from moving him around the formation (flanker, slot, occasional X) rather than parking him at X, and that Buffalo 'fumbled the bag' by drafting [[Keon Coleman]] to play X instead of building the room around Samuel's versatility. Harmon expects Samuel to be the favorite to lead the team in targets among the new-look receivers.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon said he loves Samuel as a player but doesn't view him as a true X-receiver -- he's 'a man coverage beater' who needs to be schemed off the line rather than an every-down outside option, which complicates how Buffalo distributes targets among a receiver room (with [[Keon Coleman]], [[Khalil Shakir]]) that Harmon feels lacks a clean X fit even as the offensive line and run game (13th-ranked per guest analyst Brandon Thorn, not tracked) ascend under new OC Joe Brady.
- 2024-09-05 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]: Koh has said all offseason that Samuel will be the Bills' leading receiver and has been met with crickets, arguing his Reception Perception profile makes him the most versatile receiver on the roster and the best man-coverage beater, so barring injury he is a shoo-in to lead the team in receptions and targets. Samuel is back practicing in full after turf toe. [[Matt Harmon]] partly disagrees on the near term: coming off injury, he would bet on [[Khalil Shakir]] as Buffalo's most productive Week 1 receiver, though he thinks Samuel takes more snaps outside than people expect precisely because of that man-beating ability, and notes the caveat that Samuel has been a positive force whenever healthy — staying healthy is the whole question.
- 2024-10-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-03]]): Buffalo's most obvious fix for a room that cannot beat man coverage outside, and Harmon does not understand the deployment — Samuel played a team-low 35% of snaps in week 4, a game where the Bills needed points, while [[Mack Hollins]] ran about 70% of the routes. His alignment split (roughly 42% outside, 50% inside) is fine and Harmon would push it further outside; Reception Perception history says Samuel can win against man there. Both hosts assume the turf-toe/plantar issue from the preseason is still limiting him, since otherwise the free-agent money makes no sense. Harmon cautions he is not the 'savior' of the receiver room, especially with [[Khalil Shakir]] — their best separator — injured.
- 2024-10-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon says it is 'taking years off my life' that Samuel is barely involved in Buffalo — paid real money and used to run screens out of the backfield when he can actually run routes and get open. Koh (not tracked) notes Samuel's alignment splits: roughly 40% outside, ~60% slot, with about 5% of snaps in the backfield on trickeration. Harmon acknowledges the offseason toe injury but rejects the idea that [[Mack Hollins]] having completed training camp makes him the better option to run more routes and see more downfield targets than Samuel.
- 2024-11-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-20]]): the Kansas City game (6 targets, 5 catches, 58 yards, first touchdown — season highs across the board) was exactly the role Harmon hoped for in Buffalo: real outside receiver work beating man coverage, not gadget backfield touches. He was outside on 72.1% of snaps against the Chiefs versus 55% on the season, a chunky ~15-17% shift with [[Keon Coleman]] out, ran a route on 68.9% of dropbacks (third most on the team, second among receivers), and was second in first-read targets per Fantasy Points data. Harmon attributes the slow season to health — turf toe picked up late in camp plus a chest/pec injury — comparing it to how turf toe wrecked [[Terry McLaurin]]'s 2023 before his strong 2024. The caveat: he will not call Samuel a rest-of-season play because Coleman returns and [[Amari Cooper]] is still being worked in, and both Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) could not figure out where the snaps come from in that room.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Samuel is the guy Harmon would most like to see hit a big play for Buffalo against Baltimore, including on vertical routes from the slot, on the logic that Buffalo signed him in the offseason specifically to beat man coverage and traded for [[Amari Cooper]] to add another separation option. He is Buffalo's leading playoff receiver to date. The role is small in a spread-out committee: 44% of dropbacks and three targets versus Denver. Harmon's caveat is the matchup — Marlon Humphrey has played great at slot corner since Kyle Hamilton moved back to safety — but he trusts Samuel over the bigger perimeter bodies like [[Keon Coleman]] and [[Mack Hollins]] to create explosive plays.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-23]]): Samuel's role in Buffalo is nebulous — he played about 27.8% of his snaps in the slot in the divisional round rather than being a pure outside or pure inside player — but he is an interesting matchup piece against Kansas City specifically, because the Chiefs play a ton of man coverage and Samuel is a man-coverage beater on quick-hitting plays. Harmon framed it as this being exactly the type of opponent Buffalo signed him to face. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) noted Samuel had one of his best games in the Week 11 win over Kansas City: six targets, five catches, 58 yards and a touchdown.
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): Samuel got free for a touchdown in the AFC Championship and is one of the complementary role players Buffalo cycles through week to week. Harmon notes Buffalo added him specifically as a man-coverage separator and that it 'just hasn't really worked out,' partly because of the injury issues he dealt with — though he stresses he does not think the swing itself was a mistake.
- 2026-01-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-15]]): Samuel is a player Harmon has had a lot of affection for over his career but said he is 'clearly just not the same player anymore' after so many injuries -- not the player Buffalo signed. With Buffalo's receiver room gutted by injuries to [[Tyrell Shavers]] and [[Gabe Davis]], Samuel is expected to be activated off IR and forced into significant playoff snaps out of necessity.
- 2026-01-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-15]]): Harris says Samuel, making $6.5M, will 'unquestionably' be cut, joking that his good agent is the only reason the number is that high; the take was brief with no further film or role discussion.
