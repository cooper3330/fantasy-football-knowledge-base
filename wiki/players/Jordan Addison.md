# Jordan Addison

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: **feeling it** with Nick Mullens under center. Describes Addison as a promising young receiver whose reliability as a route runner is what makes a backup quarterback workable. Ruling: knock him one tier off his top-15 WR ceiling, but keep starting him.
- 2023-12-18 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode: a reversal on his own position. He had "dialed back" on Addison after a lengthy stretch of poor fantasy output, and says the error was looking only at box scores — he'd missed that Addison had climbed out of the No. 3 role in the rotation, and that "he's very good, eyeball test." A clean example of the evaluation failure mode in [[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]].
- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): ranks Addison 2nd in his personal 2023 rookie wide receiver tier, behind [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] and ahead of [[Puka Nacua]], [[Jayden Reed]] and [[Zay Flowers]]. Calls him "a better version of Devonta Smith" — a more complete, more physical player at the catch point who doesn't need an A.J. Brown-caliber teammate drawing coverage away to be a primary option the way he argues Smith does in Philadelphia. Explicit that Addison's remaining ceiling question is entirely about Minnesota's quarterback situation, "more than anything else."
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: Adam Harstad's [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] scores his rookie year almost identically to T.Y. Hilton's — Waldman's own comp for him, independently arrived at before seeing the model number — in a range that's "more hits than misses," including some real WR1-caliber seasons among the comps. Despite that, Waldman says he "doesn't love him" at current market price and would explore selling if Minnesota's quarterback outlook doesn't improve — a case where the model and the tape both support the player, but the price doesn't.

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: with [[Kirk Cousins]] now gone to Atlanta, says [[Justin Jefferson]] "will be fine" regardless of who plays quarterback, but "Jordan Addison is the guy here that really suffers from the loss of Kirk Cousins" — repeats a standing skepticism of Addison's ceiling, calling him "just more of a classic number two" receiver rather than a true 1B, whose production will come down along with the offense's if a lesser veteran or rookie replaces Cousins in 2024.
- 2024-04-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-18]]) [Dynasty]: Addison's college RP marks (71.3% success vs. man, 80% vs. zone, 51.1% vs. press) are comparable to Troy Franklin's, but Harmon still prefers Addison's overall profile; he flags that Addison's own press-coverage score was a concern pre-draft, partly explained by playing across from Justin Jefferson, and says a similar favorable-coverage role behind a true No. 1 is the likely best-case comp for Franklin.
- 2024-05-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-30]]): Addison's rookie Reception Perception profile is concerning versus physical coverage — 29th percentile vs. man overall and just 12th percentile (51.5% success rate) vs. press, mirroring a weakness already present in his college profile. Harmon called him a "crossing route merchant": strong post route success, solid slant, average dig (70.9%), but he's not a special movement player and a physical corner exploits any technique lapse. Historical statistical comps Harmon pulled from the database: Sammy Watkins (multiple seasons), Christian Kirk, Jeremy Maclin, and loosely Marquise Brown (though Addison lacks Brown's speed) — notably no true WR1-caliber comp emerged. Addison's man-coverage success rate fell nearly 10 points from college (~73% to ~63%), with 20% drop-offs on dig and curl and 10% on slant, which Harmon attributed to NFL coverage being more physical overwhelming a smaller, technically-sound-but-thin-margin player. Harmon projects him as a clear, capable No. 2 receiver next to [[Justin Jefferson]] who will see the Vikings' best defensive coverage looks. Co-host James Koh (untracked) raised a question about possible effort/motivation issues (citing a reckless-driving incident and offseason comments) but Harmon noted rookie-season data showed no meaningful decline in games with vs. without [[Kirk Cousins]] at QB.
- 2024-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-30]]): on the field Addison profiles as a really nice long-term number two receiver — 'a very, very good number two', roughly the [[Marvin Jones]] archetype but a bit better — and Harmon caps him at a strong top-of-tier-four player who he does not expect to become a tier-three receiver in the league. Pressed on ceiling, Harmon says he can get to 26 or 27 names in a deep receiver class before he even starts to consider Addison, so top 30 yes, top 20 no; part of the reason is that players stuck as the clear number two on their own team, like [[Tank Dell]] whom Harmon thought was significantly better as a rookie, sit ahead of him. Both hosts raise the off-field driving incidents as a genuine concern rather than something to set aside — Koh calls 'irresponsible' too light a word and jokes that Minnesota should just provide him a driver — and note it creates real availability risk to start the year. In the room rankings, Harmon still calls Addison 'a beautiful number two' and says the Vikings' problem is that they have 'nothing at three'.
- 2024-11-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-21]]): Harmon says Addison simply is what he is — a fine, solid number two receiver, which is what his college profile suggested — and that the lack of a year-two jump does not surprise him. Being young and a first-rounder does not guarantee improvement every season; he contrasts Addison with [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]], who he thinks is visibly getting better and whose team is intentionally trying to get him the ball. Watching Addison, Harmon does not see one skill that makes him say 'get this guy the ball more' — not dynamic after the catch, not a dynamic separator — and uses [[Tee Higgins]] as the counterexample of a number two whose length and frame make him worth out-targeting [[Ja'Marr Chase]]. He concedes it may be unfair, since playing with [[Justin Jefferson]] means no offense will ever scheme targets to him. Ranked third-least disappointing. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) supplies the flat metrics: 17.3% targets per route run versus 17.8% last year, 1.51 yards per route run in both seasons, on pace for 765 yards, six touchdowns and 15 yards per reception — while noting Addison is 11th in the NFL in share of catches gaining 16-plus yards, which Harmon reads as more a signal about offensive designers than the players themselves.
- 2024-11-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-25]]): asked whether Addison is better than [[DeVonta Smith]], Waldman said he likes him more for reasons he admits are partly illogical, but that the gap is closer than people think. His concrete grounds: Addison is a little better versus contact and holds up better along the boundary. He drew the fantasy-versus-football distinction explicitly — Smith is the better fantasy play, but Addison is actually the better wide receiver.
- 2024-11-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-26]]): the 162-yard game leading the league in yards per route run for the week, with 75% of his catches going for a first down or touchdown, is 'the whole point of having a Jordan Addison' rather than a signal he has changed tiers. Chicago warped its defense to bracket and double [[Justin Jefferson]], and the Bears have been vulnerable to secondary targets and tight ends ([[T.J. Hockenson]] also went off). Harmon reiterates last week's take: Addison is a solid, unembarrassing WR2 but not one of the premier second receivers — he does not have the ceiling of [[Tee Higgins]], [[DeVonta Smith]] or [[Jaylen Waddle]]. He is at his best attacking zone over the middle on in-breakers, which is where nearly all his big plays came.
- 2024-12-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-10]]): Addison's month-long surge is exactly the outcome Minnesota drafted him for. Over the last four weeks he leads the Vikings in yards (410), target share and aDOT (14.0), and ranks third in the NFL in receiving yards, ahead of [[Justin Jefferson]] at seventh; he owns 37.35% of team passing yards to Jefferson's 28.9%. Koh notes the splits: 3 catches and 42.6 yards per game over his first seven, 6.7 and 116 over his last three, with catch rate up from 63.6% to 74% and yards per reception from 14.2 to 17.5. Harmon's mechanism is coverage attention — defenses like Atlanta cloud Jefferson's side with two sets of eyes and leave Addison one-on-one, and he is far better against zone and off man (3.37 yards per route run vs zone over four weeks) than press. He still doubts Addison as an X who can handle press, citing his final USC year on the outside, and calls him a genuine big-play receiver rather than the route-running technician his reputation suggests, crediting [[Sam Darnold]] for pulling the trigger downfield. Harmon had him filed as a classic number two rather than a [[DeVonta Smith]] or [[Tee Higgins]] type and now says he may have judged too quickly, reserving judgment for the full charting profile.
- 2024-12-12 — According to guest Patrick Peterson (former NFL cornerback, not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-12]]: Addison's last two weeks change Minnesota's coverage math — with him producing, a defense can no longer devote a safety to bracketing [[Justin Jefferson]] all game without Addison hurting them, which Peterson expects to open the rematch with Chicago indoors. He grouped Addison with [[T.J. Hockenson]] as the secondary threats that make the Vikings hard to game-plan against with a single-coverage answer.
- 2025-01-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-13]]) [Dynasty]: Addison is the receiver who most changed Harmon's opinion this season. He had viewed him as a run-of-the-mill, non-needle-moving number two — the kind of player you do not need to spend a first-round pick on — while understanding the fit: a good spatial route runner who eats the zone coverage [[Justin Jefferson]] forces, and who created big plays on crossers and in-breakers pre-draft. The way Addison finished the season pushed Harmon to calling him a needle-moving number two, though still not in the [[DeVonta Smith]] / [[Jaylen Waddle]] / [[Tee Higgins]] bucket of players you could imagine as a number one elsewhere. Harmon credits him with maximizing opportunities rather than only making the plays that are there. [[Matt Waldman]] says he has long comped Addison to DeVonta Smith, while doubting Smith could actually be a number one elsewhere because he gets pushed around at the boundary, and notes he thought Addison handled that better than Smith — his own comp being a T.Y. Hilton type.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-23]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon ranked Addison fourth and last of the complementary number-two tier behind [[DeVonta Smith]], [[Tee Higgins]] and [[Jaylen Waddle]], on the grounds that he is the least dynamic of the four. His caveat is a small-sample one: Addison was really dynamic in a stretch but not productive to start the year or to finish it, though Harmon allows that [[Sam Darnold]]'s play makes that not completely Addison's fault. Harmon stressed he still needs to chart Addison's season out and might feel differently afterward, and that Addison was the guy he was lowest on going into the year but genuinely impressed him and challenged Harmon's conventional thinking. All four sit bunched together in his in-progress dynasty tiers.
- 2025-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-19]]): Harmon calls Addison a much more than capable number two next to [[Justin Jefferson]] and thinks he would actually be maximized in the slot — his best Reception Perception success rates come against zone coverage, and he wants Addison running big crossing routes off motion from inside, though his production is about equal slot versus outside. Harmon says Minnesota's real need is a consistent inside winner, not that Addison must move. James Koh (not tracked) raises two concerns: Addison's off-field decision making has been a problem and a suspension is in the realm of possibility, and Addison is small and gives nothing in the run game, which matters in a Kevin O'Connell offense that wants to establish the run — a contrast Koh draws directly against [[Cooper Kupp]] as a blocking option.

Harmon notes Addison was worse against press in college than [[Matthew Golden]] is and has still been a successful pro because the Vikings scheme him away from it, and that he has become much better in the contested-catch game in year two than he was as a prospect.
