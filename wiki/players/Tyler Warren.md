- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: both push back hard on Warren as a repeat of a [[Brock Bowers]]-level prospect. Waldman: 'if Tyler Warren's Brock Bowers or [[George Kittle]], I'm John Coltrane.' He calls Warren a strong but stiff athlete who looks like he catches with T-rex hands and may not fully extend, needs deception to get open up the seam, has a recorded 19.5 mph top speed that looks anomalous against his other opportunities, and is not a good blocker — a short-yardage quarterback more than an in-line tight end. He would take [[Pat Freiermuth]] over him ten times out of ten. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) has Warren third among tight ends behind [[Colston Loveland]] and [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] in the pre-draft model: a 125.2 quarterback rating when targeted (79th percentile) and Aaron Hernandez-style movable usage including roughly 30 college carries, but all the production came in year five, which the model weights down versus years two and three. His warning is that Warren going before Bowers' draft slot will deceive people about the ceiling — though draft capital would still push him into the model's top five.
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]) [Dynasty]: Warren is 'probably the most overvalued tight end in this class' if the industry is making him the consensus TE1. Waldman says he looks stiff — straining to extend and high-point, to the point Waldman would want to run him through a workout just to gauge body mobility — and is not as fast as people expect for his size. He wins balls, but in zone, like [[Tetairoa McMillan]]; Waldman does not see him beating press corners the way a prime Travis Kelce, Antonio Gates or Tony Gonzalez did, nor being a genuine safety mismatch beyond sheer size. He is used a lot as a runner and short-range receiver, which is fine, and he is not a mauling blocker in the mold of older Penn State tight ends. Waldman's comp is Mike Gesicki rather than [[Pat Freiermuth]], and he said he would take Freiermuth 'eight days out of seven' over Warren right now. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) noted Warren is being marketed as a Brock Bowers/George Kittle type, to which Waldman replied that if that's true, 'I'm John Coltrane.'
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18]]) [Dynasty]: Warren is a prospect a team could maximize by leaning on his receiving and minimizing his blocking, because his blocking currently needs real work — he overextends, telegraphs his intentions, lets defenders dictate the action, and does not use his hands nearly enough, instead trying to overwhelm opponents with size and aggression. Waldman says that works on Saturdays and is far less consistent on Sundays; Warren has to learn to punch and keep his pads up so he can stack sound techniques into a repeatable process. On the upside, Waldman sees an early [[Tucker Kraft]]-type role available to him — outlet receiver off play action and up the seams — which could grow into a bigger part of the game plan by season's end or in year two. A second path to early production is as a red zone specialist in contested-catch situations, where Warren's process at the catch point is good enough, and he can be used as a runner or trick-play quarterback inside the five. Asked to do more against man coverage and as a blocker, it will take him longer.

- 2025-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: asked if Warren is a [[Brock Bowers]]-type player, Waldman says no — 'fuck that.' He could start year one used like [[Tucker Kraft]] on play action, flat routes, catch-and-run and short-area fades, but Waldman likens his profile to [[Michael Mayer]]: good, not great, a zone route specialist and short-area one-on-one guy. Bowers, by contrast, beats corners, safeties and obviously linebackers man-to-man. Waldman also pushes back hard on the blocking praise — Warren is a hugger whose hands are too wide, and you cannot win in the NFL hugging people; he is capable of improving but not right away. The height, speed and weight everyone looks at superficially are all there, but Waldman does not see the finer points translating, and doubts the speed and quickness are enough for a Bowers or hoped-for [[Kyle Pitts]] role. His projection: a perennial LOW-END TE1 when it comes together — which Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) notes is still scarce and useful. Waldman's analogy: equating him to Bowers is buying a Toyota Celica thinking you got a Porsche.
