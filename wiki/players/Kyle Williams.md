# Kyle Williams

## Expert Takes

- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): the Washington State receiver scored 14 touchdowns, ran a 4.40 and is suddenly visiting nine teams (Bears, 49ers, Seahawks, Dolphins among them) as an early day-two name — Waldman 'feels it' that he is overrated, but only slightly. A second- or third-round selection would be an overrated grade; as a fifth-round pick he would be perfect. Williams brings instant special-teams appeal (Waldman invokes Darius Davis, the Chargers' Pro Bowl returner, saying Williams is not as good a returner but is the better receiver right away) and can win against press, with routes good enough to contribute now. The blockers are inconsistent hands — 'clap attacking' the football — and route refinement. Waldman calls him a 'GM special': the kind of highlight-reel player a front office pushes above the scouts' fourth- or fifth-round grade.
- 2025-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-16]]): the Washington State receiver (5'10, 190, 4.40 forty, 36.5-inch vertical, a poor 9'11 broad jump, no breakout until a super-senior 1,200-yard, 14-TD season) is 'just straight up a good player' and will be drafted higher than the public expects — Harmon reads the sudden media buzz as insiders catching the outside up to where teams already had him. The chart: 76.9% of snaps outside, 71.6% on the line of scrimmage, in space on 66.7% of catches, down on first contact just 44%, multiple broken tackles on 8 of 36 plays. Success rates: 73% vs man, 81.5% vs zone, 73.5% vs press, 88% curl, 87% slant, 85.7% flat, 83.9% out. Harmon praises the release packages and calls him a natural separator who does not need manufactured touches — the anti-[[Kadarius Toney]]. Caveats: struggles stacking defenders over the top on pure go routes (which keeps him off a first-round grade, landing at 'priority second-round pick'), Mountain West competition, and Koh's note that he was the worst in the class at catching balls thrown low. Possible [[Jayden Reed]] role outcome; Harmon flags his own history of falling for small separators.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21 (Film & Data with Dwain McFarland)]]): Waldman is unsurprised by Williams' rise up boards and grades him near star level as a separator on speed, near elite as a route runner (sharp hard breaks and speed breaks, wins versus press, finds open zones, sideline awareness, good setups), with at least a couple of good release techniques, the ability to stack defenders, and near-elite elusiveness and vision after the catch. The one thing keeping him at an 82 — a rotational starter if you play to his strengths, i.e. the vertical game — is that he is only committee-level as a pass catcher, and Waldman awards a lot of points for catching. His hands are often too wide, he clap-attacks, fights the ball, drops targets away from his frame and low targets, and uses the wrong attack for the height of the ball, the same failure mode as [[Quentin Johnston]]. Waldman calls him 'what [[Chris Olave]] should be, but with remedial hands', warns he will be a [[Dontayvion Wicks]]-style tease for about three years, and says fixing it takes an [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]-level daily commitment — if he just fixes the clap-attacking he may move up a tier. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) noted the model had him underrated by 42 spots before his draft capital rose to pick 91; an 80 production rating despite 19th-percentile program quality and 35th-percentile strength of schedule.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]): overrated — this is the [[Tyquan Thornton]] / DeVante Parker pattern again, and while Mike Vrabel may have had a good experience acquiring [[A.J. Brown]], New England's draft operation still looks like the Belichick era. On tape Williams shows tremendous speed, excellent skill after the catch, dynamite punt-return ability and RPO usage, and he will get behind defenders — but he has to catch the ball better, his technique is poor and there is a lot of fighting the football. Against NFL corners who track the ball faster, get their hands right and know the pressure points to disrupt a receiver, Waldman expects him to struggle. He is not convinced New England has fixed its receiver problem and wants proof first. Bob Harris (not tracked) agrees, and adds he thought [[Kayshon Boutte]] showed real signs of life.
- 2025-04-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-29]]): Williams at 69th overall was the first receiver off the board in round three and Harmon comps him to a young [[Stefon Diggs]] — 5-10, 190 with a 4.40, one of the best separators in the class at an 82nd-percentile success rate vs press despite the frame, plus 73% vs man, 81.5% vs zone and 73.5% vs press in his prospect profile, clearing Harmon's stated 70-vs-man and 80-vs-zone benchmarks. Out route success 83.9%, with curls and slants also high; he gets open quickly, which Harmon says is exactly what [[Drake Maye]] wants after living with [[Josh Downs]] at UNC. What separates him from [[Demario Douglas]], [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] and even present-day Diggs is tackle-breaking: in space on 66% of sampled catches, down on first contact on only 44%, multiple tackles broken on 22.2%. The hosts land on 'Diontae Johnson with a plan' as the comp, since Johnson hopped backwards after the catch to assess and was a YAC negative. Harmon prefers Williams to [[Jayden Higgins]] and would not be surprised if he outproduces some receivers drafted ahead of him. Remaining question: whether his Washington State vertical production translates, since New England still lacks a true speed element.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]) [Dynasty]: a genuinely difficult evaluation and a boom/bust bet. The positives are extensive — adept route runner with good pacing to set up breaks, good footwork and craft, explosive enough to run past coverage, and after the catch he breaks tackles, makes people miss and shows a good feel for traffic and space (Steve Smith has said Williams reminds him of himself). The problem is hands. Waldman distinguishes drop types: focus drops are a concentration issue and correctable; the concern is technique. Williams tends to open his hands slightly before clamping them on the ball, creating a fight with the football — correctable, since he extends his arms with hands together to begin the target — but the 'wild clap attacks' and hand positions that are wrong for the ball's trajectory under coverage are what worry him. Outcome range: a multi-year fantasy WR1 in a [[Drake Maye]]-led offense if it hits, or a one-year WR3 and a team that moves on, the next [[Tyquan Thornton]]. Waldman would have preferred him at the end of round three of rookie drafts but placed him at the end of round two because the upside is real; the current end-of-round-two ADP is a little steep but he would consider it in some drafts.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: a headline rookie-draft value — he is getting him in the early third round of rookie drafts and thinks there is a chance Williams leads the Patriots in receiving, since the room has no high-end talent and [[Stefon Diggs]] is in his 30s coming off an ACL. He profiles as a 'level three' threat with high-volume pass-catcher upside, and if he ascends to WR1 that is a resource New England never has to spend elsewhere. [[Matt Waldman]] called him one of his hardest evaluations of the class: he sees the Steve Smith comp in the after-catch, speed and vertical route running, but Williams fights the ball badly even on completions. Waldman has him at pick 26 on his board — third-round-ish, matching ADP — and says he took him 'begrudgingly', the bet being that the catch-point technique refines. Both frame him as a 2026 payoff.
- 2025-05-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-23]]): Harmon says New England's third-round rookie graded in the same tier as [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] as a pure prospect — the two are effectively back-to-back in his stacked rankings — and that because Williams has not yet been seen in the NFL while Polk's rookie tape and charting were disastrous, he would give Williams the edge in the New England pecking order right now. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) called the Williams pick good but flagged that he and Polk have overlapping skill sets, and both hosts see a crowded room with [[Stefon Diggs]], [[Mack Hollins]] and [[Demario Douglas]] leaving few snaps to go around. Harmon frames the whole room as a blank slate under the new staff, since none of these receivers were there in Josh McDaniels' previous stint.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Williams played outside X in college and is a bit bigger than [[Elijah Moore]], but Harmon files him in the same bucket — a smaller X, and to survive there long-term you essentially have to be Antonio Brown, a comparison he refuses to put on anyone. Despite that, Harmon says he actually likes what New England has assembled: [[Stefon Diggs]] (whose rehab he says is going well enough that he expects him ready Week 1, and who has X reps in his history even after playing mostly flanker in Buffalo), Williams, a possible step forward from a younger receiver, and [[Mack Hollins]] to absorb sacrificial-X snaps. He considers the Patriots basically set on the perimeter, with [[Kayshon Boutte]]'s roster status an open question under the new regime.
- 2025-06-05 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-05]]) [Dynasty]: both prefer Williams to [[Tre Harris]] in rookie drafts on situation — a whole new staff handpicked him, [[Stefon Diggs]] is coming off an ACL and turns 32, and there is no other real secondary target ([[Kayshon Boutte]], [[Ja'Lynn Polk]], [[Javon Baker]] all dismissed; [[Demario Douglas]] maybe in the slot). Angelo sees the passing game running mostly through Diggs and Williams within three or four weeks, with a legitimate path to being the 2026 WR1 in a higher-volume offense than the Chargers'. The risk both name is the hands: Waldman says he 'clap attacks' too many balls and fights the ball at every level, catching it better shallow, and expects a camp wonder whose real test comes in games. Angelo's fix is to build confidence in the short-to-intermediate area where his post-catch transition is stellar before asking him to work the third level, citing [[Christian Watson]]'s third-level inconsistency. Waldman's fear is a [[Kadarius Toney]]-style debut disaster in Boston; Angelo calls him the boom-bust receiver of the class, '[[Diontae Johnson]] without the immaturity.'
- 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]): Harmon has 'a lot of faith in Kyle Williams long term' and thinks the rookie could genuinely play X receiver, pointing to an 82nd percentile success rate versus press in college — but says [[Terry McLaurin]] would still be a better week-one option there, and expects [[Kayshon Boutte]] to open the season as the X. Williams is a roster lock in Harmon's view. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) says he loves Williams while stressing nobody knows what they are getting from him yet, part of his broader point that New England's upgraded room is all on paper with no bona fide, set-it-and-forget-it receiver in it.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]): Williams is the archetype of the minicamp darling whose early buzz was a function of what camp tests first. His college film shows a terrific vertical threat whose blazing speed carries real nuance — he adjusts pacing and uses footwork to get free — so getting behind people all day in early camp told you nothing you did not already know, and the reports of how fast he was picking things up were reading his wheelhouse as growth. Since then Waldman notes Williams has been dropping a lot of passes and has faded in the eyes of the media as layers were added and defenses got more sophisticated, the same curve he describes for [[Romeo Doubs]] in Green Bay. Waldman is careful not to write him off: this does not mean he will not figure it out, only that the push has slowed. See [[Speed of Instinct and Overthinking]] and [[Training Camp Report Skepticism]].
- 2025-08-28 — Answering a listener who drafted him in dynasty and has seen zero camp hype, DENNY CARTER of Rotoworld (guest, NOT a tracked expert) said on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]] [Dynasty] that Williams will be fun when he catches a 75-yard touchdown but that he cannot imagine him seeing any sort of consistent volume in that offense. According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]]): he believes the coaching staff expected Williams to assert himself more in camp than he did — put truth serum in them in June and they would have named Williams the starter over [[Kayshon Boutte]] — and that failing to do so is not a death knell by itself, but being a wide receiver drafted by the Patriots is. Harris pushes back slightly on the run-heavy framing, noting [[Josh McDaniels]] is the coordinator and that there are roughly 500 passes available for any team that is not blowing opponents out, so some good receiver will be useful for fantasy — but he concedes Carter's rebuttal that throws only matter if Williams is on the field and catching them.
