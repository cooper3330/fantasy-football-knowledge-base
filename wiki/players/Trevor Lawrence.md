# Trevor Lawrence

> ⚠️ Takes below are from April 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: "screaming value" as a bargain QB1 at QB15/16 ADP. Frames 2023 as a narrative-based ranking problem rather than a talent decline — Lawrence played behind an offensive line that hit him a lot, and [[Tank Bigsby]]'s fumbles and drops plus a lot of interceptions were the difference between a solid QB1 season and the low-end year he actually had; expects positive regression on the turnovers. Notes having [[Gabe Davis]] as a top-three receiver "might be a warning sign," but points out the same alignment didn't hurt [[Josh Allen]]. Expects Jacksonville to address the offensive line in the draft. Overall: after the market overdrafted him in 2023 off a strong 2022 finish, it's now underdrafting him based on last year's box score without weighing the specific factors (O-line, Bigsby) behind it.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman says he did film analysis on Lawrence last season and, once accounting for how often he was pressured, believes Lawrence played better than his raw stats indicated — a factor supporting his confidence in Jacksonville's passing offense and, by extension, in [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]'s rookie outlook.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]): Waldman says Trevor Lawrence is worth his new 5-year, $275M contract ($200M guaranteed, $142M fully guaranteed at signing, $55M/year - Joe Burrow-level money), arguing the Jaguars' poor 2023 offensive line masked his play; multiple interceptions came from pressure putting him in harm's way on otherwise correct decisions, and a Tank Bigsby drop directly caused two picks. Waldman compares Lawrence's supporting-cast-dependent situation to a young Matthew Stafford in Detroit and calls him 'tough as a freaking nail,' noting his injury history hasn't been as bad as it could have been given the punishment he's absorbed. He likes the addition of Brian Thomas Jr. to the receiving corps and says the offensive line remains the swing factor for 2024.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host, not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-18]]), discussing [[Trevor Lawrence]]'s new contract extension: both push back on framing it as an overpay, rejecting comparisons of his raw stats to Blake Bortles'. Koh: 'a very, very good quarterback' though 'not a top-five quarterback in this league.' Harmon frames the criticism as an expectations problem -- Lawrence entered the league labeled a generational prospect ('best QB prospect since Andrew Luck/John Elway') by credible draft analysts, and while he hasn't lived up to that billing, Harmon says there's 'a wide gap between hasn't lived up to that and sucks.' He views Lawrence as a legitimately good, non-replaceable starter whose rookie year was set back by the Urban Meyer tenure, and expects the Jaguars are betting continued development closes the gap to that original evaluation.
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: Waldman argues Lawrence's QB14 ADP undersells him relative to his QB7/QB8 finish the prior year, and that last season's regression was largely circumstantial rather than a talent decline -- offensive line injuries, receiver injuries, [[Calvin Ridley]] not being a true primary receiver, rookie Tank Bigsby committing several 'unforced errors,' drops, and route miscommunication all contributed to hits that altered Lawrence's throws in ways he shouldn't be blamed for. He still flashed higher-end plays. Directly compared against [[Kyler Murray]], Waldman says he'd rather take Lawrence at QB7 than Murray, and would only want Murray at Murray's actual ADP of QB14 -- a clear preference, not a hedge.
- 2024-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (untracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-11]]): for the first time in his career Trevor Lawrence has a receiver room capable of winning at all three levels -- [[Gabe Davis]] and [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] outside, [[Christian Kirk]] in the slot -- which they compared to Josh Allen's leap once Buffalo added Stefon Diggs. Koh said he 'wouldn't be surprised' to see Lawrence enter top-five QB conversation in 2024. Harmon cautioned that the connection with [[Calvin Ridley]] broke down last season in part because Jacksonville's offensive line couldn't protect long enough, forcing Lawrence to speed up his internal clock and play faster than he ideally would; he said better pass protection and more positive down-and-distance situations are needed to get the most out of this receiver group in 2024.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-12]]): Lawrence is underrated and a 'dented can' — the phrase Harstad used about [[Najee Harris]] — a quality asset available at a discount. Waldman thinks that with the weapons on hand and adequate protection you will see progress from Jacksonville despite a slow Week 1, and would be happy starting a franchise with him. Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) argues Lawrence exposes how binary evaluation is: he is neither star nor bust, he is simply fine, and in year four is unlikely to reach the bullish pre-draft case. Harstad's positive is baseline rushing: he believes modern quarterbacks need it, citing [[Patrick Mahomes]] running 11 yards on third-and-10 for ~400 very important yards a season, and Lawrence supplies that ~300-yard opportunistic rushing floor the older middling pocket passers could not. Practical conclusion: he would not trade the farm for him, and he is not so good that Jacksonville should pass on a falling draft QB, but good enough that their hand is never forced.
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): both defend Lawrence against the pile-on. Koh says he feels bad for him because everyone is dogging on him, and that it is awfully tough to judge his talent level given the play callers and play designs he has worked with in his young career; he sees isolation ball with no layered concepts and no obvious high-low reads to make. Harmon argues the buck stops with Doug Pederson as an offensive-minded head coach even if Press Taylor calls the plays, wants the whole front office and staff cleaned out, and says it is not a well-coached or well-designed team. He adds that the pass catchers are good enough to function in a well-designed offense but not good enough to absolve the front office, citing the [[Gabe Davis]] contract as money spent on a player who does not move the needle.
- 2024-10-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-29]]): Lawrence is an argument for retiring the word 'generational' — the same mistake Harmon says was made with [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]. His actual evaluation is deliberately unglamorous: Lawrence is 'a fine starting quarterback,' probably one of the 15-18 best in the NFL, who ebbs and flows with his environment and makes mistakes he should not make (including a turnover in this game). Harmon argues the year-three leap that should have come in 2023 never did, and warns against assuming young players always tick up — sometimes year three is the same guy. He also blames environment: Urban Meyer as a rookie, then Doug Pederson, and hyper-specific limited receivers. Koh (not tracked) pushes back more positively, comparing the arm talent and early volatility to a young [[Matthew Stafford]] and arguing the public backlash has swung too far.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14 (Age Cliffs and Dented Cans)]]) [Dynasty]: Lawrence was his 2023 dented can and 'still feels dented' — he is bullish on a long career path and still open to buying low, but wonders whether the dents can be popped out given Jacksonville's situation. Adam Harstad (NOT tracked) pushed back on the fantasy payoff rather than the evaluation: in 1QB leagues you are either a top-six quarterback or you have essentially no value, and he does not see the path to top six for Lawrence with any team, not just the Jaguars. He believes Lawrence has the skill to cut it in the league and is probably a better quarterback than [[Drake Maye]] or [[Bo Nix]] 80-90 times out of 100, but he would rather gamble on the wider range of outcomes those younger passers offer. He hedged deliberately with the Carson Palmer and Kurt Warner precedents — every player's 99.9th-percentile ceiling is MVP — but calls the leap less realistic for Lawrence. Both agreed [[Kyler Murray]] is the better quarterback for a bad offense.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-05]]): the hit that knocked Lawrence out was a bad play that deserved a flag and an ejection, but the reaction split cleanly by age on the Footballguys staff board — younger analysts calling it despicable and worth multiple weeks, older ones noting it was tamer than what was routine not long ago. Waldman said the broadcast angle looked bang-bang and the close-up looked far worse, and that defender reaction time and commitment make it murkier than the discourse allows. Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) agreed on the penalty and ejection but called the three-game suspension the NFL in PR mode — Lawrence 'arguably shouldn't have been playing in the first place', the hit was nowhere near the season's worst, and precedent supported at most one game. Harstad's objection was inconsistency, not severity: set a standard and apply it evenly to everyone.
- 2025-01-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-06]]): Lawrence is capable of high-end play and anyone equating him with [[Daniel Jones]] or David Carr has missed the boat — he has far more upside than those quarterbacks and has dealt with situations just as bad. Waldman would place him in the tier of [[Geno Smith]] and recent [[Sam Darnold]] at worst, and argues he is better than both while working with worse support; at his floor he is what David Carr was at his ceiling. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) said the jury is out — Doug Pederson, widely regarded as good with quarterbacks, could not get him there consistently — but gives him the benefit of the doubt as one of the limited number of players actually capable of the job, with [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] a piece who could raise his ceiling. Both flagged Trent Baalke's retention as a limiting factor on the next coaching hire; Waldman relayed that a league contact described Lawrence as a square peg in the round hole of NFL bro culture. He is locked in through 2030, so Jacksonville has to make it work.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]): asked whether Jacksonville's next head coach is the key that finally unlocks Lawrence, Waldman answered 'if he's bringing offensive line play, yes' — and put a number on it, saying the split is roughly 97 percent offensive line and 3 percent situational play from Lawrence himself. He acknowledges Lawrence has moments and that the door has been cracked, but says without weekly protection you are asking him to kick through a deadbolt to close the small gap between good and excellent. He and co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed that [[Brian Thomas Jr.]], plus a piece or two on the line, gives the new staff real building blocks.
- 2025-01-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-27]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'fuck yes' that Liam Coen can get Lawrence and the Jaguars offense back on track. His reasoning is largely political — Jacksonville removed Trent Baalke because nobody wanted to work with him, which means Coen arrives with clout and gets his way in year one — plus he thinks the skill positions carry enough weaponry when healthy and that Lawrence 'is not a bad quarterback... he's capable of being a very good quarterback'. The stated caveat is the offensive line: he likes the tackles but wants the interior upgraded, and points to Dave Canales prioritizing exactly that in Carolina. Bob Harris (not tracked) cited John Shipley's SI.com piece and framed Baalke as a heel by perception rather than proven fact — perception is what mattered because it kept coaches away — while allowing that Baalke drafted [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and has hits like every GM.
- 2025-06-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Liam Coen and coordinator Grant Udinski can unlock Lawrence, but only conditional on the offensive line rehab holding — the stretch three or four years ago when Lawrence looked like he was turning the corner coincided with good line play. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) cited Lawrence completing only 27.6 percent of his 105 throws of 25-plus air yards with just 3.36 seconds of time, 27th among quarterbacks, and quoted Waldman's 2021 Rookie Scouting Portfolio line that Lawrence has top-drawer arm strength for distance and velocity. Waldman likes the scheme fit: the wide-zone play-action component gets him throwing on the move by design rather than necessity. A quarterback coach told Waldman that Lawrence is a different personality and a different thinker, but not in a bad way, and Waldman still rates his talent above [[Baker Mayfield]] coming out. He projects roughly 580 pass attempts because he does not trust the line, dropping to the low 500s if the run game holds up.
- 2025-09-03 — According to James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]] [Redraft (Standard)]: bold prediction that Lawrence finishes as a top-five fantasy quarterback. Koh argued the 'Lawrence has had good pass catchers' narrative is nonsense — Zay Jones and Christian Kirk as your number one is a problem — whereas he now has two real weapons in [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and [[Travis Hunter]], a soft schedule, a bad run game and a bad defense forcing volume, and finally functional coaching under Liam Coen. [[Matt Harmon]] agreed and would not push back this time after being wrong on Sam Darnold, adding that people have memory-holed Lawrence's 2022, when he was fifth in success rate and 11th in adjusted yards per attempt with a worse receiver room. Harmon's one disagreement: he expects Coen's run game to be functional rather than bad, which should keep Lawrence out of brutal down-and-distance.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest Andy Behrens (not a tracked expert) moved Lawrence from QB13 in season-long ranks to QB8 for Week 1 against Carolina. Harris pressed him on whether that was really a Lawrence bump, and Behrens agreed a big piece of it is other quarterbacks falling — [[Dak Prescott]] and [[Justin Fields]], both ranked ahead of Lawrence season-long, draw the Eagles and Steelers, two of the three defenses (with Denver) Behrens says he cannot imagine being bad. Behrens added that past the top six quarterbacks the position is squishy enough that he would never prioritize QB8-versus-QB18 in the middle rounds, so his weekly QB7–QB22 order will shuffle all year on matchup, health and supporting cast. He also said he would have felt like a fraud writing Lawrence up all offseason and then not ranking him aggressively in a good opening setup. Harris was less sure Carolina's defense is guaranteed terrible, citing Derrick Brown's return and Jaycee Horn's health.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-09]]): asked whether Lawrence is unfixable, Harmon would not completely sell the stock after one week but called it not the most encouraging performance, noting accuracy concerns that have followed Lawrence basically his whole NFL career — Fantasy Points had Brian Thomas Jr. at a sub-45% catchable target rate, lowest in the league that week. He singled out an under-thrown deep in-breaker to a wide-open Travis Hunter that Jaycee Horn intercepted, arguing that if you must miss, miss high. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) said he had been all in on Jacksonville's talent — the two receivers, Brenton Strange (whom he rates above Evan Engram in every phase), Travis Etienne Jr.'s strong opener — with Lawrence the only question, and thought Lawrence missed Thomas on five of seven targets. Both noted Lawrence looked fine before the hour-plus weather delay and much worse after.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-16]]): Lawrence is 'not a consistently accurate quarterback' — a distinction Harmon draws deliberately from being inaccurate — and is often late pulling the trigger, most visibly on an end-zone throw over [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]'s head that would have been a clean catch if released faster. He notes the ball is habitually high or long for [[Dyami Brown]] and [[Travis Hunter]] too, and that Liam Coen is visibly on the sideline miming ball placement. But he rejects the idea that every middle-of-the-field throw is a hospital ball, and says the Coen offense carries Rams DNA that requires rifling into zone. Harmon reaches for [[Baker Mayfield]] and [[Odell Beckham Jr.]] in Cleveland as the comp — a see-it-and-rip-it quarterback paired with a timing receiver, two good players who were better apart — while stressing Lawrence and Thomas still have time to fix it.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): 'Trevor Lawrence was awful in this game.' In the first half he had two different receivers open deep and airmailed both throws, then threw an interception deep in his own territory that, absent the [[Nico Collins]] fumble, probably loses Jacksonville the game. Harris framed it as career-long rather than situational: 'it's just so him — his entire career has been marked by these moments where he just doesn't see it, doesn't judge it right,' here staring down his receiver and floating it into a double team where if one defender missed it the other would not. His conclusion: Liam Coen 'has not fixed that just yet.'
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-02]]): Lawrence is one of the worst quarterbacks in the league throwing 20-plus yards downfield — one for ten this year — and does not see, throw to, or throw catchable balls to the third level. Defenses now know he sits on his first read too long and collapse on [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]. Angelo said the stats look okay but the tape does not, that Lawrence has not improved and may have regressed, and that the sell that brought Liam Coen to Jacksonville was 'I can make Trevor Lawrence great' — a sell Coen may now be walking back. [[Matt Waldman]] added the accumulated-pressure theory: Lawrence's college recklessness-versus-aggression edge erodes when you get beaten up every year, using Trent Edwards, who was tough as nails at Stanford until an Adrian Wilson concussion, as the cautionary case. Both said Lawrence is not receiver-friendly with his ball placement and flashes just often enough to keep hope alive; they were curious what he would do against Steve Spagnuolo's Chiefs.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: a big fantasy line that hides bad quarterbacking. Lawrence rushed for two touchdowns including the winner — on which he got his foot stepped on, fell, rolled around and then 'somehow gets his Saluki legs working' — so the box score looks great, but asked whether he played well Harris says no. The evidence: a throw from well past the line of scrimmage on the second series, extending the ball on a goal-line fourth-down sneak so it was popped out, throwing into blanket coverage for [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] in the end zone with [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] wide open in the flat, and repeated hard counts Kansas City never bit on that produced multiple delay-of-game penalties inside the 20 with the game on the line. His interception was on a clear missed pass interference. Harris' overall opinion is unchanged: 'there's still something lacking and he kind of doesn't quite have control over the clock or his limbs.'
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Chris Raybon (guest, not a tracked expert; [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-13]]): Lawrence again missed makeable throws -- overthrowing a sure touchdown to Dyami Brown by roughly 30 feet and nearly throwing an interception on an aggressive shot -- and Harris said the tape 'is not a great case for using Trevor Lawrence as a fantasy starter' despite entering the season high on Jacksonville's offense under Liam Coen. Raybon's counterpoint: Lawrence's erratic accuracy could mirror [[Baker Mayfield]]'s turnaround, since the scheme is good enough to mask some of it, and Lawrence still finished with 40-plus attempts and multiple touchdowns. Both agreed the bigger fantasy concern is that Lawrence's misses are directly costing [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] production.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris was blunt that Lawrence 'was dog crap' in the London loss to the Rams -- two straight three-and-outs to start, a bunch of checkdowns to [[Parker Washington]], loose high throws throughout, and a screen to [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] thrown at his feet because Lawrence didn't set his feet. He said he doesn't want to overreact to one week given Jacksonville was 4-2 with genuinely good September play, but flagged real concern that 'this far into his career' Lawrence still looks overwhelmed and scrambles at odd moments even before the game got out of reach.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06]]): Lawrence is severely limited on the third level — Angelo repeats 'severely' — while being pretty good short-to-intermediate. He believes Coen and James Gladstone are now realizing Trevor is not the quarterback they thought they acquired, and reads the [[Jakobi Meyers]] trade as an accommodation: stop chasing explosives, get sure-handed run-after-catch players and move the chains. Waldman frames it as a coach never quite knowing what he is getting at quarterback, contrasting Ben Johnson, who scouted his roster before taking the Chicago job, with Coen possibly hearing 'big arm' without confirming accuracy, and notes Lawrence is not going anywhere soon. Angelo's conclusion is that Jacksonville has to win gross games because they will not outscore anyone: run game good not great, [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] not a long-term fit, and no [[Mike Evans]]/[[Chris Godwin]]-style veteran duo like Coen had with [[Baker Mayfield]] in Tampa.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): Lawrence's low volume against Houston was circumstantial - short fields off an interception and a fumbled kickoff plus [[Parker Washington]]'s punt-return touchdown meant Jacksonville built a lead without throwing - and Harris will not judge him too harshly for taking five sacks because 'better quarterbacks than him have struggled against that pass rush'. The pre-half interception was Stingley undercutting a good short throw. But in the second half 'Lawrence should have thrown multiple picks', throwing into double coverage and floating pop-up screens to nobody, and the game ended with a fumble returned for a Sheldon Rankins touchdown. Harris's summary line: 'Trevor Lawrence isn't terrible. He's not. He also doesn't really look like the number one pick from four years ago, either.'
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-13]]), citing a point raised by his 'Going Deep' co-host [[Brandon Angelo]]: Liam Coen probably took the Jacksonville job believing Lawrence was, or would become, a better vertical thrower than he is. The evidence is a season of missed downfield opportunities, miscommunications and inaccuracy on deep balls, and the observation that many of [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]'s big plays last year did not come from Lawrence. Waldman's conclusion is that Coen is realising he is stuck with this quarterback and now has to adjust the offense toward Lawrence's strengths — intermediate and underneath work with yards after catch — if he is going to survive and thrive, which is why he expects [[Parker Washington]] and [[Jakobi Meyers]] to matter more than the vertical element.
- 2025-11-17 — According to Chris Rabon (Action Network, untracked guest): even in a 35-6 Jaguars win, Lawrence 'can't trust anybody' in this passing game, and 'you know what he does not do -- throw touchdowns' -- he has just two touchdowns in his last 20 games. Rabon said Jacksonville looks likely to lean run-heavier with Travis Etienne and Bhayshul Tuten going forward given Lawrence's downfield limitations. [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-17 (Week 11 Game Film Review)]]) noted this is a continuation of skepticism Matt Waldman voiced the previous week about the Jaguars' passing attack.
- 2025-11-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-25]]): threw three touchdowns and three interceptions in an overtime win over Arizona, which Harris says is 'truly carrying on the tradition of Blake Bortles in that same uniform.' Barely targeted his wideouts in the first half while trailing 14-10, then hit Brenton Strange on a big throw in overtime to set up the winning field goal. Harris draws a parallel between Lawrence (halfway through his fifth season) and Caleb Williams (halfway through his second) as quarterbacks who can flash brilliance but 'cannot seem to get rid of the weird throws, the bad reads' -- though he says that's far more forgivable for Williams than for a fifth-year Lawrence.
- 2025-12-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Lawrence kept missing throws and 'throwing wobblers at weird spots for no reason' in an easy Jacksonville win over Tennessee, even though the passing game still produced through [[Jakobi Meyers]] and [[Brenton Strange]].
- 2025-12-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-12-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Lawrence had one of, maybe his best, games of the season vs Indianapolis, throwing two well-placed contested deep balls that Brian Thomas Jr. converted for 39 and 30 yards. Harmon stopped short of declaring Lawrence has 'arrived,' noting he is not a metronome-consistent quarterback and that Liam Cohen has at times seemed frustrated with him this season, but said there have been encouraging moments in Cohen's offense over the last couple of games after a slow start to the scheme fit.
- 2025-12-15 — According to Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (untracked guest on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-15]]) with [[Chris Harris]]: Lawrence has now been playing, per Doherty, 'the best football of his career' for a full month, not padding stats (rarely 20 completions or 300 yards) but posting high yards-per-attempt and enough touchdowns to matter in fantasy, in a way Doherty compares to [[Drake May]]'s recent stretch. Harris had him QB10 for the week 'and felt pretty good about it.' Both agree he still can't be treated as a top-12 fantasy quarterback going forward -- Doherty notes his upcoming trip to Denver's altitude means 'no one is producing top-12 numbers in that stadium' regardless of form.
- 2025-12-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-22]]): despite having flip-flopped on Lawrence over the years, Waldman is sticking with his original evaluation that Lawrence can be a real, deep-playoff-caliber quarterback, crediting OC Liam Coen for adapting the offense to who Lawrence actually is -- taking some pressure off [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and getting tough-play middle-of-field weapons like [[Parker Washington]], [[Jacoby Meyers]], [[Tim Patrick]] and a returning [[Brenton Strange]] involved. He argues Lawrence's counting-years résumé is misleading since the Urban Meyer season was nearly as dysfunctional as Caleb Williams' rookie year and shouldn't count, and he's played for three or four different coaches since, meaning he's never had real continuity until now. He also credits young GM James Gladstone for adding playmakers -- signing then extending [[Jacoby Meyers]] early because 'his superpower is he catches the ball,' plus the [[Travis Hunter]] move -- calling Jacksonville a franchise that may finally have the right quarterback-coach-GM trio in place, though it remains 'to be determined.'
- 2025-12-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-23]]): Lawrence, ranked QB10 by Harris for the week despite an untracked co-host (Pat Doherty) publicly saying he was impressed by Lawrence but 'by no means' startable, scored heavily against a Denver defense Harris considers 'very good' -- Harris's takeaway is that when a good defense gets scored on, it's meaningful signal, and 'we should believe them when they tell us who they are down the stretch.' He calls the Jags offense 'a wagon' right now and a must-use group against Indianapolis in week 17.
- 2025-12-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-12-30]]): Lawrence leads Jacksonville in rushing touchdowns with 9 -- only Josh Allen has more among QBs this year -- which Harmon attributes to new OC Liam Cohen's scheme comfort running him outside the pocket, drawing a parallel to how Cohen's system with the 2022 Rams and later Tampa Bay turned around Baker Mayfield's rushing habits (breaking Mayfield's old Cleveland tendency to drift right and throw off-platform). Harmon says the early Cohen-Lawrence relationship was reportedly 'combative' but productive; both hosts agree Lawrence is now 'letting it rip' with no hesitation, comfortable in the offense, and benefiting from clearly defined roles for [[Parker Washington]], [[Jakobi Meyers]] and Brian Thomas Jr.
- 2026-01-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-08]]): from Week 9 forward, Lawrence was the fantasy QB1 on a per-game basis, fueled partly by 7 rushing TDs in 10 games versus just 14 combined over his prior four seasons. Both Harris and guest Jim McCormick (untracked) doubt the rushing-TD rate is repeatable, and Harris credits much of the surge to Jacksonville becoming a 'wagon' offense down the stretch rather than a durable shift in Lawrence himself, noting the team's own history of unpredictable year-to-year swings. Harris now slots Lawrence in the 'fringe fantasy starting' tier -- up from unusable, but not a true QB1.5 -- and expects to hold few shares of him next season, projecting him more as a ~28-passing-TD guy. McCormick agrees the rushing was a bigger share of the value than people will admit, and both flag that a poor playoff finish could undercut belief in the breakout, while continued good health news for [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and [[Travis Hunter]] could add helium to his price.
- 2026-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-22]]): Waldman noted Lawrence has now played for three or four different NFL coaches, muddying any final read on him, but pointed to a strong stretch run this season as reason for optimism. New OC Liam Cohen reportedly found Lawrence and [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] lacked real chemistry -- most of Thomas' production came with Mac Jones -- so Jacksonville signed [[Jakobi Meyers]] to a longer deal as a security blanket who's tough at the catch point and reliably where he's supposed to be, mirroring how Waldman's own skepticism about [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] a year ago turned out wrong once Etienne became a top-12 fantasy back. Waldman is watching whether the Lawrence-Thomas dynamic is genuinely improving or just being talked up for trade value.
- 2026-06-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-29]]) [Dynasty]: Flags real doubt about whether ball-placement accuracy is 'his game.' Last year Trevor Lawrence and [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] miscommunicated on route breaks -- Thomas read the coverage correctly and broke off it, but Lawrence threw to a different spot -- and Waldman isn't confident that issue resolves cleanly even once the pre-snap/route-recognition side improves, which caps how much he trusts the rebound thesis on Thomas.
- 2026-08-18 — According to Dave Kluge (Football Guys, not a tracked expert) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the crowded, hard-to-parse Jaguars WR ADP (Parker Washington, Brian Thomas Jr., Jakobi Meyers, Brenton Strange, Travis Hunter all drawing fantasy interest) has produced a 'huge discount' on Lawrence. Kluge and Harris agree the likelier truth is Lawrence is undervalued rather than all his weapons being overvalued -- but Harris notes it's hard to meaningfully bid Lawrence up regardless, because the quarterback position overall is so deep and flat this year that a comparable late alternative is always available.
