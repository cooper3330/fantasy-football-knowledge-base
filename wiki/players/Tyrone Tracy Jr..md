# Tyrone Tracy Jr.

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: elusive with a good stick-and-trigger step to make defenders miss in the open field, and can transition downhill at the edge of the box off a single cut. Pacing consistency (slowing into the hole, accelerating through it) is uneven because — as a wide receiver convert from Iowa — he's "still getting familiar with being a running back." A developmental prospect with upside who could become a valuable contributor if he develops between-the-tackles skills.
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: compared Tracy to Kenyon Drake as a 'fast-ish,' gap-scheme speedster who profiles as a change-of-pace piece rather than an instinctive between-the-tackles processor, fitting a pattern of similar athletic-but-unrefined profiles (Gary Brightwell, Joshua Corbin) already on New York's depth chart. He said Tracy 'could be good at some point' but is currently more of a complementary speed option, aspiring to the type of role Drake wanted early in his own career.
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman sees Tyrone Tracy Jr. currently trailing [[Eric Gray]] roughly 60/40 for the Giants' complementary backfield role behind Devin Singletary, describing Tracy as fast with change-of-pace gap-running skill and some receiving ability, but a less refined, less instinctive runner than Gray. He notes the coaching staff could still elevate Tracy's role if his usage is narrowed to what he does well; a good or bad camp could flip the current split either way. Co-host Bob Harris (untracked) jokes he's already given Tracy his personal 'stamp of approval' as a deep-league sleeper and expects NFL.com-driven hype to follow, while Waldman half-seriously tells listeners to check dynasty-league waiver availability on Gray as the more likely near-term winner.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo names Tracy his 'fun' pick for biggest running back riser by next year, describing him as an unorthodox, hard-to-plan-for mover who figures things out in real time. He sees a wide-open lead role in a Giants backfield desperate for a playmaker beyond [[Malik Nabers]] — [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] and a merely average Devin Singletary don't threaten it — and notes Tracy was a genuinely productive receiver at Iowa and Purdue, giving him real separation ability against linebackers out of the backfield. Angelo is getting him for essentially free in the fifth round of rookie drafts currently.
- 2024-05-20 — According to guest Jagger May (Football Guys, untracked) and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-20]]) [Dynasty]: May said Tyrone Tracy isn't a legit sleeper despite the hype (he'd stashed him for $7 FAAB), questioning whether the Giants' offensive line has the smart, versatile, mobile blockers gap schemes require, especially against a tough divisional slate. Waldman's comp was "an aspiring Kenyon Drake" — a raw, open-field runner who can catch reasonably well but struggles with between-the-tackles decision-making, a profile that historically takes years to round into form. He noted Tracy converted from wide receiver at Iowa partly because he dropped too many passes ("clap attacks"), reinforcing that his receiving projection is unproven.
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Responding to a report Tyrone Tracy Jr. could earn a '[[Jahmyr Gibbs]]-like role,' Waldman pushed back, noting Giants beat writers are notoriously optimistic. He agreed Tracy can be competent in gap-scheme runs and has good open-field movement comparable to Gibbs, but called the receiving-back comp overstated -- Tracy is a converted wide receiver who doesn't actually catch the ball well, part of why he moved to running back. His real comp for Tracy right now is Kenyan Drake, 'a couple tiers lower' than Gibbs, with Tracy still needing to show mature decision-making between the tackles against NFL-level competition, something he was inconsistent with at Iowa. Bob Harris (untracked) said Tracy is likely to cut into [[Devin Singletary]]'s touches enough to worry fantasy managers, though he still holds a lot of Singletary best ball shares.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]): he calls Tracy an overhyped sleeper and is holding firm on his concerns. The camp buzz is real — speed, broken tackles, adequate dump-off receiving — and people project more because he converted from receiver, but Waldman says the receiving on tape was not as strong as the projection assumes. His fear is that when the lights come on Tracy makes mistakes, the Giants conclude he is not ready, [[Devin Singletary]] is confirmed as the man and [[Eric Gray]] becomes a 'mini-me' junior Singletary. He allows the other branch: if Tracy overcomes the mistakes he may be the sleeper everyone is talking about — but he expects the 'Bigsby effect' instead.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): asked whether bad hands are why Purdue moved Tracy to running back, Waldman says 'hell yeah' — he watched him as an Iowa receiver and immediately saw why the move happened, and calls it a good move. On the Giants' loss he splits the blame: Tracy fumbled one away that cost them a chance to seal the game, and the dropped pass was not a great throw but hit him in the hands and he 'clapped that sucker better than [[Courtland Sutton]] even could.' Even so, Waldman's verdict is favorable — for a player only two years into playing running back, one of them in the NFL, the performance so far is 'pretty damn impressive' and he is on schedule.
- 2024-11-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]]): despite fumbling the game away, Tracy 'looks like a keeper at running back' for the Giants and is part of the legitimate young talent — along with [[Malik Nabers]], [[Theo Johnson]] and even [[Wan'Dale Robinson]], whom Harmon admits to being a hater of but concedes is a credible role player — that the next Giants coaching staff should be built around. Koh (not a tracked expert) adds that the Giants run game is actually pretty good, getting decent production from Tracy and [[Devin Singletary]], so the post-[[Saquon Barkley]] criticism of the ground attack is not fair, and credits Brian Daboll's run-concept design.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): Waldman cautions that despite Tracy's wide receiver background, there is a reason he is a running back now — he was not a good pass catcher and not a good route runner, so Waldman expects [[Devin Singletary]] to be the better passing-down back for [[Drew Lock]]. He allows that Tracy is very talented and is turning into a good player, comping the position switch to Robert Edwards, who moved from cornerback to running back at Georgia — but with Edwards's passing-game skills missing from the profile.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): Waldman would happily add [[Nick Chubb]] to the Giants backfield even at Tracy's expense — 'slow your roll, Tyrone, learn from the best.' He credits Tracy with learning quickly and being good, but would rather he spend another year developing behind a veteran, and would cut [[Devin Singletary]] rather than Tracy to make room. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) adds that the early mock drafts he has been in suggest many drafters are already fading Tracy anyway.
- 2025-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman says Tracy showed something as a rookie and still has growth potential, and would be a fine complement if the Giants added [[Ashton Jeanty]]. But in comparing him to [[Brashard Smith]], he is harsh about the origin of the position switch: Tracy is the guy where you see why they moved him to running back, because he cannot catch the ball well — Waldman clarifies he does not suck as a receiver, he sucks at catching the ball, and notes that not being good enough to play receiver at Iowa is a damning statement by NFL standards. He ended up a runner-returner at Purdue, is very good at it, and learned fast.
