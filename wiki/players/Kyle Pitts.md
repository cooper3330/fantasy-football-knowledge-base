# Kyle Pitts

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-05]] [Redraft (Standard)]: both hosts unreservedly "feel it" on a 2024 rebound now that Atlanta has hired Zach Robinson (ex-Rams) as offensive coordinator under new HC Raheem Morris — "totally feel it... Pitts is too good," "good talent wins out." Waldman's diagnosis of the down 2023 season is scheme/QB-driven rather than a talent decline: he traces Atlanta's passing-game struggles to Desmond Ridder being unable to read the field dynamically between zones, which forced the offense into simpler, static routes that "break back to the quarterback and sit there" rather than working combinations across the field — a constraint on the whole passing game, not specific to Pitts. See [[Scheme vs Talent]] for the full Ridder/Arthur-Smith-era Atlanta case.

- 2024-02-29 — According to **Kevin Knight** (Falcons reporter, SB Nation's Falcoholic — guest, not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]): says Pitts "was never really healthy last year" and could take real pressure off a thin receiver room in 2024 if he's finally healthy and paired with a quarterback able to get him the ball.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: flags his own *Fantasy Notebook* (Footballguys) research on [[Kirk Cousins]] — over 27% of Cousins's career targets have gone to the tight end, and he's "carried some tight ends to great heights" — as the case for a real Pitts rebound now that the signing is official; still "feeling" the move as a plus for Pitts.

- 2024-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-30]]: restates that Pitts's rookie season ranked 4th all-time among rookie tight ends in PPR scoring, despite Atlanta trading Julio Jones and Calvin Ridley taking a leave of absence during that season — the lack of surrounding talent limited Pitts to one touchdown but didn't stop him from being the first rookie tight end since Mike Ditka to gain 1,000 yards.
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: In discussing Atlanta's receiver-need decisions, Harmon floated provocatively that 'who cares about Kyle Pitts at this point' if [[Drake London]] and a hypothetical second true X receiver were getting 130 targets each -- arguing the offense could realistically be built around London first with Bijan Robinson mop-up work, leaving Pitts a lower priority. Co-host James Koh (untracked) strongly disagreed and defended Pitts's role, and Harmon ultimately softened the framing rather than press it, joking he didn't want to invite 'tight end truthers' into his mentions. The exchange reflects real uncertainty about where Pitts ranks in Atlanta's post-draft pecking order behind London -- an unresolved disagreement between the two hosts, not a settled take.
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said he's 'totally feeling' Kyle Pitts as a quasi-wide receiver in Zach Robinson's Sean McVay-style Falcons offense, noting Atlanta added blocking tight ends (from 49ers/Rams-style systems) specifically to free Pitts up to play more receiver snaps as a movable piece, and expects him to finish a close second in targets behind [[Drake London]]. He argued Pitts could be the most 'recession-proof' pass catcher on the roster regardless of who plays quarterback -- Kirk Cousins, Michael Penix Jr., or otherwise -- because of his contested-catch and fade-route ability, skills he thinks fit Penix especially well. Bob Harris (untracked) countered that Kirk Cousins' track record of boosting tight end production (citing Jordan Reed, Tyler Conklin) matters, but Waldman said quarterback quality barely moves the needle here as long as it isn't Taylor Heinicke.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): Pitts is the next tier down from his system-safe tight ends ([[George Kittle]], [[Sam LaPorta]], [[Travis Kelce]], Mark Andrews) but sits in a McVay-like system that should generate the condensed, matchup-driven looks he wants. The risk he names is that Pitts has not had a quarterback — he jokes that if reading coverage were a Squid Game event, [[Desmond Ridder]] would be the first one gone. With Atlanta having finally found a quarterback or two ([[Kirk Cousins]] and [[Michael Penix Jr.]]), Waldman thinks one way or the other we see Pitts return to what he was as a rookie with Matt Ryan.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): same verdict as [[Drake London]] — in for a long season, and for scheme and quarterback reasons rather than talent. Pitts is one of the better back-shoulder boundary receivers of his draft class in Waldman's evaluation, and Atlanta has never consistently featured him that way; [[Kirk Cousins]] is not the quarterback to do it, and Waldman says he still is not delivering with the velocity the throw requires after watching the last two games. Everything Atlanta does is short and over the middle, where Pitts has been featured on some plays with some success, but defenses are taking him and London away and Cousins is content to work to [[Darnell Mooney]] and Ray-Ray McCloud instead. Waldman framed the whole thing as a [[Michael Penix Jr.]] discussion — absent a quarterback change, he does not expect Pitts to get featured.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): Waldman grouped Pitts with [[Drake London]] as trust-throw, contested-catch players stuck with a quarterback who does not make trust throws. Kirk Cousins targets the speedsters instead, has no mobility to prolong plays, and is being schemed to avoid what he cannot do; Waldman noted one boundary throw that was behind, late and well defended and probably should not have been thrown. He said it is both true that the market over-invested in Pitts and that circumstances hold him back. If Michael Penix Jr. gets into a game, Waldman expects Pitts' targets to elevate.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): asked flatly what Pitts is at this point, Waldman answered that he is a move tight end and, for fantasy managers, a low-end TE1 — with the expectation that he eventually works his way up as this Atlanta offense expands under [[Kirk Cousins]]. It is a deliberately modest verdict on a player once drafted as a positional cheat code, and Waldman spent notably less time defending him than he did on [[Darnell Mooney]] and [[Drake London]] in the same segment.
- 2024-10-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-24]]) [Dynasty]: Pitts is his case study for how much environment and directed feedback matter beyond raw landing spot. Waldman's theory is that if Pitts had been drafted by the 49ers or Kansas City — a stable organization with a stable quarterback and a stable offensive mind, a clear expectation of his role and accountability to it — he could have been a superstar early. Instead he got a mishmash of coordinators and bad quarterbacks and no directed feedback, and Waldman argues that when the players around you are not playing up to snuff you start overcompensating and overthinking your own role, the way a musician levels up faster playing alongside great musicians. He frames Pitts as living year to year trying to figure out the right approach, and cites this as why some players have late career renaissances once they find an environment where it clicks. Co-host Adam Harstad (not tracked) adds the flip side: being the only good player on a bad unit means you are told you did well no matter what, which is as damaging as never being able to do anything right.
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): despite Pitts sitting as the TE4 to [[Cade Otten]]'s TE6 at the time, Waldman would rather have Otton for the rest of the season. Atlanta is not scheming Pitts open the way Tampa Bay does for Otton, Pitts is the less versatile and less frequent blocker so he gets fewer play-action-derived chances, and he is the less consistent player right now. Waldman was careful not to write him off: he values Pitts' talent, thinks the right fit could make him good again, and expects him to be good enough to start this season — but the upside edge belongs to Otton.
- 2024-12-02 — According to co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]], with [[Matt Waldman]] agreeing: Pitts is the emblem of how bad the tight end position has been in 2024, scoring 2.1, 9.5, 1.9 and zero points over his last four games on a 5.3 percent target share in the most recent one. Waldman ties the collapse partly to [[Kirk Cousins]]'s decline, arguing [[Michael Penix Jr.]] would help Atlanta's pass catchers.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-19 (NFL QB Lunacy)]]): [[Michael Penix Jr.]] can leverage Pitts more easily than [[Kirk Cousins]] could, because Penix throws it further and higher with placement that lets a long, big target go up and win it — exactly the throws Cousins lost when his post-Achilles velocity faded. Waldman groups Pitts with [[Drake London]] as Atlanta's top plays on big-play throws over the closing schedule, particularly on the routes Penix threw best in college: inside-breaking-to-outside routes, sail routes, deep overs, and fades from the slot against nickelbacks or safeties. Caveat carried through the whole segment: Penix's timing passing game (deep comebacks, deep outs) is the part least likely to be sharp early, and complexity at the NFL level may not translate right away.
- 2024-12-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-23]]): Pitts could have been more involved in [[Michael Penix Jr.]]'s first start but dropped a ball, and Waldman's reaction was a blunt 'Kyle Pitts, what the f**k?' — flagging it as a topic that needs its own show. His sharpest framing: watching [[Brock Bowers]] this season, 'that's what Kyle Pitts was going to be,' and apparently is not. Bob Harris (untracked co-host) agreed he had thought the same. Waldman's expectation of increased Pitts usage with Penix under center did not materialize, so the quarterback change alone is not treated as a fix for the profile.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'Kyle Pitts all day long' over [[Cade Otten]] at Underdog TE18 — 'I don't care if I'm wrong.' Waldman's rule: if you are picking a tight end past the 15th one off the board, take the one with the enormous potential. He thinks Pitts matches perfectly with [[Michael Penix Jr.]] in theory, and that year one is only a guide because they had [[Desmond Ridder]] under center — Waldman's line was that if quarterback decision-making were red light/green light, Ridder did not know he was colorblind and would not make it ten yards. With a legitimate quarterback who throws receivers open and the right scheming, Waldman says Pitts could be a monster if he is healthy enough to roll. His stated risk: whether Pitts stopped working after making it in the league — if so, Otten outplays him. Untracked co-host Bob Harris expects to still be calling Pitts disappointing years from now.
- 2025-02-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-21]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon calls Pitts a trade candidate this offseason and says he would not be surprised if he is off Atlanta's roster, listing him only as a 'for now' asset when totalling the Falcons' pass-catcher depth. He pushed back hard on the recurring sneaky-tight-end-one hype cycle, saying he had seen three separate Pitts hype posts from a fantasy social account in February 2025 alone, and that while he hopes Pitts finds career success somewhere, 'it's not going to be at Atlanta, that's for sure.' He also volunteered that Pitts is still the starting tight end on one of his own dynasty teams 'against my will' — a self-deprecating admission of sunk-cost exposure rather than an endorsement.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]) [Best Ball]: still feeling it on Pitts, who is going TE16 in Best Ball while Waldman would consider him at TE8 — 'essentially a free square play.' The case is fit with [[Michael Penix Jr.]]: Waldman thinks Pitts can win on the boundary fades Penix excels at, working outside-in the way [[Drake London]] does, whereas an end-of-career Matt Ryan threw him open on scheme plays rather than using him vertically, and the Arthur Smith era diverted work to [[Jonnu Smith]]. He also notes Pitts was not fully healthy last year and is still very young. Waldman is explicit about the reputational cost of the call — he does not mind looking bad for the right reasons, unlike analysts who refuse to be bitten twice — and going down the board he would not clearly prefer [[Dalton Kincaid]], [[Jake Ferguson]], [[David Njoku]] or [[Tucker Kraft]] (whom he is less sold on with [[Luke Musgrave]] likely back), though [[Colston Loveland]] and [[Evan Engram]] are ahead of Pitts for him.
- 2025-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-07]]): Pitts is the headline precedent for tight ends being sold as X receivers, and Koh (not a tracked expert) said the experiment failed miserably despite Pitts being a genuine unicorn athlete taken in the top 10. Harmon's framing is that even in his one good NFL season the X-receiver usage was a cool little wrinkle rather than top-10-in-the-league perimeter work, and the deeper cost is that Pitts then was not good enough as an inline tight end — he played under 50% of snaps in a bunch of games late last season. Harmon used this as the general warning about unicorn tight end deployment: diminishing returns, because these players are not better receivers than actual wide receivers.
- 2025-06-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-02]]): yes, Pitts needs a change of scenery — but explicitly not for the usual 'he's been misused and would instantly thrive elsewhere' reason. Waldman frames it the way [[Baker Mayfield]] needed one: a message that a career can end suddenly, that you are not good enough at this stage, and that you have to mature and figure out the little details that make a big difference in football. Those details, he says, have often been missing with Pitts. In fairness to Pitts, Waldman notes [[Michael Penix Jr.]] would in theory be a great fit for what Pitts does best, but Atlanta has not gone that route — partly because with [[Darnell Mooney]] and [[Drake London]] able to play the perimeter, deploying a less versatile, less multidimensional perimeter option like Pitts does not help them. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) reports the Falcons are taking calls on him.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): the Week 1 revival is real but limited — Pitts gets 'the rookie [[Dalton Kincaid]] treatment', flat routes and dump-downs, now that [[Michael Penix Jr.]] is the first Atlanta quarterback since Matt Ryan good enough to work to a second and third read and buy time. But Waldman does not believe the staff trusts the downfield ball-winning ability they saw early in his career; something about Pitts has cost him that organizational confidence. If Penix's game keeps growing, Pitts's could grow with it — Waldman says he would rather see it than believe it, calling the ball-winner-downfield version 'more possible than probable at this stage', when it should have been probable two or three years ago. Waldman notes he holds no Pitts shares.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-15]]): Waldman is 'feeling' the Pitts revival, with the caveat that the bar is low — he is at least no longer on a milk carton, and 're-emerging' is the more accurate word than 'emerging' since he already emerged as a rookie and faded. The reason he believes it now is the quarterback: [[Michael Penix Jr.]] is the first Atlanta quarterback equipped to make Pitts worthwhile because he throws receivers open, leverages their height and wingspan, and can place the ball down the field. Waldman expects it to compound — as Pitts gains confidence and plays well, Atlanta rewards him with more work, which he thinks is what is starting to show up.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-18]]): Atlanta leads the NFL in 12 personnel usage, and Harmon reads that as the Falcons genuinely trying to make it work with Pitts — getting him out into real routes while a second tight end (Charlie Woerner, 83 snaps but only 16 routes, roughly 22.5% route participation) stays in to block, possibly to help a right tackle spot hit by injuries. Pitts has run a route on 87.3% of drop backs, aligning in the slot on 23.8% of snaps and out wide on 20%, which Harmon calls about right: move him around, but do not park him outside where he becomes 'the biggest, slowest X receiver in the league.' He ties the usage to Atlanta's thin receiver depth behind [[Drake London]] (shoulder) and [[Darnell Mooney]], where [[Casey Washington]] is the top backup.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-29]]): Waldman is feeling Atlanta finally using Pitts correctly and hopes it holds for 11-12 weeks. They opened the game with a sail route, which he says is [[Michael Penix Jr.]]'s favorite and best throw, versus the season-long diet of five-yard turn-outs — the old [[Dalton Kincaid]] usage — which wastes a rebounder who should run downfield with his back to the quarterback and win the ball. They also stopped telegraphing flat throws the way they do with [[Jonnu Smith]] and instead borrowed the [[Tyler Warren]] / [[Tucker Kraft]] template: low-red-zone play action off a block where the defender responsible is fooled and Pitts is the only, wide-open target. The combination of repeated downfield looks plus easy touchdown chances is the good sign. Waldman notes he is seeing 'tight end three through the end of Sunday' and expected far more TE3 finishes than TE23 finishes from him. Bob Harris (not tracked) says it is still hard to shoehorn Pitts into lineups weekly and wants more before he buys.
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-09]]): Atlanta had been misusing Pitts -- deploying him like [[Tucker Kraft]] as a bowling ball catching flat routes and running downhill -- and Waldman flagged the post-Carolina adjustment away from that as part of why he likes [[Michael Penix Jr.]] in Week 6. He says he likes where Pitts is heading and puts him in the same emerging tier as [[Mason Taylor]], but if forced to choose for this week he would probably still start Taylor.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): asked whether Pitts stays merely good enough to tempt you but not good enough to hit high-end projections, Waldman said he expects him to remain at this level for now. The conditional upside: if another week shows [[Michael Penix Jr.]] and [[Drake London]] connecting on fades and trust throws, he expects Atlanta to extend that treatment to Pitts, and then Pitts could really come on. The risk is that this proves a one-week anomaly and Zac Robinson goes back into his shell.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-04]]): James Koh (not a tracked expert) says he likes Pitts's usage across this whole season, not just this game — Atlanta schemes him open and [[Michael Penix Jr.]] finds him when plays are drawn up for him, so the staff has finally settled on a workable role. Both hosts are clear he is no longer 'that guy': Harmon calls Pitts and [[Darnell Mooney]] role players who get over-emphasized in discussion of this offense, with [[Bijan Robinson]] the second-most-targeted option at 10 in the London game. Harmon's broader position is that with a big drop-off after London, he does not particularly want Atlanta trying to get anyone else rolling.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06]]): Waldman points out Atlanta has never used Pitts as the rebounder he was at Florida — Matt Ryan was too old to throw that ball late in his time there and nobody since could either, and they did not go to Pitts on the fades that produced [[Drake London]]'s three New England touchdowns. Waldman's complaint is that Atlanta used him the way Buffalo used [[Dalton Kincaid]] for two years: don't make him [[Tucker Kraft]], let him be Tucker Kraft plus, because Kraft is a good seam player who can take a hit but is not proven at winning position with the ball in the air, and Pitts can do that. Angelo concedes Pitts has his warts. Waldman's test for whether the fade concept is real: if Pitts gets included in the act over the next two games it is genuine; if it is only occasional with London, they are not taking it seriously.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-10]]): Pitts should have had a monster game in Berlin and finished 2-of-5 targets for 38 yards, but Waldman charts three plays that went missing — [[Michael Penix Jr.]] was short on a would-be touchdown where Pitts had a step to the left sideline in the end zone, Pitts dropped a wide-open deep over he should never have dropped, and a tipped pass killed another target. A realistic line was five catches for 80-90 yards and a score. This extends the storyline Waldman advanced the prior week: Atlanta appears to be deliberately targeting Pitts and [[Drake London]] on more athletic downfield routes where they can win the ball, and he expects it to keep working even though it did not this week. The caveat is time — it has to work soon or Pitts is gone in free agency, and Waldman notes the long-term win for Penix may be Atlanta concluding they need the player they thought Pitts would be rather than Pitts himself.
