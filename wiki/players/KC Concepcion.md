# KC Concepcion

- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: his favorite 2026 receiver, though he is careful to say favorite rather than best. Waldman likes the feel as a route runner, how well he finds openings, his consistency as a pass catcher, and how tough and physical he plays for his skill set, and thinks the move to a better situation helps. His framing comp is oppositional: Concepcion is 'the anti-[[Nyck Harbor]]' in every capacity, making everything look easy where Harbor makes everything look difficult. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees enthusiastically, calling him an absolute gamer who was the entire NC State offense as a freshman and projecting a day-two draft grade.
- 2025-12-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-01 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman compared KC Concepcion to Steve Smith II (the possession-slot/flanker version, not Steve Smith Sr.) -- a strong route runner who wins after the catch and in contested situations, once a productive Giants/Rams complementary piece before a knee injury derailed him. Waldman called Concepcion a smart, gritty player in that mold, and separately compared his skill set to [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]'s if used more downfield than Detroit typically deploys St. Brown.
- 2026-01-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-19]]) [Dynasty]: a late-summer preseason 'superstar' whose momentum evaporated during the season. Worth watching as Kevin Stefanski's staff arrives in Atlanta, but Waldman wants to see more complete route running before buying back in, and says his outlook depends partly on whether Kirk Cousins or Michael Penix Jr. starts -- Waldman believes Penix had the better rapport with him, while Cousins was reportedly more attuned to [[David Sills V]] (a shared West Virginia/former-QB background cited as one reason). Overall Waldman isn't excited: he makes some splash plays but isn't an up-and-comer yet, especially on a team likely to lean on more two-tight-end sets under Stefanski/Rees.
- 2026-02-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-16]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman flags real risk in Concepcion's catch-point technique -- he's a frequent 'clap attacker' (trapping the ball against his body rather than extending), comping the pattern to early-career [[Courtland Sutton]] (still a viable starter for years before eventually solidifying as a true WR1) and noting other clap-attackers like [[Diontae Johnson]] and [[Quentin Johnston]] struggled similarly. He also has late-window tracking issues, using underhand technique when he should go overhand -- the same specific flaw Waldman has flagged with [[Quentin Johnston]] and [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]. Combined, these concerns worry Waldman despite genuinely liking Concepcion as a prospect since his freshman tape -- he's an excellent zone receiver who adjusts his body well to the ball. Waldman calls catch-point technique the most 'elementary' fix a receiver should already have, and warns it requires tearing the game down to the studs to rebuild -- something 23-year-olds often resist. He worries a slow fix could get Concepcion unfairly labeled a 'wasted pick' by fantasy analysts.
- 2026-02-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-17]]): Harmon grades KC Concepcion as a first-round receiver -- explosive, wins against man coverage, plays through contact and takes big hits well, with some drops he attributes more to concentration than to hands. He slots Concepcion alongside Carnell Tate, Makai Lemon and Denzel Boston as one of five receivers he currently views as first-round talents in the 2026 class.
- 2026-02-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-24]]): placed in Harmon's second tier of the 2026 WR prospect class alongside Jordan Tyson and Denzel Boston, behind top-tier Carnell Tate and Makai Lemon; discussed as a name Cleveland or Pittsburgh could look at for receiver help, though Harmon says he'd rather not see the very top tier of this class (Tate/Lemon) land in Cleveland's current WR 'wasteland.'
- 2026-03-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-02]]) [Dynasty]: disagreed with the industry read (cited by Bob Harris and others online) that Concepcion's smooth gauntlet-drill performance alleviated hand-catching concerns -- said 'fuck it,' it did not. Of his seven gauntlet targets he caught six cleanly, but one showed his hands still too far apart. Waldman explained 'clap attacking' -- hands converging late to meet the ball rather than being positioned to receive it -- as the root issue visible on Concepcion's college tape, tied to a number of drops. He called it a moderately correctable technical flaw requiring dedicated Jugs-machine repetition, comping Michael Wilson (Stanford) as a receiver who fixed the same issue. Despite the flaw, Waldman still ranks Concepcion in his top five receivers this class, and possibly top three.
- 2026-03-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-03]]): KC Concepcion answered a key question at the combine, delivering a strong gauntlet drill performance despite tape showing drop issues (he 'claps at the ball' at times) -- Harmon reads those as focus lapses rather than a hands problem, and seeing him lock in for the drill was a good sign. He also measured in at a strong 5'11, 196 lbs, alleviating fears he'd secretly check in around 185. Harmon no longer thinks he has to be bucketed as a slot-only player, calling him a first-round-caliber prospect as long as the drops get corrected.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-05]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman still grades [[KC Concepcion]] as a near-instant NFL starter, but flagged a specific catch-technique flaw after re-watching his Combine gauntlet drill -- of seven catches, he counted six where Concepcion's hands were apart and 'clapped' onto the ball late rather than being together before the ball arrived, which Waldman said matches the pattern behind the drops on his college tape and traces to the ball arriving away from his frame before he's anticipated it. Waldman framed this as exactly the kind of root-cause detail scouting should isolate so a player or his coaches know where to start fixing it, and told dynasty drafters to watch for it -- if Concepcion struggles as a pro, 'you'll know why.' He noted some evaluators group Concepcion in the same tier as [[Carnell Tate]] and Makai Lemon, and suggested a savvy dynasty trader could try to acquire rookie-pick rights for Concepcion at that inflated valuation before the flaw shows up in production.
- 2026-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-26]]): KC Concepcion (6'0', 196 lbs, 4.4 40, 37-inch vertical, NC State/Texas A&M) is Harmon's 'guy this year' among this WR prospect class - he set out to rank him higher than consensus and considers him a top tier-two prospect in a class he views as lacking any tier-one (elite) prospect like Marvin Harrison Jr., Ja'Marr Chase or Malik Nabers were. RP charting: near-all-green route tree, 80%+ success on the out, dig, curl and slant, 78.3% success rate vs. man coverage (bettered since 2021 only by Jalen Waddle, Elijah Moore, DeVonta Smith and Travis Hunter) and 83.2% vs. zone - the only prospect since 2021 alongside DeVonta Smith, Travis Hunter, Rome Odunze and Chris Olave to clear both thresholds. Plays 65.2% of snaps outside but is moved around pre-snap (motion on 55.3% of those), and is an elite returner: 456 return yards and 2 TDs on 18.2 yards per return in 13 games, won the Paul Hornung Award. Harmon's lone real concern is hands/technique: 7.4% drop rate in the RP sample (6.5% career, peaking at 8% at NC State in 2024), inconsistent catch mechanics (sometimes alligator-arms or claps at the ball, sometimes doesn't) despite strong ball-tracking and an 85% contested catch rate on a 19% contested target rate. Harmon argues drops are overemphasized and not sticky year to year, says Concepcion 'does enough to make the drops worth living with,' and believes that without the hands issue he'd be a tier-one prospect who should already be a consensus first-round pick - and could be the best receiver in the class if the technique cleans up. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) floated a Terry McLaurin comp for Concepcion; Harmon agreed that fits Concepcion better than it fits Jordan Tyson.
- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Concepcion shows clear running-back carryover after the catch -- pressing creases, setting up defenders, bouncing or cutting back, and finding solutions to unanticipated obstacles, including real creativity between the tackles in the red zone at NC State. He's also an effective return specialist. Waldman likes him a lot despite some clap-attacking issues in his catch technique.
- 2026-04-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman had Concepcion in his top group despite missing pre-draft workouts after a March knee scope. He praised his patience and short-area quickness (residual from early-career running back usage at NC State), versatility inside and outside, and elusiveness, but flagged a real drop concern -- 17 career drops, driven by a 'clap attacking' catch technique (trapping the ball rather than hands-together) that shows up especially on high, over-the-head targets. Waldman compared him favorably to Khalil Shakir (whom he thinks Buffalo underuses) and, more notably, to a young Steve Smith Sr. or early-career Stefon Diggs for playmaking upside if he can play both inside and outside. He noted Concepcion has shown up in mock drafts to the Patriots at pick 31, which Chris Harris flagged as notable given his own fandom.
- 2026-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman put Concepcion in his second tier with immediate-starter upside, calling him a better route runner and more sudden after-catch player than [[Khalil Shakir]] (a Waldman favorite he thinks Buffalo underuses), and comping his hybrid flanker/slot role to Giants great Steve Smith Sr. His drop concerns trace to 'clap-attacking' the ball with his palms instead of tracking it in cleanly -- worst on high-point and away targets -- rather than a true tracking issue like Quentin Johnston's. Harmon's charting backed this: 90% catch rate on pinpoint targets, 60% on off-target adjustments, 75% on contested-pinpoint targets, 86% on contested targets requiring a big adjustment, but only 67% (small sample) when tightly covered. Both see him as more Jayden Reed/Stefon Diggs slot-hybrid than X, and Harmon said if the catch technique weren't holding him back, Concepcion would be a tier-one prospect.
- 2026-04-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-20]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is 'feeling it' that Kansas City wants a true top wide receiver and floated KC Concepcion as the fit, while voicing real skepticism about Andy Reid's actual track record drafting receivers -- outside of Jeremy Maclin, he couldn't name a good one, calling Tyreek Hill more of a talent gamble than an evaluation win and describing [[Rashee Rice]] as 'a construct of the offense' rather than a well-rounded talent. Waldman sees Concepcion and Rice as near-redundant archetypes -- both plus after-the-catch runners who drop high-pointed downfield passes away from their frame -- but rates Concepcion the better route runner, someone who could reduce Rice's target-share relevance without limiting him. His bigger worry is scheme complexity: he feels Chiefs receivers 'never seem to be able to grasp it' and develop into their physical ceiling. Separately, asked whether Concepcion's yards-after-catch ability exceeds [[Makai Lemon]]'s (raised as a 'hot take' by a CBS draft analyst), Waldman said it isn't a hot take -- Concepcion is at or near the best after-the-catch receiver in this class, ahead of Lemon, who is tough after the catch but not on that level.
- 2026-04-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman feels good about Concepcion (24th overall) becoming a starter for Cleveland, more so than co-host Bob Harris projects for [[Denzel Boston]], though whether either delivers true starter-level fantasy value is the bigger question. If Deshaun Watson is actually the Week 1 starter -- which Waldman says is a real possibility even though most fantasy players scoff at it -- Concepcion projects as the player fed the ball most as an extension of the run game out of the slot, since OC Todd Monken tends to target receivers working the middle of the field even while mixing in heavier, more spread-out personnel.
- 2026-04-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-28]]): Concepcion was one of Harmon's guys pre-draft and landed with Cleveland at 24 -- a strong scheme fit under Todd Monken even with the QB situation 'super duper murky.' Charted him as the best press-man beater in this class (92nd percentile success rate vs. man, 87th percentile vs. press), citing tape against LSU and Notre Dame's Mansour Delaney; he split snaps across all three receiver spots at Texas A&M (45% on the line, 45% off, roughly 30% slot) and graded well everywhere, including 81st percentile vs. zone and a top-4 contested catch rate in the class. Acknowledges drop and catch-point focus issues but argues 'those guys don't fail because of drops' when every other tool grades out, explicitly rejecting the Cory Coleman comp some Browns fans are drawing -- Coleman's numbers were scheme-inflated by Baylor's spread offense, while Concepcion produced running pro-style routes on one side of the field. Expects him to rotate between X and slot/Z alongside fellow rookie [[Denzel Boston]].
