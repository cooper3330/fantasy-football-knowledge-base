# Dylan Sampson

## Expert Takes

- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) is higher on Sampson than market sentiment — a 64 in the pre-draft model, above his 60 threshold, ranking as the number-five prospect without draft capital and seventh once his expected capital around pick 100 is applied, meaning the model likes him more than the mock-draft market does. His combined PFF run and receiving grade is 69th percentile and 16 percent of his carries went for 10-plus yards against a 13 percent college average, marking him as explosive. The concern is size at 190 pounds, which will cost him draft capital unless he runs fast at the combine. McFarland grades him well ahead of where [[Jaylen Wright]] came out last year. Waldman is also higher than the market and groups Sampson with [[Damien Martinez]], [[Omarion Hampton]] and [[Quinshon Judkins]] as backs who could contribute early, asking whether Sampson can reach 200 pounds or, like [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and Jamaal Charles, be smart enough to thrive at 195 in the right offense.
- 2025-06-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-30]]): Duce Staley's comment that Sampson could play the slot on third downs does not make him a 2025 fantasy asset — 'fuck that.' Waldman says this is the time of year when every back is said to play more slot, and it rarely means a Larry Centers-style 100-plus-target role; it usually signals a rotational part in the offense. Cleveland still has [[Diontae Johnson]] as the likely slot receiver if he does not repeat last year's problems, plus DeAndre Carter and David Bell, all of whom would be used there ahead of Sampson or [[Jerome Ford]]. Waldman keeps his projection at roughly 11 catches and 77 yards and will not move it on a coach's quote; best case, injuries push him to 20-30 catches. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed, noting Duce Staley says a lot of motivational things.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Sampson had jumped 38 ADP spots in the week after the [[Quinshon Judkins]] arrest, and Waldman thinks the move is directionally right but overdone. In a vacuum he believes Sampson has the potential to be a lead back in an NFL offense this year — 'a good player and an underrated guy' — but not a bell cow, and he is less of a sure thing than Judkins looked immediately post-draft. The specific limits: Sampson is a smaller back, currently more limited against zone looks and will have to prove he can develop there, and while his contact balance is good, teams will always question whether he can absorb a full-size NFL workload. Waldman's comp for that organizational hesitancy is how the Cowboys, and now the Titans, have treated [[Tony Pollard]]. Practical advice: move him up, but not far — at his new draft range there are plenty of players he has fewer questions about. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) said he is not eagerly grabbing him and prefers [[Jerome Ford]].
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: agrees with [[Chris Harris]]'s [[Jerome Ford]] comparison — quick, fast, good balance for his size, plays tough and plays big for his size, but 'for his size' is the qualifier on everything, which likely caps the number of touches he gets in any season of his career. The saving grace Waldman offers is the environment: Kevin Stefanski brought his two-tight-end run game from Minnesota to Cleveland, the line is healthier, and with [[Joe Flacco]] under center the offense is no longer catering to [[Deshaun Watson]], who Waldman says did not want to play football and did not fit the system, or [[Jameis Winston]], whose legs were five steps behind his head. That could make Cleveland a better running team than its bottom-dwelling 2024 stats suggest — but not enough for Sampson, [[Quinshon Judkins]] or Ford to be more than bye-week matchup plays at roughly 500-700 yards and four to six touchdowns apiece.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): 'feel it as a flex play in a deep league, but fuck it from a week-to-week standpoint.' Sampson caught the ball well, has juice, runs hard and is a smart runner, so there will be a role for him. But [[Quinshon Judkins]] could return as soon as this week pending the league's meeting with him — per Adam Schefter it is possible — and Waldman thinks the players with the most to lose are [[Jerome Ford]] and Rocket Sanders rather than Sampson. The loss will not be immediate, but the writing goes on the wall if Judkins performs to his standard. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) agreed Sampson survives the squeeze.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): Sampson and [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] led Cleveland in touches in a 71-offensive-play game built on slow, sticks-moving drives. Sampson had 20 touches despite playing fewer snaps than [[Jerome Ford]], and especially in the second half was the main reason drives kept going — he plucked an underthrown screen off the turf for a nice gain and looks very small but is definitely quick. The limit is physicality: he was picked up and body-slammed backwards a couple of times. Harris calls it a good debut but flags the looming specter of [[Quinshon Judkins]]. In the waiver segment, guest Jake Ciely of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) said he would take Sampson over Judkins as a shallow-league add; Harris ranked Judkins ahead. Both are ~55-57% rostered.
