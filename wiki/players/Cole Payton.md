# Cole Payton

- 2026-03-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-09]]) [Dynasty]: on reports that multiple personnel execs rank Payton as the second-best QB in the 2026 class, Waldman comps him to 'a young Dak Prescott' and says he'd have Payton within his own top three-to-four QBs on the board, so the execs' ranking doesn't surprise him -- with the caveat that it's North Dakota State, so the level of competition and defensive sophistication he's faced is well below NFL quality. Positives Waldman cites: cycling through two-to-three reads, good pocket-exit decisions (knowing when to throw away vs. tuck and run), reading pre-snap blitz looks, 'reshaping zones' by rolling one way and finding the safe throw against the grain of coverage on the opposite side, good ball placement over underneath defenders, and the ability to climb, reset and fire under pressure. His most optimistic projected outcome isn't an instant starter -- more likely sitting a year or two behind a decent situation before growing into the job, similar to how Prescott's path was originally envisioned.
- 2026-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-30]]): despite post-combine buzz that Cole Payton is a top-three or top-five quarterback in this class, Waldman disagrees -- 'fuck that, he's not quarterback three... not quarterback five either.' He credits Payton's athleticism and running ability (233-pound frame, good speed) but calls his arm strength merely passable, possibly not enough for an NFL starter. Payton has only one full season of starting experience, which Waldman frames through Drew Brees's theory that quarterbacks need roughly 50-60 combined college/pro starts to reliably project decision-making, coverage reading and game management; Waldman is considering building that threshold into future RSP tiering. He distinguishes 'raw' from 'inexperienced' -- citing Anthony Richardson as a comp for a QB who performs well within what he has seen but struggles with what he hasn't -- and applies the same read to Payton, plus Trey Lance's one big year at the same school as a cautionary comp for small-sample breakouts.
- 2026-04-16 — According to Dalton Wasserman (guest draft analyst, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: [[Cole Payton]] out of North Dakota State is the later-round quarterback Wasserman flagged as most fantasy-relevant despite being only a day-three prospect. A big, well-built lefty with a good arm, he spent most of his career in a wildcat role behind Cam Miller before finally starting this past season, when Wasserman argues he was the best quarterback in the FCS. His carrying trait for fantasy is rushing ability; Wasserman compared his likely NFL role to a Taysom Hill-style package player getting a handful of designed-run and deep-shot snaps a game. Worth monitoring his landing spot for Dynasty Superflex streaming value.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-07 (Going Deep with Brandon Angelo)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman doesn't understand why people draft Payton late in rookie drafts, calling his arm and downfield accuracy suspect; he thinks the appeal is purely that Payton is on the Eagles rather than actual evaluation. Payton didn't make Waldman's do-draft list at all -- he ranked [[Diego Pavia]] as the last quarterback he'd actually consider taking.
