# Matthew Stafford

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]), discussing [[DeMarcus Robinson]]'s off-script production: comps Stafford to a "Mahomes 1.0" for arm talent — able to fit throws into "portholes," the kind of tight-window accuracy that lets complementary receivers profit off-script — while noting Stafford is less mobile than [[Patrick Mahomes]] and "a little more mistake-prone."
- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]): feeling Stafford as solid value at his roughly QB19 ADP given a decent offensive line and the receivers back, calling him a shining example of the late-round quarterback strategy.
- 2024-05-20 — According to guest Jagger May (Football Guys, untracked) and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-20]]): discussing a rumored contract extension, May argued the Rams would be making a mistake to bank on Stafford — who has broken his back twice and had two ACL tears and shoulder issues — starting deep into his late 30s, and said he wouldn't have blamed the Rams for taking a rookie QB early the way the Falcons did with [[Michael Penix Jr.]], despite not being a Penix believer. Waldman pushed back on Tom Brady longevity comparisons, noting Stafford never had Brady's obsessive training regimen or offensive lines, calling him essentially "the first iteration of [[Patrick Mahomes]] without an offensive line" who still plays with reckless physicality even now that he's wiser; he expects the Rams to pursue the extension anyway while trying to line up a succession option behind him.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): on rumors of a Vikings trade for Stafford, Waldman says a sensible organization would not do it and it is not going to happen. His reasoning is twofold — Minnesota would be stepping backwards from [[Sam Darnold]], who has been in camp with the team and has momentum, and Stafford carries two bad back injuries and a career that could end on any hit, so the Vikings would be stalling their own development for a quarterback with no reliable runway. He grants Stafford is still fun and still tough enough to handle the beating he takes, calling him at least a tough 'granny-ass' quarterback, and notes the Rams have won three in a row.
- 2024-11-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-18]]): asked whether Stafford is the prequel to [[Patrick Mahomes]], Waldman said yes without hesitation — he has been making Mahomes-esque throws his whole career, including that weekend, and the difference was environment: a bad Detroit team where he was getting killed on a regular basis. Waldman went further on toughness, saying that while Mahomes is by no means soft, Stafford has shown rare intestinal fortitude, still playing at a reasonable level after breaking his back a couple of times and absorbing the early-career pounding — 'the dude's scrap iron.' Counterfactual: with a Ben Johnson-caliber coordinator, a good offensive line and both [[Calvin Johnson]] and [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], Waldman believes Stafford would have been a first-ballot Hall of Famer; as it is he may still get in as a volume-based selection by the loose standards of Hall of Fame voters.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Stafford did not have a great season by his standards and has had long stretches of bad play this year, masked by the Rams winning games. But Harmon explained why the Minnesota matchup was still a mismatch in his favor: Brian Flores' blitz-heavy defense destroys bad and young quarterbacks — even good ones like [[C.J. Stroud]] — while a veteran with Stafford's long catalog can ID where pressure is coming from, get the ball out and drive it into tight windows. Harmon admitted he had picked Minnesota and got the game completely wrong. His concern against Philadelphia is that the Eagles do not need to blitz to generate pressure, which removes exactly the thing Stafford is uniquely good at beating.
- 2025-01-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-21]]): Stafford, turning 37, is the X factor of the entire Rams offseason — if he walks away it changes the whole outlook, and if he is back Harmon is 'very, very rosy' and would pick Los Angeles to win the NFC West again with $46.6M in cap space and only a couple of tweaks needed. Both hosts agree he declined: co-host James Koh (not tracked) says late-season wins masked his deficiencies and he showed his age, taking a rib injury the one time he got out of the pocket. Harmon floats bringing in a bridge or developmental quarterback — a [[Daniel Jones]]-type to groom, or [[Sam Darnold]] as a top landing-spot fit if Stafford retires given the system familiarity.
- 2025-01-30 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-30]]: the trade rumors will not happen, because the only realistic trade partners are rebuilding teams and Stafford would simply retire rather than go to one — he has a ring, loves playing for McVay, remembers the down years in Detroit, and physically 'took one rush from the pocket and broke a bunch of ribs.' [[Matt Harmon]] agrees it is you play for the Rams or you don't play, and finds it strange the Rams GM keeps discussing it publicly, wondering whether it is contract leverage or calling Stafford's retirement bluff. Both flag a possible Las Vegas angle via familiarity with Darrell Bevell (Harmon jokes [[Brock Bowers]] would go to Mars), and Koh floats a straight Stafford-for-[[Tua Tagovailoa]] swap with Miami as pure spitballing — Harmon says the Rams would probably do it to get younger but that this is the wrong offseason without a quarterback prospect worth selling the farm for.
- 2025-02-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-03]]): 'feeling it' that Stafford stays with the Rams for 2025, further fueling [[Puka Nacua]]'s fantasy value. Waldman expects Los Angeles to look at this quarterback draft class and decide to wait another year, betting they can get one or two more seasons out of Stafford. His caveat is about the succession, not the season: if Stafford has only one year left and the Rams draft a good rookie, Sean McVay 'still has his puppet trunk' and will be more than glad to attach the strings to his newest rookie project.
- 2025-02-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-04]]): if Stafford is genuinely available this offseason, he would be a 'gorgeous' fit for the Chip Kelly Raiders — he satisfies the Kelly 'point guard' distributor requirement underneath while still taking deep shots off play action, and pairing him with a big-play run game would put defenses in the kind of conflict that generates one-on-one boundary looks. Harmon distinguishes the Rams run game (good, but a keep-you-on-schedule style) from the explosive ground game he expects in Las Vegas. He also frames Stafford's Rams tenure as the reason McVay's system is hard to pin down: McVay was ready to invest in the drop-back game in 2021 (shotgun, empty, 11 personnel) rather than the handholding Shanahan template used for [[Jared Goff]], though in 2024 they ran the Goff-style play-action offense with Stafford.
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]): Stafford staying in Los Angeles after being given permission to talk to other teams is the best possible outcome for both sides. Waldman would take Stafford over [[Daniel Jones]], [[Derek Carr]], [[Russell Wilson]] and [[Aaron Rodgers]] given the available veteran market, and values that the Rams' line won't make things worse for him. He argued teams with a young, established nucleus often waste it on a quarterback who hasn't yet found his footing — his example being Cleveland with a pre-lessons [[Baker Mayfield]] — so timing matters, and Stafford is only a little older than ideal. He also said it is great for [[Puka Nacua]] specifically: the rapport and fit already exist and shouldn't be disturbed. Bob Harris (not tracked) cited Dave Kluge's splits with and without Stafford — roughly 20 yards per game for [[Cooper Kupp]], 40 for Kenny Golladay, 25 for Golden Tate, over 20 for Calvin Johnson — as evidence Stafford fuels the fantasy fortunes of his pass catchers. Waldman called him a stylistic precursor to [[Patrick Mahomes]].
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): Stafford is an excellent late-round pick and will be overlooked in 2025 despite a great offense and great assets — co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) noted a Football Guys projection of QB26. Waldman's argument is that fantasy analysts see an age number and lose their minds, and that the actual issue has been Stafford's availability plus the Rams receivers' availability rather than his play. With [[Davante Adams]] added, Waldman said the offense can lean on Adams to carry the load when needed, which raises Stafford's floor.
- 2025-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]): Stafford is still a good quarterback but is very sensitive to pressure, with dramatically different numbers with and without it, and the Rams have not protected him — Coleman Shelton, who Harmon says was terrible with the Bears, may start at center over Beaux Limmer, and Trent Williams is dealing with blood clots at left tackle. Harmon rates the whole Rams offensive unit a 7.5 out of 10 for combustibility. James Koh (not tracked) supplies the reason for the pressure sensitivity: every time Stafford gets hit he gets hurt, he is already missing the first five days of camp with a back issue, and the backup options behind him — Jimmy Garoppolo and Stetson Bennett — are not viable. Harmon adds that he wants to be better at articulating how a team can go wrong, and sees four or five easy failure paths for the Rams, while still rating their ceiling very high.
- 2025-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris's number four offense he may be wrong about is the Rams, because Stafford's back is a risk priced into nobody's ADP — Stafford's own price is fine, but [[Puka Nacua]] (Harris top five) and [[Davante Adams]] (top 15) both need him. He is unwilling to render final judgment on August 14 and does not think Stafford or the receivers need much training camp, but names the fulcrum: if Stafford is not practicing normally by the run-up to Labor Day, you have to make the call and fade, and drafting right now is bad timing. The real question is whether Stafford can take a hit, possibly in a week one game against Houston's pass rush where he thinks either quarterback could get hurt. Guest Sigmund Bloom (Footballguys, not tracked) is more optimistic: Jimmy Garoppolo is probably the league's best backup, flourishes in structure, and Sean McVay's is a set-it-and-forget-it scheme; Harris counters that the Raiders version of Garoppolo did not look like the Super Bowl version.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-18]]): Waldman is not especially concerned about Stafford's back, calling him a 'premium pastrami sandwich' in the episode's deli-counter framing, but flagged that Rams transparency has 'become totally opaque' — they wouldn't even confirm whether Stafford watched a preseason game he was due to work out before. Bob Harris (not tracked) noted Peter Schrager, close with Sean McVay, is reporting nobody is remotely worried, which Harris read as McVay managing the message. Both hosts warned that uncertainty creates a vacuum that sucks in worst-case outcomes, and cited [[Christian McCaffrey]] in 2024 — glowing reports right up to Week 1, then questionable on Friday, then four games played — and [[Joe Mixon]] this year as the pattern. Waldman's practical response is to hunt the resulting discount rather than avoid the offense.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Dr. Deepak Chona of SportsMedAnalytics (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]]: Stafford's multiple epidural injections are a bigger deal than they were treated as, because they imply a herniated disc pinching a nerve — and nerve pain is not the kind of thing even a famously tough quarterback plays through, unlike the injuries Stafford has historically played through. Chona stressed the risk is multi-faceted: contact and non-contact both aggravate it, since throwing involves substantial trunk rotation and bending can set it off, and as an immobile stationary pocket passer Stafford depends on the Rams line to keep him upright and will take hits. He allowed that Stafford could play the whole season — Philip Rivers managed a similar issue — but Tony Romo was shut down with one, and the re-aggravation risk is high enough that Chona has downgraded [[Puka Nacua]] and [[Davante Adams]] on his own board.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris ranked Stafford around QB16 and argued that having [[Puka Nacua]] and [[Davante Adams]] in clear start territory creates no obligation to rank the quarterback high. Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) agreed: the Rams run a super-targeted passing attack between those two receivers, Stafford adds nothing on the ground, and if you accept the 8.5-point spread as a non-competitive game you may get a lot of [[Kyren Williams]] and [[Blake Corum]] instead. Harris pushed back slightly that Stafford can still work if he takes the touchdowns rather than Kyren Williams, and said he is playing pretty well — not the worst option, just not a top-six quarterback by the math.
- 2025-10-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-03]]): Stafford was 'really good' Thursday, including a great late drive to tie the game. Harris engages with Jeff Bell's 'narrow target tree' objection (Bell is not a tracked expert) — that there may be enough yards for [[Puka Nacua]] and [[Davante Adams]] but not enough left for the quarterback because he doesn't spread it around — and partly concedes it: there will be Rams games where they don't need to chase, run it more, and Stafford ends under 30 attempts and looks far less enticing, as in Week 1 against the Texans. But this is folded into his headline trade-away thesis: if he dealt a supposedly elite quarterback and had to use Stafford the rest of the way, he is '100 percent fine with it,' and Thursday shows why.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-06]]): Waldman is absolutely feeling that Stafford is the reason the Rams are competitive, estimating [[Jimmy Garoppolo]] would deliver about 75 percent of it with occasional really good throws. His case is manipulation of secondaries downfield and the ability to avoid pressure, reset and fire — he called Stafford 'Patrick Mahomes 1.0,' the precursor to [[Patrick Mahomes]], still visible at a venerable age. The example he gave was a throw to [[Tutu Atwell]] where Stafford made a tight spin to his left, reset quickly and fired right, knowing all along where he was going; most quarterbacks would widen the spin, burn too much time and never reach the receiver. Waldman added that Stafford makes hard things look easy, which is why the wider audience underrates him. He framed the whole Rams passing game, including [[Puka Nacua]] and [[Davante Adams]], as dependent on Stafford.
- 2025-10-31 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-31]]), with Cousin Josh (co-host, not tracked) agreeing: Stafford is the year's biggest surprise for both hosts. Harris says he moved [[Puka Nacua]] and [[Davante Adams]] down in his preseason rankings on the back of negative Stafford practice-availability news, unsure if he'd even play Week 1. Instead Stafford is currently 4th in MVP odds, has thrown 17 touchdowns to 2 interceptions, and sits 10th among fantasy quarterbacks despite already having his bye week -- Harris calls using him as a low-end QB1 'basically... complete shock.' Josh adds the Rams' scoring is unusually predictable because it funnels through Nacua and Adams (plus occasional running back touchdowns), and both hosts now view a Rams run out of the NFC as more than a hot take.
- 2025-10-31 — According to Adam Harstad (untracked Film and Theory co-host, [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-31]]) [Dynasty]: Stafford was, for a stretch, an example of the 'flat value' fully-scouted-veteran profile alongside Dak Prescott -- production without new information moving his price -- though Harstad noted this held true mainly 'sans injuries'.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-10]]): Stafford was always a great quarterback prospect in Waldman's view, but the argument he entertains now is that 'survival makes you great' — Stafford worked to prolong his career through broken bones in his back and the punishment he absorbed in Detroit, and that longevity let him see enough football that, in Tom Brady's phrasing, he knows all the answers to the test. Placed in a heavily schemed, precise offense on the level of a Ben Johnson system, nothing from defenses surprises him anymore; the only things that shake him are teammates doing something he did not expect. Waldman used this as the contrast for [[Jackson Dart]], who sits at the beginning of that road, has not played in multiple offenses, and must stay healthy long enough to accumulate the same reps.
- 2025-11-25 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-25]]): over his last five games (since Week 7), Stafford leads all quarterbacks in EPA per dropback, has thrown 18 touchdowns (next closest is 11, shared by Drake Maye and Joe Flacco), and has zero interceptions -- his last pick came all the way back in Week 3, leaving him at 30 TD/2 INT for the season. Harmon calls it 'the best season of his career' and, as of this recording, the odds-on MVP favorite; he agrees Stafford would be his MVP if the season ended today, crediting both Stafford's play and Sean McVay's well-constructed, defense-attacking offense (including elite work with [[Puka Nacua]] and [[Davante Adams]]).
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-05-07]]) [Superflex]: a self-declared 'flag player' -- Harris ranks Stafford 5th, while untracked co-host Josh says three major services and FantasyPros all have him outside the top 12 (seen at 12, 15 and 16). Harris calls that gap baffling given Stafford threw for 4,700+ yards and 46 touchdowns, comparing the industry's reluctance to trust a proven veteran to past skepticism around Tom Brady and Aaron Rodgers in their late-career MVP-caliber years. He does concede 46 touchdowns 'screams regression' and that Stafford could easily post 10 fewer scores and still be fine, and predicts that once Stafford looks good in October the market will chase him up to QB1/2 rankings reactively -- 'that's weird to me,' he says, that the industry won't get there proactively given his trust in the Rams' supporting cast ([[Puka Nacua]], [[Davante Adams]]).
- 2026-08-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Stafford is his No. 5 swing player, drawing a comparison to peak Aaron Rodgers as a player 'people just didn't believe in' despite winning. Despite being reigning MVP on a team with a top offensive projection, an elite receiving corps ([[Puka Nacua]]) and [[Davante Adams]] inside the top 20, Stafford is going as low as QB13-16 in early ADP, which Harris called nonsensical -- he has Stafford top-5 among quarterbacks. Harris projects roughly 33 passing touchdowns (down from last year's raw pace but still comfortably startable) and said the only real risk is injury, since the Rams managed it last year and a full healthy season should keep him inside the top 10 at QB. Guest Brandon Funston (not tracked) agreed the ADP disconnect makes no sense given the surrounding talent, citing Andy Behrens' recent Athletic piece on the same point. Both noted Stafford stays in the pocket and gets rid of the ball quickly, making him less injury-prone than a rushing QB like Jayden Daniels.
- 2026-08-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris's #1 ADP surprise -- Stafford, last year's fantasy MVP, is being 'completely ignored,' sitting at QB13 in Football Guys' aggregate rankings and QB15 elsewhere, versus Harris's own QB5. He calls it 'insane' given the Rams project as an elite offense again and Stafford is coming off an MVP season; even applying the standard touchdown-regression discount (12 fewer TDs), Stafford still would have led the league in passing touchdowns last year. Harris compares the skepticism to how analysts kept declaring peak-era Aaron Rodgers 'done' right after his MVP seasons in Green Bay. He dismisses the counterargument that a better Rams defense (with Myles Garrett arriving and Aaron Donald reportedly returning) will suppress Stafford's volume, noting last year's Rams defense was already elite and it didn't stop Stafford producing. He does flag real injury risk (two herniated discs the last two seasons) and, in Superflex, recommends pairing a mid-round Stafford pick with another quality QB as insurance rather than avoiding him outright.
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Stafford lands at number ten on Harris's Flag Players list as his only quarterback pick. Harris is unconcerned with waiting on a quarterback but says he will happily take Stafford in the ninth round if he's still without one, and has him ranked QB5. He acknowledges the expected touchdown-rate regression argument but counters that even with ten fewer touchdown passes last year Stafford would still have led the league and finished QB7 -- while he's being drafted as QB15. Harris pushes back on the idea that only rushing quarterbacks provide explosive fantasy seasons, arguing a reigning MVP-caliber passer on a Super Bowl-favorite offense he trusts year to year is just as capable of an explosive season, even if there isn't enough sample size in the data to prove it; he draws the same comparison to Aaron Rodgers being undervalued in 2020-2021 before back-to-back MVPs.
