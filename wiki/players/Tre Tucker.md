# Tre Tucker

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: **f\*\*k it for now, open to feeling it in a week** — after two touchdown catches in the Raiders' 63-point game, Waldman calls him a high-variance play whose normal week is about two catches for 30–50 yards. Style comp: "an aspiring Jaylen Waddle type," used in the Tyreek Hill role — late motion, working over the middle while defenders are drawn to Davante Adams and Jakobi Meyers, getting the wide-open looks. Has the speed and some decent routes, but "he's just not a complete player yet." The practical rule Waldman gave: if you're truly desperate for a WR4 he's playable now, otherwise wait one more week and see whether the volume holds.
- 2023-12-18 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode: cools the role case — snaps were split nearly evenly with Hunter Renfrow (29 to 27), so this was a productive whirlwind rather than a full-time job.
- 2024-07-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-04]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said Tucker was part of a tier of speedy, YAC-heavy rookie receivers last year who had a fatal flaw at the catch point (alongside Tyler Scott), and that per Raiders head coach Antonio Pierce, Tucker has fixed his hands and 'looks like a completely different player' -- making him the favorite to be Las Vegas' No. 3 receiver. Angelo cautioned the role is muddied by the addition of Brock Bowers alongside established target-getters Davante Adams and Jakobi Meyers, questioning whether Pierce's run-heavy, multi-tight-end approach leaves room for a true No. 3 wide receiver, though he wouldn't be surprised if Tucker still starts in three-receiver sets.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]): with [[Michael Gallup]] retired and Jalen Guyton hurt, Tucker looks locked into the nominal WR3 role, but Waldman regards [[Brock Bowers]] as functionally a wide receiver, which makes Tucker the fourth option and his redraft value close to nil. He is a bye-week matchup piece — Tucker hits well against certain predictable defensive looks — and would become a real option if Jakobi Meyers got hurt. Waldman is positive on the player himself: very good athlete, has played well and made a lot of improvements, with the work centered on pass-catching and attack technique, which he has addressed. Worth keeping an eye on for the future.
- 2024-10-03 — According to James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-03]], with [[Matt Harmon]] more skeptical: Tucker is an interesting player — 5-9, 185, 4.27 at his pro day (Koh also cites 4.37 speed) — who everyone expected to be a slot but who has played 75% of his offensive snaps outside for Las Vegas, and the Raiders have drawn up plays specifically to get him the ball, so Koh expects him to absorb a lot of the vacated snaps if [[Davante Adams]] is traded, with only a partial trickle-down in targets. Harmon is cooler: 'this Tre Tucker guy, I don't know', framing him as possibly just field-stretching, sacrificial-X work rather than a real replacement for Adams's coverage-dictating role.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): while pulling third-year receivers for charting, Harmon found Tucker ran more routes than any other year-three receiver last season — 639 — at 5-foot-9, 185 pounds, and took only 23 snaps from the slot. Both hosts react with disbelief, Koh (not tracked) noting the return was just 47 catches for 539 yards on that volume, with the caveat of Las Vegas quarterback play. Harmon's framing is that the Raiders are effectively using the smallest sacrificial X in the league purely to clear space for [[Jakobi Meyers]], [[Jack Bech]] and [[Brock Bowers]] on crossing routes, and that Vegas should still be shopping for a genuine X — he floats [[Gabe Davis]] for the job.
- 2025-06-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-04]]): Harmon does not expect a breakout, saying Tucker does not really bring anything to the outside role the Raiders need, citing 0.78 yards per route run on out-wide looks last season. He argues that even with [[Jakobi Meyers]], [[Jack Bech]] and [[Brock Bowers]] able to move around under Chip Kelly's stated 'remain amorphous' plan, the team still lacks the outside speed element, and points to rookie [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]] rather than Tucker as the candidate to supply it.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether Tucker is a viable dynasty dart, Waldman splits it — 'fuck him if you're looking for a bargain starter, feel him if you're looking for a bye-week play.' He is a late-round name in deeper leagues only, and Waldman rates him above [[Jack Bech]] on 2025 production but not by much. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) adds Tucker to the list of players, alongside [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], who noticeably added mass this offseason.
- 2025-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-25]]): Harmon did not even include Tucker in the Raiders pass-catching group and does not see him playing much at all in 2025 after the additions of [[Jack Bech]] and [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]]. Koh (not tracked) says he will eat a pen if Bech does not out-play Tucker. The context both find funny is that Tucker ran 639 routes in 2024, fifth among all NFL wide receivers behind only [[Jerry Jeudy]], [[Ja'Marr Chase]], [[D.J. Moore]] and [[Garrett Wilson]] — and turned that volume into 47 receptions. At 5-9, 185 he was miscast as the team's stretch X, the role Harmon now expects Thornton to take.
- 2025-08-22 — According to [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]]: host James Koh (not a tracked expert) said Las Vegas desperately needed receiver help last year and that he does not think Tucker is a starting-level NFL wide receiver. Guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not tracked) did not rebut the talent point but explained the role logic — Tucker can at least run 4.2-4.3 and take the lid off a defense, and that vertical function, shared with [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]], is precisely why both are starting outside while the interior is crowded with slot types.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-22]]): 'feel it' — Tucker is for real and a good add. Waldman credited Dan Hatman of the Scouting Academy as a long-time believer, and said Tucker is a speedster, a little undersized, but has been making plays over the middle and clearly has rapport with [[Geno Smith]], which has grown steadily since Week 1. His framework: sometimes a receiver simply needs to build that rapport, and Tucker stuck around through years of young quarterbacks who reasonably favored veterans like [[Davante Adams]] and [[Jakobi Meyers]]. Camp drumbeats about his development are now coming to fruition. Bob Harris (not tracked) read back his own May note that Tucker has the elite speed to stretch the field and score quickly and has been the only Raider able to create problems downfield for two years, and added he is a play-every-week option but not yet a start-every-week one.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-23]]): Tucker exploded for three touchdown catches and over 100 yards against Washington, a 40-point fantasy day, prompting Harmon to chart him for the first time in his NFL career. The verdict is mixed: he is genuinely fast, was solid against zone coverage and impressive on digs and crossers over the middle where he had wide-open windows, but he 'definitely cannot beat press coverage' — 24.2% press rate in the game with a 37.5% success rate — and does not beat man well. Harmon added context that Washington cannot cover on the back end, and that early in the game Tucker was not getting open consistently; the splash plays came while trailing. He is not arguing Tucker should come off the field, only that the profile is volatile.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): 'it would turn out to be the Tre Tucker game' — eight catches for 145 yards and three touchdowns, and Harris stressed 'not all of it in garbage time.' Three possession catches plus the game-tying score in the second quarter, then a crosser touchdown at 34-10 to open the fourth (which Harris conceded you could argue is garbage time) and a bomb with four minutes left. 'It's more than anybody else was doing.' He is 'a fast little dude' Harris will mention on waivers but only for deeper leagues; both he and guest SCOTT FISH (Fantasy Cares, NOT tracked) left him off their top fives, Fish as an honorable mention, with Harris's rationale that there are 700 receivers and you cannot expect consistent contribution from any of them — 'you can't get consistent contribution from [[Justin Jefferson]], you think you're going to get it from Tre Tucker?'
- 2025-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-25]]): Harmon charted Tucker for the first time and admits he assumed Tucker would lose his job after Las Vegas added [[Jack Bech]] and [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]] alongside [[Jakobi Meyers]] — instead he is a true full-time player at 95% of the routes. Context for the skepticism: as a second-year player Tucker ran 639 routes, most of any receiver in his class, yet averaged just 0.84 yards per route run on ~12.5 air yards per target, so it was not a pure deep-decoy role. Harmon's Week 3 chart versus Washington: 85.7% of snaps outside and 54% off the line of scrimmage, so a flanker rather than an X; poor versus press at a 37.5% success rate against a Commanders corner group he does not rate; but a workable 77.3% versus zone, short of the 80% threshold he likes. Route diet was deeper in-breakers — 18.2% posts, 15.2% digs, 24.2% slants — with the big plays coming on crossers versus zone. He is 'fast af' and [[Geno Smith]] compared him to Santana Moss this offseason; Harmon's verdict is a useful speed-based role player, a better zone beater than man beater, and 'perfectly middle of the league.' Koh (not a tracked expert) was surprised at the usage, saying last year's Tucker tape was screens, drags and clear-outs, so slants, digs and deep overs is a genuine change.
- 2025-10-16 — According to guest Dave Kluge of FootballGuys.com (not a tracked expert), with [[Chris Harris]] largely agreeing ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-16 (Week 7 Flex Decisions)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: both hosts say Tre Tucker's usage has genuinely changed over the last few weeks -- no longer a purely boundary/go-route deep threat ('you get one catch for 80 yards and a touchdown, or nothing'), he's now running crossing routes and slants and creating after the catch, which Kluge compared to Doug Baldwin's or Tyler Lockett's early-career evolution. Geno Smith has reportedly comped him to Santana Moss and is using him as a replacement for [[Jakobi Meyers]]' possession role. Kluge ranked him WR34 (PPR/standard); Harris had him at WR32, calling him an 'emergency, break-glass' streaming option (48% rostered in Yahoo) who could push into weekly top-30 value if the Jets trade Meyers, whom both hosts still view as the more talented player who'd thrive with a better quarterback than Justin Fields.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-06]]): Tucker has established himself as an every-down player and is locked into the X receiver spot largely because [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]] is 'pretty much unplayable' right now. Harmon's charting from the Jacksonville game: 85% of snaps out wide, 14% slot, a route on 100% of dropbacks — team leader in route participation. The rationale is speed: Tucker can stretch the defense and win against man coverage when he gets over the top, and Harmon argues the Raiders only need one such burner, which frees them to pair [[Brock Bowers]] and [[Jack Bech]] in condensed looks. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) agrees Tucker 'has to be on the football field' and will play near 100% of snaps. Both frame him as a supporting character to Bowers, who Harmon calls the straw that stirs the drink.
- 2026-02-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with Clint Kubiak reportedly taking over as Raiders head coach, Harmon said [[Tre Tucker]], who 'obviously flashed as a deep threat last year,' could continue playing that vertical role in the new offense, alongside [[Jack Bech]] potentially working underneath as a slot/flanker piece. Harmon still framed receiver as the Raiders' biggest remaining need on offense even with both in the mix.
- 2026-03-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-10]]): [[Tre Tucker]] led the Raiders in receiving in 2025 with 92 targets, 57 catches for 696 yards, 5 TDs and a 62% catch rate, and played a league-high 94.5% of offensive snaps at wide receiver (ahead of Ja'Marr Chase's 94.3% and Justin Jefferson's 94.1%). Harmon and co-host James Koh (untracked) agreed the volume overstates his value -- Koh said Tucker 'should not even be relatively in the cards' as a team's leading receiver, and Harmon argued he's best deployed as a low-usage, high-yield deep-speed decoy in the mold of Rashid Shaheed's role with the 2025 Seahawks after his trade-deadline arrival, citing Tucker's speed and, per Harmon's in-season tracking, his ability to win vs. zone coverage in the intermediate area. Both view Tucker's heavy target share as a symptom of 2025's dysfunctional Raiders receiver room rather than a role he should keep going forward.
- 2026-03-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-17]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon noted [[Tre Tucker]] led all wide receivers in snap percentage last season for Las Vegas, which he views as misuse of the player -- he'd rather see Tucker sprinkled in as a deep threat than deployed on nearly every snap.
- 2026-07-24 — According to Chris Harris and Andy Behrens (untracked co-host) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Behrens calls Tucker a 'weird player' who looks sloppy and double-catches some balls, but says he's flashed real traits and big-play ability that make him someone he could see 'leveling up' if he gets buzzy in camp -- more interesting to Behrens right now than fellow Raiders receiver [[Jalen Nailor]]. Harris also has Tucker inside his top-80 season profiles.
- 2026-08-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-03]]): his #3 training-camp story is the entire Raiders receiving corps behind Brock Bowers, since presumptively bad offenses (Raiders, Dolphins, Jets, Browns) are where Harris likes to hunt sleepers the market writes off entirely - he cites last year's Colts and Patriots as reminders that 'we're just wrong all the time about teams being hopeless.' Among Tucker, [[Jalen Nailor]] and Jack Bech, Harris currently ranks Tucker the highest of the three, all outside his top 60 and within 10 spots of one another, with none inside his top 40 receivers. He notes Clint Kubiak's history of hyper-targeting one receiver (Seattle, New Orleans, Minnesota) but expects that target hog to be Bowers, not any of the wideouts. Harris gives Tucker a slight edge for his speed, though admits 'I haven't ever really seen him run routes.'
- 2026-08-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-25]]): Tucker's RP profile surprised Harmon with its balance -- he ran every route except the flat at or above NFL-average participation, with six routes above 10% usage, not the screen/nine-route-only role Harmon expected. His best routes are the big breaking ones off play action: 78% success on the post, 77% on the dig (both on real volume), fitting Clint Kubiak's downfield, play-action-heavy scheme -- Harmon draws a conceptual (not talent-level) comp to how Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Tory Horton produced downfield in Kubiak's Seattle offense. Per Yahoo colleague Nate Tice's Next Gen Stats tracking data, Tucker is 'legitimately one of if not the fastest players in the NFL' by top-speed thresholds, backing his 64% success rate on the nine route. He's weaker vs. man/press coverage (19th/20th percentile) and is a near-exclusive outside Z (81% of snaps off-ball outside), but graded 44th percentile vs. zone. Harmon prefers him over [[Jalen Nailor]] as the outside answer and expects him to lead Raiders WRs in snaps -- 896 last year, 9th-most among all WRs -- projecting him as an ideal 'number three,' high-yield/low-target player if Las Vegas adds a true WR1 in next year's draft; he says Best Ball drafters should move Tucker up boards given the likely snap share.
- 2026-08-31 — According to Scott Pianowski (guest, not tracked; [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-31]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Pianowski's number two is Tucker, betting that an improved Raiders passing infrastructure under Clint Kubiak (with Geno Smith gone) makes Tucker the best available receiver behind Brock Bowers -- with the caveat that Bowers will hoover targets and a healthy Ashton Jeanty will also catch passes. He notes live ADP has now flipped so Tucker goes ahead of Jalen Nailor, calling that a correction after Nailor was overvalued for reasons neither host could identify from his Minnesota tape. Pianowski projects a real range of outcomes up to roughly 950 yards and 7-8 touchdowns, framing Tucker as a bench stash with a short leash but real touchdown-driven weekly upside -- the kind of player who separates WR17 from WR42 on a couple of long scores alone.
- 2026-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Scott Fish (Scott Fish, not a tracked expert -- Fantasy Cares/Scott Fishbowl) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-09-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Both hosts independently landed on [[Tre Tucker]] as their #4 Week 1 waiver add (27% rostered), and Harris noted with surprise they'd picked the exact same player in the exact same slot. Fish argued Tucker has 'clearly won the number one role' for a Raiders offense he expects to trail often and throw a lot, since a returning receiving-corps teammate has reportedly disappointed. Harris agreed Tucker was the only returning Raiders receiver who was 'any good at all' last year even under [[Geno Smith]], and floated that [[Kirk Cousins]] plus a retooled offensive line could make the Raiders better than expected, though he stopped short of calling Tucker a traditional true WR1.
