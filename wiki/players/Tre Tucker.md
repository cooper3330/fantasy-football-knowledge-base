# Tre Tucker

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: **f\*\*k it for now, open to feeling it in a week** — after two touchdown catches in the Raiders' 63-point game, Waldman calls him a high-variance play whose normal week is about two catches for 30–50 yards. Style comp: "an aspiring Jaylen Waddle type," used in the Tyreek Hill role — late motion, working over the middle while defenders are drawn to Davante Adams and Jakobi Meyers, getting the wide-open looks. Has the speed and some decent routes, but "he's just not a complete player yet." The practical rule Waldman gave: if you're truly desperate for a WR4 he's playable now, otherwise wait one more week and see whether the volume holds.
- 2023-12-18 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode: cools the role case — snaps were split nearly evenly with Hunter Renfrow (29 to 27), so this was a productive whirlwind rather than a full-time job.
- 2024-07-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-04]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said Tucker was part of a tier of speedy, YAC-heavy rookie receivers last year who had a fatal flaw at the catch point (alongside Tyler Scott), and that per Raiders head coach Antonio Pierce, Tucker has fixed his hands and 'looks like a completely different player' -- making him the favorite to be Las Vegas' No. 3 receiver. Angelo cautioned the role is muddied by the addition of Brock Bowers alongside established target-getters Davante Adams and Jakobi Meyers, questioning whether Pierce's run-heavy, multi-tight-end approach leaves room for a true No. 3 wide receiver, though he wouldn't be surprised if Tucker still starts in three-receiver sets.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]): with [[Michael Gallup]] retired and Jalen Guyton hurt, Tucker looks locked into the nominal WR3 role, but Waldman regards [[Brock Bowers]] as functionally a wide receiver, which makes Tucker the fourth option and his redraft value close to nil. He is a bye-week matchup piece — Tucker hits well against certain predictable defensive looks — and would become a real option if Jakobi Meyers got hurt. Waldman is positive on the player himself: very good athlete, has played well and made a lot of improvements, with the work centered on pass-catching and attack technique, which he has addressed. Worth keeping an eye on for the future.
- 2024-10-03 — According to James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-03]], with [[Matt Harmon]] more skeptical: Tucker is an interesting player — 5-9, 185, 4.27 at his pro day (Koh also cites 4.37 speed) — who everyone expected to be a slot but who has played 75% of his offensive snaps outside for Las Vegas, and the Raiders have drawn up plays specifically to get him the ball, so Koh expects him to absorb a lot of the vacated snaps if [[Davante Adams]] is traded, with only a partial trickle-down in targets. Harmon is cooler: 'this Tre Tucker guy, I don't know', framing him as possibly just field-stretching, sacrificial-X work rather than a real replacement for Adams's coverage-dictating role.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): while pulling third-year receivers for charting, Harmon found Tucker ran more routes than any other year-three receiver last season — 639 — at 5-foot-9, 185 pounds, and took only 23 snaps from the slot. Both hosts react with disbelief, Koh (not tracked) noting the return was just 47 catches for 539 yards on that volume, with the caveat of Las Vegas quarterback play. Harmon's framing is that the Raiders are effectively using the smallest sacrificial X in the league purely to clear space for [[Jakobi Meyers]], [[Jack Bech]] and [[Brock Bowers]] on crossing routes, and that Vegas should still be shopping for a genuine X — he floats [[Gabe Davis]] for the job.
- 2025-06-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-04]]): Harmon does not expect a breakout, saying Tucker does not really bring anything to the outside role the Raiders need, citing 0.78 yards per route run on out-wide looks last season. He argues that even with [[Jakobi Meyers]], [[Jack Bech]] and [[Brock Bowers]] able to move around under Chip Kelly's stated 'remain amorphous' plan, the team still lacks the outside speed element, and points to rookie [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]] rather than Tucker as the candidate to supply it.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether Tucker is a viable dynasty dart, Waldman splits it — 'fuck him if you're looking for a bargain starter, feel him if you're looking for a bye-week play.' He is a late-round name in deeper leagues only, and Waldman rates him above [[Jack Bech]] on 2025 production but not by much. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) adds Tucker to the list of players, alongside [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], who noticeably added mass this offseason.
- 2025-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-25]]): Harmon did not even include Tucker in the Raiders pass-catching group and does not see him playing much at all in 2025 after the additions of [[Jack Bech]] and [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]]. Koh (not tracked) says he will eat a pen if Bech does not out-play Tucker. The context both find funny is that Tucker ran 639 routes in 2024, fifth among all NFL wide receivers behind only [[Jerry Jeudy]], [[Ja'Marr Chase]], [[D.J. Moore]] and [[Garrett Wilson]] — and turned that volume into 47 receptions. At 5-9, 185 he was miscast as the team's stretch X, the role Harmon now expects Thornton to take.
- 2025-08-22 — According to [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]]: host James Koh (not a tracked expert) said Las Vegas desperately needed receiver help last year and that he does not think Tucker is a starting-level NFL wide receiver. Guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not tracked) did not rebut the talent point but explained the role logic — Tucker can at least run 4.2-4.3 and take the lid off a defense, and that vertical function, shared with [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]], is precisely why both are starting outside while the interior is crowded with slot types.
