# Chase Brown

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): "I love Chase Brown" — prefers stashing him over [[Tank Bigsby]] on a dynasty bench, self-described as "a sucker for developmental types" even though he acknowledges that archetype has a low hit rate overall.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: pairs him with new addition [[Zach Moss]] as Cincinnati's post-[[Joe Mixon]] backfield, with Brown specifically bringing "the speed element" the team wanted more of.
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: In a feel-it-or-f-it pairing, Waldman says Brown (RB36 ADP) has more raw upside than [[Zach Moss]] (RB26) but still picks Moss, wanting to see Brown 'figure out which direction to run' more consistently before buying fully into the beat-writer hype building around him in Cincinnati camp.
- 2024-07-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-04]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman relayed trainer Drew Lieberman's account (via The Athletic) that Brown, despite having little real route-running background entering training with Lieberman alongside [[Andrei Iosivas]], was one of the fastest learners Lieberman has worked with and 'now looks like a receiver' -- a positive note for Brown's pass-catching development in Cincinnati's backfield.
- 2024-07-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]): Chase Brown has been working with receiving-backs trainer Drew Lieberman (who also trains [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and Andrei Iosivas), and Waldman relayed that the Bengals have been 'really excited about how he looks running routes' this offseason -- a potential receiving-role boost. JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) grouped Brown with Marshawn Lloyd as backs he doesn't project for a season-long bell-cow role but who carry real weekly flex/best-ball upside if the starter (Zack Moss) is hurt. [Best Ball]
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]): Brown has better fantasy upside than [[Zach Moss]] right now — Waldman calls the two roughly even in talent but leans Brown because of where each can be drafted. The catalyst is Brown's offseason with WR coach Drew Lieberman, after which the Bengals are 'super excited' about him as a passing-game asset out of the backfield who could occasionally align outside; he also has the big-play speed Moss lacks, at 22.05 mph last year, the second-fastest recorded speed in the NFL per Next Gen Stats. Waldman cites Paul Dehner Jr. saying Brown would be a bigger part of the offense regardless of the Moss signing. Bob Harris (not tracked) calls the price differential the real edge — Moss going about two rounds earlier — and says he has bought Brown shares in the last few days on the first-team reps.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): asked why Brown is suddenly the goal-line back and whether he takes the job from [[Zach Moss]], Waldman said 'I think that's going to happen' and that it was the expectation from the start, on speed and receiving ability. He put a ceiling on it though: not a dominant 20-touch back, but roughly 12 to 16 touches to Moss's 8 to 12 in what he called the ideal-format outcome. He speculated that absences over the course of the season and within games may have forced changes to Cincinnati's offensive mindset.
- 2024-11-22 — According to Adam Harstad, untracked co-host ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]): Brown is Harstad's model case for the backup-running-back waiver archetype and he said Brown could plausibly be a top-12 back the rest of the way. He picked him up off waivers in a couple of leagues — not on profile, which he called unremarkable, but because the Bengals were openly talking up Brown and making roster moves consistent with him being in their future even after bringing in veteran competition. Harstad's framing: guys on waivers are there because we already have plenty of reasons to think they suck; what you're hunting for is a reason to think you're wrong, and organizational support is the best such reason at running back.
- 2024-12-12 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-12]]) [Dynasty]: Brown is a 'back-end RB1' and can hold that as long as Zac Taylor and [[Joe Burrow]] are there — he is now an every-down back, more proficient in pass protection than as a rookie, a good pass catcher out of the backfield, and supplies the explosive element the offense lacked with [[Zach Moss]] and a declining [[Joe Mixon]]. Angelo notes Brown has been on roughly 98% of snaps since Moss went down and expects that to regress to about a 75-25 split next year with a signing or draft pick; he'd rather Brown had a complement taking ~30% of touches. Chief flaw: tempo — 'a little Phillip Lindsay in a good and a bad way', a roadrunner who doesn't play behind his pads enough. He accepts Waldman's [[Tony Pollard]] comp (Pollard the better receiver, Brown the better interior runner, both best around a 65-70% role) and adds a [[Chuba Hubbard]] comp.
- 2025-01-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-27]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'fuck it' on Brown repeating RB1 production, which prompted Bob Harris (not tracked) to call him a hater. Waldman likes Brown but pegs him closer to RB15–20, in the same band as [[Bucky Irving]], for two reasons: he expects Cincinnati to add another back to pair with him even if Brown leads, and he expects a healthier, more potent passing game — receivers a year better, the [[Jermaine Burton]] experiment over, [[Tee Higgins]] or a replacement — that keeps Brown from the touch volume required to crack the top 10–12. Both note his ascent came once [[Zach Moss]] was hurt, and Harris reported that beat writers said Brown's explosiveness was why the team was comfortable moving on from [[Joe Mixon]]. Waldman's warning: anchor him again with Moss and you have recreated the Mixon split, which is why people may overdraft him off the stretch run.
- 2025-07-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-28]]): whether Brown is a 'true feature back' depends entirely on the definition. If feature back means top-12 fantasy production as a run/pass and red zone threat — the framing Waldman credits to Jason Wood's Footballguys spotlight — then yes. If it means the vast majority of touches and a 300–350 touch workload, then no, he is not quite at that level. Bob Harris (untracked) pushes back with usage: from Week 9 on, without [[Zach Moss]], Brown took 100 percent of the running back touches in the first game (27 carries, 120 yards, five catches, 37 yards) and was on the field at least 80 percent of the time, up to 98.5 percent in Week 15 against Cleveland, arguing that 80-plus percent of team touches executed capably is what a feature back is. Waldman's counter is two words — Trayveon Williams, whom he does not consider an NFL-caliber back — i.e. the usage reflected a bare depth chart. Waldman notes [[Tahj Brooks]] has looked good in his first camp days but is 'not Chase Brown yet'.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Brown will not be used as a weapon back — with the best receiver on the planet and [[Tee Higgins]] as 'a great Robin to the Batman,' Cincinnati has no reason to detach him, and Angelo does not think his route tree is developed enough yet for intermediate work, so he projects screen-game outlet usage. More importantly, both hosts rejected the idea that the late-2024 touch spike is the baseline: Angelo called him a good balanced 1A rather than a 65-70% touch-share back, and expects [[Tahj Brooks]] to take low-red-zone and some third-down work as the weather turns. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed and noted his own tier-three ranking drew a friendly objection from scout Dan Hatman of the Scouting Academy, who thought Brown was excellent — Waldman conceded Brown was excellent at what he was asked to do but stayed on volume-skepticism grounds [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): Harris and guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) both rate Brown a 3 out of 10 with no disagreement — a little guy taking a big workload again, with 'let's see it again' as the only real caveat. Bell's case is offense-driven: the Bengals are one of the few offenses he will elevate a player for, and a Bengals running back who does not leave the field is a player he wants. Harris notes Brown will leave the field sometimes, as he did early last year before Cincinnati ran out of bodies.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): the good news in a low-volume Bengals offense (24 minutes of possession to Cleveland's 36, [[Joe Burrow]] at 113 pass yards) is that Brown played 36 snaps and the job 'definitively belonged to Brown' for the week. He did a lot of the work on the opening drive and cashed the goal-line touchdown. [[Samaje Perine]] had a couple of early drive-extending catches but Harris calls him not relevant, and [[Tahj Brooks]] played only special teams.
- 2025-09-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-26]]): Brown is his number two but strictly as a value scoop, not a bowl-them-over buy like [[Bucky Irving]]. Harris says he does have questions about Brown, and that Fantasy Pros having him RB18 yet 46th overall is itself an overreaction — in this environment RB18 should not be the 46th player overall, and whoever made those ranks is not very good at this. The play is to talk up how terrible everything looked against a good Vikings defense and how bad a Monday night road trip to Denver could be, and buy only if it is cheap: he would give [[Rome Odunze]] straight up. Josh would too, adding that people are completely turned off by the Cincinnati quarterback situation and that he would rather have the running back. Josh's ponies-for-a-horse version, [[DeAndre Swift]] plus [[Chris Olave]], both agree probably needs the receiver bumped up because Brown owners spent a mid-second on him.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): Brown is in the same don't-panic-sell bucket as [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Tee Higgins]] — Harris says he will find the end zone eventually 'almost just through the law of averages', even though the Bengals offense under [[Jake Browning]] looked as bad as you can look in today's NFL against Denver. He acknowledges the situation has burned Brown's managers through no fault of their own — the fault is [[Joe Burrow]]'s big toe — and that an 0-4 team may have to trade for someone usable rather than wait. But he draws the line at giving Brown away for nothing.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Brown is the hardest kind of start/sit — Harris ranks him RB24 and guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not a tracked expert) RB19, and both said they still play him while refusing to be angry at anyone who benches him. Bell's case against: touches of 14 and 13 the last two weeks, nothing close to a startable line through four games outside the Week 1 touchdown against Cleveland, and a [[Jake Browning]] offense that did not cross midfield in Minnesota — you are effectively committing to 4.8 points barring garbage time. Harris's case for: Brown is a genuinely good football player stuck in hell, his last two games came against Minnesota and Denver, two of the three elite defenses in the league, and Detroit is good but not that good, so this week may be less hellish. Both noted his ADP rose to the middle of the second round; Bell compared the trap to [[Ladd McConkey]] — players you built a roster around and now cannot bench or start.
- 2025-10-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-03]]): Harris and guest Josh Van Blank (NOT a tracked expert) frame Brown as a buy-low, with the caveat that neither would pay [[George Pickens]] for him. The market has soured to treating him as a fringe RB2 after the Bengals' offense looked dreadful on Monday Night Football, but Harris argues those were bad spots — road games against Minnesota's defense and then Denver in a get-right game — and that opinion swings too hard on the last thing you watched: 'I don't think it's as bad as this, but I don't think it's as good' either. Van Blank's point is that there will be games against bad teams where Brown touches it 25 times and goes 80 and 50 with a touchdown, and there are simply so few backs like that. He would happily take Brown for [[Jaylen Waddle]]; Harris has Brown ahead of Waddle already and notes FantasyPros is the reverse. [[Tee Higgins]] is flagged as the other Bengals buy you'd love to acquire without having to start immediately.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): the seven catches in Brown's box score are a mirage — 'this was the day of emergency oh-shoot middle screens.' He finished with 48 scrimmage yards on 15 touches, which Harris calls 'exceedingly rough' but explicitly 'nothing to do with him.' The hope is that against worse defenses and worse teams there will be 'a little more meat on the bone,' and that [[Jake Browning]] doesn't consistently suck this hard.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): Waldman strongly agreed with Bob Harris (not tracked) that Brown's fantasy season continues after a slow start because Cincinnati's second-half schedule lightens up. Brown looks good and will get receiving-game touches regardless; the open question is whether the Bengals stay in games enough to give him volume. With [[Samaje Perine]] beaten up, Waldman allowed it could also become 'Tahj Brooks time' for a share of the work.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): the feared workload drain never materialized because Samaje Perine (referred to on the show as the returner) got hurt on a second-quarter kickoff return and never came back. Brown's tape was ugly in spots — a horrible drop on third-and-six on the second drive in Bears territory and a stuff from the one — but in the second half he 'caught approximately one million check-down screens' to salvage the day. Harris notes the go-ahead touchdown went to [[Andrei Iosivas]] rather than any started receiver.
- 2025-11-07 — According to guest Jim McCormick (not a tracked expert) on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-07]] [Redraft (Standard)]: Brown is pivotal because [[Samaje Perine]]'s high ankle sprain hands him back the passing-down role that makes him survivable — without it he is a home-run big-play back who is 'an expensive [[Jerome Ford]]' in the weeks he does not score. With [[Joe Flacco]] throwing constantly and the Bengals always chasing, Brown drew roughly 14 targets in a game recently; if he settles into catching four or five a game, McCormick argues, his floor rises materially. His caveats: Brown did not help himself by blatantly calling out the rest of the team afterward, and it all depends on the Flacco run continuing. [[Chris Harris]] agreed enough to move Brown up ten spots in his rest-of-season ranks on Tuesday, from RB/overall 38 to 28.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-13 (Paper Champions and All-Timers Across Eras)]]) [Dynasty]: cited by untracked co-host Adam Harstad as a live example of paper value that has not converted. Harstad notes that Dan Hendry's trade values — which Harstad says he uses constantly and rates highly — were very high on Brown coming into the 2025 season, and that the market has cooled on him since; a manager who acquired Brown to anchor a championship run is not thrilled at this point in the year. Harstad explicitly refuses to close the book, allowing that Brown could still finish as the number one fantasy running back down the stretch and that you cannot grade a value call until it has fully run its course. Waldman does not offer a competing evaluation.
