# Matthew Golden

## Expert Takes

- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]): Golden is the safest player in the 2025 receiver class and Angelo's favorite route runner in it — good at all three levels, with a [[Chris Olave]] archetype comp in the sense that a healthy Olave could start for essentially any NFL team and produce, though Golden is a little more abrupt than Olave. He praises the bevy of releases, the ability to keep defensive backs guessing by throwing a lot at them, and the quality of his stem. [[Matt Waldman]] independently puts Golden in his top tier of route runners in the class and agrees he is on the short list, naming him alongside [[Travis Hunter]] and [[Luther Burden III]] as the players in the top-12-of-2024 conversation. Both frame him as the pure-value pick over [[Tetairoa McMillan]].
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman has Golden as probably a top-five receiver on his board and one of the safer ones in terms of skills that translate to the NFL, preferring him to teammate [[Isaiah Bond]]. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) agrees and explains why the model likes him: Golden played with [[Tank Dell]] as a Houston freshman, transferred to Texas, and had to beat out Bond and Helm for targets, so his adjusted production is credited for target competition; he did very well with limited opportunities, and the model's program-quality component rewards being recruited to Alabama-tier and title-contending programs. McFarland notes this 'did great with his chances despite modest raw production' profile is what historically caught [[George Pickens]] and [[Terry McLaurin]], two hits the market missed.
- 2025-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-07]]): Harmon pushes back hard on Golden's post-combine rise. He admits he 'didn't necessarily see 4.29 on Matthew Golden's tape and that's what happened', and that the 40 time alone has pushed him toward being the first receiver drafted — but says flatly, 'Golden's not the top receiver in this class, not even close.' He still grades him as a first-round player and 'a nice player', so the objection is to the ranking, not the prospect. Harmon uses him as the case study for how a single testing number reshuffles a board, immediately alongside skepticism about [[Emeka Egbuka]]'s reported pro-day 4.3.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman likes Golden, whose 4.29 forty drew the combine attention, and has him ranked ahead of Texas teammate [[Isaiah Bond]] — but he is only 'kind of' feeling the claim that Golden will make everyone forget Bond, because Bond is nipping at his heels in his rankings and has a path to being better. His distinction is that Golden's size gives him more upside as a blocker, while Bond is the better route runner right now and is very good after the catch. Waldman also ranks Golden behind [[Luther Burden III]], whom he calls the class WR1 despite the noise around Golden and [[Tetairoa McMillan]]. Bob Harris (untracked) is openly a 40-time partisan and admits the bias.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): the Texas receiver attacks the ball at its earliest window when it arrives at the numbers, can turn behind the break path and catch with overhand position away from his frame, and transitions downhill obeying the ball in one smooth motion. Waldman singles out his tracking — good at following the ball over one shoulder and rotating his body to the opposite side as it arrives — and credits good hand-eye coordination. In a class where Waldman flagged clap attacks and hand position as the dominant flaw across dozens of prospects, Golden's capsule is pure praise at the catch point. See [[Catch Technique and Ball Tracking]].
- 2025-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-26]]) [Dynasty]: Golden (5'11″, 191 lbs, 4.29 forty; 58-987-9 final year at Texas) is a 'very useful NFL player' whose best trait is the contested-catch game — 83.3% contested catch rate on 22.6% of sample targets — despite his size, with a 7.4% drop rate Harmon attributes to focus drops on layups. He clears deep and intermediate route averages (80.6% on digs) but only 68.5% vs man and sub-70% vs press, a weakness he says a team must scheme around. Comps: rookie-year [[Jordan Addison]] on route profile, and vs-man rates matching [[Tank Dell]] (68.7%) and [[Xavier Worthy]] (68.4%) — better ball tracking than Worthy, less separation than Dell. Harmon questions the number-one ceiling and grades him late-first/early-second; ideal fits are the Rams' complementary role or Houston opposite [[Nico Collins]].
- 2025-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-16]]): Golden took over as Texas' true number one over [[Isaiah Bond]], who was supposed to hold that role, and was good enough by season's end to be a first-round pick. Harmon flags the fantasy-versus-scouting disconnect on him: on full-career production and efficiency he would be one of the least productive round-one receivers ever, but NFL teams pay for tactical value, and Golden's speed clears space for teammates on in-breakers and sideline work. That combination is what turns a late-one/early-two receiver grade into a potential top-15 pick.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): Golden is his pick, alongside [[Jalen Milroe]], to go HIGHER than people anticipate. He names Golden and [[Tetairoa McMillan]] as the two obvious targets if Dallas uses pick 12 on a receiver, arguing the Cowboys need a secondary playmaker to take pressure off [[CeeDee Lamb]] — [[Brandin Cooks]] is aging and likely gone, [[Jake Ferguson]] is fine, and [[KaVontae Turpin]] plus Lamb will not score the roughly 35 points a game Dallas needs to keep pace with Philadelphia, Washington and Detroit in the NFC even with [[Dak Prescott]] fully healthy.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-04-28]]): Golden at 23 lands appropriately as Harmon's third receiver behind [[Tetairoa McMillan]] and [[Emeka Egbuka]] ([[Travis Hunter]] aside), better value than the rumors of him going WR1. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) does not get the fit: 5'11', 191, ran 4.29, but by Reception Perception he is not a good man-beater and is average at best versus press, while Green Bay's actual need is an X who wins against man and press, plus red-zone size the pick doesn't provide. Harmon counters that an alpha is easier named than found at 23 with a team that never trades up, and that what the flat room lacked was verticality and in-breakers: Golden's best routes run out–corner–nine–post–dig, and dig and post are his two best. Harmon had wanted him in a McVay-style offense full of in-breakers, and Matt LaFleur comes off that tree. He is basically replacing [[Christian Watson]] as the lid-lifter, and is a good contested-catch receiver in a room without one. Both note the pick is odd for Green Bay: first first-round receiver since 2002 and under their usual 195-pound threshold.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman believes Green Bay is ready to move on from the [[Christian Watson]] era and probably the [[Romeo Doubs]] era too, which makes Golden a future starter who could be starting right now because there is comparatively little to refine in his route running relative to most rookies. He stopped short of calling him a WR1, placing him in the [[Jordan Addison]] and [[DeVonta Smith]] camp — players who in the right circumstance could produce as a WR1, but who are better deployed as a dynamic WR2 alongside a bigger alpha. Waldman's bottom line is that what matters is Green Bay drafted him to be one of their top two starters of the future, and that is good enough. He also mused about who in that room teaches Golden to refine routes the way [[Adam Thielen]] did for [[Stefon Diggs]].
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: a caution on the profile — Golden gives Green Bay some of the explosiveness it lacked without [[Christian Watson]], but Angelo says he 'isn't what people think he is', reading him as a horizontal player more than a vertical one. His comp is [[Brandin Cooks]]: a good player for a long while, but not the centerpiece of an offense. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees with the Cooks framing. Both hosts preferred [[Savion Williams]] as the Packers rookie to target at cost. Waldman took Golden at the equivalent of pick 12 in a 32-team rookie draft.
- 2025-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-20]]) [Dynasty]: guest JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) said he was iffy on Golden as a prospect analytically but loves the Green Bay landing spot — with [[Christian Watson]] coming back from a knee injury, Golden should take the field-stretching role immediately, and both Watson and [[Romeo Doubs]] are in their final rookie-deal year, opening 2026 opportunity. He thinks [[Jayden Reed]] has been typecast as a slot-only player with limited development ahead, and rates Golden as a better prospect than Watson was, capable of more than the vertical role. Waldman agreed, praising Golden's work on horizontal stretches and his ability to threaten both vertically and horizontally in a way Watson cannot, and said he drafted Golden alongside [[Kaleb Johnson]]. Waldman's caution is the room's cloudiness — he projects Reed to lead at around 800 yards with Doubs and Golden next, then a mosh pit including [[Savion Williams]], [[Dontayvion Wicks]], [[Luke Musgrave]] and [[Tucker Kraft]] — but says long term Golden is the pick.
- 2025-06-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-27]]): Waldman's pre-draft comp for Golden was [[Jayden Reed]] himself, with a Tim Brown upside case and a Lee Evans/[[Curtis Samuel]] downside — a player usable at all three receiver spots in isolated spots but potentially a master of none. He expects Golden to start immediately for about 600-650 yards as things stand, with Golden and Reed holding the most room to grow their yardage share depending on how the room sorts out. [[Matt Harmon]] thought at the draft that Golden would win in many of the ways [[Christian Watson]] does for Green Bay while being a more consistent contested catcher, though he notes Golden has drop issues of his own, in the same pattern as Reed last season: dropping the simplest targets while adjusting and catching the hardest ones.
- 2025-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-11]]): Harmon compares Golden's hands profile to [[Jayden Reed]]'s — a receiver who will make great contested catches but also let a layup route clank off his hands, with real drop issues early in his college career, which matters on a Green Bay roster that had the second highest drop rate in the NFL. Projecting the depth chart, Harmon expects Golden to play outside, most likely at flanker, and says his own preference would be Golden taking some X snaps in two-receiver sets with Reed at flanker, while admitting that will never happen. Koh notes the widespread expectation that Golden is an every-down player but is skeptical because Matt LaFleur does not generally deploy receivers that way. Both frame Golden's arrival as further squeezing [[Dontayvion Wicks]].
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Golden has had a nice camp by all reports, but Waldman discounted it because most of it is vertical routes and success in the vertical game, which is exactly what you would expect from him. He explicitly recalled [[Romeo Doubs]] receiving the same camp praise earlier and for longer without ever producing a starter-worthy season. Waldman said he likes Golden but suspects he may be more of a sacrificial X in Green Bay's structure, with [[Jayden Reed]] the receiver whose game actually needs unboxing [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Golden's Reception Perception prospect profile is 'more up than down' but not a true number one profile — 82.5% success rate vs zone (very good) against 68.5% vs man and 67.4% vs press, roughly 44th and 50th percentile, with low curl and comeback success rates and only average slants, so his technique and timing working back to the quarterback need work. Harmon comps him to [[Jordan Addison]] but more explosive, and calls him a really good number two who can act as Green Bay's quote number one. Contested target rate 22.6% with 83.3% conversion is great, but he warns Packers fans about a 7.4% charted drop rate (9.3% in college) — like [[Jayden Reed]], he makes the hard downfield catches and clanks layups. Notes he is 5-11, 191 pounds, essentially Reed's exact size despite playing bigger, and ran a surprising 4.29. Best routes are posts and digs — money routes in this offense. Harmon says he is taking Golden and [[Emeka Egbuka]] as much as possible in drafts because both are going way too late, outside the top 100. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) notes the profile only reads as a disappointment because of late-first-round expectations.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): Golden is guest Ben Gretch's (Stealing Signals, NOT tracked) number one 'might be wrong about'. Gretch's problem is the prospect profile — the traits are on film but did not translate to age-adjusted production: nine touchdowns and efficiency at Texas but low volume across a 16-game playoff season, and before that two years at Houston without a combined 1,000 yards at a lower level. The counter-signals he respects are that Texas prioritized him in the portal and played him, and that the NFL spent a first-round pick with talk he could have gone higher. Green Bay's picture is muddy: [[Jayden Reed]] in a walking boot but efficient the last two years, [[Romeo Doubs]] still liked, [[Christian Watson]] out the first half but 'ahead of schedule', [[Dontayvion Wicks]] struggling, [[Tucker Kraft]] a genuine breakout, plus the lowest situation-neutral pass rate in the NFL in 2024 and a poor preseason showing with [[Jordan Love]]'s off-hand injury. Harris is on the same side — a Longhorns fan who liked Golden more than [[Isaiah Bond]] and respects that Golden overtook Bond, but sees the eighth-round cost as fine and rookie receivers as usually slow; he grants that sometimes they turn out to be [[Malik Nabers]], and Gretch floats a [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] outcome. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-29]]) [Best Ball]: Golden is his FanDuel pick at over 700.5 receiving yards (-114) and one of the ten receivers he is ahead of consensus on — he expects Golden to be an every-down player, moving around the formation and playing flanker in two-receiver sets, with [[Romeo Doubs]] as a sacrificial-plus X. The market is scared of the ambiguous Green Bay room, but only one Packer cleared 700 yards last year ([[Jayden Reed]] at 856; [[Christian Watson]] 603, Doubs 601, [[Dontayvion Wicks]] 415 on 76 targets), and Harmon thinks Green Bay's 32nd-ranked neutral pass rate was a function of [[Jordan Love]]'s injury. Profile: quality zone beater, deep separation with strong post and dig success rates, a holes-in-his-game caveat beating man on quick routes, and an over-80% contested catch rate that may make him the roster's best contested catcher with Reed hurt. Koh projected 850 yards, Harmon closer to 750 pre-injury and now nearer 850.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Going Deep Rookie Receivers and RB Depth Charts)]]): Angelo said he takes Golden over [[Tetairoa McMillan]] 'ten times out of ten' at cost and had him ranked higher pre-draft, with Waldman agreeing — the 1,100-plus-yard season people are projecting for McMillan is likelier to land on Golden. Angelo's case: Green Bay has not had a true movement Z who can win at all three levels and inside-out since [[Davante Adams]], and that role is central to Matt LaFleur's system. The path opened with [[Jayden Reed]]'s Jones fracture (Angelo expects him shut down for surgery) and [[Christian Watson]] rehabbing an ACL — back maybe October, but with the drastic year-one dip, not himself until 2026 — leaving [[Romeo Doubs]], [[Dontayvion Wicks]] (a catching problem Angelo likened to [[Adonai Mitchell]]) and [[Tucker Kraft]]. Angelo would not be surprised by 150 targets, ten-plus a game, in a wide-open NFC North. Waldman agreed Golden is 'the ticket' but called the Packers the most chaotic offense post-Reed, week-to-week messy, and questioned whether [[Jordan Love]] can play timing-based football.
