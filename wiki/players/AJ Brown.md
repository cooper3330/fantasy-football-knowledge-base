# AJ Brown

## Expert Takes

- 2026-01-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: in the wild-card pick segment, Harmon backed AJ Brown over 66.5 receiving yards (-114) against San Francisco, despite calling Philadelphia's passing game 'weird and bizarre' overall. He noted San Francisco runs a lot of Cover 3/single-high looks and, while banged on more by slot receivers, has given up big production to good outside WRs (citing [[Alec Pierce]]'s 4-catch, 86-yard game and [[Michael Wilson]]'s 185-yard game against the Niners this season). Harmon said he was critical of Brown's tape early in the season but that Brown 'really turned it around' in the back half, looking 'more motivated' and 'more dialed in,' and now looks like a true number-one receiver who can win difficult matchups -- which he framed this one as not being, for Brown specifically.
- 2026-01-13 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-13]]): after Philadelphia's playoff exit, Harmon said pointing out the Eagles' broken offensive structure 'does not invalidate' fair criticism of the players too -- Brown 'left a couple of plays on the football field.' He framed the viral sideline blowup with Nick Sirianni as Sirianni-initiated (Sirianni ran down the sideline to confront Brown, not the reverse), calling it odd for a head coach to be doing mid-collapse rather than proof Brown is 'the problem.' On the trade/cap question: Harmon doesn't think Philadelphia moves on from Brown given the cap hit, but thinks GM Howie Roseman is smart enough to do it if Brown doesn't want to stay or is hurting locker-room vibes -- and Brown's desire to stay could hinge on whether Philadelphia fires Sirianni or replaces the offensive coordinator. Brown turns 29 this offseason. Harmon used Brown's drops (alongside a Puka Nacua drop against Carolina) to argue against 'Maddenification' of analysis -- expecting star receivers to be metronomes on every snap.
- 2026-01-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-15]]): Brown had a solid back half of the season but a terrible playoff game, and Harmon reiterated his season-long stance that Brown is 'no longer elite-tier,' drawing a direct comp to [[Stefon Diggs]]' decline in Buffalo -- a player who clearly took a step back without being 'washed up,' and who found success again elsewhere afterward. Harmon noted the gap between what's visible on film and what the player accepts, referencing Brown's own comment that he's 'not the same player' this year without seeing it as physical decline. With [[DeVonta Smith]] outplaying Brown at times down the stretch and Philadelphia keeping Nick Sirianni as head coach (removing a natural reset point), both hosts think a trade -- addition by subtraction for both sides, even accounting for the cap hit -- could be the right move, with Harmon believing Brown could have a nice run with a different team.
- 2026-01-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Ben Gretsch (untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-15]]): Brown is floated as a trade target for the Patriots given Mike Vrabel's history drafting him in Tennessee, with Philadelphia -- not New England -- absorbing the dead-cap hit since the Eagles 'don't care very much about cap.' Gretsch, self-described as a longtime fan of Brown's, says 2025 was a genuinely down year: his yards per route run had exceeded 2.5 every season of his career and fell to roughly 2.0 this year, which he attributes partly to scheme issues under now-fired offensive coordinator Kevin Patullo. Harris notes Brown's fantasy season was 'fine, not disappointing' on the surface despite the eye-test decline, pointing to a clutch playoff catch against San Francisco on an accurate throw from Jalen Hurts. Both think Vrabel still values him and a change of scenery or offensive shakeup could reignite him, though he's owed roughly $29M guaranteed, which complicates any trade.
- 2026-02-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-12]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon says the accepted read on Brown now is that he 'wasn't the same player' for large stretches of 2025 -- not washed, but no longer elite tier, more like 'low tier two, maybe tier three' once the season is fully charted, compounded by a long injury history of lower-body soft-tissue issues and advancing age. He expects New England to seriously consider trading for Brown given their outside-receiver need and Mike Vrabel's history with him, but thinks a first-round pick is too rich a price -- a 2nd-plus is more plausible -- and cautions that a team fresh off a Super Bowl loss is prone to overpaying for 'one more piece' rather than confronting real regression risk. He also flags Philadelphia's side of it: Brown was miscast as a pure X in a static route tree under the old staff and was not the same player even accounting for that, and Harmon wonders if it's the right time for player and team to part, with [[DeVonta Smith]] fully capable of being the true WR1 in Philly if Brown is dealt, and [[Jahan Dotson]] as another name in a possible receiver-room rebuild aided by an extra pick from a Brown trade.
- 2026-02-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-27]]): asked to choose between building a passing game around AJ Brown or [[Chris Olave]] entering 2026, Harmon said that before this past season he'd have taken Brown 'easily' as a tier one receiver, but no longer views him as tier one and isn't sure whether he's fallen to tier two or tier three without a full-season chart. Trajectory-wise, Harmon called it 'one guy is going up, one guy is going down' and said he'd lean toward the improving player (Olave) right now. Co-host James Koh (untracked) flagged Brown's 2026 outlook as contingent on motivation and destination -- whether he stays motivated in Philadelphia or elsewhere -- as a real question mark at his age.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Andy Behrens (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-05]]): Both hosts treat Eagles-Brown trade smoke as credible -- Philadelphia is reportedly asking for a first-round pick, and Harris notes team GM Howie Roseman is generally a straight shooter who wouldn't fully deny talks occurred. Behrens says he'd be 'pretty hesitant' to call Brown a top-8-to-10 receiver right now given a disastrous, visibly unhappy second half of the season, but still 'pretty sure' he's a top 16-20 receiver -- both frame the trade compensation debate as real only if a team believes the peak version is still in there. Harris floats New England as a fit given their receiver need and cap space (a clear upgrade over departing Stefon Diggs) but says his own read ('a little bird') is that a first-round-pick price likely rules that out; he compares the ask unfavorably to recent big-name trades like Micah Parsons or Max Crosby.
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-12]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon and Koh floated a possible trade sending Brown to New England, noting the Patriots were bottom-10 (9th-lowest, 49%) in 11-personnel usage last year and could use a true alpha X. Harmon pointed out Brown has flipped between X and Z multiple times in his career -- Tennessee moved him to Z when Julio Jones arrived, and Philadelphia flipped him with DeVonta Smith after Kellen Moore arrived -- so he could coexist with Doubs interchangeably in New England's scheme if the trade happened. Framed as speculative; no deal reported.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Brown has been persistently linked to a trade to the Patriots since around October, and Angelo believes it becomes more plausible after the June 1st cap-hit date makes the move more team-friendly for Philadelphia given Mike Vrabel's rising contender status in New England. Angelo thinks the trade happens and that [[Kayshon Boutte]] is part of the return package involved. If it happens, it reshapes New England's receiver room around a Brown/[[Kyle Williams]]/[[Romeo Doubs]] trio, which both hosts see as beneficial for [[Drake Maye]]'s development.
- 2026-04-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-14]]) [Dynasty]: Brown is 'almost assuredly gone' from Philadelphia, with the industry consensus pointing to a trade to New England -- Harmon says he has believed for about a month there was essentially a 'handshake agreement' in place for a June 2nd trade designation. This is reported as expectation/rumor rather than a completed move at the time of taping, but Harmon is already planning the Eagles' WR room (Wicks, Hollywood Brown, Elijah Moore, with [[DeVonta Smith]] as the new #1) around Brown's departure.
- 2026-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-16]]): with Brown having left Philadelphia for New England, Harmon noted that Brown was actually the receiver who moved into the slot more often early in his Eagles tenure (with [[DeVonta Smith]] playing more outside X), and said he hopes the Patriots use Brown in more of that Z/slot-adjacent role rather than X, saying it 'would help the aging process for him.'
- 2026-07-17 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Patrick Doherty (Rotoworld, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-17]]): both hosts treat Brown as a finished product in fantasy whose ranking won't move regardless of camp buzz. Harris will watch for offhand health notes -- reports that he looks smoother than his final two years in Philadelphia and has 'reached an accommodation' with the knee issue that's dogged him -- but is explicit that even a positive version of that story wouldn't bump his rank, since it would reflect poorly on his own evaluation if a finished star's stock swung on August reports.
- 2026-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-23]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon reiterated he has AJ Brown ranked above [[Garrett Wilson]] in his personal fantasy rankings but closer than market ADP suggests, wanting to 'bake in risk' on decline that he and co-host James Koh discussed on a prior episode about the Drake May pairing. He still expects Brown to out-produce Wilson if he replicates even a lesser version of his prior self -- solid man-coverage success and short/intermediate separation, just less of a vertical threat -- helped by a potentially improved offensive line and run game in New England, even though he now says Wilson is the better talent 'in this moment.' James Koh (not tracked) labeled Brown a 'sacrificial X receiver' -- the theory that New England anchors Brown outside to open space for [[Romeo Doubs]] and others as Drake May distributes to whoever coverage leaves open -- and Harmon agreed that's plausible for the season's first three games, but cautioned a Super Bowl-contending roster will abandon the plan and revert to last year's approach if force-feeding Brown does not produce results.
- 2026-07-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host, not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-28]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: reporting from Evan Lazar and others suggests New England plans to use Brown as roughly a 70/30 outside-X receiver rather than moving him around, which both hosts are skeptical of. Harmon says he 'would be a little surprised' if Brown ends up 80% outside/on-ball and argues Brown should be used more off the ball at this stage of his career -- his 2025 player comps (Odell Beckham 2019, DK Metcalf 2024, Terry McLaurin 2024, DJ Moore 2021) were mostly not those players' best seasons. Koh calls the reported plan 'in direct conflict' with what worked before and flags the risk that Josh McDaniels won't adapt if a static-X role underperforms, given McDaniels's history of asking players to fit 'the Patriot way' rather than adjusting the scheme to them; both note that playing Brown as a static X in Philadelphia already didn't maximize him. Koh adds that Brown, having left a winning Eagles team, is motivated as much by individual statistical relevance as by winning, making a diminished or static role a poor fit for what he wants out of New England.
