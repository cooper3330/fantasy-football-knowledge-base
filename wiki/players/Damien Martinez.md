# Damien Martinez

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: his 'lunch pail' back, and one he expects to slide because evaluators will nitpick — not quite quick enough, doubts he can repeat his Oregon State and Miami play in the league. Waldman thinks that is counterintuitive: at 6'0 225 he would give him 20 touches, and while they may not be the most dynamic 20 touches you have seen, he compares the profile to mid- or late-career Corey Dillon — he will run through you, has better feet than you imagine, is efficient and catches the ball very well. Waldman's headline framing is that if he stays in great shape he could be the [[James Conner]] of this class: more nimble than expected, written off after a bad game behind a bad line, possibly changing teams, and still producing at 30. [[Brandon Angelo]] agreed, saying Martinez looked great and that Miami wrongly played Mark Fletcher over him, and grouped him with [[Cam Skattebo]] as an energy giver who endears himself to locker rooms and always has a job.
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) says 'f that' to Martinez being the safest back in the class behind [[Ashton Jeanty]] — 49th percentile adjusted production, 55th percentile PFF, 46th percentile Zierlein at a 6.18 grade (eventual starter or good backup), and a 55th percentile pre-draft model score just under his 60th percentile threshold, with current mock draft capital in round five that would be a real problem in a deep class. He has Martinez at 4.6 for 232 pounds and says he cannot run slower. Waldman agrees Martinez is not the safest but likes him: he breaks tackles, gets yards after contact, and is a good pass catcher on leak and screen routes, giving a [[James Conner]] vibe of doing more than expected if he stays healthy, and Waldman thinks he could still be a top-12 back eight years from now. Both frame this as a good, not elite, running back class — Jeanty at the top and a big undifferentiated group behind him.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: with the gap between RB2 and RB12 in this class thin, he would far rather have Martinez in the fourth round than pay a late-first for [[Omarion Hampton]] — the floors are similar and Martinez may actually be the more ready starter, further along at slowing his internal clock and making sound second-level decisions where Hampton will have rookie moments. Ceiling is the trade-off: Hampton has Pro Bowl upside, Martinez is a starter-quality, serviceable-from-day-one back. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed and added that Martinez is as good or better a pass protector, an underrated check-down outlet with good hand-eye coordination, and a tackle-breaker who pushes piles with an underrated smooth accelerating gear visible on outside zone at Oregon State (less so at Miami, where he was more of a snow plow). Waldman expects him to fall to the fifth or sixth round because scouting-trained analysts like him more than the general media — he named Dan Hatman and Matt Bowen (neither tracked) as fans — and comped him between Ryan Grant and Ryan Mathews, with Angelo offering a [[James Conner]] down-to-down comp. Waldman: eight years from now we might call him the best back in the class purely on longevity and consistency.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): Martinez is the bigger complement in his proposed Baltimore A/B backfield alongside a smaller change-up like [[RJ Harvey]], available in a similar late range because of the class's depth. Waldman also uses him as the archetype for his day-three warning: he imagines Martinez going in the fourth round to a team like Pittsburgh that then drafts another back in the sixth, with fantasy managers getting more excited about the sixth-rounder while 'five years later Martinez will still be around'. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees the pairing is realistic and that several backs in this class fit the same A-back/B-back template.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]) [Dynasty]: a worthwhile taxi-squad stash in deep dynasty leagues if you can get him in the fourth round of a rookie draft or later. Waldman was initially worried about off-field reports, had trusted, well-connected people check, and came away satisfied: the drunk-driving offense was from a couple of years ago and he was cleared of the charges before a bowl game at Oregon State. He is openly dismissive of the 'doesn't love football' label — he remembers the same being said about Arian Foster for studying philosophy and Robert Smith for wanting to be a med student, and is dubious of 25-to-35-year-old evaluators judging whether someone loves the game enough. The path: [[Kenneth Walker III]] is unlikely to re-sign in Seattle, and Martinez could become the [[Zach Charbonnet]]-to-Charbonnet successor or step in if Charbonnet gets hurt.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Dynasty]: Martinez has essentially no ADP — Waldman could not find him on a 400-player list — and some depth charts have him fourth, fighting George Holani for a final spot, but Waldman believes Klint Kubiak's offense is a near-perfect fit because it resembles what he ran at Oregon State, and expects him to win an active roster spot over Kenny McIntosh. He also argues Martinez is a better receiver than his catch totals suggest, since he was usually the play-action distraction on wide zone and toss fakes, with the Miami tape showing he can catch. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees, sees McIntosh as a lesser [[Rachaad White]] type, and thinks Seattle is unlikely to re-sign [[Kenneth Walker III]] given durability unless he plays a full season — putting 2026 on track for a close-to-even [[Zach Charbonnet]]/Martinez split. Waldman's stated worst case: [[Breece Hall]] lands in Seattle.
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]): Waldman agrees Martinez remains a dynasty asset in deep leagues while on Seattle's practice squad. He calls him a good football player who runs the scheme very well — powerful, smart, with decent hands — and reads the fact that Seattle brought him back as meaningful; it would take only one funky injury to [[Kenneth Walker III]], of the kind already seen, for Martinez to be back on the depth chart and in fantasy lineups by year's end. He notes Martinez and [[Zach Charbonnet]] are stylistically alike, which is part of why [[George Holani]] beat him out. [Dynasty]
