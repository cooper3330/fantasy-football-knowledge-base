# Ben Sinnott

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name as "Ben Sanat"; normalized here to the real name of the Kansas State
> tight end, Ben Sinnott.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]] [Dynasty]: an impressive workout (4.68 in the 40, 6.83 in the cone drill) backs up tape Waldman grades as top-three-to-five at tight end in this class — a "bulldozer" runner with balance and contact balance who still moves his feet well, and reliable enough as a receiver to pair with the athletic profile.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): a high-floor sleeper pick, comped to [[T.J. Hockenson]]'s workout profile — slightly faster in the 40, marginally slower in the short shuttle, faster in the three-cone, and a two-and-a-half-inch better vertical. Praises balance and footwork specifically from a snow-game outing, but says he's more agile and explosive than he looks even in normal conditions — "put a pillow around a cast-iron pot." Solid, underrated blocker for a non-inline tight end, with the footwork and ability to bend for flat breaks as a route runner. Expects him to be available in the fourth or fifth round after six or seven tight ends go off the board, in a class Angelo and [[Matt Waldman]] both expect to see fewer than 12 total tight ends drafted, down from 18 in each of the prior two years. [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, agrees, saying Sinnott is better than "half the guys if not two-thirds" of the more hyped tight ends this cycle and might sneak into round three.

- 2024-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-30]] [Dynasty]: revisits the Michael Mayer comp from earlier hype cycles and reverses it — "he didn't have those things [Mayer's zone-specialist profile], you know who did? Ben Sinnott" — grading Sinnott faster, more explosive, bigger, and a better runner after the catch than Mayer, and a near-rival to T.J. Hockenson athletically (better vertical and three-cone, marginally worse 20-shuttle, slightly faster overall). Sound release footwork and hand counters versus off-coverage, good technique and flat trajectories on shallow-route breaks, effective weight drop and hard break. Waldman's flag: because Sinnott was a Kansas State prospect and former walk-on, "you didn't hear much about him" — an underrated evaluation relative to buzz.
- 2024-04-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-23 (Evaluation vs. Valuation)]]) [Dynasty]: Sinnott is one of Waldman's favorite players in the whole class and his TE2 off the board — in a seven-round mock draft he slotted Sinnott at the top of round three, not purely on talent grade but on instant-impact projection, comparing his ceiling to [[Sam LaPorta]]. He praises exceptional agility and an unusually clean release off the line for a player his size, and rates his all-around game — blocking, receiving, everything — as top-50 caliber, expecting a rookie-year impact if he lands in the right offense. He cautions the deep tight end/receiver flow of this draft could still push Sinnott past pick 50 despite the grade. Guest NFL.com analyst Chad Reuter (not a tracked expert) agreed he's 'pretty good.'
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]) [Dynasty]: Ben Sinnott projects as a boom-bust dynasty TE with dual comps -- George Kittle (upside) and Kyle Juszczyk (fullback/checkdown-heavy floor, which co-host Bob Harris, not a tracked expert, called 'blip bust' rather than boom-bust). Waldman is wary the Washington offense hasn't historically featured the tight end, and worries about GM/coach misalignment on this pick -- similar to what he flagged with Sam LaPorta pre-2023 -- with Zach Ertz's continued presence (588-yard career high, in Arizona) likely depressing Sinnott's rookie usage the way Ertz depressed Trey McBride before McBride broke out once Ertz left. Waldman has Sinnott as his TE2 in the post-draft RSP and believes his talent justifies that grade, but says he is not drafting Sinnott in redraft leagues right now -- calling this strictly a wait-and-see dynasty stash, hoping for a LaPorta-level breakout but not banking on one.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Sinnott grades as a top-12 overall rookie across all offensive skill positions on Waldman's board, but Waldman is reluctant to draft him early in tight-end-premium leagues because rosters are often already set at the position with veterans like [[Travis Kelce]], [[George Kittle]] or [[Sam LaPorta]], which makes Sinnott too good a value to pass on around the early third round even when not strictly needed. He questions Washington's offensive fit for the position, noting even Zach Ertz's best year there produced only 588 yards while the other tight ends combined for just 400-500, and worries about Sinnott sharing touches or being miscast into a fullback/H-back role.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: GM Adam Peters (from the 49ers) reportedly comped Sinnott to a cross between George Kittle and Kyle Juszczyk, and a draft-room clip had ownership asking whether Sinnott would even see the field with Zach Ertz on the roster -- the answer given was yes. Harmon expects Washington to lean into more 12-personnel looks with Sinnott and Ertz both on the field, with the team hoping Sinnott can deliver more of an immediate 'Sam LaPorta'-type rookie impact than [[Zach Ertz]], who Harmon views as a fading placeholder.
- 2024-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-16]]): Harmon floated that Washington could lean into more 12-personnel/heavy sets than expected in 2024 given a shaky offensive line and a thin receiver room beyond [[Terry McLaurin]] and [[Jahan Dotson]] -- noting GM Adam Peters compared second-round rookie Sinnott to both [[George Kittle]] and Kyle Juszczyk, a wide range that leaves his projected role (in-line tight end vs. fullback/H-back) unresolved. Harmon pointed to new OC Kliff Kingsbury's final Arizona season, when he leaned on multiple tight ends (Trey McBride and [[Zach Ertz]]), as a template that could carry over in Washington. [Dynasty]
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): Sinnott 'maybe can take a step' in year two, and Harmon frames the real question as a snap-allocation problem rather than a talent verdict — Washington spent early day-two capital on him and wants him on the field, but got competent veteran play from [[Zach Ertz]], and keeping the veteran risks stopping the younger player's progress. Harmon says the position needs more dynamism and more big plays regardless of who provides it. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is unsure Sinnott is 'the guy' at all.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): Waldman agreed Sinnott has a chance to be something more, but read Washington's re-signing of [[Zach Ertz]] two ways — either the team does not love what it is currently seeing from Sinnott, or it is simply covering its bases so Sinnott can grow into the role organically if Ertz falters. He stressed this was speculation, not fact. He was in favor of the pro-Sinnott agenda co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) attributed to Josh Norris.
- 2025-12-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-15]]) [Dynasty]: reacting to a modest 1-catch, 36-yard, 1-target game, Waldman said he's 'totally in' adding Sinnott, calling him a future top-10 tight end with possible top-5 ceiling once he settles into the role. He argued [[Zach Ertz]] and blocking specialists (Bates, Colson) had been ahead of him on the depth chart despite Sinnott being more of an Ertz-style receiving threat -- a strong route runner and YAC producer currently held back by throwing from [[Marcus Mariota]] rather than [[Jayden Daniels]]. As Daniels gets fully healthy and Sinnott gets a full training camp with him, Waldman expects him to be a cheap, high-upside add capable of legitimate top-10-to-top-5 production.
- 2026-01-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-19]]) [Dynasty]: with [[Zach Ertz]] almost certainly gone and a new play-caller likely coming in, Waldman thinks the talent and fit both point up for [[Ben Sinnott]] -- he calls him a potential matchup problem, 'a middle-class to upper-middle-class [[Brock Bowers]]' -- but says it hinges on scheme: he expects incoming staff (with Jayden Daniels still developing off injury) to want more multi-tight-end, run-heavy sets rather than a spread passing attack, which should get Sinnott on the field more even if [[John Bates]] stays the No. 2-in-name option as the team's best run-blocker. Waldman separately tore into outgoing OC Cliff Kingsbury as an analyst-friendly but ultimately incomplete coach whose offenses go stale after a strong first year and whose quarterbacks (Johnny Manziel, Kyler Murray) don't develop -- part of why he thinks Washington wanted to avoid Daniels stagnating under him.
- 2026-03-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-09]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman prefers Washington give Sinnott the receiving-TE workload rather than pursue a similar-skilled veteran like [[Chigoziem Okonkwo]] in free agency -- he sees Sinnott as essentially the same player archetype (good route-runner, strong after the catch) and thinks he should be deployed as the pass-catching tight end in two-TE sets under Dan Quinn, with a blocking-first tight end taking the in-line snaps rather than signing Okonkwo to compete for the role.
