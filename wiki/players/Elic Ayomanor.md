# Elic Ayomanor

## Expert Takes

- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): the Stanford receiver's game too often takes three good steps forward and one giant leap backward. He has effective setups and breaks on timing routes, but lackluster execution at the catch point — he clap attacks targets too often, does not attack the ball early enough to prevent defenders making a play on it, and does not generate position to prevent that. Waldman says his game short-circuits against tight coverage, as if he forgets which type of attack to use based on the ball's trajectory when the defender is playing him tight. See [[Catch Technique and Ball Tracking]].
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): he agrees the Stanford receiver is overrated, expecting some team not to have gotten the memo that 'after the stem' he is a problem. Waldman credits real speed, decent size and some good route skills, but says separation late in routes and especially play at the catch point are badly overrated — a lot of drops, and he 'seems to self-destruct when the ball's in the air' on vertical routes, which is exactly what his game is built on. His projection is a long NFL career as a WR4/WR5 who rarely sees the field and is not targeted much when he does. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) offers [[Michael Wilson]] as his loose comp — 'a pretty good guy, but a guy.'
- 2025-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-16]]): Harmon is 'pretty skeptical' of Ayomanor and acknowledges there are evaluators much higher on him, with catching the football named as the specific thing that is not always easy for him. But he ranks Ayomanor ahead of [[Jalen Royals]] because the outcome is at least legible — if Ayomanor hits, that is a starting X receiver in the league.
- 2025-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-09]]): Harmon was lower than consensus on Ayomanor but thought he should be a fourth-round pick, so the landing spot and cost feel fair. The profile is a well-built true X (6-2, 206, 4.44 forty, 38.5-inch vertical, 88 sample snaps outside) who is a good blocker with a 70.9% success rate versus man and strong marks on posts, digs and slants at healthy volume — a fit for play-action shot plays. The problem is the catch point: a 6.8% drop rate and a 57.1% contested catch rate, second-lowest among receivers who got full Reception Perception prospect profiles (only [[Tai Felton]] was worse, and Felton is far smaller). Harmon says his attack is a mess, he claps at the ball and loses the fight coming to the ground, and hopes higher-level coaching helps. He is also bad at the straight-line X stuff — go-and-stops, comebacks — lacks the field speed to get open deep despite the forty time, and does not weaponize working back to the quarterback. Harmon sees him as a [[Van Jefferson]] replacement who could win early on blocking; James Koh (not tracked) was more negative, saying of the three Titans rookie receivers Ayomanor is the one he could do without.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Harmon mildly disagrees with Koh (not tracked), who is lukewarm on the Stanford rookie. Harmon concedes the ceiling may not be high but comps him to [[Romeo Doubs]] — and notes Doubs became a totally satisfactory NFL X receiver. Both are strong blockers; both have catch-point issues, with Harmon calling Ayomanor a really, really good blocker. His point is that Tennessee's X does not need to be a high-target player, so the profile can work in that role. He expects Ayomanor could be pushing for snaps by the end of the season behind an early-year [[Van Jefferson]]/[[Calvin Ridley]] alignment, while [[Xavier Restrepo]], [[Chimere Dike]] and [[Tyler Lockett]] are not outside candidates.
- 2025-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-01]]): Ayomanor went roughly where Harmon slotted him — a fourth-rounder, not the day-two player some evaluators argued for — and he calls him a developmental type who drops too many passes, is a good blocker, and needs work attacking the catch point, with poor contested-catch grades. The flashes are beating man coverage with build-up speed, so Harmon thinks there could be a backside X in time. Near term, Harmon frames Ayomanor as the rookie handcuffed version of [[Van Jefferson]]: if Ayomanor is not ready to start at X, Jefferson will, and Harmon says Tennessee had better hope it is Ayomanor because we already know what Jefferson is. That role matters because Harmon believes [[Calvin Ridley]] is otherwise forced into X snaps the team would rather he not take.
- 2025-08-21 — According to Jake Ciely of The Athletic (guest, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]] [Redraft (Standard)]: Ayomanor was his No. 2 super-deep sleeper, a second Titan alongside [[Cam Ward]]. His pre-draft steam came from dominating Colorado with chunk plays against [[Travis Hunter]]; Ciely does not call him a tier-one receiver in the class but sees [[Michael Gallup]], James Jones or [[Robert Woods]] upside, which as a number two option for Tennessee with Ward playing a decent rookie season would make him a waiver pickup a few weeks in — especially since [[Tyler Lockett]] showed signs late last year of being finished. [[Chris Harris]] wrote an Almanac profile on him and not on fellow fourth-rounder [[Chimere Dike]], noting Dike was drafted first but generates no camp talk. Harris's comp is [[Alec Pierce]] — damning with faint praise, fast at 6-2, 210 but can he do anything else — and he cautioned Ayomanor was the best player on two very terrible Stanford teams.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-29]]): asked about Charles Davis's 'shades of Chris Godwin' comp, Harmon said the shades are appropriate but the ceiling is not — the overlap is size, physicality, excellent college blocking that should carry to Tennessee, ruggedness after the catch (in space on 52.1% of his catches in games charted, going down on first contact on fewer than half of those), and the in-breaking route tree of posts, digs, slants and corners. The break from Godwin is reliability: Ayomanor dropped a lot of footballs and has contested-catch issues, which Harmon attributes to thinking about what he will do with the ball before catching it — 'not a Chris Godwin thing at all'. He also needs to develop against zone (21st percentile success rate). Harmon had graded him late third or early fourth round; going day three was fair. Path to snaps: beating out [[Van Jefferson]], who ranked 151st of 160 in yards and first downs per route run over two years.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): there is real trust building between [[Cam Ward]] and Ayomanor, and 'those acrobatic catches that we heard about from college, those were not oversold' — he had a one-handed grab on the sideline early and later hauled in Ward's huge diagonal off-script air ball across the field. Harris says he can imagine talking about Ayomanor for waivers, but warns that for all of Tennessee's receivers, [[Calvin Ridley]] included, it is going to be up and down: the Titans do not look well coached (13 penalties in Week 1, 12 in Week 2) and were not aggressive until they fell behind. He cannot promise you will be thrilled starting any Tennessee receiver.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-16]]): buying Ayomanor as a real contributing receiver after 6 targets, 4 catches, 56 yards and an across-the-body scramble touchdown from [[Cam Ward]]. Harmon charted his Week 1 game and he struggled to separate against a good Denver defense — Riley Moss handled him in man — but the routes he won were the same in-breakers he won on in his prospect profile, and Harmon does not think Ward has consistently accessed that part of his game yet. Comp: [[Romeo Doubs]], which Harmon means as a compliment given both went in the fourth round. James Koh disagrees and is not buying, citing the prospect chart — 70.9% success vs man is good, but 74.6% vs zone and roughly 65% vs press are both well below the thresholds he wants, plus shaky hands — while conceding Tennessee simply lacks bodies outside [[Calvin Ridley]] and that Ayomanor's size and evident trust with Ward matter.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris and guest John Daigle (Establish The Run, not tracked) independently landed on the Titans rookie as their number-four Week 3 waiver add at 8% rostered. Daigle's case: he trails [[Calvin Ridley]] by only one target, has more fantasy points thanks to a Week 2 touchdown, and is a fourth-round rookie whom everyone who watched his college film believes only fell to day three because of the injury in his final year — a good bet as [[Cam Ward]]'s target tree gets established. Harris's filmic case: the acrobatic catches we were sold showed up in Week 2 and he looked like a freak — he is not going to run past anybody and wears defenders like a backpack, but the ball skills are out of this world. His comp is what the Bills wanted [[Keon Coleman]] to be immediately. Harris's caveat is that he does not really want Titans weapons, but says if that offense becomes competent, Ayomanor could be an actual number one.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): Harris made Ayomanor his number four overall and his only receiver on the Week 4 waiver list at 21 percent rostered. Guest SCOTT FISH (Fantasy Cares, NOT tracked), who had him number one, laid out the case: several of his NFL draft scout friends had Ayomanor projected as a second-rounder and he went in the fourth; [[Calvin Ridley]], not Ayomanor, draws the top defender; five-plus targets every week; low fantasy output so far but top 10 in first-read targets, top 10 in unrealized air yards, and he gets most of Tennessee's red-zone and end-zone targets. In Week 1 he had three catches but three more near-misses — two batted away late and one thrown just out of bounds by [[Cam Ward]]. Fish would cut Ridley for him if forced; Harris was 'not sure that I would, but I'm also not sure that I wouldn't,' and joked that unrealized air yards 'might be the subtitle of the Cam Ward autobiography.'
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-30]]): Ayomanor has 151 receiving yards through four games, identical to [[A.J. Brown]], and Harmon says he is 'playing pretty well' and profiles as a [[Romeo Doubs]] type — a good, solid starting X receiver in the league. He still takes Brown for more rest-of-season yardage, but only narrowly, because the Titans offense is dreadful (he quotes [[Cam Ward]] saying the team is 'ass') and Ayomanor will have games in the zero-to-five-yard range. The comparison is offered as an indictment of Philadelphia's offense as much as praise for the rookie.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): Ayomanor is Harris's number-five Week 5 waiver add at 31% rostered — soon ineligible for the list — but with heavy reservations: there are no Tennessee receivers he wants to use, and he is adding Ayomanor only as bye-week cover, not feeling great about starting him. The positive read is that he is an acrobatic kid with really good hands who made the one downfield play in the Texans game (a first-drive bomb shot), even though the Titans scored zero points. Guest Jeff Erickson (RotoWire, not tracked) is warmer — he gets trolled weekly for ranking Ayomanor too low in his RotoWire value meter, likes the Week 5 spot against an injury-hit Arizona secondary, and calls him a good young player. Caveats: [[Cam Ward]] popped an interception up off him, and Harris calls the Titans the NFL's worst team.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: last week's question was whether Ayomanor is the Titans receiver to start in a bye-week emergency, and 'the Titans answered that question with a resounding no.' Two catches for 18 yards plus what looked like a quasi-drop in the second quarter, though Harris allows the ball wasn't well thrown. He puts the blame at the team level rather than the player's — the offense stinks and no Tennessee pass catcher outside [[Calvin Ridley]]'s splash plays is worth starting.
- 2025-10-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-14]]): Ayomanor has 'exceeded' Harmon's expectations and been a 'passable player' in Tennessee's bottom-10 receiver corps, but Harmon stopped short of calling him a true starting-caliber NFL wideout, noting he's a day-three rookie who beat out [[Van Jefferson]] for the starting X job without ever looking like an immediate plug-and-play starter the way [[Puka Nacua]] did as a rookie. Harmon compared his early-career trajectory to [[Romeo Doubs]]'s rookie year rather than Doubs's current, more developed form -- real but unproven long-term upside. Ayomanor led Tennessee in routes run in Week 6, but only after [[Calvin Ridley]] exited the game with an injury after four routes, and the whole situation is clouded by Tennessee's broader dysfunction around [[Cam Ward]] following Brian Callahan's in-season firing.
- 2025-10-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-14 (Week 7 Waiver Wire & More W6 Film)]]): Ayomanor didn't get to show his good deep-ball skills against the Raiders -- all three catches came in garbage time -- but with [[Calvin Ridley]] hurt, Harris and guest Jake Seeley (The Athletic, not a tracked expert) both rank him their top receiver add for Week 7 (Harris's #3 overall, Seeley's #5), a pre-draft comp Seeley made to Michael Gallup/James Jones -- a real but capped WR3-ceiling talent whose weekly upside still depends on Ward improving. Both say they're not spending much FAAB, since Ayomanor is 25% rostered and neither feels obligated to chase him hard.
- 2025-10-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-28]]): Harmon's pre-draft profile had Ayomanor as a speed-based receiver still seasoning as a route runner who could stretch the field horizontally and beat man coverage, but who needed refinement and had hands issues -- he says nothing since has changed that view, citing another drop in Week 8 and an apparent miscommunication with Cam Ward (replay showed Ayomanor slowing on the route rather than Ward badly overthrowing him). Ayomanor has mostly played X receiver while [[Chimere Dike]] runs the slot; Harmon says pre-draft-driven hype currently favors Ayomanor over Dike but he personally is now more drawn to Dike's usage and production.
- 2025-10-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-28]]) [waiver wire, #4 on Harris's list, 31% rostered]: Ayomanor is 'the likeliest to benefit' when [[Cam Ward]] uncorks one of his rare big-arm deep shots, several of which travel 35+ yards downfield on a flat trajectory. Guest Scott Fish (Fantasy Cares, not tracked) says he's been 'in on him since Week 1,' ranked him ahead of Calvin Ridley going forward in that offense a month ago, and likes that Ayomanor ranks high in unrealized air yards and uncatchable targets -- upside if even a fraction of those become realized or catchable. Both frame him as a deep-league speculative add given Tennessee's poor overall offense.
- 2025-11-21 — According to Chris Harris on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-21]]: Ayomanor has a hamstring problem and did not practice Thursday.
- 2025-12-23 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-12-23]]): Ayomanor had a couple of nice grabs against Kansas City but remains very up-and-down; Harmon comps his trajectory to a Romeo Doubs-type path -- occasional splash plays mixed with catch-point mistakes that tighten up over time -- projecting him as a potential useful rotational receiver and possibly a future starter, but not someone to lock in as a 2026 starter without Tennessee still adding competition at the position.
- 2026-01-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-05]]) [Dynasty]: in the [[Chimere Dike]] comparison, Waldman says Ayomanor 'looks like it should be a finished product' on paper but has consistent separation and catch-in-high-leverage issues that showed up throughout his Stanford tape, and it's puzzling because the tools are all there. He'd still stash Ayomanor as a late-round dynasty option to see if it comes together, but ranks him behind Dike for now.
- 2026-03-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-03]]) [Dynasty]: On the Titans' rookie receivers, Harmon said Elic Ayomanor could be a contributing piece but wasn't someone he saw enough from as a rookie to project as a starter, though 'there are some tools there' -- he wins fairly well on over-the-middle routes and profiles best as an off-ball player rather than the X-receiver role Tennessee played him in heavily as a rookie. He finished with 515 receiving yards, second on the team, in what both hosts called an 'absolute disaster' passing offense that didn't have a single 600-yard receiver.
- 2026-05-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-21]]): Harmon's Reception Perception rookie-year chart on Ayomanor came back almost entirely red (poor across the board except slant and flat/screen routes), which he said matched his and co-host James Koh's earlier read that Ayomanor was put into a role in year one he was never ready for. Running Ayomanor through Reception Perception's new player-comp tool returned a top-five match list of Preston Williams, Geronimo Allison, Marquise Lee, Kelvin Benjamin, and Courtland Sutton -- all disappointing-to-bust outcomes -- which Harmon used to illustrate that a bad rookie-year profile carries real historical flame-out risk, while noting Davante Adams remains the extreme outlier exception that shouldn't be counted on to repeat.
