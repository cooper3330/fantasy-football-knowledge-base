# Cade Klubnik

## Expert Takes

- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: his 2026 QB2 — a really efficient passer he compares to [[Jared Goff]] with a bit more athleticism, able to pick a defense apart at all three levels. The first thing Angelo noticed was how catchable the ball is at every level, and he likes that Klubnik is not afraid to give his outside receivers chances to high-point and win contested balls. He credits the supporting cast too, calling TJ Moore and [[Antonio Williams]] day-two-or-better NFL receivers with Bryant Wesco also good. [[Matt Waldman]] had not watched him and offered no evaluation.
- 2026-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-30]]): Cade Klubnik could eventually start in the NFL but needs to add arm strength. Charting his accuracy into 'catchable' vs 'pinpoint' buckets plus velocity, Waldman found Klubnik had more velocity failures than usual on intermediate outs and quick outs -- his accuracy is strong across much of the field only when conditions let him throw with less velocity, which worries teams wanting timing throws (deep comebacks, deep outs, posts) into tight windows. He also flags a tendency to leave the pocket prematurely and disrupt his own blocking. At about 6'2'/210 with mobility, play-action ability and accuracy on the move, Waldman thinks Klubnik profiles as a career backup ('quarterback two') unless he adds arm strength comparable to a Brees or Brady, in which case a journeyman-starter ceiling is possible. Waldman pushes back on the idea 2025 was a statistical disappointment, attributing it partly to drop-plagued receivers rather than Klubnik's own regression.
- 2026-04-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-13]]) [Dynasty] [Best Ball]: calls him a genuine boom-bust Super Flex pick, having boomed and busted within his own college career -- entering last season as many outlets' top-rated QB prospect off a strong statistical year, then busting with a run of drops and habitual inaccuracy. Attributes part of that to Clemson's offense not conceptually preparing quarterbacks for the NFL level, which he says isn't Klubnik's fault but is real context. Identifies two fixable mechanical issues -- inconsistent velocity and passes that sail over the middle as a result -- and says if he corrects them there's real placement skill and off-structure pocket movement/creation to work with, but questions his baseline accuracy against NFL defenses that disguise coverage. On how often a prospect at this level 'figures it out' late, cites Brock Purdy as a higher-end version and Drew Brees, Tom Brady and Marc Bulger as older examples, cautioning that most of those comps are roughly 20 years old and such turnarounds are rare.
- 2026-05-11 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-11]]) [Dynasty]: on last year's ankle sprain and wrist injury, Waldman said he does think the ankle mattered -- charting most of Cade Klubnik's games, he saw a release flaw where Klubnik pushed off his front toe instead of driving off a planted back foot, sapping velocity and sailing throws, consistent with how he was likely taped and managed through pain. If camp shows the velocity and placement back to normal now that he's healthy, Waldman said he'd be 'a little bit more excited' about him. Given his situation -- a team that perennially needs a quarterback with only a shaky incumbent as a stopgap -- Waldman called him a dynasty stash worth having, especially in superflex.
- 2026-05-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-28 (Going Deep)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said injury -- specifically ankle and wrist issues that emerged post-draft -- may have muddied Klubnik's evaluation more than performance did. On tape, he flagged a mechanical issue where Klubnik often pressed off his big toe and elevated his body to generate more velocity, forcing the ball high and costing him consistent snap and explosion through his release; Waldman wondered whether that was compensation for the ankle injury, in which case Klubnik's disappointing season is more explainable than damning. He noted Klubnik entered last year as one of two consensus 1-2 QBs (alongside [[LaNorris Sellers]]) and still likes the underlying prospect: a big enough arm for all quadrants, escapability to extend with his legs, and the ability to play on time from a clean pocket. Brandon Angelo added the Jets' roster is thin enough elsewhere that they'd rather hand Klubnik a runway behind [[Geno Smith]] than force a rookie QB decision in 2027 -- expect real game action around week 12 to find out what they have, and a strong 2024-caliber showing could keep the Jets out of the 2027 QB draft market entirely.
- 2026-07-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-20]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman put rookie [[Cade Klubnik]] on a 'Davis Mills career track' with the Jets -- expect him to get real run for part of the season while the team evaluates whether he can be the answer, since a perceived strong 2027 quarterback class means New York doesn't want to be forced into taking one next year. He compared it to Arizona giving Jacoby Brissett (great for fantasy last year, 1-11 for the team) way to Carson Beck this season -- both teams using a bridge starter before pivoting to a developmental option.
