# CeeDee Lamb

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-01 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]] [Redraft (Standard)]: makes the host case for Lamb as the 2024 #1-overall pick over [[Christian McCaffrey]] and [[Tyreek Hill]]. Credits the Cowboys' offense clicking after Mike McCarthy took play-calling duties back from Kellen Moore, with Dak Prescott saying on a Thanksgiving broadcast that it's "the most comfortable he's ever felt in an offense." Argues Lamb's usage down the stretch of 2023 "felt more like target totals" than reception totals, and downplays how much the supporting cast (Brandon Cooks, others) matters to his role.
- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode: ranks Lamb #2 overall for 2024, behind McCaffrey and ahead of Hill — "it's between McCaffrey and Lamb" as the real question at the top of the board, with no specific film mechanism given beyond the ranking itself.
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]): on the Wild Card blowout loss to Green Bay, cautions against reading the box score (17 targets, 9 catches, 110 yards) at face value — "ignore the box score... it was just ridiculous at the end," most of the production coming with the game already decided rather than reflecting genuine offensive rhythm.

- 2024-04-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-15]]: with Lamb skipping voluntary sessions and due only $17.9M against a Cowboys cap already carrying a $55M Dak Prescott dead-cap charge, calls the contract situation "slight" concern at the very top of drafts — enough that he'd still take [[Justin Jefferson]], [[Ja'Marr Chase]] or [[Tyreek Hill]] over Lamb until it's resolved. Reaffirms Lamb as a top-five talent who can look "unstoppable" on Mike McCarthy's over-the-middle route concepts, and groups him with Chase and Jefferson as three of the league's most complete receivers.
- 2024-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-16]]): [[CeeDee Lamb]] and his camp are waiting to see what [[Justin Jefferson]] signs for before agreeing to a Cowboys extension, expecting to leverage that number into a deal just under it.
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty] [Best Ball]: Dallas doing nothing at receiver beyond a Day 3 flier (Ryan Flournoy, pick 216) after losing Michael Gallup signals internal faith the passing offense stays central despite the Zeke Elliott reunion suggesting a run-game emphasis. Harmon floated that this could be the year Lamb approaches 200 targets -- a threshold no receiver has hit since 2015 (Antonio Brown 193, Julio Jones 203, DeAndre Hopkins 192) -- given Dallas lacks proven complementary weapons (Brandon Cooks, a maybe-ready Jalen Tolbert, Jake Ferguson) and an unconvincing run game behind Zeke/Rico Dowdle. He framed three possible outcomes for the passing offense: it seriously regresses in volume, Cooks/Tolbert step up to absorb targets, or Lamb's already-massive share (181 targets last year vs. Ferguson's 102 and Cooks' 81) balloons further.
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): called Lamb the best all-around receiver in the NFL, now paired with a Mike McCarthy offense that knows how to use him and a quarterback (Dak Prescott) who can find him -- noting Lamb reportedly had roughly two fewer targets than the rest of the team combined last year. Waldman compared his role to peak DeAndre/Davante Adams under McCarthy: unstoppable in the red zone on bubble screens the way Adams was (17 TDs that year), plus old-school toughness working the middle of the field where no linebacker or safety can stay with him, new-school run-after-catch ability, and strong outside route-running/contested-catch skill. Bob Harris agreed, framing him as maybe not the single best at any one category but top-five at every category. Waldman said he'd take Lamb over [[Christian McCaffrey]] about 40% of the time with a true first pick.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: On the Dallas contract standoff, Waldman said he would not shy away from drafting [[CeeDee Lamb]] even amid holdout rhetoric -- 'if you're looking at CD Lamb and you end up getting a discount on him because of all this I would take it.' His framework: don't overweight risk on a single early pick since championships can be built multiple ways (draft, free agency, trades, lineup decisions), and public negotiation theater (fines, ego, posturing) usually resolves before Week 1 -- he cited the 49ers getting Deebo Samuel's deal done the eve of camp (July 31) as precedent, versus the slower Nick Bosa negotiation that dragged into September. He said he would only reassess if, by August, credible reporting suggested Lamb was prepared to sit out the season; for now, drafting within the next month, he is still taking the player at pick two.
- 2024-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-16]]): Lamb is a top five-to-six receiver leaguewide and, per Harmon, 'good enough to build the entire offense around' -- which Dallas belatedly did around week six or seven last year. He took home 48% of the Cowboys' WR targets in 2023 on 181 targets, which wasn't even the league's highest ([[Garrett Wilson]] led at 58%, [[Drake London]] second at 52.6%), and Harmon projects him for 180 targets in 2024 with real room to reach 190-200 given the total lack of a second target earner behind him -- comparing the situation to a 2015-style 200-target season. Both hosts flagged workload/durability as a fair question for a 'slight' player carrying that volume in back-to-back years, and noted Lamb is publicly unhappy over his stalled contract extension with some trade speculation swirling, though neither host expects an actual trade. [Redraft (Standard)] [Dynasty]
- 2024-07-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]): Lamb is his WR2 and 'top three for sure,' and he is fully feeling the top-three price despite the holdout — nothing changes it short of an injury or Lamb still being absent by late August (he says he gets nervous around the second week of August). He expects a deal because Lamb is arguably more important to Dallas's long-term future than [[Dak Prescott]], given the team has quarterback fallbacks — Waldman volunteers he 'still believes' in [[Trey Lance]]. The case for Lamb is the all-around game rather than any one trait: a top-10 receiver at split end, flanker or slot, an unbelievable aerial ball winner, tough enough to have been an NFC East possession receiver in the 1980s, plus run-after-catch. He compares the fit to San Francisco's Jerry Rice/John Taylor roles. Bob Harris (not tracked) has Lamb as his overall WR1 and hopes negotiations get contentious so he can buy the dip.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): Waldman is 'better with holding-in players than holding-out players,' and Lamb is still holding out. He calls Lamb a great talent and a good all-around player, but worries about the quality of training he is getting, the distraction, whether he and [[Dak Prescott]] will be on the same page early given how versatile his usage is, and injury risk from a compressed ramp-up. His conclusion is about price, not talent: he would rather take similarly valued receivers without that overhang, and he does not think the discount is deep enough — if Lamb fell to the second or third round he would consider it, but at a top-five cost there are safer lanes with the same upside, naming [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Tyreek Hill]]. Bob Harris (not tracked) described the holdout as a pre-baked cake likely resolving within days.
- 2025-01-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-09]]): Lamb just missed Harmon's man-coverage honorable mentions and James Koh (co-host, not tracked) pushed back, arguing Lamb is underrated against man and is wrongly viewed as a finesse player — he led the NFL with 513 receiving yards against man coverage and had six touchdowns against man, tied with [[A.J. Brown]] for fifth-most per Next Gen Stats. Harmon partly disagrees on the framing: he does not think Lamb is underrated anymore, since people already view him as a tier-one receiver and certainly should. Harmon backs the man-coverage credentials with prior Reception Perception data — 94th percentile success rate versus man and 94th versus press last season — and notes he was fourth in yards per route run against man this year. Where the offensive efficiency stats look lower, Harmon attributes it to Dallas quarterback chaos rather than Lamb. He calls it a warrior season: Lamb played through a ton of injuries making dynamic plays before being shut down for weeks 17 and 18 with Dallas's season already over.
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-13]]): [[CeeDee Lamb]] deserves his contract, but it has warped the Dallas roster — the Cowboys are one of seven teams with negative cap space and are still spending the fourth-highest share of their cap at receiver, per co-host James Koh (not tracked). Harmon expects Dallas to restructure [[Dak Prescott]]'s roughly $90 million cap hit and possibly bring Lamb down from a $35 million hit. On usage, Harmon calls Lamb a true 50-50 inside-outside player — unlike [[Malik Nabers]], whom he wants at 70-30 — and while he and Koh have said for years Lamb could just play X without hurting his production, he is a genuine weapon in the slot and hiring Brian Schottenheimer is partly about preserving that ecosystem. Harmon places Lamb, with [[Justin Jefferson]], in a contract tier above [[Terry McLaurin]].
- 2025-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-21]]): Harmon repeats that Lamb could line up at X every single snap and still be a very good — potentially one of the league's best — receivers, but that he is maximized in the slot, so Dallas needs a true perimeter receiver who can play both X and flanker in order to free Lamb up. He does not see that player on the roster: [[Jalen Tolbert]] is a mostly outside guy who is fine as a three but not a real two, [[KaVontae Turpin]] is a situational returner-slot type, and he questions whether [[Jonathan Mingo]], acquired for a fourth-round pick, can even be factored in, since Mingo also profiles as a slot/gadget adjacent player rather than the perimeter answer. James Koh (co-host, not tracked) frames Lamb as best utilized as a 50/50 inside-outside player and reports Cowboys writers reading the jersey-number tea leaves that [[Brandin Cooks]] is gone; Harmon says he could tell Cooks was done just by watching the games.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]): Lamb is a strong mid-first-round bet to return last year's WR1-type value. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) raised the two-edged sword — you want your receiver to have all the volume, but you also want the defense kept honest, and Dallas is relying on [[Jalen Tolbert]] and [[KaVontae Turpin]]. Waldman's counter is a role argument: the receivers most damaged by a weak supporting cast are pure outside players who draw the best press corner with safety help over the top. Lamb plays all three alignments, so Dallas can move him to manufacture opportunity. He also expects [[Jake Ferguson]] back to create a little more, Turpin to have room for growth as a big-play slot option, and Tolbert to improve in year two. Even if none of that lands, Waldman says Lamb gets a ton of targets — maybe less efficient, but productive. Both noted Lamb only slowed when [[Dak Prescott]] slowed in 2024.
- 2025-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-09]]): acquiring [[George Pickens]] as a pure boundary X is a schematic win for Lamb, who Harmon calls a maximized slot receiver — he has led the NFL in yards from the slot over the last two seasons — and who no longer has to be dragged out to the perimeter even though he can win there. Harmon frames the Pickens-Lamb pairing on the [[Michael Gallup]]-[[Amari Cooper]] template of the 2019 Cowboys, a straight-line X alongside a move-around player, except Lamb is several tiers above Cooper. He also expects Lamb to out-target Pickens essentially every week, and in run-heavy games that could leave Pickens with as few as four looks.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): nothing about the [[George Pickens]] arrival slows Lamb — Waldman said 'feel it', conceding a little hyperbole but calling Lamb one of the best receivers in the league and a set-it-and-forget-it asset. His case is breadth: one of the better route runners in the league, able to play all three alignments well, physical after the catch and at the catch point, and an excellent ball tracker. Waldman's advice was that the best thing Pickens could do is study how Lamb runs routes, even if he can never do everything Lamb does. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) noted Lamb went as the WR1 in ADP last preseason and is now WR3 or WR4; Waldman thinks the range of outcomes that gets him past [[Ja'Marr Chase]] is still open.
- 2025-05-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-14]]): Harmon uses Lamb as the template for what Arizona should do with [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]. He puts Lamb in his current NFL tier one alongside [[Ja'Marr Chase]], [[Justin Jefferson]], [[Nico Collins]] and [[A.J. Brown]], but notes Lamb is a player he also thought needed to play more physically early in his career — the difference is that the Cowboys met him more than halfway by moving him all over the formation rather than parking him at X. Harmon says Lamb could line up at X every snap and be good, but you maximize him by moving him. The rookie charting comparison is close: Lamb went 72.5% vs man, 78.5% vs zone and 80% vs press as a rookie, against Harrison's 73.1%, 81.1% and 76.1% — same ballpark, with Lamb the one who statistically put it all together. Lamb is also cited as one of only eight rookies since 2014 to clear the 90th percentile against press coverage.
- 2025-05-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman still likes Lamb at his WR3 ADP even with [[George Pickens]] arriving, projecting 176 targets, 125 receptions, 1,500 yards and 11 touchdowns — top-five PPR value and 'as safe as it comes' because he plays all three receiver alignments at a high level and is the clear number one target. He would project higher if he trusted the offensive line; less time for [[Dak Prescott]] to throw actually pushes the target count up. Waldman argues Pickens helps Lamb rather than costing him — Pickens takes the [[Jalen Tolbert]] X role, which frees Lamb to work from flanker and slot where he is at his best. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) noted Lamb's 20.8 points per game in 2023 fell to 17.6 in 2024, but 18.5 in games Prescott played, dropping to about 16.5 over the final seven without him.
- 2025-06-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-06]]): Lamb led all wide receivers at 5.29 yards per route run against man coverage when lined up outside last season — and he did it in a year without [[Dak Prescott]] for much of it. Harmon uses the number to rebut the long-running 'slot merchant' label he and Koh have argued against for years: Lamb is weaponized in the slot but can be just as impactful, if not more, from outside. He expects Dallas to 'smoke man coverage all year,' with [[George Pickens]] complementing him as another top-10 man-coverage receiver on vertical routes, and calls the whole pass-catching group really good if healthy, with the run game behind a potentially big offensive line the more interesting analytical question.
- 2025-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon says he has been waffling on whether Lamb rather than [[Justin Jefferson]] deserves the WR2 slot behind [[Ja'Marr Chase]] in his rankings, noting Lamb had a monster season only a year prior and that Dallas will 'throw the ball all over the yard', while Minnesota looks likely to run more and take fewer downfield shots around a rookie quarterback. He also notes Lamb's history with Kellen Moore as a data point in the Moore-offense discussion.
- 2025-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-08]]): Lamb is good enough to line up at X on every snap — he cleared 80% success rate versus press as a rookie in 2020, putting him in a rare group Harmon lists as [[Odell Beckham Jr.]] (2014), [[Michael Thomas]] (2016), [[Tyreek Hill]] (2016), [[Justin Jefferson]] (2020), [[Ja'Marr Chase]] (2021), [[Garrett Wilson]] (2022) and [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and [[Ladd McConkey]] (2024) — but he is maximized by being moved around the formation. Adding [[George Pickens]] as the primary X is what frees that movement; Harmon also expects stack and bunch looks with Lamb as the on-the-line slot and Pickens behind him. James Koh (not a tracked expert) frames Pickens's downfield air yards as opening underneath space for Lamb to 'pick you apart with body shots.' Harmon notes Brian Schottenheimer should keep most of the same route concepts — including the Lamb deep post that clears out two safeties for the tight end's in-breaker — with more play action, faster pace and more full-speed motion.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): Lamb is the exception to Harris's Dallas pessimism — he has him high because he is a really good player and 'I'm not worried about environment for him', noting Lamb was good in 2024 while everybody else in that offense was terrible. Ben Gretch (guest, NOT tracked) agrees, calling Lamb an obvious round one pick in every draft and expecting a consolidated target share between Lamb and [[George Pickens]]. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): Lamb at 10-1 is Harris's bet for the NFL's leading receiver — not an adventurous pick, in his words 'shooting the three wood instead of the driver'. He notes Lamb finished only 50 yards behind [[Tyreek Hill]] in Hill's big season, so he has been close before, and says he does not have a high fantasy rating for anyone in the Dallas offense besides Lamb, including [[Dak Prescott]]. Raybon (Action Network, not tracked) agreed, with the caveat that it depends on Schottenheimer not being stubborn about running the ball given the state of the Cowboys' run game.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates Lamb a 1 out of 10, and the Micah Parsons trade is a positive for him — without Parsons the Cowboys defense has historically been bad and they have no corners either, making Dallas 'almost the discount Bengals situation' with shootout scripts and a quarterback who can support him. Harris rates him a 2: Lamb can get banged up but has not missed the large swaths of season that [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Justin Jefferson]] have, he is not afraid of [[George Pickens]] taking targets because there are plenty to go around, and he has seen the quarterback be good with Lamb. Both expect a bounce-back CeeDee season after 2024.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Lamb had a 'disaster of a second half' in the Thursday opener at Philadelphia — after three catches for 76 yards in the first quarter, including a corner route and a good adjusted catch that saved a would-be Prescott interception, his hands 'did not quack' the rest of the night. Harris counts three drops: a red-zone slant at the end of the first half, a deep cross in the fourth quarter that went through his hands and off his helmet, and a deep ball with three minutes left that 'probably would have won the game'; a fourth on the final fourth down he calls a tough catch, not a drop. Harris is emphatic this is not a buy-sell moment — 'if Lamb could catch a ball, Dallas probably wins' — and that Lamb is a terrific player who occasionally has 'shocking hands moments' and will be fine long-term. He also notes Lamb drew a red-zone interference call that cost him a touchdown.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-18]]): James Koh flags a shocking alignment flip — Lamb is at 65% of snaps out wide through two weeks after 49% outside last year (Next Gen Stats), a 16-point shift, and Koh admits he expected the opposite (65% inside) once Dallas added [[George Pickens]]. Harmon expected Dallas to make [[Jalen Tolbert]] a sacrificial X with Pickens at flanker and Lamb on-ball in the slot; instead Pickens and Lamb have mostly lined up on opposite sides, while Tolbert (57.1% slot) and [[KaVontae Turpin]] (70%) have moved inside. Harmon flatly rejects the offseason claim that Dallas would use Pickens exactly like Lamb — it just 'sounds like a good clip' — and wonders how much of the outside usage is matchup-driven, noting week one was clearly about attacking Philadelphia's second outside corner. He also notes Brian Schottenheimer's motion emphasis: 53.3% of Dallas drop backs have had pre-snap or at-snap motion. Lamb is also among the NFL's top-five drop rates among players with double-digit targets.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Lamb re-injured what is reported as a high ankle sprain and, though he is already talking about being back in Week 4, Harris does not see him suiting up — 'it looked bad, looked like he was in a lot of pain.' Guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not tracked) agreed he will be a headache pushing to play all week into the 'Jerry Jones humiliation bowl' against Micah Parsons and the Packers, but expects at least one game on the shelf. Mild counter-signal both noted: Lamb stayed on the sideline and was literally standing on the ankle, which Harris tried to read as a sign of hope. Even before the injury Dallas was leaning on check-downs to [[Jake Ferguson]].
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-23]]): Lamb suffered a high ankle sprain and is expected to miss multiple weeks. Koh called him the player who makes the Dallas offense go — the move-around receiver whose presence is what allows [[George Pickens]] to work as a pure outside X. Lamb ran one route in the second quarter after the injury and was gone. Harmon added that Lamb is the zone beater on this offense, and that he and [[Dak Prescott]] have been automatic for years on quick in-breaking routes, so those looks now have to be manufactured for a receiver less suited to them. Notably, [[Jalen Tolbert]]'s slot rate (46.7%) was running ahead of Lamb's this season.
