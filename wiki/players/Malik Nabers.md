# Malik Nabers

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023, when Nabers was a draft-eligible LSU receiver
> and had not yet been drafted. Treat as historical pre-draft scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]: has Nabers over [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], and the separator is pro-readiness, not ceiling. "Malik Nabers is NFL-ready on all three levels of the field. Marvin Harrison Jr. is NFL-ready." Projects a rookie line of 1,100+ yards and 10+ touchdowns, and says he "can be the engine of an NFL offense fairly quickly." Grants Harrison the higher long-term ceiling but expects Nabers to be the better player "until about a week" — i.e. for a meaningful early stretch — and expects both to be top-10 NFL receivers inside five years.
- 2023-12-21 — **Angelo**, same episode, on why the readiness is real: alignment versatility. "You can put Nabers in the backfield, you can put him in the slot, put him out wide, put him at Z — there's so many things you can do." His stated 2023 improvement was consistency rather than new skills; Angelo calls him the most consistent receiver in college football that season and says he should have won the Biletnikoff.
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode: agrees, "by a healthy margin," and adds the technical case. Nabers has "a massive vocabulary of footwork maneuvers and handwork maneuvers that he applies at a very savvy level for this stage," and — tying him to the episode's running-back thread — "he is excellent at using pacing, not only with his footwork but with his routes. This is a receiver who excels at pacing variation. The best receivers tell stories well like that." Waldman's skill-set comps are Odell Beckham Jr. and [[Justin Jefferson]], with an explicit hedge: "he may not get that level of production, but the skill set matches it pound for pound in terms of what he could potentially do year one." He notes NFL.com's Chad Reuter (not a tracked expert) independently had Nabers as the clear WR1 of the class too.
- 2023-12-21 — **Angelo**, same episode, the summary line: "right now, if you look at Malik Nabers, there's nothing that he can't do at a potential Pro Bowl, All-Pro level."
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): an early (3-game, explicitly non-final) Reception Perception look. Estimated 6'0"/200 lb. His signature trait is elite deceleration — "60 to zero" — which Harmon ties to a Sean Payton framework that great route runners either accelerate or decelerate exceptionally well; posts a 92% success rate on curl routes (22.7% of his charted routes), calling it the most fun part of his tape. Plays over 40% of his sampled snaps in the slot in addition to X and flanker. Ranked third of the three prospects charted (behind [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] and [[Rome Odunze]]) in separation, contested catch, and hands — still graded a clear plus prospect overall — and flagged as needing more refinement on outbreaking routes (comebacks, outs, corners) to profile as a true X in the NFL. Notes [[Matt Waldman]] had privately previewed his own high opinion of Nabers to Harmon before Harmon charted him, independent of Harmon's own numbers. See [[Reception Perception Methodology]].
- 2024-02-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-27]]): grouped with [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] and [[Rome Odunze]] as one of three receivers Harmon will give top-10 rankings this class, declining to rank them 1-2-3 against each other since he expects landing spot — not the pre-draft order — to decide whose career looks better. Expected to test well at the combine, unlike Harrison, who is skipping it.

- 2024-02-29 — According to guest **Eric Froton** (NBC Sports college football analyst — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]): cited, alongside [[Rome Odunze]], as leading the nation in deep-target conversion at roughly two-thirds — used as the bar-setting comparison against which [[Keon Coleman]]'s weaker downfield efficiency was measured.
- 2024-02-29 — According to **Kevin Knight** (Falcons reporter, SB Nation's Falcoholic — guest, not a tracked expert), same episode ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]): says Atlanta "can't turn down" Nabers if he's on the board at 8th overall — best-player-available plus a positional need at receiver — and that Falcons fan sentiment has shifted toward being comfortable with a receiver at 8 rather than demanding an edge rusher, given the rookie-contract value ahead of extensions for [[Drake London]] and [[Kyle Pitts]].

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]) [Dynasty]: flagged during a league-wide wide receiver need breakdown as carrying real landing-spot risk — named among the top-10 picks facing "critical need" wide receiver teams (Tennessee, New York Giants, New England), with an explicit warning to dynasty managers "nervous about landing spots" that a Patriots or Giants landing would suppress his outlook versus a stronger offensive situation.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast on the 2024 WR class A-Z ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): plays at both a high rate of speed and a high rate of control simultaneously — a combination few NFL veterans with top-shelf athletic ability ever develop. There are other pros who execute with speed and efficiency, but they lack Nabers's acceleration, movement, and play-point toughness together.

- 2024-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-26]]) [Dynasty]: full profile now live; still grades him tier one but keeps him third of the trio, calling him "the rawest technician" of the three — needs to sharpen leverage and route-stacking discipline against zone downfield. Plays 50.3% slot / 49.7% outside. Elite after the catch: broke tackles on 20.5% of in-space plays (highest of the last four classes; closest comps [[Garrett Wilson]] and Kadarius Toney, not a tracked page, at 19%), in space on 13.9% of routes, brought down on first contact only 48.7% of the time. Man coverage 74.4% success (78th percentile); press coverage 72.1% (72nd percentile — "fine," not a red flag). Zone coverage is the real concern: 78.6% success, only the 46th percentile, partly a function of a downfield-heavy route mix (just 11.4% slants, 3.2% flat, 3.9% screens) similar to [[Ja'Marr Chase]]'s college profile. Player comps: [[Garrett Wilson]] (Nabers is the better after-catch player; Wilson was the better zone-coverage prospect) and [[D.J. Moore]] (Moore was a poor-success-rate rookie vs. man — 52.7% — who developed roughly 10 points a year into an elite man-beater; floats Nabers as a similar "slow burn" case, though notes Moore was already slightly better than Nabers against zone as a prospect). Repeats the same dynasty landing-spot caution as [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] and [[Rome Odunze]].

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): reaffirms Nabers as his highest-graded receiver of the 2024 cycle overall, discussing the Chargers' No. 5 pick — but in a pure scheme-fit comparison against [[Rome Odunze]] for [[Justin Herbert]] specifically, says Odunze "is like a glove" as the three-level X fit Herbert has never had.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): explored as
  the ideal Giants landing spot of the top three — called "the least
  quarterback-proof" (not literally, but "least screwed by quarterback play")
  because his role (get the ball in his hands quickly, work short/intermediate
  over the middle) doesn't depend on [[Daniel Jones]] or the Giants coaching
  staff doing much beyond scheming him easy completions; floated as
  co-existing with [[Rome Odunze]] or [[Darius Slayton]] on the outside and
  [[Jalen Hyatt]] as a clear-out complement in that offense. In the pure
  explosive-play PFF-adjacent stat cited (Arjun Menon, not tracked), Nabers
  posted 66 explosive plays (40.9% rate) over the last two years — fewer than
  both [[Rome Odunze]] (72, 43.1%) and [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] (68, 47.2%).
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: said he was tempted to drop Nabers slightly on his board after the Giants landing spot but ultimately kept him atop his rookie receiver rankings. He argued Daniel Jones and Drew Lock can still produce the way Jacoby Brissett did with Amari Cooper, and while Nabers may not hit 1,400-1,600 yards the way he could have with Kyler Murray or Justin Herbert, Waldman projects a realistic 1,100-1,300-yard season — enough to be the class's top rookie producer in 2024 given the drop-off in New York's receiving corps behind Darius Slayton (who is also seeking a new contract and skipping voluntary workouts). He compared the outlook to A.J. Brown's early Titans career under Marcus Mariota/Ryan Tannehill as proof a good receiver can still produce without a great quarterback.
- 2024-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-30]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon rates Nabers a dynasty tier-two player -- he'd have preferred the Giants take Rome Odunze as the better overall prospect, but says Nabers' skill set and short-intermediate 'layup route' ability (some of the class's best slant and curl success rates) uniquely suit a Giants depth chart (Darius Slayton, Wan'Dale Robinson, Jalen Hyatt) that lacked a legitimate outside playmaker; fifth-fewest WR yards and seventh-fewest slot yards in 2023. Harmon flags Nabers as the least refined of the top-three receivers, needing seasoning against zone coverage, but a high-quality man-beater who projects as Brian Daboll's first read on an estimated 38% of combo-route plays. Koh (untracked) argues Nabers has a better path to a huge target share than Odunze given weaker surrounding weapons, but the least inspiring quarterback (Daniel Jones) of the top three; both dismiss 'dynasty advice' assuming Jones' play will sink Nabers' value as premature.
- 2024-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (untracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-07]]) [Best Ball]: Koh projects a median 85 receptions, 1,028 yards and 8.8 TDs (12.0 yards/reception) against a Vegas line of 840.5 yards, and Harmon urges listeners to 'bet the over' on yardage but takes the under on the 8.8-TD projection given the Giants' unsettled QB situation (a possible Daniel Jones/Drew Lock split). Both compare his likely rookie profile to Jaylen Waddle's 2021 season -- 104 catches, 1,015 yards, 9.8 yards/reception on 140 targets in a broken Miami offense feeding him easy underneath/design targets -- versus Waddle's efficiency leap to 18.1 yards/reception once Tyreek Hill arrived. They expect the Giants to build the offense around funneling him touches (tight bunch/slot alignments with Darius Slayton, Jalen Hyatt and Wan'Dale Robinson around him) given a lean backfield (Devin Singletary, Tyrone Tracy Jr.), with Koh saying anything under 900 yards as a rookie would be 'problematic' given his mismatch upside.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Dynasty] [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman called Nabers 'the best wide receiver in this class, not even close,' a view he said he's held since about November, and said he'd take Nabers over [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] '10 times out of 9' on pure evaluation. He invoked the Adam Thielen lesson — Thielen still posted a WR18 season on a historically bad Bryce Young offense despite lacking the separation and playmaking skills Nabers has in abundance — to argue Nabers' floor is protected even with rookie-QB uncertainty. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) flagged the ADP gap as the actionable signal: Harrison is going WR9 (first/second round) while Nabers sits at WR20 (third/fourth round, 28th overall) despite Waldman preferring Nabers outright; Harris said if the two were both third/fourth-round picks he'd feel great about that price, calling the current gap 'too damn big.'
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): unequivocally prefers Nabers over [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] at their respective 2024 ADPs (Nabers WR20/pick 28 vs. Harrison WR9/pick 13), projecting Nabers for roughly 1,000 yards, 82 catches and 7 touchdowns, over a quarter of the Giants' receiving target share, and 'probably a top-15 receiver.' He called Nabers the room-tying primary option who 'can't be covered right now in camp' and can do everything at a high level, giving him more ways to win than Harrison because he doesn't need elite quarterback play the way Harrison does. Bob Harris agreed WR15 is 'not an outrageous outcome' and went on a long soapbox citing prior years' receivers who thrived with shaky QB play -- [[Deebo Samuel|DJ Moore]] with Justin Fields, Mike Evans with Baker Mayfield, Amari Cooper with Bryce/Cousins-caliber QBs, Tyler Lockett/DK Metcalf with Geno Smith, and especially Adam Thielen's 103-catch, WR18 season with Bryce Young -- arguing Daniel Jones (or Drew Lock) won't be worse than that. Waldman said the ADP gap is wide enough that he's buying far more shares of Nabers than Harrison.
- 2024-07-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]): he sees Nabers as just as strong a prospect as Marvin Harrison Jr. coming out, and thinks Nabers 'has a chance to be as good or better than Marvin Harrison Jr.' as a rookie despite catching passes from Daniel Jones, whom Waldman called 'tone deaf, pocket deaf.' JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) backed this with his prospect model: Nabers is 1 of only 13 WRs since 2011 to score above 98/100, a group that includes Harrison; of the 12 in that group who've played a rookie season, 5 scored 14+ PPR points/game with a 13.9 average, while Nabers' current ADP implies only a 12.4 point/game expectation -- suggesting he's undervalued. Both cited the Giants' need for a true perimeter presence (last had one in Odell Beckham Jr.) and Nabers' after-the-catch ability as reasons he could be peppered with targets.
- 2024-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-16]]): Nabers graded as a tier-one prospect for RP over the last four draft classes, and Harmon ranked him his WR3 in this class behind [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] and [[Rome Odunze]] -- a close call driven mainly by Nabers being the least refined route runner of the three. His college charting: 74.4% success rate vs. man coverage (84th percentile), 78th percentile vs. press, 87.5% success rate on slants, 82.8% on digs and 65% on the nine route, with 50.3% of his sample snaps from the slot (a near-even 52/48 split on/off the line). Harmon expects Giants OC Brian Daboll to deploy him as a movable chess piece across the formation, specifically to attack over-the-middle space the Giants have lacked since Odell Beckham left. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) called him an 'elite talent' and floated that improved sideline chemistry now that DC Wink Martindale is gone could unlock more of Daboll's play design. [Dynasty] [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): Waldman calls the Giants' pre-Nabers depth chart a disaster — [[Jalen Hyatt]] as a pure deep threat, Isaiah Hodgins an underneath zone player, [[Darius Slayton]] a low-end WR2 at best, Allen Robinson finished, [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] overrated — and says Nabers instantly makes everyone else fit a real role. He projects roughly 127 targets, 82 catches, 1,022 yards and seven touchdowns as a rookie and says he doesn't think [[Daniel Jones]] can screw him up; he is also counting on [[Drew Lock]] starting the final six or seven weeks. Guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) is at 131 targets, 1,017 yards and about five touchdowns, and lays out the ADP spread — Underdog 27, Sleeper 38, FFPC 47, ESPN 64, Yahoo 67-71 — calling him a clear value in home leagues, with a WR1 finish ahead of [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] possible if Jones can push to 235-240 yards per game.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]): Nabers is the best rookie wide receiver in the class and the one most likely to post great production. On price, he says Nabers is worth a rise in ADP up to roughly picks 36-48; inside the top 20 overall you are paying his ceiling. He distinguishes the platforms — on Underdog and similar 'dog show' formats where everything is hyper-tweaked, Nabers's ADP is already high, but in ESPN and home leagues he is available around pick 64 or later, which is the real edge. Bob Harris (not tracked) cites Jeremy Fowler's Giants camp piece calling Nabers a Porsche rather than a sedan, and prefers paying a third-to-fourth-round price for Nabers over the 15th overall pick required for [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] — comparing it to the [[Bijan Robinson]]/[[Jahmyr Gibbs]] ADP argument he made in 2023. Both note Nabers has no target competition in New York.
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]): the biggest question with Nabers is deployment, not talent. Harmon expects him to be the Giants receiver in motion the most, working both from the slot and as a flanker depending on pre-snap alignment and look, and says having [[Jalen Hyatt]] able to stretch the field opposite him — or serve as the on-the-line slot player in certain packages — is a good thing for Nabers even though Hyatt will never be a true target earner. Harmon frames this as the same principle he applies in Philadelphia and Cincinnati: the identity of the third receiver determines how freely you can move your best receiver around the formation.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: not concerned about Nabers catching passes from [[Daniel Jones]] — 'price is still right, feeling that all day long.' The Jones worry (raised by Josh Norris after two preseason picks) would apply to a baseline NFL receiver, but Nabers is exceptional: Waldman puts him on par with [[A.J. Brown]] as a rookie, and asks who Brown was catching passes from in Tennessee when he finished as the number eight fantasy receiver. Two further proofs that talent overcomes bad quarterbacking: [[Adam Thielen]] was WR12 through 12 weeks in 2023 with [[Bryce Young]] on the league's lowest-scoring offense, and [[Justin Jefferson]] was a WR3 over the last five games of 2023 catching from Joshua Dobbs, Jaren Hall and Nick Mullens. Nabers has drawn [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and Jefferson comps; Waldman thinks Thielen is the better proof-of-concept.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): Nabers is the rookie who makes the biggest splash first — he might be the whole Giants offense, which Angelo says has literally nobody else who profiles as a number one, two or three big-play option in the NFL. Angelo had Nabers as his wide receiver one on film, calls the top three of [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], [[Rome Odunze]] and Nabers interchangeably talented, and expects Nabers to get all the volume and carry what he calls a horrendous offense to really big weeks. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed Nabers and Odunze are the two most likely to make the first splash, especially if Chicago makes a point of targeting Odunze, though Waldman's own pick was [[Xavier Worthy]].
