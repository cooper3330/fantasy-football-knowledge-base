# Arian Smith

## Expert Takes

- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): if Smith's hands were more reliable the Georgia receiver would carry a borderline starter grade in the RSP. The encouraging part is that Waldman isolates exactly one hand-technique flaw — his hands get too wide at the point of attack when he extends, producing clap attacks where he fights the ball or it flies between his hands. When his hands are close enough he makes acrobatic adjustments high, low and away from his frame, against tight coverage and through contact. Crucially he has no tracking problem, unlike [[Quentin Johnston]], his choices of how to attack the ball are sound, and he has no focus drops. Waldman adds a theory on perception: dropping a deep pass carries more psychological gravity for fans than dropping short or intermediate targets, because deep balls change games.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): the Georgia receiver is 'definitely one of the centerfold players in this draft, and in a good way' — 4.4 speed, good routes, real highs in the pass-catch phase alongside real lows. Georgia fans will volunteer that his drops are unbelievably frustrating, but Waldman says the source of the drops is reasonably correctable: only one or two issues, not ingrained bad habits, and he distinguishes Smith sharply from [[Tai Felton]], who drops the ball 'every conceivable way.' Floor is a very good return specialist; Waldman allows Smith may always have some lapses, but they should not be the kind that keep him off the field.
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said [[Arian Smith]] continues to flash and is worth a dynasty stash, especially on a practice squad -- his hands are better than his Georgia scouting report suggested, and many of his college drops were explainable technique issues rather than a permanent limitation. He cited a Week 8 rep against the Bengals where Smith worked a cornerback's back, forced him to turn and run, then hit the brakes for a comeback catch at the sideline, showing polish to go with his speed. He contrasted Smith's long-term stash appeal with [[Tyler Johnson]]'s more immediate PPR week-to-week utility.
- 2025-12-02 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-12-02]]): Smith, a fourth-round pick used mainly to run vertical 'for-the-love-of-the-game' routes that create leverage for [[Garrett Wilson]], was a healthy scratch this past week, which Harmon called not a great sign; he noted Smith was an aggressive early-fourth-round pick relative to a similar profile in Jimmy Horn Jr., taken much later by Carolina.
