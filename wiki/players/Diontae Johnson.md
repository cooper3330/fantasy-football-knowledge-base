# Diontae Johnson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his name as
> "Deontay Johnson"; normalized here to the real name of the Steelers
> receiver, Diontae Johnson.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): cited as part of the charted evidence for how badly Matt Canada's route concepts under-featured the middle of the field in Pittsburgh — just 9.4% of Johnson's routes were dig routes in the full 2022 Canada offense. Not given an explicit new projection, but grouped with [[George Pickens]] as a receiver who stands to benefit from Arthur Smith's incoming route-concept shift toward the middle of the field. Separately referenced as part of the outgoing receiver corps alongside Allen Robinson (not expected back) and Calvin Austin (not yet shown as "a big-time target getter").
- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]): with trade rumors surfacing and Arizona reported as the team showing "the most interest," grades him as capable of "at least low-end wide receiver two," with a ceiling "somewhere just outside the top 15" at the position. Reads Pittsburgh's willingness to move him as a function of liking their depth — pointing to Calvin Austin (not a tracked page), the short, speedy Memphis product they drafted two years earlier — and the strength of the incoming receiver draft class.
- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): actually
  traded, Pittsburgh to Carolina, for only a Day 3 pick swap plus CB Dante
  Jackson — both hosts "stunned," calling it a fleecing, especially since
  Carolina is "not a well-managed team." Harmon's charted case for why the
  return was so light relative to Johnson's quality: 2019-2022 success rate
  vs. man coverage of 75.0/77.7/76.9/76.1%, No. 1 in success rate vs. zone in
  2020 and No. 2 in 2021 (behind only [[Cooper Kupp]]'s historic year), and
  above the 80th percentile vs. press in multiple seasons — full-field
  separation at all three levels, with a 2022 out-route success rate of
  89.7% and dig-route success rate of 92.3%, despite being underused on
  those routes (9.3% dig, 17.4% slant) in Matt Canada's route-limited
  Pittsburgh offense. Ranked 7th in success rate vs. man coverage in 2022
  (behind Stefon Diggs, Justin Jefferson, A.J. Brown, Davante Adams, Brandon
  Aiyuk and Tyler Lockett) and one of only five players top-7 in both man
  *and* zone success rate that year — company otherwise limited to Diggs,
  Jefferson and Adams. On the drop reputation: calls it overblown by
  negativity bias — PFF drop rate 6.7% (2021), 8.5% (2022), just 3.5% (2023)
  — driven partly by high target volume in inefficient, hit-prone areas of
  the field and shaky Pittsburgh QB play, not a "crippling" issue. Projects
  him as Carolina's clear focal point for [[Bryce Young]] over incumbents
  like [[Adam Thielen]] (short-area only), [[Jonathan Mingo]] (can't win
  outside) and DJ Chark (not a tracked page): "if he doesn't catch 100
  receptions in 2024, something bad happened."


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: skeptical Johnson himself can be the alpha [[Bryce Young]] needs to lean on — "he's an on-and-off player, he's a light switch player," the type who needs a strong veteran presence around him rather than being expected to provide it himself. Worries Carolina's front office (owner David Tepper) will be too quick to "rip the duct tape off" and micromanage if things don't click immediately. Frames [[Bryce Young]] as needing to be that stabilizing "duct tape" himself but isn't sure he's there yet.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): the swing
  addition that lifts Carolina's receiver room out of the "disaster" tier and
  into merely "fragile" — asked directly whether Carolina would be in the
  disaster group without him, both hosts agree "oh yeah... they're in
  disaster." Still, when stacked against the other three "fragile"-tier WR1s
  (Amon-Ra St. Brown, [[Zay Flowers]], [[Drake London]]), Harmon ranks Johnson
  fourth of the four.

- 2024-04-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-15]] [Best Ball]: with Johnson going around WR40 in early best balls, calls it a value price alongside [[Adam Thielen]] (going around a WR5-6-tier price) — the logic being that if you think [[Bryce Young]] isn't worth drafting, then his receivers aren't either, and that's "discount time" if you're willing to bet on Young's 2024 improvement.
- 2024-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-30]]): Harmon notes Diontae Johnson had his most efficient season in 2023 while playing flanker with George Pickens occupying the X, and says Johnson can still win against press-man coverage and play X if needed -- which should give Carolina flexibility in how they deploy him alongside newly drafted [[Xavier Legette]] and [[Adam Thielen]]. Harmon considers Johnson a borderline No. 1 ('right there' with Deebo Samuel, a better player in Harmon's view) 'in the right situation,' and urges the Panthers to extend him beyond his contract's final year rather than let him walk in 2025.
- 2024-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-07]]), with James Koh (untracked) concurring: after signing with Carolina, Harmon projects Diontae Johnson as the clear top target-getter on the Panthers, ahead of [[Adam Thielen]] and rookie [[Xavier Legette]], because new HC Dave Canales has publicly named him the starting X receiver, primary read and 'target magnet' of the offense. Harmon notes Johnson has a strong track record playing significant X-receiver reps and winning routes there per Reception Perception's historical charting database, distinct from his 2023 Steelers role as the flanker next to George Pickens.
- 2024-06-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-20]]): Johnson had a quietly career-best 2023 in Pittsburgh despite the reputation hit — 8.2 yards per target, 1.97 yards per route run, career-best drop rate and career-high average depth of target (12.7) — which Harmon ties directly to being moved off a pure X role (outside/line-of-scrimmage snaps dropped from 80-90% early in his career to 60.6% last year, with more slot and motion/backfield usage) and his nine-route rate falling to 15.4% from 18.9-22.2% in prior years, since Harmon views him as a small-bodied X who is inefficient running boundary go-routes. Traded to Carolina where new OC Dave Canales and WR coach Brad Idzik (both ex-Tampa Bay/Seattle) are expected to name him the X but move him around via motion, similar to how Canales diversified Mike Evans' route tree in Tampa. Harmon: one of the best separators in the NFL yet somehow only one career 1,000-yard season (2021, 1,161 yards) after a 717-yard, missed-games 2023; expects better efficiency numbers translating to real production given the scheme fit, even while cautioning Bryce Young's downfield accuracy is unproven. - 2024-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-25]]): Johnson is one of the best separators in Reception Perception history — two top-15 finishes vs zone, at or above a 75% success rate vs man every charted year and usually above 85% vs zone, with a full green route tree in 2023. Harmon's central argument is deployment: through years one to four he took over 88% of snaps outside and under 11% in the slot, an X-heavy usage no elite separator ([[Justin Jefferson]], [[A.J. Brown]], [[Tyreek Hill]], [[Ja'Marr Chase]], [[CeeDee Lamb]]) is subjected to, because it produces exactly the per-target inefficiency people criticize him for. In 2023 that normalized to 76.3% outside, 21.1% slot and 60.6% on the line, with his most balanced tree and career-best yards per target and per route run once Matt Canada was gone. Harmon trusts Dave Canales and Brad Idzik to keep that flexibility and make him the offense's number-one read; Koh, surprised by the low enthusiasm, expects him to clear 1,000 yards with [[Bryce Young]].
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): Waldman uses Johnson as the low-end comp for [[Garrett Wilson]] — a receiver who had the same hand-placement and ball-tracking issues (famously drilling with tennis balls to improve), earns targets at a WR1 level, but where it has never fully translated into major fantasy points. Guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) is far more bullish: Johnson is his most-drafted receiver in fantasy because he goes too late, has a WR8 overall finish on his resume, gets open against every coverage type, and Dave Canales has said the Carolina offense is built around him. McFarland's caveat is durability — never a full-season loss, but 'always something,' and a limping Johnson playing through it is not the same player.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-12]]): Harmon still calls Johnson a top-20 NFL receiver and 'arguably a top-five separator' — explicitly not a top-five receiver, but a guy who gets open and makes himself available. The problem is entirely quarterback: two catches for 19 yards on six targets and 24 routes in Week 1 (a target rate he concedes is fine), with Johnson pulled for most of the fourth quarter in a blowout. Harmon's forecast is bleak and specific: with [[Bryce Young]] unable to create and Carolina's defense likely to concede points, 'we might get another season of a lot of volume and not a lot of production.' Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is nearly speechless about a separator who cannot get the ball, and notes rookie [[Xavier Legette]] actually led the team in targets.
- 2024-09-23 — According to Bob Harris (untracked co-host) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]: Johnson was 'almost unstoppable' in Carolina's upset of the Raiders, drawing 14 targets in what Harris called a career game, with [[Adam Thielen]] also productive before leaving early. Harris had recommended him publicly beforehand. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed with the characterization. Context both hosts flagged: Andy Dalton threw three first-half touchdowns, the most for a Carolina quarterback since Cam Newton in 2015, which they used to raise questions about [[Bryce Young]] rather than to project Johnson forward.
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): Johnson posted a career-high 122 receiving yards with [[Andy Dalton]] and was targeted on 37 percent of his routes, but the detail Harmon cares about is that he finally saw an intermediate pass over the middle of the field — something he says never happened once in his career under Matt Canada with goofball quarterbacks, where he was running go routes and curls straight into cover-two zones. He also had 53 yards after the catch, which Harmon jokes is out of character for a receiver who historically worked backwards. He credits Dave Canales for actually calling intermediate over-the-middle concepts and Dalton for being willing to drop back and push those throws, and treats Johnson as the team's X so [[Xavier Legette]] does not have to be.
- 2024-10-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-01]]): Johnson, not [[Xavier Legette]], is playing the true X-receiver role in Carolina, with 27 targets across Andy Dalton's two starts — the classic Diontae experience of leaving plays on the field while also making them. Harmon uses him as the case study for why RP route success rates are only part of the equation: his career charting reads elite, but the tape shows mistakes elite receivers do not make, the same critique he applies to [[Garrett Wilson]]. Koh argued Johnson is past his prime and that the conversation around him would be a complete 180 with a real quarterback and coordinator instead of Matt Canada; Harmon pushed back on 'past his prime' — Johnson is 28 — while agreeing prime years were wasted, drawing the parallel to [[Terry McLaurin]], who he says is at the end of his prime but at least finally paired with a quarterback.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31]]): both are surprised Baltimore landed him for only a fifth-round pick. Waldman reads it as insurance — the Ravens' defense is banged up with no pass rush, so they must lead with offense, and a fifth for a starting-caliber receiver fortifies against a receiver injury. Angelo says Johnson is still a great route runner who was in the top quarter of the league in several categories with [[Andy Dalton]] in Carolina and could be a WR1 for some teams; he believes Johnson wanted out because Carolina's plan centred on Bryce Young rather than paying him, and expects another decision next offseason with [[Rashad Bateman]] extended and [[Zay Flowers]] due. For fantasy both counsel patience: Angelo calls it wait-and-see on role, and Waldman predicts Baltimore treats him as a break-glass luxury they unveil in the playoffs against defenses with no tape on his usage — meaning limited fantasy value before then barring an injury.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-31]]): on the trade from Carolina to Baltimore, Johnson is 'clearly a very, very good wide receiver' and one of the best separators in the NFL — the best route runner on the Ravens roster — which is why the move makes sense against a defense diet where Baltimore has faced Cover 1 at the eighth-highest rate in the NFL. But Harmon stressed the rest of the package is thin: a poor YAC player whose yards after catch are 'neutral to negative and almost never good,' with hands issues, and a history of friction ('war is welcome' in both Pittsburgh and Carolina). Fit is the real question — Johnson, [[Zay Flowers]] and [[Mark Andrews]] all want the 10-15 yard range, and Baltimore is bottom-10 in pass attempts at just under 30 per game. Harmon noted Johnson played a career-high 21.3 percent slot rate in 2023 with [[George Pickens]] at X, so 11 personnel could put him in the slot with [[Rashad Bateman]] staying at X. Josina Anderson reported the Ravens expect him as a supporting character, not to unseat Flowers or Bateman. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) called Carolina's return — a sixth-round pick upgraded to a fifth, plus eating salary — 'horrendous' and could not believe contenders like Dallas didn't bid.
- 2024-11-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]]): Johnson made a bad call talking his way out of Carolina — Harmon says he should have sucked it up, put out good film on productive games and hit free agency, and instead he is barely playing for the Ravens, with nobody having wanted to trade for him anyway. He also characterizes Johnson and [[Adam Thielen]] as the veterans who were visibly rolling their eyes at [[Bryce Young]] early in the season, contrasting that with the younger, more cooperative Carolina room now.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]) [Dynasty]: Johnson was suspended a week for conduct detrimental to the team after refusing to enter the Eagles game with [[Rashod Bateman]] banged up — Harmon called it the worst handling of a contract year of all time. The sequence: Pittsburgh shipped him out for a fifth-round-level return nobody would beat, he sulked in Carolina under [[Bryce Young]], then was genuinely productive with [[Andy Dalton]] (Harmon called the per-route numbers crazy), then forced his way to Baltimore, the worst possible spot for immediate opportunity. Both hosts agreed he could have walked into 1,100 yards on a bad team and picked his next destination. Harmon still rates the player — 'success rate versus coverage away from the game' — but says he is not [[Justin Jefferson]], and if you behave this way you must be elite; Baltimore gave up nothing and may just cut him. Kansas City was floated as the only culture that could absorb the personality. James Koh (co-host, not tracked) added that Bryce Young's turnaround may not have happened with Johnson's body language in the building.
- 2024-12-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-24]]): after Johnson's release from Baltimore, Harmon argues Houston is the more desperate and better-fitting of the two rumored landing spots versus Kansas City, though signing him would be 'a pure desperation move' given how things flamed out in Baltimore. He calls Johnson a very good separator on corners, outs and comebacks — exactly the [[Tank Dell]] route profile Houston just lost — and thinks he is best deployed as a flanker rather than the X he mostly played for the Panthers and Steelers, noting his most efficient production season came in his final Pittsburgh year with [[George Pickens]] at X, which makes him a clean fit across from [[Nico Collins]]. Caveats: Houston's pass protection has been a season-long problem, and nobody knows where his head is. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is more bullish still, calling peak Johnson legitimately great because natural outside separators who need no scheme help are scarce, but says culture matters and he is unsure about Houston's. Both call this an all-time contract-season bag fumble.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Houston cutting Johnson after two games made him the fourth team in a calendar year to move on from him — Pittsburgh traded him to Carolina for essentially nothing, Carolina moved on, Baltimore cut him, now the Texans. Harmon called Carolina the first bag fumble: Brad Idzik and Dave Canales had built concepts around making him the primary read and he was producing with Andy Dalton, a path to roughly 130 targets and a big free-agent deal. Reportedly visibly upset after a playoff win in which he caught one pass on about 22% of snaps, down from 40%. Harmon: he is a good player but not [[Antonio Brown]] — not elite enough to survive being more of a problem than he is worth. James Koh (not a tracked expert) argued Johnson is not crazy, just wilted under contract-year pressure and self-destructed; at 28 he might have landed a five-year deal. Harmon would not be surprised if he is out of the league.
- 2025-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-07]]): Johnson signing with Cleveland for the veteran minimum of $1.17M with zero guaranteed money is where his career now sits — the Browns can cut him before the season and owe nothing, and Harmon calls him the best receiver left on the market only when he is on the field and engaged with the process. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) went further, calling Cleveland his last stop: this is a player who complained about targets in a Texans playoff win and was cut after the game, in this same calendar year. On fit, Harmon liked the X's and O's: his best season was 2023 when Reception Perception charted a career-high 21.1% slot rate, 2.5% of snaps in the backfield and only 60.6% on the line of scrimmage, versus prior marks of 84.5%, 90.4%, 77.5% and 80.6% and a previous slot high of 10.6% — [[George Pickens]] taking over X freed him to move. Harmon would now run him mostly from the slot as a quick separator, with [[Cedric Tillman]] as the true backside X and [[Jerry Jeudy]] on vertical routes. The caveat is a bizarre quarterback room and the fact that Johnson gets open but few quarterbacks want to make the throw.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): despite listing at 5-foot-10, 183, Johnson has essentially never been a slot receiver — in 2021, his best production season with 107 catches, he was in the slot on only 7.9% of snaps, what Harmon calls the Bateman zone, with the caveat that Pittsburgh was running a lot of quick-hitting concepts with a dead-armed Ben Roethlisberger. Harmon's view is that Johnson's best and most efficient season was actually 2023, when his slot rate hit a career-high 21.3%. Both hosts flag the more basic question of whether Johnson even makes Cleveland's roster. The Browns' bigger problem, per Koh (not tracked), is that Johnson, [[Cedric Tillman]] and [[Jerry Jeudy]] all want to play outside and nobody is a natural inside option.
- 2025-05-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-30]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon's buy/sell/hold designation for Johnson in the dynasty rankings was literally written as 'shrug', with a note reading 'what a mess'. Koh (not tracked) urges him to cut Johnson entirely: he signed a non-guaranteed vet-minimum deal with Cleveland and then skipped OTAs, and Koh argues Johnson was one average season away from a four-year, forty-million-dollar contract and does not think he makes the roster. Harmon had not heard about the missed OTAs and takes the news on air, agreeing the most you would do is hold him at the very bottom of a roster but that it probably will not work out.
- 2025-06-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-11]]): used as the negative foil to [[Puka Nacua]] on post-catch processing. Harmon (with James Koh, untracked co-host, driving the point) says Johnson catches the ball, takes a step back to assess the field and only then tries to make a move — a habit that works at lower levels of competition but fails in the NFL because the athletes are too good. Nacua, by contrast, is described as already knowing where his defender is at the catch point, so he can decide instantly whether to protect the ball, go down or keep churning. Harmon says he hates picking on Johnson repeatedly but that it is the clearest illustration of the trait.
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-13]]): Johnson appears to have secured a Cleveland roster spot largely by default — Harmon says the back end of the room (Jamari Thrash, David Bell, DeAndre Carter) has no needle-movers — but anything he provides would be gravy and Harmon would not count on him. He dismantled the idea that Johnson is a slot receiver, citing career slot rates of 10.1%, 12.9%, 7.9%, 12.5%, a career-high 21.3% in his final Pittsburgh year once [[George Pickens]] emerged at X, and 19.6% across three teams in 2024. The slot perception came from Matt Canada's offense funneling him bubble screens and drags: 7.92 air yards per target in 2020 as an outside receiver. Harmon also flagged that Cleveland's 12-personnel lean, with rookie [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] likely out-snapping the third receiver, caps the role. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) speculated Johnson mentally folded under contract-year pressure and could still buy into a slot role, which would let Jeudy and [[Cedric Tillman]] move around.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-27]]): Johnson was cut by Cleveland and Harmon thinks it is over — 'if you can't make the Browns it's over' — given the questions behind [[Jerry Jeudy]] and [[Cedric Tillman]]. He notes Johnson went from a target on 27% of his routes to start the season in Carolina to being cut by a string of teams inside 18 months, and says he cannot imagine anyone takes a chance on him again, 'and deservedly so'. Harmon frames it as possibly the biggest bag fumble available in a red-hot receiver market where role players out-earn [[Saquon Barkley]] and [[Derrick Henry]]: had he kept his head down in Carolina or Baltimore he would have had a floor of roughly three years and $36M and plausibly a [[Khalil Shakir]]-level $13.3M per year. Harmon adds that [[Bryce Young]] did not start taking off until Carolina traded Johnson away, which he believes was a big red flag to front offices.
