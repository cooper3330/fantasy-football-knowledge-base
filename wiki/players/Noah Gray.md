# Noah Gray

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): asked by co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) whether there is bandwidth for the second tight ends in Kansas City with Rashee Rice out, Waldman said of Gray 'absolutely,' and that we already saw it the previous week. He called Gray probably the most versatile option the Chiefs have after [[Travis Kelce]], someone you can line up in a variety of spots, with real value after the catch, an established trust level with the quarterback and full command of the offense. Waldman expects a lot more from Gray. Jared Wiley was mentioned in the same question but Waldman's endorsement was specifically for Gray.
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]): feeling him as a bye-week tight end flyer. Targets are trending up and he has authored big plays over the middle; Waldman argues that whether he is the second, third or fourth option matters less than the fact that Andy Reid schemes clear-outs specifically for him to run into open field, and that he is more athletic than [[Travis Kelce]] at this stage of Kelce's career while entering his own prime. His key edge is comprehension: Waldman says the reason many Reid receivers cannot make plays consistently is they do not master the voluminous playbook, and Gray does. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) countered that he would have to be in dire straits to start him — though with Week 14 byes, many will be — calling him worth a flyer but probably not an extended playoff role.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]): Harmon said, with some exasperation, that Gray is currently Kansas City's best pass catcher on a roster containing [[DeAndre Hopkins]], first-rounder [[Xavier Worthy]] and [[Travis Kelce]] — he has picked Gray up in a fantasy league and simply wants him to stay on the field, since the Chiefs are leaning on two-tight-end sets and Gray is on the field a ton. He admitted he does not understand it at all. Context both hosts gave: this is an 'amoeba' offense where nobody holds a fixed position, which only [[Andy Reid]] and [[Patrick Mahomes]] can get away with.
