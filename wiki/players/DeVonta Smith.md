# DeVonta Smith

## Expert Takes

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]), part of a discussion on how few "WR2s" are actually capable of being a true WR1 (see [[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]] and [[Tee Higgins]]): names Smith as one of the very few who can — "he can be awesome anywhere in the league" — pointing to his 900+-yard rookie season with Jalen Hurts and a thin supporting cast as proof of concept. [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, pushes back with real reservations: doesn't think Smith is physical enough to consistently win tight coverage at the boundary, questioning whether he can separate and earn position there. Still calls him arguably "the best two in the league," while noting the field hasn't actually answered whether he can be a true WR1, since he's spent his whole career playing next to [[A.J. Brown]] in Philadelphia and "might be in Philly forever."

- 2024-04-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-15]]: on his new 3-year, $75M extension ($51M guaranteed) — reads it as Smith making "the smart" choice to commit long-term to a great situation rather than force a trade the way [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and [[Tee Higgins]] have been doing elsewhere. Views the perception that Philadelphia's passing game "wasn't great" in 2023 as more about inflated preseason expectations than an actual decline, with Smith playing a valuable, if secondary, role to [[A.J. Brown]] rather than being miscast as a true WR1.
- 2024-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-16]]): calls [[DeVonta Smith]]'s new 3-year, $75M extension ($51M guaranteed) fair value, and argues his rookie-year RP profile (84th-percentile success rate vs. press, 83rd vs. zone as a primary outside/X receiver) already proved he could be a true WR1 independent of [[A.J. Brown]], ranking him ahead of Michael Pittman Jr. and Calvin Ridley in the current WR market.
- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]): at equal price gives Smith only the slightest value edge over [[Jaylen Waddle]] because of Philadelphia's surrounding context (A.J. Brown opposite him, Jalen Hurts at QB), even while considering Waddle the technically better receiver; untracked co-host Bob Harris agrees, taking Smith at his current ADP discount.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Smith posted a career-low share of snaps outside and career-high share in the slot in 2023, and Harmon notes his Reception Perception metrics were down in what wasn't his best season. Harmon floats that new OC Kellen Moore could lean further into slot 'layup' looks for Smith if [[A.J. Brown]] instead takes over as the primary inside read -- the two hosts spend much of the segment debating which of the two ends up playing more inside under Moore, without a firm resolution.
- 2024-07-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-02]]) [Dynasty]: DeVonta Smith's 2023 season showed career-low success rates versus man, zone and press coverage, though each mark stayed within 2.5% of his career best and his press success rate landed at the 82nd percentile -- still elite -- so Harmon isn't 'raising alarm bells.' Individual routes told a starker story: a nearly 7% drop on nine routes (62.5% to 55%) and a 14% drop on dig routes (88% to 74%). Harmon now grades Smith as a 'tier three, maybe bottom of tier two' receiver rather than a true 1A/1B, comparing his up-and-down game to peak-vs-valley Amari Cooper, though he stresses Smith isn't that inconsistent. Smith set career highs in slot usage (25%) and off-line alignment (38.2%) in 2023, moving away from his rookie-year X-receiver role (86.1% outside, 77.4% on the line). Finished at 112 targets, 81 catches, 1,066 yards, 7 TDs, a career-high 72% catch rate. Harmon and co-host James Koh (untracked) expect new OC Kellen Moore to reinstall Smith as the boundary/X press-beater -- 78%+ success versus press in all three seasons, 90% success on slants -- while [[A.J. Brown]] handles the pre-snap motion/slot role, since Moore's past five offenses have each produced a top-20 fantasy WR.
- 2024-09-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-26]]): Smith was 'knocked out cold' with a scary-looking concussion in the Week 3 game against New Orleans, which opens up a starting role opposite [[A.J. Brown]] — himself also out. Harmon calls the resulting depth chart 'a dramatic fall-off,' going from Brown and Smith to [[Jahan Dotson]], [[Johnny Wilson]] and Paris Campbell. He does not question Smith's standing as a player; the take is entirely about availability and about the Eagles needing a credible third option to emerge while both starters are unavailable.
- 2024-10-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-15]]): responding to Koh's question (Koh is not tracked) about why Philadelphia cannot pair [[A.J. Brown]] winning go balls with Smith working the intermediate middle of the field, Harmon says he does not get it either — the offense is hard to figure out, they have a good first pitch because the players are stars, but [[Jalen Hurts]] is middling as a drop-back passer and Kellen Moore's motion-heavy scheme is, in his words, largely bait for stat nerds from a coordinator who has never built a great offense on his own. Philadelphia sits 15th in EPA per drop back, which both hosts call unacceptable with this roster, and no Eagles receiver outside the top two has a catch of 16-plus yards all season. Harmon flags an oil-and-water Moore/Hurts scenario as on his radar, not yet his conclusion, and would raise alarms only if the Week 8-12 stretch stays stuck. Koh calls Eagles passing assets a buy given the upcoming schedule.
- 2024-12-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-17]]): Smith drew 12 targets for 11 catches and 109 yards with a touchdown against a good Pittsburgh pass defense, including 33 yards and the score against man coverage. James Koh (not a tracked expert) framed the game as proof of concept — Philadelphia decided to throw to its two best players and both cleared 100 yards. Harmon's broader point applies to Smith as well as [[A.J. Brown]]: the offense demonstrably can do this against man-heavy defenses, and with Washington, New York and Dallas (all top-15 in man rate) remaining, leaning into it is the obvious path. The limiting factor is [[Jalen Hurts]] and a run-first identity, not the receivers.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-23]]) [Dynasty]: asked to tier the complementary number-two receivers, Harmon ranked Smith first ahead of [[Tee Higgins]], [[Jaylen Waddle]] and [[Jordan Addison]], saying he remains 'such a fan of his game' — the aggressiveness he plays with and separation ability that Harmon calls the best of the group. Crucially Harmon argues Smith is only a two by circumstance: if not for [[A.J. Brown]] he would be a number one, and he already was one as a rookie, playing pure outside X on essentially every snap and producing over 900 yards in an offense that took a while to find itself. Harmon noted all four of these receivers sit bunched together in the dynasty tiers he is currently refurbishing.
- 2025-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-05]]) and co-host James Koh (not tracked): this sets up as a big Smith game in Super Bowl LIX. Koh's case: Smith plays about 55% of his snaps in the slot, Kansas City gave up the second-most receptions and sixth-most receiving yards to inside receivers in 2024, and Trent McDuffie will be travelling with [[A.J. Brown]], leaving Smith against defenders he can work over. Harmon agrees and notes Smith holds up either way — 2.32 yards per route run vs zone coverage, close to Brown's 2.54, so a zone-heavy Chiefs plan does not kill him. Koh loves Smith at +600 for most receiving yards in the game, arguing he might have a coin-flip chance even as the No. 2 option, and both preferred the over on 48.5 receiving yards to the 4.5-reception line.
- 2025-02-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-11]]): Smith led the Eagles in receiving in Super Bowl LIX with 4 catches on 5 targets for 69 yards and a touchdown, the first Alabama player ever to score in a Super Bowl. Harmon and James Koh (not tracked) had pre-game flagged him as a slot matchup, and Harmon honestly grades that call as only half right — Smith managed just two catches for 17 yards and 0.94 yards per route run from the slot, with his long reception coming lined up outside. The structural edge was Kansas City shadowing [[A.J. Brown]] with Trent McDuffie, which left Smith against Jaylen Watson and lesser corners, a matchup Harmon says he is significantly better than. He praised the play call to take the deep shot to Smith for the dagger score rather than run clock.
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-13]]): [[DeVonta Smith]] played a much larger slot share than Harmon expected — 48.8% of snaps in the slot across the regular season and postseason, against 17% for [[A.J. Brown]] and 30.4% for [[Jahan Dotson]] — which he calls a surprise. Smith is locked up through 2028, and with Brown signed through 2029 Harmon does not imagine Philadelphia does anything meaningful at receiver this offseason beyond a possible fifth-round dart throw.
- 2025-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-08]]): Smith had a sneaky career year in 2024 on efficiency rather than raw totals, and Harmon thinks he goes underdiscussed in fantasy because he is permanently the two behind [[A.J. Brown]]. Usage changed sharply: after 13%, 18.4% and 25% slot rates in prior sampled seasons he jumped to 46% in the slot and a career-high 61.3% of snaps off the line of scrimmage, having never previously exceeded 40%. He responded with a 95th-percentile success rate versus man coverage and 91st percentile versus press, dominating in-breakers and deeper outs — 93.1% on the dig, 83.7% on slants, 82% on the post, 81.6% on the curl. Harmon credits Kellen Moore with weaponizing him inside and says that is exactly why [[Jahan Dotson]] never got the slot role.
- 2025-08-14 — According to Sigmund Bloom of Footballguys (not a tracked expert), speaking with [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Smith is where the real value sits if Philadelphia is the offense the market has wrong. He was a third-round pick last year and now goes in the fourth or fifth, with [[A.J. Brown]] sliding from late first to mid-second and [[Jalen Hurts]] from QB3 to roughly QB4 behind [[Jayden Daniels]]. Bloom argues everything 'ran pure' for the Eagles last season — he cites the stat of roughly 53 second-half passes all year when trailing — and that there are far more ways for them to have to throw more than to repeat as one of the lowest pass-rate teams in the league. He accepts the hamstring injuries to both receivers explain part of the fade but thinks the drop is too steep, and says people dismiss Smith as 'just the other guy' when the moment volume returns he will make plays. Harris called it a really good point.
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-20]]): Smith is his number one 'best number two receiver' — the guy he would pick if one of this group had to be a true number one. Charting: 95th-percentile success versus man and 91st versus press in 2024, both repeated across multiple seasons, 81.5% versus zone, 82% success on the post, 93% on the dig at nearly 12% route participation (Harmon calls that 'mind melting'), and an 85.7% contested catch rate on low volume, the step forward he had been waiting for. He also ranks 17th in yardage on 20-plus air yard passes since 2022 at 5.5 yards per route run there, so not a negative downfield. Harmon takes the over on an 850.5 receiving-yard line, calling it near his floor: he cleared 850 in three straight years before 2024 (nearly 1,200 in 2022, ~1,100 in 2023), 833 last year despite missing time in the worst-case Philly passing environment, and [[A.J. Brown]]'s hamstring gives further upside. He acknowledges the 'Tee Higgins bros' would take downfield size instead, and that Smith, [[Tee Higgins]] and [[Chris Godwin]] can be flipped one through three without much argument.
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: reading his Almanac profile, Harris says the summer hate on Smith may go too far. Smith lost one game to concussion and two to hamstring in 2024 yet finished 17th in fantasy points per game, though he had a stretch of seven games failing to top four grabs in six of them and rescued the season with eight touchdowns — the 'second banana' punished for a so-so year. Harris argues 2024 is about the worst his usage can get and still be usable, that he is an exceptionally fast, accomplished deep threat who benefited from Eagles pick plays in the red area, and that a repeat of his 95-1,207 season is live if Philadelphia needs a deep outside threat; he is roughly a round higher than market and would take him in round four. Guest Scott Pianowski (Yahoo, not tracked) makes Smith his round-five safe pick, expecting Philadelphia's extreme pass rate over expected after the bye to regress and target concentration to stay narrow behind [[A.J. Brown]] — 'definitely a par, with birdie potential'. Both concede the bear case: 900 yards and four touchdowns instead of eight.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Smith was never targeted down the field in the opener — 'three shorties, 16 yards' — part of a night where the Philadelphia outside receivers simply weren't involved, with the passing game reduced to several two-yard throws to [[Dallas Goedert]] and one bomb to [[Jahan Dotson]]. Harris expects things to get better, and thinks they might have already had the thunderstorm delay not shut down all scoring, but ties the outside-receiver problem to [[Jalen Hurts]]' habit of pulling the ball down and to Philadelphia's refusal to run play action against a defense selling out on [[Saquon Barkley]].
- 2025-09-12 — According to guest cousin Josh (untracked) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-12]]: Smith is his number two, a negative change of mind — he had been in on Smith for years and had him a low WR2, and is now 'real down'. His concern is offensive shape rather than talent: he expected Philadelphia to be run-heavy but closer to 500 pass attempts than 400, and Nick Sirianni is signalling game-plan-specific, low-volume passing. He is less worried about [[A.J. Brown]] because that talent is super elite and he knows who gets fed; he does not see how Smith survives as a WR2 or even a WR3 on six targets a game, and would now take [[Rome Odunze]] and a healthy [[Jordan Addison]] above him. [[Chris Harris]] pushes back without saying Josh is wrong: he has lowered Smith slightly from his season-long ranks but will not overreact to a weird game in Dallas where everything was dump-offs and [[Jahan Dotson]] went 3-59. His counter is that Smith averaged seven targets a game last year and was clearly at least a WR3, and a WR2 when available; he was hurt and missed a month.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-23]]): Smith was the co-beneficiary when Philadelphia finally opened up in Week 3 — he and [[A.J. Brown]] combined for 14 catches, 169 yards and two touchdowns on 19 targets, with 11 of those receptions and 136 of those yards coming on throws outside the numbers, which Harmon called the classic [[Jalen Hurts]] profile since Hurts does not really attack the middle of the field. Harmon's caveat is that this was not a schematic change but simply a decision to throw, quoting Hurts saying he 'just got the ball' in the second half, so the volatility remains: the Eagles' philosophical preference for winning ugly means these outbursts are not something Harmon expects to hold week to week.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Smith went off partly because he drew Riley Moss rather than Patrick Surtain — 'whether he's bad or just not as good as Patrick Surtain, the ball is going to find' him. Guest **Sam Gold** (NOT a tracked expert) says the Eagles clearly game-planned to get Smith open: multiple screen calls for free yards and free receptions that raise his floor underneath, plus crossing-route concepts using other players as decoys so he could sit between zones. Harris reads it as a response to receiver complaints — Smith complained and got 12 targets while [[A.J. Brown]] complained and nothing happened — and both note the oddity of Philadelphia throwing 39 times and handing off nine while leading 17-3 in the third. Gold still won't rank Smith ahead of Brown yet.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-07]]): Smith went for 114 yards on 10 targets against Denver while playing 66.7% of his snaps in the slot, and Harmon's in-game overreaction was to wonder aloud whether Smith is now the best receiver on the Eagles. He is coming off a career-best Reception Perception season and career-best yards per route run in 2024 — a season enabled by [[A.J. Brown]] taking more X reps so Smith could move around — and still looks awesome this year. Harmon contrasts the two directly: Philadelphia continues to deploy Smith with intentional, varied alignment while giving Brown no easy looks, which is part of why he suspects something is off with Brown specifically rather than the whole passing game. He notes Smith has also been frustrated at times, as has most of Philadelphia, with an offense he thinks is far worse than its personnel.
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-10-09]]): Smith is the Eagle actually playing good football in isolation — he was third in success rate versus man coverage among all players charted in 2024, has two yards per route run against man this year and seven targets for 74 yards recently, and Philadelphia is scheming him the ball including from the slot. Harmon's FanDuel pick was Smith over 50.5 receiving yards (minus-114) against a man-heavy Giants secondary. He also noted Smith is never named in any of the team's target politics, unlike [[A.J. Brown]]. James Koh (not tracked) agreed and added that [[Saquon Barkley]]'s knee issue makes last week's passing volume less fluky.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): Smith sat on three catches for 33 yards until the decisive play of the game, a post from the slot on which 'he goes up, he gets it - 120 pounds and now my knee hurts', putting the Eagles up 10. Harris is careful that this was not designed volume: the ball went to the deep post because of how the defense played, not because Philadelphia was drawing up plays specifically for Smith or [[A.J. Brown]], which is the same complaint he lodges about the Eagles' third-and-long give-up draws to [[Will Shipley]].
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-13]]): Smith has been the more productive Eagle dating back to roughly mid-November of last year — despite [[A.J. Brown]] having more targets across that span through the playoff run and into Week 10, Smith has more catches and yards, at over 2.2 yards per route run to Brown's roughly 1.9. Harmon says Smith looks far better on film this year and is climbing into top-ten NFL receiver territory, calling it a genuine ascension rather than just volume. Koh's read is that the team has mentally moved on from Brown and is deliberately pumping Smith. Harmon's caveat is that he does not love the Eagles' offensive design post-Kellen Moore and thinks the offense has few answers outside Smith, which is problematic for a 7-2 team.
