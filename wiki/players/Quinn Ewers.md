# Quinn Ewers

## Expert Takes

- 2025-03-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-31]]) [Dynasty]: Ewers sits on the same line of demarcation as [[Kyle McCord]] between developing into a starter and a backup career, and Waldman expects his recruiting profile, Texas pedigree and solid fundamentals to earn enough draft capital to buy a starting opportunity on his rookie deal. The technique is largely there: conceptual and technical control of his footwork, flush/roll/hitch-based climbs out of compressing pockets, patience in a tight pocket, economy against backside edge pressure, pinpoint accuracy in the short and vertical ranges, accurate intermediate throws layered over a linebacker, and preemptive early climbs that buy him time to slow his pace and drop in a touch pass. The limits are conceptual. His black zone wisdom is missing — backed up inside his own 20 he hunts an extra read after already beating one point of pressure with that side's routes exhausted, when it is time to throw it away. He has progression lapses when edge pressure forms and misses the flat linebacker positioned to cut off the crosser he is waiting on. His pocket clock is effective but must accelerate when few receivers work to a heavily zoned side, and he needs better pre-snap identification of blindside corner blitzes. Waldman: absolutely a backup, very possibly a starter.
- 2025-04-11 — According to guest Cory Kinnan of Daft on Draft (not a tracked expert), on [[Matt Harmon]]'s show ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-11]]): he would not draft Ewers at all and does not understand the day-two buzz. Three years as a starter produced three injuries — collarbone, shoulder and core (plus a stated oblique issue in 2024) — and Kinnan says the drive has visibly left the football, questioning whether the slender frame can hold up. He argues Steve Sarkisian's offense is as quarterback-friendly as it gets (heavy pre-snap motion and eye candy, the scheme that made Mac Jones a first-rounder off arguably the most efficient college season ever), yet Ewers checks down as designed clear-outs open up, and shows bad pocket habits — sitting seven yards deep against Georgia with Kelvin Banks unable to hold his block. 2024 line: ~66% completions, ~3,500 yards, 31 TD, 12 INT, with several interceptions Harmon calls Garoppolo-esque. Kinnan says he will not fabricate a grade to meet consensus, and when pushed for a best case offers Jimmy Garoppolo — good in exactly one ecosystem — or a poor man's [[Derek Carr]]. [[Matt Harmon]] adds that while charting [[Adonai Mitchell]], [[Xavier Worthy]], [[Matthew Golden]] and [[Isaiah Bond]] the quarterback play was visibly bad and plays were left on the field, and wonders how Ewers held off Arch Manning.
