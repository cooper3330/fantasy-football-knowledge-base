# Jonathan Taylor

## Expert Takes

- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman says Taylor 'absolutely could' regain his RB1-overall form because [[Anthony Richardson]]'s rushing threat should force defenses to spread attention, opening creases that could make Taylor 'the most efficient running back in football' and produce big games. The caveat: red-zone touchdown equity could be at risk if Indianapolis starts vulturing goal-line carries with Richardson the way Philadelphia has with Jalen Hurts and Buffalo has with Josh Allen, which would cap Taylor's ceiling even in an efficient season. Waldman ranks him ahead of [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] but behind [[Breece Hall]] and [[Bijan Robinson]] in this group of RB1-overall candidates.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) reported Taylor suffered another high ankle sprain, described as mild, ahead of a Jaguars matchup, and voiced doubt that high ankle sprains are ever mild. Waldman did not dispute the injury framing and moved straight to the replacement question, endorsing [[Trey Sermon]] and [[Tyler Goodson]] as low-end stopgaps in the Jacksonville matchup rather than arguing for a quick Taylor return.
- 2024-12-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-23]]): he 'feels it' that Taylor is as talented as [[Saquon Barkley]] — just as fast, more powerful, better at playing both in and out of structure, a very good receiver, and a better processor of information coming into the league (which Waldman thinks is still true). Barkley is the more creative runner and the better downfield receiving option, but Waldman notes downfield receiving for a back only shows up once every several games. The difference in output, he argues, is line play: Taylor simply hasn't had as good a line, and give him a crease and he is the equal. He concedes that if you argue purely from box-score stats, Barkley is the easy answer — and pre-empts the 'you're an idiot' replies. Format-neutral talent take, relevant to [[Dynasty]] valuation gaps created by team context.
- 2025-06-13 — According to co-host James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-13]]; Koh is not a tracked expert): Taylor is the forgotten man in every Colts conversation — the discussion is always the receivers, the quarterback situation and the head coach — and 'on a legitimate team could legitimately run for 2,000 yards'. [[Matt Harmon]] agreed the Colts will likely be run-heavy, expecting a lot of RPOs and quarterback run game with [[Daniel Jones]] under center, much as they would have been with [[Anthony Richardson]].
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates Taylor a 1 out of 10 at ADP 19 — a back who could rush for 2,000 yards, and the [[Daniel Jones]] situation cuts both ways: 'don't let Daniel Jones throw the ball, just give it to Jonathan Taylor', the same logic as Philadelphia's. Harris rates him a 4 on availability alone, noting Taylor seems to miss time every year and gets hurt more consistently than most, and countering Bell's QB argument with the years [[Saquon Barkley]] spent below his peak with Jones. Harris still allows Bell's case, since Taylor was the overall 1.01 and the number one fantasy player two years prior.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): Taylor and [[Tyler Warren]] are 'looking like must starts every single week' and are 'the engine for Indianapolis' after the Colts beat a Denver defense Harris rates as one of the league's best. Taylor ran a wheel route for a 40-yard gain into the 10 in the second quarter, though he was stuffed twice at the goal line before the Colts sneaked [[Daniel Jones]] in instead, cost his managers there; he later got a screen touchdown before halftime, and after Denver's interception deep in Colts territory he made a 60-yard run happen essentially on his own, weaving through half the defense. Harris credits [[Shane Steichen]]'s play-calling with tying the whole attack together now that it is not saddled with a fossilized veteran or [[Anthony Richardson]].
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Going Deep Listener Q&A)]]): Angelo says his single biggest takeaway from Indianapolis is that Taylor is playing really good football and will be the rock of the offense, full stop. Waldman goes further and calls it All-Pro-level play. Waldman adds a film note that Taylor's gravity is part of why [[Tyler Warren]] is getting open — on at least one throw two defenders keyed on Taylor in the same area, collapsing on him and leaving Warren wide open on an intermediate route. Both frame the Colts as a good team rather than a great one, with Taylor as the most trustworthy piece and [[Daniel Jones]] the part they doubt.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Taylor's 46-yard third-quarter touchdown run was his favorite run of the week — he counted five defenders made to miss with moves and strength — and he mocked the old pre-draft argument that Taylor's college workload was too big. Guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not tracked) called it the run of the year so far: traditional broken tackles, then a spin-move broken tackle, then a high-step broken tackle, 'emptying the cupboard all in one move,' and said when healthy Taylor is the best pure running back in the NFL. The shared caveat is durability — Harris has drafted him less in recent years because he prefers backs who appear in more than 14 games.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): Taylor finally got the Colts going in the fourth quarter after a stagnant second half, and produced what Harris calls an unbelievable, insane 53-yard touchdown run that you have to watch — except it did not count, wiped out by an [[Adonai Mitchell]] hold that Harris says was a real hold but did not really help Taylor. Indianapolis punted, then lost on a [[Daniel Jones]] interception. A reminder that the box score badly understates his day.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Indianapolis came out throwing against the Raiders, but Taylor 'would, of course, as you know, definitely pay off' once they got ahead — after a blocked punt inside the Vegas 10 the plan turned to more Taylor, and he drew interference on a pass and scored on a third-down lead draw for 14-3. With the lead the third quarter tilted toward Taylor and the run for another score at 26-3, and he added a close-in touchdown late before the backups came in. Harris frames the whole Colts offense as increasingly believable week by week, even allowing that the Raiders offered little resistance.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Taylor scored three touchdowns breaking loose in the high red zone as Indianapolis dominated the Chargers; Harris said [[Daniel Jones]] was 'unlucky' not to produce more fantasy points himself specifically because Taylor kept finishing off the drives Jones set up.
- 2025-10-24 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: calls Jonathan Taylor the 'easy' and fully defensible choice for midseason fantasy MVP, noting he leads all players in VBD scoring despite an ADP of only 21 overall -- 'if Taylor is your MVP, 100%, he's been amazing.' Harris ultimately gives his own MVP award to [[Javonte Williams]] instead on a pure-value basis, but treats Taylor's case as equally legitimate on production alone.
- 2025-10-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-28]]): Taylor delivered another dominant game against Tennessee, including a touchdown run combining 'side-to-side quickness and north-south acceleration' and an 80-yard score on the Colts' first play of the second half where he broke up the sideline and made defenders take bad angles; he also scored a third time on a pop pass before Indianapolis's starters sat. Harris jokes about pre-draft workload concerns ('remember when he had too many carries in college to be a first-round pick') given how effortless the Colts offense continues to look with him.
- 2025-10-31 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-31]]): Taylor is cited as the league's overall RB1 anchoring Indianapolis's surprise 7-1 offense alongside [[Daniel Jones]] and [[Michael Pittman Jr.]]; Harris flags a tough upcoming stretch (Atlanta, at Kansas City, Houston, then a Week 1-fantasy-playoffs trip to Seattle) as the first real test of the unit.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): Pittsburgh rendered Taylor useless until they had built a lead, doing to him what they had earlier done to [[Quinshon Judkins]] — Bob Harris (Football Guys, not a tracked expert) noted a season-low 45 rushing yards with Taylor hit at or behind the line of scrimmage on six of 14 carries. Waldman framed it as a gap-sound, run-first defensive game plan rather than a Taylor problem, and said the knock-on effect was that Indianapolis abandoned the easy portion of its offense and asked [[Daniel Jones]] to win from behind the sticks.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-04]]): Taylor managed 16 touches for 57 scrimmage yards in Pittsburgh and even lost a fourth-down touchdown to a [[Daniel Jones]] quarterback sneak — an unexpectedly brutal result in a very poor day for what had been an elite offense. Harris's reaction is to buy: if anything, trade for Jonathan Taylor, he is going to be fine.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-10]]): the good vastly outweighs the bad — 244 rushing yards and three touchdowns including the overtime game-winner against Atlanta, the most rushing yards in a game this season, and 15 rushing touchdowns through 10 games, the most since LaDainian Tomlinson in 2006. Waldman still lodged a critique that enraged Colts fans: the pass protection needs work, as Taylor overextended and effectively allowed two sacks, and with only [[Daniel Jones]] behind him keeping the quarterback upright matters. He framed it as the nitpick it is — a great all-around player having a great season — and noted the same criticism would have been the story had the Colts lost the game rather than won it on a walk-off touchdown.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): the overtime win over Atlanta in Germany was 'pure and free, uncut, unadulterated' Taylor, and reinforces why he has been Harris's number one rest-of-season back since the beginning of October. He fumbled a screen on the opening drive (recovered by a lineman) and was stuffed on a fourth-down pitch late in the third, but the Colts then simply handed him the game: six carries for 113 yards in the fourth quarter alone, including a 'god mode' bounce outside for a home run, then six more carries for 34 in overtime. Harris notes six of the Colts' seven offensive snaps in overtime were Taylor runs, including the winner.
- 2025-12-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-12]]): Harris made Taylor his 2026 RB3, noting he's fully healthy after past lower-body injury issues, on pace for roughly 375 touches (versus 320+ last year in fewer games), and still just 26. He credited an improved Colts offensive line -- Alec Pierce and Ty Warren emerging, Pittman solid -- for making Taylor's workload sustainable even with mediocre quarterback play (whether that's Daniel Jones, Phillip Rivers, or Riley Leonard). Both hosts noted Taylor was outright dominating the league (leading rushing yards and TDs) until a rougher three-week stretch against the Chiefs, Texans and Jaguars, and Harris argued that softer-schedule context is itself an endorsement of keeping Taylor in the RB1-3 tier rather than a red flag. Cousin Josh agreed Taylor is 'arguable to be the one' by name. [Dynasty] [Best Ball]
- 2025-12-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-15]]): Taylor is 'always in your lineup no matter what,' logging 28 touches against Seattle (versus 9 for [[Ameer Abdullah]] and roughly 10 more spread across every other skill weapon) as Indianapolis leaned heavily on the run to keep its quarterback's workload manageable in a close, low-scoring game against a good Seattle defense.
- 2025-12-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-23]]): even with the Colts eliminated from playoff contention and possibly turning to Riley Leonard at quarterback, Harris says that's not a reason to bench Taylor -- 'it doesn't mean you're sitting Jonathan Taylor or anything dumb like that.'
- 2026-01-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Taylor was a top fantasy MVP candidate for the season overall but was 'a little disappointing' once the Colts got banged up. Cousin Josh (guest co-host, not tracked) added detail in the parallel Baggy Awards discussion: Taylor finished RB2 in standard scoring, 0.6 points ahead of [[Christian McCaffrey]], but 'fizzled' at the end of the year, playing more like a low-RB1/high-RB2 down the stretch of the fantasy playoffs versus McCaffrey's strong finish (16.5/29.5/27.7). Both hosts agreed the larger point was that hitting on the round-one running back pick, Taylor or McCaffrey, was what separated winning rosters in 2025.
- 2026-01-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-08]]): Taylor was fantasy RB1 through the first 14 weeks before collapsing to RB21 from Week 15 on, costing many fantasy title runs. Harris and guest Jim McCormick (untracked) attribute the fall mainly to Indianapolis' quarterback situation falling apart late in the season rather than any decline in Taylor himself, joking that the Colts' front office (Jeff Saturday, an aging Philip Rivers) makes anything believable about the franchise's direction. McCormick says as long as Indianapolis has any competent quarterback play, Taylor should be fine, and both agree he shouldn't be meaningfully punished in redraft rankings for how the season ended; he remains a projected top-five pick for 2026.
- 2026-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-28]]): Taylor finished a distant second in the NFL in touches last year (369) behind only Christian McCaffrey's 413, finished 2nd among running backs in fantasy points, and posted 1,963 yards from scrimmage -- trailing only McCaffrey and Bijan Robinson (both over 2,000) -- despite just 55 targets versus 103 for Robinson and 129 for McCaffrey, underscoring how much of his production was pure rushing. Through his first 10 games (Colts 8-2), he was on pace for 1,936 rushing yards, 442 receiving yards and 29 total touchdowns, capped by a 244-yard, 3-touchdown Week 10 game, before Daniel Jones' injury derailed the offense. Harmon expects another workload near 369 touches and calls Taylor the Colts' most important offensive player, but flags the risk that backs rarely sustain that level of usage year over year outside Derrick Henry-type outliers, and hopes [[DJ Giddens]] or [[Seth McGowan]] can siphon some volume off him.
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-22 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: dismisses the idea Taylor's workload will be significantly cut, reading the coaching staff's public 'we're aware of his workload' comments as an intent to involve backups DJ Giddens and Jarquez Hunter modestly, not a real reduction. Waldman compares Taylor to Christian McCaffrey in not wanting time off, noting Taylor is most effective on a full workload where defenders wear down late in games. Bob Harris (untracked co-host) agrees, predicting the team gives Taylor 'more shit, not less,' pointing to Rachaad White's 2023 usage pattern as the model for how a bell-cow back keeps his volume even amid public 'load management' talk.
- 2026-07-10 — According to guest Dave Kluge (footballguys.com, untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-10]]): Kluge highlights that Jonathan Taylor was the top-scoring fantasy RB in games [[Daniel Jones]] played healthy last season, but only a borderline RB3 in games Jones missed. Even in a worst-case Daniel Jones setback scenario, Kluge says he wouldn't drop Taylor further than the middle of the second round, given the volume he'd still command on a run-funneling Colts offense.
- 2026-07-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: despite public talk about scaling back Taylor after he led the NFL with 323 carries last season and racked up 1,400-1,600 rushing yards two years running, Waldman dismisses any real cutback -- 'the odds are... fuck that.' He expects Indianapolis to lean hard on Taylor early in the season while [[Alec Pierce]] recovers and receiver depth stays thin, comparing the situation to San Francisco's annual reluctance to actually rest its workhorse backs despite stated intentions. Waldman says it would take a strong camp performance from whoever wins the RB2 job -- he leans toward [[Seth McGowan]] over [[DJ Giddens]] -- to meaningfully cut into Taylor's workload, and even then he'd hate to see Taylor's touches sacrificed just to get a change-of-pace back involved in the passing game.
- 2026-08-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-07]]): Taylor is signed and in camp along with [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and [[Bijan Robinson]], closing out the hold-in concern Dave Richard raised earlier in the week. Harris reiterates all three are surefire first-round picks for 2026 drafts.
- 2026-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Cousin Josh (untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-14]]): Harris gave Taylor a 4, Josh a 5, the first player on the board where Josh rated riskier than Harris. Josh cites 'Colts bad vibes' after Daniel Jones left and likens the situation to 2025 Saquon Barkley-style uncertainty, plus a workload that swung 11/10/14 games across recent seasons; Harris counters that Taylor is risky specifically for injury, not for anything around him, and notes he just signed a new contract. Both frame him as safe from a talent/opportunity standpoint but genuinely game-missed-dependent.
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-20]]): Jonathan Taylor is 'still great either way,' but runs more efficiently under center than out of shotgun, and Indy's heavy shotgun rate (driven partly by Daniel Jones) is a real drag on his overall run-game efficiency; more 11-personnel/quick passing out of the gun could offset this somewhat for the passing-game pieces around him.
- 2026-08-27 — According to Alfredo Brown, guest analyst (not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-27]]): Brown highlights Taylor's checkdown work in [[Daniel Jones]]'s offense as more than incidental -- Shane Steichen's scheme used Taylor as an intentional option-style outlet even when the primary read (often [[Alec Pierce]] deep) wasn't there, and Taylor turned some of those checkdowns into 40-50 yard gains. Brown calls him 'incredibly talented' and notes he was on an 'unbelievable' run to start last season before the passing offense cooled against tougher defenses.
- 2026-09-04 — Cousin Josh (untracked co-host, [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-09-04]]) named Taylor his 2026 'stealth trash bag' despite Taylor having won him a title last year -- currently going in the first half of the first round. Josh compares Taylor's 2025 to Saquon Barkley's 'perfect ride' the year before: three prior seasons averaging only 11-12 games, then a fully healthy, career-best year that he thinks is unlikely to repeat. He flagged a brutal schedule to open the season (Ravens, Texans, Chiefs), noted Taylor has historically struggled when the Colts trail, and cited concerns about the Colts' QB situation (Daniel Jones' Achilles recovery, DWYs for Anthony Richardson, [[Isaiah Pacheco]]... [sic, referring to another Colts RB finally practicing]) and general 'bad vibes' about the team, including a personal bet on the Colts' win total to go under.
- 2026-09-11 — According to Jim McCormick (untracked guest) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-09-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: As his #2 preseason 'freak out,' Jim McCormick admitted he has almost no shares of the Colts offense -- no [[Jonathan Taylor]], no [[Tyler Warren]], only a cheap [[Josh Downs]] share -- despite the team being close to the league's best offense for a stretch last year even with Gardner Minshew at quarterback. He called last year's version 'a mirage' that isn't fully sustainable, but worries he 'completely overlooked' a real fantasy asset, particularly Taylor, whom nobody drafted at a premium price relative to the offense's quality. Chris Harris agreed that anyone who believed in the Colts last summer could have bought most of that offense at non-premium prices except for Taylor himself.
