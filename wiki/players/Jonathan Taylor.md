# Jonathan Taylor

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman says Taylor 'absolutely could' regain his RB1-overall form because [[Anthony Richardson]]'s rushing threat should force defenses to spread attention, opening creases that could make Taylor 'the most efficient running back in football' and produce big games. The caveat: red-zone touchdown equity could be at risk if Indianapolis starts vulturing goal-line carries with Richardson the way Philadelphia has with Jalen Hurts and Buffalo has with Josh Allen, which would cap Taylor's ceiling even in an efficient season. Waldman ranks him ahead of [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] but behind [[Breece Hall]] and [[Bijan Robinson]] in this group of RB1-overall candidates.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) reported Taylor suffered another high ankle sprain, described as mild, ahead of a Jaguars matchup, and voiced doubt that high ankle sprains are ever mild. Waldman did not dispute the injury framing and moved straight to the replacement question, endorsing [[Trey Sermon]] and [[Tyler Goodson]] as low-end stopgaps in the Jacksonville matchup rather than arguing for a quick Taylor return.
- 2024-12-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-23]]): he 'feels it' that Taylor is as talented as [[Saquon Barkley]] — just as fast, more powerful, better at playing both in and out of structure, a very good receiver, and a better processor of information coming into the league (which Waldman thinks is still true). Barkley is the more creative runner and the better downfield receiving option, but Waldman notes downfield receiving for a back only shows up once every several games. The difference in output, he argues, is line play: Taylor simply hasn't had as good a line, and give him a crease and he is the equal. He concedes that if you argue purely from box-score stats, Barkley is the easy answer — and pre-empts the 'you're an idiot' replies. Format-neutral talent take, relevant to [[Dynasty]] valuation gaps created by team context.
- 2025-06-13 — According to co-host James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-13]]; Koh is not a tracked expert): Taylor is the forgotten man in every Colts conversation — the discussion is always the receivers, the quarterback situation and the head coach — and 'on a legitimate team could legitimately run for 2,000 yards'. [[Matt Harmon]] agreed the Colts will likely be run-heavy, expecting a lot of RPOs and quarterback run game with [[Daniel Jones]] under center, much as they would have been with [[Anthony Richardson]].
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates Taylor a 1 out of 10 at ADP 19 — a back who could rush for 2,000 yards, and the [[Daniel Jones]] situation cuts both ways: 'don't let Daniel Jones throw the ball, just give it to Jonathan Taylor', the same logic as Philadelphia's. Harris rates him a 4 on availability alone, noting Taylor seems to miss time every year and gets hurt more consistently than most, and countering Bell's QB argument with the years [[Saquon Barkley]] spent below his peak with Jones. Harris still allows Bell's case, since Taylor was the overall 1.01 and the number one fantasy player two years prior.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): Taylor and [[Tyler Warren]] are 'looking like must starts every single week' and are 'the engine for Indianapolis' after the Colts beat a Denver defense Harris rates as one of the league's best. Taylor ran a wheel route for a 40-yard gain into the 10 in the second quarter, though he was stuffed twice at the goal line before the Colts sneaked [[Daniel Jones]] in instead, cost his managers there; he later got a screen touchdown before halftime, and after Denver's interception deep in Colts territory he made a 60-yard run happen essentially on his own, weaving through half the defense. Harris credits [[Shane Steichen]]'s play-calling with tying the whole attack together now that it is not saddled with a fossilized veteran or [[Anthony Richardson]].
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Going Deep Listener Q&A)]]): Angelo says his single biggest takeaway from Indianapolis is that Taylor is playing really good football and will be the rock of the offense, full stop. Waldman goes further and calls it All-Pro-level play. Waldman adds a film note that Taylor's gravity is part of why [[Tyler Warren]] is getting open — on at least one throw two defenders keyed on Taylor in the same area, collapsing on him and leaving Warren wide open on an intermediate route. Both frame the Colts as a good team rather than a great one, with Taylor as the most trustworthy piece and [[Daniel Jones]] the part they doubt.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Taylor's 46-yard third-quarter touchdown run was his favorite run of the week — he counted five defenders made to miss with moves and strength — and he mocked the old pre-draft argument that Taylor's college workload was too big. Guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not tracked) called it the run of the year so far: traditional broken tackles, then a spin-move broken tackle, then a high-step broken tackle, 'emptying the cupboard all in one move,' and said when healthy Taylor is the best pure running back in the NFL. The shared caveat is durability — Harris has drafted him less in recent years because he prefers backs who appear in more than 14 games.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): Taylor finally got the Colts going in the fourth quarter after a stagnant second half, and produced what Harris calls an unbelievable, insane 53-yard touchdown run that you have to watch — except it did not count, wiped out by an [[Adonai Mitchell]] hold that Harris says was a real hold but did not really help Taylor. Indianapolis punted, then lost on a [[Daniel Jones]] interception. A reminder that the box score badly understates his day.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Indianapolis came out throwing against the Raiders, but Taylor 'would, of course, as you know, definitely pay off' once they got ahead — after a blocked punt inside the Vegas 10 the plan turned to more Taylor, and he drew interference on a pass and scored on a third-down lead draw for 14-3. With the lead the third quarter tilted toward Taylor and the run for another score at 26-3, and he added a close-in touchdown late before the backups came in. Harris frames the whole Colts offense as increasingly believable week by week, even allowing that the Raiders offered little resistance.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): Pittsburgh rendered Taylor useless until they had built a lead, doing to him what they had earlier done to [[Quinshon Judkins]] — Bob Harris (Football Guys, not a tracked expert) noted a season-low 45 rushing yards with Taylor hit at or behind the line of scrimmage on six of 14 carries. Waldman framed it as a gap-sound, run-first defensive game plan rather than a Taylor problem, and said the knock-on effect was that Indianapolis abandoned the easy portion of its offense and asked [[Daniel Jones]] to win from behind the sticks.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-04]]): Taylor managed 16 touches for 57 scrimmage yards in Pittsburgh and even lost a fourth-down touchdown to a [[Daniel Jones]] quarterback sneak — an unexpectedly brutal result in a very poor day for what had been an elite offense. Harris's reaction is to buy: if anything, trade for Jonathan Taylor, he is going to be fine.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-10]]): the good vastly outweighs the bad — 244 rushing yards and three touchdowns including the overtime game-winner against Atlanta, the most rushing yards in a game this season, and 15 rushing touchdowns through 10 games, the most since LaDainian Tomlinson in 2006. Waldman still lodged a critique that enraged Colts fans: the pass protection needs work, as Taylor overextended and effectively allowed two sacks, and with only [[Daniel Jones]] behind him keeping the quarterback upright matters. He framed it as the nitpick it is — a great all-around player having a great season — and noted the same criticism would have been the story had the Colts lost the game rather than won it on a walk-off touchdown.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): the overtime win over Atlanta in Germany was 'pure and free, uncut, unadulterated' Taylor, and reinforces why he has been Harris's number one rest-of-season back since the beginning of October. He fumbled a screen on the opening drive (recovered by a lineman) and was stuffed on a fourth-down pitch late in the third, but the Colts then simply handed him the game: six carries for 113 yards in the fourth quarter alone, including a 'god mode' bounce outside for a home run, then six more carries for 34 in overtime. Harris notes six of the Colts' seven offensive snaps in overtime were Taylor runs, including the winner.
