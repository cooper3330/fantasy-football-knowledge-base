# Devin Singletary

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: **feeling it** — recommended Singletary over the Kansas City backs for a manager choosing between them. His read is that Singletary had already taken the job during Dameon Pierce's return, forcing Houston to think about simply riding the hot hand. "The little engine that could" — keep rolling with him.
- 2023-12-18 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode: the usage settles it — 26 carries and 120+ rushing yards for Singletary against one carry for three yards for Dameon Pierce. "The Texans have made their vote clear."
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: a real reversal from the December take — no longer feeling him as Houston's 2024 starter. Cites the Texans' pattern of one-and-done lead backs (Arian Foster the lone exception; Dominic Williams, D'Onta Foreman and David Johnson all cited as cautionary examples) and expects Houston to add a free-agent back — ideally, in his view, whichever of [[Josh Jacobs]] or [[Derrick Henry]] loses out on Baltimore — to lead the room instead, assuming OC Bobby Slowik stays.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): a rough Divisional Round game against Baltimore — 9 carries for 22 yards, -25 rush yards over expected per Next Gen Stats — but Harmon frames it with real context rather than as a fresh indictment: once installed as the clear-cut starter over Dameon Pierce, Singletary strung together "quite a few really good games," averaging close to 5 yards/carry over a stretch when Baltimore itself was one of the league's worst run defenses (31st in rushing success rate allowed, Weeks 10-17). Independently corroborates Waldman's read that Houston still needs a real upgrade at the position, both as a runner and as a receiving-game contributor.
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: framed Singletary as the 'antithetical' player to the rest of the Giants' backfield — a proven, instinctive between-the-tackles processor added specifically because New York's scouting staff otherwise keeps drafting fast, gap-scheme 'grocery' runners (Tyrone Tracy, Gary Brightwell, Joshua Corbin) who process poorly between the tackles. He credited Singletary as a back who came out ahead post-draft, alongside Zamir White and Joe Mixon, without detailing their situations further.
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): fully endorses Singletary's roughly RB32/107 ADP price, arguing the Giants still need to prove they can run the ball and that funneling volume to [[Malik Nabers]] in the passing game only helps a proven, on-field back like Singletary. He called Singletary a 'safety valve' for drafters going wide-receiver-heavy early who still want a running back they're 'pretty sure' will be on the field, ahead of unproven competition (Tyrone Tracy Jr., Eric Gray, Deshaun Corbin). Bob Harris agreed with the framing.
- 2024-07-15 — According to Bob Harris (untracked co-host, [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Best Ball]: Harris said he owns a lot of Devin Singletary shares in ongoing best ball drafts based on anticipated volume, and noted that over the five years Singletary and [[Saquon Barkley]] have been in the league together, Singletary has more career rushing yards and a better yards-per-carry average. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed Singletary is in a good spot but expects [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] to cut into his workload enough to worry fantasy managers as camp progresses.
- 2024-09-25 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): Koh has been impressed with how Brian Daboll has leaned on Singletary over the Giants' last two games and credits the run game for improving the whole offensive ecosystem around [[Malik Nabers]]. His framing is that Singletary is a professional running back rather than a good one — his repeated line is that Singletary is the [[Darius Slayton]] of running backs — but he can run inside, run outside and catch out of the backfield, so he covers every job the position requires. Harmon does not push back but keeps his overall outlook on the Giants season pessimistic.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): in projecting who benefits from the [[Drew Lock]] switch, Waldman says Singletary is probably the best of the Giants running backs in the passing game, ahead of [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] despite Tracy's receiver background, because Tracy was neither a good pass catcher nor a good route runner coming out.
- 2025-10-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-28]]) [waiver wire, guest Scott Fish's #3, purely speculative]: Fish stashes Singletary purely as insurance in case [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] doesn't hold the job -- Singletary has had explosive-play flashes in his career and is a competent pass-catcher in what both hosts agree is a better Giants offense than last year's. Harris calls Singletary 'coach's catnip' for doing the little things right and says if the Giants sour on Tracy the way they did in September, Singletary is the kind of veteran they'd lean on -- but is clear this is bench-stash only, not a starting option.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-04]]): Singletary is his number three waiver add and Jeff Erickson of RotoWire (not a tracked expert) has him fifth at 5% rostered. Harris is blunt that this is a desperation move — he calls him Devin Single Carry, says he is not a particularly good player but is fine, tough and strong for his size, does not make mistakes, and is coaches' catnip, citing the year Houston was supposedly [[Dameon Pierce]]'s and turned into Singletary's. On the 49ers film Singletary out-snapped [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] 31 to 25 and had the fourth-quarter carries Harris remembers, though much of it was garbage time. Erickson likes him as the better runner of the two, with the Bears matchup ahead and possible passing-game work if Tracy is limited. Harris counters that the Bears are a winnable run defense but the Giants are an injured two-win team and not a preferred avenue for fantasy glory, and there is no justifying a big split between the two backs.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-06]]): Harris ranks Singletary AHEAD of [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] for Week 10 and calls him his choice as an emergency flex in a week gutted by Bengals and Cowboys byes, with a good matchup against the Bears. Guest Ben Gretch of Stealing Signals (not a tracked expert) nominated him first, noting Singletary out-snapped Tracy after [[Cam Skattebo]]'s injury, played all the snaps inside the 10, ran the 'fast to the flat' play out of the play-action bail-out, and was the lead blocker on [[Jackson Dart]]'s rushing TD. Gretch frames him as the veteran 'first rule, do no harm' back whose blocking and receiving fit an offense now centered on Dart, where last year's was centered on Tracy. Harris notes Andy Behrens (not tracked) still has Tracy ahead, so it is close.
- 2025-11-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-11-18]]): Singletary was on the field for all three Giants drives that got inside Green Bay's five-yard line and scored twice, confirming he's the clear goal-line back over [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]]. Harris calls him tough to chase given the touchdown role, but says his presence also makes Tracy hard to fully trust -- Harris still ranks Tracy ahead of Singletary for Week 12 on between-the-20s usage, while flagging that the picture could get reshuffled again once Jaxson Dart returns from concussion.
- 2025-12-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Singletary took over as the Giants' hot hand for two drives in the Monday loss to New England, ripping off five straight carries to get the offense settled, scoring on a wildcat snap in the fourth quarter, and looking like he had 'Wally Pipped' [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] in this game. With Tracy carted off late in the fourth quarter with a probable concussion and the Giants on a bye in week 14, Harris expects 'super desperate' fantasy managers to add Singletary and agrees he should be picked up wherever a team needs a running back, though he's wary the Patriots' run defense (already thin without Milton Williams) isn't elite, and he'd rather stash Singletary for a week 15 fantasy-playoff opener than trust him blindly. Guest John Daigle (Establish the Run, not tracked) gave Singletary only an honorable mention on his own top-five list for the same just-in-case reasoning.
- 2025-12-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: With Tyrone Tracy Jr. banged up, Harris says Singletary now makes his handcuff list despite New England's run defense not being great, and notes some owners might even consider dropping Tracy for him given the current health situation.
- 2025-12-09 — According to Chris Harris and Jeff Bell (Football Guys, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris's #3 Week 15 waiver add (25% rostered) -- projects roughly a 50/50 backfield split with Tyrone Tracy Jr. against a Washington defense Harris expects to be in 'Cancun mode' after Jayden Daniels's injury, with the Giants already playing with 'house money.' Bell calls it only an honorable mention, not a top-two add for him, and Harris agrees Singletary won't crack his top-30 running backs but is a viable 'emergency mode' Week 15 flex given his recent usage upside.
- 2025-12-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-16]]): [[Devin Singletary]] played only 16 offensive snaps to [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]]'s 51 against Washington, confirming Tracy as the clear lead back.
