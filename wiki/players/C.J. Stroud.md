# C.J. Stroud

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]: raises a partial discount without disputing the talent — "we look at C.J. Stroud and we think he's great, and he is," but Case Keenum stepping in and playing competently suggests some of the production belongs to the system that arrived from San Francisco with DeMeco Ryans and offensive coordinator Bobby Slowik. He was careful not to call Stroud a system quarterback, only to say the system itself seems to create offense.
- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]): agrees it's multi-causal — the Shanahan scheme helps, there's good young surrounding talent, and there's a novelty effect in that AFC defenses haven't seen this successful NFC scheme often enough to be familiar with its wrinkles. See [[Scheme vs Talent]].
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): a mild data point in Stroud's favor. Asked a Footballguys roundtable which of Dak Prescott, [[Lamar Jackson]], Stroud and [[Brock Purdy]] was the *most* scheme-dependent, Waldman's own answer was Jackson — he did not name Stroud, three days after conceding on the same podcast that Houston's system deserves real credit. Not a reversal, but he does not treat Stroud as the scheme-dependent one in that group.
- 2024-01-01 — Both hosts on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]] [Redraft (Standard)]: on the flip side of the Purdy comparison, Stroud's 2024 outlook hinges on Tank Dell's health, and both concede Houston's supporting cast "is not on the level of San Francisco's" — but neither treats the redraft gap between the two quarterbacks as more than a coin flip.
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]): a Wild Card "clinic" against Cleveland (16/21, 274 yards, 3 TD, 0 INT) that outweighed a strong pre-game statistical case for the Browns — "everything can be pointing you towards one team, but then the other team has C.J. Stroud." Both hosts explicitly rank him a top-5 NFL quarterback candidate (alongside Burrow, Herbert, Stafford), and push back on retroactively discounting the pick because his college coach "didn't win more" with him — arguing the generational-prospect label is unfairly reserved only for players already established in the league. Praise for OC Bobby Slowik's fuller offensive expression in the game is tempered by caution about crediting a young, unproven coordinator too much before defenses have adjusted.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): heading into the Divisional Round at Baltimore, both hosts say it's "easy" to place Stroud in a top-5 NFL quarterback tier alongside Burrow, Herbert and Stafford — Burrow excepted only for having a longer proof-of-concept track record. Notes Baltimore's defense, while excellent, has had a soft schedule of opposing quarterbacks all season (Trevor Lawrence, Joshua Dobbs, Geno Smith), tempering how much to read into its per-game numbers ahead of the Stroud test. Also flags Houston's diminished receiver room behind [[Nico Collins]] — [[Tank Dell]] and [[Noah Brown]] both out, [[Xavier Hutchinson]] barely used — as the live variable for how much production Stroud can generate against a much better defense than Cleveland's.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): the predicted matchup problem materialized — Stroud "kind of sort of looked like a rookie," confused at times and unable to connect once Baltimore took [[Nico Collins]] away, with no counterpunch from the rest of the receiver room. Framed as a structural mismatch rather than a talent verdict: Baltimore's disguised, mixed-coverage scheme (heavy cover-6 usage) is "uniquely positioned" to give any young quarterback trouble, "even one of the all-time great rookie quarterbacks" — the first real statistical bad game logged on Stroud's page, alongside otherwise-unchanged top-5 praise.

- 2024-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-04]]), on Houston's trade for [[Stefon Diggs]]: calls the resulting receiver trio ([[Nico Collins]], [[Tank Dell]], Diggs) an "embarrassment of riches" and arguably the best in the NFL alongside [[Dalton Schultz]] at tight end, entering Stroud's second year on his rookie contract. Reaffirms that everything from Stroud's rookie season showed he could join the sport's elite QB tier, though notes he's still "not better than Josh Allen right now."


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: moving up in his rankings after the [[Stefon Diggs]] trade — with three startable wide receivers now in the base offense ([[Nico Collins]], [[Tank Dell]], Diggs), Houston has the kind of top-24-caliber-across-the-board stability Waldman looks for in a top-five quarterback, comparable to [[Joe Burrow]] with Boyd/Higgins/Chase or [[Kirk Cousins]] with Hockenson/Jefferson/Addison.

- 2024-04-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-15]]: on chatter naming him dynasty QB1 after the [[Stefon Diggs]] trade, "feeling" the idea he's now firmly top-5 and arguably top-3, but "f\*\*k" a definitive QB1 claim — too many quarterbacks (Mahomes, [[Josh Allen]], [[Jalen Hurts]], [[Lamar Jackson]], arguably [[Joe Burrow]]) have a real claim to the top spot depending on preferred skill set.
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Stroud is his central example against 'high-end pocket passer' QB1s -- despite great weapons and hype, Stroud didn't hit 19 fantasy points/game last year and wasn't a weekly league-winner, which he cites as evidence that immobile top-tier QBs underperform mobile QB1s/high-end QB2s at hitting the 20-22 point/game thresholds that separate a real QB1. Since 2014, only 5 of 23 immobile QBs drafted in the top 5 rounds (rushing under 3 points/game) exceeded their ADP expectation. His advice: fade Stroud in favor of pairing two cheaper 'pocket passer' QB2s (e.g. Brock Purdy/Tua Tagovailoa) later in drafts, since one of that pair mathematically has a good chance to match or beat Stroud's output.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): Stroud is a fade, at minimum until [[Nico Collins]] returns to form. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) cited JJ Zachariason's data — 14.4 fantasy points per game with no top-12 weekly finish in five weeks — and asked whether the fade is about the receivers or the pass protection. Waldman answers the receivers first: with Collins back you get a second real weapon alongside [[Tank Dell]], quick-game outlets and designed two-man route combinations to the outside. He credits Stroud with doing a great job relative to what he is dealing with, keeping Houston competitive despite guards and tackles struggling badly with twists, blitzes and cross-blitzes, but says he would not want to start him if he did not have to. Caveat added: even with Collins back, Collins's best trait is the deep game, and waiting for those routes to develop is a problem behind this line.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): Stroud is the cleanest current example of how consistent pressure deteriorates quarterback play, 'even most good quarterbacks.' Waldman stresses Stroud has already proven he can be good, so the recent slide across the Vikings, Packers, Jets and Lions games is situational rather than a talent re-rating. Sustained pressure forces all but the very greatest quarterbacks into occasional bad decisions, and one or two are enough to turn a game — he points to the late hero-ball throw that Carlton Davis III worked across the field left-to-right to cut off, a play Stroud should never have attempted and was baited into by the constant pressure. Waldman adds that Stroud is 'having to do more work than he should be expected to,' and when a quarterback must account for more than his job, mistakes and emotional impulsivity follow. Compounding it: [[Nico Collins]] did not make it back and [[Stefon Diggs]] is out, though [[Joe Mixon]] is still a very good player. See [[Quarterback Pressure and Career Trajectory]].
- 2024-11-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]]): Stroud's splits look alarming but are largely a [[Nico Collins]] absence artifact. Against Detroit he went eight of nine targeting slot receivers for 177 yards — which Harmon says is the correct way to attack the Lions, since their perimeter coverage is tough, their pass rush is good and you cannot run on them — while his numbers to outside receivers are 'atrocious' and under pressure he completed one pass for 17 yards with two sacks including a strip sack. Harmon's rebuttal to the panic: there is a zero percent chance Stroud is the worst quarterback in the league throwing to outside receivers when Collins is available, and Collins led the team in targets under pressure through the first four or five games. Koh (not a tracked expert) calls Stroud a stud trigger man and argues Houston has the guys — [[Joe Mixon]] playing out of his mind, [[Dalton Schultz]] slightly above average — and simply needs to look in the mirror.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): Stroud is the season's cleanest illustration that quarterback play cannot be read independently of pass protection. Angelo says the spectacular rookie year came with a good offensive line; take that away and year two is not the same player, because of two things — time and timing. Without time to throw, timing with receivers, spacing and alignments all break down, and the in-play adjustments cannot be made. He applies the identical frame to [[Caleb Williams]] behind what he calls a bottom-two offensive line, arguing that until time and timing are present you simply cannot see who a quarterback is, and that patience is therefore mandatory.
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]): only slightly overrated, and reluctantly so — the hype was last year's. Waldman blames an awful offensive line and receiver injuries for the regression and calls Stroud a quality young starter in a young Matt Ryan mold, rejecting the claim that he is meaningfully more mobile: early-career Ryan could move in and out of the pocket about as well. That bodes well for his career, but Waldman says Stroud will need great talent around him to be a top-five fantasy quarterback year in and year out; his likelier home is where he sits now, higher highs and fewer lows, around QB8 to QB15. Bob Harris (not tracked) offered the fantasy comparison to [[Patrick Mahomes]] — not in NFL quality but in being a good NFL quarterback who is not a trustworthy weekly fantasy starter — while noting Mahomes has been the QB4 in fantasy since Week 7.
- 2024-12-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-03]]): the Houston offense has been broken around [[C.J. Stroud]] rather than Stroud simply regressing — the offensive line is 'legitimately a bottom five unit', the scheme is doing nobody favors, and once [[Nico Collins]] went out, [[Stefon Diggs]] was a No. 2 (then went on IR) and [[Tank Dell]] played 'below replacement level' for most of the year. Harmon's worry is that Stroud 'started to develop bad habits because of the situation being bad at times around him'. He also frames it as the general risk of investing in a pocket quarterback: Stroud had 199 rushing yards and no rushing touchdowns, so everything else has to run pure. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) countered that Stroud has been the most negatively impactful fantasy QB of 2024 — QB26 in points per game, bad since Week 7, but healthy and famous enough that managers never benched him for a streamer like [[Sam Darnold]] or [[Baker Mayfield]] — and asked whether the industry was too quick to anoint Stroud a talent elevator.
- 2024-12-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-30]]): Stroud's second-year decline was mostly infrastructure, not a true sophomore slump — Waldman would 'cut him a little slack.' Houston's interior line, especially the guards, could not handle twists, games or cross blitzes on a repeat basis, and constant pressure wears down any quarterback regardless of experience. Houston also lost its top four receivers for stretches: [[Nico Collins]], [[Tank Dell]], [[Stefon Diggs]] and super-reserve [[Noah Brown]], with whom Stroud had a strong year-one connection. Waldman still saw quick, well-thought-out reactions on tape, and cautions that the running element people took from Stroud's Georgia game was overstated — he is 'not even [[Patrick Mahomes]]' and is closer to early-career Matt Ryan as a mover. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) disagrees on the label but agrees on the outcome: he says 'fuck yeah it was a sophomore slump' and wants everyone to believe it, because Stroud finished around QB17 after going as a top-six or -seven quarterback, so drafters who do not dig deeper will let him fall well into the double-digit rounds in 2025 as a rebound value.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]): asked whether year two or the rookie year was the outlier, Waldman answered that both were. He pushed back on the pre-draft narrative that the Ohio State-Georgia game revealed a new mobile dimension — 'what brand of crack is everybody on' — arguing careful study of Stroud's whole career shows a Matt Ryan-esque mover, better than expected but not someone who will generate lots of yards with his legs, and therefore a quarterback who needs a good pocket. In 2024 that pocket deteriorated off the edges and, more damagingly, up the middle; Waldman argues interior pressure is more destructive than edge pressure and that Houston's center-guard combination gave up a lot of it. He also notes the receiving cast rotated through injury ([[Nico Collins]] out part of the season) without the year-one style breakout games, aside from [[Noah Brown]] in the playoffs. Verdict: not as bad as he looked this year, not as good as year one — and a lesson for every 2024 rookie quarterback.
- 2025-01-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-21]]): Houston died as it lived — a 51.2% pressure rate allowed on Stroud against Kansas City per Next Gen Stats, and a league-high 55% pressure rate allowed on the season when opponents stunted, which Harmon reads as a communication and coaching failure as much as a talent one. He grants Stroud had a sophomore slump but says he has all the faith in the world in him as a franchise quarterback and does not care where he ranks on the quarterback pantheon. Harmon's fix is clarity of approach with Stroud in the room: Houston should stop treating him like a Shanahan-system 'binky' who cannot check out at the line, pointing to [[Matthew Stafford]] under McVay, and lean into the spread, point-and-shoot stuff he did at Ohio State. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is less sure the fault is coaching, asking whether Stroud is always hunting chunk plays or whether Bobby Slowik's playbook simply has no quick-game answer on third-and-five.
- 2025-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-30]]): the Bobby Slowik firing was necessary but the sophomore slump was not mostly Stroud's fault — Houston led the NFL in blown blocks per FTN and led the league with 217 sack yards lost against stunts per Fantasy Points, with only 2 of those 24 sacks charted as quarterback-caused. Harmon says Slowik got overrated in 2023 on the back of Stroud, [[Nico Collins]] and [[Tank Dell]], just 'played the Shanahan hits,' and could not evolve into spread 11-personnel; the run game ranked 31st in success rate with [[Joe Mixon]] healthy. Koh (not a tracked expert) thought 'need' was too strong and worries a new coordinator overloads Stroud with another playbook instead of letting him master feel for the game. Harmon still sees top-10 quarterback potential if Houston gets him in the gun as a point guard, comparing the shift to the Rams going from the [[Jared Goff]] floor-elevator offense to activating every blade of grass with [[Matthew Stafford]].
- 2025-02-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-04]]): Harmon does not care what stats you throw at him about Stroud's 2024 — he is still a quarterback he very much believes in, and Houston has 'the answer' at the position. The problem was the system: Bobby Slowik ran the Shanahan greatest hits and never got beyond them, and the run game and offensive line were a mess. Harmon is hopeful Nick Caley blends the Patriots and Rams worlds for more scheme diversity in year three. Both hosts want to see Stroud handed more control — Koh (not tracked) notes Shanahan-tree offenses deliberately build answers into every play so few checks are needed, whereas McVay affords [[Matthew Stafford]] line-of-scrimmage freedom, and they would like Stroud moved toward that end plus more 11 personnel distributing to all areas.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'feel' Stroud over [[Dak Prescott]] at Underdog QB17, mainly because the weapons should be healthy — [[Nico Collins]] back for a full season is the key, though Waldman conceded [[Tank Dell]] will not be available — plus a strong run game and a weaker division adding up to more points. Untracked co-host Bob Harris leaned Stroud too and flagged the swing factors: whether [[Stefon Diggs]], an unrestricted free agent likely to be a bargain off a torn ACL, returns and is ready for week one, whether they add [[Chris Godwin]], and whether the new offensive coordinator and any offensive line work make a difference. Both said neither Stroud nor Prescott would break their hearts as a QB2.
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]): Harmon says the Laramie Tunsil trade makes him 'really nervous' for Stroud, on top of a new offensive coordinator, a new playbook and new protection rules. Koh notes Stroud had the fourth-highest pressure rate among qualified starters in 2024, behind only [[Anthony Richardson]], [[Will Levis]] and [[Sam Darnold]] — and unlike those three, Stroud's time to throw was around league average, so the pressure was not self-inflicted. Harmon adds that Stroud lost the most yards on sacks against stunts, which he reads as communication errors on the offensive line, and cites Lance Zierlein's reporting that the Tunsil move was about the culture of that room and accountability from its supposed leader. Harmon concedes Houston is not better on the line without Tunsil, who remains one of the league's best isolated pass blockers despite a load of pre-snap penalties.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]): Waldman agrees with Sigmund Bloom (untracked) that Stroud is a free-agency loser after Houston shipped out Laremy Tunsil and multiple other offensive linemen, leaving his future dependent on a rebuilt line and a new offensive coordinator. His reasoning is that there is only so much any quarterback can get away with in a bad pocket, and while Stroud moves around reasonably well as a pocket passer he needs help. Compounding it, [[Tank Dell]] had ACL surgery in early March and both hosts expect him out for probably all of 2025. Waldman's conclusion is a tier drop: 'you're not looking at him as decidedly a quarterback two rather than the high-end quarterback one that he was looking like' the prior year.
- 2025-06-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-02]]): asked whether year one or year two was the mirage, Waldman refuses the framing and says it is a bit of both. His [[Rookie Quarterback Evaluation Windows]] frame: year one tests acclimation — does the player remember the plays, adjust to base defenses, and execute with the accuracy he showed in college when defenses are not throwing wrinkles at him, and Stroud's answer was yes. Year two tests whether he can raise his game once defenses adjust, and there the answer was 'somewhat, but not as well as year one.' Waldman projects over 4,000 yards and more than 20 touchdowns but says he will not argue with anyone slotting Stroud anywhere from QB13 to QB19, and cautions that calling Stroud a Joe Burrow-caliber prospect is 'a little bit further away' than the hype implies. He liked that Stroud hung in and battled behind a collapsing line — 52 sacks, constant A-gap pressure and stunts — with [[Nico Collins]], [[Tank Dell]] and [[Stefon Diggs]] all missing time. Bob Harris (co-host, not a tracked expert) adds the context: Footballguys projects QB20 while best ball ADP is roughly QB16 after a QB9 rookie year; the line was rebuilt with Cam Robinson, Laken Tomlinson and Trent Brown; Bobby Slowik is out for Nick Caley, who is handing Stroud back control of protections after Houston scored 13.5 points per first half and a league-worst 7 per second half.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]]) with guest JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert): Stroud is one of two quarterbacks JJ will wait forever for, arguing there is no difference between QB7 and QB20 this year. He likes Stroud as a player, recognizes what went wrong last season, but says assuming the exact same thing goes wrong again is incorrect and trusts the coaching to figure it out. JJ adds a market argument from his ADP work: in the later rounds you are better off targeting quarterbacks who had bad touchdown rates the previous year, because regression cuts the other way — good-touchdown-rate quarterbacks get boosted in ADP and then decline, while players like Stroud fall and become better values. If pressed to choose he leans [[Jordan Love]], but likes Stroud too.
