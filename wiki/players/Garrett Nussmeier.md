# Garrett Nussmeier

## Expert Takes

- 2026-02-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-09]]) [Dynasty]: after charting seven 2024-25 games, Waldman calls Nussmeier's game 'a roller coaster.' Positives: good pocket feel, willingness to throw off-platform, a solid arm for a 6'1', 205-pound passer, and intuitive processing. Concerns: he needs to protect the ball better when moving around, and his drop footwork/step width is inconsistent play to play, which sometimes produces pinpoint accuracy and sometimes wildly inaccurate throws even in the short game -- a habit Waldman thinks his ability to still be accurate through bad mechanics has discouraged him from fixing. Waldman comps him to Drew Lock -- dominant against inferior competition, exposed against disciplined SEC-caliber defenses, 'like a smart kid presenting a mess.' Ceiling if he cleans up the mechanics: a legitimate starter; floor if rushed into a lineup: 'the worst of Tony Romo... a drunk teenage Tony Romo.'
- 2026-02-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-16]]) [Dynasty]: Nussmeier is the class's most volatile QB prospect. His ceiling is genuinely high -- pocket movement, athleticism, arm strength and accuracy on deep throws for his size put a 'Brock Purdy or Drew Brees' outcome in the range of possibility, though Waldman called a Brees outcome improbable. But the floor is just as real: he flashes 'Drew Lock or Zach Wilson-esque' recklessness, missing coverage reads and forcing erratic downfield throws. If he cleans up those decision-making leaks he can start; if not, he may never get a real chance. In a head-to-head 'feel it or f**k it' against fellow QB prospect [[Ty Simpson]], Waldman took Nussmeier, preferring his higher ceiling despite the risk.
- 2026-04-16 — According to Dalton Wasserman (guest draft analyst, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: [[Garrett Nussmeier]] is QB3 and the only other quarterback on Wasserman's top-100 board. LSU's 2025 offense collapsed around him -- no run game, a coaching mess, injuries -- and by the back half of the season he was visibly getting the ball out early to avoid contact, a confidence issue as much as a mechanical one. When clean in the pocket he's still a gunslinger willing to throw into tight windows, comes from strong bloodlines (his father is the Saints' offensive coordinator), and started two years behind Jayden Daniels at LSU. Wasserman's best-case outcome has Nussmeier sitting behind a veteran for a year or two (he floated the Steelers or Rams as plausible fits) to rebuild confidence, calling him the rest-of-class quarterback with the best long-term shot to start. Chris Harris was skeptical of the 'Brock Purdy' ceiling comp some analysts use for Nussmeier, joking that comp usually just means 'a guy who I think sucks.'
