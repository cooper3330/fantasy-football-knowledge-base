# Mason Taylor

## Expert Takes

- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]) [Dynasty]: as of this recording — with rankings being finalized that day — Mason Taylor is the top tight end on Waldman's board, narrowly over [[Colston Loveland]] and well clear of consensus TE1 [[Tyler Warren]]. Waldman calls him extraordinarily smooth as a route runner, able to win against both man and zone, maybe not the best athlete in the group but very good at catching the football, with enough in-line blocking promise to be a real three-down tight end. Waldman's rule is that wanting a tight end to threaten deep usually means you are looking at a bulked-up receiver; he wants crispness in the short and intermediate ranges plus run-after-catch, which Taylor gives. He called Taylor the safest and best option in the class, with a Mark Andrews style of play possible in the right offense if the quarterback rapport develops. Bob Harris (not tracked) independently flagged Taylor as the tight end who caught his eye in the gauntlet drill, looking smooth and natural. Both noted his father is Hall of Fame defensive end Jason Taylor.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18]]) [Dynasty]: Taylor, the son of former Dolphins pass rusher Jason Taylor, is with [[Colston Loveland]] one of the two most pro-ready tight ends in the class to handle the prototypical role, though Waldman still prefers he land somewhere that emphasizes receiving H-back work over inline blocking. Smooth shows up in every facet: as a blocker he has a knack for hooking and turning assignments on the perimeter, is good with his hands at the line, can counter the opponent's counter in the run game to force defenders away from the rushing lane, and is light-footed enough in pass protection to slide laterally and wall opponents off. As a route runner he has a solid grasp of release footwork and upper-body counters, drops his weight effectively into breaks and has flat speed turns on in- and out-breaking routes. Waldman stresses this is not all finesse — Taylor uppercuts and rolls through his hips to lift defenders off their feet, and takes hard shots at the catch point, winning collisions with corners, safeties and linebackers while maintaining possession.
- 2025-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman sees real potential for Taylor to be a [[Mark Andrews]]-style fit — he works the middle of the field well, is a very good pass catcher against contact, has movement in the open field and good acceleration, and is already a better blocker than Andrews was early in his career, enough to handle front-side in-line work against a defensive end or strong-side linebacker while also splitting outside. Waldman's favourite landing spot would be Kansas City as the heir apparent to [[Travis Kelce]]: people would always compare him to Kelce unfairly and he may never meet that bar, but he would be good enough that nobody calls him a bust. He notes Taylor is the top tight end nobody ever asks him about while the discourse fixates on [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] and [[Tyler Warren]]. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) says he has been feeling Taylor since the combine gauntlet drill, citing the Jason Taylor and Zach Thomas bloodlines.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): Philadelphia is his favorite tight end landing spot and Taylor is the underrated target available at the end of round one or into round two — a 'steady Eddie' who is smooth, a good route runner, a decent blocker, reliable in the low red zone and productive after the catch, without doing any one thing at an elite level. The Eagles' virtue is developmental: a rookie behind [[Dallas Goedert]] on an error-free team gets an accelerated learning curve, two-tight-end snaps, and becomes the successor when Goedert's deal can be voided, plus redundancy if [[A.J. Brown]] or [[DeVonta Smith]] eventually price themselves out. Comps offered: [[Mark Andrews]] as a steady-Eddie type absent Baltimore's tight-end emphasis, and Hunter Henry — 'good enough to start anywhere'. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees Taylor embodies the week-to-week consistency Philadelphia needs.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: going too low in rookie drafts — Angelo thinks Taylor has a chance to earn a ton of targets and lists him among his rookie-draft values, though he spent less time on him than on [[Jaydon Blue]] or [[Pat Bryant]] in the same segment.
- 2025-06-05 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-05]]): both like Taylor for a nice rookie year in New York, on the same logic that makes [[Garrett Wilson]] attractive — there is almost no competition for targets in that room. Angelo's comparison point is Cole Kmet producing close to 700 yards with [[Justin Fields]] in Chicago, which was roughly TE6–TE7 territory; he sees a similar path for Taylor. Waldman lists Taylor alongside Wilson and Fields himself as the three Jets-related pieces worth owning, while explicitly steering away from the backfield.
- 2025-06-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-30]]): Waldman is feeling Taylor as a startable rookie tight end. He was Waldman's number two tight end on the board — a smooth pass receiver and good route runner who is physical and, contrary to some reports, was always a good blocker in Waldman's view. He argues [[Justin Fields]] finds the tight end on a second read better than almost anyone, citing [[Cole Kmet]]'s usage in Chicago, and that the path to workload is clear with only Jeremy Ruckert, a blocker, ahead of him. Waldman projects 65-75 targets and 600-700 yards, enough for low-end TE1 production, with [[Garrett Wilson]] around 1,050-1,100 yards, contingent on the Jets not adding receivers. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) is drafting Taylor very late and calls him an obvious high-end [[Dynasty]] asset, but is slightly less bullish than Waldman on 2025.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Taylor has the better short-term outlook of the two rookie tight ends even though [[Colston Loveland]] wins long-term. The case is scheme and quarterback: Tanner Engstrand comes from Detroit, so the Jets offense should look schematically similar, and [[Justin Fields]] historically fed the tight end — [[Cole Kmet]] finished as the TE5 two years ago with Fields throwing to him. Angelo considers Taylor more talented than Kmet, so he expects a really strong start to the year while Loveland ramps back from injury. [[Matt Waldman]] adds the mechanism: Fields is not a great full-field processor or manipulator, but he reliably knows where the tight end outlet is and how long it takes to get there — the very trait separating him from a career backup — which keeps the tight end fed.
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he volunteers Taylor as a third rookie tight end worth attention and rates him above [[Tyler Warren]] — a better route runner, better against man coverage, tough at the catch point, a real pro, and a better blocker, which means he gets on the field more often as long as he stays healthy. Waldman projects Taylor as the number two producer in the Jets passing game. The supporting argument is quarterback-specific: not that [[Justin Fields]] is a great quarterback, but that he is great at checking down to the tight end, which is what made Cole Kmet a fantasy asset in Chicago. [[Chris Harris]] notes Taylor's reported high ankle sprain resolved fast enough that it may have been a lower or medium sprain, and he is already back at practice as of recording.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): 'I absolutely feel it' that Taylor could lead all rookie tight ends in 2025. The consensus he is pushing against has the race between [[Tyler Warren]] and [[Colston Loveland]], with a [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] contingent as well, but Waldman thinks Taylor is the best all-around tight end of that group and notes [[Justin Fields]] leans hard on the tight end — enough that Taylor could end up the reception leader among rookie tight ends. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) agreed, arguing the narrow band of talent in the Jets receiving corps beyond [[Josh Reynolds]] and [[Malachi Corley]] implies a fairly robust role, with the caveat that New York is expected to be very run-heavy — though nobody is 100% run-heavy. Harris highlighted the price: Taylor is going outside the TE2 range, so the cost is right. Both noted [[Jake Ferguson]] backers exist, and Harris admitted he keeps hoping for Ben Johnson and Sam LaPorta magic to rub off on Loveland.
