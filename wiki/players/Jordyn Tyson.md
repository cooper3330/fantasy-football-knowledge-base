# Jordyn Tyson

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: one of the top two receivers in the 2026 class as he sees it right now, named alongside [[Antonio Williams]]. Angelo did not expand on the evaluation in this segment beyond that placement.
- 2025-11-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-24]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman disagreed that Tyson belongs in the same tier as [[Carnell Tate]], though he called Tyson very good, a good route runner, and absolutely capable of becoming an NFL WR1 who will likely start immediately. The gap versus Tate is craft: Tate shows patience and suddenness winning at the catch point the way Devante Adams baits defenders at the goal line, while Tyson 'has all the moves but doesn't understand how to execute them yet.' Waldman also flagged Tyson's hands as a reliability concern -- sometimes catching one-handed when he should use two, with 'little clap' drop episodes -- ranking him near the bottom of the top-receiver shortlist he's charted so far for hands. At roughly 6'2', 200 lbs (bigger than Tate's 6'3', 195), Waldman said Tyson's more traditional big-play-primary profile gives him a higher ceiling in the right offense even if his floor trails Tate's.
- 2026-02-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-17]]): Harmon has Jordyn Tyson stuck at three or four in his early 2026 class rankings, with a talent gap behind Carnell Tate and Makai Lemon -- he calls Tyson a good player with some holes in his game, saying he's 'not sure what he is yet,' unlike Tate/Lemon where the projection already feels settled.
- 2026-04-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman ranked Jordyn Tyson sixth despite some analysts having him as a WR1 candidate, calling him a 'highlight number one' rather than a proven one. He said Tyson has the physical tools but hasn't refined how he sequences releases and moves -- contrasting him with Davante Adams's patient-then-sudden setup move, Tyson's suddenness comes so fast defenders can't even register the fake occurred, which limits how much separation it actually creates. Waldman also flagged real availability risk: Tyson has had numerous injuries and is currently still working through a lingering hamstring issue some believe is connected to chronic effects from a prior knee injury. Combining the unrefined technique with the health record, Waldman said he'd rather draft Tyson as his sixth or seventh receiver off the board than in the top three to five where public rankings have him.
