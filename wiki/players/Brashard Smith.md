# Brashard Smith

## Expert Takes

     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: both name him the best receiving back in the class. Angelo has him above a 70th-percentile talent grade and stresses the diversity — detach him from the formation and he wins on double moves, on the boundary and in the low red zone, runs angle and option routes, beats backers on wheels, and is hard for a safety to cover on curvilinear stems. Remarkably, he played running back for one year after converting from receiver at Miami and still produced 1,300+ yards; Angelo saw him take what was given by the Clemson game and at the Senior Bowl rather than swinging for home runs, and expects immediate value as a kick returner and receiver. Waldman had his top receiving grade in the class, says he would carry a draftable grade purely as a wideout, and highlights him cut-blocking defensive ends and A-gap linebackers at SMU as evidence of fast learning. The flaw both laugh about: he over-relies on the spin move — Rashard Mendenhall-like — and fumbled once doing it three times in a row.
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman likes Smith as an in-space weapon in the [[De'Von Achane]] and lower-end [[DeAndre Swift]] vein, and says Smith reminds him a little of Jamaal Charles (a comp he mostly attaches to [[Ashton Jeanty]]). He is impressed by how fast Smith progressed from his final year at Miami to his first year as a starting back at SMU, notes Smith is still learning the position, and sees room for growth in his decision making — the speed of that development curve being the encouraging part. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) is less enthusiastic: he prefers [[Dylan Sampson]] and says he is not as high on Smith, though Waldman points out he is still above market on him.
- 2025-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: asked if the SMU back is in the same young-[[Aaron Jones]] mold as [[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]], Waldman says yes at 5-foot-10, 196 pounds, though Smith has to add weight to deliver the contact balance and power Jones has; movement, vision and footwork are good and the contact balance is already fine at this size. On the receiver-to-back conversion, Waldman is careful: Smith is not [[Savion Williams]], who could plausibly start at either position in two years, but he is also not [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] — Smith was genuinely a better receiver. Waldman's read is that with a lot of work Smith might eventually have made an NFL roster at receiver, and that he has learned fast as a runner while offering enough receiving skill to create matchup advantages from where he aligns.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]): an emphatic 'f**k yes' — Smith will be far higher on his board than his seventh-round designation suggests. After one year converting from wide receiver at Miami he already looked more refined as a runner than Waldman remembers [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] looking in his final Purdue season, and unlike Tracy he genuinely catches the ball well (Waldman: Tracy would not have made an NFL team as a receiver; Smith would have made an active roster or practice squad as one, and [[Savion Williams]] might have started). He is explosive, shifty, and Waldman thought pre-draft he could develop into a starter. Landing spot is the bonus: he learns behind [[Isiah Pacheco]] and [[Kareem Hunt]], Hunt is likely gone next year, Pacheco has about a year left, and Kansas City has an undeniable record of platooning and developing young backs.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Dynasty]: Smith at RB67 is priced right, and in a vacuum his individual skills justify the Sigmund Bloom 'won't leave a draft without him' enthusiasm — but Waldman drew a format split, saying he likes him more in dynasty than redraft. In redraft he would rather wait and pay up after [[Elijah Mitchell]] or [[Kareem Hunt]] is cut or hurt, because Andy Reid, another West Coast purveyor, tinkers with his backfield all season until he has an obvious dude. Expect situational opportunities that are few, far between, and frustrating for fantasy GMs unless an injury lands or he is installed as the RB2 by mid-August. Both hosts invoked the Brett Veach seventh-round-back precedent ([[Isiah Pacheco]]) as the reasonable gamble being bought here; Waldman also pushed back on sub-200-pound size models, citing [[Jahmyr Gibbs]].
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Smith is Angelo's headline example of a rookie whose camp reps actually matter, because Kansas City's secondary pass-catching role is genuinely vacant — linebackers are not scared of [[Kareem Hunt]] out of the backfield and it is not [[Isiah Pacheco]]'s game either. Angelo says he is watching what coaches and beat reporters say once 11-on-11 work with shells begins, not rookie minicamp. [[Matt Waldman]] likes Smith too but cautions he partly fits the recurring 'the back will play detached in the slot' narrative — people see a wheel-route catch and think Marshall Faulk or Brian Westbrook, when a 'key role' historically means one play or two targets a game with the rest window dressing. Waldman's test is whether the runner can work between the tackles and earn volume.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Smith is one of the rookies who could grow into the weapon-back role, and Angelo said he loved him coming out of SMU — but he expects most of the value to be schematic rather than statistical. Smith's job will be putting stress on backers and safeties to spring [[Rashee Rice]] and [[Travis Kelce]], buy [[Patrick Mahomes]] time and pull attention off [[Xavier Worthy]] over the top, with the side effect of neutering blitz packages, since a Cover 0 look against him can be checked to a wheel for a touchdown. [[Matt Waldman]] summarized the split cleanly: Smith will be more valuable to the Chiefs offense than to fantasy managers right now [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-16]]): Smith is a really interesting player out of SMU and Harmon is not 100% sure why Kansas City is not using him more, allowing that seventh-round picks take time. James Koh is more forceful: Smith has to be better than the ghost of [[Kareem Hunt]] and the shell of [[Isaiah Pacheco]], he has sub-4.4 speed, and Andy Reid — the supposed junior genius and master manipulator — should be figuring out a way to get him on the field and activated. Both frame it against a Kansas City skill group they think has atrophied.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-22]]): 'f**k it' on Brashard Smith's time coming this year. Waldman likes Smith's burst but cited a broadcast exchange in which Chris Collinsworth asked Andy Reid about using Smith more, and the takeaway was that the Chiefs do not feel he is quite big enough right now to be a lead back or a significant contributor. Waldman expects Smith to keep a small role of the kind he had that night, but not to take over — the Kansas City backfield should stay a three-way split weighted to [[Isaiah Pacheco]] and [[Kareem Hunt]]. He allowed that added muscle next year or enough injuries could change it, and stressed that Kansas City's running problems are more about the offensive line than the backs. Not feature-back material at this point.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): there was a leak that Smith will be 'quote-unquote more involved in the offense going forward,' which Harris says 'of course could mean anything' — but the context helps, with [[Isaiah Pacheco]] not producing results and [[Kareem Hunt]] feeling 'largely cooked when you watch him play.' He would have found it tough to trust Smith in a Week 5 lineup absent major bye-week trouble, but says he added Smith in a couple of leagues last week 'just in case' and will be watching Monday night.
