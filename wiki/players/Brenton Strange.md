# Brenton Strange

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10]]) [Dynasty]: an impressive young tight end in the same 'could be a quality starter in two to three seasons' bucket as [[Eric All]] and [[Charlie Kolar]]. Waldman highlights very consistent attack of the football, good body control on the move, and the ability to break a tackle or make a defender miss after the catch. The caveat is opportunity — [[Evan Engram]] will be back, so Strange projects as the second or third tight end depending on how Jacksonville uses Luke Farrell as a blocker — but Waldman likes that Strange has genuine all-around ability rather than a single specialty.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): Strange not drawing a single target was the glaring thing about Jacksonville's loss. Waldman's case is that Strange is a good receiver who runs good routes and shows red-zone presence, has accumulated real game experience and was productive early in the season, and that two-tight-end sets would pair well with the Jaguars' power run game and [[Tank Bigsby]]. He accepts Luke Farrell may be the better blocker but argues you can at least create the illusion of a two-tight-end run game with Strange in the lineup rather than playing inexperienced if athletic receivers like Austin Trammell, who was late off the ball on routes and had a drop. He puts the blame on both Trevor Lawrence and the offensive coordinator for not putting the best personnel in position to succeed.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): 'feel it' on Strange as a sneaky TE1 candidate with [[Evan Engram]] released — he called it a good one and the right idea for now. The hedge is that Jacksonville is going to reload from a deep tight end class and could find a bargain rookie who becomes 'the Brenton Strange of Brenton Strange.' Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) said the take had him suddenly acquiring Strange shares in six ongoing best ball drafts. [Best Ball]
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-30]]): Strange leads Jacksonville in both receptions (19) and receiving yards (182) through four games, ahead of [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]'s 12 catches and 164 yards. Harmon liked him as one of his favorite sleepers entering the season, but frames the leaderboard as an indictment rather than a compliment — 'very, very sad,' with no reason a tight end should be leading this team in receptions and yards given the receiving talent on the roster.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-02]]): Strange is among Jacksonville's best players alongside [[Parker Washington]] and has staying power in the league, comped loosely to Heath Miller though Waldman said he is more athletic than that. The caveat both hosts stressed is a ceiling one — Strange and Washington are quality complementary pieces, not stars, and it will be hard for Jacksonville to win late in the season when the most impactful playmakers on the field are those two while [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]'s and [[Travis Hunter]]'s best skills go unused.
- 2025-11-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: in his first game back from IR since Week 5, caught 5 passes for 93 yards including two downfield sideline grabs Harris compared to Trey McBride's game -- 'more of a receiver kind of play than a tight end kind of play,' though he noted Strange isn't quite as athletic as McBride. Ranked him the #4 Week 13 waiver add on both his own and guest Scott Fish's lists, calling him a possible 'Robert Tonyan season' (Harris's term for an out-of-nowhere breakout tight end) given a clear-cut role, a history of big games with Lawrence, and Brian Thomas Jr. still sidelined. Fish speculated Strange would have ranked even higher had Lawrence found him instead of Parker Washington on an overtime scramble drill.
- 2025-12-02 — According to guest John Daigle of Establish the Run (not tracked) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Daigle pitched Strange as a usable tight end -- TE4 and TE8 in fantasy scoring since he returned, top-12 in three of his last four games before injury, with a 22% target share over the past two weeks that ranked eighth among tight ends. Daigle said any of Dallas Goedert, T.J. Hockenson, Cole Kmet or David Njoku -- despite being rostered in over half of Yahoo leagues -- are expendable for Strange right now. Strange scored on a seam route against Tennessee, though Harris cautioned that came in a blowout Jacksonville didn't need to push; he still expects Strange in the top-10 among tight ends, helped further by Parker Washington's hip injury opening up more opportunity.
- 2025-12-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-26]]): Strange was his #5 undervalued player for 2026, a player he said he's now discussed on 'like five shows in a row.' He said he's looking at Strange's actual catches and traits rather than raw numbers, seeing parallels to earlier-career [[Trey McBride]], [[George Kittle]], and pre-injury [[Hunter Henry]], and believes he's becoming a real part of a good Jaguars offense rather than just riding [[Trevor Lawrence]]'s and the team's hot streak. He projected Strange as a top-12ish tight end without much draft hype behind him. Cousin Josh (untracked co-host) agreed, projecting him in the TE11-14 range and calling it 'a good pick' since he should come cheap in the sixth round or later.
- 2026-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-22]]): Strange is a pretty solid tight end who still has growth potential, and Trevor Lawrence reportedly likes throwing to him.
- 2026-01-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: said he wouldn't have put Strange on a level-jumper list because 'he's already arrived' -- unlike the receiver/tight-end tweener comp of [[Evan Engram]] he was initially projected as, Harris says Strange turned into a true in-line Y tight end this year, flashing real value as a blocker while still splitting out as a mismatch weapon, and calls him one of several quiet Jaguars pieces (alongside Travis Hunter and Brian Thomas Jr.) who helped make the offense good. [[Matt Harmon]] agreed Strange excites him more as a prospect than Ferguson, and suggested Ferguson-backers should hope his career follows Strange's arc -- a flashy rookie year followed by added size and a true-Y role in year two.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris ranks [[Brenton Strange]] TE8, well above most public boards that have him around 15-17, calling him a flag-plant pick this early and comparing him favorably to [[Trey McBride]]'s archetype -- a 'freak' athletically who flashes real route detail (double moves) even though he's never posted a 100-yard game and got hurt last year, arguably never fully right afterward. Harris said the frustrating part of tight end evaluation is exactly this: liking a player's traits without the box score to back it up yet, and he'd be willing to bench Strange quickly if he's unproductive for three straight weeks despite the season-long belief in him.
- 2026-06-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-01]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman does not project Brenton Strange to be 'a huge thing' in Jacksonville's 2026 passing game, ranking him behind the receiver trio of [[Parker Washington]], [[Jakobi Meyers]] and [[Brian Thomas Jr.]].
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-22 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: doubts Jacksonville will have a fantasy-startable tight end at all, comparing the annual hype around the Jaguars TE room (Strange, Tanner Kotchef-type high-acrobatic-catch guys, blocking specialists) to chasing a mythical polar bear. Strange is the presumptive starter and currently going as TE20, but Waldman thinks consistent production will be a struggle given competition for targets from a crowded receiver room (Jacoby Myers, Parker Washington, a resurgent Brian Thomas Jr., and Travis Hunter) even if Trevor Lawrence throws for near 5,000 yards. Waldman would rather roster [[Mark Andrews]] in that range.
- 2026-06-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Strange got a $45 million extension but both hosts view him as an ancillary target behind [[Parker Washington]], [[Jakobi Meyers]] and [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] -- Waldman projected him around 56 targets, with upside if Jacksonville leans further into 12 personnel given the team's deepened tight end room.
- 2026-08-06 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert; guest) [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-06]]: Zachariason put Strange on his film room heroes list despite calling tight end 'a useless position,' comparing his hands, jumping ability and body control to a young Trey McBride before McBride's breakout -- though he noted Jacksonville doesn't push the ball downfield to Strange the way Arizona eventually did for McBride, citing that McBride had 27 targets/15 catches on throws 15+ yards downfield last year versus Strange's 8 targets/6 catches. Chris Harris said Strange was 'an absolute revelation' after he'd ranked him TE29 going into last season; Strange finished TE23 despite missing time with injury. Harris now ranks him TE8, describing him as essentially a slot receiver who happens to play tight end and won't block. Both see him as undervalued given a crowded, talented Jacksonville pass-catching corps that will keep most drafters off him early.
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Strange is number eight on the Flag list, a player Harris feels no obligation to draft but is happy to take very late (some sites have him going undrafted). If [[Sam LaPorta]] is still on the board Harris takes LaPorta first, but he compares Strange's untapped upside to 'a souped-down version of Trey McBride' -- not as fast or as good an athlete, but with the same great ball skills and leaping ability, still plenty fast enough. He cites Jacksonville's crowded target competition as a caveat but says if he's taking one free tight end in the double-digit rounds, Strange is his guy.
