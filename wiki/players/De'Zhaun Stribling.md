# De'Zhaun Stribling

## Expert Takes

- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Stribling is tall, big and explosive but doesn't show up on tape the way he did at the Combine -- his releases lack range of motion and pacing contrast, and he plays too predictably at the line, at times looking like he's 'playing in concrete.' Waldman ties some of it to lapses in decision-making and says he needs to learn to play big and fast in every phase.
- 2026-04-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-06]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman feels it that De'Zhaun Stribling gets drafted early as a projection pick -- he has the height, speed, blocking, and decent hands, with flashes of strong after-the-catch ability, and some evaluators see a diamond-in-the-rough who lacked great production at Ole Miss but flashed developmental traits on tape. Waldman is more skeptical, ranking him lower because his tape is inconsistent -- he 'often looks like he's playing in cement' due to processing speed and technique/footwork issues -- and thinks he's two to three years from becoming the player his athletic profile suggests. Waldman says Stribling's game isn't refined enough to warrant a second- or third-round pick and would consider it a reach if he goes that early; he'd be more comfortable seeing him land in the fourth or fifth round.
- 2026-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: runner-up on Waldman's 'scared not to draft' list -- fast and big, but 'his feet are in concrete' at times because he isn't processing the game quickly enough yet. Waldman said he'd rather bet on Stribling than [[Chris Bell]] in that range specifically because Stribling shouldn't cost a premium pick, leaving more margin if the processing never catches up.
- 2026-04-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-21]]) [Dynasty]: De'Zhaun Stribling (Ole Miss, a two-time transfer via Washington State and Oklahoma State) is a late riser Harmon is now comfortable taking in the third round, noting Dane Brugler (The Athletic, untracked) has him going in the top 64. At 6'2', 207 lbs he ran a 4.36 forty (91st percentile) with a 36-inch vertical and an 82nd-percentile broad jump, plus 10-inch hands. His Reception Perception marks are mixed -- 68% success vs. man, 73% vs. zone (below Harmon's 80% bar), 68% vs. press -- but he showed a standout in-breaking route tree: 83% success on digs, 81% on slants, 76% on curls. He moved around the formation (76% of snaps outside, split nearly evenly left/right so not skewed-alignment, but only ~50% on the line, mixing in slot and even backfield/motion work), and both hosts rated him among the best blocking receivers in the class, praising his physical, well-timed run-game blocking. After the catch he broke a tackle on 50% of chances, multiple tackles on 16.7%, was in space on 56% of catches, and dropped only one pass all season (2.2% drop rate, 7-of-10 on contested targets). Harmon comped him to Jonathan Mingo but with a notably better press-man release, and projected him as a 25-30%-snap vertical slot receiver who'd fit a McVay/Shanahan-style scheme, Baltimore, or a Ben Johnson-run Miami offense alongside [[Zay Flowers]]-type personnel.
- 2026-04-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman understands the signal behind Stribling's early second-round draft capital -- teams typically invest that high because they expect early contribution or believe strongly in ceiling -- even though he says he's personally unlikely to draft Stribling in rookie drafts. The problem is target competition: with [[Mike Evans]] entrenched as the X and [[Ricky Pearsall]] also on the roster, Waldman doesn't see more than WR3-in-name-value as a rookie, especially since Stribling will likely be asked to learn all three receiver spots and wasn't especially versatile in one role at Ole Miss; he flags Ole Miss' broader track record of producing receivers who aren't conceptually pro-ready. The one overlooked positive Waldman cites: Stribling is an excellent, arguably class-best run blocker, a trait he thinks will matter to the coaching staff.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-07 (Going Deep with Brandon Angelo)]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo has Stribling early in the second round (pick 2.03), well above [[Matt Waldman]]'s mid-to-late third-round grade (pick 32), arguing Stribling's contact-catch ability and post-catch physicality mirror what Brandon Aiyuk provided in Kyle Shanahan's 12-personnel sets, and that San Francisco has lacked a 1,000-yard receiver since Aiyuk -- no 49er topped 650 receiving yards last year. Angelo says Stribling's immediate role and blocking give him staying power over [[Ricky Pearsall]], who he expects to slide into more of a slot role and who he doesn't think can handle 12-personnel blocking. Waldman pushed back that Stribling is still raw as a full route-tree runner, noting it took Aiyuk years to round his tree out in Shanahan's system; Angelo countered that San Francisco's expanding 12/13-personnel usage plus Stribling's blocking (needed given the 49ers' inefficient run game) should shorten that runway. Both noted NFL front offices, per Albert Breer and other draft insiders, were reportedly higher on Stribling than public boards, with San Francisco unwilling to trade off the pick.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-07]]) [Dynasty]: San Francisco took Stribling well ahead of consensus and ahead of [[Denzel Boston]] -- Harmon says he liked Stribling pre-draft but would have preferred him at the end of the round rather than where San Francisco actually selected him. Harmon's charting shows 'there's something here' -- he broke Harmon's brain a bit with his down-by-down effectiveness -- but Harmon still can't defend the pick as a 3rd-rounder rather than a late-4th/5th-round value, echoing the same 'reached too early but not worthless' verdict he gives Kalen Black. Harmon also notes (as an aside, not a scouting point) that 49ers-aligned media accounts have reflexively rushed to defend the pick online in a way they likely wouldn't for the same player on another team.
- 2026-05-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-18]]) [Dynasty]: Bob Harris predicted Stribling beats out [[Christian Kirk]] for San Francisco's WR3 job, citing size, 4.46 speed and blocking ability fitting what GM Eric DaCosta and Kyle Shanahan's scheme value, especially with [[George Kittle]] not fully ready off his Achilles injury and more 11-personnel likely. Waldman disagreed -- fuck that -- saying Kirk's health, route running and versatility make him the more formidable competitor for the role, and questioning whether Stribling's blocking translates into actual fantasy production: 'are you getting fantasy points for pancakes? I don't think you are.'
- 2026-06-16 — According to Tim Kawakami ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-06-16]]): rookie De'Zhaun Stribling, a second-round pick San Francisco moved out of the first round to take, is described as an older (23), well-traveled (three schools), low-key prospect whose main selling point right now is makeup -- 'this guy has got his head on straight.' Padless OTAs haven't shown much beyond one rep where he got behind the defense and looked smooth, but Kawakami says the 49ers drafted him primarily projecting him as a blocker: Kyle Shanahan's scheme wants receivers who get open and block, and the team specifically pointed to missing perimeter/downfield blocking last year as a reason Christian McCaffrey didn't break more long touchdown runs. Kawakami notes Stribling reportedly shows up on college tape doing exactly that.
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-06-22]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon views second-round rookie [[De'Zhaun Stribling]] (33rd overall, one pick after [[Ricky Pearsall]]'s 31st) as more of a project than Pearsall was, but notes San Francisco evaluated the two similarly given how close their draft slots were. He projects Stribling as [[Juwan Jennings]]'s replacement -- a power-slot, on-ball receiver who offers real after-catch ability the Niners lack elsewhere and could take run-game-friendly flanker snaps, with occasional X reps if Mike Evans is out. Harmon personally prefers Pearsall as a prospect and expects him to start over Stribling, but flagged a real (if unlikely) scenario where Stribling's superior traits push him ahead in the pecking order.
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-22 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: cited alongside [[Isaiah Neyor]] as a rookie showing well in Bengals OTAs and drawing hype as a threat to unseat [[Andrei Iosivas]], but Waldman groups him with the 'audentatitis' pattern of OTA standouts who fade once August live reps against man coverage start.
- 2026-06-25 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Stribling averaged an exceptional 7.3 yards after catch per reception in a limited college tree and is a strong blocker who fills the [[Juwan Jennings]] role well -- San Francisco taking him with the first pick of round two, and refusing to trade down from a premium slot, signals a real role plan. With [[Christian McCaffrey]] (30), [[Mike Evans]] (33) and [[George Kittle]] (33) all aging and no [[Brandon Aiyuk]], Angelo compares Stribling's ceiling to an [[AJ Brown|A.J. Brown]]-style route tree/size archetype (not that he'll be A.J. Brown) with a realistic outcome closer to a [[DK Metcalf]]-type producer, projecting a real breakout as soon as 2027 when Evans and Kittle may be gone -- potentially pushing 110+ targets if [[Brock Purdy]] keeps developing. Rookie-year expectation is modest: roughly 45-50 catches, 500-600 yards behind [[Christian Kirk]] and [[Ricky Pearsall]] in year one. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed with the assessment, adding that continuity with quarterback and coordinator is the biggest predictor of a receiver's role growing over time.
- 2026-07-21 — According to Ben Gretsch (Stealing Signals newsletter, not a tracked expert), discussed with [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-21]]): Stribling, San Francisco's first pick of the second round (33rd overall) in the 2026 draft, is seen by some as a bit of a reach, but Gretsch likes his ball-in-hands ability on tape and sees him as a limited-but-interesting player. He said if camp reports frame Stribling as stepping fully into the [[Jauan Jennings]]-type blocking-Z role (reportedly how the team views him), he'd be cautiously optimistic Stribling sees the field early given how late he's currently going in drafts.
- 2026-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-30]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon reiterates Stribling already had a good shot at the 49ers' No. 3 job and, with Christian Kirk's early camp calf strain and lingering uncertainty around Ricky Pearsall's health, calls it 'pretty much a lock' now. He pushes back on what he calls 'aggregation nonsense' around Stribling's post-draft hype (Stribling was Harmon's most-viewed YouTube video, driven by going at the very top of Day 2 after Harmon had him rated as a Day 2 sleeper), arguing the real substance is that Stribling profiles as a coach's favorite: a smart, high-effort blocker who literally inherited Juan Jennings' old jersey number and role. Athletically he's 6'2', 207 lbs with 10-inch hands, a 91st-percentile 40 and 82nd-percentile broad jump. Charting-wise he graded 68.4% versus man and 67.9% versus press — solid, not special — but excels on in-breakers: 83% on digs, just under 81% on slants, both staple routes in the Niners' offense. Harmon expects him to open as the No. 3 receiver at worst, capable of some slot and boundary reps, and a 'glue guy' in the room. Note: this segment was recorded before news broke that Pearsall is dealing with PCL-related knee swelling and team-described 'concern' per Nick Wagner (ESPN) — Harmon says to double down further on Stribling given that news.
- 2026-07-31 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-31]]) [Best Ball] [Dynasty]: rookie De'Zhaun Stribling, whom 'draft experts hated,' is now higher in Harris's ranks -- 'not that high,' he clarifies, calling Stribling 'an absolute cipher' that nobody knows can play. But he's stepping into a second-round-shaped hole in San Francisco's receiver depth chart behind an injury-prone [[Ricky Pearsall]], and Harris likens taking a flyer on him to grabbing a life preserver when the top guy is hurt. He's up into late-round draftable territory, though Harris pushes back on anyone suggesting a ninth-round pick: 'I'm probably not going to take him in the ninth round, but I did increase his rank.'
- 2026-08-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-03]]): bumped up alongside [[Deebo Samuel]] following [[Ricky Pearsall]]'s season-ending knee injury - Stribling checks in at WR51 on both Harris's standard and PPR boards, a ranking Harris admits is 'completely sight unseen.' He floats that a veteran receiver could still sign in San Francisco to add competition, so this is a thin, speculative bump behind [[Mike Evans]] and Samuel rather than a real endorsement.
- 2026-08-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Ian Hartitz (untracked co-host) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-03 (Feel It or Fk It with Ian Hartitz)]]): with Ricky Pearsall hurt, both see Stribling moving up the depth chart into an actively-chased top-50 WR range, projecting [[Mike Evans]], [[Deebo Samuel]] and Stribling as San Francisco's top-three-WR set in three-wide looks (ahead of Christian Kirk and DeMarcus Robinson); Waldman flagged fresh hamstring tightness as a concern. Both were skeptical of a Jeff Schefter report that some teams had Stribling graded as the draft's best receiver -- Hartitz called it 'fuck that,' Waldman noted scouts have told him receiver grading has more team-to-team variance than any position, citing the Patriots' historical preference for cheap slot receivers (Julian Edelman/Wes Welker mold) as the kind of team-specific value system that could explain such an outlier grade. Waldman also floated DeMarcus Robinson as arguably the closest route-running match to what Pearsall provided.
- 2026-08-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-04]]), with charting numbers from co-host James Koh (untracked): Harmon called Stribling a great scheme fit and 'draftable in all formats right now,' saying San Francisco needs him to be an immediate contributor with Pearsall out. His route chart showed a lot of low-volume 'red' routes that don't matter much, but strong metrics on routes he'll actually run in this offense -- digs and slants at decent volume with good success rates -- plus promising ability to sink his hips on curl routes vs. man and strong ball skills/YAC. Koh added Stribling posted an 83% success rate on the dig (15% route participation, not a small sample), 80-81% on the slant, and 76% on the curl. Both hosts see him filling Jauan Jennings's old in-breaking, off-ball role more than a true Pearsall replacement -- he's not as complete a route runner -- or the X-receiver out-cut/explosive-play role Pearsall and Mike Evans were slated for, since Stribling still needs to improve on out routes toward the boundary.
- 2026-08-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-06]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman says he's still drafting Stribling late in every league but doesn't believe he'll be more than a [[Jauan Jennings]]-level fourth/fifth option with occasional good moments -- he thinks [[DeMarcus Robinson]] is more likely than Stribling to step into [[Mike Evans]]' role if Evans gets hurt. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees, adding he doesn't trust anyone in San Francisco's receiving corps not named [[Christian McCaffrey]] given the injury litany around the room -- [[George Kittle]]'s Achilles (return timeline unknown, maybe week one, maybe week five), [[Mike Evans]]' injury history, [[Ricky Pearsall]] departed, and [[Deebo Samuel]] returning at 30. Both liked Stribling coming out of college but say they have no real idea what to expect given the surrounding chaos.
- 2026-08-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: With Christian McCaffrey and Mike Evans absent from 49ers-Titans joint practices, Harris says De'Zhaun Stribling was the takeaway for San Francisco -- he reportedly kept looking like a pro. Harris has been taking Stribling late in the mock drafts he's been running on the Harris Football YouTube channel, calling it 'a total dart throw' rather than genuine conviction, but one he doesn't mind that late.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman didn't have a massively strong pre-draft grade on the 33rd overall pick because his route running didn't show enough, but he called him big, fast, strong, good after the catch, and a strong contested-catch winner -- Waldman says Stribling is the rookie receiver he'd bet on being 'wrong about' in a good way, helped by Brock Purdy already saying he trusts him as a route runner. San Francisco originally planned him as a big-slot third receiver in the Kenny Golladay/Chase Claypool mold -- big enough that defenses shade coverage away and create matchups elsewhere -- but with the receiver room hit by injuries he may be forced into a Mike Evans-style No. 1 role sooner than ideal, which Waldman thinks caps him around 800-900 yards given his still-limited field-reading. Both hosts are drafting him as a 14th-round lottery ticket.
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-20]]) [Dynasty] [Redraft (Standard)]: Angelo says Stribling looks noticeably more refined than his Ole Miss tape -- cleaner route detail, better ball-tracking and body control at the catch point, and standout post-catch ability that flashed at Ole Miss and is now showing consistently at the next level. He projects as SF's best blocking WR and will play significant 12-personnel snaps to cover for a less-than-100% George Kittle early in the season, plus he's SF's best downfield threat -- both point out this WR room has no one over 650 receiving yards not named Christian McCaffrey, with Mike Evans and Christian Kirk banged up in camp and Deebo Samuel now in his 30s. Waldman calls Stribling the player he's most likely to have missed this year: three weeks ago he wanted him outside the 13th-14th round, but says that value window has already closed after Brock Purdy said in minicamp he already trusts Stribling as a route runner. Waldman is now circling him around the 9th-10th round in redraft and sees him as a potential 2027 focal point of the SF offense if injuries open the door, even acknowledging he's not a complete receiver yet. Angelo frames the leap as a case of fast NFL acclimation ('the Great Divide' between college tape and pro professionalism) -- Stribling and Cyrus Allen have both earned their QBs' trust quickly, which Angelo says is the first real predictor of a role.
