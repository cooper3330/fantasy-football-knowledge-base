# Daniel Sopkiewicz

## Expert Takes

- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Sopkiewicz has some of the best hand-eye coordination on contested catches in the class -- a wide catch radius and real one-hand flair -- and drops his weight well on timing-route breaks, though his speed turns and his releases/setups still need real work. Waldman's bigger concern is that too many defenders knock the ball loose from him on tight-coverage targets he should be securing, which he attributes to a lack of the 'craft' that separates prospects from reliable NFL starters.
- 2026-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-30]]): Daniel Sopkiewicz is a legitimate deep sleeper Waldman thinks can develop into a team's number-two receiver. His breaks and releases are already good, but he lacks the setup game NFL starters have -- the mid-route patience to bait a defender out of position before breaking -- which Waldman says separates good route runners from true starters. He's also a dark-horse contender for what Waldman jokingly calls the RSP's 'Brandon Lloyd invitational' for acrobatic, contorted catches, but drops timing-route targets in tight coverage when defenders slap at his hands and forearms -- exactly the throws he needs to secure to advance levels. Waldman still believes the athletic ability is there for him to be an NFL contributor even if he never fully fixes those issues.
- 2026-04-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman named Illinois State's Daniel Sopkiewicz (6'3', 205, his WR36) a late-round/UDFA sleeper worth stashing, praising acrobatic one-handed catch ability and solid route discipline; he compared Sopkiewicz's ceiling to a better version of Corey Davis if he can add more creativity within his route breaks and get stronger at the catch point, since his main drops currently come when defenders swat at his hands and forearms while tightly contesting the catch.
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-20]]) [Dynasty]: the hosts float Sopkiewicz as a potential dark-horse in Detroit's backfield, comping the situation to Isaiah Crowell's breakout with Cleveland -- a player who didn't get a target in the first preseason game but got real usage by the third, where 'anything can happen.' It's a speculative, low-confidence flag rather than a strong conviction call.
