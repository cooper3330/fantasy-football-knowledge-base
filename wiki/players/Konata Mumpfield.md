# Konata Mumpfield

## Expert Takes

- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]): Mumpfield is his best route runner in the 2025 class, though close rather than head and shoulders above the field. At 6'1, 196, a former Akron freshman All-American, Waldman says he understands pace and how to tell a story with a route — legitimate craft rather than playground work — with good releases, good stems, very good breaks and a habit of working back to the ball; he may also be the best pass catcher in the class and would be Waldman's dark horse in a hypothetical college aerial-artistry contest. Two caveats: Mumpfield occasionally combines route ideas too sophisticated for the defender to bite on (which Waldman thinks may project better in the pros), and speed is the open question — a 4.55 would be a victory, 4.6 is Waldman's expectation, and anything under 4.5 would make him someone to watch closely. Realistic projection: a tertiary player, think [[Jauan Jennings]] with better route skills.
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: Mumpfield is Waldman's underrated pick of the episode, one of three receivers he believes will make people 'upside down' on this class — and, he notes, the lowest-graded of those three. At 6-foot-1, 185 pounds Waldman thinks he might be the best pure pass catcher in the draft, a dark horse to win his imagined 'Brandon Lloyd Acrobatic Catch Invitational', and a savvy route runner who sets up stems and breaks and 'efficiently tells a story' to defensive backs with excellent technique and footwork. The whole case rests on speed: a 4.5 or 4.55 gives him a chance as a slot-or-outside possession receiver with a Marvin Jones Jr.-type long career, while a 4.6 is 'kind of a death knell'. His other worry is that the quickness shows up in routes but not after the catch.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): the Pitt receiver has an excellent toolbox of releases and incorporates savvy pacing to bait defenders out of position, and the same is true of his setups and breaks — he often manipulates defenders into overrunning the stem and earns a lot of separation back to the ball. Waldman calls that combination of quickness, pacing, deceleration, body control and precision the magic of route running. The honest caveat: Mumpfield has a full toolbox but is at least a step or two slower than the class's top prospects. His point in Mumpfield's favor is that his timing routes create more open space than some top prospects manage on athleticism alone — the thing good receiver coaches preach constantly.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: Mumpfield is one of the better route runners in the cycle and in the right situation could deliver a [[Jalen Coker]]-type rookie season — a yes as a late dynasty rookie-draft dart, with the caveat that things have to shake right. [[Matt Waldman]] added reality to his own earlier enthusiasm: take him very late, or skip the pick entirely and add him in free agency on expanded deep rosters. In deeper leagues he is worth it; in shallower leagues, wait until preseason when roster limits are loose and cut him if it does not show up.
- 2025-06-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-05]]) [Dynasty]: the one name on the minicamp list both independently believe in, and notably the only one who is not a speed guy — short-area quick, catches the ball, and knows how to pace routes. Waldman has been a fan for a while, praising his route pacing and contested catch-point adjustments, and jokes he would be a dark horse to win the RSP's hypothetical Brandon Lloyd Invitational. Angelo's case is situational: he learns from [[Puka Nacua]] and [[Davante Adams]], and the Rams' WR3 spot in 11 personnel is completely open — he will not be sold on [[Tutu Atwell]], Tyler Higbee is the move tight end, and [[Jordan Whittington]] is a select-role RPO receiver. Angelo thinks it is not crazy for Mumpfield to start in 11 personnel by season's end because of his catch-point consistency. Waldman's buy case is price: even if he is replaceable by year two or three, take the shot as cheaply as you can get him.
- 2025-08-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)] [Dynasty]: Mumpfield is the fourth name on Harris's super-deep sleeper list and 'that is very deep' — a seventh-round pick who Harris is not sure will even make the 53. [[Puka Nacua]] and [[Davante Adams]] are the only Rams receivers he actually cares about owning; [[Jordan Whittington]], a 2024 sixth-rounder, was last year's version of the surprise and may still hold the third receiver job, with [[Tutu Atwell]] somehow still hanging around. What caught Harris's eye was an exhibition touchdown against the Chargers inside the five: Benjamin St-Juste, a bad but five-year veteran corner, tried to bump him in press and just missed because Mumpfield's release at the snap was really great and really quick. Harris's profile: not a long-speed guy, undersized, but 'rocked up' strong and really quick in a phone booth. Half-joking plea that Sean McVay surely will not cut him after that.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-20]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman doesn't expect Mumpfield to earn much this year -- targets will stay spread among a crowded Rams group of receivers and tight ends (Davis Allen, [[Jake Ferguson]], Colby Parkinson, Tyler Higbee, [[Jordan Whittington]]). His one edge over Whittington is real route-running versatility: unlike Whittington, who's limited to zone concepts, Mumpfield can win in both zone and man coverage. Bob Harris joked he's still annoyed Mumpfield stole a touchdown from a teammate at the goal line.
- 2025-12-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-22]]) [Dynasty]: with [[Puka Nacua]] out, Mumpfield keeps making himself known, and Waldman says when the Rams staff compares him to Davante Adams they mean the press-coverage releases, red zone work and ability to go up and get the ball -- while noting he's not as big, fast or athletic as Adams. As a rookie he didn't win every contested target, but the misses were 'tough plays' rather than bad ones, and Matthew Stafford's willingness to keep targeting him in those spots is a good sign. Waldman's comp range for Mumpfield's ceiling is Antonio Bryant and Brandon Lloyd -- both 'nice starters' for years -- and he even floats what Dante Pettis could have become 'if Pettis was a little more interested and engaged in becoming the best player he could become,' with Mumpfield currently at the low end of that range but able to climb it with more development.
- 2026-01-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-26]]) [Dynasty]: not a must-add, but worth monitoring -- Waldman says fuck having him on your roster unless you have a true luxury bench spot or are in an especially deep league. He's a talented route runner with really good hands whom the team reportedly sees as offering a lot of what they expect from [[Davante Adams]], minus Adams' strength, making him a name to track over the next year or two contingent on Adams' and [[Matthew Stafford]]'s remaining time with the team; his value is described as volatile since it likely spikes only once both depart.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Among the day-3 depth receivers (Mumpfield, [[Isaac TeSlaa]], [[Jaylin Lane]], [[Devaughn Vele]]), Waldman rates Mumpfield's technical ability the best of the group by a wide margin, but since he isn't fast he'll need to keep being a massively good route runner and show more athletic development to convince Sean McVay he can be what the Rams hoped a prior slot receiver could give them -- team sources reportedly compared him to Davante Adams. Projects as a WR3/4 worth a deep-bench stash in 40-plus-team leagues.
