# Travis Etienne Jr.

> ⚠️ Takes below are from April 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: "at his ceiling of value right now" going as RB6 in early best balls, despite his touches being way up in 2023 and Jacksonville OC Doug Pederson signaling a plan to decrease his workload — so "fuck it" on the ADP, but not so strongly that he'd fade him if he falls further, since Waldman doesn't trust [[Tank Bigsby]] to hold onto the job: Bigsby has "a lot to prove" on ball security, catching and decision-making between the tackles. Reads Etienne as not far from reasonable value at current price.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): [[Tank Bigsby]] is not better than Etienne, and Waldman calls this another Rachaad White-Bucky Irving style referendum that does not need to happen. What is true is that Bigsby is a better downhill runner than Etienne has been and is taking some of the closeout touches. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) framed the context: two years ago Etienne carried a 70-plus percent share of the backfield touches, which Jacksonville considered too many, so they drafted Bigsby specifically to cut into it — meaning the current split is the outcome the team wanted all along, and a limiting factor on Etienne rather than a talent verdict against him.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10]]): a first-round pick who never developed the requisite pre-contact and processing skills in muddy situations to justify the draft capital. Angelo graded his ceiling as a Pro Bowler coming out of Clemson — a great linear accelerator and super dynamic screen player who beats you with acceleration — but says the through-contact skill set and the process-coupling never came, and they were supposed to by now. The framing: 'Etienne is going down the mountain and [[Tank Bigsby]] is going up the mountain,' though neither is a top-of-the-mountain back, making it a weekly dart throw. He is currently nursing a shoulder injury, and Angelo notes durability has dogged him throughout his career going back to Clemson and the lost rookie year with the Lisfranc. Later in the show Angelo used him as the counterexample to [[Isiah Pacheco]] — Etienne had the 'Pacheco starter pack' but never finished it.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): Etienne's role should recover once the schedule turns. Waldman concedes [[Tank Bigsby]] is currently the better interior runner of the two, but says Etienne remains the genuine space threat and still looks good in open field, whereas Bigsby is a screen-pass player. Because Bigsby's production is coming against fronts that are weak up the middle, Waldman expects that once Jacksonville starts facing stout interiors such as Philadelphia, the run game — or at least the passing-game work out of the backfield — will refocus on Etienne as the back better suited to that type of usage. He frames this as a reason to be patient with Etienne rather than to chase Bigsby's hot name.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with Etienne out and [[Tank Bigsby]] producing 26-118-2, Doug Pederson maintained that 'I'm a believer when injury happens, you don't replace your position' — Waldman thinks that is 'probably true' and expects Etienne to return as the starter, though not to the exclusion of Bigsby. His evaluative case for Etienne holding value: he remains 'the superior pass catcher and superior open field' player, and Waldman reads Pederson as simply asserting a committee. The practical warning for managers is that Etienne's return caps Bigsby rather than erasing him.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]): Etienne is not a complete player, but relative to [[Tank Bigsby]] he offers more in the passing game and a better chance on the perimeter running game, which is why Waldman rates Bigsby's odds of being Jacksonville's main back in 2025 as 'really low.' Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) described the week-to-week Etienne/Bigsby split as being the ball in a ping-pong match — one week it is Etienne, the next Bigsby. Waldman cautions the whole projection is clouded by a likely regime change in Jacksonville and by the Jaguars' need to spend resources on the offensive line before the backfield.
- 2025-06-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: at RB38 Etienne is the one he is 'feeling' in the Jacksonville backfield cluster, because you are paying a slight discount for the player who sounds like he will be the guy, and Liam Coen has publicly said he does not see the negatives others see. Waldman contextualized the price against the backs going around him — [[Zach Charbonnet]] (a backup whose role is iffy), [[Javonte Williams]] (may not hold the job all year), [[Rhamondre Stevenson]] (has competition) — and against [[Jordan Mason]] at RB38, who Waldman said has more upside but sits behind a just-extended [[Aaron Jones]]. He would also weigh [[David Njoku]] going in the same range if he needs a tight end. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) noted Etienne and [[Tank Bigsby]] both delivered RB3 numbers in 2024, finishing a spot apart at 35 and 36, and was dismissive of trade-away chatter.
- 2025-06-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-30]]): he is feeling Etienne remaining Jacksonville's RB1 this year, even while conceding that on pure rushing yardage [[Tank Bigsby]] or [[Bhayshul Tuten]] may be the back you want the rights to down the stretch. Etienne's case is combined rushing and receiving — Waldman says he is bumping Etienne's reception total up in his projections and taking those catches away from the third receiver, whether that is [[Dyami Brown]] or [[Parker Washington]]. He also cites the new coaching staff's public defense of Etienne, that they have not seen anything justifying the criticism levied at him, and argues the scheme is more conducive to what Etienne does well. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) grouped Etienne with [[Javonte Williams]], Tuten and [[Jaydon Blue]] as a knot of same-priced backs you draft hoping something good happens rather than as starters.
- 2025-07-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-28]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman now leans [[Tank Bigsby]] over Etienne and suspects Liam Coen's spring praise may have been trade-shopping — talk Etienne up, let Bigsby keep playing well, then try to sell Etienne before the season. His evaluation: Etienne should be a great outside zone runner and catches well with great open field skills and hard running at times, but his decision making — setting up blocks, reacting to unblocked defenders — has always lagged what you want from a starter, and his game is a light switch that turns on and off. He is also a holdover from two regimes ago under Urban Meyer. Waldman concedes the range: [[LeQuint Allen]] can pass protect, which was Etienne's unique edge, but four months from now the story could be Etienne with 400 yards in three weeks and Bigsby hurt. Bob Harris (untracked) reports Tony Smith expects Etienne to start while Mike Dempsey doubts he opens the season on the roster.
- 2025-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris prefers [[Tank Bigsby]] outright — he thought Bigsby 'was significantly better to watch play football last year' than Etienne — while noting Etienne still goes mostly inside the top 100, roughly flex territory, and Bigsby two full rounds later. Etienne did take the entire first-team series in Jacksonville's first preseason game after camp chatter had Bigsby ahead, which Harris treats as evidence that reported roles are unknowable rather than as a Etienne endorsement. He adds that no Jacksonville back is being drafted as a top-24 option, and separately (with Sigmund Bloom of Footballguys, not tracked) flags Jacksonville as an offense the market may be wrong about, since assuming Liam Coen instantly fixes what the late Doug Pederson era broke may take longer than one offseason.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: having charted and published full profiles on every back on the Jacksonville roster, Harmon said Etienne is the guy with 'no question in my mind' — a take he conceded is boring and unpopular because people want the younger names. He noted Etienne is still only 26 and joked that he has become the 'boring old guy', invoking the running back life cycle he used to discuss with Austin Ekeler: every fantasy back goes from 'get him the ball' to 'get him out of the way'. Harmon still allowed that Etienne and [[Bhayshul Tuten]] could split fantasy-relevant work. James Koh (not tracked) said he is largely avoiding the backfield because he can see a genuine three-man rotation; Harmon countered that if one back pops, that is exactly where a fantasy discount pays off.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the widely assumed summer outcome that Etienne would be traded (to Dallas, in the version analysts circulated) never materialized, and Harris and guest Andy Behrens (not tracked) say Jacksonville's backfield resolved back to the June expectation — a split between Etienne and [[Tank Bigsby]], with [[Bhayshul Tuten]] behind them to start the year. Harris cited Etienne's looming free agency as one of the 'three facts' that fueled the offseason speculation cycle rather than as a reason to move a Week 1 rank.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): Liam Coen has made his choice and it is Etienne — 39 snaps to [[Tank Bigsby]]'s 12, [[LeQuint Allen]]'s nine and [[Bhayshul Tuten]]'s four, made clearer still by Monday's Bigsby trade. The big run stirred the echoes of two years ago: a two-foot jump-cut stop that beat a linebacker in the hole and nearly went the distance. Harris grants the caveats — Carolina's run defense looked bad and he will not usually get that much time to stop and start — but calls it awesome. Guest Jake Ciely of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) agrees: unless Etienne falls on his face two weeks running, this is what everybody wanted from Coen, and the best case for a backup is Tuten taking about a third of the touches, the [[Rachaad White]] pattern in Tampa that faded as the year went on.
- 2025-09-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-12]]): Etienne is his number two, a positive change of mind. The argument is not just volume — it is that Jacksonville traded away [[Tank Bigsby]], which is an active choice, and that a new coaching staff has, at least for a moment, hitched its wagon to Etienne. That reverses the offseason consensus, which was that Etienne was at the end of his rookie contract, would not be paid, and would be the one shipped to Dallas. Harris tempers it: he does not want people treating Etienne the way they did two years ago and expecting the old ceiling to repeat, because there are two rookies behind him and Bigsby was cleared out for somebody — it might be [[Bhayshul Tuten]], it might be [[LeQuint Allen]], it could be some combination. But when the other veteran gets traded, he looks at the remaining veteran much more positively. Guest cousin Josh (untracked) says Etienne probably should have been his own number one and he only left him off assuming Harris would take him.
- 2025-09-19 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-19]]): the Jacksonville run game is his number-three mind-blower. After week one the assumption was that Etienne, the impending free agent, would be the back traded away — instead [[Tank Bigsby]] was moved, and Harris now thinks Etienne earns a new contract if he keeps playing this way. The evaluation: 'it's almost like 2024 didn't happen' — Etienne looks like he did two years ago, not like last season, when the lack of power runs and the lack of decisiveness were 'out of control' and Bigsby had clearly become the old coaching staff's favorite (not the new staff's). He allows that last year may have been injury-related and that two weeks could revert. He has also seen Etienne suddenly look good in short yardage, which squeezes the path for [[Bhayshul Tuten]].
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-29]]): Etienne is clearly 'Mr. Outside' in this Jacksonville offense, with most of his big plays coming off the edge, and Waldman agrees [[Bhayshul Tuten]] will not overtake him this year — not because Tuten lacks ability but because Waldman does not think Jacksonville will give Tuten enough touches. As long as Etienne is healthy it stays a committee with Etienne getting the edge. Bob Harris (not tracked) notes how quickly things move by joking that he is old enough to remember when [[Tank Bigsby]] was part of this conversation.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): it is like last year never even happened. Etienne produced a rocket-ship touchdown around the left side in the second quarter against San Francisco, and Harris sees no real threat from any other running back — the Jaguars have clearly decided that for this year they are handing it over to him.
- 2025-10-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-03]]): guest co-host Josh Van Blank (Harris's cousin, NOT a tracked expert) names Etienne his number five player to trade away — Harris's rest-of-season rank has him 22nd, FantasyPros 36th. Van Blank's concern is the pattern: Etienne faded as the season went along two years ago and last year was 'a disaster,' so with running back prices inflated he wants to test the market on 'found money' rather than give him away, targeting [[Drake London]] on a bye plus a sweetener like [[Tyjae Spears]] or [[Matthew Golden]]. Harris takes the opposite side — he says he has Etienne ranked a few spots higher than London and would more likely trade London to get Etienne, adding that most people would not give up Etienne for London straight up. Both agree it is worth checking what each is worth.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): 'I think anyone would love how Etienne looked in this game' — powerful right up the gut, breaking tackles, ferocious at times the way he was for much of last season, driving an 11-minute first-half drive inside the 10 and converting a fourth-and-two option pitch by running a defender over. The heavy caveat: Raiders tackling was 'truly, truly Bengals-level bad', so it is hard to know how much of this Etienne carries forward. [[Bhayshul Tuten]] remains 'definitely a sidecar' at 17 snaps across five quarters, though Harris admits Jacksonville sometimes prefers Tuten in short yardage — Tuten converted a third down and was stuffed on a third-and-one.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): 'boy, I think Travis Etienne is back', and the tell is body position rather than the box score - the difference between him running upright, searching for a hole and looking indecisive, versus running crouched forward looking like he wants to hit somebody and take and deal punishment. He has looked that way two straight games, and doing it against what Harris calls probably the best defense in the league makes the quote-unquote only 77 scrimmage yards more impressive than it reads; there were hopeless red-zone carries with no room, but also bursts into defenders' chests down to the five and the two. Harris notes [[Bhayshul Tuten]] played 15 snaps and [[LeQuint Allen]] 17, so others will appear, but 'Etienne has successfully staved off any and all challenges' and belongs in your lineup even with a home Chargers game next.
