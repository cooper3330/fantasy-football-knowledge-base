# Jordan Love

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]: less interested in Love himself than in the public reaction to him as a case study — early in 2023 he was "the next franchise quarterback," by midseason "terrible," and by year's end "one of the best young quarterbacks in the league," which Harstad attributes to small-sample overreaction with young passers rather than to Love actually swinging that wildly.
- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode: values the Packers giving Love extended time on the bench to develop rather than forcing him onto the field early, comparing it to how Green Bay handled Aaron Rodgers, Brett Favre (partly a "happy accident"), Matt Hasselbeck, and Mark Brunell. Frames this as a instance of a broader belief in intermittent playing time paired with real development time as a organizational practice worth valuing in dynasty. See [[Player Development and Coachability]].
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]) [Dynasty]: effusive after Love's Wild Card demolition of Dallas (16/21, 272 yards, 3 TD, 0 INT, 157.8 rating) — "I can't think of 10 other quarterbacks I'd rather have going into next season," and floats being "irresponsible" by even naming 10. Explicit dynasty framing given age and contract: prefers Love over [[Dak Prescott]], Trevor Lawrence and Jalen Hurts, and puts him just below a top tier of Justin Herbert/Joe Burrow-level "established crazy dudes." Credits Matt LaFleur's scheme and patience with young, inconsistent talent (Wicks, Love himself) as an organizational strength, not just a talent story.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]) [Dynasty]: reaffirms the top-10 quarterback framing ahead of the Divisional Round at San Francisco — "very easy to get him in your top 10, no question about it" — while cautioning that recency bias from the Cowboys blowout shouldn't obscure how good the 49ers are across all four phase-of-game units. Still likes Love's chances to keep pace through the air given a real outside-corner matchup advantage for Green Bay.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: addressing Love's costly late interception in the Divisional Round loss to San Francisco — explicitly refuses to let one "Utah State Jordan Love" moment define him, framing it as first-year-starter growing pains under playoff pressure rather than a level regression. Cites Green Bay's confidence dating back to letting Aaron Rodgers go and GM Brian Gutekunst's June comments about trusting a young receiver corps to grow with Love as evidence the front office knew what it had. States Love was "top five" in QB production over the final month of the season, with passing numbers comparable to [[Lamar Jackson]]'s over the same span. Explicit value call: doesn't expect Love to rise into the true elite QB tier, but expects him to be a hot commodity anyway and plans to target him as a value pick — warns the industry may overcorrect off one bad closing-game play the same way it overpaid for [[Gabe Davis]] off one good game two years earlier.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]) [Dynasty]: reaffirms the top-10-quarterback framing after the Divisional Round loss to San Francisco — "the very Favrian interception at the end be damned," Harmon calls Love's play over the back half of the season great and says "we feel like he can be a top-10 quarterback." Credits the organization's approach of letting Love and the young receiver corps develop together rather than importing a stopgap veteran.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: feeling it at QB9 in early best-ball ADP — still a bargain on the back half of the top 10, with a real path to top-five QB production if [[Christian Watson]] stays healthy and plays like he did late last season, and [[Jayden Reed]]/[[Dontayvion Wicks]] mature alongside the tight ends. Ties this to organizational competency: Green Bay letting a four-time MVP walk and trusting a young receiver corps to develop with a young quarterback (citing GM Brian Gutekunst's stated plan) reflects front-office confidence, not just a talent bet. Notes all of Green Bay's receivers remain cheap relative to their upside — [[Jayden Reed]] going as only a WR3 despite a strong stretch-run finish, [[Dontayvion Wicks]] going as a WR90-ish "free square."
- 2024-07-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]) [Best Ball]: asked whether the market is underestimating Love at QB10, Waldman answers 'fuck yes.' He calls regression-to-the-mean talk about one-good-year quarterbacks 'superstition rather than fact' — the work is understanding why the year happened and whether it is sustainable. Bob Harris (not tracked) supplies the numbers: over the final eight games Love led all quarterbacks with 168.5 fantasy points, 21.1 per game, third only to [[Lamar Jackson]] and [[Josh Allen]] on the season stretch. Waldman's supports are the weapons ([[Christian Watson]], [[Jayden Reed]] and even [[Dontayvion Wicks]] all capable of top-15 receiver production, with [[Romeo Doubs]] the safe high-floor/low-ceiling WR3 he is least confident in long term), a run game, and a good coordinator. The one flaw — tight man coverage with blitz early in the year — he fixed by the rematch with Detroit. Love is holding out of camp practice pending an extension and Waldman reads Green Bay's calm as confidence.
- 2024-07-30 — According to James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-30]]): despite the new contract, it is worth being open to the idea that Love does not progress as a passer and may even regress, because a large share of his strong late-season numbers came against below-average pass defenses and the overall sample on him is small. [[Matt Harmon]] agrees the sample is small but is more positive on the tape: he does not think the Packers' rocky early-season stretch was a Love problem, arguing Love showed the consistent traits you want from a starting quarterback even during that opening run and that the offense's issues were more about asking [[Christian Watson]] to do things he shouldn't. Harmon adds the anecdote that Love's self-diagrammed ideal play used [[Romeo Doubs]] as a backside clear-out X for [[Davante Adams]], which he reads as evidence of how Love sees his personnel. Both agree the table is set for a good season and that Green Bay is probably the betting favorite in the NFC North.
