# Alvin Kamara

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): flags real 2024 uncertainty driven by New Orleans's dire salary-cap situation rather than by anything on tape — notes he could be "on thin ice" there, though Waldman expects the Saints to restructure their way out of the crunch rather than move on from core pieces, pushing the cap problem further down the road as usual. On performance: over the weeks he actually played (roughly Weeks 4-17), Kamara finished close to a top-4 fantasy running back. States his workload "is not over" if he stays in New Orleans, and specifically flags a Klint Kubiak/Shanahan-tree-style scheme as a good fit that could extend his effective range as a receiving-heavy back.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-18]]): among Saints skill players, [[Alvin Kamara]] is the one Harmon is 'most intrigued by and [has] no idea how to project' for 2024. Kamara caught 75 balls on 86 targets in just 13 games in 2023, a receiving role Harmon partly attributes to [[Derek Carr]]'s checkdown-heavy tendencies behind a shaky offensive line. With Kamara turning 29 in 2024 and new OC Clint Kubiak (Shanahan-tree, run-oriented) taking over play-calling, Harmon expects fewer checkdown targets but is uncertain how Kubiak otherwise deploys him in the run game, calling it an open question the season will answer.
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: Bob notes Kamara is coming off one of his worst pro seasons by total yards from scrimmage after a three-game suspension, but was still productive on return -- 100-plus scrimmage yards in three of his final six games and three touchdowns across that stretch, per Waldman's numbers. New OC Clint Kubiak brings Shanahan-tree principles, raising hope for a high-volume role similar to other backs in that system, but Waldman ties the outcome directly to whether New Orleans' upgraded offensive line actually performs -- last year's poor line and Derek Carr's struggles let defenses sell out against the run. If the line holds up, Waldman sees real top-15 upside; ADP around RB17 reflects some of that uncertainty, with Kendre Miller and Jamaal Williams as depth threats if Kamara falters.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said he's 'increasingly intrigued' by [[Alvin Kamara]] and 'not giving up on him' despite an ongoing contract dispute, arguing the context behind last year's down numbers is overlooked -- a 3-game suspension, a slow start, and a 'raggedy-ass' offensive line that has since been upgraded. He noted new OC Clint Kubiak arrives from the 49ers/Rams coaching tree that has produced strong RB results elsewhere (McVay's Rams, Zac Robinson now in Atlanta), and pointed out that in the weeks Kamara did play last year he was still top-10 in points per game at the position, with some upside for a 'semi-CMC-ish' usage bump this year.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]): the more he studies Klint Kubiak's system the more he thinks New Orleans will add extra gaps in the run game specifically to get Kamara going again, with the tackle spots shored up and tight ends helping the scheme. He treats a revived Kamara as a knock-on positive for [[Derek Carr]] and [[Chris Olave]], since a functioning ground game is what makes the rest of that passing offense workable.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): 'Kamara looks like Alvin Kamara' again — close to five touchdowns in Week 2 — and Waldman credits two things beyond the off-field distractions being behind him. First, Klint Kubiak's scheme: motion, play action and rollouts that disguise intent and use players to their strengths. Second, structure — New Orleans is frequently running two-tight-end sets with a fullback in the backfield, which gives Kamara an extra gap: 'don't give Alvin Kamara an extra gap at the line of scrimmage, that's just insanity.' Waldman had liked the Saints' personnel a year early and blames the 2023 collapse largely on Trevor Penning underperforming the line's preseason rating, saying he was 'an offensive line a year shy' and an offensive coordinator short.
- 2024-09-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-17]]): Koh (not tracked) points out that 77% of Kamara's runs are now outside the tackle box under Clint Kubiak, up from roughly 55% last year, as Kubiak leans on what appears to be his father's outside-zone run scheme and lets Kamara find seams and cut upfield. Both hosts treat this as obvious and overdue given Kamara's skill set, and tie it to the broader point that moving the pocket and the launch point is one of the real answers to a bad offensive line. Harmon connects it back to his podcast conversation with Brandon Thorn about how to help a poor offensive line, noting he and Thorn were both skeptical of this Saints line coming into the year and that Kubiak has pushed the right buttons in both the run and pass game. Kamara is quoted postgame saying it is fun again.
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): 'absolutely feel it' on the hot take that Kamara would be every bit as good as [[Christian McCaffrey]] if he played in San Francisco. Waldman rates him an excellent receiver, as good a runner as McCaffrey and a better tackle breaker, and said he would have loved to see him in the Shanahan offense rather than languishing in New Orleans. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed '100%,' noting he expected a great year when Klint Kubiak arrived, that it started hot as fire and has been merely serviceable since in a horrible offense missing [[Taysom Hill]] and [[Derek Carr]]. Waldman added that Kamara wants to retire a Saint and says all the right things.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31 (Tapping Out, Archetypes and Modeling)]]): Waldman volunteered Kamara as one of his own misses and now one of his absolute favorite players. His pre-draft read was that Kamara bounced runs outside too much, did not hold onto the ball well, and would not change Sean Payton — and Waldman notes Payton independently said much the same on drafting him, that he did not like the kid on film because he bounced things out and did not stick it in there, and that he let him have it in his first camp. What separated Kamara, in Waldman's telling, is the response: many players never fix that because their ego is hurt, they do not know where to start, or they are afraid to face it head on, whereas Kamara treated the criticism as an instruction and the rest is history. Waldman used it as evidence that player development trajectories, not the static evaluation, decide outcomes. See [[Player Development and Coachability]].
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): the Dennis Allen firing does not change the Kamara outlook. Even in the loss to Carolina he had fantastic success and great volume — the kind of workload Waldman likens to what [[Christian McCaffrey]] gets in San Francisco — and Klint Kubiak's motion, rollout and play-action design, which produced 45 points a game over the first two weeks, has set Kamara up for success specifically. Waldman does not expect the coaching change under Darren Rizzi to alter that usage.
- 2024-11-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-05]]): Kamara touched the ball 35 times for roughly 200 scrimmage yards against Carolina and was effectively the entire New Orleans offense, but Harmon says he would not want to be carrying that load for the 2024 Saints, a team he calls one of the worst in the league that has just fired Dennis Allen. Co-host James Koh (not tracked), a self-described huge Kamara fan, argues the extension was the wrong move and that the Saints should have traded him instead; Harmon counters that no other team offers that contract and Kamara is maybe the 15th problem in New Orleans. Both note the workload profile is out of character — Kamara has never been a high-volume touch back, and the Saints have largely abandoned getting him the ball in advantageous run-after-catch positions.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): the quarterback question has to be crossed out before Kamara can even be evaluated. He expects Kamara to be the same player but for New Orleans to be incapable of running anything as advanced as the angle-and-option-route work he and Drew Brees used to eviscerate linebackers with. Angelo projected the Saints as a bottom-five scoring offense and called the situation a mess, which makes a genuine weapon-back role at this stage of Kamara's career hard to project, and makes week-to-week consistency outside of raw volume hard to find because points are scarce [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-13 — According to Dave Kluge of Footballguys (guest, not a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-13]]: Kamara going as RB17 at the end of round four, sometimes into the fifth, is way too low — he was the PPR RB2 last year before injury, shows no efficiency decline, still breaks tackles and rips off breakaway runs, and his history with [[Spencer Rattler]], [[Jake Haener]] and other quarterbacks means the shaky Saints quarterback room is not a real concern. Kluge relays a Dan Henry line that Kamara is an aging back written off like [[Derrick Henry]] and [[Saquon Barkley]] were, but got no ADP boom because he was hurt during the fantasy playoffs. [[Chris Harris]] agrees and goes further: he would take Kamara in the third without question and consider him late second in full PPR, blaming 'take-committed' analysts who punish a player who has burned them.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): Kamara is Harris's number two 'might be wrong about' and he says outright it is not an interesting argument, just a real one. He has been Kamara's biggest booster in fantasy for essentially the whole run of his show and is still above ADP; the worry is simply 'what if it's finally time' — the cliff did not look close in 2024 but it can come quickly. He pairs Kamara with [[Mike Evans]] as the two players he has been above ADP on for their entire careers, noting that this year he is finally below ADP on Evans but not on Kamara. Both hosts note the shaky New Orleans offense cuts both ways: it was bad in 2024 too, and the reason may be that they simply hand him the ball every time. Ben Gretch (guest, NOT tracked) had Kamara on last year's version of this list from the bearish side and says he wishes he had been thinking about him differently. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): 'Kamara played great' and helped get New Orleans inside the two before the play-action touchdown to [[Rashid Shaheed]]. Harris pushes back on the standard fade — that it is all volume on a bad team — with 'he plays on a bad team and he's still really good, so why would you think they wouldn't give him volume? Because he's their best weapon.'
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: still the Saints running back 'we should all be starting, clearly'. He took the second-possession wildcat carry, handled essentially the first few possessions, was the red-zone back once New Orleans got close, and finished with a 39-26 snap edge over [[Kendre Miller]] — 36-17 before garbage time. The caveats Harris raises: he had a drop on third down that forced a field goal, Harris noticed him limping a little at the end of the first half, he remains 'a check down king in a not very adventurous [[Spencer Rattler]] offense', and Funston floated the possibility of a deadline trade once the Saints have nothing to play for.
- 2025-10-13 — According to Chris Raybon (guest on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast]], not a tracked expert; [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-13]]): Kamara got the first two carries inside the 10-yard line but then lost a goal-line touch to a Taysom Hill wildcat touchdown, and Raybon noted Kamara has reportedly asked to be traded (he told reporters he wants to stay when asked directly) while getting a diminished workload the last couple of weeks even as backup Kendre Miller shouldn't be taking as many snaps as he is. Raybon said Kamara 'still looks good out there' but, combined with [[Chris Olave]]'s Rattler-dependent situation and a Saints offense still struggling to reach 20 points, this is 'probably the high point' for both -- he'd 'probably consider selling' Kamara now.
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-10-27]]) [Redraft]: just eight touches as New Orleans threw the ball 51 times in a lopsided loss, and rookie Devin Neal didn't get a single carry either -- a 'weird, bad game plan.' Harris says it's fair to feel 'over the idea of using Kamara right now' given how thoroughly this game plan bypassed the run game.
- 2025-10-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-28]]): Kamara has four straight games with 11 or fewer carries, is dead last in the league in percentage of run plays over expectation, has the worst forced-tackle rate of his career, and his yards per carry and yards per reception are career lows 'by a country mile' -- statistical decline Harris attributes to New Orleans' broken offense rather than Kamara personally. Harris disagrees with guest Scott Fish's expectation that [[Devin Neal]] will meaningfully cut into Kamara's role soon, calling Kamara entrenched as 'the mayor of New Orleans' regardless of performance.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): 'what a mess', but Harris says he cannot watch it and blame Kamara entirely. Kamara opened the game with the first two Saints carries and on the second made an unblocked defender in his direct path completely whiff, and did it again in the second quarter — the individual play was fine, the environment is not. He had a couple of okay third-quarter runs before getting peanut-punched and losing a fumble. The context: three straight three-and-outs to start, throws short of the sticks, and New Orleans holding the ball a total of 11 minutes across the first three quarters. Harris: 'it's a bad offense'.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): Kamara outrushed [[Rico Dowdle]], which Harris calls 'a little vindicating' for himself and co-host Paywall Andy (not a tracked expert), who keep saying on the Wednesday show that Kamara has not suddenly been drained of all talent. When he got an actual lane he looked like himself, though there were plenty of snaps with no lane at all, including a stuff near the goal line. He converted a big fourth-and-one on a handoff from Wildcat quarterback [[Taysom Hill]], and — astonishingly to Harris — New Orleans actually ran a couple of screens for him, decently blocked, giving him three catches for 32 and 100+ scrimmage yards. The caveat: it is the first time he has done that since Week 2, so Harris wants to see it at least once more before believing it.
- 2025-11-24 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Kamara hurt his knee late in the first quarter against Atlanta - 'wish-boned real bad' while falling out of bounds - and was checked in the medical tent but never returned. Harris noted Kamara also missed the end of last season with injury and said 'we'll have to see' about this one, without yet knowing severity as of recording.
- 2025-11-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-25]]): out with an MCL injury; Harris says he 'could be back for your fantasy playoffs, or his season could be over' -- the timeline is genuinely uncertain. Devin Neal has taken over the lead role in his absence.
- 2026-01-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Ben Gretsch (untracked, Stealing Signals) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-15]]): Kamara turns 31 in July with one year left on his deal -- $7M base salary, only $3M guaranteed -- and a post-June-1 cut would save the Saints $8.5M against the cap ($10M cap hit) and $4M in real cash. Harris says he wouldn't be shocked if Kamara is cut but doesn't view it as a lock. Gretsch pushes back that New Orleans likely keeps him: he's a franchise figure in the city, the team wants him to 'retire a Saint,' and with the roster not really competing, $4M is cheap to keep him. Neither analyst sees an obvious internal replacement -- Devin Neal 'looked fine' but profiles as a long-term backup, and Kendre Miller is talented but oft-injured (tore his ACL again in 2025) with reported work-ethic concerns under the prior staff -- while Kamara has reliably taken 200+ touches every year he's stayed healthy.
- 2026-02-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (untracked co-host) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-27]]): Koh said Alvin Kamara was reportedly 'drinking pina coladas and being retired' midway through last season and that it's time for New Orleans to move in a different direction at running back, though his contract makes him impossible to cut and he'll remain on the roster. Both hosts agreed his workhorse-back days are 'dead' and have been for a couple of years, with Harmon calling him 'one of my favorite players of all time' while still acknowledging the shift.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Andy Behrens (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-05]]): Harris is skeptical Kamara was genuinely hurt as much as the Saints simply pivoted once they started winning games unexpectedly. He's viewed as an obvious cap-casualty candidate given a massive, entirely unguaranteed contract, though reports are mixed on whether New Orleans actually cuts him -- Kamara reportedly said he'd retire rather than play elsewhere, which Harris treats with skepticism. He notes New Orleans lacks a clear alternative at running back beyond Kamara but is also well-positioned to draft Jeremiah Love if they move on.
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-12]]) [Dynasty]: Kamara's status is unresolved -- there's talk of retirement -- and Harris frames it as the swing factor for [[Travis Etienne Jr.]]'s New Orleans outlook. If Kamara returns, even banged up, Harris expects him to remain heavily involved as a pass-catcher (where a lot of running back fantasy value lives), which would push Etienne down to more of an RB2 role in that backfield.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris put [[Alvin Kamara]] at only RB48, calling it 'grim reaper time' and saying he genuinely doesn't think Kamara is on New Orleans' roster this season, despite still being under contract as of recording -- floating retirement as a real possibility. He acknowledged that if Kamara is somehow still the Saints' nominal starter, he'd rank meaningfully higher than 48, but said the 48 slot reflects total uncertainty about his NFL future rather than a talent evaluation.
- 2026-07-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris predicts Alvin Kamara gets cut or traded before or during training camp, arguing the Saints have no real motivation to pay the roughly $11 million in actual (non-guaranteed, cap-neutral) cash still owed him after a clear decline and a lengthy injury absence last year; Harris says he has already 'basically ignored the existence' of Kamara in his almanac rankings. Untracked guest Jim McCormick disagrees, calling Kamara an 'institutional Gainwell' -- a competent enough receiver out of the backfield that the Saints will keep him around as the third-down back rather than eat the cost of moving him, especially since no team is likely to trade for that contract. The disagreement is unresolved as of this episode.
- 2026-07-17 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-17]]): Kamara is back with the Saints for 2026 after re-signing at roughly $6 million less than his prior number, ending speculation (voiced on last week's NFC South preview) that he'd leave for a clearer role elsewhere. Harris calls him a franchise icon and one of the great receiving backs of the 21st century, but says fresh tape from last season shows him visibly slowed -- more prone to avoiding contact and not particularly good even before his injury. He believes Kamara is 'probably a little more of a drain' on [[Travis Etienne Jr.]]'s workload than a younger complementary back would have been, since Kellen Moore is unlikely to clarify a real timeshare split during camp. Harris says he'll revisit Etienne's rank upward if reports later in August suggest the Saints are actively downplaying Kamara's role, but is skeptical that clarity is forthcoming.
- 2026-07-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said New Orleans' backfield remains a mild fantasy landmine but leans 'feel it' rather than a full fade, noting [[Alvin Kamara]] signed a one-year deal worth roughly six to eight-and-a-half million while [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] got four years and 52 million -- signaling Etienne as the presumptive lead back with Kamara as clear number two. He called Kamara a career 4.2 yards-per-carry back who has never quite been the dual-threat weapon originally expected, though he's notched two top-10 fantasy finishes in the last three seasons; Waldman flagged the Saints' poor offensive line play (23rd in run-block win rate, 30th in yards before contact) as the bigger variable for both backs.
- 2026-08-03 — According to Ian Hartitz (untracked co-host) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-03 (Feel It or Fk It with Ian Hartitz)]]): he's been drafting Kamara heavily all summer and pushes back on the 'washed' narrative, noting Kamara was a top-five fantasy running back as recently as 2024 and that his yards-per-carry gap versus comparable committee backs (roughly 4.1 versus 4.0) is closer than perceived once the offensive line is accounted for; he calls it close to a 50/50 proposition on further decline versus continued production, while noting he was drafting Kamara around pick 180-plus, well after the field had written him off. [[Matt Waldman]] affectionately called Kamara 'a zen master of a running back' and said he doesn't want to see him decline, even while granting the risk is real at his age.
