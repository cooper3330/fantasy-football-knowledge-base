# Jerry Jeudy

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February–March 2024. Team/role are as of that date
> and are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until
> superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): a skeptical outlook, delivered mostly as context for why [[Russell Wilson]]'s 2023 struggles weren't purely a quarterback problem — grades Jeudy and [[Courtland Sutton]] as "skilled NFL players" but not a strong number-one/number-two receiving duo, ranking roughly 25 other teams' starting receiver pairs above Denver's. Pushes back directly on hearing Jeudy compared to A.J. Brown ("the next A.J. Brown") — "nah, he's more the next Reggie Wayne" — and adds a vaguer, unexplained red flag: "I think there's something going on there with that," without elaborating further this episode. Also earlier in the same episode, in an unrelated aside about drafting rookies now versus after the NFL Draft, co-host **Bob Harris** (not a tracked expert) mentions ending up with "a share of Jerry Judy" in a best-ball draft after missing out on [[Brock Bowers]] — a passing ADP/roster anecdote, not an evaluative claim.
- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]): reacts to Jeudy being traded from Denver to Cleveland. Sees real upside if [[Deshaun Watson]] is finally healthy and effective — Jeudy and [[Amari Cooper]] could be "a nice complement to one another" over the middle, with Cooper "certainly an upgrade" over [[Courtland Sutton]] as Watson's top target. But flags real downside: a "malcontent" reputation, drops, and press-coverage release skills that haven't been "as good as advertised" — restates the "aspiring Reggie Wayne, not an Antonio Brown" read from the 02-19 episode ("I never thought he was" the field-stretcher type people envisioned), while crediting his contested-catch tracking and route stem/break skills from Alabama. Verdict: "he's a good receiver, but he's not a top receiver" — and, needling his own Browns fandom, predicts "Russell Wilson will likely be better than Jerry Jeudy" in their respective new homes.
- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): confirms the trade compensation — a 5th- and 6th-round pick swap — and says the charted numbers back up years of below-consensus skepticism on Jeudy ("I think history has proven me to be correct"). Success rate vs. man coverage is fine (70.6% in 2022, 70.7% in 2023), but vs. zone is the real problem: 68.4% (2022) and 70.3% (2023), well below the ~80% bar for a good starter. That matters because he's deployed primarily in the slot (54.3% of his snaps) despite the poor zone numbers — Harmon's read: "he probably shouldn't be a slot receiver, he should probably be a flanker." Route-level detail: poor on outs, digs, curls and comebacks; genuinely good on dagger, post and slant routes, which could translate to Cleveland's play-action-heavy scheme. Notes Russell Wilson repeatedly missed him schemed wide open on crossing routes in Denver last season, visibly frustrating Jeudy on film. Verdict: "an inconsistent splash big-play threat, not a consistent starting receiver" — probably more a three than a two, with [[Amari Cooper]] the clear X in Cleveland's room. Caveats the whole receiver-room investment on [[Deshaun Watson]]'s play: Cleveland had the third-lowest team passer rating throwing to wide receivers in 2023 (only NE and NYJ were worse), the league's lowest completion percentage (56.9%), and the 7th-lowest rate of throws into open windows — "if Deshaun Watson ends up being what we saw last year, it doesn't matter how many assets you throw at it, it's just not going to work."


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: repeats why he wouldn't draft him — "an aspiring Reggie Wayne" whose route running isn't as good as advertised — and expects him to be at best Cleveland's third receiving option behind [[Amari Cooper]], depending on whether [[Elijah Moore]] or [[Cedric Tillman]] develops into the No. 2. Reads Cleveland's pattern of "stepping sideways" instead of forward as part of the concern.

- 2024-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-21]]): official RP profile confirms the "very inconsistent player" read and says the "general consensus" moving off calling Jeudy an elite route runner has been "generally proven correct" — 2023 numbers (70.6-70.7% vs. man, 70.3% vs. zone under new OC Sean Payton) are barely different from 2022 (70.6% vs. man, 68.4% vs. zone), still an 8th-percentile zone success rate that Harmon says simply isn't compatible with being "a high quality, consistent starting NFL receiver." Contrasts him with [[George Pickens]], who grew from a similarly flagged rookie season — Jeudy has "not only just not grown, he's plateaued and even at times gotten worse against zone coverage." Describes watching the tape and seeing real frustration (Sean Payton scheming him "wide-ass open" over the middle and [[Russell Wilson]] simply not seeing him) but says technique is the deeper issue — "not a good technician," "no deception to his game, no rhythm to his route running," calling it "chaotic, frenetic movement." Best on man coverage (63rd percentile, "pretty good, nothing special") which argues for using him as a flanker rather than a slot receiver given the zone weakness. Skeptical of Cleveland's "distressed asset" wide receiver strategy pairing him with [[Amari Cooper]] and [[Elijah Moore]] — "I appreciate the thought... but there's a reason Jerry Jeudy fell out of favor in Denver" — and relays hearing from people in the building that the room is "a lot more name than game" right now. Sees some fit for Jeudy's play-action-vs-soft-coverage skill set in Cleveland's scheme, but frames him, Moore and [[Cedric Tillman]] as needing to be "supporting characters" behind Cooper and [[David Njoku]], with the whole outlook riding on [[Deshaun Watson]].
- 2024-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-23]]): Jeudy, acquired by Cleveland for only a fifth and a sixth, is 'probably an inconsistent three' rather than the legitimate No. 2 he may get billed as — a player Harmon says has been badly overrated as a route runner, with highlight-reel releases and nothing behind them on the other 90% of routes. The alignment question bothers him: Cleveland sounds set to use Jeudy as a vertical slot, but Harmon has always wanted him as a pure outside receiver, because his man-coverage work holds up (70.7% vs man, roughly matching 2022; about 70% vs press on limited reps) while his zone work does not — an 8th-percentile success rate vs zone in 2023, only a slight improvement on 2022. Harmon rejects the bad-offense excuse: Jeudy posted 68.4% vs zone under Nate Hackett and 70.3% under Sean Payton's much better designed concepts, so 'him being a sub-10th-percentile zone beater is a Jerry Jeudy issue.' Slants, flats and nines are solid; outs, digs and curls have been really poor two years running. Best use is five feeds a game on quick-hitters lined up outside. James Koh (not tracked) said Jeudy takes three and a half to four seconds to get out of his breaks and rated [[Elijah Moore]] the better player. Only 25 years old.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]) [Dynasty]: a clear negative reversal — he says he just does not believe in Jeudy anymore. He does not think Jeudy can stay healthy, and he believes Denver wanted to move him for a while but could not find a worthwhile deal. He credits Jeudy with very good route skills but suspects he is a player who felt he had arrived and settled for being good rather than terrific. Waldman flags this explicitly as unsubstantiated intuition — nothing anyone has told him, just his read on the performances — and says he hopes he is wrong, because he loved the Alabama tape and saw a Reggie Wayne-type. He is more curious about [[Cedric Tillman]] in Cleveland.
