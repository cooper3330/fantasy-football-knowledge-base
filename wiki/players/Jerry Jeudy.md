# Jerry Jeudy

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February–March 2024. Team/role are as of that date
> and are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until
> superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): a skeptical outlook, delivered mostly as context for why [[Russell Wilson]]'s 2023 struggles weren't purely a quarterback problem — grades Jeudy and [[Courtland Sutton]] as "skilled NFL players" but not a strong number-one/number-two receiving duo, ranking roughly 25 other teams' starting receiver pairs above Denver's. Pushes back directly on hearing Jeudy compared to A.J. Brown ("the next A.J. Brown") — "nah, he's more the next Reggie Wayne" — and adds a vaguer, unexplained red flag: "I think there's something going on there with that," without elaborating further this episode. Also earlier in the same episode, in an unrelated aside about drafting rookies now versus after the NFL Draft, co-host **Bob Harris** (not a tracked expert) mentions ending up with "a share of Jerry Judy" in a best-ball draft after missing out on [[Brock Bowers]] — a passing ADP/roster anecdote, not an evaluative claim.
- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]): reacts to Jeudy being traded from Denver to Cleveland. Sees real upside if [[Deshaun Watson]] is finally healthy and effective — Jeudy and [[Amari Cooper]] could be "a nice complement to one another" over the middle, with Cooper "certainly an upgrade" over [[Courtland Sutton]] as Watson's top target. But flags real downside: a "malcontent" reputation, drops, and press-coverage release skills that haven't been "as good as advertised" — restates the "aspiring Reggie Wayne, not an Antonio Brown" read from the 02-19 episode ("I never thought he was" the field-stretcher type people envisioned), while crediting his contested-catch tracking and route stem/break skills from Alabama. Verdict: "he's a good receiver, but he's not a top receiver" — and, needling his own Browns fandom, predicts "Russell Wilson will likely be better than Jerry Jeudy" in their respective new homes.
- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): confirms the trade compensation — a 5th- and 6th-round pick swap — and says the charted numbers back up years of below-consensus skepticism on Jeudy ("I think history has proven me to be correct"). Success rate vs. man coverage is fine (70.6% in 2022, 70.7% in 2023), but vs. zone is the real problem: 68.4% (2022) and 70.3% (2023), well below the ~80% bar for a good starter. That matters because he's deployed primarily in the slot (54.3% of his snaps) despite the poor zone numbers — Harmon's read: "he probably shouldn't be a slot receiver, he should probably be a flanker." Route-level detail: poor on outs, digs, curls and comebacks; genuinely good on dagger, post and slant routes, which could translate to Cleveland's play-action-heavy scheme. Notes Russell Wilson repeatedly missed him schemed wide open on crossing routes in Denver last season, visibly frustrating Jeudy on film. Verdict: "an inconsistent splash big-play threat, not a consistent starting receiver" — probably more a three than a two, with [[Amari Cooper]] the clear X in Cleveland's room. Caveats the whole receiver-room investment on [[Deshaun Watson]]'s play: Cleveland had the third-lowest team passer rating throwing to wide receivers in 2023 (only NE and NYJ were worse), the league's lowest completion percentage (56.9%), and the 7th-lowest rate of throws into open windows — "if Deshaun Watson ends up being what we saw last year, it doesn't matter how many assets you throw at it, it's just not going to work."

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: reiterates skepticism on the Cleveland trade — "there's a reason [Denver's] been wanting to get rid of him," repeating the "aspiring Reggie Wayne" read (route running and explosion short of billing). Still projects him as at best Cleveland's third receiving option, contingent on whether [[Elijah Moore]] or [[Cedric Tillman]] emerges as the clear second.
