# Jerry Jeudy

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February–March 2024. Team/role are as of that date
> and are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until
> superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): a skeptical outlook, delivered mostly as context for why [[Russell Wilson]]'s 2023 struggles weren't purely a quarterback problem — grades Jeudy and [[Courtland Sutton]] as "skilled NFL players" but not a strong number-one/number-two receiving duo, ranking roughly 25 other teams' starting receiver pairs above Denver's. Pushes back directly on hearing Jeudy compared to A.J. Brown ("the next A.J. Brown") — "nah, he's more the next Reggie Wayne" — and adds a vaguer, unexplained red flag: "I think there's something going on there with that," without elaborating further this episode. Also earlier in the same episode, in an unrelated aside about drafting rookies now versus after the NFL Draft, co-host **Bob Harris** (not a tracked expert) mentions ending up with "a share of Jerry Judy" in a best-ball draft after missing out on [[Brock Bowers]] — a passing ADP/roster anecdote, not an evaluative claim.
- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]): reacts to Jeudy being traded from Denver to Cleveland. Sees real upside if [[Deshaun Watson]] is finally healthy and effective — Jeudy and [[Amari Cooper]] could be "a nice complement to one another" over the middle, with Cooper "certainly an upgrade" over [[Courtland Sutton]] as Watson's top target. But flags real downside: a "malcontent" reputation, drops, and press-coverage release skills that haven't been "as good as advertised" — restates the "aspiring Reggie Wayne, not an Antonio Brown" read from the 02-19 episode ("I never thought he was" the field-stretcher type people envisioned), while crediting his contested-catch tracking and route stem/break skills from Alabama. Verdict: "he's a good receiver, but he's not a top receiver" — and, needling his own Browns fandom, predicts "Russell Wilson will likely be better than Jerry Jeudy" in their respective new homes.
- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): confirms the trade compensation — a 5th- and 6th-round pick swap — and says the charted numbers back up years of below-consensus skepticism on Jeudy ("I think history has proven me to be correct"). Success rate vs. man coverage is fine (70.6% in 2022, 70.7% in 2023), but vs. zone is the real problem: 68.4% (2022) and 70.3% (2023), well below the ~80% bar for a good starter. That matters because he's deployed primarily in the slot (54.3% of his snaps) despite the poor zone numbers — Harmon's read: "he probably shouldn't be a slot receiver, he should probably be a flanker." Route-level detail: poor on outs, digs, curls and comebacks; genuinely good on dagger, post and slant routes, which could translate to Cleveland's play-action-heavy scheme. Notes Russell Wilson repeatedly missed him schemed wide open on crossing routes in Denver last season, visibly frustrating Jeudy on film. Verdict: "an inconsistent splash big-play threat, not a consistent starting receiver" — probably more a three than a two, with [[Amari Cooper]] the clear X in Cleveland's room. Caveats the whole receiver-room investment on [[Deshaun Watson]]'s play: Cleveland had the third-lowest team passer rating throwing to wide receivers in 2023 (only NE and NYJ were worse), the league's lowest completion percentage (56.9%), and the 7th-lowest rate of throws into open windows — "if Deshaun Watson ends up being what we saw last year, it doesn't matter how many assets you throw at it, it's just not going to work."


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: repeats why he wouldn't draft him — "an aspiring Reggie Wayne" whose route running isn't as good as advertised — and expects him to be at best Cleveland's third receiving option behind [[Amari Cooper]], depending on whether [[Elijah Moore]] or [[Cedric Tillman]] develops into the No. 2. Reads Cleveland's pattern of "stepping sideways" instead of forward as part of the concern.

- 2024-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-21]]): official RP profile confirms the "very inconsistent player" read and says the "general consensus" moving off calling Jeudy an elite route runner has been "generally proven correct" — 2023 numbers (70.6-70.7% vs. man, 70.3% vs. zone under new OC Sean Payton) are barely different from 2022 (70.6% vs. man, 68.4% vs. zone), still an 8th-percentile zone success rate that Harmon says simply isn't compatible with being "a high quality, consistent starting NFL receiver." Contrasts him with [[George Pickens]], who grew from a similarly flagged rookie season — Jeudy has "not only just not grown, he's plateaued and even at times gotten worse against zone coverage." Describes watching the tape and seeing real frustration (Sean Payton scheming him "wide-ass open" over the middle and [[Russell Wilson]] simply not seeing him) but says technique is the deeper issue — "not a good technician," "no deception to his game, no rhythm to his route running," calling it "chaotic, frenetic movement." Best on man coverage (63rd percentile, "pretty good, nothing special") which argues for using him as a flanker rather than a slot receiver given the zone weakness. Skeptical of Cleveland's "distressed asset" wide receiver strategy pairing him with [[Amari Cooper]] and [[Elijah Moore]] — "I appreciate the thought... but there's a reason Jerry Jeudy fell out of favor in Denver" — and relays hearing from people in the building that the room is "a lot more name than game" right now. Sees some fit for Jeudy's play-action-vs-soft-coverage skill set in Cleveland's scheme, but frames him, Moore and [[Cedric Tillman]] as needing to be "supporting characters" behind Cooper and [[David Njoku]], with the whole outlook riding on [[Deshaun Watson]].
- 2024-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-23]]): Jeudy, acquired by Cleveland for only a fifth and a sixth, is 'probably an inconsistent three' rather than the legitimate No. 2 he may get billed as — a player Harmon says has been badly overrated as a route runner, with highlight-reel releases and nothing behind them on the other 90% of routes. The alignment question bothers him: Cleveland sounds set to use Jeudy as a vertical slot, but Harmon has always wanted him as a pure outside receiver, because his man-coverage work holds up (70.7% vs man, roughly matching 2022; about 70% vs press on limited reps) while his zone work does not — an 8th-percentile success rate vs zone in 2023, only a slight improvement on 2022. Harmon rejects the bad-offense excuse: Jeudy posted 68.4% vs zone under Nate Hackett and 70.3% under Sean Payton's much better designed concepts, so 'him being a sub-10th-percentile zone beater is a Jerry Jeudy issue.' Slants, flats and nines are solid; outs, digs and curls have been really poor two years running. Best use is five feeds a game on quick-hitters lined up outside. James Koh (not tracked) said Jeudy takes three and a half to four seconds to get out of his breaks and rated [[Elijah Moore]] the better player. Only 25 years old.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]) [Dynasty]: a clear negative reversal — he says he just does not believe in Jeudy anymore. He does not think Jeudy can stay healthy, and he believes Denver wanted to move him for a while but could not find a worthwhile deal. He credits Jeudy with very good route skills but suspects he is a player who felt he had arrived and settled for being good rather than terrific. Waldman flags this explicitly as unsubstantiated intuition — nothing anyone has told him, just his read on the performances — and says he hopes he is wrong, because he loved the Alabama tape and saw a Reggie Wayne-type. He is more curious about [[Cedric Tillman]] in Cleveland.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Jeudy has carved out a legit role in Cleveland and is neck and neck with [[Elijah Moore]] just behind [[Amari Cooper]] in first-read targets, and he has not dropped a pass yet this season. Harmon calls him a solid player rather than a star, with a specific flaw: he is not a reliable separator against zone coverage. He caps it by saying the whole Cleveland receiver picture is capped by [[Deshaun Watson]]'s play.
- 2024-10-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-17]]): with [[Amari Cooper]] traded to Buffalo, Harmon is not buying a Jeudy breakout — he has 'always been a little bit lower than consensus' on him. The charting: a 70.7% success rate vs man coverage in 2023 but only 70.3% vs zone, which lands in the 8th percentile, and Harmon's benchmarks are 70% vs man and 80% vs zone. That is why he has never liked Jeudy in the slot: without spatial awareness and zone-beating ability 'you cannot play in the slot with those kind of zone numbers.' Jeudy has been roughly 60% outside / 40% slot; Harmon is interested in a full-time outside move, especially since fantasy points data has the Browns seeing the fifth-most snaps vs man coverage because nobody fears [[Deshaun Watson]] downfield. Caveat: he has no confidence Watson hits the throws. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) was blunter — Cooper was the WR36 and Jeudy the WR51-58, and Jeudy 'struggles to get open' with a very limited skill set.
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Harmon reiterates a low opinion of Jeudy — he is not an outside X, which is why [[Cedric Tillman]] took that role after the [[Amari Cooper]] trade, and Harmon would not be surprised if Tillman ends up Cleveland's best receiver. Jeudy was targeted on only 9.1% of his routes in Week 7. When [[Jameis Winston]] came in he immediately went at Jeudy twice, and Harmon calls the sequence 'very Jerry Jeudy': one deep shot caught for his only 18-yard gain, then an outright drop.
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): Jeudy is 'the biggest variable' of the four Browns pass catchers in play with [[Jameis Winston]] starting — a middling-to-meh ceiling and a low floor. Waldman leaned on Steve Smith's public assessment that Jeudy is soft as shorthand for why he ranks him behind [[David Njoku]] (safest), [[Elijah Moore]] (highest floor) and [[Cedric Tillman]] (highest ceiling), and cautioned that the tough Pittsburgh/Denver/Pittsburgh/Kansas City stretch is not the place to bet on the group's least predictable piece.
- 2024-10-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-29]]): Harmon, who says he has been 'certifiably below consensus' on Jeudy for years, softens slightly — Jeudy 'isn't a God awful NFL player,' has been overrated at times but is not useless, gets open, and can be a solid complementary third receiver. His framing is that Jeudy's production was suppressed by [[Deshaun Watson]] and by a crowded target room, and the rest of the season with [[Jameis Winston]] under center is the first real chance to see what the Cleveland pass catchers do with a functional NFL quarterback.
- 2024-11-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-21]]): Harmon has been lower than consensus on Jeudy and says Reception Perception has repeatedly shown he is at times a bit overrated as a route runner — but he is not a useless NFL player, he is a starting NFL receiver, and he is having big moments now with [[Jameis Winston]]. Jeudy is the flanker who moves inside, distinct from [[Cedric Tillman]] as the outside X. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) goes further, calling Jeudy an admitted blind spot: he does not think Jeudy is that good, cannot resolve it in his projections or analysis, tells his subscribers about the bias up front, and accepts he has to eat crow while Jeudy keeps producing. Harmon's standing caveat is that Winston inflates the statistics of everyone in this passing game.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]): Jeudy's 13-target, 9-catch, 235-yard, 1-TD revenge game against Denver was the best receiving day anyone has ever had against a former team, passing Terrell Owens' 213 against the 49ers. Harmon stressed the matchup nuance: per Next Gen Stats Jeudy had only 2 catches for 20 yards on 3 targets across from Patrick Surtain II, and 7 for 215 on 10 targets against everyone else, with Denver missing Riley Moss. He credits [[Kevin Stefanski]] — with [[Cedric Tillman]] out, sixth-rounder Mike Woods took the X on 75.8 percent of dropbacks, letting Jeudy stay a roughly 64/36 outside-inside flanker who motions away from the top corner. Harmon repeated his standing view that Jeudy is not bad but 'wildly inconsistent', citing Reception Perception success rates of roughly 63rd percentile vs man but 8th percentile vs zone — he gets lost, adds wasted motion and does not settle. That, he argued, is why Denver's vertical-slot usage was a square peg in a round hole, while Cleveland's play-action daggers, posts and slants off the line of scrimmage get the best out of him. Harmon apologized on air for advising against starting him and said he has a blind spot on the player. James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) agreed, quoting Ollie Conley that this is the game that would make you mad as his coach.
- 2024-12-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-17]]): Jeudy was 44th in the NFL in receiving yards through Weeks 1-8 and, before last week, fourth — a roughly 40-spot jump Harmon attributes to the 'Jameis Winston effect', calling [[Jameis Winston]] a Ponzi scheme for receiver production and joking about a Winston rehabilitation program for wide receivers. He is skeptical it is sustainable, since Winston wears out his welcome on turnovers and this was a brutal turnover game. Harmon also thinks the fit would sour if Cleveland brought in [[Aaron Rodgers]]: Rodgers has been frustrated by [[Garrett Wilson]] running a route at eight yards when the depth is six, and Harmon says he 'definitely' would not like playing with Jeudy.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-19]]): the biggest loser of the [[Jameis Winston]] benching. Koh's split: through Weeks 1-7 Jeudy paced to 51 catches for 646 yards and sat 44th in the NFL in receiving yards through Week 8; across the seven Winston starts he paced to roughly 1,900 yards and 119 receptions over 17 games, and is now one of only five 1,000-yard receivers. Harmon credits deployment as much as the quarterback — Cleveland cut his slot rate and put him on off-man looks and single-break routes (post, cross, dig) with one decisive break point rather than slants against zone with wasted motion, and Harmon says he has played those decisively and confidently. The catch: that route tree needs a quarterback willing to rip it, and Harmon doubts DTR, who throws behind the line of scrimmage on average, will. Koh's framing: fantasy managers just got 'rug pulled'.
- 2024-12-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-31]]): 'a little bit of both.' Harmon stands by having called Jeudy overrated in Denver but insists the backlash overcorrected — 'there is a difference between saying a guy is overrated and saying a guy sucks.' Jeudy charted as a 70% success rate player versus press man, which is not bad, with really bad issues against zone coverage, and Harmon still sees him as hot and cold rather than consistent. Cleveland has gotten his deployment right with more runway routes instead of long-developing slants against zone. But the stats are inflated by a bad offense: 18 targets in a game where Cleveland scored three points, producing 12 catches for 94 yards on a 38.3% target share, with several drops — 'old Jerry Judy popping back up.' Harmon does not consider him a true No. 1 and would want someone else to lead that receiving room. Koh invokes Harmon's rule that more targets than team points is a bad sign; the Browns cleared it six times over.
- 2025-01-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-09]]): Jeudy is Harmon's first Most Improved honorable mention after his first 1,000-yard NFL season. Harmon is careful about the caveats — Jeudy was not consistent across the year, a lot of it came in a handful of big games, and it was driven substantially by [[Deshaun Watson]] getting out of the mix and the [[Jameis Winston]] stretch. But he says the film showed real change: more urgency, more aggression, more decisiveness as a route runner, and specifically better work rifling into routes and getting open against zone coverage, which has always been his weaker side relative to man.
- 2025-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-05]]): Koh opened the Cleveland segment by admitting he was completely wrong on Jeudy — he thought Jeudy was done and finished, and instead Jeudy had the best year of his career in 2024 after the trade. Harmon did not dispute the bounce-back, but framed the room around him as bereft of high-end talent: [[Elijah Moore]] left in free agency, [[Amari Cooper]] was traded midseason, and the depth behind Jeudy and [[Cedric Tillman]] is [[David Moore]]-tier young players and Jamari Thrash. He also notes Cleveland is $30M over the cap, the second-worst situation in the league behind New Orleans, and in a transition year at quarterback, all of which caps what they can add around Jeudy.
- 2025-04-18 — According to guest Cory Kinnan (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Harmon]]'s show ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-18]]): Jeudy's 2024 breakout 'wasn't phony,' but he is definitively not a No. 1 and not a receiver who draws defensive attention, so Cleveland needs someone else who can — his case for the Browns drafting [[Travis Hunter]]. Kinnan's positive framing is fit rather than volume: Jeudy is at his best as a flanker or in the slot, Hunter is at his best at X or in the slot, so the two can be moved around each other for alignment versatility, and together with [[David Njoku]] underneath they would give the offense a lot of green grass to work with. The unresolved variable he keeps returning to is Cleveland's quarterback situation.
- 2025-04-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-22]]): the route-running criticisms aimed at [[Travis Hunter]] apply to Jeudy years into his NFL career — he is a 'TikTok route runner' with lots of breaks, flashy movement and dancing rather than getting to depth and getting open within the phase of the concept. Harmon credits Cleveland with negating that by simplifying his usage to runway routes off play action: run straight, build speed and snap open in one step at the break point, rather than slants and curls that invite the dancing. He expects Hunter to be much better than Jeudy. James Koh (not tracked) added that the Reception Perception data has borne out why Jeudy has not worked as an inside player, despite persistent calls to make him a slot, leaving Cleveland with three receivers who all want to play outside and no true inside option.
- 2025-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-07]]): 2024 was a genuine breakthrough for Jeudy in his fourth season, but Harmon flagged that the final numbers were overinflated by what he called the Jameis pump-and-dump scheme. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) argued Cleveland still needs a more consistent route runner and separator, saying Jeudy is probably better as a 2 than as a 1. In Harmon's preferred alignment for the room he is the fun vertical runaway-route player rather than the backside X, with [[Cedric Tillman]] on in-breakers and [[Diontae Johnson]] working the slot. Harmon also used Jeudy as the template for why he will not write off [[Elijah Moore]]: a wildly up-and-down early career in Denver before it came together in year four.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): Waldman is 'totally feeling' Jeudy at his WR38 fifth-round price. His description is an aspiring Reggie Wayne without the equity of a Marvin Harrison alongside him — really more of a flanker who can get deep with some speed, which he framed as basically the skill set Jeudy has. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) supplied the volume case: 145 targets last season, 16.4 fantasy points per game in the games he saw more than six targets, and a depth chart behind him ([[Cedric Tillman]], [[Diontae Johnson]], David Bell, Jamari Thrash) thin enough that the target share should hold or grow. The caveat Harris raised is that most of that damage came with [[Jameis Winston]], who is off the roster, leaving [[Joe Flacco]] as the likely thrower.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Cleveland finally got Jeudy's usage right in 2024, dropping his slot rate to 34.6% after 75%, 49% and 54% in the three prior seasons. Harmon says his Reception Perception profile has always pointed this way — Jeudy is far more useful outside than inside — and that what he publicly asked for was exactly this, more flanker work and big in-breaking routes. He would prefer Cleveland not kick him back inside to solve their slot vacancy, agreeing with Koh (not tracked) that Jeudy in the slot is simply not a good player. He adds that the Browns will likely run heavy two-tight-end sets after drafting [[Harold Fannin Jr.]], which may mean the third receiver spot barely matters.
- 2025-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-16]]): Harmon concedes Jeudy is the better player but argues his 2024 breakout narrative is inflated by how the season finished, including a big Monday night game against Denver. From week seven to week 12 — after the [[Amari Cooper]] trade and up to [[Cedric Tillman]]'s injury — Jeudy had 397 yards to Tillman's near-identical production, 25 catches to Tillman's 26, and one touchdown to Tillman's three, while Tillman led the team with 44 targets and 29.7% of the air yards. Harmon, who admits he is habitually hard on Jeudy, says this was not the Jerry Jeudy show and that the market gap between the two in drafts is wider than the on-field evidence from that stretch supports.
- 2025-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-25]]): used as the template for how to deploy [[Quentin Johnston]], Jeudy in 2024 played off the line of scrimmage on 59.4 percent of his snaps and ran digs and posts on roughly 30 percent of his routes, where he was awesome. Harmon notes he ran the most routes in the NFL at 712 because Cleveland trailed constantly in a pass-heavy setup, drawing 145 targets, and pushes back on the idea he is a clean-hands player — he had 10 drops, one of only two or three players in double digits, though the volume kept his drop rate low and his contested-catch rate was decent. Harmon says he watched a single Jeudy game with three drops. Koh (not tracked) believes he led the league in drops and was top three in drop rate; Harmon corrects the rate claim.
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-13]]): Jeudy is not a true number one and Harmon is unsure he wants him as a high-end number two, despite the counting stats. The skepticism is charting-based — final two Denver years of 70.3% and 68.4% success rate versus man, only one season above 75% (2021) and never cracking the 80% Harmon looks for, at 71% in 2024 even in his best statistical year. Harmon argues he is misused as a slot (40%+ of snaps in slot or backfield every year 2021-2023) and is best as a flanker away from press, given decisive routes rather than timing routes: he graded near 82% on digs (21.5% route rate) and 75% on posts, but only 71% on slants where Harmon wants 80%. Cleveland got that right in 2024 — 63.3% outside, 59% off the line. Big caveat: he led all receivers with 712 routes run amid the Jameis Winston pace bump, volume Harmon does not expect to repeat, so he prefers other players at Jeudy's top-100 ADP.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Jeudy made probably the first good Cleveland play of the day, a leaping third-and-long grab down the left sideline in the second quarter — and then dropped a throw in the same position on third and eight, because he's [[Jerry Jeudy]]. Harris pairs him with [[Calvin Ridley]] as the Spider-Man pointing meme: two receivers who are just so inconsistent. The bigger drag is [[Joe Flacco]], who was dreadful — running like a wounded mammoth toward the sideline and flinging one across his body into double coverage for an interception, plus a fourth-down fumble — and Harris says that is not the kind of effort that keeps any of his pass catchers worthwhile for fantasy.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-18]]): Jeudy is lined up outside on 81% of his snaps, a big jump from 63% last year, and Koh notes he does not believe Jeudy exceeded 75% outside in any single game in 2024. The driver is personnel — Kevin Stefanski has gone from 16.5% 12 personnel (below the 25% league average) to 50% this year, third-highest in the NFL, because of [[David Njoku]] and [[Harold Fannin Jr.]]. Cleveland is playing Jeudy as the X and [[Cedric Tillman]] as the flanker, which Harmon squints at: he thinks the big-bodied Tillman fits the X and Jeudy does not, and suspects part of Jeudy's alignment is about run-game blocking. Production has followed the alignment the wrong way — no touchdowns and three drops in two weeks, fifth in the NFL in drop rate among players with double-digit targets ('classic Jerry Jeudy').
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-02]]): Jeudy runs fantastic routes but 'literally crumbles' in contested situations — he either falls down or cannot establish position — which is why Waldman rates [[Isaiah Bond]] the better third-level contested-catch option in Cleveland even though Bond is the lesser route runner right now. Waldman framed Jeudy as an aspiring Reggie Wayne with Wayne a good tier better, and said people who saw an Antonio Brown in him were mis-projecting a secondary receiver, the same way people looked at [[Malik Nabers]] and misread the role.
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-10-09]]): Jeudy has been bad — another drop in this game, five on the season, a 15.6% drop rate — but Harmon says Cleveland is not doing him any favors. His slot rate has fallen 50% to 35% to 15% over three seasons (Next Gen Stats), and this year he is being asked to play X in only 9.6% slot alignment. Harmon has always been hard on him as a hyper-specific player who is not a good X and cannot really work from the slot either; what he does well is run runway routes off play action as the flanker, and 35% slot is the perfect number for him. He is the only X candidate left with Tillman out and [[Isaiah Bond]], Jamari Thrash and [[Malachi Corley]] behind him. Harmon also used him to explain relative success rates: Jeudy's 2024 man success rate of 70.9% was better percentile-wise than his 73.4% versus zone, because league-wide zone numbers are higher — and his 64% press number was flat-out the lowest, so a press-man outside role is the worst fit possible.
- 2025-11-11 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-11]]): Koh issues an explicit mea culpa, saying no one has been harder on Jeudy, after a 12-target, 6-catch, 78-yard, one-touchdown game — while noting it came against a stripped-down Jets defense missing Sauce Gardner and Quinnen Williams, and that Jeudy still mixed in a drop. He credits [[Cedric Tillman]]'s return (57% outside, 41% slot) with opening up looks for Jeudy on the perimeter. [[Matt Harmon]] frames it as a game that was statistically due rather than a change in view: Jeudy is sixth in the NFL in routes among wide receivers, around 20th in targets, 63rd in yards at the position, first in drops per PFF with eight (one more than [[Juwan Johnson]] and [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]), and at just 0.98 yards per route run.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): Jeudy drew 12 targets for six catches with one charged drop and clearly a second one on tape, and made a nice adjustment on a deep ball for his first touchdown of the season. Harris keeps the enthusiasm conditional: 'his negative plays will always be there', and 'obviously we don't think he's a very good football player' - do it again in Week 11 against Baltimore 'and maybe we get interested'. He admits he had one league where he had to use Jeudy in an emergency and was very thankful.
