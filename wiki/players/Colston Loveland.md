# Colston Loveland

## Expert Takes

- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: both hosts rank Loveland ahead of [[Tyler Warren]]. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) has him as the top tight end in the pre-draft model, crediting him for producing in years two and three, which his model weights far more heavily than a year-five breakout like Warren's. Waldman also has Loveland higher than both Warren and [[Harold Fannin Jr.]], though he places Warren and Fannin in a range where they can contribute and become fantasy producers in whatever role they are given. Waldman's caveat on the class as a whole applies here: he is not sure Loveland can do everything an in-line tight end is asked to do, and none of these prospects is a [[T.J. Hockenson]]-style do-anything tight end.
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]) [Dynasty]: Loveland is a close second on Waldman's tight end board as he finalizes his RSP rankings, edged out by [[Mason Taylor]] — Waldman described the gap between the two as close. That still places him well ahead of consensus TE1 [[Tyler Warren]] in Waldman's view.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18]]) [Dynasty]: Loveland is his number one tight end in the 2025 class and 'one of the best talents among the skill positions in this class'. Along with [[Mason Taylor]] he is the most pro-ready of the group to handle the so-called prototypical role if asked — but Waldman warns that is a double-edged trait: Loveland's blocking is deeper than [[Brock Bowers]]'s, which is exactly what could anchor him to the line of scrimmage instead of freeing him as a receiver, and he thinks Loveland has a much higher likelihood of fantasy success in a scheme that uses him as a receiving H-back. As a route runner Waldman singles out his ability to snap turns on breaks inside or outside, punching the boundary arm to generate suddenness on the turn — a technique he says only a handful of young tight ends or receivers show each year, and one that gets a receiver ready for the ball faster and generates separation when a defender overruns the break.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]) [Best Ball]: Loveland as the next rookie tight end off the board at TE12 and 129th overall — round 11 or 12 — is a price Waldman will take, and he says he would take Loveland over [[Tyler Warren]] every day. That fits his stated rule that round 12 and later is the right window to enter the rookie tight end pool. Waldman still frames the class as good but topless: no great tight end on the board, nobody with a [[Brock Bowers]] profile and nobody with [[Kyle Pitts]]-level ability, though he allows [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] could be a [[Sam LaPorta]]-style outcome.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): Loveland is the more realistic of the two premium tight ends to fall to Dallas at 12 (more likely than [[Tyler Warren]]), and if Jerry Jones pulled the trigger he would be a dynamic secondary pass-game option that bolsters both [[Dak Prescott]]'s statistical profile and a Cowboys offense Angelo expects to be very pass-predicated, since he does not see the run blocking improving unless they prioritize the interior line early. He also names Loveland as one of the three tight ends who would benefit from the Jets' situation at seven.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: a good player who will not be what the market wants, because Ben Johnson does not funnel the highest target volume to a tight end — Waldman says he would not be surprised if [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] out-earns Loveland in offensive share by 2026 once [[David Njoku]] is gone, and that [[Tyler Warren]] likely tops both. [[Brandon Angelo]] frames the ceiling as this year's [[Sam LaPorta]] rather than [[George Kittle]] — maybe a low-end top-12 fantasy tight end, and only if [[Caleb Williams]] and the offense hit on all cylinders through an expected learning curve. Both hosts prefer [[Luther Burden III]] as the Chicago rookie to own.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman disclosed taking Loveland at 1.04 in a Footballguys IDP dynasty league scored at 1.5 PPR for tight ends. His stated reasoning was roster-driven rather than a new evaluation — he already had a rich receiver group, and he owns [[Travis Kelce]], whose run he knows is ending, so he needed another strong tight end prospect.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Dynasty]: long-term Loveland over [[Mason Taylor]] without hesitation, but the short-term outlook is a ramp. Angelo flags the under-discussed grade-three AC joint dislocation with a roughly six-month recovery, meaning no contact until training camp and then a build-up period — he does not expect a 100% Loveland until perhaps midseason. Even healthy, Chicago has [[D.J. Moore]], [[Rome Odunze]], [[Luther Burden III]], [[Cole Kmet]] and [[DeAndre Swift]] competing for touches, and Angelo expects no one to become the alpha until later in the year or 2026, when he sees Loveland, Odunze and Burden as the big three. He calls Loveland a potential massive buy once the first few weeks show a ramp rather than the immediate breakout some are expecting.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]): Loveland is 'honestly head and shoulders better than [[Cole Kmet]] as a receiver,' and Waldman notes he caught a touchdown in the same practice Ben Johnson publicly called sloppy — evidence he is living up to expectations while the market writes him off. He is listed as one of the reasons Waldman expects [[Caleb Williams]] to take a step forward, alongside [[Luther Burden III]] and a repositioned [[D.J. Moore]]. The caveat is the same one he applies to the whole Bears skill group: a lot of mouths to feed, and while he expects all of them to offer startable value depending on league size, he would have to be ecstatic to call any of them every-week starters, and none may finish top-20 at the position.
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Loveland is his favourite of the rookie tight ends and he expects him to shine and be a top-12 fantasy tight end. The case is that Loveland is the better all-around tight end — he can win the contested catch and run better routes — and that Chicago, unlike Indianapolis, has 'a crappy situation with a good quarterback who survived it' rather than a genuinely crappy quarterback situation. Waldman explicitly ranks him above [[Tyler Warren]], whom he calls a hyped-up [[Michael Mayer]] type. [[Chris Harris]] frames the draft strategy: he is happy to wait until the eleventh or twelfth tight end off the board and take one of these rookies, because the downside is replaceable production he can drop, while the ceiling is far higher than the cost implies.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: The July question was whether Loveland could generate enough camp buzz with beat reporters to push a rookie ahead of the unexciting middle-tier tight end options, and Harris says the buzz got fairly loud and the market believed it — his ADP climbed steadily throughout August until he finished as roughly TE11 or TE12 in most drafts. If you were waiting at tight end, Harris says Loveland may have been the one you were waiting for. He flags that this story mattering and paying attention being a good idea are separate from whether the read turns out to be accurate.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Going Deep Rookie Receivers and RB Depth Charts)]]): Loveland is probably the safest of Chicago's young pass catchers, because Ben Johnson's offense leans on shorter, higher-percentage throws and [[Rome Odunze]] will absorb more of the lower-percentage targets. In Waldman's mapping of Detroit's 2024 distribution onto the Bears, Loveland occupies roughly the 700-yard tier with about 100 yards going to [[Cole Kmet]]. [[Brandon Angelo]] agreed the passing attack runs through [[Rome Odunze]], [[Olamide Zaccheaus]] and Loveland, and both hosts tied a possible top-five [[Caleb Williams]] season to Loveland staying healthy alongside a hitting Odunze.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Going Deep Listener Q&A)]]): expect a lot more Loveland, but it is gated on the run game. Waldman's stated sequence is that Chicago first has to settle on one or two running backs — or add a veteran to get tougher on the ground — before play-action to the tight end becomes usable, at which point Loveland's role grows. [[Brandon Angelo]] adds that Loveland and [[Luther Burden III]] were brought in to change the culture and that Chicago has no secondary option in the pass game behind [[Rome Odunze]], so both rookies have to play more. Caveat: the Bears are the second-worst rushing team in the league by their numbers, so the precondition is not yet met.
- 2025-09-19 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-19]]): guest Josh Fisch (not a tracked expert) reports taking the over on 18.5 receiving yards for Loveland last week and getting zero catches, possibly zero targets, framing him alongside the broader Bears offensive collapse — 'remember when you drafted that guy?' Harris corrects the draft-slot detail on air: Loveland was the number 10 pick, not eight (eight was [[Tetairoa McMillan]]), and Chicago took him ahead of [[Tyler Warren]]. Neither host offers a defense of the usage beyond noting Chicago's offense as a whole ranks 28th in EPA through two weeks.
- 2025-09-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-26]]): Loveland hasn't practiced this week and there is a real chance he misses the Raiders game with a hip injury — but Harris notes we are probably not racing to use Loveland anyway.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-20]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman rejected the idea that Loveland is a dynasty disappointment seven weeks in, pointing to flash plays (a leaping one-on-one win) that show his ceiling -- good ball skills, strong body control at the catch point, and the ability to win one-on-one coverage. He noted Loveland was banged up through training camp and just needs development time, and with Cole Kmet a little banged up, Loveland could see an expanded role and produce in good tight end matchups, especially against Jacksonville if safety Andrew Wingard is on the field again.
- 2025-10-31 — According to Adam Harstad (untracked Film and Theory co-host, [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-31]]) [Dynasty]: Loveland was drafted much higher than [[Oronde Gadsden II]] by both the NFL and fantasy managers and was ranked well above him in tight end rankings as recently as a month ago, but real-world dynasty value has since shifted -- with many now valuing Gadsden at or above Loveland after Gadsden's recent big games, illustrating how lowercase-v value moves on new production even when draft-capital pedigree still favors Loveland.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): 'definitely feeling' that Loveland is better than [[Tyler Warren]] as a player, though not yet as a fantasy option until it is clear defenses can copy the Steelers blueprint on Warren. Waldman rates Loveland as good a contested-catch player as Warren, more explosive downfield, better one-on-one, a better runner after the catch and a better route runner — Warren is a bulldozer and a zone specialist who wins some man trust throws, while Loveland can do everything. He expects a 'Sam LaPorta plus' stretch because Ben Johnson used LaPorta the same way as a rookie, noting Durham Smythe and Brock Wright cover the in-line work. Caveats: Loveland was beat up starting in training camp and may only now be healthy, and Waldman would treat him as a streaming option rather than a weekly automatic. Bob Harris (not tracked) said he had Loveland as his Football Guys in-column pick until [[Cole Kmet]] came off the injury report Friday, and regrets letting Kmet scare him off.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): Loveland won the game on what began as 'a nice throw, nothing amazing' and turned into a long game-winning touchdown because the Bengals safeties tried to run into him hard rather than wrap up. Guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert) later used the same play as the archetype of unsustainable production, calling a [[Dalton Kincaid]] corner-route score 'just as sustainable as the Colston Loveland 58-yard touchdown where the Bengals defense simultaneously retired'.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-04]]): Loveland is his number five waiver add at 24% rostered, and the entire case is a [[Cole Kmet]] availability question — Loveland has not played enough when Kmet is healthy to be usable. Harris notes he was five for 60 and a touchdown before the hit that Chicago's opponent was too undisciplined to avoid, that he withstood the hit a couple of times and kept going, and that he has hinted he is more talented than his usage suggests and should be getting more reps. Caveats: Kmet had already missed a game with a back issue before the concussion so he may not miss another, concussions are never knowable, and Harris is not thrilled resting his life on [[Caleb Williams]]. He does not dislike the Giants matchup. Jeff Erickson of RotoWire (not a tracked expert) had Loveland only as an honorable mention.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-04]]): Loveland's 118 yards are the most any receiver has managed against Cincinnati this season, and Harmon calls him 'a baller' who got fully activated in a game plan built to attack a defense whose linebackers and safeties he describes as horrific. He places Loveland in the pattern of players who beat the Bengals — tight ends, running backs and slot receivers — alongside [[Pat Freiermuth]], [[Sam LaPorta]] and [[Harold Fannin Jr.]]. The corollary is that Chicago's spread-the-ball game plan, with four different passers and [[DJ Moore]] throwing a touchdown, came at [[Rome Odunze]]'s expense.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-10]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman reaffirmed the ranking he set in April — Loveland is his tight end one from the 2025 class — despite a quieter box score than [[Tyler Warren]] (4-of-4 for 55 yards, longest 20, this week). His case is the quality of the looks: the plays Loveland is asked to make carry more gravity and difficulty than Warren's, and he believes swapping the two offenses would produce similar or greater production for Loveland. He also rates Loveland the better blocker — more capable of heavy-duty in-line work against NFL-sized linemen and linebackers, with better hand use and attack angles than Warren showed in college — though Chicago already has [[Cole Kmet]] for that work. Waldman said Loveland 'is starting to happen', a slow rise delayed by injuries, and predicts Warren will end up with similar or better yardage so Colts fans will keep claiming he was wrong while the underlying play argues otherwise.
- 2025-11-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-11-18]]): Harris says Loveland 'just hasn't shown that much' recently -- still looks like a good prospect, but Cole Kmet was more involved in the Week 11 win over Minnesota, mostly working checkdowns. Loveland did convert a third-and-13 late in the game.
- 2025-11-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-24]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman reaffirmed Loveland as the best rookie tight end in this class, pointing to weekly impact as a blocker, route runner and receiver, and noted [[Caleb Williams]] is trusting him more with Ben Johnson calling his number in high-leverage spots. Long-term, Waldman projects Loveland alongside [[Brock Bowers]] as the tight ends who, if they stay healthy, will still be delivering top-five TE production a decade from now -- the 'Kelce and Kittle' (or Dave Casper) of this era.
- 2025-12-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-04]]): grouped alongside [[Luther Burden III]] as a 'great secondary and ancillary' piece for Chicago rather than the clear number-one target -- that distinction went to [[Rome Odunze]], contingent on his foot/ankle injury clearing up.
- 2025-12-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-16]]): [[Colston Loveland]] had three chunk gains early against Cleveland as Caleb Williams generally kept the offense under control.
- 2025-12-26 — According to Cousin Josh (untracked co-host) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-26]]): Loveland was his #4 undervalued player, projected as roughly TE7 behind [[Trey McBride]], [[Brock Bowers]], [[Tyler Warren]], a healthy [[Sam LaPorta]], and [[Tucker Kraft]]. He expects Loveland gettable around round 6-7 after early reachers take McBride/Bowers in round 2 and a middle tier grabs Warren/LaPorta in round 4-5, and floated a top-three-tight-end ceiling given his draft capital, [[Ben Johnson]]'s second-year system, and [[Cole Kmet]]'s contract making him a cheap post-June-1 cap cut. [[Chris Harris]] agreed the pick isn't risky since fifth-round-plus tight ends rarely cost you anything if they miss.
- 2026-01-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-05]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman reaffirms Loveland was the top tight end in his draft class, and it's showing on tape despite a brutal rookie environment (bad offensive line, a quarterback effectively repeating his rookie year under a new infrastructure). Where Tom Brady called him a 'mini-Gronk' on a broadcast, Waldman's own range is George Kittle as the aspirational ceiling, [[T.J. Hockenson]] as the realistic outcome he's already close to and could exceed, and Tyler Eifert -- who Waldman still calls 'pretty fucking good' before his injuries -- as the bust-case floor. He argues year-one usage wasn't about forcing production but about building an offense (more two-TE sets, more running, protecting Caleb Williams's reads) that lets rookies like Loveland and [[Luther Burden III]] develop into their eventual roles without overloading the QB.
- 2026-01-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-22]]): Loveland is expected to play more as Ben Johnson's offense matures, forming part of a Bears receiving trio with [[Rome Odunze]] and [[Luther Burden III]]. Chicago kept veteran Cole Kmet on the roster purely as a trusted redundancy plan while Loveland earns more trust, not because Kmet rivals his talent.
- 2026-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: cited as an exception to the rule that college tight ends need roughly 640-plus receiving yards in their best season to project as full-time NFL receiving producers -- Loveland's poor Michigan offensive environment and injury limited him to 649 yards, but Angelo said he clearly 'well surpasses' that production level when healthy in a fair environment, making him a case where situational context matters more than the raw usage number alone.
- 2026-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-19]]): Loveland led the Bears in receiving and was, per Harmon, the team's most trusted target and best pair of hands by season's end despite a slow start coming off injury. Harmon highlights Loveland's rare alignment versatility -- he can play in-line or truly split out as an isolated X -- which lets Ben Johnson build 3x1 formations with Odunze and Burden bunched to one side while Loveland isolates elsewhere, creating mismatch advantages across the formation. Harmon calls his presence central to the offense and sees real star-tight-end upside for 2026.
- 2026-04-23 — According to Andy Behrens (untracked co-host) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-23]]) [Dynasty]: Behrens ranked Loveland ahead of teammate [[Tyler Warren]] in the second-year tight end tier, Harris the reverse (Warren #10, Loveland #12 on his list). Behrens praised Loveland's second-half surge -- five targets a game, 10-plus in the closing weeks of the most important Bears games -- and said much of what made him briefly buy into JJ McCarthy pre-draft was actually Loveland's downfield ability; at 'just turned 22,' Behrens likes the whole Bears passing-offense cast around him. Harris preferred Warren's situation and cited uncertainty around the Giants/Daniel Jones Achilles-adjacent quarterback picture as part of why he ranks Loveland behind Warren, while noting both, plus [[Harold Fannin Jr.]], are basically deployed as big slot receivers.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris singled out [[Colston Loveland]] as his favorite value bet within the TE3-6 tier ([[Tyler Warren]], [[Harold Fannin Jr.]], [[Sam LaPorta]]), saying he thinks Loveland is the guy most likely to be gettable at a discount this summer -- with a joking caveat the read might differ against an opponent with a Chicago-market bias inflating his local price.
- 2026-06-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-01]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman projects Colston Loveland to out-touchdown [[Luther Burden III]] in Chicago's passing game, with the two close to tied in targets and receptions, forming the Bears' one-two punch ahead of [[Rome Odunze]] in a cameo/co-star role.
- 2026-08-18 — According to Dave Kluge (Football Guys, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Kluge is surprised Loveland and [[Tyler Warren]] are being treated so differently at ADP -- Loveland going as an obvious top-4 tight end around the 4th round, while Warren, whom Kluge thinks was actually the better fantasy performer last year, goes two-plus rounds later. Kluge argues Loveland's strong finish was propped up by a 'pretty fake' garbage-time game against the Bengals, and that with Garrett Wilson, Ladd McConkey, Tee Higgins, Tetairoa McMillan, Cam Skattebo and Travis Etienne Jr. going in the same range, it's hard to justify Loveland's price -- he credits 'goldfish brain' among fantasy managers, who remember Loveland's strong finish and the Bears' playoff run but forget Warren played the stretch run with an ineffective, injured Daniel Jones and a noodle-armed Phil Rivers-esque QB situation. Harris says he has Warren narrowly ahead of Loveland in his own overall standard rankings (73rd vs. 75th) but won't be drafting either at their current ADP.
