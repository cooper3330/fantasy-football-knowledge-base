# Denzel Boston

- 2025-12-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-08]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman compared Washington receiver Denzel Boston to a young Antonio Freeman -- a physical player who uses size to win contested catches and make people miss after the catch over the middle. He pushed back on the notion that Boston is the top receiver in his draft class, saying he isn't there yet on the evaluation: a decent but not great route runner with strong hands but not overly dynamic with the ball in his hands, who Waldman thinks needs a top-tier quarterback to reach fantasy-relevant production.
- 2026-02-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-17]]): Harmon compared Denzel Boston's route running to a 'diet Coke, Bud Light' version of Mike Evans -- not projecting Boston to string together Evans-level 1,000-yard seasons, but seeing a similar X-receiver-who-can-also-move-around-the-formation archetype. He noted the comp had already been taken out of context on social media after he first made it on his Yahoo show, and lists Boston in his current five-man first-round WR tier for 2026.
- 2026-02-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-24]]): still 'working in pencil' on his 2026 WR prospect rankings, Harmon places Boston in a second tier behind top-10 caliber prospects Carnell Tate and Makai Lemon, alongside Jordan Tyson and KC Concepcion. On a potential Pittsburgh fit specifically, Harmon notes Boston is a big receiver (~6'4, 210+ lbs, official measurements pending at the time of recording) who shows zone-coverage recognition that DK Metcalf never developed, and profiles as more scheme-versatile than Metcalf -- can play X but also move around the formation -- which Harmon says would make him an interesting complementary piece to Metcalf rather than a redundant one.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Boston projects as a 'boring' but durable NFL contributor -- not a high-end X or Tee Higgins-level secondary receiver, but comparable to Courtland Sutton, whom Waldman called 'a really freaking good player' pre-ACL and 'a good player' post-ACL. Waldman's aspirational ceiling comp is Nico Collins stylistically, with a mid-range comp between Corey Davis and Tee Higgins (just under Higgins). Both hosts agree he sticks in the league for a while even without star-level upside.
- 2026-03-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-24]]) [Dynasty]: Boston (Washington, 6'4', 212 lbs, 35-inch vertical, 4.28 20-yard shuttle, skipped the 40 at both combine and pro day) sits 28th on the consensus big board -- a fringe-first-round/early-second-round pick -- and Harmon says he'd be comfortable taking him in the back half of round one, though he rates other prospects in this class ahead of him. He was almost purely a boundary X (84% of sampled snaps outside, 13.6% slot, 2.1% backfield as an insert/lead blocker on run downs, similar usage to how Amon-Ra St. Brown, Puka Nacua, Rome Odunze and, historically, Chris Godwin have been deployed). Contested catch was strong -- 78.9% catch rate on just a 14% contested target rate (only a 1.5% drop rate) -- which Harmon says shows he's not merely a jump-ball X but can also separate. Man coverage success was 72.5%, comparable to Drake London (73%) and Tetairoa McMillan (70.4%), though Harmon notes both of those top-10 picks were better zone beaters and deep threats. Boston's weak spot is down-the-field zone coverage: only 56.4% success on the nine route (18% route participation), lacking a second gear versus zone. He was much stronger on in-breakers -- 44% route participation on dig/curl/slant, with 79% success on the dig, 87% on the curl and 80% on the slant -- plus 20 TDs over his last two college seasons as a red-zone weapon. After the catch he broke a tackle on 41.2% of plays where he had space (only 37% of catches). Harmon's ceiling comp is Michael Pittman Jr., ideally with more downfield juice than Pittman showed at USC, and thinks Boston's outlook improves if an NFL team moves him around the formation (stacks/bunches for free releases) rather than parking him purely at X.
- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Boston has multiple mid-stem setup strategies and drops his weight effectively into hard breaks, but his speed turns still round off rather than driving flat, which Waldman says tight NFL man coverage will exploit. Waldman frames his ceiling as a fork: if he tightens up route precision he profiles like a featured option in the mold of [[Nico Collins]]; if he develops into more of a secondary sidekick, the comp is [[Tee Higgins]]. Which path he takes depends entirely on how much further he refines an already-promising game.
- 2026-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-30]]): despite ranking outside the RSP's top 10 receivers and drawing outside comparisons to Puka Nacua, Waldman firmly says Denzel Boston (6'4', 209) is not overrated -- 'fuck no.' He stresses rankings are secondary to the RSP's underlying depth-of-talent score, and Boston's score sits in one of the higher tiers, comparable to Jayden Reed's pre-draft score, meaning he's not far from an every-down starter grade even though several receivers ranked above him play different positions or roles. Waldman argues ranking receivers by role (flankers, split ends, slot, hybrids separately) rather than one flat list would make evaluations like Boston's clearer.
- 2026-04-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman ranked Denzel Boston 14th overall, well below his consensus top-five status on many public boards, though he stressed the gap is narrower than the rank implies -- Boston is 'literally one tier different' from his top seven, and Waldman said he'd have no argument with a WR5 ranking. The markdown came from durability (an injury cost him meaningful time and rehab) and a philosophy of grading only what he's actually seen on tape -- Boston's production skews toward the catch point rather than after-catch vision, and Waldman said he can't credit unproven traits even if plausible. He noted Boston's separation, routes and hands already grade top-tier, and directly compared him to [[Malachi Fields]], saying the two are closer in talent than the market believes, with Fields the likelier bargain two rounds later if Boston goes early.
- 2026-04-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman thinks Boston (2nd round, Cleveland) winds up a starter by year's end and has more upside than [[Cedric Tillman]] or than [[Jerry Jeudy]] has shown at this stage of his career, but says he has a bigger hill to climb than [[KC Concepcion]] to deliver true fantasy value -- particularly if Deshaun Watson starts and the offense funnels through the slot/middle of the field rather than to an outside X.
- 2026-04-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-28]]): paired with [[KC Concepcion]] as an 'awesome tandem' for Cleveland -- not just a size-based X/slot split (Boston 6'2'/206) but two players capable of playing each other's role long term. Boston's best routes break over the middle rather than needing to win purely vertical, drawing a 'poor man's Michael Pittman / poor man's Tetairoa McMillan / poor man's Drake London' comp; that archetype has historically broken out further after moving off the ball into the slot. Notes Boston and Concepcion should ideally push [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] down the target hierarchy in a good-outcome scenario, and that [[Jerry Jeudy]]'s ill-fitting move to X last year (with Cedric Tillman at Z) should be corrected now that Boston and Concepcion can absorb more X snaps.
- 2026-05-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-18]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman relayed a movement-coach critique of Boston's stride -- his feet splay outward at the line of scrimmage in a way flagged as inefficient and more conducive to injury -- comparing the issue to the stride mechanics of [[Kenneth Walker III]] and [[Christian McCaffrey]], both talented backs with recurring injury histories, and [[Ashton Jeanty]]. Waldman said he plans to watch Boston's movement more closely as a durability flag going forward, while stressing it's an unproven, emerging theory rather than settled evidence. Related: [[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]].
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-22 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Dynasty]: part of the Cleveland youth trio (with [[Casey Concepcion]] and [[Isaiah Bond]]) Waldman expects to take over the receiver room from [[Jerry Jeudy]] and [[Cedric Tillman]]; Waldman says Boston specifically can give the offense what they'd hoped Tillman would provide.
- 2026-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-16]]): Boston graded well on in-breakers as a prospect (79% success on digs, 81% on slants, strong on curls), which Harmon says fits well with [[Shedeur Sanders]]' heavy reliance on in-breaking routes (roughly 36% of his attempts on post/dig/curl/slant). Harmon views Boston, not [[Casey Concepcion]], as the more likely long-term outside starter of Cleveland's two rookie receivers given his size, projecting he'll run more outside-the-numbers routes -- which Harmon flags could be tough given the charting data suggests Sanders' Cleveland receiving corps skews toward in-breakers rather than boundary routes.
- 2026-07-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-31]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon expects Denzel Boston to be less fantasy-viable than [[Casey Concepcion]] in year one since he'll play more of a true X-receiver, on-ball-outside role, but likes his long-term profile as more than just a boundary X -- comping him to a tier below Michael Thomas/Drake London/Tetairoa McMillan, alongside players like Courtland Sutton and [[Michael Pittman Jr.]]. Harmon has both Boston and Concepcion ranked inside his top 160 overall and calls them an interesting dynasty tandem if Cleveland ever lands a real quarterback; he thinks a good camp could see Boston favored over [[Jerry Jeudy]] at X since Jeudy was, in Harmon's view, deployed there strangely and ineffectively last year, though he notes Boston's route tree there (contested, difficult downfield routes) may be tougher for [[Shedeur Sanders]] to feed than Concepcion's quicker, designer looks.
- 2026-08-06 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-06]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Boston is his top camp buy -- archetypically different from anyone else on Cleveland's roster (only [[Cedric Tillman]] is close, and Tillman isn't a day-one starter). He'll make life easier for presumed starter [[Deshaun Watson]], especially on 50-50 balls where Watson doesn't need to be accurate, because Boston dominates smaller corners and creates mismatches. Angelo projects Boston to lead the Browns' receiving room in targets and yards, and to lead it in touchdowns behind only [[Harold Fannin Jr.]], forming a strong trio with Fannin and [[Casey Concepcion]] on a Browns team that will play from behind often. He notes [[Jerry Jeudy]] is not, in his view, a starting-caliber receiver despite being the nominal WR1.
- 2026-08-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-10]]) [Dynasty]: per multiple Cleveland beat writers, Boston currently looks like the receiver who will lead the Browns' WR corps in targets and catches, ahead of [[Isaiah Bond]], though Waldman still views the whole Browns offensive situation -- including an unsettled QB competition -- as one fantasy managers should mostly avoid outside of [[Quinshon Judkins]].
- 2026-08-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-13]]): Denzel Boston is getting reps with Cleveland's first team as the X receiver, per coach Todd Monken, and Harmon thinks long-term he and [[Casey Concepcion]] (Z-slot) are both well set up for real playing time. For fantasy purposes this year, though, Harmon prefers Concepcion -- Boston is barely being drafted on ADP and Harmon frames him more as a very-late/final-round dart throw than an immediate contributor.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-17]]): Two independent sources called Boston the 'MVP of the offseason' in Cleveland, and Waldman said watching his tape he could easily have graded into his top five receivers in this draft class, held back only by things his tape didn't show or minor, refinable issues. Even so, Waldman still favors [[Casey Concepcion]] over Boston for now, citing OC Todd Monken's history (dating to Georgia) of funneling the most volume and the easiest, most accurate targets to the inside slot receiver -- Concepcion's role -- versus outside routes that are conceptually simpler but less schemed-open. With Cleveland's quarterback situation still shaky, Waldman said the two Browns rookies worth drafting in redraft are [[Quinshon Judkins]] and [[Harold Fannin Jr.]]; both Boston and Concepcion project as late-round or waiver-wire adds unless quarterback play improves.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]): Boston is making exciting boundary plays that are 'catnip for media,' but Waldman thinks reading the field will get harder for him as an outside receiver once defenses start disguising pre-to-post-snap coverage, which will also be tougher on his quarterback. Waldman prefers [[Casey Concepcion]] as the safer bet in Todd Monken's scheme, but would still draft both Boston and [[Cedric Tillman]] in the double digits and thinks either could have real starting fantasy upside for years if Cleveland finds a real quarterback.
