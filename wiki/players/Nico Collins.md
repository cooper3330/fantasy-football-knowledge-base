# Nico Collins

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]: names Collins as the Texans receiver "doing the harder thing" — drawing the tougher coverage assignment that lets [[Tank Dell]] operate more easily — and argues on that basis Collins is harder to replace in the Houston offense than Dell is, using the same reasoning he applies to Mike Evans over Chris Godwin and historical WR1/WR2 pairs like Isaac Bruce/Torry Holt and Larry Fitzgerald/Anquan Boldin. See [[Role Difficulty and Replaceability]].
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the entire justification for [[C.J. Stroud]]'s Wild Card clinic against Cleveland's man-heavy defense (5th-highest man rate in the NFL) — "he is just broken out to such an absurd degree," 7 targets/6 catches/96 yards/TD on a day no other Houston pass-catcher saw more than 3 targets. Calls the Stroud-Collins chemistry, built in under a full season of games together, "peanut butter and jelly." Explicit redraft note: expects Collins to jump from a 2023 "waiver-wire pickup" price point to a 2nd-or-3rd-round pick in 2024 drafts.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: "playing like top-10 receiver in the NFL this year" heading into Houston's Divisional Round game at Baltimore. Cites specific cover-6 efficiency (3.9 yards per route run, 5.6 yards after catch per reception on a 43-route cover-6 sample) as evidence Houston is schooling him into difficult in-breaking assignments over the middle against elite linebackers (Roquan Smith, Patrick Queen), not funneling him easy static routes — tying back to the [[Role Difficulty and Replaceability]] framework. Notes Houston's receiver room behind him is depleted ([[Tank Dell]] out all year, [[Noah Brown]] now on IR, [[Xavier Hutchinson]] barely used), raising his target-share ceiling but also Houston's floor risk if Baltimore takes him away.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): the floor-risk prediction plays out — Baltimore's coverage disguises "boxed him up" for most of the Divisional Round loss (10 targets, 5 catches, 68 yards, 47 of them coming after the catch, meaning very little separation created downfield). Reaffirms the season-long "top-10 receiver" grade regardless of the one tough matchup, and reiterates Houston's need to add a mid-tier veteran receiver and a dynamic tight end around him and [[Tank Dell]] rather than relying on [[John Metchie III]] and [[Xavier Hutchinson]] as the WR2/3.

- 2024-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-07]]), a comparative aside while charting [[Tee Higgins]]: asked to pick Higgins or Collins straight-up, Harmon says the talent gap the public perceives between the two — similar body types, similar outside-X roles — is "negligible" and that Collins's 2023 season was better than any season either player has had, making him "very tempted" to take Collins right now. Notes Collins profiles more as a true X (57.8% of snaps on the line in 2023) than Higgins does.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: pushing back on speculation that Houston's reported (unconfirmed) interest in a top free-agent receiver like [[Keenan Allen]] signals doubt in Collins and [[Tank Dell]] — agrees the front office likely doesn't view either as a true elite No. 1, projecting Collins in the roughly 68-target, ~1,100-yard range (a "big possession-plus receiver" comp to [[Mike Williams]]/Tee Higgins) rather than a true alpha, even off a strong 2023.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]): pushes back on a "dynasty bros" reading that Houston's reported trade interest in [[Keenan Allen]] signals stock-down news for Collins — argues Allen would have played a different, more slot-specific role than Collins (or [[Tank Dell]]), and that adding a veteran receiver is unremarkable for a team where essentially three receivers play nearly every snap; not read as negative news for Collins's outlook.

- 2024-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-04]]), reacting to Houston trading for [[Stefon Diggs]]: with Diggs now likely playing a slightly-diminished, more underneath/flanker role, Harmon thinks Collins — "a pure X" — could still lead the Texans in targets over Diggs, though says the target distribution among Collins, Diggs and [[Tank Dell]] "could go any which way" and calls the trio collectively the best receiver group in the NFL alongside TE [[Dalton Schultz]]. Notes some of the best plays in Houston's 2023 offense came from Collins and Dell working dagger/over-route combinations, and floats Diggs rotating in on backside curl/comeback routes off those same concepts.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: going at the tail end of round one in early best balls, which Waldman calls "uncomfortably high" even while acknowledging the aggressive-projection logic behind it — hasn't seen him drafted outside the second round in 30-plus best balls. Calls him the "safest" of the Houston WR trio (with [[Tank Dell]] and [[Stefon Diggs]]) specifically because he's the youngest, though drafters will likely have to pay a premium for that safety.
- 2024-05-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-21]]): mid-charting Collins's year-three profile (five games in at the time of recording), Harmon notes early context that Collins was not on the field full-time to start 2023 (weeks 1-4 snap rates of 71%, 72%, 70%, 62%, 66%, 76%) as Houston sorted out its receiver rotation, yet he still finished near 1,300 yards while running a nine/go route on only 7.9% of his sampled routes as the outside X -- evidence, per Harmon, that a smart coaching staff doesn't need its top outside receiver stacking go routes. Harmon says Collins is much better than Tank Dell working over the middle of the field, calling the two receivers' skill sets 'synergistic' rather than redundant. Full profile not yet published as of this episode.
- 2024-05-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-29]]): reacting to news the Texans extended Collins at roughly $24M/year (just under Davante Adams money, in line with DK Metcalf money), Harmon pushed back hard on the take that "Collins didn't do anything before C.J. Stroud" -- his 2022 reception perception profile already showed 77.6% success vs. man and 82.5% vs. press as an X receiver running a varied route tree. James Koh (untracked co-host) added a 61% success rate on the nine route and 73% on the corner from that pre-Stroud sample, plus respectable slant and strong curl numbers. Harmon calls Collins a stud who played like one of the league's best receivers last year and says the extension is fully earned, independent of Stroud's arrival. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman sees Collins as Houston's true alpha receiver following his new contract, at age 25 versus [[Stefon Diggs]] (31) and [[Tank Dell]] (a slot/big-play specialist, not a primary X). He projects Collins for 116 targets, 15.0 yards per catch, roughly 1,200 yards and 7 touchdowns — similar target volume to Diggs (114) but far superior yardage efficiency. Waldman put last year's WR12 finish near Collins' realistic ceiling, projecting him to land comfortably 'inside the top 20' with high-end WR2 upside overall. He noted Diggs could still outperform Collins this season given talent, but that the target hierarchy — per source Jonathan Alexander of the Union Chronicle — runs Collins, Diggs, Dell in that order.
