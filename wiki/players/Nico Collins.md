# Nico Collins

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]: names Collins as the Texans receiver "doing the harder thing" — drawing the tougher coverage assignment that lets [[Tank Dell]] operate more easily — and argues on that basis Collins is harder to replace in the Houston offense than Dell is, using the same reasoning he applies to Mike Evans over Chris Godwin and historical WR1/WR2 pairs like Isaac Bruce/Torry Holt and Larry Fitzgerald/Anquan Boldin. See [[Role Difficulty and Replaceability]].
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the entire justification for [[C.J. Stroud]]'s Wild Card clinic against Cleveland's man-heavy defense (5th-highest man rate in the NFL) — "he is just broken out to such an absurd degree," 7 targets/6 catches/96 yards/TD on a day no other Houston pass-catcher saw more than 3 targets. Calls the Stroud-Collins chemistry, built in under a full season of games together, "peanut butter and jelly." Explicit redraft note: expects Collins to jump from a 2023 "waiver-wire pickup" price point to a 2nd-or-3rd-round pick in 2024 drafts.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: "playing like top-10 receiver in the NFL this year" heading into Houston's Divisional Round game at Baltimore. Cites specific cover-6 efficiency (3.9 yards per route run, 5.6 yards after catch per reception on a 43-route cover-6 sample) as evidence Houston is schooling him into difficult in-breaking assignments over the middle against elite linebackers (Roquan Smith, Patrick Queen), not funneling him easy static routes — tying back to the [[Role Difficulty and Replaceability]] framework. Notes Houston's receiver room behind him is depleted ([[Tank Dell]] out all year, [[Noah Brown]] now on IR, [[Xavier Hutchinson]] barely used), raising his target-share ceiling but also Houston's floor risk if Baltimore takes him away.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): the floor-risk prediction plays out — Baltimore's coverage disguises "boxed him up" for most of the Divisional Round loss (10 targets, 5 catches, 68 yards, 47 of them coming after the catch, meaning very little separation created downfield). Reaffirms the season-long "top-10 receiver" grade regardless of the one tough matchup, and reiterates Houston's need to add a mid-tier veteran receiver and a dynamic tight end around him and [[Tank Dell]] rather than relying on [[John Metchie III]] and [[Xavier Hutchinson]] as the WR2/3.

- 2024-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-07]]), a comparative aside while charting [[Tee Higgins]]: asked to pick Higgins or Collins straight-up, Harmon says the talent gap the public perceives between the two — similar body types, similar outside-X roles — is "negligible" and that Collins's 2023 season was better than any season either player has had, making him "very tempted" to take Collins right now. Notes Collins profiles more as a true X (57.8% of snaps on the line in 2023) than Higgins does.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: pushing back on speculation that Houston's reported (unconfirmed) interest in a top free-agent receiver like [[Keenan Allen]] signals doubt in Collins and [[Tank Dell]] — agrees the front office likely doesn't view either as a true elite No. 1, projecting Collins in the roughly 68-target, ~1,100-yard range (a "big possession-plus receiver" comp to [[Mike Williams]]/Tee Higgins) rather than a true alpha, even off a strong 2023.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]): pushes back on a "dynasty bros" reading that Houston's reported trade interest in [[Keenan Allen]] signals stock-down news for Collins — argues Allen would have played a different, more slot-specific role than Collins (or [[Tank Dell]]), and that adding a veteran receiver is unremarkable for a team where essentially three receivers play nearly every snap; not read as negative news for Collins's outlook.

- 2024-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-04]]), reacting to Houston trading for [[Stefon Diggs]]: with Diggs now likely playing a slightly-diminished, more underneath/flanker role, Harmon thinks Collins — "a pure X" — could still lead the Texans in targets over Diggs, though says the target distribution among Collins, Diggs and [[Tank Dell]] "could go any which way" and calls the trio collectively the best receiver group in the NFL alongside TE [[Dalton Schultz]]. Notes some of the best plays in Houston's 2023 offense came from Collins and Dell working dagger/over-route combinations, and floats Diggs rotating in on backside curl/comeback routes off those same concepts.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: going at the tail end of round one in early best balls, which Waldman calls "uncomfortably high" even while acknowledging the aggressive-projection logic behind it — hasn't seen him drafted outside the second round in 30-plus best balls. Calls him the "safest" of the Houston WR trio (with [[Tank Dell]] and [[Stefon Diggs]]) specifically because he's the youngest, though drafters will likely have to pay a premium for that safety.
- 2024-05-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-21]]): mid-charting Collins's year-three profile (five games in at the time of recording), Harmon notes early context that Collins was not on the field full-time to start 2023 (weeks 1-4 snap rates of 71%, 72%, 70%, 62%, 66%, 76%) as Houston sorted out its receiver rotation, yet he still finished near 1,300 yards while running a nine/go route on only 7.9% of his sampled routes as the outside X -- evidence, per Harmon, that a smart coaching staff doesn't need its top outside receiver stacking go routes. Harmon says Collins is much better than Tank Dell working over the middle of the field, calling the two receivers' skill sets 'synergistic' rather than redundant. Full profile not yet published as of this episode.

James Koh (untracked co-host) added a 61% success rate on the nine route and 73% on the corner from that pre-Stroud sample, plus respectable slant and strong curl numbers. - 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman sees Collins as Houston's true alpha receiver following his new contract, at age 25 versus [[Stefon Diggs]] (31) and [[Tank Dell]] (a slot/big-play specialist, not a primary X). He projects Collins for 116 targets, 15.0 yards per catch, roughly 1,200 yards and 7 touchdowns — similar target volume to Diggs (114) but far superior yardage efficiency. Waldman put last year's WR12 finish near Collins' realistic ceiling, projecting him to land comfortably 'inside the top 20' with high-end WR2 upside overall. He noted Diggs could still outperform Collins this season given talent, but that the target hierarchy — per source Jonathan Alexander of the Union Chronicle — runs Collins, Diggs, Dell in that order.
- 2024-06-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-06]]): Collins' 2023 Reception Perception charting shows one of the largest year-three jumps he's ever seen -- success rate vs. man went 68% (rookie) to 71% (year two) to 77.6% (year three, 94th percentile), and vs. press 71.9% to 74.5% to 82.5% (96th percentile), a list that otherwise includes only Justin Jefferson, Tyreek Hill, Davante Adams, A.J. Brown, Stefon Diggs, Antonio Brown, Tyler Lockett and Odell Beckham Jr. Harmon comps Collins to Allen Robinson's delayed breakout in Chicago (a receiver who also never had good QB play early), and notes Collins beat Puka Nacua's success rate on dig, curl and slant routes. He also dominated after the catch (in space on 14.5% of routes, broke tackles on 14.3%) and posted a strong-but-not-elite 79.9% success rate vs. zone. Harmon calls RP metrics 'sticky' year over year and expects Collins to stay in this neighborhood. He argues Collins belongs in a 'tier two' bucket with [[Mike Evans]], [[DeVonta Smith]] and [[Terry McLaurin]] -- better than actual Tee Higgins' early-career numbers -- and flags that CBS/ESPN ranking him outside the top 35 and Harmon's own Yahoo rank of WR32 overall undersell him relative to Underdog's top-20 Best Ball ADP. Notes Collins is signed long-term (contract extension) and profiles as the clear X receiver: 83.2% snaps out wide, 89.1% on the line.
- 2024-06-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-27]]): Comparing Collins' 2023 Reception Perception profile (77.6% success rate vs. man, 79.9% vs. zone, 82.5% vs. press) to [[Brandon Aiyuk]]'s similar 2022 numbers (77.1% man, 79.4% zone, 79.3% press), Harmon said any receiver posting 77%+ vs. man with 80%+ vs. zone/press is a bettable 'true superstar' profile and would have projected both into that tier entering their respective third seasons. He gave Collins' 2023 profile a slight statistical edge over Aiyuk's 2022 one specifically because of after-catch explosiveness: Collins was in space on 14.5% of routes, broke multiple tackles on 14.3% of those, a single tackle on 48.6%, and went down on first contact only 37.1% of the time -- numbers he called 'crazy.' Despite Aiyuk's reputation as a yards-after-catch receiver, Harmon argued Aiyuk's route running (not YAC) is now the better part of his game, and while Collins edges Aiyuk on this specific profile comparison, Harmon still says he'd take Aiyuk as the overall better player. Both are placed in his 'tier-two superstar' bucket, with Aiyuk seen as having more ceiling to jump a tier if more heavily featured.
- 2024-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-11]]) [Best Ball]: Collins posted a 77.6% success rate vs. man (94th percentile) and 96th percentile vs. press in 2023, and Harmon flatly rejects any 'fluky season' framing -- he ran the full RP database back to 2014 and found every receiver to ever post an 82.5%+ single-season success rate vs. press coverage (Stevie Johnson, Justin Jefferson, Tyreek Hill, Davante Adams, A.J. Brown, Stefon Diggs, Allen Robinson, Antonio Brown, Tyler Lockett, Odell Beckham Jr.) went on to multiple productive NFL seasons at worst. Collins had already flashed 71% vs. man and 74% vs. press the year before his breakout, so Harmon calls it a legitimate third-year developmental arrival rather than a fluke, and says he's the best receiver on Houston's roster over [[Stefon Diggs]] and [[Tank Dell]]. He projects Diggs' presence to draw extra coverage and raise Collins' ceiling further -- his median 2024 projection is 1,365 receiving yards with 1,750 as the upside case -- and says he's 'much higher on Nico Collins than much of the fantasy community.'

Harmon dismisses target-competition worries entirely: Collins charts in the 94th percentile vs man and 96th vs press, is a superstar and a top-10 real-life receiver right now, and as the clear X will be on the field every snap, ending the low snap rates that capped him last year. He expects [[Stefon Diggs]] to move to more of a slot/flanker role and thinks Diggs and [[Tank Dell]] will eat into each other more than into Collins; he likes Dell and expects him to keep climbing, but not as a full-time feature player based on preseason usage. James Koh (not tracked) adds that Diggs improves Houston's scoring-position rate and, with no goal-line back and [[C.J. Stroud]] not a tush-push quarterback, 13–14 touchdowns is very reachable.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Harmon reiterates his hot take that Collins will lead the NFL in touchdown receptions and says he is the receiver he is highest on this offseason — his third-year RP profile was awesome after two good ones, he can win in the X role, and crucially he has the clearest role on a crowded team. Asked whether Collins can play inside, Harmon says sure: solid success rate versus zone, good separation on quick-hitters, a monster after the catch and clearly the biggest body in the room. With Houston cutting their fullback after heavy 21/22-personnel usage, he expects more 11 personnel and some concepts where Collins kicks inside, including in the run game since [[Stefon Diggs]] is not a blocking receiver. Collins lined up outside 80% and in the slot 20% last year; among the best route combinations in 2023 were Collins on big overs with [[Tank Dell]] running deep out-breakers.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-12]]): Houston's receiver room was the one Harmon says looked exactly as he predicted, and Collins is unambiguously the alpha — 'a top-10 real-life NFL receiver' and the team's X, lined up outside on damn near 100% of his snaps. Harmon's description of the skill set: he will win deep, including a 55-yard shot play against two defenders downfield, and he will also run a crisp route with great hands to win a game in the fourth quarter. In the route division of labor Harmon assigns Collins the go routes and the big deep overs over the middle, with [[Tank Dell]] the outside breaking-route runner and [[Stefon Diggs]] the underneath layup separator. - 2024-09-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-17]]): Collins looks incredible and he and [[C.J. Koh noted Collins was barely featured in the first half and that it was 'a chore' to get him the ball, requiring designed looks. His case: the Rams secondary is not good on the back end even though they will bring confusing stunts, blitzes and games up front, and if the Texans line can hold up long enough for [[C.J. The worry list: Houston fired the offensive coordinator, 'fired the left tackle into the sun,' lost both guards, traded for Ed Ingram, a guard Minnesota wanted rid of over run-game play, may bench Cam Robinson behind Aireontae Ersery, lost [[Joe Mixon]], is relying on [[Nick Chubb]] and just lost [[Christian Kirk]]. Harris still likes [[C.J. Stroud]] and admits he has been 'whistling past the graveyard' — he also warns against the lazy habit of declaring an offensive line bad because you don't know the names, since teams believe they improved and the NFL reinvents itself yearly. But teams are wrong sometimes, and a top-eight offense was dismantled.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): three catches for 25 yards, and Harris says this game laid bare the worst of the worries about any Houston player, specifically the one we care about most. He did not even notice Collins until a first-down catch in the third quarter. Ten different Texans got targets and none topped 32 yards; Collins got one single downfield throw very early and simply was not open, and it was not close. The most generous reading Harris offers is that Houston was winning for a while and it was always a one-score game, so they were not stubborn about forcing him the ball, and he would like them to be more stubborn. He cannot confirm in real time that Collins was doubled on every route, and notes nobody is. Conclusion: still a really good player you have to use, but not a good beginning. He also has questions about the rebuilt offensive line, where the guards were mostly okay but the tackles were again a problem and [[C.J. Stroud]] was feeling heat.
