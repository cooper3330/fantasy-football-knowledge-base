# Nicholas Singleton

## Expert Takes

- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Going Deep)]]): the 2025 back who stands out to him immediately, listed at 6-0, 224. Waldman likes that a back that size is built to run into traffic and actually plays like it — he understands play designs, sets up double teams well, and gets defenders into blockers. The trait he keeps returning to is that Singleton runs with his knees and feet high in traffic, kicking his heel to his butt when he anticipates a defender shooting at his legs, the way [[Alvin Kamara]] did. Those two things — sticking to the play design and getting through trash for extra yardage — are exactly what he wants from a high-volume runner. Power is there and acceleration and speed are good if not elite, which Waldman calls more than acceptable for a starter. [[Brandon Angelo]] lists him among the many legitimate day-two-caliber playmakers in what both call one of the strongest running back classes in recent memory.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10]]): if the listed 6-foot, 224-pound measurements hold, Singleton is more than [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] — he could become what people hoped Etienne would be. [[Brandon Angelo]] slots him second in the class behind [[Ashton Jeanty]] and ahead of [[Ollie Gordon II]] and [[Quinshon Judkins]], comping him to [[Isiah Pacheco]]: very linear, not much make-you-miss, but he will run through your face mask and by you on the same play, with genuine size-speed and good work through contact. Angelo's caveat is that the Pacheco brand of football carries injury cost, though it is the style that earns snaps regardless of draft round. He wants to watch Singleton's All-22 once the season wraps.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: his favorite 2026 running back to watch, and a player he expects to like more the more he watches, despite others being down on him. At 6-0, 224 he is patient with zone blocking, has good footwork and gets his feet over trash, shows notable acceleration in one-on-one open-field matchups and can beat downhill corners with it. Waldman recalls grading Singleton and [[Quinshon Judkins]] as freshmen for Felix Sharp's show and preferring Judkins slightly, without much conviction. The signal he emphasizes is methodological: Singleton's *incomplete* grade already sits level with the *complete* grades of [[Woody Marks]], [[Jarquez Hunter]] and [[Bhayshul Tuten]] from the 2025 class, which is why he expects the final number to be higher.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]) [Dynasty]: on the 2026 class, Waldman likes what he has seen from Singleton as a gap runner and notes he is legitimately 220 pounds and legitimately fast once he gets going. In zone blocking he does some things okay — he can set up double teams and press fairly deeply — and Waldman thinks he presses a little longer off tackle, so the outside zone element is probably going to need more work and he wants to see improvement there. On the comparison to teammate [[Kaytron Allen]], whom a lot of people like a little more, both Waldman and guest Dan Hatman (Scouting Academy, NOT tracked) said they are simply different players serving different purposes, and you can see why Penn State wanted to use both. Waldman likened the pairing loosely to Marion Barber and Laurence Maroney in usage terms.
- 2026-02-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-09]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman rates Singleton above backfield mate [[Kaytron Allen]], citing more explosion and better receiving ability. He notes Singleton was used in a 'Mr. Inside, Mr. Outside' committee at Penn State by design rather than lack of talent, and expects Singleton's NFL career to outperform his college production once he is the clear lead back.
- 2026-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-01]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman called Singleton a well-built runner who pulls through reaches from all three levels of defenders and -- rare among today's backs, in his view -- accelerates into unavoidable collisions with good pad level, letting him bounce off hits and force gang tackles.
- 2026-04-09 — According to Paul Pertichese, guest analyst on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-09]] (not a tracked expert) [Dynasty]: Nicholas Singleton was once viewed as a future first-round back after exploding as a Penn State freshman, but Pertichese says he simply never developed the vision, patience and run instincts to match his elite athleticism -- which is why Penn State kept feeding touches to [[Kaytron Allen]] instead of him despite Singleton's superior big-play juice. He is a good receiving back who will carve out a role, but Pertichese worries his flaws surface the more touches he's given, capping his ceiling as a three-down back. Host [[Chris Harris]] drew a direct parallel to [[TreVeyon Henderson]]'s rookie season with the Patriots, and Pertichese agreed the outcomes rhyme, though he rated Henderson's Ohio State tape (vision/patience) ahead of Singleton's coming out.
- 2026-04-09 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-09]]) [Dynasty]: identifies Nick Singleton as the rare back in this class who could add real mass productively -- 8-10 lbs up to roughly 230 -- without losing what makes him effective, because his game is built on initial/early acceleration and linear power rather than lateral change-of-direction, the trait that typically erodes first when backs add weight. Angelo compared carrying added mass well to how [[Saquon Barkley]] does it, and contrasted Singleton with [[Bucky Irving]] and [[Emmett Johnson]] -- both reactive, lateral movers for whom he said added weight 'wouldn't really work' because it would blunt the change-of-direction quality their games depend on.
- 2026-04-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-13]]) [Dynasty]: his talent grades higher than his projected Day 3 draft slot, though he thinks the nature of the running back position will cap where he actually goes -- comparing the value to Green Bay's bargain 2025 signing of [[Damien Martinez]]. Calls Singleton a very good receiver who was strong early in his career despite a poor quarterback and a lot of manufactured offense at Penn State. Argues the committee usage with [[Kaytron Allen]] reflected a program need to keep recruits happy in the NIL era rather than a real talent gap, comparing it to how [[Nick Chubb]] split time with [[DeAndre Swift]] and Sonny Michel at Georgia -- warns against reading that split as evidence Singleton isn't as good as scouts think. Believes Singleton is capable of being more than what he's shown the past couple of seasons.
- 2026-04-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-20]]) [Dynasty]: Asked if Nicholas Singleton is underrated, Waldman said yes -- his college production was capped by Penn State needing to rotate multiple backs, a normal pattern unless a back is a true outlier (his 'Randy Moss-type talent' comp for the rare exception), not by a lack of ability. Waldman highlighted Singleton as a very good receiver and a smart, situationally-aware runner who plays within what's asked of him, with the size, speed and hands all present. He said he 'would not be surprised at all' if Singleton develops into an NFL starter.
- 2026-05-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-18]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman flagged Singleton as a late-round RB3 flyer worth a dart throw in Tennessee for contingent upside.
- 2026-07-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Dave Kluge (footballguys.com, untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-10]]): Kluge, who scouted the 2026 rookie RB class, says he preferred [[Kaytron Allen]] to Nicholas Singleton at Penn State because word out of the program was that Singleton mentally checked out once Drew Allar got hurt and the offense started loading the box, while Allen got more efficient with the extra work -- Singleton fell to Day 3 despite five-star recruit pedigree and real athletic upside. Both agree his 2026 outlook is now purely a health/vibes question after a broken foot suffered in spring: if he's a camp non-factor, both expect the Titans backfield to look like the last couple years (Pollard lead, Spears change-of-pace); but if he heals faster than expected and looks involved, Kluge would move both [[Tony Pollard]] and [[Tajay Spears]] down, though he's still unsure he'd even draft Singleton himself given how hard it is for a rookie who misses camp to earn work mid-season.
- 2026-07-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman admits he might be 'a little too into' Singleton, a fifth-round rookie behind [[Tony Pollard]] and [[Tyjae Spears]] in Tennessee, and flags his own optimism as classic 'rookie lust' -- the appeal of the unknown -- rather than a grounded projection of a real role this season.
