# Jalen Royals

## Expert Takes

- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]): Royals is his most under-bought 2025 receiver, discounted mainly because a foot injury cost him seven games. Over his last four games he averaged 160 yards on 10 catches at over 16.5 yards per reception and was ascending into one of the better receivers in college football before the injury. Angelo profiles him as a really good short-to-intermediate boots-on-the-ground receiver, better in the slot than outside, reliable in the intermediate areas, with chops at the catch point adjusting in flight, and quarterback-friendly as an extension of the run game. He expects teams to grade him late second to early third; Angelo himself has an early round-two grade. He notes Royals is reportedly cleared for the combine/pro day and wants to see him move in a T-shirt and shorts. [[Matt Waldman]] adds that a full season would likely have meant 1,500 yards and 100 catches and a late-first dynasty valuation, and calls him a safe pick who can produce.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): the Utah State receiver tempos his breaks well across zone coverage, but shows his eyes too early — he needs to wait until he has passed the buzz/rollover defender before turning them. Waldman saw this repeatedly on 2024 tape and reads it as a sign Royals does not yet know when to settle and when to keep working across the field. His example: against tight man, Royals settles on a crossing route when the quarterback expects him to continue on the move, which Waldman warns will produce confusion and interceptions in the NFL until it is cleaned up.
- 2025-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-16]]): the Utah State senior (6'0, 205, 4.42, 93rd-percentile 10-yard split) is ranked 13th of the 14 receivers then on the site, and Harmon is openly lower than most big-name evaluators. The red flags are deployment: 84.6% of sample snaps at left outside receiver, 14.9% slot, one snap at outside right, one in the backfield, with a route tree of 29.1% curls, 24.7% slants and 8.9% screens — unlike [[Jack Bech]], no multi-position history. Charting: 68.9% vs man (46th percentile), 61% on nines (good, above the 55% bar), 73.5% curls, 74% slants, 60.7% corners, 33% comebacks on tiny sample; 8 of 13 contested catches. Koh's read is play strength and short-area shake as clear positives but a DeVante Parker problem — great contested-catch because he is contested constantly. JJ Zachariason (not tracked) raised a [[Rashee Rice]] data comp, which Harmon accepts athletically while noting Rice needed Andy Reid to invent a role. Harmon would take him in round four and prefers this archetype early day three; he would rank [[Tory Horton]] ahead of him.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]) [Dynasty]: absolutely underrated, and the reason is [[Rashee Rice]]. What Royals does best is what Rice does best, and Waldman argues Royals is almost as good on contested catches with the ball in the air — he is what Rice was and what they hoped [[Skyy Moore]] would be. Combined with [[Brandon Angelo]]'s view that Rice's injury is harder than advertised, the fourth-rounder from Utah State becomes a sneaky good addition to a room that still has [[Xavier Worthy]], Rice, [[Marquise Brown]] and [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]].
- 2025-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-09]]): a classic Chiefs receiver pick and good value in the fourth round — Harmon admits he was lower on Royals only because others in the industry had him as a day-two pick, and he actually likes him at this cost. He revisits JJ Zachariason's (not tracked) [[Rashee Rice]] comparison from Royals' prospect guide: an identical 10-yard split, basically identical build, and a one-side-of-the-field X who ran a limited route tree in college, just like Rice — and notes a Chiefs executive said post-draft they viewed him as Rashee Rice insurance, which Harmon thinks is sensible given Rice's major injury and off-field trouble. On the charting, his speed is well expressed and he can take the top off, he is strong on digs at a 77.3% success rate, and he gets open in the intermediate area; Harmon would use him from the slot or off the line in designed touches because of his run-after-catch ability. James Koh (not tracked) also loved the pick, citing sticky hands, slipperiness after the catch and a 4.42 forty at 6-0, 205, and trusts Andy Reid to find the right spot.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: underrated, and one of the two players in the class ([[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]] the other) who slipped for reasons unrelated to ability — injury and lack of game volume rather than tape. Angelo notes Royals was on pace for roughly 1,400 to 1,500 yards before getting hurt, and says the market is discounting him mainly because drafters are unsure how the Kansas City receiver room shakes out, including the [[Rashee Rice]] situation. [[Matt Waldman]] groups him with Croskey-Merritt as the class's biggest slipped-for-circumstance values.
- 2025-05-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-21]]): Harmon says he did not like Royals when he was being discussed as a second-round pick but really likes him as a fourth-rounder, especially in the Kansas City system, and notes the profile compares favorably to [[Rashee Rice]]. He frames Royals and [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]] as the Chiefs' two Rice insurance policies — big-slot types who cover a suspension or injury absence — rather than the outside receiver the room actually lacks; press-man separation on the perimeter was not the strength of Royals's game either. Koh (untracked) agrees both hosts are relatively high on Royals and notes Kansas City has deliberately spread the position around since trading [[Tyreek Hill]], stacking Royals, Worthy, Rice and Marquise Brown as interchangeable insurance instead of naming a primary receiver.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Best Ball]: Royals (6-foot, 205) is a sneaky late-round watch and is 'definitely a handcuff to [[Rashee Rice]].' Waldman argued Rice's injury was more difficult than the discourse suggests, and Rice takes a beating as essentially a running back in space, so the question is how fast he recovers from punishment even if he looks full speed in camp — if he breaks down within weeks, Royals is the guy who could take over, because he is a very good runner after the catch, physical, very fast, sudden, and has the skills at the catch point to win contested balls. Waldman likes him as a late option you can cut after the first month if it does not break. Bob Harris (not tracked) noted Kansas City keeps stockpiling similar-sized receivers into a crowded room.
- 2025-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-18]]): Royals is Harmon's under-the-radar name to absorb targets during a [[Rashee Rice]] suspension, alongside [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]] and [[Travis Kelce]]. Harmon wrote up a Rice comparison pre-draft — Rice is 6-0, 204 with interesting athletic testing and Royals is near-identical in size with a matching 1.49 ten-yard split (93rd percentile) — and notes Royals, like Rice as a prospect, lined up on one side of the field and ran a lot of hitches as an outside receiver, so he could benefit from being moved into slot, in-breaking and designed-touch work where his speed and tackle-breaking play. JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) had the same Rice comp before the draft. Caveat: a fourth-round rookie out of Utah State, and Harmon's usual 'we never know with rookies' warning applies; Koh (not tracked) is high on his elusiveness.
- 2025-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-25]]): Royals is buzzing as [[Rashee Rice]] insurance and Harmon calls him a pretty darn good player — 6-foot, 205, 4.42 forty, with an identical 1.49 ten-yard split (93rd percentile) to Rice, which is why JJ Zachariason's data guide (Zachariason is not a tracked expert) gave him a favorable Rice comp. He played only seven games in 2024 with ankle injuries, which Harmon believes caused his draft slide; Harmon had him as a fair day-three pick. He was okay versus man at 68.9 percent but needs work versus press and settling into zone windows, and is raw as a route runner after running an X-receiver tree in a very wide-open offense, so he will need role catering. The standout is after the catch: in space on 58 percent of sampled catches, down on first contact on under half, multiple broken tackles on 17.2 percent, plus contested-catch ability and play strength beyond what you would expect at his size. Harmon wants to see what Andy Reid unlocks.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-02]]): Royals was back on the field in Week 4 but ran only four routes, and Harmon says he has a long way to go to crack the rotation as a rookie receiver behind [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]], [[Xavier Worthy]], [[Marquise Brown]] and [[Tyquan Thornton]]. He reiterated the prospect-series view that Royals shows shades of [[Rashee Rice]] in his profile, and expects him to make his impact as an inside player at some point in his career — noting that in his handful of Week 4 routes he lined up mostly outside, which is not where Harmon thinks he wins. Bottom line: it is going to take him a minute, and he is not taking Smith-Schuster's role right now.
- 2026-03-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-10]]): With veterans Hollywood Brown, Juju Smith-Schuster and Tyquan Thornton all gone from Kansas City, Harmon singled out [[Jalen Royals]] as 'the young name that's in here that people haven't really thought about, but I am intrigued by,' and said he wants to see what Royals can do with expanded opportunity in 2026.
- 2026-06-02 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-06-02]]) [Dynasty]: with Rashee Rice sidelined (legal issues, rehabbing), Royals is getting starting reps at Chiefs OTAs, and Andy Reid said Royals 'made a big jump,' citing knee tendinitis as the reason his rookie season was a non-factor (36 routes, 2 catches for 4 yards, plus a 42-snap Week 18 sample). Harmon says he had mixed feelings on Royals as a prospect: an extreme skewed-alignment receiver at Utah State (85% of sample snaps from left-outside), running a stripped-down tree (29% curls, 25% slants, ~15% nines) with strong marks on the routes he did run (77% success on the dig, 61% on the nine). His calling card was yards-after-catch/open-field ability -- he broke a tackle on 48% of his in-space targets -- which Harmon says is why he and JJ Zachariason (untracked) both liked a Rashee Rice comp for him coming out; matching measurables reinforce it (Royals 6'0/205 with a 4.42 forty and 93rd-percentile 10-yard split vs. Rice's 6'0/204 and identical 93rd-percentile split). Harmon frames Royals as Rice's direct backup and worth monitoring now that he's healthy and getting run.
- 2026-06-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-08]]): Waldman likes fourth-round pick Jalen Royals as a viable [[Rashee Rice]] replacement if needed, saying Royals actually had better hands than Rice on similar looks coming out of college and shows skill after the catch; he and Cyrus Allen (not a tracked page) would handle the screen-heavy role Rice currently owns if the Chiefs needed a fill-in.
- 2026-08-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-03 (Feel It or Fk It with Ian Hartitz)]]) [Dynasty]: he'd hold onto Royals rather than drop him for the more hyped [[Cyrus Allen]], noting Royals was getting real spring/camp looks in place of an injured Rashee Rice before Royals himself got banged up after a good camp.
