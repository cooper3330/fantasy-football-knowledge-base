# Josh Allen

## Expert Takes

- 2024-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-04]]), reflecting on the [[Stefon Diggs]] trade to Houston: Allen "cementing himself as an elite quarterback" coincided directly with Diggs's 2020 arrival in Buffalo, after showing early stabilization signs in 2019 (his first year with John Brown and Cole Beasley). Harmon relays that Allen has told him personally he doesn't care about route depth or play design when throwing to Diggs — "I'm cool with him just improvising and getting open, I will find him" — and frames Allen as a fearless, willing-to-play-off-script quarterback who aggressively locks onto Diggs downfield, a dynamic that elevated both players' reputations simultaneously ("they were perfectly timed for each other"). Notes the two had a publicly emotional relationship (a since-referenced clip of Allen thanking Diggs) that makes the trade "sad" given how untenable the situation had become behind the scenes by the time it happened.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: going late second/early third round in early best balls, but "I'm not touching Josh Allen. I'm not going anywhere near him" — would only reconsider if he fell to round five or six, which he doesn't expect to happen. Prefers a receiver, running back, or top tight end at that price instead. Would reconsider a share if Buffalo landed a high-value rookie receiver early and it pushed Allen's price down. Separately floats [[Dalton Kincaid]] as effectively already Buffalo's wide receiver one, and discusses hypothetical Bills draft-fit archetypes for a rookie WR1 ([[Brian Thomas Jr.]], [[Keon Coleman]], [[Xavier Legette]], [[Johnny Wilson]], [[Javon Baker]]) to pair with Allen. See [[Stefon Diggs]] for Waldman's read on why Allen and Diggs's relationship soured.

- 2024-04-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-15]]: in the [[C.J. Stroud]] dynasty-QB1 debate, cites Allen's "consistent level of production" even with [[Stefon Diggs]] already gone for most of last season as a reason he still can't be displaced from the top of dynasty quarterback rankings.
- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]): has a low-level worry about Josh Allen's makeup and commitment level, speculating he could be an early-to-mid-career version of Ben Roethlisberger in terms of wearing on coaches and teammates (not criminal behavior, but decision-making); still expects the on-field production to hold up and would bet on him remaining a stable fantasy option.
- 2024-07-01 — According to Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: Bob notes Allen has finished QB1 or QB2 three of the last four years, largely on rushing equity, and his ADP has fallen from the second round in prior years to late third/early fourth this year -- asking whether he's still worth his QB1-overall ceiling with Stefon Diggs traded to Houston. Waldman is not fully bought in as QB1 overall, calling the field 'so close between him and probably four or five other quarterbacks' -- he'd want at least two of Khalil Shakir, Curtis Samuel, Keon Coleman and Dalton Kincaid to play up to potential for Allen to justify that tag, and thinks Diggs will actually play better in Houston away from Allen, calling that 'a little bit of a knock' on him. They cite a counter-argument from Eric at NFL.com that Allen benefits from not force-feeding targets to Diggs or to Gabe Davis on plays he knew would be incomplete. Waldman floats a theory that Diggs wanted a more mentally/conceptually sharp quarterback (contrasted with C.J. Stroud) rather than one who leans on physical talent.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10]]): the 9-for-30 game against Houston looked like a quarterback who got bored of Joe Brady's offense — repeatedly passing up open short throws for the hard, low-probability downfield answer, then missing it or forcing a throw that had to be slung into a tight window. Waldman's comp was Tim Robbins' pitcher in Bull Durham wanting to bring the heat every pitch. [[Brandon Angelo]] said it looked like [[Anthony Richardson]] morphed into Josh Allen, or 'back to Wyoming Josh,' and attributes it to a superhero mentality that takes the covered deep crosser over the check-down. Both agreed this is the next step in his development: Waldman calls him talented enough to have leaned on talent for a long time, Angelo calls him the most talented quarterback in the NFL who still has to take what is given. Corollaries: the offense revolves on [[Khalil Shakir]]'s health, Allen does not like safety blankets as much as he should, and losing [[James Cook]] to turf toe would be horrendous — though [[Ray Davis]] could supply the physical run element they lack.
- 2024-12-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-09]]): Allen is the 'elite' tier of the fantasy black hole framework — after becoming the first player in NFL history with three passing and three rushing touchdowns in a regular-season game (342 yards, 82 rushing yards, 44-42 loss to the Rams), a 55-point fantasy line that Jason Wood noted is the highest for any fantasy QB in 20 years. Waldman's distinction is that Allen is elite precisely because he still supports his teammates: [[Khalil Shakir]] can be counted on week to week and he expects [[Amari Cooper]] to join that group once his wrist is fully healed. Allen gets his regardless of the route — throwing himself a touchdown, running it — to the point that some weeks he is 'three quarters of the way' to being a fantasy starter by himself, despite constant turnover in the receiving corps.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Harmon took Allen over 20.5 completions at -105, reasoning Buffalo will not be able to establish its usual run-focused plan against Baltimore's run defense, so the game becomes Allen matriculating the ball down the field on short extension-of-the-run throws rather than vertical shots — something he is very comfortable doing. James Koh (not a tracked expert) argued Allen is the X factor and has to put Buffalo on his back, that he is playing at an MVP level, and that Baltimore cannot simply pressure him because Allen will rumble; running zone at him lets him pick you apart. Harmon also relayed Allen's own comments on chemistry with [[Stefon Diggs]]: if a route is supposed to be seven yards and you run it at nine or ten and you are open, he is still throwing it.
- 2025-01-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-27]]): asked what Buffalo lacks to maximize Allen, Waldman's first answer is a joke with a point — 'have [[Patrick Mahomes]] as their backup' — meaning the roster problem is small and the era problem is large: absent the Mahomes era, Allen would already have been to a couple of Super Bowls, and each loss has come down to about a drive. What he does want is Allen throwing with more daring: connecting with [[Dalton Kincaid]] on go-up-and-win-it throws up the seam rather than 'stinking dunk ball', and expanding the roles of [[Keon Coleman]] and [[Khalil Shakir]]. Bob Harris (not tracked) noted Matt Bitonti of Footballguys ranks the offensive line fifth, that defensive injuries hurt, and that [[Amari Cooper]] had the governor put on him in this scheme. Both defended Sean McDermott against calls for firing, Waldman citing the Marty Schottenheimer precedent in Cleveland.
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): Allen played great football all year but came out 'jittery' in Kansas City — his first pass attempt was nearly picked and his second was picked — before settling in to finish 22 of 34 for 237 yards with two total touchdowns and no turnovers. Harmon's structural critique is that on the fourth-down blitz from a condensed formation there was no hot answer, and that Buffalo lacks the perimeter man-beater who would force a great game planner like Steve Spagnuolo into a bind. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) argues the opposite reading: Allen's maturation is exactly why this offense is more un-guardable without a No. 1, since you cannot take a single player away. Both flag the repeated Allen sneaks as over-used once they stopped working.
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]): Allen is the quarterback at the top of Waldman's early Football Guys rankings, ahead of [[Lamar Jackson]] and well ahead of [[Jayden Daniels]], on the grounds that he is a proven multi-season producer rather than a projection. Waldman cited the record explicitly: QB6 in 2019, then QB1 three times (2020, 2021, 2023), QB2 in 2022 and QB3 last year. His stated principle is that if he is going to pay the premium for a high-end quarterback, it goes to the consistent producer.
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-09]]): revisiting his pre-draft grade, Waldman said he did not have a high grade on Allen and saw him as a project with significant upside who did not understand how to read the field or work in a pocket, with moments of hero ball that were 'ridiculously bad' from over-reliance on athleticism. His argument is that the evaluation community reversed Allen and [[Lamar Jackson]] — the profile people assigned Jackson (raw athlete needing a total rebuild) actually described Allen. He also used Allen as a warning against hard evaluation cutoffs: on a surface-level screen, Allen is exactly the quarterback a threshold rule rejects outright, and he turned out very well.
