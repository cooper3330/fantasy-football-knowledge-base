# Mack Hollins

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-10-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-03]]): 'enough already with Mack Hollins' — he has run the fourth-most routes on the Bills (about 70% in week 4) while giving nothing as a separator or from a speed standpoint, and Harmon's objection is structural as much as it is about the player. If anyone in Buffalo should be doing the sacrificial-X, on-the-line work it should be Hollins, freeing [[Keon Coleman]] to play off the ball; instead Hollins is the one used more as the off-line player. Harmon acknowledges the blocking value in a physical run offense but says the outside pairing of Hollins and Coleman cannot beat man coverage, which is exactly what the Ravens exposed.
- 2024-10-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon and James Koh (not tracked) both object to Hollins' route volume in Buffalo — Koh says he had the same rant about him in Las Vegas. Hollins runs ~27% of his routes from the slot, so he is doing less pure sacrificial-X clear-out work than [[Keon Coleman]], yet he is on the field constantly while also serving as the team's punt gunner. Harmon points to a third-down slot fade in the Houston game where Hollins wasn't where he was supposed to be, and to a play where Hollins drifted inside off his line instead of running straight, costing a walk-in touchdown on one of [[Josh Allen]]'s 9-of-30 passing day. His broader point is that when your route-runners are not separators, these become coin-flip plays and the offense turns low-percentage. He argues [[Curtis Samuel]] should be running downfield routes ahead of Hollins, and does not buy that finishing training camp earns Hollins the job.
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): Hollins was 'the least discussed pass catcher on the team' and yet showed up in big moments in the AFC Championship, going four targets, three catches, 73 yards and a touchdown — co-host James Koh (not tracked) called him legitimately Buffalo's best receiver in that game 'by a million miles.' Harmon treats Hollins' power and blocking as a genuine, transferable role, and holds it up as the template he would rather see [[Keon Coleman]] occupy. The caveat both hosts return to is that a Hollins/Coleman outside pair against a top defense is a structural problem, not a solution.
- 2025-02-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-28]]): Hollins led all Bills wide receivers in routes run with 408 and led the entire team in touchdowns with five, which even Harmon — who predicted before Week 1 that Hollins would play more than people realized — did not see coming. He credits it to Buffalo valuing his blocking in Joe Brady's run-heavy, big-bodies offense, plus a few splash plays. But he is a dirty-work supporting player, and Harmon says you obviously cannot have that guy as your top perimeter wideout. He guesses Hollins re-signs in Buffalo, and notes that flipping Hollins' 31 percent slot rate with [[Keon Coleman]]'s 10.8 percent would already be better usage for both.
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]) on Hollins signing with New England for two years, $8.4M: he likes it on and off the field. On field, New England needs perimeter options — Hollins is a pure perimeter guy in a room stacked with interior types like [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] (a slot/flanker they need to get back on track), [[Demario Douglas]] and [[Javon Baker]]. Off field, Harmon says last year's New England receiver room was 'very immature and poorly coached' with a real lack of professionalism, and he expects Hollins to bring effort and leadership. The hosts spend some time on his eccentricity — no shoes, eating with his hands, laundry advice TikToks — with Koh saying he brings levity to a locker room that seemed dour. Harmon notes the signing also checks two free-agency cliche boxes elsewhere in the market: knows-the-system and best-game-of-his-career-against-the-team-signing-him.
- 2025-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-28]]): Hollins projects as New England's nominal starting X receiver alongside [[Stefon Diggs]] at flanker and Demario Douglas in the slot, but Koh's framing is that Hollins will 'do the Mack Hollins' — be on the field a lot without any promise of target volume. Harmon calls the X spot genuinely open competition rather than something New England paid up for, notes Kayshon Boutte took a lot of X snaps last season, and expects the Patriots to add another receiver in a draft where they hold two third-rounders. Both value him as a work-ethic and leadership presence in a room they describe as young and immature in 2024.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): with [[Stefon Diggs]] the clear lead and no Kayshon Boutte, Hollins was 'the actual replacement' — a bunch of targets, little joy in the first half, then a huge third-and-14 stutter-go for 54 yards in the fourth quarter that put New England inside Tampa's 10.
