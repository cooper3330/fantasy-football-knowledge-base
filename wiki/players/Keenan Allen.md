# Keenan Allen

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]): **feeling it** as a top-5 wide receiver. The general principle first — "you don't always have to have blazing speed to be a top wide receiver; it's about how they fit in their offense" and about route-running skill. What separates Allen (and [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]) from receivers who are only zone route runners is that he wins against both man and zone, and is used inside and outside — versatility is the trait doing the work. See [[Zone vs Man Route Running]].
- 2023-12-18 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode [Best Ball]: notes 2023 was "a change in offense, no change in outcomes" for Allen — if anything he was better. His drafting rule for the January best-ball market: keep betting on Allen and St. Brown until you get more information, while flagging that Allen's offense could change again in 2024.
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: "irresistible" as a fourth-round pick, assuming Los Angeles keeps him (cap questions are live but Waldman is hoping they work something out). Base case: over the games Justin Herbert played healthy in 2023 (roughly Weeks 1–12), Allen was fantasy's WR2. New HC Jim Harbaugh and OC Greg Roman have a run-heavy reputation, but Waldman argues that history (see [[Justin Herbert]]) points to more efficient, "dink and dunk" quarterback play under this staff rather than fewer opportunities for Allen specifically — a scheme that should suit Allen's game more than a pure vertical receiver like Mike Williams (not a tracked page).

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): the Chargers' cap situation (the NFL's highest wide receiver spend, $76M, entering 2024) is flagged as a real risk to Allen's roster spot, alongside Mike Williams (not a tracked page) — notes Allen "is not a young man" either. If he leaves, the Chargers would be left with a room of just Josh Palmer (not a tracked page) and [[Quentin Johnston]], pushing the team from "substantial" into "critical" wide receiver need.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: reacting to Allen's trade to Chicago, still sees no signs of decline — calls 2023 arguably his best season (a Chargers franchise-record 108 catches, 1,243 yards, WR2 overall from Weeks 2-12/13) and expects him to sustain that playing opposite [[D.J. Moore]] with rookie [[Caleb Williams]], predicting good early rapport between the two. Concedes the aging cliff "comes fast and comes suddenly," with a new system/QB as the kind of unexpected wrinkle that could trigger it, but says he's "a little bit more of an optimist" than that scenario. See [[Aging Curves and Career Longevity]].

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]), reacting to the finalized trade to Chicago: Harmon's charted read pushes back a bit on the "best season" framing — Allen posted career highs in receptions and yards (150 targets, 108 catches, ~1,250 yards, 7 TDs in 13 games), but Harmon reads him as "clearly not the same player he used to be," just still very productive in a narrower role. 61.4% of his 2023 routes were dig/curl/slant/screen/flat "layup" routes, where he graded elite (87.3% success on the slant, 83.3% on the curl, 93.1% on the flat). Overall success rate vs. man fell to 71.9% (bounced back from an injury-marred 66.9%, but still well off his 2015-2020 career peak of 76.6-77.6%); success rate vs. zone remains strong at 80%. He played off the line (backfield/slot usage) on a career-high 72.5% of snaps. Harmon's framing: not a decline so much as the Chargers deliberately shifting his role to fit his age-32 physical profile — still a "textbook, teach-tape" route runner other receivers study, but no longer explosive enough to win downfield regularly, making him the "epitome of the quarterback-friendly archetype." Calls the pairing with [[D.J. Moore]] in Chicago an excellent complementary fit: Moore as the vertical/man-beating outside "high read," Allen as the reliable underneath "low read" — a duo Harmon says he can't recall a rookie quarterback (Caleb Williams presumed) getting paired with before. Also relays that Allen said publicly the Jets and Texans were also in trade talks.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): disagreement
  between the hosts over a rookie [[Caleb Williams]]'s target distribution if
  Chicago adds a receiver at 9 — Koh bets Allen leads the team in targets over
  the first six games given his ability to win underneath; Harmon flatly
  disagrees, "there's no scenario where Keenan Allen gets more targets than
  [[D.J. Moore]]," and reads a Koh-favored outcome as more likely to mean the
  offense itself is struggling, not that Allen is thriving.
- 2024-05-14 — According to Adam Rank ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-14]]), not a tracked expert: [[Keenan Allen]] is 'probably a little long in the tooth' with the Bears and likely can no longer handle a full X-receiver workload, but Rank still expects him to be moved around the formation and used primarily inside, complementing [[DJ Moore]] and [[Rome Odunze]] rather than anchoring the offense on the outside.
- 2024-07-30 — According to James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-30]]): Koh contends publicly that Allen will lead the Bears in targets, receptions and yardage over the first four weeks of the season, because for the first time in years he is not the number one and will face a lower level of cornerback play with no double teams — while a rookie quarterback adjusting to NFL speed goes to the guy who is 'dang wide open every damn time'. He expects [[D.J. Moore]] to take over as the season progresses and notes Allen is longer in the tooth and likely fades late. [[Matt Harmon]] agrees Allen should be a pure slot at this stage of his career and frames him as almost always the low read on a high-low concept, with Moore and [[Rome Odunze]] as the high reads — so the target projection depends entirely on how willing [[Caleb Williams]] is to work in structure and check down, which is a real question given how much he was pushed out of structure at USC. Both note Allen is part of what Koh calls the second-best receiver trio in the NFL behind Houston.
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): Harmon reiterates his stance that Allen is now a role player rather than a needle-mover, and points out he was the leading first-read target in Week 1 — the game in which the Bears offense was at its worst. He and Koh raise the possibility that the Bears are overloading a rookie quarterback with options and that [[Caleb Williams]] looked more decisive in Week 3 locking onto first reads. Harmon's condition for welcoming Allen back is usage-based: he is fine with it if Allen absorbs the DeAndre Carter snaps, but [[DJ Moore]] and [[Rome Odunze]] need to be more involved, not less. Both hosts flag this as theorizing rather than a settled conclusion.
- 2024-11-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-05]]): Harmon is blunt that Allen is no longer an effective player and says he has been calling the steady decline for a couple of years — with aging receivers you never know whether the slope stays gentle or the player falls off the cliff, and Harmon is 'pretty concerned that we're at the cliff' with Allen. The problem for Chicago is that Allen still expects eight to ten targets a game and still leads the team since Week 5 with a 19.3% target share on his routes (nobody on the roster is above 20%), but the results on those looks are generally not good beyond quick outs at the goal line and on third down. Harmon argues the offense looked more decisive with Allen off the field.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]): blunt on the impending free agent — he wants to see Allen get in shape, saying he is not moving with fluidity or sharpness. The prescription is a usage change: cut the option routes, which he is no longer quick enough to run, and use him as a pure slot receiver on quick out-breakers, where Harmon thinks he still runs routes really well. He is skeptical Chicago will get there given the churn — Shane Waldron out, Thomas Brown promoted twice in a month from passing game coordinator to offensive coordinator, and Matt Eberflus fired leaving Brown as interim head coach.
- 2024-12-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-23]]): since Thomas Brown replaced Shane Waldron as Chicago's play caller, Allen and [[D.J. Moore]] have 'dominated' — both top-15 receivers over that stretch, with Allen finishing this week as the overall WR2. He caught a 45-yard touchdown from [[Caleb Williams]], the longest of Williams's career. Waldman ties the production directly to the play-calling change rather than to anything new in Allen's profile.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Allen can still be useful in the right environment and Harmon thinks he was overused in Chicago. His Reception Perception read is that Allen's metrics have hovered at a declined but still interesting and usable level for a while — not a clean cliff, which is Harmon's general point that receiver age cliffs are effectively impossible to predict. The open question with him and the rest of the 2025 free-agent class is how much target share they will want to command on a new team. Harmon would not be surprised if some of these veterans have an impact season left in them.
- 2025-02-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-04]]): Houston tried to trade for Allen before landing [[Stefon Diggs]], and he is now a free agent, but Harmon does not expect a Texans reunion. He relays that Allen has allegedly said he will only play in Los Angeles or Chicago or he will retire, doubts the Chargers have much interest and is unsure the Bears do either, and separately does not think Allen's style of play fits the kind of offense Houston is building.
- 2025-02-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-26]]): Allen is a real name to watch for the Rams slot role vacated by [[Cooper Kupp]] — co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) raised him first, citing strong Los Angeles ties and a desire to keep playing, and asked whether there is anything left in the tank. Harmon called it a good call from a pass-production standpoint but attached two clear negatives: Allen ranked 48th in first downs per route run in 2024, even lower than Kupp at 42nd, and he was among the worst blocking slot receivers in the league last season — a steep drop from Kupp, one of the best blocking receivers in football. Harmon acknowledged he has been in a zone of only talking poorly about Allen because he has been a declining player of late, while noting the Bears offense made nobody look efficient in 2024.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): still unsigned on May 27 and genuinely hard to place, because he is a slot-only player at this stage who nonetheless commands an outsized role on reputation. Harmon notes Allen was in the slot on only 52.6% of snaps in Chicago last year because the Bears needed slot reps for [[Rome Odunze]] and screen/designed work for [[DJ Moore]], and that Allen was a poor blocker, ran wrong routes and failed to settle in the right areas. He believes Allen's presence contributed to [[Caleb Williams]] and Odunze's connection suffering. Allen has reportedly said he only wants Chicago or Los Angeles; Harmon calls the Chicago interest one-sided. Harmon floated New Orleans (Kellen Moore reunion) with the caveat of progress-blocking a young room; Koh (not tracked) preferred Arizona, Denver or Atlanta, and Harmon countered that Denver would block [[Marvin Mims Jr.]], [[Pat Bryant]] and [[Devaughn Vele]].
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: both hosts call Allen badly undervalued after re-signing with the Chargers. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked) cites a 27.2% target share last year, 11th best in the NFL, on a Bears team with [[D.J. Moore]], [[Rome Odunze]] and [[Cole Kmet]], and 150 targets with [[Justin Herbert]] two years ago; he expects Allen to slot in second in targets behind [[Ladd McConkey]], working the slot and all levels of the field, which in turn frees McConkey for chunk plays. He reads the signing as an indictment of [[Quentin Johnston]] and [[Tre Harris]]. Kluge has him inside his top 40 receivers and says that still feels too low, noting [[Davante Adams]] and [[Mike Evans]] go in round four and [[Stefon Diggs]] in round seven while Allen fell to rounds 12-14 in his weekend drafts. Waldman agrees on the undervaluation and adds, via former NFL defensive end Ryan Riddle, that a younger Allen was versatile enough to play flanker or slot at different playing weights — an older Allen may stay inside, but both he and McConkey could move around.
- 2025-08-13 — According to Dave Kluge of Footballguys (guest, not a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-13]]: Allen's ADP is very wrong now that he has landed back with the Chargers — he is going around round 14, and Kluge says he will reach three or four rounds ahead of that because ADP moves slowly through chaos (Mike Williams retiring, Rashawn Slater and [[Najee Harris]] injured, Allen signing). He frames it as a general edge: exploit the lag while ADP is still settling. [[Chris Harris]] is not surprised the market is cool on '87-year-old Keenan Allen', whom he loves to death, and does not push back on the value.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]]): Allen is his number three late-round player, going roughly 13th round and in many leagues undrafted because people forget he is still in the league. The case is simple — Harris has seen Allen post a 100-catch season with this exact quarterback, and feels safe saying Justin Herbert likes throwing to him. He will be subsidiary to [[Ladd McConkey]] and Chargers camp suggests Allen is more the flanker with McConkey in the slot, though both move around. The caveats are real: Allen caught 70-something passes in Chicago without many touchdowns, it was not impressive on film, and a player with the best hands suddenly started dropping the ball, plus he is old for the NFL. Guest JJ Zachariason (not tracked) loves the call, comparing him to Anquan Boldin as a receiver who never relied on separation and can therefore last, and notes Allen's yards per route run last year was actually better outside than in the slot; he thinks the Chargers wanted line-of-scrimmage versatility because [[Tre Harris]] is off to a slow start and [[KeAndre Lambert-Smith]] is a field stretcher.
- 2025-08-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: reading his brand-new 2025 Almanac profile, Harris calls Allen a top-50 receiver option for him — maybe not a thrilling fantasy bench option in the middle rounds, but potentially useful, specifically as a bye-week fill-in. The Chargers blinked in August after reacquiring [[Mike Williams]] only to see him retire and hyping [[Quentin Johnston]] only to see him stink; with [[Ladd McConkey]] plus mid-round rookies [[Tre Harris]] and [[KeAndre Lambert-Smith]], Harris says it was obvious [[Justin Herbert]] needed more. The caveats are pointed: Allen is a slow 33-year-old entering his 13th season, probably not best suited away from the slot, but McConkey needs the slot, so word out of camp is Allen has settled into the flanker role, where he can motion for short-area space but will see more man coverage than he is used to. Harris says Allen's 2024 film was still flattering in a phone booth and that he was good in the red zone later in the year — he can still score close-in touchdowns, he just cannot make plays down the field. He notes Allen was part of the 'undeniable Bears' hype of 2024 that collapsed into reaching for [[Caleb Williams]] mortars.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Harris, who calls him my eldest son, says Allen looked like a pretty good 12th-round fantasy pick even though he was obviously tough to start in this first game. He dropped a deep ball late in the first quarter but immediately made up for it with a deep cross for a touchdown. Part of the broader Chargers-wideouts bump alongside [[Quentin Johnston]] and [[Ladd McConkey]], who was kept out of the end zone only by a slightly overthrown ball at the end of the first half.
