# Andrew Armstrong

## Expert Takes

- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]): Armstrong is his pick for the most underrated player in the 2025 class by a large margin — 6'4, 204, an 1,100-yard receiver on 76 catches at Arkansas this year, roughly 1,800 yards over two SEC seasons, and before that a 1,200-yard, 10-touchdown season at Texas A&M Commerce. Waldman rates his hands close to the best in the class (good rather than great, with a fixable habit of not quite getting his fingertips together), praises his timing routes, toughness at the catch point and work on the boundary, and comps the profile to Hakeem Nicks — someone you can isolate on an island with a two-way go against a mismatched corner. The caveat is speed and exposure: he played through a hamstring injury early and could not drop his weight in breaks for the first four to five games, may not get an all-star game invite, and Waldman wants to see roughly a 4.5 before committing. He notes one league source he asked had never heard of him.
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]) [Dynasty]: the Arkansas receiver is the second of Waldman's two players with the talent to vastly outplay draft position, alongside [[Tahj Brooks]]. Waldman expects a late-round pick or UDFA, but says a well-calibrated radar should have him in late-round consideration rather than as an unknown name. The profile: he can play inside or outside, tracks the ball very well, is one of the better route runners in the class, and has great size and ball skills. Waldman was shocked he got no all-star game invite and said he personally recommended Armstrong to people connected with the all-star games. Daniel Jeremiah (not a tracked expert) framed sub-4.6 as a win for him; he ran roughly 4.52. Waldman also noted personal context — Armstrong's older brother Arthur was killed in a police shooting during a mental health episode in mid-December 2024 — as a likely distraction, while judging him purely as a football player.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): Armstrong is close to the top of Waldman's underrated list in the 2025 receiver class, though he teased a second, unnamed player (the subject of an RSP giveaway contest) as possibly even more underrated. The evaluation: an excellent route runner against both zone and man, one of the better sideline and boundary receivers especially working with his back to the quarterback, at roughly 6'2 and 206 pounds, projecting as a good possession-plus receiver. Waldman said Armstrong does a lot of the things [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] does, without the same rating and perhaps not quite the speed. He praised Armstrong skipping Combine testing on the stated grounds of working on becoming a better receiver rather than performing to a test, and guessed he would run around 4.52 if he had tested.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): the Arkansas receiver is a skilled pass catcher who wins over the middle and wins with his back to the quarterback up the rails against tight coverage, with sharp, flat breaks back to the passer. Waldman classifies him as a possession-plus style receiver who can also win after the catch, credits good length and height, and notes his long speed tested better than people expected.
- 2025-04-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-22]]): Armstrong is his best deep sleeper in the 2025 class and he is 'confused' more people are not talking about him — 12th in the FBS in receiving yards and a first-team All-SEC nomination at Arkansas. A pure X: 93.9% of sample snaps outside, 6.1% slot, none in the backfield, on the line for 76% of snaps, at roughly 6-4, 202-204, 4.54, 88th percentile wingspan. Reception Perception shows 71st percentile vs zone with high-volume, high-success in-breakers (85% dig, 84% curl, 84% slant) and 83rd percentile vs press, but only 66.7% vs man, which Harmon calls 'not that great,' and little downfield impact. His mechanical fix is stride length — Armstrong takes choppy steps as if counting them rather than striding out, which Harmon believes caps him as a man-coverage beater. Caveats: he turns 25 on Halloween of his rookie year (lost COVID season, transferred up from Texas A&M Commerce), and Harmon pre-empts the level-of-competition argument himself. Frame: not a home run swing, but 'get on base' value as a third-round X who can beat press.
- 2025-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-09]]): Harmon put a day-two grade on Armstrong and was stunned nobody clicked his name — he led the SEC in receiving yards, finished 12th in the FBS, and made first-team all-SEC, yet went undrafted; Harmon jokes he may be more shocked by that than by Detroit trading up for [[Isaac TeSlaa]], who had half Armstrong's receiving yards on the same team. The Reception Perception marks: success rates on digs, curls, comebacks and outs all between 83.8% and 87.5%, all run at high rates relative to prospect averages, 71st-percentile versus zone and 83rd-percentile versus press, and a pure outside player at 93.9% of sample snaps outside and 76% on the line. The knocks are that he got caught up on vertical routes and did not separate, leaving his overall man coverage score below average, plus choppy steps Harmon wants strided out, an age of 25 by Halloween, and a weird career path (a COVID-lost season, lower-level start). At 6-4, 205 with a 4.51 forty and 37.5-inch vertical he blocks well and fits the big-X shape Miami lacks behind [[Tyreek Hill]], [[Jaylen Waddle]] and [[Malik Washington]], though [[Nick Westbrook-Ikhine]]'s contract blocks him. James Koh (not tracked) calls it a very tough spot and notes the strange one-touchdown season on 78 catches for 1,100 yards; Harmon thinks it is a poison, declining environment but that making the roster is realistic and jokes that rostering him on a taxi squad is what dynasty is for.
- 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]): Armstrong, signed by Miami out of Arkansas, was a prospect Harmon liked — he led the SEC in receiving and Harmon was shocked he went undrafted, giving him a day-two grade in his stacked rankings. The caveat is age: Armstrong turns 25 on Halloween of his rookie year. Harmon says he profiles as a pretty decent X receiver prospect in his own right and is fighting to make the roster, grouping him with [[Malik Washington]] as the deep sleepers of the Dolphins group.
- 2026-08-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-11]]) [Dynasty]: Armstrong was a 2025 prospect Harmon liked on tape at Arkansas — a big-bodied receiver profile Kansas City could use — but a tough evaluation because he turned 25 as a rookie, muddying what his profile actually indicates. He's picked up some camp buzz early on.
