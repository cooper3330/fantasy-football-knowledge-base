# Russell Wilson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February–March 2024. Team/role are as of that date
> and are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until
> superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): a hedged, not-fully-resolved read. Waldman expects Wilson to open 2024 as a Week 1 starter somewhere, but is doubtful he's still a starter by the time that season (or his career) is over, and says plainly he hasn't watched enough of Wilson's 2023 Denver tape to form a fully confident opinion either way. Relays a harder-line outside view from **Dwain McFarland** (not a tracked expert): that the league-wide shift toward Cover-2-high shells, which took away the deep game Wilson's whole career was built on, effectively ended his run as a viable NFL starter. Waldman pushes back on pinning 2023's struggles entirely on Wilson himself — Denver's receiving corps ([[Jerry Jeudy]], [[Courtland Sutton]]) isn't strong enough to support a downfield-attacking passer regardless of arm talent, and the receiver room got worse in-season (Tim Patrick's injury, K.J. Hamler's retirement). Explicitly "not all out on" Wilson. Floats Pittsburgh as a landing spot he's "reading between the lines" on and would like to see, or alternatively a Cleveland fit if the Browns move on from [[Deshaun Watson]].
- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]): Wilson officially signs with the Pittsburgh Steelers (Denver still owed $39M of the remaining deal, paying all but $1.2M of it). Sees real upside — Wilson turning [[George Pickens]] into a "superstar" is plausible given a receiving room "by a significant margin" more talented than what he had in Denver ([[Jerry Jeudy]], [[Courtland Sutton]]) — while repeating that his post-2021 statistical decline coincided with the league playing more Cover 2 against him. Notes Pittsburgh's public "not a lost cause" framing of [[Kenny Pickett]] mainly protects Pickett's trade value rather than signaling he's the preferred starter.

- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): far more
  skeptical of the on-field impact than the "upgrade over Kenny Pickett"
  framing suggests. Harmon questions whether Wilson is even a top-20
  quarterback next year. Charted [[Jerry Jeudy]]'s 2023 Denver tape and saw
  Wilson repeatedly miss him schemed wide open on crossing routes — Wilson's
  long-standing difficulty seeing and working the middle of the field has
  only worsened with age and lost mobility; became "a check-down artist" last
  season. Cites Joe Dolan of Fantasy Points (not tracked) comparing Wilson's
  2023 surface stats to Carson Wentz's deceptively-decent 2021 Indianapolis
  season, and Warren Sharp's (not tracked) stat that Wilson threw 27 passes
  behind the line of scrimmage in 2023, the most of any of 509 qualifying
  QBs since 2005. Skeptical Pittsburgh will run "a real normal offense";
  guesses the Steelers finish around 9-8 or 10-7 at best, with real doubt
  Wilson is even the Week 1 starter or finishes all 17 games (explicitly not
  an endorsement of Kenny Pickett as the alternative). Contract framed as a
  low-cost, low-risk bet — effectively $1.2M since Denver is still paying out
  the rest — "who cares" about the signing itself; the question is upside,
  not price.


- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: reacting to reports Wilson is "in the pole position" over [[Justin Fields]] in Pittsburgh rather than a settled starter — says it doesn't change his opinion. Wilson is "one play away from being a two-time Super Bowl-winning quarterback," so the team is giving him deserved credit as the established veteran, but he still has to prove he can rebound from his last two years in Denver; notes every quarterback situation should be a real competition except for a handful of established stars.
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman is cautiously optimistic on a Russell Wilson rebound in Pittsburgh, citing Doug Drinen's Pro Football Reference stat that Wilson had the NFL's fourth-lowest bad-throw percentage last year (13.2%) as encouraging given how little he had to work with in Denver -- underachieving receivers, a shaky offensive line, an up-and-down run game -- and argued that throwing the ball away in that context reflects the supporting cast more than Wilson. He believes Sean Payton never wanted the Denver marriage to work, pointing to Payton publicly criticizing Wilson on a primetime broadcast, and says with Arthur Smith naming Wilson QB1 over Justin Fields, Wilson could immediately be the best quarterback Smith has coordinated aside from Ryan Tannehill. Cecil Lammey (untracked) offered the counterargument that Wilson threw the ball away the seventh-most in the league last year and didn't run Sean Payton's offense effectively, which Waldman called a fair point but not decisive.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): Waldman says Wilson is done being a media darling but is not done as a football player, blaming Denver's receivers — he watched the tape and saw Wilson give up after one pump fake because nobody was running the right route. He points to a five-game stretch with [[Jerry Jeudy]] hitting deep crossers, and rates Wilson underrated on the intermediate-to-deep seam and the sail route, throws Pete Carroll rarely called. Waldman projects about 3,500 yards. Guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) is more concerned Wilson is cooked, arguing no quarterback has been hurt more by defenses playing the deepest safety far deeper — that erases the moon ball and the deep crosser, Wilson's two money plays, by turning single-high looks into double coverage.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): asked about Dan Graziano's ESPN report that the Pittsburgh quarterback competition is more open than believed, Waldman said he does not buy that [[Justin Fields]] is making a move — if the staff thought so, reps would be split more visibly than reported — and that Wilson is the guy they will roll with. He treated the story as ordinary training-camp speculation rather than news, though he credited Graziano as a professional who did not need to reach for it. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) added the financial context that Pittsburgh is paying almost none of Wilson's $38 million and is actually paying Fields more this year, and noted Wilson is going around QB29 with Fields at QB31.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): Wilson is the better choice for the Steelers over [[Justin Fields]] and he is 'feeling it' on the veteran. His case is that Wilson has been in big games, understands how to manage a game, reads the field better than Fields at this stage, and meshes well with a play-action, vertical-throwing offense — which is what Pittsburgh wants to run. He adds a schedule wrinkle: the Steelers face three AFC West teams including Denver in their first six games, and whatever one thinks of Wilson's Broncos tenure, he has an unusually good understanding of those defenses. Waldman allows Fields may have the higher long-term upside; the original plan was Wilson leads while Fields resets.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]): Wilson's Steelers debut — three touchdowns and 264 yards against a Jets defense that had been the league's stingiest — justified Mike Tomlin's decision, which Waldman called 'the easiest layup in the world.' The moon balls 'are back and they look good'; he can change plays at the line and 'wins from the pocket better than [[Justin Fields]] does.' Waldman conceded slow early moments, some inaccurate third-down throws and underthrown balls to [[George Pickens]], and Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) cautioned it is one game against possibly the right circumstances for a team that was 28th in passing and 20th in scoring. Waldman expects improvement with reps and argued the broader lesson is that 'experience matters at the quarterback position.' He also pushed back on the reported mutiny story and on the media caricature of Wilson, saying Wilson has been 'boiled down to a meme' and turned in a subdued, gracious postgame that credited Fields.
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Harmon came in skeptical Wilson was an upgrade given his Denver film, and now grants that he did upgrade the position — but only in one dimension. Next Gen Stats had him 8-of-9 for 150 play-action yards, most by a Steelers QB since 2016, and he activates the downfield shot plays Pittsburgh lacked under [[Justin Fields]]. The passing chart, though, shows 'quite literally nothing' in the intermediate and deep middle past five yards, which Harmon calls the same touchdown-or-checkdown profile as the 2023 Broncos that got him replaced. James Koh (not tracked) argues you can't build a functional offense on that shot-play frequency — Wilson is 'spamming it like a kid playing Madden' and the interceptions will come. Harmon expects a spurty but more dangerous offense, and isn't sure Fields is done for the year.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31]]): Pittsburgh went to Wilson over Justin Fields for down-to-down consistency and offensive elevation, not because Fields is bad. Waldman says the Steelers knew what they had in Fields and knew they could be competitive with him, but not that the offense could access all its weapons; Wilson is a field general who operates — takes the short stuff, knows when to throw it away, and does not lose 22 yards in field-goal range chasing a hero play — and that is what NFL coordinators want. Waldman's core mechanism is the moon ball: Wilson's touch on deep balls forces corners to be patient about when to turn and how to attack, which is why he had success with [[George Pickens]], [[Calvin Austin III]], [[Van Jefferson]] and two big tight ends, and historically with smaller receivers like Doug Baldwin, Tyler Lockett and Paul Richardson. Angelo adds Wilson lets you win in bad Pittsburgh weather because he will not make mistakes, and thinks Fields put good tape out for his next team.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): Waldman is 'totally feeling' the Steelers as a Super Bowl dark horse with Wilson, and publicly enjoyed Sigmund Bloom's apology for past criticism of him. He does not expect Pittsburgh to beat the Ravens for the division, but thinks the combination of an elite defense, the run game, the big plays Wilson generates in the passing game and Wilson's experience as a two-time Super Bowl quarterback makes them the type of team that could be the Cinderella that reaches the AFC Championship game and gives the Chiefs, Bills or Bengals real trouble. He credits Pittsburgh's stability — Mike Tomlin as only the third coach in his memory, 'the best franchise in football' — as part of the reason. Waldman adds an aside that everything Bloom said about Wilson would have been better applied to [[Kyler Murray]], of whom he is not a fan.
- 2024-11-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-25]]): asked whether Wilson was exposed in the Thursday snow game (21-of-28, 270 yards, a touchdown, but heavily sacked), Waldman said he is both a good snow quarterback and good enough generally. He liked the command of the short passing game, better situational play within drives, and much better ball distribution than [[Justin Fields]] gave the Steelers — Fields hurt the team more often. Waldman said Wilson's snow throws looked tighter than [[Jameis Winston]]'s in the same conditions, including a clean deep ball to [[Calvin Austin III]] that looked like there was no snow at all. The cost of doing business is sacks and wild off-schedule forays, but he does not throw into defenders. Bob Harris (not tracked) disagreed on the outcome, insisting the offense simply has to score more points.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]): Waldman would retain Wilson (and [[Justin Fields]]) in Pittsburgh and thinks Wilson is the team's best chance to reach the playoffs and win there in 2025. He judged Wilson played well enough this season, conceded he is maybe not quite the same player he once was, and argued that giving him a full year with this team could actually make him a little better. The comparative case: fixing what surrounds Wilson is an easier project for the Steelers than fixing what Fields needs to fix conceptually.
- 2025-01-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-27]]): 'feel it' that a Wilson–Pete Carroll reunion in Las Vegas is plausible — they have made amends, and Waldman speculates John Schneider may have been the odd leg of that three-legged race in Seattle, while allowing Wilson was part of the problem too. His character read: teammates like Marshawn Lynch recognized Wilson as a capable quarterback but felt he 'big timed' them and never opened up, and a little humility since could help. As a player he still sees enough — Wilson can throw go-up-and-get-it balls to [[Brock Bowers]], [[Jakobi Meyers]] or a free-agent/draft addition. The ceiling is limited: Waldman thinks the Raiders are still the worst team in a brutal AFC West (Harbaugh, Reid, Payton, all with quarterbacks), but Wilson gives stability at the position to build around.
- 2025-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-30]]): the rumored Pete Carroll reunion in Las Vegas, potentially with Darrell Bevell as coordinator, leaves him cold — 'the Russell Wilson experience is the Russell Wilson experience, you're going to get what you're going to get,' and he would far rather see Bevell's Miami-influenced ideas applied to [[Sam Darnold]]. He also lumps Wilson in with [[Jalen Hurts]] as a quarterback around whom every offense collapses into the same offense. Both hosts note the Seattle bridge looked burned but that desperation on both sides could override it; Koh (not a tracked expert) leans on the devil-you-know argument given a thin free-agent quarterback market. Harmon prefers a [[Geno Smith]] reunion with Carroll instead, arguing there is more love in that relationship.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): Wilson has a distinctive game — a moonball deep thrower and scrambler rather than an intermediate timing-route passer or a rushing-yardage gainer — so scheme fit matters more than usual. Waldman thinks he would be very good with the Giants specifically because it pairs with [[Malik Nabers]]'s 'I can win any ball against anyone at any time' mentality; he describes Nabers in that role as a plus [[George Pickens]] who is only minus on height, and would add a possession complement such as [[Cooper Kupp]] or [[Chris Godwin]]. His baseline valuation: stripped of line, receivers and coach, Wilson is worth at least six wins on his own by putting a team in position, and 9-11 wins with quality around him. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) notes conflicting reporting on Pittsburgh's real interest in Wilson versus Fields.
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]): Waldman is fine with Wilson to the Raiders on one condition — that Las Vegas doesn't repeat what Pittsburgh did in siccing Arthur Smith on him. He cited reporting that after Wilson threw for nearly 500 yards against Cincinnati, Smith told him not to change the system or make adjustments based on what the defense was doing, which Waldman called the nail in the coffin for Smith: taking a player who is performing well and telling him to be a pixel in the offense rather than a quarterback, with worse results. On that basis Waldman thinks Wilson is better than most of what we saw in Pittsburgh. He also framed Wilson as the right Raiders choice specifically if you draft a quarterback behind him to overtake him — his preferred version being [[Cam Ward]] sitting a year or two.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]): Wilson is the higher-floor option of the two free-agent veterans while [[Aaron Rodgers]] is the higher ceiling, and Wilson's edge is that he and [[DK Metcalf]] already know how to work with each other from their Seattle years. Waldman notes Wilson has picked up a reputation for backdooring coaches and speculates Wilson may have been the source of the 'told to stop veering from the script' leak, calling that kind of communication pattern unhealthy but widespread among players and coaches. Bob Harris (not tracked) is harsher on the 2024 Steelers season: they won early, the offense went stale, and they made the playoffs on defense, adding that [[Justin Fields]] was fantasy QB6 through six games before being benched for Wilson.
- 2025-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-28]]): Wilson's one-year Giants deal (10.5M guaranteed, escalating to 21M only in a 17-0 season) and the contract gap versus Jameis Winston make him the clear intended starter, with Winston likely QB3 if New York also drafts one. Harmon calls himself unimpressed at this stage of Wilson's career and says he was always limited — reliant on outside go routes and moon balls — and has since lost the middle of the field, ranking 33rd among quarterbacks in short middle yards per game (33.6), grouped with Aidan O'Connell. Derek Klassen's 2022 charting had the dig route as one of Wilson's worst throws at a 57% success rate, with under-10-yards over the middle his weakest area. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is milder, calling him a roughly QB23-26 who is now 36, protects the ball better (11 games, 64% completions, 225 yards per game, 16 TD to 5 INT) and should at least make the Giants more competitive.
- 2025-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-08]]): Harmon has been skeptical of Wilson since his last Seattle year and sees plenty of spots on the Giants' early schedule where he plays himself onto the bench for [[Jaxson Dart]] — but he expects real go-route production with [[Malik Nabers]] in the meantime. Per Fantasy Points, Wilson led all quarterbacks with 72.9 yards per game on go and post routes in 2024 and had the seventh-best passer rating on them; Nabers posted a 64.4% success rate on the nine and 92.9% on the post with almost no such targets. James Koh (not tracked) pushes the check-down concern and Harmon confirms it with data: Wilson was fifth in check-down rate in 2024 at 11.3%, led the NFL in 2023 at 18.6% under [[Sean Payton]], and was already at 10.6% in 2022 before Payton. Both note the Steelers' check-downs went to running backs ([[Jaylen Warren]], [[Najee Harris]]), suggesting [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] rather than a slot receiver benefits in New York.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): 'fuck it' — Wilson does not start the whole season. Waldman is careful to say Wilson did not play poorly; the Giants offense did, with heavy pressure and a bad line — Andrew Thomas was out with a foot injury, and Waldman initially misread a play where a lineman got pushed into the backfield and ran into [[Cam Skattebo]] on a goal-line snap. He expects Russ gets another two to three games. He pushes back on the rush to play [[Jackson Dart]] purely to give [[Malik Nabers]] someone to grow with — you do not want Dart getting his ass kicked without a left tackle — but grants that if Dart plays well all bets are off. Note: the Giants had a Dart package they did not use and barred him from speaking to media, and Brian Daboll would not commit to Wilson for Week 2.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Harris says he was done with the Russell Wilson experience 17 minutes into the game, and by the nine-minute mark of the third quarter the broadcast was already showing [[Jackson Dart]] on the sideline. He calls it horrendous, infinite two-yard passes to [[Wan'Dale Robinson]], and says they cannot use him much longer unless it improves. Guest **Sam Gold** (not tracked) adds that at this stage Wilson would rather hold it forever and throw a backhanded two-yard pass to a tight end than throw into a tight window, and that Washington got a 50 percent pressure rate with a four-man rush, which makes his long-developing style unworkable. Harris grants that Andrew Thomas was out and the line is probably not good, but argues Wilson creates his own pressure the way [[Caleb Williams]] did last year, and that as players age their defaults grow more prominent.
