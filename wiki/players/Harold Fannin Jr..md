# Harold Fannin Jr.

## Expert Takes

- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) says 'f that' to the too-small knock — at a listed 238 pounds Fannin is within four or five pounds of [[Brock Bowers]] (245), [[Trey McBride]] (246), [[Dalton Kincaid]] and [[Sam LaPorta]], though weighing in at 230 would change the calculus. Fannin posted a 100 grade, the best adjusted production score the model has ever produced for a tight end even after being dinged for the MAC, plus a 125 quarterback rating when targeted (79th percentile) and a 6.23 Zierlein grade; McFarland notes athleticism matters more for tight ends than any other position in his model, so combine testing matters here. Waldman loves the footwork, lateral agility, vision on smash screens and ability to block as a detached option or from the wing, but charted six tight-coverage opportunities in which Fannin caught none of them — 'he went Jared Cook' — and attributes drops to clap-catching. He thinks Fannin is quick enough to threaten a seam off play action but sees a James Casey or [[Chigoziem Okonkwo]] hybrid-role risk; McFarland's version of that risk is the Juwan Johnson pattern of disappearing whenever the team leads and runs. McFarland expects Fannin to end up his TE3 after the draft.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): Waldman has moved UP on Fannin post-Combine, reversing his earlier position — he had been lower than Dwain McFarland (not a tracked expert) when McFarland pushed Fannin on the show weeks earlier, and had doubted Fannin was a top-three tight end on his board, but now thinks he might be. The reason is contrarian: the bandwagon jumped off because Fannin ran roughly 4.71 rather than the expected 4.5-4.6, which Waldman calls borderline starter speed, but he weights the 20-yard shuttle and three-cone far more, and those times are nearly identical to [[Sam LaPorta]], whom Fannin also resembles in style — good route runner, catches well, runs well after the catch, creative footwork, good vision. His caveat is fit: as long as the landing spot is not idiotic, and Detroit is his example of a team that used LaPorta correctly, Fannin could be one of the three most productive tight ends from a good class.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman spends the episode's longest segment defending Fannin against the 40-time backlash. Fannin and [[Sam LaPorta]] are both converted wide receivers at essentially the same height and weight, and their agility numbers sit in the same tiers: Fannin's 4.39 20-yard shuttle to LaPorta's 4.25, a 6.97 three-cone to LaPorta's 6.91, and a vertical one inch below LaPorta's 35 inches. The gap is the 40 — LaPorta's 4.59 is a hundredth inside Waldman's elite tier, Fannin's 4.71 a hundredth outside his starter tier, which Waldman says effectively places him in it. On-field GPS backs it: Fannin's career max speed at Bowling Green was 20.3 mph with fastest-moment averages of 19 to 19.5, versus LaPorta's 21.1 max and 19.6 to 20. Waldman's conclusion is that Fannin's measured speed meets the NFL starter standard while his initial acceleration and change-of-direction quickness exceed it, and that comparing the two players' actual route trees shows the 40 barely matters — most of LaPorta's rookie vertical wins came on long-developing play action against zone and broken zone, routes Fannin could have won too. He is one of the four Waldman would want maximized as a receiver with blocking responsibility minimized.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): of the three tight ends he would want in the Jets offense, he personally prefers Fannin — metrically and in height and weight he is the closest of the group to [[Sam LaPorta]], and in Angelo's opinion the best blocker of the three. He frames Fannin as the third- or fourth-round swing New York should take if it does not spend pick seven on [[Tyler Warren]], and notes the fantasy upside is real given Cole Kmet drew 90 targets in a comparable role under this coaching lineage. Angelo says none of these three are the talent Kmet was, meaning they should exceed that usage.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]) [Dynasty]: an underrated asset, explicitly against Alfredo Brown (not tracked), who argued on their draft party that the Cleveland landing spot sinks Fannin's value. Waldman concedes that is right through a redraft lens but says dynasty is the opposite: [[David Njoku]]'s contract is up after this season and Kevin Stefanski is returning to his two-tight-end offense. Fannin is more [[Sam LaPorta]] — he wins routes against defenders of any size, can do it detached as your number three receiver in 11 personnel, has stop-start quickness, excellent hands, wins the ball in the air and is even more dynamic after the catch. Cleveland can then replace Njoku with a blocker (a Brock Wright or Cole Kmet type) and hand Fannin the receiving job. In a 1.5 PPR league his tight end order is [[Colston Loveland]], [[Mason Taylor]], then Fannin — and he says he would consider Fannin over Taylor. [[Tyler Warren]] is his TE4, and he notes friend Chad Ryder likes Warren far more than he does.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]) [Dynasty]: underrated relative to where he is going — TE35 in Best Ball, a free-square flyer, and going around 4.02 in rookie drafts behind [[Mason Taylor]] (3.03) and just ahead of [[Elijah Arroyo]] (4.06), a range Waldman calls a very good spot and hopes he falls that far to him. Waldman has him as easily TE3 in this class. The skill argument contrasts him with [[David Njoku]]: Fannin's receiving profile is more like [[Sam LaPorta]] — timing routes, routes breaking back to the quarterback, breaks inside and outside, able to win against man rather than only feasting on zone — and he can also win the ball in the air and make people miss after the catch rather than just running through contact. Waldman expects him to be the lead receiving option by next year, whether as the detached tight end in Kevin Stefanski's two-tight-end sets or in a one-tight-end look under a new staff, with a Brock Wright-type blocker brought in alongside. He notes Njoku is out of contract after this season.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: aggressively higher than the market — he would take Fannin at the end of round one in a 1.5-PPR rookie draft, has him third on his tight end board ahead of [[Tyler Warren]] and [[Colston Loveland]], and is stunned people draft [[Terrance Ferguson]] over him. The reasoning: Fannin runs the timing routes [[David Njoku]] cannot (seams, zone sits, corner routes), comping his route craft to [[Sam LaPorta]], plus shake after the catch and better blocking than his size suggests. Cleveland's Stefanski/Berry commitment to two-tight-end sets and play-action fits now; and if Njoku leaves in free agency, Waldman expects the Browns to pair Fannin with a cheap blocking tight end and let him run the show. He calls it win-win, since a regime change toward three-receiver spread sets still suits Fannin's dynamism. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees and thinks Fannin's 2026 offensive share could exceed Loveland's.
- 2025-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-20]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman called Fannin an absolute value whose market cratered after the Combine, and said he is a player 'you can't lose with' in dynasty even if he gives you nothing in 2025 redraft. His reasoning is roster math plus skill fit: [[David Njoku]] is likely gone after this year and the offensive line is old, so if Kevin Stefanski survives with [[Joe Flacco]] or [[Kenny Pickett]] and the team leans back into a two-tight-end look, Fannin becomes the receiving tight end with a blocker added around him. On film Waldman sees Njoku as a linear player who runs overs, seams and corners and is a load in a straight line, whereas Fannin wins on harder-angled breaks and timing routes back to the quarterback — 'more [[Sam LaPorta]] in that sense' — and can also run. If Cleveland instead abandons the scheme and goes three wide, Waldman says Fannin fits that even better. Zachariason agreed on personnel grounds: Cleveland has no real slot beyond Jamari Thrash and David Bell, and Stefanski arrived as a 12-personnel coach. Both suggested he is close to free in redraft or a first-tier waiver add — 'the Irv Smith we were waiting to have happen.'
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Dynasty]: he rejects the framing that Fannin is the [[Isaiah Likely]] of Cleveland — a 30-point week then nothing — and instead says Fannin could be a [[Sam LaPorta]]-like option even with [[David Njoku]] there. His reasoning: elite tight ends can run man-to-man and timing routes that break back to the quarterback, requiring the ability to bend and decelerate; Fannin can do that, as can LaPorta, [[Travis Kelce]] and [[Trey McBride]], while Njoku cannot. Cleveland goes to two tight ends often and its receiver room is unproven, so Waldman expects Fannin to be the best slot option when they shift from 12 to 11 personnel, since Njoku stays in line as a blocker. At TE38 (round 24) he calls him a monitor-the-camp bargain and a dynasty prospect he wants rostered.
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman is 'feeling it' on Fannin as a late-round tight end lottery ticket, taking him between rounds 18 and 20 (he took him around the 20th in a Footballguys mock alongside [[Mason Taylor]] and [[Colston Loveland]] after waiting on the position). His case: unlike [[David Njoku]], Fannin can break down at the top of stems and run the timing routes [[Travis Kelce]] ran at his peak and [[George Kittle]] and [[Sam LaPorta]] run now — it may take a year or two to fully develop, but the price is a lottery ticket. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked) flipped from 'f**k it' a week earlier to fully feeling it after preseason usage: six snaps, four different routes, two pass-blocking snaps, lined up in the slot before nearly every snap, first route a 25-yard fade, plus a chip that blew up a defender before he released wide open to the flat. Kluge says Cleveland has no real slot receiver and is treating him as a full receiver, not a tight end. Both frame him as the upside pairing for a boring veteran tight end.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-18]]): Waldman split the question by threshold — as a viable fantasy starter, 'fuck it'; as a viable roster stash or waiver add who can be a bye-week value and eventually a starter through injury or inept play, he is feeling it more and more. Cleveland is using Fannin as a weapon rather than as a tight end, and he is a good wide out, so with [[Diontae Johnson]] on the roster and Jamarr Thrash getting camp love for the third receiver job, Fannin could end up the de facto slot as the third or fourth receiving option. Waldman contrasted him with [[Ben Sinnott]], a player he liked in the RSP who is locked behind [[Zach Ertz]] and blocker John Bates and has no path absent injury or retirement. Bob Harris (not tracked) noted Fannin is essentially undrafted — pick 294, TE35, behind [[Tyler Higbee]] — and while he likes free-square plays, he holds more shares of [[Ja'Tavion Sanders]] (TE30) and [[Theo Johnson]] (TE31), plus rookie [[Mason Taylor]], whose only real target competition is [[Garrett Wilson]].
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): a top-15 fantasy TE season is a live possibility rather than a fantasy. Cleveland is leaning back into two-tight-end sets under Kevin Stefanski, has moved Fannin all over the field all summer, and keeps saying he is 'more than just a tight end' and 'more like a receiver'. Waldman's read is that Fannin may effectively be the third receiver in the offense behind [[Jerry Jeudy]] and [[David Njoku]], splitting that role with [[Cedric Tillman]], regardless of what Diontae Johnson, Jamari Thrash or [[Isaiah Bond]] do. The risk is that this only pays off with injury — if Njoku or a receiver goes down, the top-15 outcome arrives. Practical advice from both: take him only as a very last-pick lottery ticket, but put him near the top of the waiver wire watch list. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) said Cleveland beat writer Fred Greetham is excited about him, and warned people are anchored on Njoku's five-game Flacco stretch (TE5 over that span) without accounting for Fannin's versatility.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): 'fuck yes' he remains a weekly fantasy starter. The open question was whether Cleveland could keep him on the field outside two-tight-end looks, and Week 1 answered it — they used him in the backfield and in the slot. Waldman says he already runs better routes than [[David Njoku]] and will complement rather than cannibalize him. He caught the ball well, blocked well and was glue-handed on receivers and defensive backs, was very good in the run game, took Logan Wilson out of the game at one point with his physicality, and worked open well with [[Joe Flacco]]. Not a fluke: Waldman projects a low-end TE1 in 14- and 16-team leagues and possibly a top-12 tight end, and calls him a [[Sam LaPorta]] clone, maybe better in some areas.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): Fannin finished 7-63 to [[David Njoku]]'s 3-37, and the tape backed the box score — when [[Joe Flacco]] was late in the down, moving and avoiding pressure, it was usually Fannin who moved with him and bailed him out. He was one of Cleveland's two leading touch-getters alongside [[Dylan Sampson]] in a 71-play game. Harris: if you are unhappy with your tight end, he has no problem with adding Fannin as a possible tight end one. Guest Jake Ciely of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) considered him but picked [[Juwan Johnson]] instead, conceding it could be wrong since both are available; Ciely also argued Fannin threatens [[Jerry Jeudy]] and Njoku but not [[Cedric Tillman]], whose role is different.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the rookie out-produced [[David Njoku]] on the box score, 5 for 48 to Njoku's 4 for 40, and Harris credits a wonderful high-leaping grab in the third quarter. The deflating caveat: Fannin's biggest play came with Dillon Gabriel in the game in garbage time, and while the game was still competitive he was only 3 for 36. Harris's broader point is that [[Joe Flacco]]'s play caps the whole Cleveland pass-catching group.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-22]]): 'f**k it' on Fannin fading into the fantasy background — the toothpaste is out of the tube. Snap counts may not satisfy people wanting a top-10 tight end, but Waldman said every time Fannin touches the ball the play is impactful: multiple broken tackles, first downs, and routes [[David Njoku]] simply cannot run. Cleveland tried [[Isaiah Bond]] and Jamari Thrash more against Green Bay, but when it was time to score late in the fourth quarter Fannin was on the field making timely plays. Waldman argued that with [[Quinshon Judkins]] running through people and a defense that just crushed [[Jordan Love]], Cleveland can keep playing two-tight-end alignments and stay close in games without needing an added layer of explosion. He expects steady work — maybe a low-end TE1. Bob Harris (not tracked) noted Fannin's target share was well ahead of Njoku entering the game.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-29]]): amid general pessimism about Cleveland's receiving assets, Waldman singles out Fannin as the exception — he 'continues to prove to be a consistent dominant force' in the Browns offense and is probably the most consistent player on it other than [[Quinshon Judkins]]. Waldman credits Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) as the one who has been on Fannin.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-02]]): from what he has watched of Cleveland, Fannin is the Browns' best offensive playmaker right now and has been their best player, with [[Quinshon Judkins]] next. That matters to the quarterback conversation because Angelo's knock on [[Dillon Gabriel]] is that he needs playmakers who bail him out when the operation gets muddy, and Fannin and Judkins are the two he trusts to do work independent of clean structure.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): 4-44 against the Jets, and 'you do keep getting glimpses of a real player' - the highlight a great over-the-shoulder grab on a deep out for 24 yards with a minute left in the first half.
