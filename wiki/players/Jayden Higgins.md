- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) names Higgins as his underrated player — projected a third-rounder, so not a true sleeper, but at 61 in the pre-draft model he clears the 60 threshold, meaning the model likes him more than current NFL draft capital does. His career production is 62nd percentile, he did well with the ball in his hands, and Lance Zierlein's film grade puts him at the 56th percentile: a player who meets all the thresholds without blowing past any of them. McFarland flags that Higgins is an Eastern Kentucky transfer and a four-year player rather than an early declare, which sometimes pushes prospects down boards.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): the Iowa State receiver would easily be the best pass catcher in this class if his hand strength were a little better when defenders swat at his hands or the ball — and he is still an asset as-is. Waldman also praises him with the ball in his hands: he can make multiple unblocked defenders miss in tight space for meaningful yardage, and knows when a straight line and splitting defenders is the better option. At his size he finishes well, keeps his feet moving through contact when wrapped high, and has good timing and range with a stiff arm to ward off reaches.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: Higgins has the movement tools to play inside and out, but the open question is whether a surface-level good athlete can match the athleticism, technique and cognitive level of the best outside corners in the world. Angelo would start him in the slot — the [[Cedric Tillman]] template, who looked awesome in the slot mid-season before his concussion — where he can win earlier and more easily, then bump him outside as confidence grows. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed: some plays Higgins looked quick-footed, technically sound and got open outside, others he looked stuck in mud, most likely processing how he was being played. He offered [[Drake London]] as a higher-level version of the inside-out receiver who had a monster year working inside, and said Higgins is good enough to be a genuine No. 1 from the slot even if the outside never comes.
- 2025-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-04]]): a full Reception Perception breakdown of the Iowa State prospect lands him as a tier-five, 'good day two option' — the same tier Harmon gave [[Keon Coleman]]. Measurables are prototypical X: 6-4, 215, 4.43 with a 39-inch vertical and a 6-7 wingspan (91st percentile per Mockdraftable), and Harmon says he felt even bigger than that on film. The charting is the problem: 15th percentile success rate vs man, 16th vs press, 46th vs zone (just shy of 79%), with every route red except the curl and screen. Slant success 71% on ~25% route participation ('you want that around 80%'), nine route 57% on 19% participation, dig 68.2%, post 69%. He ran 75.7% of snaps on the line and 68.8% outside. Harmon pushes back on Twitter dunking — one or two routes the other way and the chart looks yellow or green — and stresses Higgins is a below-average separator specifically in the outside-X role he was asked to play. Zero drop rate across eight games charted; Koh loves the hands and contested catch, Harmon grades contested ability nearer 'okay than great' because of alligator arms at 90 degrees rather than full extension, which also draws the constant pass interference flags. Both flag wasted movement at the line — 'four or five moves' before the break — as the fixable flaw killing the short-area routes. Production: 87/1183/9 as an Iowa State senior after an Eastern Kentucky transfer, 25 touchdowns over three seasons, yardage improving every year (394/757/983/1183).
- 2025-04-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-08]]) [Dynasty]: Higgins got most of the pre-draft press among Iowa State receivers, but Harmon says plainly that [[Jaylin Noel]] is the better prospect and the one he would rather have — no disrespect intended, and he still thinks Higgins can be a really good pro if he moves into the slot and gets off the line of scrimmage. His preference is archetypal: he likes Noel's scheme-versatile, quarterback-friendly profile more than the big receiver who cannot separate or the project power slot player. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) agrees, saying that when he watched Iowa State to study Higgins his eyes kept getting pulled to Noel, who was also the team's leading receiver.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): Higgins (6-4, 214, 4.47) is only in the top-40-pick conversation because he is one of the few true X receivers in this class — Waldman 'feels it,' saying the NFL likes the prototype and that his size, adequate speed for that size and contested-catch ability make teams consider him earlier than pure grade warrants. Waldman has him as his sixth-ranked wide receiver but would prefer him as a late-second or third-round pick: he lacks a plan with releases and certain footwork is unrefined, so he can look like he is 'playing a little bit in wet concrete.' When his game is on, Waldman sees a starter and a possible good fantasy option, with a talent range around the [[Mike Williams]] / [[Tee Higgins]] sphere at worst. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) jokes the 4.47 takes him off his list.
- 2025-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-16]]): a receiver Harmon has expressed concerns about — he is not sure Higgins can separate to the level a perimeter receiver needs — but one he can still picture a role for, because Higgins catches absolutely everything and is decent against zone coverage, which projects to a power slot job. Cited as an example of a flawed prospect Harmon still ranks above [[Jalen Royals]], because with Royals he cannot envision the role at all.
- 2025-04-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-22]]): Higgins is his riskiest receiver in the 2025 class, and explicitly 'lower than consensus' on him. The risk is not mainly his own game but how the league will read it — Harmon fears a team sees 6-4 and a 4.4 forty and installs him as its X immediately, when Harmon's path for him is a move-around big slot who can play off the line of scrimmage and needs seasoning. His stated evaluation gap: Higgins does not attack and extend at the catch point enough to be the [[Mike Williams]]-style contested-catch X, and Harmon calls even [[Keon Coleman]] 'the poor man's Mike Williams' by comparison; Higgins catches cleanly when uncontested but contested work remains a development area. He drew the direct parallel to Coleman landing in Buffalo behind an established productive slot in [[Khalil Shakir]], which locked Coleman into an outside role. Harmon also noted Higgins is a former basketball player and plays receiver like one — more rebounder than separator.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-04-28]]) [Dynasty]: Higgins at 34 to Houston moves him up meaningfully — Harmon says his post-draft rank will be a lot higher than his pre-draft rank, where he was lower than consensus and did not have him as the class WR5. The reason is purely landing spot: his risk was a team seeing a big 4.4 guy and making him an X. At Iowa State he took 75.7% of his sample snaps on the line of scrimmage and 68.8% outside, and the results were 15th percentile success rate versus man and 16th percentile versus press. Houston already employs [[Nico Collins]] at X, so Higgins takes some X snaps but projects as a power slot — Harmon notes new OC Nick Caley comes from the Rams as tight ends coach and passing game coordinator with a New England background, offenses with a history of using a power slot. Against zone Higgins graded 78.6%, which is the coverage he will now see. Harmon also likes his blocking near the line for the run game and would 'bet a million dollars' on play-action shots to Higgins from the slot.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: a value at his ADP of 203 — Angelo would take him around there and expects him to return value because Higgins could see 100-plus targets as a rookie in a Houston offense that will throw heavily with [[Tank Dell]] out. [[Matt Waldman]] drafted him higher, at the equivalent of pick 16 in a 32-team rookie draft, and paired him with [[Jaylin Noel]] a round later. Neither host treats the crowded room behind [[Nico Collins]] as disqualifying, arguing the vacated target volume (~145) supports two rookie receivers.
- 2025-06-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-02]]): it is 'clearly Higgins over Noel' in both dynasty and redraft, and Waldman confirms he is comfortable calling that safe. The reason is role, not just talent — Higgins projects as the player lining up opposite [[Nico Collins]] on the outside, and his size and speed combination plus an ability to work inside when needed make him more versatile for what the Texans currently need. Waldman expects Higgins' ADP to be the higher of the two rookies and to stay that way through the summer barring injury. The caveat he attaches: if [[Christian Kirk]] were to go down, [[Jaylin Noel]] could wind up being just as big a producer, so the gap is situational rather than absolute. [Dynasty]
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]) [Dynasty]: on DeMeco Ryans' praise of Higgins' offseason and the idea he can be an impact player, Waldman details the alignment versatility that makes it plausible — Higgins can play flanker, or take the split end role and push [[Nico Collins]] to flanker, or work the slot as a [[Drake London]]-style inside-out player, and Waldman expects to see all three. The traits: quick feet, good size, wins the ball well, breaks tackles, and when he has a good feel for what to do at the line of scrimmage he can separate against press. The caveat is that it may take him more time to get there in the NFL. Waldman's comps for the role are [[Tee Higgins]] or [[Mike Williams]] opposite a true number one like Collins, and he calls Higgins a nice dynasty acquisition with some redraft upside this season.
- 2025-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-01]]): Harmon was lower than consensus on Higgins as a prospect — he played outside on 68.8% of sampled snaps and on the line for 75.7%, mostly X, and Harmon saw him get 'stuck at the break points' of routes, especially in-breakers, comparing the concern to [[Keon Coleman]] the year prior. The bull case is that Houston will not ask him to be a pure X because [[Nico Collins]] occupies that role, so Harmon expects Higgins to take a lot of snaps off the line of scrimmage and a decent amount of slot work, and says he would be stunned if Higgins does not get reps as a power slot. Harmon frames Higgins taking even 30% of X snaps as the lever that unlocks moving Collins around the formation. James Koh notes Houston paired Higgins with Iowa State teammate [[Jaylin Noel]] to build a big-slot plus vertical-slot combination.

Harmon and James Koh (not tracked) agreed Houston looks to be running a Kansas City-style rotation in which nobody among Higgins, [[Jaylin Noel]], [[Xavier Hutchinson]] and [[Christian Kirk]] clears a 70% snap share this year, though both flagged the bye week as a possible inflection point where the rookies get more.
- 2025-10-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-23]]): Harmon doesn't think Higgins can be an outside X receiver as currently deployed -- he described a play where Higgins (outside) and [[Jaylin Noel]] (inside) ran a mirrored slant concept and Higgins 'got obliterated' while Noel was wide open. Harmon believes Higgins is being miscast as a boundary X running deep routes when he should be the interior power-slot/movement piece, a role currently occupied by Xavier Hutchinson -- functionally making Higgins [[Nico Collins]]'s backup rather than a real starting complement. He compared the situation to the [[Keon Coleman]] trap: teams see a big-bodied outside receiver and assume he's a deep-X, but Higgins hasn't shown the separation ability to justify that usage, and with a battered offensive line and CJ Stroud under constant pressure, those deep shots rarely connect. Harmon caveats it's only Week 8 of his rookie year with plenty of runway left, and plans to dig deeper for his in-season rookie report in November.
