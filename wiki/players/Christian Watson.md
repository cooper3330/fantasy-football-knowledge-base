# Christian Watson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Dynasty]: pairs Watson's speed with [[Jayden Reed]]'s as the two dimensions [[Dontayvion Wicks]] can't match, but flags durability as the entire question mark on his 2024 outlook — "if he cannot figure out the availability side of the equation," Wicks (or [[Romeo Doubs]]) is positioned to take the fantasy-relevant role share instead. Notes the Packers front office has discussed the availability issue openly rather than treating it as settled.
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]): "super physically talented," but had no impact in the Wild Card win over Dallas — consistent with, not a new addition to, the availability/consistency concern already tracked on his page.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Best Ball]: unchanged read heading into best-ball draft season — "still think he's that guy," a unique speed/athleticism difference-maker, but availability remains the entire question mark. With Green Bay's receiver room this crowded, Waldman's stated draft strategy is to take "the cheapest piece on a regular basis" among Reed/Wicks/Doubs/Watson rather than pay a premium for any one name, expecting the group to cannibalize each other's value in redraft more than in best ball.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): a real, specific knock on the true-WR1 case for the first time in this wiki — asked to make the argument for Watson as a number-one receiver "without mentioning height" or speed, Harmon says he can't: "I've got nothing here." Reads as a real downgrade relative to [[Jayden Reed]] and [[Dontayvion Wicks]], both of whom get a route-running-based case for the role that Watson doesn't. Notes he "needs to develop his route tree" and had no impact in the Divisional Round loss to San Francisco.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: going as only a WR4 currently, a real value; Waldman ties [[Jordan Love]]'s upside to Watson staying healthy and playing like he did late in 2023.
- 2024-05-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-21]]): Harmon hasn't yet charted Watson's year-two data but doubts it will change his read -- he sees Watson as a 'big play merchant' in the Martavis Bryant mold, a downfield winner who lacks [[Dontayvion Wicks]]'s multiple areas of route-running strength and isn't 'technically sound.' He also flags recurring hamstring issues (Watson reportedly seeing specialists) as a real risk to his outside-X role, floating that a 'less is more' usage approach could make sense for Green Bay even though the team is unlikely to bench a former second-round pick.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Best Ball]: Watson's camp reports attribute his repeated hamstring injuries to a muscle-mass imbalance between his left and right quads, which he's reportedly addressed. Waldman called a fully healthy Watson a legitimate fantasy WR4, but ranked him the lowest of Green Bay's three ascending receivers ([[Jayden Reed]], [[Dontayvion Wicks]], Watson) precisely because of that injury history, drawing a skeptical parallel to Alshon Jeffery once claiming he'd 'figured out' his own hamstring issues. Working from a Jordan Love passing projection of roughly 4,200-4,500 yards split among four receivers and two tight ends, Waldman modeled two scenarios: either Watson breaks out as a 1,100-1,200-yard WR1 while Reed/Wicks fall under 400 yards, or all three land in the 600-800 yard range with 5-9 total touchdowns split between them. He tagged Watson as his personal 'lottery ticket' — a [[Best Ball]] play specifically, going around WR47, one spot ahead of [[Jameson Williams]] — rather than a redraft target.
- 2024-06-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-11]]): despite the touchdown-heavy reputation (4 TDs on just 49 targets, an 8% TD rate, from weeks 5-13 of 2023), Watson graded sub-30th percentile against man, zone and press coverage and has never been 'a full-field separator' at any level, college included -- Harmon explicitly rejects the fan theory that a hamstring/leg-imbalance injury alone explains the numbers, though he won't rule it out. Contested-catch rate was a poor 47.1% (worse mid-season, recovering late on highlight grabs vs Detroit/Kansas City). Route splits were volatile: dig success rate rose from 67% (2022) to 74% (2023) on a small sample, but out-route success cratered from 85% to 56%; curl routes stayed a mediocre ~75% both years despite Harmon wanting the Packers to stop calling them entirely, calling curls 'one of the more dangerous routes' when timing is off. Harmon's overall framing: Watson is 'an elevated Marquez Valdes-Scantling' -- a volatile deep-threat who needs role catering (pre-snap motion, downfield/nine routes, limited targets) rather than a full route tree, and is not currently a true No. 1 or consistent outside starter; he argued [[George Pickens]] is a clearly better player forced into a similar 'do less' framework.
- 2024-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-30]]): Watson is a 'less is more' shot player who should not be asked to be Green Bay's boundary X. His charting shows he is genuinely good on the routes that suit him — 74.1% success rate on digs, an improvement while running more of them, and 80.8% on posts on 11.3% of his routes — but he is not good on slants, average on curls and 'pretty disastrous' on comebacks, i.e. anything requiring him to stop and work back to the ball. Harmon pushes back hard on the consensus that all Watson needs is health: his 26th percentile success rate vs man and 8th percentile vs press need real development or he stays in 'the [[Gabe Davis]] category of boom-bust WR3'. He also notes Watson's contested catch rate 'fell off a cliff' and there were moments Jordan Love could not trust him in 50-50 situations, which Koh ties back to shaky hands in college. Harmon thinks Watson was freakier in the open field and after the catch than downfield as a contested winner, and criticizes a 0.4% screen route rate in the games charted as far too low — get him the ball in space. He argues the Packers no longer have to force his outside development now that Doubs, Wicks and Reed can cover those roles. Both hosts note the recurring injuries. Koh projects him fourth in the room in yardage: ~40 receptions, 613 yards, five touchdowns.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Harmon calls Watson a less-is-more player and not a complete one — his efficiency metrics are boosted by big plays, big moments and small samples because he is constantly on and off the field, producing very good games and very bad ones. He is not arguing Watson should be benched, but he does not want him on every snap, and thinks a reduced role helps both the offense and Watson's body over a season in which Green Bay could win a Super Bowl. Harmon adds there is a real chance three different Packers lead the team in catches, yards and touchdowns respectively. Koh (not tracked) wonders whether health and inconsistency eventually force Watson to the bench, while noting his draft capital is a counterweight, and identifies Watson as the room's clear deep-splash specialist.
- 2024-09-09 — According to Bob Harris (co-host of [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]], not a tracked expert) [Redraft (Standard)]: relaying ESPN's Rob Domoski, the Packers pecking order is [[Romeo Doubs]] as the de facto go-to target, [[Jayden Reed]] as the athletic jack-of-all-trades, and Watson as the designated big-play threat — Bob Harris saw three passes go to the end zone to Watson in week one and thought Domoski nailed the roles. He flagged Watson as the obvious injury risk in the group despite holding shares, and called [[Jayden Reed]] the safest of the three on his combination of skills including jet-sweep usage. [[Matt Waldman]] cautioned it will be a hot-hand situation and that everyone should dial back expectations on Packers receivers for three or four weeks.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): reacting to reporting that Watson could go on IR with a high ankle sprain, Waldman made the counterintuitive argument that the injury 'may actually wind up being a better thing for the Packers.' As good as Watson can be, his skill set overlaps awkwardly with the rest of the group; without him Green Bay gets clearly defined roles — downfield work for Jayden Reed, timing routes and tough work over the middle for Romeo Doubs, and [[Dontayvion Wicks]] both overlapping and differentiating. Waldman said the remaining trio is more complementary than the Watson version. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) countered that the roles were already well defined, with Watson as the designated deep threat.
- 2024-10-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-01]]): Watson suffered what sounds like a high ankle sprain against Minnesota after being rolled up on, and will miss time — a bummer for a player who had reportedly solved his hamstring problems in the offseason by correcting the muscle-mass imbalance in his legs. Harmon has been 'a little bit mixed' on Watson for the length of his career, and notes the injury actually clarifies the Green Bay receiver room: [[Dontayvion Wicks]] gets a fuller outside opportunity and [[Jayden Reed]] is unambiguously the number one. Harmon defended [[Jordan Love]] on the interception thrown on the play, saying it was a good read by the Vikings linebacker rather than a bad throw.
- 2024-11-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-26]]): Watson is 'tricky' because he is so volatile — Harmon points out that right after the yards-per-route-run crowd resurfaced to argue Watson is actually great, he had one of the most heartbreaking drops you will ever see. Harmon still credits him as one of the big reasons Green Bay beat Chicago the prior week with several critical late plays, and notes he blocks well and is bought into the room's culture. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) disagrees on deployment, saying it is fine to take Watson off the field to guarantee [[Jayden Reed]] snaps in two-receiver sets. Harmon's counter is that Reed may not always give them something and gets used as run-game eye candy.
- 2024-12-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-10]]): Harmon says Green Bay is higher on Watson than he is. He grants Watson has value and is good enough to hold a role on any NFL offense, but calls him too volatile to be counted on as an every-down player, and is befuddled that the Packers lean on him and [[Romeo Doubs]] ahead of [[Jayden Reed]], who he considers the team's best separator by RP. His distinction is between having a role — which Watson deserves — and having a prominent role as a top-one-or-two target getter, which Harmon and Koh both think is a misallocation. Harmon floats one possible explanation for the snap split in two-receiver sets: the Packers may prefer Watson's blocking to Reed's.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]) — 'feel it' on Watson having the highest ceiling of Green Bay's receivers: at his best he is [[Romeo Doubs]], [[Jayden Reed]] and [[Dontayvion Wicks]] combined, and if he stays healthy he is a top-12 receiver. The two things standing in the way are drops, which Waldman expects to curtail, and the injuries, which he calls by far the bigger issue. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) supplied the workload evidence: a 22% target share over the last seven games versus a 15% high across the first six, and joked that 'the science won out' now that the hamstrings have held up after Watson's offseason in the laboratory.
- 2024-12-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-31]]): Harmon rolls his eyes at the post-game narrative that Green Bay could not beat Minnesota's man coverage because Watson was out. He says he does not care what Watson's yards per route run against man says — 'we know that guy's not a consistent route in, route out, man coverage beating receiver.' Watson is a big-play guy who can take the top off, but not someone a defense dedicates extra resources to. Harmon also cites his continued trouble both staying healthy and being consistent. He stops short of dismissing him entirely: Watson is not a useless player you can simply pull out of the rotation, which is part of Green Bay's problem — too many developing options and only one football.
- 2025-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-19]]): Harmon expects Watson to miss much of 2025 after a multi-ligament knee injury — he tore his ACL in the final weeks of the season — and says he is not in Green Bay's plans for the year, comparing the situation to [[Tank Dell]] with Houston where anything you get should be treated as gravy. He is entering the last year of his rookie deal alongside [[Romeo Doubs]], and James Koh (not tracked) frames his repeated inability to stay healthy as the reason the Packers' four-number-ones narrative collapsed.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-04-28]]): Harmon says [[Matthew Golden]] is basically replacing Watson in Green Bay's offense, which sounds strange because the two look nothing alike standing side by side. His point is role, not build — Watson has never been the X for this team ([[Romeo Doubs]] has played that somewhat sacrificial role, while taking a step forward as a target earner last year); Watson was the vertical guy and the player they got on in-breakers on the move. Harmon also grants that whatever you thought of Watson, he could play the go-up-and-get-it role to a degree, which the smaller Golden cannot, so Green Bay will eventually need size near the goal line.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): Waldman said Green Bay is ready to move on from the Watson era, and that the [[Matthew Golden]] pick is the signal. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) noted the optimistic talk on Watson's rehab track; Waldman read 'optimistic' as meaning he starts working in November and might be seen late down the stretch at best.
- 2025-06-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-04]]): co-host James Koh (not tracked) raises Watson being ahead of schedule on his ACL rehab specifically to challenge the consensus that he is a non-factor in Green Bay for 2025 and beyond. Harmon notes Matt LaFleur reported the ahead-of-schedule status while disclaiming any medical authority, so week one is still unlikely, and that [[Matthew Golden]] will likely absorb much of Watson's role despite the size difference. But Harmon insists LaFleur genuinely likes Watson and that when he is available he will play, because he provides real tactical value and big-play juice down the field and with the ball in his hands — while being too inconsistent to be a number one or even a number two receiver, and better when schemed open than in tight coverage. Harmon's concrete conclusion: a healthy Watson makes someone else in that room expendable.
- 2025-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-13]]): Watson sits right next to Martavis Bryant in the Reception Perception historical post-route database, which Harmon says fits the comp he has long made between the two. Watson wins downfield when healthy but is inconsistent, and Harmon explicitly lists him alongside [[Romeo Doubs]] as a receiver who is not a good man beater — which is precisely why he thinks Green Bay should be playing [[Jayden Reed]] more in condensed personnel groupings, since Reed is the separator that room lacks.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Watson is likely to start the season on PUP, part of a wave of Green Bay injuries alongside [[Jayden Reed]]'s boot, [[Dontayvion Wicks]], Savion Williams and [[Jordan Love]]'s non-throwing thumb. Harmon's framing is that Watson is one of several talents the Packers have thrown at the number one job without solving it, and that his absence mainly benefits [[Romeo Doubs]] and rookie [[Matthew Golden]] in early-season snap share. He calls the room deep and good as a collection of talent but without any frontline guys, and says a group already known for rotating players on and off the field looks scary heading into the opener.
