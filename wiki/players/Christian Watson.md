# Christian Watson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Dynasty]: pairs Watson's speed with [[Jayden Reed]]'s as the two dimensions [[Dontayvion Wicks]] can't match, but flags durability as the entire question mark on his 2024 outlook — "if he cannot figure out the availability side of the equation," Wicks (or [[Romeo Doubs]]) is positioned to take the fantasy-relevant role share instead. Notes the Packers front office has discussed the availability issue openly rather than treating it as settled.
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]): "super physically talented," but had no impact in the Wild Card win over Dallas — consistent with, not a new addition to, the availability/consistency concern already tracked on his page.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Best Ball]: unchanged read heading into best-ball draft season — "still think he's that guy," a unique speed/athleticism difference-maker, but availability remains the entire question mark. With Green Bay's receiver room this crowded, Waldman's stated draft strategy is to take "the cheapest piece on a regular basis" among Reed/Wicks/Doubs/Watson rather than pay a premium for any one name, expecting the group to cannibalize each other's value in redraft more than in best ball.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): a real, specific knock on the true-WR1 case for the first time in this wiki — asked to make the argument for Watson as a number-one receiver "without mentioning height" or speed, Harmon says he can't: "I've got nothing here." Reads as a real downgrade relative to [[Jayden Reed]] and [[Dontayvion Wicks]], both of whom get a route-running-based case for the role that Watson doesn't. Notes he "needs to develop his route tree" and had no impact in the Divisional Round loss to San Francisco.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: going as only a WR4 currently, a real value; Waldman ties [[Jordan Love]]'s upside to Watson staying healthy and playing like he did late in 2023.
- 2024-05-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-21]]): Harmon hasn't yet charted Watson's year-two data but doubts it will change his read -- he sees Watson as a 'big play merchant' in the Martavis Bryant mold, a downfield winner who lacks [[Dontayvion Wicks]]'s multiple areas of route-running strength and isn't 'technically sound.' He also flags recurring hamstring issues (Watson reportedly seeing specialists) as a real risk to his outside-X role, floating that a 'less is more' usage approach could make sense for Green Bay even though the team is unlikely to bench a former second-round pick.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Best Ball]: Watson's camp reports attribute his repeated hamstring injuries to a muscle-mass imbalance between his left and right quads, which he's reportedly addressed. Waldman called a fully healthy Watson a legitimate fantasy WR4, but ranked him the lowest of Green Bay's three ascending receivers ([[Jayden Reed]], [[Dontayvion Wicks]], Watson) precisely because of that injury history, drawing a skeptical parallel to Alshon Jeffery once claiming he'd 'figured out' his own hamstring issues. Working from a Jordan Love passing projection of roughly 4,200-4,500 yards split among four receivers and two tight ends, Waldman modeled two scenarios: either Watson breaks out as a 1,100-1,200-yard WR1 while Reed/Wicks fall under 400 yards, or all three land in the 600-800 yard range with 5-9 total touchdowns split between them. He tagged Watson as his personal 'lottery ticket' — a [[Best Ball]] play specifically, going around WR47, one spot ahead of [[Jameson Williams]] — rather than a redraft target.
- 2024-06-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-11]]): despite the touchdown-heavy reputation (4 TDs on just 49 targets, an 8% TD rate, from weeks 5-13 of 2023), Watson graded sub-30th percentile against man, zone and press coverage and has never been 'a full-field separator' at any level, college included -- Harmon explicitly rejects the fan theory that a hamstring/leg-imbalance injury alone explains the numbers, though he won't rule it out. Contested-catch rate was a poor 47.1% (worse mid-season, recovering late on highlight grabs vs Detroit/Kansas City). Route splits were volatile: dig success rate rose from 67% (2022) to 74% (2023) on a small sample, but out-route success cratered from 85% to 56%; curl routes stayed a mediocre ~75% both years despite Harmon wanting the Packers to stop calling them entirely, calling curls 'one of the more dangerous routes' when timing is off. Harmon's overall framing: Watson is 'an elevated Marquez Valdes-Scantling' -- a volatile deep-threat who needs role catering (pre-snap motion, downfield/nine routes, limited targets) rather than a full route tree, and is not currently a true No. 1 or consistent outside starter; he argued [[George Pickens]] is a clearly better player forced into a similar 'do less' framework.
- 2024-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-30]]): Watson is a 'less is more' shot player who should not be asked to be Green Bay's boundary X. His charting shows he is genuinely good on the routes that suit him — 74.1% success rate on digs, an improvement while running more of them, and 80.8% on posts on 11.3% of his routes — but he is not good on slants, average on curls and 'pretty disastrous' on comebacks, i.e. anything requiring him to stop and work back to the ball. Harmon pushes back hard on the consensus that all Watson needs is health: his 26th percentile success rate vs man and 8th percentile vs press need real development or he stays in 'the [[Gabe Davis]] category of boom-bust WR3'. He also notes Watson's contested catch rate 'fell off a cliff' and there were moments Jordan Love could not trust him in 50-50 situations, which Koh ties back to shaky hands in college. Harmon thinks Watson was freakier in the open field and after the catch than downfield as a contested winner, and criticizes a 0.4% screen route rate in the games charted as far too low — get him the ball in space. He argues the Packers no longer have to force his outside development now that Doubs, Wicks and Reed can cover those roles. Both hosts note the recurring injuries. Koh projects him fourth in the room in yardage: ~40 receptions, 613 yards, five touchdowns.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Harmon calls Watson a less-is-more player and not a complete one — his efficiency metrics are boosted by big plays, big moments and small samples because he is constantly on and off the field, producing very good games and very bad ones. He is not arguing Watson should be benched, but he does not want him on every snap, and thinks a reduced role helps both the offense and Watson's body over a season in which Green Bay could win a Super Bowl. Harmon adds there is a real chance three different Packers lead the team in catches, yards and touchdowns respectively. Koh (not tracked) wonders whether health and inconsistency eventually force Watson to the bench, while noting his draft capital is a counterweight, and identifies Watson as the room's clear deep-splash specialist.
- 2024-09-09 — According to Bob Harris (co-host of [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]], not a tracked expert) [Redraft (Standard)]: relaying ESPN's Rob Domoski, the Packers pecking order is [[Romeo Doubs]] as the de facto go-to target, [[Jayden Reed]] as the athletic jack-of-all-trades, and Watson as the designated big-play threat — Bob Harris saw three passes go to the end zone to Watson in week one and thought Domoski nailed the roles. He flagged Watson as the obvious injury risk in the group despite holding shares, and called [[Jayden Reed]] the safest of the three on his combination of skills including jet-sweep usage. [[Matt Waldman]] cautioned it will be a hot-hand situation and that everyone should dial back expectations on Packers receivers for three or four weeks.
