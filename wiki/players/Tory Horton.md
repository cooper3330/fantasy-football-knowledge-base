# Tory Horton

- 2025-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-16]]): the Colorado State receiver is one of the late-prep charts that 'lit my fire' — Harmon says he really, really likes him, thinks he could go day two, likes him more than some players already published on the site, and will probably rank him ahead of [[Jalen Royals]]. No full charting numbers were given on air; the profile was still in progress at the time of recording.
- 2025-04-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-22]]): Horton is his best sleeper receiver of the 2025 class (defined as round three or later) and a player he is 'willing to be ahead of consensus on.' At 6-3, 196 he ran 4.41 with a 37.5-inch vertical coming off a season-ending 2024 knee injury Colorado State never specified. Reception Perception: 72% success vs man, 81% vs zone, 82% vs press (94th percentile), 38.5% target-per-route-run — the highest in the class. He mastered in-breakers: 25.7% slant rate at 87.9% success, 83% on digs, 73% on posts, but a weak curl score and 56% on nines. Harmon projects a Z/flanker, not an X, and flags one-side-of-the-field deployment (77.4% right WR, four of 443 snaps at left outside) plus a slant/nine-heavy air-raid route tree as reasons he is a round two-three grade rather than a first-rounder, comping the development curve to [[Michael Gallup]], who did little as a rookie then went for 1,100 yards. James Koh (not tracked) praised his start-stop ability, contested-catch and boundary work; both noted a 5.7% drop rate skewed by one three-drop game vs Boise State.
- 2025-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: film-and-Reception-Perception-only, Harmon gave Horton a priority second-round grade, so Seattle getting him in round five is big-time value; he says he has already taken Horton in the fourth round of multiple dynasty rookie drafts and would just stick him on the end of a roster. The slide he attributes to 2024 season-ending knee surgery plus an out-of-sight, out-of-mind effect and a lanky sub-200-pound frame at 6-2 that may worry teams about future injuries. Positives: 94th-percentile success rate versus press, strong dig and out route success rates, good on both in-breakers and out-breakers, high-school outfield background that shows in his ball tracking, and reliable plucking hands outside one bad focus-drop game that pushed his drop rate just over 5%. The red flag Harmon names explicitly is alignment concentration — 77.4% of snaps at right outside. He thinks Horton's best home is as a movement flanker and a possible three-position player, which Seattle needs since [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] has been a slot player, [[Cooper Kupp]] is slot-only now and [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]] is a sacrificial X only. James Koh (not tracked) goes further — if healthy, he would not be surprised if Horton finishes as Seattle's second-most important receiver — while acknowledging Kupp may hold two-receiver snaps for run blocking. Harmon comps his long-striding run-after-catch to Tyrell Williams.
- 2025-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]): Horton is a very talented fifth-round pick who fell mostly because of injuries, and Harmon's prospect charting is strong — a 72.2% success rate versus man and 82% versus press, a 94th percentile press mark, plus a 73% contested catch rate. The red flag is alignment: he took 77% of his snaps at right outside receiver at Colorado State, and one-side-of-the-field players generally need development, with Harmon citing [[Cedric Tillman]], who did nothing as a rookie before playing well in year two, as the template. He may not be ready as a rookie. James Koh (not a tracked expert) is more enthusiastic still — beautiful start-stop ability, crazy slippery after the catch, great along the boundaries — and argues that Sam Darnold's scattershot arm relative to [[Geno Smith]] makes a contested-catch winner necessary. Both see him as a legitimate starter for Seattle, if not in 2025 then in 2026.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-18]]) [Best Ball]: heavy first-team reps do not make Horton the Seattle starter — 'fuck no.' Waldman's rule is that consistent first-team reps mean the team is preparing a player to contribute off the bench with meaningful snaps, and only a steady diet of them suggests an actual replacement; on that basis he does concede Horton could pass [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]] this year, in part because Horton catches the ball better than MVS, though he would not commit to whether that gap is a little or a lot. Bob Harris (not tracked) said his own list already has Horton starting ahead of Valdes-Scantling and that local media are talking up the first-team work, but conceded Waldman is probably closer to the truth; he also noted Horton injured his ankle. Waldman likes grabbing Horton and [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]] as deep-threat, big-play best ball pieces, but says they are much harder to get excited about in redraft, where he wants consistent roles.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-27]]): the [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]] cut is 'the real cut among this that really matters' because it opens Torry Horton season — Harmon says Horton 'certainly should move up your fantasy draft boards' if you are drafting soon. He had Horton graded as a priority round-two type who fell to the fifth round on injury history. The profile: 78.3% of sampled snaps outside, 55.5% on the line, 77.4% from the right outside position — very much a one-side-of-the-field player, the archetype that usually needs seasoning, which is Harmon's stated caveat. But he posted an 82.1% success rate vs press (94th percentile) against lower-level competition, and dominated on slants (87.9%), digs (83.3%) and outs (85.7%). Harmon's role argument: Seattle will blur slot and outside lines with condensed sets, bunches and stacks for [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] and [[Cooper Kupp]], but still needs a perimeter on-ball receiver, and Clint Kubiak already showed willingness to use a skinny receiver at X with [[Chris Olave]] (career-high 79.1 snaps outside, 68.3% on the line, 20.3% dig and 12.8% out route rates).
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-29]]): Horton has 'a nice little runway' in Seattle after the surprising release of [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]], and the team seems comfortable with him potentially starting from day one. Harmon reiterated he was super bullish on Horton in the draft, grading him as a day-two player in his class and a tier above [[Malik Washington]] among the day-three receivers who have gained steam this week.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): Horton returned a punt for a touchdown to make it 14-0 and then made 'an amazing catch' on a corner fade from [[Sam Darnold]] for a receiving score. Harris's read on the role is what matters: 'Tory Horton plays a lot, he's in Seattle's base formation three-wide, I think he seems like a player, he's fast, he showed awesome ball skills.' The caveat is the depth-chart math — 'it's difficult to start any team's third wide receiver, let alone Sam Darnold's third wide receiver' — though he noted [[Cooper Kupp]] 'doesn't look fast out there.' Harris named him in the waiver segment and said 'I'm interested in Tory Horton in the longer term.'
- 2025-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-25]]): Horton is a clear-cut starting third receiver for Seattle, not a garbage-time artifact — in the first half against New Orleans he ran eight routes to ten each for [[Cooper Kupp]] and [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]], a 72.7% route participation, with three catches for 32 yards and a touchdown before halftime, plus a return touchdown. Over the full game all three receivers ran 16 routes. He is lined up out wide on 85% of his routes, 17.9% in the slot, the most perimeter usage of anyone heavily involved in the offense, and Harmon has been impressed with his man-coverage wins. Harmon maintained he was a day-two talent who fell to the fifth round because of injury, and offensive coordinator Clint Kubiak, asked for a rookie comparison, reluctantly named [[Justin Jefferson]] — explicitly about work ethic, study and preparation, not projected outcome, and Harmon stresses neither he nor Kubiak is predicting that career. Koh (not tracked) adds that Seattle cutting [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]], who knew Kubiak's playbook from New Orleans, told us where the team ranked Horton, and that he had expected a redshirt year. Harmon sees a chance Horton is the team's number two receiver by the end of this season and certainly next season.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): with no [[Cooper Kupp]] in the game, Horton scored on a crosser in the second quarter and then, on a third-and-long from the slot, beat his man with 'a great release, a great route' on a deep corner for a step and a half and was gone to make it 14-0. Harris explicitly opens the door: if Kupp stays out or is minimized — which Harris thinks he probably should be — Horton becomes one of the ancillary Seattle pieces worth adding. He stops short of starting him right away, but draws the comparison to belatedly realizing you should be using [[Kayshon Boutte]] in a good offense.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-04]]): Horton is his number two waiver add and Jeff Erickson of RotoWire (not a tracked expert) had him number one. Harris was more excited by Horton's Sunday-night film than anything else he watched — he calls the route sick and uses it to mock the praise heaped on [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]'s slant, where the defender simply fell down. Horton came out of nowhere to steal a job; a lot of draft nicks liked him but nobody singled him out. Harris frames the add as wanting pieces of good offenses and Seattle is a good offense, and says the team is obviously better with Horton playing a bigger role than [[Cooper Kupp]] — but he is adding, not starting, expecting one or two catches and a likely drop in a couple of weeks. Erickson notes only 35 snaps with Kupp out and a 38-7 blowout suppressing volume, an average matchup versus Arizona, and that he showed the separation skill back in training camp and preseason. Harris ranks [[Christian Watson]] above him for Week 10.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-04]]): Horton's Sunday Night breakout — 4 targets, 4 catches, 48 yards, 2 touchdowns starting for an injured [[Cooper Kupp]] — was, after charting it, 'real deal big boy wide receiver stuff': a quick slant win, a contested-catch out-route touchdown over Marshon Lattimore, and a corner-route touchdown beating press man from the slot, none of them busted coverage. Harmon says he graded Horton as an early round-two prospect and cannot understand the fifth-round fall, given a 4.41 forty and 37.5-inch vertical run on a knee he doubts was fully healthy. Horton has 5 touchdowns on 22 targets and 13 catches. Usage has been capped by Seattle ranking 31st in 11-personnel rate and by veteran deference to Kupp; he has not topped a 50% snap share since Week 1. Harmon's second takeaway: Horton as a credible on-ball perimeter threat lets Seattle move JSN around, which Kupp cannot enable.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-06]]): the [[Rashid Shaheed]] trade complicates the Horton breakout both hosts had just endorsed. Harmon does not think Horton pushes Shaheed out and expects Shaheed to out-snap him rest of season, but proposes a tidy resolution — keep the Monday night alignment (Jaxon Smith-Njigba 100% route participation, Horton 94%, A.J. Barner 75%, Cody White 69%, Elijah Arroyo 34%) and simply swap Shaheed in for Cody White, leaving Horton on the field a ton. He calls Horton probably Seattle's best on-ball receiver, which is a distinct role from Shaheed's. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is more worried: he would be far more confident in Horton if the incoming player were anyone other than a receiver who played this well in Klint Kubiak's offense last season, and he warns his own Horton enthusiasm is recency bias after a lights-out week. Harmon flags the same risk in reverse — Horton could be the depth piece.
- 2025-11-21 — According to Chris Harris on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-21]] [Dynasty]: Harris said if [[Rashid Shaheed]] leaves Seattle in free agency, Horton -- whom he liked in the brief window before Shaheed's trade and Horton's own injury -- would be his pick as the 2026 value riser instead of Shaheed.
- 2025-12-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-30]]): Horton was a 'just missed' pick -- Harris had already featured him on a list back in November, right before Horton suffered a season-ending injury. Harris cited a highlight release/separation rep against Marshon Lattimore he said 'Collinsworth fell off his chair' watching, and called Horton a genuine midseason revelation before he got hurt. Chris Raybon (Action Network, not tracked) agreed Horton was talented and speculated that, had he stayed healthy, Seattle likely wouldn't have traded for Rashid Shaheed and Horton could have settled in as the presumptive No. 2 receiver behind Jaxon Smith-Njigba next year; instead his path is now cloudier, with Shaheed (also a pending free agent) now ahead of him in the pecking order.
- 2026-01-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: ranked Horton his #4 level-jumper despite a season mostly lost to injury, arguing he was really a second-round talent who fell to day three (round 5) purely on injury risk. Harmon praised his downfield speed combined with open-field shiftiness and technical route running that lets him win both vertically and on underneath routes, calling him a receiver who is not at risk of being subbed out for extra tight ends. He and [[Chris Harris]] both had Horton on a 'players whose film we love' list in late December and cited his Monday Night Football touchdown against Washington (turning around Mikey Sainristil) right before his injury as evidence. Harmon says a real path opens for Horton if the Seahawks move on from [[Rashid Shaheed]] or Cooper Kupp becomes a cap casualty, and that he and JSN could be nearly interchangeable perimeter starters if the roster clears the right way.
- 2026-01-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-27]]): Horton, a fifth-round pick, was 'playing really well' for Seattle before an injury that prompted the team to trade for [[Rashid Shaheed]] as a replacement.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-05]]) [Dynasty]: Horton flashed as a rookie X-factor -- five TDs on 13 catches, including a 4-catch, 48-yard, 2-touchdown game -- before a mysterious shin injury (not the ACL tear that ended his college career) shut down the back half of his season; per coach Mike McDonald he's 'fixed up' but not expected to do any spring work. Horton was only a fifth-round pick but Harmon says he played like a Day 2 talent, and calls him one of his more interesting dynasty trade targets right now precisely because he's likely cheap -- Harmon says he'd 'trade for him as a throw-in' -- with outcomes bifurcated between total bust and huge bargain depending entirely on health. He notes the Seahawks remain high on Horton and loved what they saw from him before the injury.
- 2026-04-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-23]]) [Dynasty]: Harris closed his top-20 redraft with Horton at #20, citing one standout move on Monday Night Football just minutes before Horton was lost for the season to injury. Harris argued the real roadblock to superstardom isn't teammate Rashid Shaheed, whose main value last year was on special teams -- 'if the reason you can't make it is because of Rashid Shaheed, you probably weren't going to make it anyway' -- but said the flashes of route-running and ankle-breaking ability would need to become undeniable in camp for Horton to be fantasy relevant.
- 2026-05-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on Reception Perception's new player-comparison tool ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-14]]): Horton's top comp-tool matches for his 2025 rookie season were D.J. Moore's 2025 season as his only 'strong' comparison, plus Rashid Shaheed, Brandon Aiyuk (2021), John Brown (2015) and Keelan Cole (2017) as moderate-or-better matches -- mostly Z-alignment separation/speed players. Harmon called the Shaheed match 'a little twist of the knife' for Horton dynasty backers, since Shaheed's presence in Seattle could block Horton from a true lead role even though the comp data reads as a real talent signal. Horton's full written year-two profile was not yet live at time of recording (expected Friday, 2026-05-15).
- 2026-05-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] (with untracked co-host James Koh) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-19]]) [Dynasty]: Horton's rookie RP sample (8 games, ending with a breakout vs Washington before a season-ending injury) graded 69% success vs man, 78% vs zone and 76% vs press - just under Harmon's 70/80 benchmarks but 'very encouraging' for a rookie. He played almost exclusively outside (82.3% outside, 59% on-ball) and separated at all three levels: 100% success on comebacks (7.5% route share), 80% on digs, 82% on slants and 74% on posts. Harmon rates him a legitimate X-receiver talent - a second-round grade who fell to the fifth round purely on injury history - but flags a crowded 2026 target picture: Seattle re-signed [[Rashid Shaheed]] to a 3-year, $51M deal (~$17M AAV) after trading for him, plus [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] and [[Cooper Kupp]] remain, and the team runs heavy 12-personnel (top-10 in 2+ TE usage on passing downs). Notably, Horton's own top player-comp-tool match is 2025 Rashid Shaheed. Harmon floats Seattle as a possible 'WR2-by-committee' situation and calls Horton a 'deep sleeper,' but his fantasy relevance is injury-contingent both for himself and ahead of him on the depth chart.
- 2026-07-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said [[Tory Horton]] is a name to watch in Seattle but is more likely to pass [[Cooper Kupp]] than to leapfrog [[Rashid Shaheed]] for the number-two receiver job; he's coming off a shin injury but is expected to participate in camp.
- 2026-07-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris remains high on Horton, ranking him WR57 and ahead of [[Rashid Shaheed]] (WR63) heading into camp, pointing to his rookie efficiency -- 5 touchdowns on just 13 catches/22 targets -- and a role that appeared to be expanding before he scored against Washington and was immediately lost for the season. Harris flagged Horton's health picture remains murky: he had a fractured shin and some kind of subsequent surgery, but it's unclear if the surgery was on the leg or a groin issue, and neither host nor guest could find a clear answer. Guest Ben Gretsch (Stealing Signals newsletter, not tracked) is open to positive Horton news but is generally lukewarm -- he cited a 'hard to board a moving train' concern since Horton arrived mid-season with Seattle's rotation already set -- but noted the team's decision to re-sign Shaheed to a new contract while continuing to invest buzz in Horton suggests both could carve out real roles. Both agreed a negative health update on Horton would be the thing to flip these ranks in Shaheed's favor.
