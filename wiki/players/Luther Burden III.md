- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]): Burden is his pick for the 2025 receiver getting too much buzz — Waldman likes him a lot but calls him 'closer yet farther away' than the market believes. He has the physical attributes to be a high-end starter, roughly what [[Rashee Rice]] grew into before his injury, but the risk is that he plays so hard and so fast that the game has never slowed down for him: he has stop and he has go, with no second gear. Waldman ties it to Belichick's point about controlling direction and oscillating tempo in routes — what made [[Travis Kelce]], Antonio Gates and Tony Gonzalez great late in their careers — and says Burden will not step into an NFL offense and post 1,400 yards; he has to be coached on down-to-down play first. [[Brandon Angelo]] separately notes Burden is the opposite of [[Tyler Lockett]] — reckless and erratic — but that recklessness is his canvas, and asks whether he can calm it down the way [[Malik Nabers]] learned to as a rookie.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls Burden his WR1 of the class and says he is still underrated despite a high board consensus, ahead of [[Tetairoa McMillan]] and [[Matthew Golden]]. He dismisses the concern Dwain McFarland (untracked) teased from his rookie model about the share of Burden's opportunities coming at or behind the line of scrimmage — whether he's a gadget player — saying he doesn't care, and notes JJ Zachariason (untracked) and McFarland reported their own models don't care either. His case is technique: Burden is among the best at winning boundary targets over his shoulder, with late separation, late hands and the timing to position himself to the ball, unlike big receivers who surrender position to the defender while making acrobatic-looking catches. He is also one of the class's most skilled runners after the catch, second only to [[Savion Williams]] (a Cordarrelle Patterson-type runner). Waldman would take him over [[DeVonta Smith]] as a WR2 who can be a WR1, and says Burden would have scored inside his top five of last year's class alongside Odunze, Thomas, Harrison, Nabers and Worthy.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): Burden's standout trait is scramble-drill savvy — when his quarterback breaks the pocket he knows when to work back to the passer and when to go downfield and uncover, disguising from the cornerback that the ball is coming. His positioning between defender and ball is timely and effective, and he protects the ball from coverage the instant he catches it. Waldman projects him to become a quarterback's favorite in the middle of the field and in the red zone specifically because of how he scrambles, uncovers and earns position. He also names Burden one of the two best open-field runners in the class, alongside [[Savion Williams]].
- 2025-04-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: the most confusing evaluation in the class and an 'excellent example of a grade-the-flashes player' — if you grade the flashes he is the clear WR2 in the class behind [[Travis Hunter]], the one prospect other than Hunter you could build a passing game around, but the range of outcomes is enormous. Charting: 71.2% success rate vs man on limited man reps, 79.4% vs zone (under 80 and, Harmon says, 'not bad but not great'), 90.9% on out routes, 78% on digs, 84% on curls, 55% on nines (slightly below collegiate average), 87.5% contested catch rate on just 10% of targets, in space on 70% of catches, down on first contact 37.5%, multiple broken tackles on 25%. Usage was 82.9% slot, 81.7% off the line, 6.3% backfield — and he faced more zone coverage than perhaps any prospect Harmon has charted. The real worry is feel vs zone: he runs himself into zones and gets caught up on downfield routes. Archetype is a 'souped-up [[Khalil Shakir]] / faster, better-vs-man [[Rashee Rice]]'; Harmon rejects the [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] gadget comp and endorses JJ Zachariason's (not tracked) [[Brandon Aiyuk]] comp as the developmental path — but only if Burden dials in, and Harmon reports character and work-ethic/preparation concerns (short of a [[Jermaine Burton]]-level red flag) that he believes are why Burden slid out of media first rounds. Contrast with [[Emeka Egbuka]]: lower ceiling, much higher floor. Koh (not tracked) argued Burden's best area is the intermediate field and raised [[Stefon Diggs]] as a down-final-season precedent, while noting one 1,200-yard season in three years.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: the 2023-to-2024 drop-off is mostly Missouri, not Burden. Late in 2023 the staff understood what to do with him in the route concepts; 2024 was a bland offense with no creativity and — critically — no other middle-tier playmaker to draw attention, which cripples a receiver whose value is post-catch and winning the ball. Angelo: the talent is there and Burden could be the top receiver of this class in three years. [[Matt Waldman]] offered profile analogues for a down or missing final season — [[Ja'Marr Chase]] opting out to train, and [[Rashad Bateman]] losing weight to COVID and playing at a lower weight — and praised how Burden tracks the football and works himself open downfield and in the red zone by baiting a defender one way, the skill [[Cooper Kupp]] and [[Ladd McConkey]] showed with Herbert. Waldman expects that to magnify in the NFL relative to playing with Brady Cook.
- 2025-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether Burden could be a better prospect than [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] was, Waldman says he already is, and calls Burden the BEST RECEIVER IN THIS CLASS. Burden may never match Harrison's ceiling specifically as an X/split end winning one-on-one tight to the line — Waldman would take Burden there only half the time — but across every other role Harrison is not even in the room. - 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): responding to [[Matt Harmon]]'s framing that anyone ranking Burden as the WR1 is doing so off 'flashes,' Waldman confirms he does rank Burden number one and says Harmon's point was not really negative. His explanation of the methodological split: [[Reception Perception]] counts how successfully a player does things, so a low count of alpha-type downfield routes reads as a small sample and therefore flashes; the RSP instead evaluates the techniques behind those routes to judge whether they project. For Waldman the sample does not need to be large if the craft and concepts inside it are clearly there and repeated. He adds that Burden would have been his WR3 in last year's excellent class with [[Travis Hunter]] not far behind, and argues Burden is underrated because so much of the market reads him through statistical modeling rather than film detail. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) presses on whether Waldman is simply lower on the rest of the class.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21 (Film & Data with Dwain McFarland)]]): Burden is one of his top prospects in the class with a 93 RSP score — 'if there's another guy on your team like that, your offense better be excited.' Waldman rejects the gadget-player label: Burden's technique is 'dense and exact and craft-filled', he uncovers late in routes off-script the way [[Ladd McConkey]] did for [[Justin Herbert]], tracks the ball early, and his separation and release work all grade out before you ever mention his run-after-catch. Asked about [[Travis Hunter]], Waldman said the two would still be his 1-2 and both would rank top six from the prior class. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) agreed: an 81 production rating, 1.35 career adjusted receiving yards per team pass attempt (57th percentile program quality at Missouri), 64th percentile above expectation, a five-star recruit who produced immediately rather than late. Burden's 9.1 aDOT, 32% of targets behind the line and 21% deep put his closest data comps at [[Calvin Ridley]] and [[Brandon Aiyuk]] — and Waldman notes his own comp, and [[Matt Harmon]]'s, was also Aiyuk, a player also dismissed as a screen-and-slant receiver who became one of the league's better route runners. Zierlein's 6.47 film grade (84 in the model) reflects untapped ceiling; his draft capital was falling toward round two at the time of taping.
- 2025-04-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-22]]): Burden is the most misunderstood receiver in the 2025 class, and Harmon rejects both poles of the discourse. He 'completely rejects' the idea that Burden's biggest problem is being a gadget player — there are real outbreaking routes, in-breakers, reps against man and nice ball tracking at the catch point on his film, including a 90.9% success rate on the out route and a 7.6% dig rate that is within the normal NFL range — but he also says there is 'a lot of bad shit on the film too' and that he understands why the league is souring on Burden. His verdict is that the honest read is somewhere in the middle. The question is not whether the good plays exist, it is whether the drafting team shares that vision and whether Burden puts in the work; Harmon would draft him, develop him as a real receiver and feed him touches in the meantime as a strong run-after-catch threat. He and James Koh (not tracked) both dismissed the Matt Miller practice-route cut-up circulating that day as meaningless, with Harmon noting Reception Perception does not give takes on practice tape.
