# Tank Bigsby

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): had Bigsby rated 46th overall pre-draft and calls him overrated by "about a round and a half" relative to where he actually went. Cites specific in-game evidence — at least three Trevor Lawrence interceptions traceable to Bigsby failing to secure a catchable pass out of the backfield — and calls him an inconsistent, indecisive runner. Concedes the opportunity case (he'd get 1,000 yards as the lead back if Travis Etienne were hurt) but says he wouldn't proactively roster him over [[Kendre Miller]] or [[Chase Brown]] as a dynasty stash.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: reiterates distrust in Bigsby as the reason he'd still take [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] even at his current best-ball ceiling ADP (RB6) — Bigsby "has a lot to prove" on holding onto the football, catching the ball, and making the right decisions between the tackles.
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said Jacksonville wants Tank Bigsby to cut significantly into [[Travis Etienne Jr.]]'s workload this year if his running backs coach's praise for his improvement holds up on the field -- the checklist is becoming a better between-the-tackles decision-maker who stops trying to bounce runs outside and shores up ball security both as a runner and receiver. If he does, Waldman said 'we're vastly underrating him,' though he cautioned Bigsby may never fully take over given what he called the 'Christian McCaffrey effect' -- when one back is clearly the roster's best player, an offense structurally can't take him off the field, similar to how [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] played roughly 75% of Jacksonville's RB touches last year. Waldman noted Bigsby looked great in padless training camp reps last year (fooling observers, including his own podcast co-host) but couldn't translate it to games, finishing with only 50 carries (second-most on the team), 20% of which came in a week 17 blowout win over Carolina.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]): Bigsby is his cautionary case study for preseason hype. Everyone went gaga over Bigsby last summer, but what a back shows in camp and preseason is a small window that closely resembles the college game; once the real NFL season began, Waldman says Bigsby's game self-destructed — he could not catch the ball, could not hold onto it, and made wrong decisions on interior plays he should have kept inside. He needed a year. Waldman is now a little higher on Bigsby, hoping he has gained confidence and figured out what to do, and he explicitly warns of the same 'Bigsby effect' with [[Marshawn Lloyd]] and [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]].
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-12]]), with film in his Gut Check column at Footballguys and the RSP Film Room: Bigsby looked much better than his 2023 rookie year, when preseason hype gave way to mistakes Waldman attributes to the speed and complexity of the pro game making him overthink. Week 1 he was not mistake-prone, held the ball securely through several hits on the ball, and made good cutbacks. Waldman's key distinction: he did not look *better*, he looked like the Auburn Tank Bigsby again — which to him caps him at an RB2 unless he lands on a great team with heavy volume. He says if Bigsby had [[Jordan Mason]]'s 49ers role he would be an RB1, but he would still prefer Mason on Jacksonville as the more refined, accomplished runner. Verdict: not bad, and worth a roster spot. Harstad (NOT tracked) separately used Bigsby as the roster-spot example in his win-win trade framework.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): Bigsby is a good back and a solid RB2-type player on a good team who could even sneak into top-12 weeks, but he is not better than [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] and is not there to be the lead piece. Waldman says he laid this out in his Gut Check column about a month earlier and that the film supports it: Bigsby is the better downhill runner and is getting closeout touches. His comp is [[Kareem Hunt]]. Both hosts note Bigsby was all the rage in the summer of 2023 before it emerged he did not know how to secure the football properly — it took a year of coaching to fix, and only now has he become the complementary piece Jacksonville drafted him to be.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: sell the hype. Waldman profiled him after Week 1 and saw the Auburn back again, with one real change — ball security, which was his failing as a rookie. Otherwise: determined, likes to bounce and cut back and needs to be more selective about it, enough burst to flip a field on a big crease, good downhill with momentum, but nothing that says future [[Joe Mixon]]. Waldman's usage call is RB3-for-matchups — good days against fronts that can be run on inside, with [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] taking the edge and open-space work otherwise, since Bigsby is a screen player rather than a dynamic receiver. He advises trading him around Week 7-8 after he builds value against New England, Green Bay and Chicago. [[Brandon Angelo]] agreed with the sell logic — 'if the player's not elite, sell at the peak' — noting Bigsby has actually improved since entering the league while Etienne has declined, but neither is a top-of-the-mountain guy. Angelo also flags that a bad Jacksonville defense means trail scripts, which is poison for a rhythm runner needing 17-20 touches. His career comp is a Jamaal Williams-plus secondary/energy-giver role, not a Detroit-style true committee.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): Bigsby is a sell-high regression candidate on a specific schedule window. He is 37th in PPR while averaging around eight yards per carry, which Waldman calls fool's gold — Bigsby is a better interior runner than [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] and has just enough burst to find cutbacks and pop one long run against teams that are weak up the middle, but he is still not an intelligent runner between the tackles and the mistakes show up on film. He is a screen-pass player rather than a space threat. Waldman expects the next two to three games against soft interior fronts to inflate his value, and advises trading him before the Packers game; after that, facing stout fronts like Philadelphia, you may wish you had sat him and the game plan should shift back toward Etienne.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: after Bigsby went 26 carries for 118 yards and two touchdowns filling in for [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] against New England, Waldman called this probably the last strong sell-high window — maybe one more week if Jacksonville can stay point-for-point with Green Bay. Doug Pederson said publicly he does not replace a starter due to injury, so Waldman expects Etienne back as the nominal lead in a committee. On the tape, 'there's nothing that he did that really impressed me on a high level': a powerful back with good determination who bullies out-of-position safeties, which Waldman says most backs do when handed creases up the middle. He also flagged only two targets on the season and doubted Jacksonville's defense keeps Bigsby in enviable game scripts. Caveat: a favorable late schedule means if he is your RB3-RB5 and you can afford to hold, he could be a must-start in the fantasy playoff weeks.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): the sell window has closed — Waldman restates that the deadline to trade Bigsby away was the Green Bay game, because he is about to get shut down until roughly the fantasy playoffs. He doubts very seriously that Bigsby gives more than RB3 or RB4 production over the next several weeks. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) flagged the oddity of Bigsby running more routes than [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] in this game as contrary to what they had been told to expect.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]) — asked 'feel it or f**k it' on Bigsby as Jacksonville's 2025 starter, he says f**k it: the likelihood of Bigsby being the main guy next year is 'really low.' He reminds listeners he warned after Week 2 that Bigsby would only give you so many good weeks before the door shut, and that predictability is the problem even if Bigsby produces down the stretch. Bigsby is not a complete player; [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] is not a complete player either, but Etienne gives more in the passing game and a better chance on the perimeter running game. Waldman also thinks Jacksonville must spend its attention on the offensive line, and notes an entirely new regime may be coming in, which clouds any projection.
- 2025-06-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: at RB43 Waldman is not feeling Bigsby and thinks he is on his way out in Jacksonville — the sort of player a team simply moves on from, though Waldman added that he personally rates Bigsby as better than 'just moving-on-from-able'. He noted the trade market logic cuts both ways: there is little return available for either Bigsby or [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] right now. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) countered that SiriusXM colleague Mike Dempsey, who covers the Jaguars daily, is dismissive of the idea that anyone in that backfield is being moved on from. Bigsby and Etienne finished a spot apart at RB35 and RB36 in 2024.
- 2025-07-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-28]]) [Best Ball]: 'feel it' — the Bigsby buzz is real, and Waldman is coming to the conclusion Bigsby is the back Jacksonville wants to keep, a reversal from his expectation that Bigsby would be the odd man out. He runs hard, was a better gap than zone runner but got used on plays that translate to outside zone, which suits Liam Coen's outside-zone-friendly scheme, and looked more comfortable in year two. Waldman speculates the team may be positioning to trade a back, with [[Bhayshul Tuten]] quiet in camp — possibly because the fumbles are a bigger internal issue than the media's single-fumble framing suggests, and because Tuten was a raw decision maker who may need time in the system. Bob Harris (untracked) reports three people close to the organization, via Mike Dempsey, all named Bigsby as the likely Week 1 starter, while Tony Smith thinks it will be [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] and Dempsey is unsure Etienne is even on the roster to open the season.
- 2025-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Bigsby is the number 7 flag player at an 11th-round price. Camp reports had him as Jacksonville's unquestioned top back, then [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] played the whole first-team series in the first preseason game with Bigsby behind him — which Harris uses as a reminder that we never know the roles and teams will not tell us. He pushes back on Dave Kluge (not tracked), who said the Jacksonville backfield has the same three roles as 2024 Tampa Bay; Harris says the analogy breaks down because [[Rachaad White]] is the big back who catches it while [[Bucky Irving]] ran better inside. His actual case is simple: Bigsby was significantly better to watch than Etienne last year and fits a goal-line profile — not much bigger, but runs harder and straighter and is unafraid of contact — while Etienne sits inside the top 100 and Bigsby goes two full rounds later. He concedes no Jacksonville back is being drafted as a top-24 option, and mocks the Twitter cycle that briefly crowned [[Bhayshul Tuten]] the starter.
- 2025-08-25 — According to guest JJ Zachariason (NOT a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]]: Bigsby is probably the best early-down back in Jacksonville right now and should work in that role to start the season, barring a trade — there have been rumors about him or [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] moving in the next 24 hours. JJ's fear is pass catching: Bigsby has never been that guy, has never been an overly efficient pass catcher, and has had issues in the NFL there, which leaves room for [[Bhayshul Tuten]] to take that role. [[Chris Harris]] says he would have put Bigsby on the late-round list — he is going in the 11th — but Bigsby is already on his flag-player list so he was ineligible.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Bigsby is a flag player who landed on a couple of his teams and would have landed on more if listeners had not been sniping him — he jumped up to the eighth round in the charity league, earlier than Bigsby normally went in August, just to stop being beaten to him, and got him in the 13th in a league where nobody knew his game. He is clear this is not a commitment to using Bigsby in lineups right away. On the wider Jaguars backfield, he says the July question was whether it would be a three-headed monster or whether [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] might be cut; Etienne went nowhere, exhibition signals were highly mixed, there were late-August beat reports of increased reps for [[Bhayshul Tuten]], and the new staff simply did not tip their hand — all three backs got drafted and all three probably should have been.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon dismissed Bigsby from the Jacksonville backfield conversation after charting him — he was 'so bad in pass pro' that Harmon cannot imagine him on the field, and was a very limited player anyway. Harmon's order is [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] clearly first, with [[Bhayshul Tuten]] the more interesting unknown.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: after a summer of trade speculation — including a full day of fantasy Twitter parsing a Bigsby tweet that may have been nothing more than an eyes emoji for signs of displeasure or an impending trade — Harris and guest Andy Behrens (not tracked) say the Jacksonville backfield has landed exactly where it was in June: a probable split between Bigsby and [[Travis Etienne Jr.]], with [[Bhayshul Tuten]] not the guy to start the year. Harris used the episode as an example of manufactured offseason narrative, calling the ride fantasy Twitter went on 'absolutely crazy.'
- 2025-09-04 — According to guest Jake Trowbridge (not a tracked expert) on the DFS segment ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-04]]): Bigsby at $4,600 is simply mispriced — Trowbridge admits he has no idea how Jacksonville is dividing that backfield, but is pretty sure Bigsby will play, and at that number that is enough. He concedes the obvious flaw that Bigsby doesn't help you in full PPR. Earlier in the show [[Chris Harris]] used the Jaguars listing [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] and Bigsby as co-starters as an example of a pre-Week-1 depth chart he sees no reason to believe.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): Bigsby played 12 snaps and did little in Week 1 before Monday's trade from Jacksonville to Philadelphia, which Harris reads as a clear statement that the Eagles were not happy with what they had behind [[Saquon Barkley]] — not least because [[Will Shipley]] got hurt in Week 1, which now looks like it might be serious. Harris says [[A.J. Dillon]] is no way to insure a Barkley injury and Bigsby is not that kind of player either, but Bigsby is now the closest thing Barkley has to a handcuff. Practical angle: in a deeper league, approach the Barkley manager and see what they will give up for him. Harris notes wryly he would not have listed Bigsby as a 'flag player' had he known this was coming.
