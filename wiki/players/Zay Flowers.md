# Zay Flowers

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): belongs in his top tier of the 2023 rookie wide receiver class, but a little more boom/bust within it than [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]], [[Jordan Addison]], [[Puka Nacua]] and [[Jayden Reed]] — the swing factor is how broadly [[Lamar Jackson]]'s offense will trust and target him, not his own play, which Waldman expects to keep improving as the two build familiarity.
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: resolves an open question from earlier in the season — a running "please don't trade [[Drake London]] for Flowers" plea — decisively in London's favor now that the full rookie season is in: "no question to me that London had the more impressive rookie year," and London is roughly six months younger. Not a downgrade on Flowers, whose downside risk Waldman says has "largely been reduced" (Adam Harstad's [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] puts him in an ~80%-hit-rate score range), but his efficiency component is only slightly above average — his score is propped up mostly by usage/target volume rather than per-route quality, tempering the "elite" framing a bit.
- 2024-01-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-25]]): ranked #4 of the playoffs' remaining receivers, but with real long-term upside language — "this guy can really be in that superstar tier at some point," citing a double-move go-route sold and broken back to the outside against Houston as "pro-level stuff" and evidence he can win in a variety of ways, not just manufactured touches. Explicit AFC Championship matchup caution against Kansas City: a 65/35 outside/inside alignment usage runs into the Chiefs' league-best perimeter coverage (3rd-lowest completion percentage, 4th-fewest catches, 3rd-fewest yards allowed to outside receivers since Week 10) — part of the reason Harmon likes [[Lamar Jackson]] under his passing-yardage and passing-touchdown props for the game.
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): a rookie-arc framing after the AFC Championship loss played out as predicted — a costly goal-line fumble (reached out rather than securing the ball, drew a taunting penalty on the prior play that pushed the offense back 15 yards) turned what should have been a 115-yard/touchdown game into its "worst" possible version, and he "cut his hand open" slamming his helmet on the bench afterward. Still, Harmon calls it clear that Flowers "has star potential" and can be "a huge cog for them going forward," while noting he wasn't fully unleashed for the whole season — closer to it now than in September.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): Harmon says
  he's "very high" on Flowers as a player, part of why he ranks Baltimore's
  otherwise thin "fragile"-tier receiver room ahead of Atlanta's and
  Carolina's despite little proven depth behind him and [[Rashad Bateman]].
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: Baltimore not adding a receiver until Tez Walker at pick 113 signals internal faith that Flowers is ready for a 'second-year leap' into being a big-time intermediate-area target earner. Harmon loved his rookie route running on tape -- capable of winning against both man and zone, and down the field -- but noted the Ravens' scheme underused him in the intermediate area, leaning instead on designed/shot plays that exploit his explosiveness. He wants to see Flowers get more work in the middle of the field this year for the receiver room to function, and projects him as an easy 1,000-1,100-yard receiver if the offense trends that way; he already led Baltimore with 858 yards as a rookie.
- 2024-05-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-29]]): Flowers' rookie reception perception profile shows an elite zone/downfield receiver who was undersold by his early gadget usage -- 85% success vs. zone (90th percentile, would rank top-4 among charted 2022 players), 87.5% on posts (top-10 all-time among receivers with 8%+ post-route share), and 86.8% on digs (13.4% participation, his second-most-run route) despite only 9.4% of routes being designed screens. Man coverage success was 71.7% (68th percentile) -- good, not special. Co-host James Koh (untracked) and Harmon flag press coverage as the real weak spot (62.9% success, 35th percentile), along with a surprisingly poor 72.2% slant success rate, down from a 78.8% mark at Boston College. Harmon suggests some of Baltimore's scripted touches for Flowers were as much about building [[Lamar Jackson]]'s comfort in Todd Monken's new offense as about Flowers himself; the path to true WR1 status runs through solving press coverage. [Dynasty]
- 2024-06-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-27]]): Flowers' 2023 Reception Perception profile (71.7% success vs. man, 85% vs. zone, 62.9% vs. press) was compared to 2015 Eric Decker (71.9% man, 82% zone, 68.2% press), a since-retired receiver Harmon called an underrated four-year 'dog' who averaged 1,085 yards and 10.3 touchdowns per season from 2012-2015. Harmon gave a slight edge to Decker in this specific comparison, driven mainly by Decker's notably better press-coverage success rate. He stressed the players are stylistically very different -- Decker was a 6'2'/214 'big slot' who ran a heavy intermediate route tree (84.8% success on digs, 79.3% on posts) and thrived in the red zone, while Flowers is more of a true flanker. Harmon explicitly said this is not a claim Decker is clearly better than Flowers, and noted Flowers already projects for more big-play juice after the catch than Decker ever showed; he added Flowers could plausibly surpass Decker's career output entirely as his career progresses.
- 2024-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-23]]): Flowers is a very good player he would happily give 120 targets, but not yet a clear No. 1 — 'a super highest-of-the-high-order number two or a low-end, maybe average one.' Harmon strongly rejects the label of a designed-touch/screen merchant (agreeing with Robert Mays that manufactured touches are not his best use) even though the open-field dynamism is real: down on first contact on only 35.7% of in-space attempts, multiple broken tackles on 28.6%. What he actually likes is out-breaking route work — 80% success rate on outs, 68.8% on corners, 100% on a small comeback sample, 71.7% vs man, and better still vs zone. To become a No. 1 he needs slants and press to improve, with a 35th-percentile success rate vs press flagged as the number that must move. Harmon also thinks Flowers and [[Lamar Jackson]] were simply out of sync on intermediate and deep in-breaking routes, plays both would want back, and notes Flowers caught 4 of 5 contested targets (80%), so the mentality may be there. James Koh (not tracked) is more skeptical of his physicality at the catch point and would take [[Tee Higgins]] over him; Harmon had them back to back at 13 and 14 in his top-15 receivers 25-and-under for Yahoo.
- 2024-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): his headline hot take is that Flowers clears 1,200 receiving yards, up from about 850 as a rookie — knowingly repeating last year's failed [[Drake London]] 1,200-yard take on a second-year receiver in a run-heavy offense, but with [[Lamar Jackson]] rather than [[Desmond Ridder]] at quarterback. The case is an 85% success rate vs zone, fifth among all charted receivers behind [[Brandon Aiyuk]] (85.4%) and ahead of [[Davante Adams]] (84.4%), [[Justin Jefferson]] (84.3%) and [[Tyreek Hill]] (83.6%); he saw zone on 60.5% of routes because defenses will not turn their backs on Jackson. The caveat is press: his press success rate is far the lowest of that group and his 71.7% vs man is 68th percentile, good not great. Harmon rejects the gadget/design-touches label, citing strong rates on posts, digs, outs and corners, and expects year two of Todd Monken to unlock real route usage. James Koh (not tracked) projects roughly 1,000–1,008 with a 1,325 high side.
- 2024-10-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-15]]): the biggest process change in Baltimore's passing game is Flowers' usage — after Week 1 Harmon was worried they would feed him nothing but what he calls 'Mickey Mouse' screens and bubbles, but over the last two weeks he has been targeted on real over-the-middle and outbreaking routes in the intermediate area: crossers, digs, deep posts. He still wants the screens too, because Flowers is dynamic with the ball in his hands, but the route diversity is what makes it matter. Harmon rates him as not quite in [[Tank Dell]]'s rookie-year category on outbreaking routes — Dell's Reception Perception profile made him one of the best in the league there — but 'damn near close.' He counts Flowers and [[Rashad Bateman]] as two receivers who can win man coverage outside, which is what lets Baltimore throw from condensed formations.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31]]): Flowers 'is becoming a star' and has done a magnificent job developing into a true number one receiver. Angelo says his role is the most solidified in Baltimore and he does not expect the [[Diontae Johnson]] trade to eat into his targets — Flowers gets designed touches and is now getting the downfield work too, which he does better than anyone on the team because of how good he is at the break point. Angelo also flags that Baltimore is already paying [[Rashad Bateman]] after doubling down on him contractually, and that Flowers will command big money soon, which shapes whether Johnson stays past this season.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31 (Tapping Out, Archetypes and Modeling)]]) [Dynasty]: co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) said that even after Flowers trailed [[Drake London]] through 2024 he still gives Flowers a 40-45% chance of being the better and more productive receiver over the next ten years — 'barely worse than a coin flip' — and pushed back on readers who read his anti-trade stance as claiming Flowers had no case. Harstad characterized Flowers' early 2023 production (roughly 8 catches for 86 yards in the game that triggered the panic) as schemed dump-offs rather than a receiver breaking down a defense — good as evidence he is in the plan, not as evidence of separation. He also floated the counterfactual that Flowers underproducing could be a Baltimore problem, an offense spread too horizontally with targets too widely shared, rather than a player problem. Waldman noted the current gap is about four touchdowns.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-31]]): Baltimore has 'unlocked a little bit more' from Flowers of late, and he is the Ravens' best receiver after the catch — a skill set that mixes and matches well with new arrival [[Diontae Johnson]], the better pure route runner. Harmon believes Flowers can be a genuine vertical threat, but with a hard caveat: he has to be where he is supposed to be. He cited the viral clip from the Cleveland game where Flowers looked wide open deep and [[Lamar Jackson]] missed him — on the replay Flowers stumbled twice and stacked a bunch of breaks, 'running a damn route at the Senior Bowl practice,' so Jackson could not have expected him there. Harmon flagged that as a carryover issue from his rookie year. Deployment: roughly 60/40 outside to slot, in a room with no true slot receiver (Nelson Agholor is at 41 percent). Both hosts agreed this is the best pass-catching group of Jackson's career, and that the puzzle is fitting everyone in alongside heavy 12 personnel.
- 2024-11-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-29]]): Flowers sits fifth in receiving yards at 789 and on pace for about 1,118 after 858 as a rookie, and Harmon rates him his second-least-surprising leaderboard name — strong rookie RP profile plus a tether to [[Lamar Jackson]], the best quarterback among the players discussed. He repeats his offseason pushback on the 'screen and designed-target merchant' label, citing 2023 success rates of 86.8% on digs, 87.5% on posts, 80% on outs, 68% on corners and 61.3% on nines as high-degree-of-difficulty routes run well. James Koh notes the usage shift: yards per target 7.9 to 9.1, air yards per target 8.7 to 9.9, and yards after catch per reception from about 5.0 to 6.75, with more motion usage in the mold of how the Rams deploy [[Puka Nacua]] — though Harmon cautions the size and catch-point physicality are not comparable. Caveat: Harmon has not charted Flowers this year and wants more consistency before calling him a true number one, while still naming him Baltimore's best and most dynamic receiver ahead of a strong [[Rashad Bateman]] season.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Flowers may not play against Buffalo after a knee injury, which Harmon said was the whole reason Baltimore had signed [[Diontae Johnson]] as insurance before cutting him. In the alpha-versus-committee segment, Harmon used the Ravens as his example of a good group with no alpha: Flowers is a good player, not a great one, and not a clear-cut number one on any other offense; [[Rashad Bateman]] is good but a tier below him; and [[Mark Andrews]] is still a top-five to top-seven tight end from a pass-catching perspective but has taken a step back. Given the choice, Harmon and James Koh (not tracked) both said they would rather have a true alpha with mediocre pieces around him than that trio.
- 2025-01-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-21]]) [Dynasty]: you cannot discuss Baltimore's postseason run without noting their best or at least most dynamic pass catcher did not play in it — Flowers missing the playoffs was a big loss and part of why the Ravens could not sustain drives against Buffalo. Harmon expects him to keep improving entering his third season and names him one of three ascending Baltimore pass catchers alongside [[Isaiah Likely]] and [[Rashad Bateman]], arguing the top two receiver spots are set and Baltimore needs depth bodies rather than a big swing at the position.
- 2025-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-05]]): asked to fill in the blank on Flowers and [[Rashad Bateman]] as a one-two, Harmon said 'good enough' — good enough to win the whole thing — because Baltimore has [[Lamar Jackson]] and one of the league's most efficient offenses, and their problems are postseason problems in games Flowers missed. He puts Flowers in the ballpark of a player who commands defensive attention alongside the quarterback and running back, so Baltimore does not need to go get a true number one. James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) pushed back: he thinks Flowers, a smaller receiver, is a number one only on the low end of that spectrum, and that Baltimore genuinely needs a true alpha. Both agreed the real need is depth in defined roles — a dirty-work receiver, a reliable slot, someone who wins over the top — rather than another star, naming [[Darius Slayton]], Dyami Brown and [[Josh Palmer]] as cheap fits, since Nelson Agholor-type replacements in a playoff game are unacceptable.
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]): Harmon says he really likes Flowers and that he and [[Rashad Bateman]] are 'good enough' as a duo, but that Baltimore needed a third pitch — which is what he thinks [[DeAndre Hopkins]] provides. Koh sharpens the concern: Reception Perception has shown Flowers is much more capable against zone coverage than against man press, which gets dicey when he is your number one and facing press on third-and-long, however good he is after the catch. Harmon also raises an unresolved question about the room — with Bateman as the Ravens' X and Hopkins a career X, who actually plays the slot in Baltimore.
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-13]]): Flowers is the most exciting young receiver in an AFC North with zero notable first- or second-year wideouts, and both hosts agreed he is currently a high-end number two rather than a true number one — Harmon comped him to what [[Jaylen Waddle]] would look like as a team's top target, and called him under-discussed for a player on one of the league's best offenses. Charting shows a small step forward: 85% then 84.2% versus zone (89th percentile), man up marginally to 72% (70th percentile), and press improving from 62.9% as a rookie to 70.8%. He excels on posts, digs, outs, corners and nines (63% on the nine, 80% on corners, 71% on posts) and jumped to 91.3% on curls, but gets beaten up on slants and quick routes as a smaller player. The ceiling gate is mistakes — 6.3% drop rate and a 55.6% contested catch rate that Harmon says must climb above 70%. Alignment note: 58.7% outside, primarily a flanker, and Harmon would like more slot reps if [[DeAndre Hopkins]] frees them up. His FanDuel yardage line is 900.5.
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Flowers is his round-five safe pick, rated closer to the fourth against a market that takes him late fifth. Harris thinks the ceiling gets exaggerated — [[Lamar Jackson]] and [[Derrick Henry]] create a ball-control incentive, so you get play-action shots that spring Flowers rather than seven-step drops waiting for him to torch a deep post — but the same run game is what makes him reliably wide open in the middle of the field. On another team his range of outcomes would be far wider, potentially disastrous or ballistic given he is one of the 10-20 fastest players in the league. Guest Scott Pianowski (Yahoo, not tracked) co-signs, calling it a bet on talent and splash plays: only 10 touchdowns in two seasons but seven-to-nine is reachable, and 1,059 yards with four scores should be his floor with 1,200 attainable.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon likes Flowers and expects a nice season — he has developed into one of the better zone beaters in the NFL and has steadily improved against man and press over two seasons — but flagged real limitations. Flowers is still mistake-prone, and Harmon thinks overworking him in the underneath area is almost to his detriment because he is such a weird run-after-catch player, sometimes doing what Harmon calls the [[Diontae Johnson]] hop-back. That is why Harmon's bold prediction is [[Rashad Bateman]] outproducing him, with both having good years catching passes from an MVP-caliber quarterback. James Koh (not tracked) noted Flowers's 2024 line of 1,059 yards and 74 receptions against Bateman's 756 and 45.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) calls himself 'the anti-Zay Flowers guy of the whole universe' alongside Mike Dempsey on the radio — his answer to any 'this guy or Flowers' question is never Flowers, though he concedes it should have been this week and will be some weeks. [[Matt Waldman]] noted Flowers had a phenomenal game and 'tends to do that like once a month.' Both frame the issue as consistent weekly production being the hard thing to find in a run-heavy Baltimore offense.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Flowers was a central beneficiary of Baltimore's offensive explosion, with four huge gains including a screen touchdown to open the third quarter and a big catch he nearly housed the play before. Harris's framing is that the guys drafted as fantasy starters on the Bills and Ravens can simply keep being started; both quarterbacks delivered and Flowers was the receiver who mattered.
