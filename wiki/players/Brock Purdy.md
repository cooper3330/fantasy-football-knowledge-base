# Brock Purdy

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): defends Purdy against the consensus "system quarterback" reading. Asked to rank Dak Prescott, [[Lamar Jackson]], [[C.J. Stroud]] and Purdy by scheme dependence, a Footballguys roundtable unanimously named Purdy; Waldman named Jackson instead. His case for Purdy: he may well be worse off-structure than Prescott or Jackson and has made mistakes there, but he is "very good at processing in structure against tight coverage, making really quick reads that are very difficult to do" — and that skill "fits in any system," which is precisely what a system-*dependent* player's skills do not do.
- 2023-12-21 — **Waldman**, same episode, the supporting comparison: Jimmy Garoppolo played six years in the same San Francisco offense and, while he posted some gaudy yards-per-attempt numbers, "they weren't anywhere near the 9.88 that Purdy was doing." Same scheme, same play-caller, different output — Waldman's argument that the delta belongs to the quarterback.
- 2024-01-01 — Both hosts on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]] [Redraft (Standard)]: asked to pick a mid-round 2024 quarterback between Purdy and [[C.J. Stroud]] (points-per-game gap roughly half a point, ~18.5 each), both lean slightly to Purdy. Waldman's reason is San Francisco's supporting cast, with Kyle Shanahan "still sitting there pulling the strings"; Bob Harris's (not tracked) reason is track record — "he seems to overcome it the next week" after a bad game, a known quantity with more games on tape than Stroud.
- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]) [Dynasty]: a durable, checkable forecast. Compares Purdy's career shape to the early careers of Tom Brady, Kurt Warner, Russell Wilson, and Ben Roethlisberger — quarterbacks who were hyper-efficient on low offensive volume in a favorable situation, then became more heavily relied upon (and just as good or better) once they signed second contracts and the team could no longer surround them with as many other paid stars. His explicit prediction: as San Francisco leans on Purdy more over time, "I don't expect him to be less special than he is now." Points to the 2023 Bengals game (widely cited as Purdy's worst) as the one where his under-pressure processing actually stood out most on film.
- 2024-01-04 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode: floats the counterfactual "Trading Places" thought experiment (via Dan Orlovsky) of swapping Purdy for a game-manager-graded prospect like Mac Jones into a poor situation — his point being that a good organization, scheme, and supporting cast measurably accelerates a decent prospect's development, and a bad one just as measurably stalls it. Not a knock on Purdy specifically, but a caution about how much of his early success to attribute to the player versus the situation. See [[Scheme vs Talent]] and [[Player Development and Coachability]].
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: rejects the "exposed as a game manager" framing outright after the Divisional Round win over Green Bay — "Brock Purdy was exposed as nothing other than what he is: a really good quarterback capable of handling the scheme and trusted by his coach to get the job done." Flags early-offseason expert consensus rankings (ECR) placing Purdy outside the top-12 quarterbacks for 2024 as "a mistake," and says he hopes the market keeps discounting him so he can draft him again at value.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): a direct disagreement with Waldman's read of the same game — Harmon's explicit, repeated verdict is that Purdy "had a bad game" in the Divisional Round win over Green Bay, pushing back hard against what he calls "Purdy derangement syndrome" among 49ers fans reacting to the take. Specific mechanism: visibly struggled to grip and throw the ball in wet weather (re-gripping mid-throw on replay), missing Brandon Aiyuk repeatedly on plays where he was open, before playing well on the game-winning final drive. Gives him credit for a "playmaker gene" — second-reaction ability and scrambling to reset a throw — that Harmon rates above [[Tua Tagovailoa]]'s, while noting he lacks off-platform arm-angle versatility. Season-long, explicit grade: "a good quarterback," "certainly deserves to be a starting quarterback," but not top-12/15 — a "top 20" quarterback rather than elite tier. Frames [[Jared Goff]]'s own late-career mental development as a reason not to treat Purdy as a finished product this early in his starting career.
- 2024-01-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-25]]): heading into the NFC Championship at home against Detroit — expected clear weather removes the grip/accuracy issue flagged two days earlier — repeats that [[Brandon Aiyuk]] "probably had more of those throws available to him" against Green Bay than Purdy actually took, a continuation rather than a softening of the prior game's criticism. Both hosts favor San Francisco to win comfortably regardless.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]), after the NFC Championship win over Detroit: a defense of Purdy against the online "hatred" Waldman says he watched in real time — "he got a little of that dog in him," specifically praising a fourth-quarter run where he lowered his head and made a play when it mattered. His central argument, though, is about supporting cast rather than a solo grade: whatever complaints exist about Purdy are "remedied" by [[Christian McCaffrey]] (130+ yards from scrimmage against a tough run defense regardless of scheme), [[Deebo Samuel]], [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and George Kittle around him, plus a strong defense. Explicitly frames Purdy's skills as "subtle" rather than the traditionally-hyped quarterback traits (arm strength, size, Combine numbers) that the mainstream tends to overrate and that "bust" more often — which he argues is *why* Purdy draws persistent doubt rather than despite it. Flags a real organizational risk if San Francisco doesn't win the Super Bowl: the front office has a history of being "skittish" at quarterback (cites moving on from Trey Lance despite heavy draft capital) and speculates renewed Purdy trade/replacement rumors could resurface, though he still expects the team to stay the course given how cheap the 262nd-overall investment was.
- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]), on the Super Bowl LVIII loss: a continuation, not a softening, of the January 23 "good, not elite" grade. Says there "weren't any wow throws" and no "home run" plays from an offense that usually produces them, and pushes directly on the reputation his box score creates — "Purdy's accuracy almost weirdly gets overstated at times... because of his completion percentage, because of his yards per attempt — I wouldn't say he is an elite ball placer." Draws an explicit archetype comp to [[Jared Goff]] and [[Kirk Cousins]] (new page): players whose physical tools are close to fixed, whose growth has to come from a pre-snap/post-snap "catalog" of processing experience catching up over time — the same trajectory Harmon credits [[Patrick Mahomes]] with having already completed. Purdy specifically "doesn't have the gun" Goff has but "has more playmaking ability," and unlike Jimmy Garoppolo he stays composed under pressure rather than the play immediately breaking down — Harmon's explicit prerequisite for him to close the gap. See [[Quarterback Processing and Confidence]].
- 2024-04-23 — According to guest QB trainer Will Hewlett, speaking with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-23]]): Purdy is still re-optimizing his throw and body this offseason after an injury-shortened, essentially nonstop two-year stretch since his final Iowa State game (including a playoff UCL injury), and his blend of elite game management with clutch, game-changing improvisation stems from the calmness he built playing through so many high-leverage college moments; Waldman separately called Purdy's ball placement and leverage-reading in the Bengals game one of the best QB performances he saw all season.
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Purdy is recommended as a cheap 'pocket passer' pairing (with Tua Tagovailoa) late in redraft against CJ Stroud -- both play in dynamic offenses with multiple strong receivers and better proven run games, checking the same boxes that make Stroud attractive at a fraction of the ADP cost. Waldman flagged the situational risk to monitor: 49ers offensive-line health and receiver depth ([[Brandon Aiyuk]] trade rumors, [[Deebo Samuel]] injury risk) -- if that group is depleted and [[Ricky Pearsall]] becomes the lead target-earner, it's a red flag for Purdy's ceiling even though it might be good news for Pearsall individually.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): asked if Purdy is still underrated, Waldman answered 'fuck yeah.' His explanation for why people dislike Purdy is that he lacks the blunt markers — no big arm, no high-end mobility — while being a good scrambler with great pocket feel and high-end subtleties that most viewers cannot see because catching them takes four or five rewatches and six hours on a game instead of three. He compared it to a palate: the audience wants high sugar and high salt, the sideline throw across the field or Kyler Murray running around making eight defenders miss, and does not care what happens after. He also noted Purdy is not afraid to fit the ball downfield into [[Jauan Jennings]] in contested spots.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): the Arizona loss should not be pinned on Purdy — two interceptions came on tipped passes that were good defensive plays, and a [[Jordan Mason]] red-zone fumble was the actual difference. Waldman points to his own 20-minute RSP Film Room progress report on Purdy showing the sophistication of his play, how good he is as a vertical thrower down the field and how well he operates in the pocket. He may never make the huge off-script gains a [[Kyler Murray]] does, but he buys time well, finds receivers downfield and picks up necessary yardage. Waldman also frames [[Joe Burrow]] as the top of the spectrum Purdy is on and 'may never quite get there'. He still calls San Francisco the strongest team and the likely NFC West winner.
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]): Purdy is underrated by at least some prominent film analysts — Waldman names Benjamin Solak (not a tracked expert), who used the 76-yard [[Deebo Samuel]] touchdown against Seattle, where the ball hung and a safety nearly cut it off, as evidence Purdy got bailed out. Waldman's counter is leverage: many x's-and-o's analysts read coverage type and defender location but not defender body position. Purdy manipulated the safety with his drop and a flip of his hips to sell a route breaking over the middle, then, avoiding pressure and throwing off his back foot, knew the deep defender was effectively three steps behind and could afford the hang time. Waldman credits his work in and outside of structure, layering throws into tight windows and high-level anticipation; he is a better athlete than people think and simply lacks a big arm. He concedes [[Trevor Lawrence]] could plausibly be better in that offense, but only with proof he can make Purdy's reads. Fantasy context from Bob Harris: QB7 at 17.6 points per game, near his QB6 finish last year.
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Purdy was 'disastrous' against man coverage versus Kansas City — 5-of-14 for 80 yards with three interceptions per Next Gen Stats, and 41 of those 80 yards came on a single deep ball to [[George Kittle]]. Harmon attributes much of it to personnel rather than the quarterback: [[Brandon Aiyuk]] was the designated man-beater and was never at 100% before the knee injury, and no other 49ers receiver consistently wins there. Compounding it, San Francisco has faced more man coverage on third down than any team this season. James Koh (not tracked) notes Purdy and [[Patrick Mahomes]] combined for zero passing touchdowns and five interceptions in the same game.
- 2025-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-11]]): Purdy attempted in-breaking throws to [[Brandon Aiyuk]] that Jimmy Garoppolo would never have dared, despite Harmon believing the two would throw a ball about the same distance in a street. Guest Cory Kinnan (not a tracked expert) attributes the gap to processing rather than arm talent, citing Purdy's own admission that he spot-throws about half his attempts because he cannot see where he is throwing — he knows how coverages rotate, where the hole will be, and drives the ball in on anticipation. Kinnan uses the same logic to explain why deep-ball accuracy is more anticipation and receiver-tracking than raw arm strength, and wonders whether Will Howard's playoff surge was a flash of the same understanding.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]): Purdy is worth a $50M-a-year deal. Waldman ran the current $50M club — [[Dak Prescott]], [[Joe Burrow]], [[Jordan Love]], [[Trevor Lawrence]], [[Tua Tagovailoa]], [[Jared Goff]], [[Justin Herbert]], [[Lamar Jackson]], [[Jalen Hurts]] — and noted only five of them outproduced Purdy (QB13) in 2024, and only four in 2023 when he was a mid-level QB1. Of the list he would rather have Hurts and Jackson, would debate Herbert (Jim Harbaugh's run-heavy offense suppressed his yardage) and Love, and dismissed Lawrence as unproven. His broader argument is that if you pass on a quarterback of this level you are betting on the draft, and roughly 62% of first-round quarterback picks fail, costing another three years — even for the fast-moving Shanahans. He thinks the deal gets done, and that Purdy's doubters cannot separate the quarterback from the strong team around him or get past the Mr. Irrelevant framing. He expects the $50M club to become a $75M club before long.
- 2025-05-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-19]]): after Purdy's five-year, $265M extension ($181M total guarantees, $165M in the first three years, ~$53M/yr, in line with [[Trevor Lawrence]]), the market still underrates him because his physical traits lose every head-to-head comparison — [[Bo Nix]] is faster, [[Sam Darnold]] has a stronger arm, [[Jayden Daniels]] has both, [[Jalen Hurts]], [[Josh Allen]] and [[Lamar Jackson]] are bigger and faster, and [[Joe Burrow]] may be his pocket equal with better weapons. Waldman argues anticipation, pass placement and processing speed are subtler on tape, so drafters default to arm and legs. Purdy was QB13 in 15 games without a healthy [[Brandon Aiyuk]] or [[Christian McCaffrey]] and QB6 in 2023 with a full complement; per-game he was roughly QB10. Waldman would take him anywhere after the top seven — QB6 to QB12 is at or below his value. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) noted San Francisco is 27-15 in his starts with third-most passing yards (9,452) and the NFL's best yards per attempt (8.9) since Week 13 of 2022.
- 2025-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman offered Purdy as the antidote to paying up for a premium pocket passer, saying people may throw up at it but 'you could probably look at Brock Purdy and say he's discount [[Joe Burrow]]' — mobile in the pocket in the way Burrow is, buying time and creating rather than gaining fantasy yardage as a runner. He noted Purdy finished QB6 two years ago and argues that if he had not lost half his offensive skill players, including [[Christian McCaffrey]] and [[Brandon Aiyuk]], he would likely have been a top-12 quarterback again — yet he is being drafted well outside that. Guest JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) framed the same principle as taking two mid-range QB2 pocket passers late instead of one expensive high-end QB1.
- 2025-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]): Purdy is the reason Harmon rates the 49ers only a 6 out of 10 for offensive fragility, below his 7.5 for the Rams — Purdy played in literal chaos last season and the offense still ranked top ten by most efficiency metrics. Harmon says people got take-locked on Purdy and missed real development, and that unlike [[Matthew Stafford]] he can move, scramble positively and create off-script, which staves off the pressure problems Harmon fears in Los Angeles; he concedes Stafford can make throws Purdy never could, first pick versus last pick. James Koh (not tracked) agrees Purdy developed, particularly as a pre-snap adjuster getting his guys in position, but disagrees on risk: he puts the 49ers at nine out of ten for fragility, higher than the Rams, and warns that Purdy got away with a lot of interceptable throws last year and that variance may bite in 2025.
- 2025-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris's number two offense he may be wrong about is San Francisco — what if the true 49ers showed up last season and nobody wants to hear it, with the defense gutted, [[Brandon Aiyuk]] gone and [[Christian McCaffrey]] 'held together by crepe paper,' rather than Kyle Shanahan reliably producing a functional offense. Guest Sigmund Bloom (Footballguys, not tracked) says Purdy is the referendum: is he the driver of a good offense or the passenger in one? Bloom does not expect a full collapse but sees critical points of failure — [[Jauan Jennings]]'s calf aggravation, Trent Williams's injury history, [[George Kittle]] playing with no regard for his health — that could stop San Francisco from being the fantasy-overachiever factory the market prices in.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): Harris took the over on 23.5 passing touchdowns even while calling San Francisco's 10.5-win total too high, saying Purdy is an accurate thrower who throws with great anticipation and that as a Patriots fan he would take the 49ers' weapons without [[Brandon Aiyuk]] over what ten other teams have; he calls the 'is Purdy real' discourse exhausting. Guest Chris Raybon (Action Network, not tracked) also went over, projecting just under 25: the injured 49ers are mostly players who do not matter, with [[Christian McCaffrey]] and [[George Kittle]] healthy and [[Jauan Jennings]] a contract issue rather than an injury; Purdy has a 6% career touchdown rate but was at 4.4% last year on 455 throws, so Raybon splits the difference and expects more attempts.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Purdy threw two stinkers and Harris did not love the performance, though he still delivered the throw to [[Ricky Pearsall]] that basically won the game and finished with two touchdowns plus rushing production, so the fantasy line held. Harris frames the interception as triple coverage and a good defensive play, and says the concern is what you see when Purdy is not on given the years the 49ers have committed to him. Guest **Sam Gold** (not tracked) is harsher: both picks were bad, one completely missing an underneath linebacker and the other forced into triple coverage with a fourth defender nearby. Gold's theory is that Purdy was pressing to push it down the field because he did not want to be the Alex Smith check-down guy, then got stuck in that mentality when he should have taken what Seattle's solid defense and good corners gave him under Mike Macdonald's scheme.
- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-11]]): Purdy is dealing with a non-throwing shoulder plus a toe injury, and Harmon says the toe is the worrying one because Purdy is an underrated scrambler who resets the pocket to access areas outside the numbers that previous Shanahan quarterbacks could not — he compares him to Tony Romo in style. Koh noted Purdy was floating dangerous balls to the sideline in Week 1 and tied it to not being able to drive off the toe for a quarterback who never had a big arm. Both praised his anticipation and timing as his superpower, contrasting him with [[Josh Allen]] who can simply rip through zone. Harmon suggested it might behoove San Francisco to sit him a week, beat the Saints with Mac Jones, and have him healthy from Week 3 on. The team was reportedly signing another quarterback, which Koh read as smoke meaning fire.
- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Purdy is almost certainly out Sunday at New Orleans with turf toe, and the 49ers leaked to a media contact that he will miss two to five weeks. Harris reads that wide range as a sign that even when Purdy returns he will be playing through a painful injury. He notes Purdy is only 25 and just signed his large extension, so this is bad injury luck rather than vindication of any preseason skepticism about San Francisco as a double-digit-win team. Mac Jones takes over, and Harris is dismissive: he calls Jones's Patriots arm a pop gun and says Jones would at least get votes in a hall of fame for the most rattled a quarterback has ever looked. Nobody is talking about shutting Purdy down, so he expects him back for the second half.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): if you were thinking of Purdy as a QB1, Harris does not think you should think that way right now. His notes from the Jaguars loss are littered with passes thrown too high — the joke that Purdy writes checks his arm cannot cash usually survives on accuracy, and here it did not, whether from rust or otherwise. He also had drops from [[Christian McCaffrey]] and [[Kendrick Bourne]] and a fumble in his own territory with three minutes left that lost the game. Guest Jamie Eisenberg (CBS, not a tracked expert) agrees and adds the environmental frame: Trent Williams is getting beaten repeatedly, and Purdy is a quarterback who needs the environment to be perfect — when it is not, you see the flaws that made him Mr. Irrelevant. Eisenberg notes [[George Kittle]] is IR-eligible in Week 5 and that a healthy McCaffrey, Kittle and receiver room would help, though [[Ricky Pearsall]] is banged up. Jacksonville's defense has 13 takeaways already after seven all last season.
