# Brock Purdy

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): defends Purdy against the consensus "system quarterback" reading. Asked to rank Dak Prescott, [[Lamar Jackson]], [[C.J. Stroud]] and Purdy by scheme dependence, a Footballguys roundtable unanimously named Purdy; Waldman named Jackson instead. His case for Purdy: he may well be worse off-structure than Prescott or Jackson and has made mistakes there, but he is "very good at processing in structure against tight coverage, making really quick reads that are very difficult to do" — and that skill "fits in any system," which is precisely what a system-*dependent* player's skills do not do.
- 2023-12-21 — **Waldman**, same episode, the supporting comparison: Jimmy Garoppolo played six years in the same San Francisco offense and, while he posted some gaudy yards-per-attempt numbers, "they weren't anywhere near the 9.88 that Purdy was doing." Same scheme, same play-caller, different output — Waldman's argument that the delta belongs to the quarterback.
- 2024-01-01 — Both hosts on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]] [Redraft (Standard)]: asked to pick a mid-round 2024 quarterback between Purdy and [[C.J. Stroud]] (points-per-game gap roughly half a point, ~18.5 each), both lean slightly to Purdy. Waldman's reason is San Francisco's supporting cast, with Kyle Shanahan "still sitting there pulling the strings"; Bob Harris's (not tracked) reason is track record — "he seems to overcome it the next week" after a bad game, a known quantity with more games on tape than Stroud.
- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]) [Dynasty]: a durable, checkable forecast. Compares Purdy's career shape to the early careers of Tom Brady, Kurt Warner, Russell Wilson, and Ben Roethlisberger — quarterbacks who were hyper-efficient on low offensive volume in a favorable situation, then became more heavily relied upon (and just as good or better) once they signed second contracts and the team could no longer surround them with as many other paid stars. His explicit prediction: as San Francisco leans on Purdy more over time, "I don't expect him to be less special than he is now." Points to the 2023 Bengals game (widely cited as Purdy's worst) as the one where his under-pressure processing actually stood out most on film.
- 2024-01-04 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode: floats the counterfactual "Trading Places" thought experiment (via Dan Orlovsky) of swapping Purdy for a game-manager-graded prospect like Mac Jones into a poor situation — his point being that a good organization, scheme, and supporting cast measurably accelerates a decent prospect's development, and a bad one just as measurably stalls it. Not a knock on Purdy specifically, but a caution about how much of his early success to attribute to the player versus the situation. See [[Scheme vs Talent]] and [[Player Development and Coachability]].
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: rejects the "exposed as a game manager" framing outright after the Divisional Round win over Green Bay — "Brock Purdy was exposed as nothing other than what he is: a really good quarterback capable of handling the scheme and trusted by his coach to get the job done." Flags early-offseason expert consensus rankings (ECR) placing Purdy outside the top-12 quarterbacks for 2024 as "a mistake," and says he hopes the market keeps discounting him so he can draft him again at value.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): a direct disagreement with Waldman's read of the same game — Harmon's explicit, repeated verdict is that Purdy "had a bad game" in the Divisional Round win over Green Bay, pushing back hard against what he calls "Purdy derangement syndrome" among 49ers fans reacting to the take. Specific mechanism: visibly struggled to grip and throw the ball in wet weather (re-gripping mid-throw on replay), missing Brandon Aiyuk repeatedly on plays where he was open, before playing well on the game-winning final drive. Gives him credit for a "playmaker gene" — second-reaction ability and scrambling to reset a throw — that Harmon rates above [[Tua Tagovailoa]]'s, while noting he lacks off-platform arm-angle versatility. Season-long, explicit grade: "a good quarterback," "certainly deserves to be a starting quarterback," but not top-12/15 — a "top 20" quarterback rather than elite tier. Frames [[Jared Goff]]'s own late-career mental development as a reason not to treat Purdy as a finished product this early in his starting career.
- 2024-01-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-25]]): heading into the NFC Championship at home against Detroit — expected clear weather removes the grip/accuracy issue flagged two days earlier — repeats that [[Brandon Aiyuk]] "probably had more of those throws available to him" against Green Bay than Purdy actually took, a continuation rather than a softening of the prior game's criticism. Both hosts favor San Francisco to win comfortably regardless.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]), after the NFC Championship win over Detroit: a defense of Purdy against the online "hatred" Waldman says he watched in real time — "he got a little of that dog in him," specifically praising a fourth-quarter run where he lowered his head and made a play when it mattered. His central argument, though, is about supporting cast rather than a solo grade: whatever complaints exist about Purdy are "remedied" by [[Christian McCaffrey]] (130+ yards from scrimmage against a tough run defense regardless of scheme), [[Deebo Samuel]], [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and George Kittle around him, plus a strong defense. Explicitly frames Purdy's skills as "subtle" rather than the traditionally-hyped quarterback traits (arm strength, size, Combine numbers) that the mainstream tends to overrate and that "bust" more often — which he argues is *why* Purdy draws persistent doubt rather than despite it. Flags a real organizational risk if San Francisco doesn't win the Super Bowl: the front office has a history of being "skittish" at quarterback (cites moving on from Trey Lance despite heavy draft capital) and speculates renewed Purdy trade/replacement rumors could resurface, though he still expects the team to stay the course given how cheap the 262nd-overall investment was.
- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]), on the Super Bowl LVIII loss: a continuation, not a softening, of the January 23 "good, not elite" grade. Says there "weren't any wow throws" and no "home run" plays from an offense that usually produces them, and pushes directly on the reputation his box score creates — "Purdy's accuracy almost weirdly gets overstated at times... because of his completion percentage, because of his yards per attempt — I wouldn't say he is an elite ball placer." Draws an explicit archetype comp to [[Jared Goff]] and [[Kirk Cousins]] (new page): players whose physical tools are close to fixed, whose growth has to come from a pre-snap/post-snap "catalog" of processing experience catching up over time — the same trajectory Harmon credits [[Patrick Mahomes]] with having already completed. Purdy specifically "doesn't have the gun" Goff has but "has more playmaking ability," and unlike Jimmy Garoppolo he stays composed under pressure rather than the play immediately breaking down — Harmon's explicit prerequisite for him to close the gap. See [[Quarterback Processing and Confidence]].
- 2024-04-23 — According to guest QB trainer Will Hewlett, speaking with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-23]]): Purdy is still re-optimizing his throw and body this offseason after an injury-shortened, essentially nonstop two-year stretch since his final Iowa State game (including a playoff UCL injury), and his blend of elite game management with clutch, game-changing improvisation stems from the calmness he built playing through so many high-leverage college moments; Waldman separately called Purdy's ball placement and leverage-reading in the Bengals game one of the best QB performances he saw all season.
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Purdy is recommended as a cheap 'pocket passer' pairing (with Tua Tagovailoa) late in redraft against CJ Stroud -- both play in dynamic offenses with multiple strong receivers and better proven run games, checking the same boxes that make Stroud attractive at a fraction of the ADP cost. Waldman flagged the situational risk to monitor: 49ers offensive-line health and receiver depth ([[Brandon Aiyuk]] trade rumors, [[Deebo Samuel]] injury risk) -- if that group is depleted and [[Ricky Pearsall]] becomes the lead target-earner, it's a red flag for Purdy's ceiling even though it might be good news for Pearsall individually.
