# Kyle McCord

## Expert Takes

- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is emphatically out. Charting the NC State game he counted about five interceptable throws; before watching the Pitt game he and a friend set an over/under of seven interceptable passes and McCord threw nine, and other games were not much better. Waldman's criticism is decision making rather than box score noise — McCord doubles down on bad reads and is 'awful in the red zone and in the black zone', which Waldman illustrates with an Anakin Skywalker high-ground analogy ending in a pit of lava. He calls this McCord's fatal flaw and says he does not think it gets better.
- 2025-02-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-27]]): [[Kyle McCord]] is interesting but the charting is damning. Waldman charts at least five games per prospect; in the NC State game he logged about five bad throws where the decision and the read were wrong for the down, distance, field position and game script, several of them interceptable. Before watching the Pitt game — a four-interception disaster — he told a friend the over-under on bad throws was closer to seven or nine, and McCord charted nine. He describes McCord three times in the red zone throwing into a corner playing seven yards off with inside shade and the high ground on him. [[Brandon Angelo]] frames it as 'arm arrogance' in a high-octane Syracuse offense against weaker competition, where the better answer was an easy five-to-eight-yard option route; McCord would try it twice in a drive and throw it again after a pick six. Both name him as a prototype the 'robo quarterback' half of the league still likes.
- 2025-03-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-31]]) [Dynasty]: McCord sits at the class's line of demarcation between future starter and top-end backup, but with a flaw that could force him out of the league entirely — he doubles down on bad reads. He repeatedly tries to fit the ball into leverage that favors the defender squatting over the receiver, and Waldman's framing is that unlike most arm-talented prospects who avoid making the same mistake twice, McCord makes it three or four times in a game, telling [[Will Levis]] and [[Desmond Ridder]] to hold his beer. Waldman likens the belief to [[Jameis Winston]]'s conviction that the ball can vanish into a defender's chest and rematerialize in the receiver's hands. He also leads receivers into unnecessary contact. The win-lose of his game comes down to whether he can learn to read and respect defender leverage — Waldman is not sure whether he cannot see it, does not know how, or willfully ignores it. Waldman contrasts him with [[Max Brosmer]]: McCord may have more starter upside but a much lower floor, while Brosmer's ceiling is lower and his downside shallower.
