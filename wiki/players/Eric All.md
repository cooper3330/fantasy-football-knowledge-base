# Eric All

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-30]] [Dynasty]: an Iowa transfer who played H-back at Michigan and in-line at Iowa. Quick feet, a variety of footwork and hand counters at the line and top of stem, strong mitts and contested-catch ability; a mature, patient runner after the catch who knows when to lower his pads and split defenders versus create in the open field. Waldman's caveat is entirely medical: an ACL tear he's rehabbing plus a back surgery he and the Michigan staff reportedly disagreed on (All called it "life-saving/life-altering"; Waldman is skeptical the "minimally invasive" description holds, likening it to calling brain surgery minimally invasive) is dropping his draft stock. Waldman's framing: "low risk, high reward value if the medicals are okay."
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo groups All with [[Cade Stover]] as tight ends he views as sneaky value in tight-end-premium rookie drafts — low-cost shots he's fine missing on but that carry real upside if they hit.
- 2024-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-20]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said he's "super excited" about fourth-round rookie Eric All and made small aggressive trade-ups just to secure his rights, calling Iowa tight ends a generally good bet despite the injury All is coming off. With [[Tee Higgins]] possibly departing Cincinnati behind [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and Chase Brown, Waldman sees a path to real Bengals TE relevance and ranks All above [[Tanner McLaughlin]] and Mike Gesicki in that competition, hoping for a Trey McBride-style late breakout rather than an immediate Sam LaPorta-type impact.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]) [Dynasty]: feeling All long term for dynasty, but not for 2024 — Cincinnati already has enough at the position to do everything, so he would need injuries plus his own beating of expectations to matter this year. All could sneak in if he proves a quick learner, and the Bengals did like him enough to spend an early pick. Waldman frames tight end as probably the second-hardest offensive position to develop at the rate required to be an all-around player, but notes All came from Iowa, whose pro-style offense tight ends handle well, and points to the precedent of a Bengals tight end with a lesser all-around game who still produced.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): All caught all four of his targets for 32 yards, and Waldman thinks the dynasty buy window is already closed in genuinely competitive leagues — you will not get him at a reasonable price there — but in softer leagues you may still have this week or next to get in, and he would. The case is not the box score, which was unspectacular, but that he was consistently good and that Cincinnati is using more two-tight-end sets with him, partly a function of the wide receiver situation. He leans on [[Joe Burrow]]'s public endorsement — that All is already good and keeps getting better every week, with a bright future — as strong corroboration: 'when your quarterback says that, you've got to feel pretty good about it.'
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]) [Dynasty]: All's workload is climbing — heavy two-tight-end usage and now appearing as the lone tight end in three-receiver sets — and Waldman would be buying him as a throw-in in dynasty trades. He is a weekly snap-count monitor because any Mike Gesicki setback opens the job. What excites Waldman is the blocking: Cincinnati uses All as a fullback in I-formation sets and he is 'just taking people out', which is what keeps a tight end on the field. His deep game has not been unlocked yet, but with a full season back from the ACL Waldman expects the intermediate and vertical route tree to open up by next year, since All can go up and win a football. He will not commit to [[George Kittle]] but sees All landing on that all-around spectrum within a few years.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10]]) [Dynasty]: one of two young tight ends he thinks should already be starting — he cannot understand how Zac Taylor looks himself in the mirror at 1-4 and keeps starting Mike Gesicki, who at this point is a fourth receiver running sail routes. All has been spectacular as both a blocker and a receiver, including the lead blocking against Baltimore, and Angelo saw the glimpses at Iowa; without the college injuries he thinks All would have been an early second-round pick. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees and cannot get him on any roster because he is rostered everywhere, and adds that Cincinnati has not featured him downfield yet. Angelo's headline claim: All is effectively the third receiver in that offense behind [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Tee Higgins]] — more consistent as a tertiary option than [[Andrei Iosivas]] — and playing him 75%-plus of snaps would be a massive upgrade, particularly as a red-zone threat.
- 2026-01-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-19]]) [Dynasty]: [[Eric All]] is talented but has been unable to stay healthy, and Waldman effectively says he's giving up on him outside of the deepest (40-plus-man) leagues, where you might stash him through the summer hoping for a health turnaround. He grouped All with Ja'Tavion Sanders as talented-but-fragile tight ends where availability, not ability, is the deciding factor.
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-20]]) [Dynasty]: All is shining in camp (and reportedly fell off a truck in Greece in the offseason and was fine) but Angelo pegs him as at best the third-or-fourth read in a healthy Bengals passing game behind Chase Brown and the top two WRs, ahead of Andrei Iosivas or Colby Young for that role -- calling him redraft-only a streaming waiver-wire add. In dynasty, both like the profile: near-zero acquisition cost, a tier-one offensive infrastructure that scores a ton of points, and talent Angelo rates equal to or above Brenton Strange, whom he places at the tail end of the viable TE1 tier (top 15-17). Waldman frames it as an easy end-of-bench stash in 1.5-PPR TE-premium leagues with 40+ roster spots -- a lottery ticket where even a modest hit ('winning 10 bucks') is worth the free cost.
