# Jeremiah Love

- 2025-12-19 — Untracked co-host Josh (Cousin Josh) named Love his number five bust for 2026 on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-19]]), admitting he knows little about him beyond that he plays running back at Notre Dame and is expected to go in the first round of the NFL Draft. His warning is purely about draft-capital-driven fantasy hype: don't draft an unproven rookie running back in the first round of startup or rookie drafts on landing-spot speculation alone, the same logic he applied a year earlier to [[Ashton Jeanty]].
- 2026-02-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-09]]) [Dynasty]: asked if Love is a better prospect than [[Ashton Jeanty]], Waldman says no -- he still rates Jeanty higher as a player -- but expects Love to be the better fantasy option because Jeanty is stuck in Las Vegas 'still trying to figure it out,' while Love projects to a stronger offensive line and offense. Waldman comps Love to a hybrid of Miami-era Reggie Bush and Tony Pollard: a between-the-tackles runner who can also author breakaway runs and catch out of the backfield without shying from contact. He frames the Love/Jeanty dynamic as an Adrian Peterson/Marshawn Lynch situation with different styles -- Lynch (Jeanty) the better all-around player, but Peterson (Love) landing in a better spot to post better stats. Both are projected instant starters; Love could go as high as a top-10 pick.
- 2026-03-17 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-17]]) [Dynasty]: Koh strongly opposes the Giants speculatively taking [[Jeremiah Love]] fifth overall, arguing they already have 'totally viable' backs in [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] and a recovering [[Cam Skattebo]], and that a running back that high ignores far bigger roster needs -- calling it 'just so stupid' despite liking Love as a prospect. [[Matt Harmon]] counters that the Giants' offseason -- signing [[Darnell Mooney]], [[Isaiah Likely]], and Calvin Austin III while keeping [[Theo Johnson]] -- reads as clear evidence New York is already planning to spend the pick on Love regardless of the running back room's depth.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Love is the only true blue-chip prospect in the 2026 rookie class, in a tier of his own at running back with a talent ceiling Angelo compared to Jamal Charles and a playing style Waldman compared to Alvin Kamara. Angelo put him in the same range as recent RB1 prospects like Bijan Robinson and Jahmyr Gibbs for talent. Waldman said that after Love, the rest of the class is 'all fit-based' -- no other back is a clear Day 1 starter, so evaluation shifts to depth-chart opportunity rather than talent ceiling. In dual mock rookie-draft trios built around picks 1.01/1.03/1.05 in superflex PPR, both hosts had Love as the unanimous first selection regardless of team need.
- 2026-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-01]]) [Dynasty]: In his A-Z breakdown of the 2026 RB class, Waldman called Love 'the one safe bet in terms of talent, draft capital, and opportunity' among this year's group and the class's lone first-round running back. Love wins against contact repeatedly -- often absorbing multiple points of contact on the same touch -- because he attacks collisions rather than avoiding them, a trait Waldman said most backs no longer show well. Waldman said fantasy managers who are one or two runners away from contention should target Love early, but for everyone else this is a weak class to invest premium picks in outside of him; better strategy is prioritizing receivers/quarterbacks early and trading for established backs later, or dealing current rookie picks for more and earlier 2027-2028 picks.
- 2026-04-09 — According to Paul Pertichese, guest analyst on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-09]] (not a tracked expert) [Dynasty]: Jeremiah Love is the clear top running back prospect in the 2026 class, a 'massive gap' ahead of everyone else, and if position value were removed he would be the best player on the entire board given a weak QB/OL/EDGE/WR crop this year. Pertichese comps him as a blend of Todd Gurley (size, initial speed burst, vision, power) and Bijan Robinson (receiving ability), and rates him well above last year's Ashton Jeanty, who Pertichese had as a fringe first-round guy, 'a tier below' Love. At around 220 lbs (vs. Saquon Barkley's 233), Love's only real non-injury risk is trying to do too much behind a bad offensive line -- if he lacks patience and bounces runs outside instead of taking the two or three yards on offer, he could be hit-or-miss as a pro, especially if he lands with Arizona, Tennessee or the Giants given their O-line questions. Host [[Chris Harris]] flagged Love as a lock top-of-draft dynasty rookie pick, calling him 'clearly the one' over WR [[Carnell Tate]] and QB [[Fernando Mendoza]] given the shallow rest of the class.
- 2026-04-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-27]]) [Dynasty]: contrary to the fantasy industry's consensus panning Arizona as Love's landing spot, Waldman feels it's actually a good one. He notes the Cardinals have a better offensive line than the other teams reportedly in play (Giants, Commanders, Titans), a quarterback in [[Kyler Murray]] who distributes to multiple weapons rather than funneling to one, and skill talent around him in [[Michael Wilson]], [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] and [[Trey McBride]]. He argues [[Trey Benson]]'s lingering health issues (reportedly still limping in November) and/or [[James Conner]]'s uncertain long-term tenure open a real path, and that [[Tyler Allgeier]]'s prior 'downhill toss/screen back' role alongside [[Bijan Robinson]] shows a complementary back can still hit value -- Allgeier was out-snapped 860-328 by Robinson yet still scored a career-high 8 touchdowns. With [[Greg Dortch]] gone, Waldman compares Love to an Alvin Kamara-type weapon who can line up in the backfield or the slot, citing Mel Kiper Jr.'s point that new OC Klint Kubiak (unlike former OC Matt LaFleur, who mostly kept him at RB) will move Love around the formation. Waldman projects a real chance at 300 touches, contrasting this favorably with Ashton Jeanty's Seattle landing, where Pete Carroll simply didn't throw him the ball much.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris ranks rookie [[Jeremiah Love]] RB19 for redraft and calls it 'insane' that some rankings have him going as high as RB10, warning that a nightmare landing spot in Arizona -- behind an entrenched [[James Conner]] and [[Tyler Allgeier]], neither of whom Harris expects to lose touchdown work even if [[Trey Benson]] is traded or cut -- caps his redraft ceiling. Harris called it a repeat of last year's [[Ashton Jeanty]] rookie-hype warning: it would be crazy to draft Love inside the top 15 overall this summer. [Dynasty]: Harris and co-host Josh (untracked) both expect Love to go #1 overall in their La Liga dynasty rookie draft, calling him clearly the best long-term asset of this year's rookie class even while being unusable early in redraft.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-07]]) [Dynasty]: charted as an 'A-plus prospect,' though not a perfect one. Love posted just a 40% success rate on inside zone at Notre Dame, but that carried little weight since only 4.9% of his carries came there -- Notre Dame ran almost exclusively shotgun and was nearly 70% man gap, 30% zone, and Harmon graded him clearly better as a downhill, between-the-tackles runner in man gap/power schemes than on zone concepts. His outside-run tape gave Harmon pause: Love gets to the edge 'too fast,' not letting blockers set up cutback lanes, which read as him possibly being 'too athletic for his own good.' Charting numbers: 26% broken-tackle rate (just under Harmon's 28% elite benchmark) and a strong 20% explosive play rate. Harmon comps him to prospect-era Saquon Barkley -- a boom/bust 'home run hitter' early in his career rather than a steady producer. Faced loaded boxes on 50% of his runs, the highest rate Harmon has ever charted, yet stayed productive, and Harmon notes he saw tougher defenses (unlike Jadarian Price) so his numbers carry less 'grade inflation.' Lands with new OC Mike LaFleur in Arizona -- Harmon likes the fit better than Ashton Jeanty's Raiders landing spot last year and expects a gap-scheme-heavy offense given LaFleur's Rams background, though the offensive line (still thin at right tackle) and QB situation are unresolved, and Harmon expects Love to lose some short-yardage/goal-line touches to a teammate as part of a normal rookie-RB role split. On the Love-vs-Jeanty prospect debate, co-host James Koh (not tracked) leans Love for the higher ceiling/explosiveness, while Harmon leans Jeanty as the more complete overall back (see [[Ashton Jeanty]] entry).
- 2026-05-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-25]]) [Dynasty]: asked about former GM Scot McCloughan's suggestion that [[Jadarian Price]] could surpass Jeremiah Love, Waldman said he'd 'still go Love' right now because his footwork and vision are ahead of Price's at this stage, even though Price may have an edge in decision-making and pass protection. Waldman said the gap between the two backs is closer than most people think, and that both had opportunities at Notre Dame to show the traits (reading pre-snap looks, adjusting in the moment) that ultimately separate backs within the same tier.
- 2026-07-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Ben Gretsch (Stealing Signals newsletter, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris currently has Love at RB17, which he says is high enough that he mostly won't end up rostering him, but not so low relative to his usual rookie stance. He referenced his own Almanac blurb line that 'it only took two words -- Arizona Cardinals -- to prevent Jeremiah Love from becoming the most hated man in fantasy in 2026,' capturing how the top-five draft-capital pedigree gets discounted by the perceived hopelessness of the offense; Harris's broader stance is that he wants to invest in offenses the market has declared hopeless because analysts are wrong about that all the time. Gretsch agreed there's talent around Love (Trey McBride, Michael Wilson, a healthy Marvin Harrison Jr. upside) but flagged that Arizona projects with the hardest schedule in the league for 2026 -- roughly 10-point underdogs across their first eight games by Vegas numbers -- which complicates the backfield's efficiency regardless of talent. Both said Love's ADP could realistically climb into the second round if he looks undeniable in camp scrimmages, though a crowded four-back committee makes that a tough ask.
- 2026-07-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-27]]) [Dynasty]: despite OC Nathaniel Hackett publicly saying it's still an open competition with [[James Conner]] and Tyler Allgeier ('he's definitely a rookie'), Waldman says he still believes it's Love's backfield, reading Hackett's comments as standard rookie-motivation talk rather than a real depth-chart signal. He notes 15-second practice clips (flagged by a reporter he thinks was Josh Weinfuss) showed Love displaying power and explosiveness. Waldman expects an early-camp/early-season split with touches for others, especially if Conner survives roster cuts and isn't traded, but expects Love's role to grow and eventually take over.
- 2026-08-03 — According to guest Dave Richard (CBS Fantasy, not a tracked expert) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-03]]): Richard's #2 story is the crowded Cardinals backfield around rookie Love (with Tyler Allgeier, Trey Benson and James Conner also in camp) - he doesn't expect real roster clarity, arguing Arizona has no incentive to simplify things given a brutal schedule, a weak offensive line, a first-year offense and three capable pass-catchers, with a win total he guesses around four and a half; he worries Love won't see more than five games with 18-plus touches. Harris pushes back on both hosts' tendency to overstate their ability to predict a team's outlook (citing last year's misreads on the Colts and Patriots), but agrees with Richard's conclusion, framing it as dueling market pressures: some will assume Arizona is bad (bearish on Love) while others assume a top-five pick must get heavy volume regardless (bullish on Love) - what Harris calls the 'Clyde Edwards-Helaire syndrome.' Harris ranks Love at RB16 as a potential low-end RB2, saying he's trying to divorce Love's outlook from his skepticism about Arizona's offense, though he remains worried the Cardinals' defense could force negative game scripts. A trade sending Conner or Benson elsewhere is flagged by both as the next real test of Love's ADP.
- 2026-08-03 — According to Ian Hartitz (untracked co-host) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-03 (Feel It or Fk It with Ian Hartitz)]]): he expects Love to be Arizona's clear touch/passing-down leader over Tyler Allgeier (who could still be the early-down starter weeks 1-2) given his number-three-overall draft capital, but is actively fading him as a top-three-round pick, comping him more to a 250-280 touch back than a 380-400 workhorse given Michael Floor/Nathaniel Hackett and Frank Reich (fresh off one of the worst Stanford offenses in college football) running one of the league's worst-projected offenses -- Vegas implied totals had Arizona near the bottom. He cited a study of 14 running backs posting RB1 seasons on bottom-10 scoring offenses in the last decade, nearly all needing 50-plus catches to do it. [[Matt Waldman]] projected a mid/low-Alvin Kamara-type stat line (roughly 600-800 rushing yards, 400-600-plus receiving, 8-10 total touchdowns) and agreed a second-round pick on Love right now is an 'ego pick' given the offensive line, quarterback and system instability.
- 2026-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Cousin Josh (untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-14]]): Harris gave Love a 6, Josh a 7, and Harris is openly recommending against drafting any rookie running back in the first three rounds this year, calling it more palatable than last year's [[Ashton Jeanty]] hype (which he says he pushed back on harder at the time). Both cite a poor supporting team as an added drag and a broader pattern-recognition skepticism of 'generational prospect' rookie running back and receiver hype after seeing it repeatedly not pay off early. Harris has Love at 33 overall on his personal board -- a full round behind the ADP slot.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-17]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said he would not feel comfortable drafting Love at his current ADP of pick 13, and that a high-ankle-sprain-type injury costing him at least a week should reasonably drop his price 4-5 rounds, though he doubts the market will actually move him that far. He noted [[Tyler Allgeier]] is the listed starter into camp specifically because the team trusts him as a pass protector, receiver, and processor of quick adjustments -- things they aren't yet sure Love can do -- and said if a shaky offense and the injury both eat into his runway, his touch share won't warrant a top-13 rookie price. Asked directly, Waldman said he'd rather have CeeDee Lamb, Derrick Henry, AJ Brown, Ladd McConkey, Colston Loveland, Carnell Tate, Parker Washington, or even Michael Wilson over Love at that cost, and would only start drafting him around the 6th-7th round given how he personally prices every player. He conceded Love flashed real pass-catching ability and looked the part in the exhibition, but said it's immaterial to his own approach since he simply won't draft Love where he's currently going.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Love suffered a confirmed high ankle sprain (reported in an exhibition game), is done for the rest of August into September, and is in a race to be ready for Week 1 -- Harris dropped him in his ranks as a result. Per [[Matt Waldman]] in the same episode [Dynasty]: Love is in the tier with [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and [[Bijan Robinson]], maybe at the bottom of it, and once healthy projects an Alvin Kamara/Mark Ingram-style split with free-agent signee Tyler Allgeier -- Allgeier in the early-down/pass-pro Ingram role, Love as the receiving-down Kamara, albeit at lower production than that Saints tandem's peak. Waldman expects Love's redraft price to fall and says he's rarely drafting at market for a rookie RB1 anyway.
