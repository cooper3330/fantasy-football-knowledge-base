# Calvin Ridley

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024, ahead of his free agency. Team/role are
> as of that date and are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-07]]): a 2023
  season Harmon calls widely misjudged as a fantasy bust (76/1,000+/8 TD on a
  career-low 55.9% catch rate, highly inconsistent week to week) but graded
  "green" — above league average — on every charted route except the curl,
  with strong success rates vs. man (71.9%, 69th percentile, down from a
  ~76-78% career norm) and vs. zone (80.4%, in line with career). The one real
  decline is vs. press coverage: 66.7%, down roughly 10 points from his
  Atlanta-years 75-79% range, which Harmon attributes partly to nearly two
  years away from football (a 2022 ACL tear/suspension layoff) rather than
  pure decline at age 29. Curl-route success also fell sharply (72% vs. an
  ~85% career norm), which Harmon calls a timing/chemistry issue with Trevor
  Lawrence as much as a talent issue.
  Both hosts fault Jacksonville's deployment on top of the individual
  drop-off: Ridley was lined up almost exclusively as a static X receiver
  (81.8% of snaps outside, 79% on the line) with a thin, low-difficulty route
  tree (48.6% comeback/curl/9-route, under 10% slants) and drew extra
  coverage attention (double-teamed on 13.6% of routes) — a much more
  one-dimensional usage than his more balanced 50/50 X/flanker Atlanta years.
  Harmon's read: still "a high-quality starting outside receiver" even after
  the down year, and better with more Z/flanker usage to unlock intermediate
  and slant routes; expects Jacksonville to re-sign him and separately wants
  the team to add a cheaper X-receiver complement (a rookie) so Ridley can be
  moved around rather than parked outside every snap. See [[Reception
  Perception Methodology]] and [[Scheme vs Talent]].

- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]): pushes back on a Carolina Panthers landing-spot prediction floated by co-host **Bob Harris** (not a tracked expert) — "fuck it, to be honest." Argues Ridley "needs to be somewhere where he's not the wide receiver one," better as a schemed WR2/3 who draws a top corner away or wins off misdirection than as an off-structure improviser, and that Carolina's [[Bryce Young]] will require a lot of off-structure improvisation from his receivers. Explicitly: "he's a good NFL wide receiver, but I don't think he's a primary wide receiver even though the billing was cast that way" — a more skeptical ceiling framing than the 03-07 charting verdict that called him a "high-quality starting outside receiver."

- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): reacts to
  the actual signing — a surprise 4-year, $92M deal with Tennessee, $50M
  fully guaranteed, putting his APY in the [[Deebo Samuel]]/[[Terry
  McLaurin]] range. Both hosts call it "way out of market" relative to his
  down 2023 season, but Harmon defends the underlying player using the same
  71.9%-vs.-man/80.4%-vs.-zone numbers from the 03-07 profile — "still a
  high-quality separator." Likes the fit: Tennessee can use him as a
  flanker/slot/motion piece rather than parking him as a static X the way
  Jacksonville did, especially with [[DeAndre Hopkins]] able to play X.
  Justifies the price given Tennessee's WR2 last year was Chris Moore (not a
  tracked page) with 424 yards, and given Levis-focused logic: "when you have
  a rookie quarterback contract... spend money with reckless abandon" to get
  a real read on [[Will Levis]]. Notes the deal may look cheap in hindsight
  once 2024's expected top-receiver extensions (Justin Jefferson, CeeDee
  Lamb, Tee Higgins, Ja'Marr Chase, Amon-Ra St. Brown, Brandon Aiyuk, Nico
  Collins) land. Also flags a 2024 Draft knock-on effect: Tennessee signing
  Ridley makes it unlikely they take a receiver at pick 7, "freeing up"
  [[Rome Odunze]] or [[Malik Nabers]] for another landing spot.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: calls the Tennessee contract "a joke" relative to the player — a quality starting NFL receiver, but not a true No. 1, more a "high-end [[Diontae Johnson]]" who needs to be couched among actual leaders of a receiving corps rather than being the corps leader himself. Doubts he plays into his mid-30s at a high level. Floats a theory connecting the deal to Tennessee's earlier decision to move on from [[A.J. Brown]] under a previous coordinator — reads the Ridley splurge as a "people-pleasing," "two steps forward, one step back" overcorrection typical of the organization. Separately agrees [[Treylon Burks]] is not yet a leader of that receiving corps, while granting [[DeAndre Hopkins]] already is one.
- 2024-06-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-20]]): Ridley's 2023 usage in Jacksonville under passing-game coordinator Press Taylor was very static/outside-only (go routes, comebacks, curls per his RP profile), and Ridley himself said upon signing with Tennessee that he was pitched on being deployed more diversely across alignments. Harmon thinks new OC Nick Holtz — who was Jacksonville's passing-game coordinator last year but reported directly to Taylor — likely wasn't the one pigeonholing Ridley, and expects Tennessee (with DeAndre Hopkins locked in as the true X, freeing Ridley to move around) to deliver on that promised versatility. Notes Ridley is being paid like a high-end WR2 even though his profile output has trended more toward that tier than true WR1.
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: Raised as context for [[Trevor Lawrence]]'s rough prior season, Waldman calls Ridley 'just not a primary receiver' -- he has the athletic ability but has lacked conceptual consistency at big moments since his time in Atlanta. He cites Jacksonville letting Ridley walk to Tennessee in free agency as evidence the Jaguars themselves didn't view him as a true WR1 worth matching Tennessee's contract offer for.
- 2024-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-11]]) [Dynasty]: Ridley signed a 4-year, $92M deal with Tennessee that both hosts called an overpay, but Harmon still views him as a 'high-end number two' -- his 2023 RP success rates (69th percentile vs. man, 65th vs. zone, 47th vs. press) were down from his pre-suspension peak, when he was trending toward 'superstar number one' territory. Harmon noted that in his explosive Week 1 with Buffalo, 48.6% of Ridley's charted routes were nine/comeback/curl against a weak Colts secondary and only 9.8% were slants, a lean he says shouldn't be repeated with Tennessee's less favorable matchups. He projects Ridley as a Z/flanker in Tennessee, a role Harmon thinks suits him better than the heavier X usage Jacksonville had him running. James Koh (untracked) called the signing a smart move for a franchise still repairing the fallout of the A.J. Brown trade.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon argued Ridley's route diet needs to change to be 'more maximized' in Tennessee -- he charted almost 50% of Ridley's routes last year (with Jacksonville) as go/curl/comeback routes, with slants on fewer than 10%, and said moving him off the X position and into more in-breaking work would help. That projection depends on a Titans offensive line guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn (not tracked) still called a below-average, rebuilding unit -- 2nd-youngest and 29th-most-experienced in the league -- even after drafting LT J.C. Latham 7th overall; Thorn expects the line to be merely 'better, maybe in the 20s' league-wide this year rather than a finished product, with real growth more likely in 2025-26 under OL coach Bill Callahan.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): Waldman thinks Tennessee paid for the wrong player. Ridley is a good receiver but his big Atlanta year is not who he is — Waldman cites catch-point issues, route issues, and coming up short in the big moments you rely on from a true WR1, adding that Jacksonville would have kept him if they saw him that way. He still projects roughly 140 targets, around 80 catches and close to 1,100 yards, but says the market treats him like a peak [[Stefon Diggs]] and buyers will get 'broken parts' instead: what you pay for is about the best you'll get. Guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) is slightly under market on all Titans pass catchers and calls the roster the 'discount Bengals' — cheap, shine-off acquisitions.
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with [[DeAndre Hopkins]] hurt, Harmon is now 'inching Ridley up' to be the more productive receiver in Tennessee, having previously thought Hopkins' verticality might make it neck and neck. He likes the [[Will Levis]] connection and calls Ridley a decent value pick going in a fair range rather than badly underpriced. The caveat is alignment: in Jacksonville in 2023 Ridley ran a go, comeback or curl on over 40% of his charted routes (nearly 19% curls, 13% digs), and Harmon wants him off the line running more posts, digs and out routes from the slot, where he was successful. If Hopkins misses time and Ridley is pushed back to X, Harmon warns they will 'make a good player worse.' Levis threw digs on only 5% of throws in 2023 but graded best on in-breakers and nine routes.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Harmon thinks the market may have under-discussed Ridley this year after talking about him constantly a season ago, and that he has a chance to have a pretty big year in Tennessee. The reasoning is scheme: as Cincinnati's OC, Brian Callahan's Bengals had the second-highest pass-to-run ratio and second-highest pass rate over expected in the NFL even with [[Jake Browning]] starting, while Tennessee had the third-fewest pass attempts last year. Harmon expects a molding of Callahan's spread philosophy with his father Bill Callahan's run-game and offensive-line background, meaning some condensed formations and physical football rather than pure finesse 11 personnel — but a far more pass-happy team than the Mike Vrabel era.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Tennessee is misusing Ridley as a pure deep threat — he is carrying a 23.4 average depth of target and 60 percent of the Titans' air yards, the top figure among all NFL pass catchers, despite Harmon's view that Ridley is a good receiver but not a Will Fuller-style vertical specialist. Harmon points to Ridley's 2023 charting where the out route and dig route were two of his strongest routes and argues Tennessee should be feeding him more intermediate work and base layups for [[Will Levis]]. He calls Ridley a volatile player at this stage of his career, mixing mind-numbing drops with spectacular catches such as the touchdown over Sauce Gardner, plus a likely second score dropped in Week 2. Ridley leads the team at 21.7 percent target share.
- 2024-10-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-15]]): Ridley has 26 targets for nine catches and 141 yards, a career-low 33% catch rate and a career-low 5.2 yards per target, on a 44% share of the Titans' air yards — eighth-highest in the NFL and, in Harmon's word, worthless volume, largely desperation fourth-quarter heaves. On Ridley's public complaint about needing targets earlier, Koh (not tracked) is irked, hearing it as 'unless I get the ball early I am not engaged' from a player who has himself disappointed since his layoff. Harmon partly defends him: he agrees no first-quarter target is maddening and reads Ridley's point as being about when the targets come, not how many. Both agree Ridley is not blameless — he will never be his pre-layoff self and he drops passes — but Harmon puts the bulk of it on [[Will Levis]], warning that receivers start rebelling on offenses like this.
- 2024-10-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-24]]): Ridley is 'a perfect example of like route running's not all that matters' — he has played so poorly in Tennessee that Harmon ranked the post-[[DeAndre Hopkins]]-trade Titans as possibly the worst pass-catching group in the NFL despite Ridley being the nominal number one. Harmon's specific complaints: he does not catch the ball when it is thrown to him, and he never makes a play in the open field, while complaining about targets. Asked to choose between Ridley and [[Courtland Sutton]], Harmon eventually took Ridley 'just because I do think Ridley at his best is better than Sutton' — while conceding we have not seen that best. He also argued the pre-trade Titans room with Hopkins was closer to league-average serviceable than the disaster it now is, and separately that Ridley in Jacksonville had been the 'sacrificial' X whose bad usage indirectly benefited [[Brian Thomas Jr.]].
- 2024-10-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-29]]): Ridley's 10-catch, 15-target, 143-yard game against Detroit came out of nowhere — Koh (not tracked) notes he had nine catches for 133 yards in the previous five games combined, and it happened in a 52-14 loss in which [[Jared Goff]] threw for 85 yards, so Harmon treats it as possible consolidation rather than proof. He calls Ridley a 'both things can be true' case: the Titans quarterback play has been 'hilariously bad' ([[Will Levis]] inaccurate and still nursing a shoulder injury, throwing targets 'not even in the zip code'), but Ridley has also left plays on the field. Harmon says the tight, detailed fundamentals he had before the suspension and mental-health absence never came back, and he is less explosive as an older player — still a fine starting receiver, but overpaid this offseason.
- 2024-11-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-21]]): Harmon ranks Ridley second-least disappointing simply because 'he is Calvin Ridley' — an up-and-down player at this point whose film has been mixed, and who cannot be consistently counted on as a number one. What he likes recently is varied alignment and a genuine effort to get him into rhythm early: since Week 8 Ridley has been targeted on 27% of his routes with 15.6 air yards per target, a normalisation from the pure shot-play role he had early, and that has held whether [[Will Levis]] or Mason Rudolph is playing. He blames some of the poor numbers on the offense and on Levis, who he thinks is the same up-and-down, mistake-prone player he was in Jacksonville. Harmon thinks Ridley would be a good number two across from a true number one X — he found the [[DeAndre Hopkins]] pairing interesting — but says last year's film confirmed his number one days are gone, a career arc bent by his mental-health leave and gambling suspension right as he was ascending. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) notes a 48.6% catch rate and a two-touchdown pace, but a swing from a 518-yard pace over six games to a 1,200-yard pace and 61% catch rate over the last three.
- 2024-12-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-31]]): 'fine player, but both things can be true.' Ridley became genuinely productive once [[DeAndre Hopkins]] was traded away, sits 20th among wide receivers with 941 receiving yards and added 84 in Week 17, at roughly 1.8 yards per route run — Harmon calls that a totally fine season from a totally fine player. The caveat is that Tennessee is a very bad team with a mess at quarterback, so some of the season's numbers are, in Koh's word that Harmon adopts, 'fakey' and inflated. Harmon does not elevate him beyond that tier and does not put him in the good-player-being-masked bucket the way he does [[Brian Thomas Jr.]].
- 2025-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-07]]): Ridley is Tennessee's nominal number one in what Koh calls maybe the worst receiver room in the NFL, worse even than Pittsburgh's, with [[Treylon Burks]] as the number two behind him. Harmon credits the Titans for doing a good job moving Ridley around the formation in 2024. Koh's framing is that at 31 and given his inconsistencies, Ridley would ideally be a number two and in a pristine room would be a high-impact number three — more than serviceable as a two, but Tennessee still has to find both a true number one and a slot receiver. Harmon adds the room needs 'everything': a high-volume slot, a true X, and reinforcement whether the team drafts [[Cam Ward]] or signs [[Sam Darnold]], because Darnold's 2024 success rested on having many options in the route.
- 2025-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-21]]): Harmon says Ridley can still play and was actually pretty good in 2024 despite some weird box-score games — one eight-target, zero-catch outing where the throws were not near him — and that he is best as a flanker who moves around. That is exactly why Harmon puts Tennessee in the must-draft-a-receiver-early bucket and wants either a true X or a power slot next to him. James Koh (co-host, not tracked) is bleaker, calling the Titans 'Patriots adjacent' with an aging Ridley and nothing else, saying he was aghast to see [[Treylon Burks]] penciled in as the number two on a projected-starters exercise with Charles McDonald, and that you cannot put [[Cam Ward]] out there throwing to nobody behind an offensive line they have otherwise fortified with Dan Moore and Kevin Zeitler.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Ridley had a better 2024 than people give him credit for, but Harmon agrees with Koh (not tracked) that he should not be Tennessee's X — at this stage of his career he is much better suited as a flanker. Harmon's guess at the Week 1 look is [[Van Jefferson]], whom Koh calls the ultimate snap eater, and Ridley outside, with Ridley and [[Tyler Lockett]] in two-receiver sets; Lockett he now regards as a speed-slot candidate rather than an outside player. Both hosts think Tennessee genuinely needs someone who can win on the line of scrimmage, and expect a rookie to push for that time by late season.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Ridley has 'devolved into a hit-and-miss deep threat' who hits just often enough on volume to be a fantasy WR2, sometimes a low-end WR2 — he drops passes, gets injured, and has not repeated his Atlanta peak. Waldman would take [[Tyler Lockett]] over him as the Titans' likely reception and yardage leader, while granting Ridley more downfield upside. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) disagreed and kept Ridley as the Titans WR1, noting he was super inefficient last year on a heavy target load with no touchdowns while [[Nick Westbrook-Ikhine]] was the 'touchdown weirdo,' which Harris framed as room for positive touchdown regression. Both agreed Ridley is also going cheap, so neither is being overpaid for.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Ridley has a legitimate chance at a 1,200-yard season and should be the number one in Tennessee's offense this year — but Angelo says the number one of the future is not on the roster, and that Ridley is getting up there in age. He sees the 2025 Titans as a bad team (they held the number one overall pick) with a possible step forward in 2026 once [[Cam Ward]] gets more weapons. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees Ridley is easily the top guy on paper while arguing [[Tyler Lockett]] could threaten him in receptions and yardage if the Lockett-Ward rapport takes. Separately Angelo comps an aging [[Tyreek Hill]]'s realistic outcome to Ridley.
- 2025-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-01]]): Harmon is really excited about Ridley, calling his 1,000 yards a minor miracle in an offense that ranked 29th in success rate. Brian Callahan followed through on not deploying him as a static X — his on-line rate fell from 78.8% in Jacksonville (81.8% outside) to 50.4%, splitting X and flanker like his best Atlanta seasons. Results: 73.2% versus man (up on his Jacksonville year), 78.5% versus press, one of his better career marks, zone around 80%, 87% on digs at 15% participation, over 83% on outs at 14%, 86.1% on slants, 80.6% on curls, and 58% on nines at a heavy 22.5% nine-route rate Harmon bets comes down. Contested catch rate jumped from 46.2% to a career-best 81.8%, though a 7% drop rate keeps the 'Calvin Ridley experience' alive. Harmon's headline is the [[Cam Ward]] fit on slants, curls and deeper in-breakers, and a [[Terry McLaurin]]-in-2024 comp: a known good player taken to another level by a quarterback upgrade. He also expects a zillion targets because the room has no other X option.
- 2025-08-20 — According to guest Scott Pianowski (Yahoo Fantasy, not a tracked expert) on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Ridley is a round-six safe pick, one of two Titans he is targeting despite Tennessee being bad enough last year to draft first overall. The argument is that Ridley survived horrible quarterback play in 2024, so rookie [[Cam Ward]] only needs to keep the ship somewhat afloat — expectations kept on the ground, he is not [[C.J. Stroud]] or [[Jayden Daniels]] — and even a low-volume passing game should have a narrow enough target distribution to leave Ridley in a good place. Pianowski notes he has drafted less Ridley than he would like, and concedes targeting two players from a lesser offense is not the exciting play, but says the floor is appealing.
- 2025-08-22 — According to Cousin Josh, a recurring guest on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast]] and not a tracked expert ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Ridley's Baggy Award song is Elton John's I'm Still Standing, chosen because it is nice to have him back — a solid season last year, and Josh expects him to be back again and to keep reminding everyone he has not gone away. A brief, positive aside rather than a full evaluation.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): Ridley is guest Ben Gretch's (Stealing Signals, NOT tracked) number three 'might be wrong about' — the veteran he suspects he is underrating because his process pushes young breakout candidates like [[George Pickens]] up past him. Gretch's bull case: Ridley earned a ton of volume in 2024, his YAC and ball-in-hand data rebounded past even his best Atlanta years with missed-tackles-forced up, he is the clear number one with [[Tyler Lockett]] looking done and only [[Elic Ayomanor]] and [[Chigoziem Okonkwo]] behind him, and [[Cam Ward]] replacing [[Will Levis]] should sharply improve ball placement. He offers the [[Courtland Sutton]] comp — [[Sean Payton]] hiding [[Russell Wilson]] in late 2023, then [[Bo Nix]] letting the offense open up in 2024. Harris pushes back hard: 'bite your tongue for mentioning the name Calvin Ridley in the same sentence as Courtland Sutton' — all the gifts go to Ridley, all the hands and gumption to Sutton; Ridley is a knucklehead who can look great and then look lost for a whole game. Harris has him around 68 versus a market of 60 and owns him on a single team. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-05]]): Harmon took the under on Ridley's 53.5 receiving yards prop (-114) for Week 1 while stressing he remains very much in on the [[Cam Ward]] to Ridley connection over the full season. The reasoning is matchup-specific: Tennessee travels to Denver, and Patrick Surtain II is one of the rare genuine travelling shutdown man-cover corners, so he will be on Ridley all day because the Titans do not offer much else to worry about. Harmon framed Week 2 as the buy-low window, since he does not expect Ridley to do much in Ward's first career start against what might be the best defense in the league.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): Ridley was shut down most of the day by Pat Surtain II, with his second catch coming in the fourth quarter on [[Cam Ward]]'s best throw of the game. He then had two drops, the first a killer and the second with a minute to play that ended the game. Harris's mitigation is real — this was one of the league's best defenses — but his overall read is that Tennessee does not look like a source of fantasy points in the near term given the quarterback play.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris is scathing — Calvin Ridley stinks at football, he makes that much money for being terrible, he's just bad all the time. You can use him in a fantasy league, but know you are trying to get away with something, because he is so inconsistent; Harris pairs him with [[Jerry Jeudy]] as the Spider-Man pointing meme of inconsistency. Guest John Daigle (Establish The Run, not tracked) frames it as an opportunity rather than an indictment: Ridley continues to earn targets in the intermediate part of the field but leads rookie [[Elic Ayomanor]] by only one target. Harris adds that there is room for Ayomanor to become an actual number one if the Titans offense ever becomes competent.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris said his rank on Ridley is always low — 'I don't think he's good, I think he could be good', and that what is wrong with Ridley is 'the six inches between his ears', an inability to make himself do it consistently. Guest Scott Pianowski of Yahoo Fantasy (not a tracked expert) nominated Ridley as hardest-to-rank and has fallen to roughly WR32 from a consensus top-30 preseason view: he had bet Ridley could repeat last year's production if [[Cam Ward]] improved on Tennessee's prior quarterbacks, but now questions counting on an age-31 receiver and cites snaps where Ridley visibly gives up or shows partial effort. Pianowski went further and said Ridley may no longer even be the best receiver on the roster — talent-wise probably yes, performance-wise there is a real chance not — while noting seven targets in a tough Denver week 1 and good chemistry flashes with Ward in week 2.
- 2025-09-22 — According to guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]] [Redraft (Standard)]: 'it's basically a full-blown disaster' — Ridley was drafted in roughly the WR26-32 range and Doherty now sees him as WR40-48, with a non-zero chance Elic Ayomanor simply passes him on the depth chart. His mechanism is compounding: Ridley is not good, and when he is good his quarterback is not ready to be good, as when he got free down the right sideline in the fourth quarter and [[Cam Ward]] missed the throw. [[Chris Harris]] admitted he looks for bad plays because he does not like Ridley, but said Ridley did not fail to provide them, and would sit or cut him in a shallow league.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): Ridley is now droppable in certain leagues — 'you can give him the Thursday boot'. Harris says that watching games blind to contracts and veteran status, Ridley is not the receiver in Tennessee who plays like a number one, though he is not sure [[Elic Ayomanor]] is either, and he would not drop Ridley for Ayomanor specifically because Ayomanor carries the same root problem: [[Cam Ward]] is not ready and the offense is terrible. Guest Jeff Erickson (RotoWire, not tracked) agrees it is very close to a drop, adding that Ridley was limited in snaps against Houston with an undisclosed injury, that there are real performance issues — bad drops, being on the wrong page with Ward — and 'perhaps some effort issues as well'. Ridley did beat his man on a sail ball inside the Texans 40 in the second quarter.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-06]]): Ridley was the one Tennessee player Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) thought looked genuinely good in this game, and Bob cited Mike Clay's note that Ridley's rough start to the season may have had as much to do with facing Patrick Surtain II, Charvarius Ward and Riley Stigley Jr.-type coverage as with Ridley himself. Waldman agreed it was a promising game and that Ridley looked good against coverage, while noting the coverage was not as tough. Both hosts flagged that the Titans offense remains not fantasy friendly, so the improvement is real but the environment caps it.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: five catches for 131 yards on two enormous plays — a fourth-quarter deep post to the Arizona 20 and the 30-seconds-left bomb down the right sideline that improbably put Tennessee in winning field goal range. Harris will not let him off the hook either, flagging a sideline drop, and frames the whole Titans pass game the same way: 'this team really stinks and we're not feeling amazing about any of the pass catchers.'
