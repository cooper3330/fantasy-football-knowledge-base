# Calvin Ridley

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024, ahead of his free agency. Team/role are
> as of that date and are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-07]]): a 2023
  season Harmon calls widely misjudged as a fantasy bust (76/1,000+/8 TD on a
  career-low 55.9% catch rate, highly inconsistent week to week) but graded
  "green" — above league average — on every charted route except the curl,
  with strong success rates vs. man (71.9%, 69th percentile, down from a
  ~76-78% career norm) and vs. zone (80.4%, in line with career). The one real
  decline is vs. press coverage: 66.7%, down roughly 10 points from his
  Atlanta-years 75-79% range, which Harmon attributes partly to nearly two
  years away from football (a 2022 ACL tear/suspension layoff) rather than
  pure decline at age 29. Curl-route success also fell sharply (72% vs. an
  ~85% career norm), which Harmon calls a timing/chemistry issue with Trevor
  Lawrence as much as a talent issue.
  Both hosts fault Jacksonville's deployment on top of the individual
  drop-off: Ridley was lined up almost exclusively as a static X receiver
  (81.8% of snaps outside, 79% on the line) with a thin, low-difficulty route
  tree (48.6% comeback/curl/9-route, under 10% slants) and drew extra
  coverage attention (double-teamed on 13.6% of routes) — a much more
  one-dimensional usage than his more balanced 50/50 X/flanker Atlanta years.
  Harmon's read: still "a high-quality starting outside receiver" even after
  the down year, and better with more Z/flanker usage to unlock intermediate
  and slant routes; expects Jacksonville to re-sign him and separately wants
  the team to add a cheaper X-receiver complement (a rookie) so Ridley can be
  moved around rather than parked outside every snap. See [[Reception
  Perception Methodology]] and [[Scheme vs Talent]].

- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]): pushes back on a Carolina Panthers landing-spot prediction floated by co-host **Bob Harris** (not a tracked expert) — "fuck it, to be honest." Argues Ridley "needs to be somewhere where he's not the wide receiver one," better as a schemed WR2/3 who draws a top corner away or wins off misdirection than as an off-structure improviser, and that Carolina's [[Bryce Young]] will require a lot of off-structure improvisation from his receivers. Explicitly: "he's a good NFL wide receiver, but I don't think he's a primary wide receiver even though the billing was cast that way" — a more skeptical ceiling framing than the 03-07 charting verdict that called him a "high-quality starting outside receiver."

- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): reacts to
  the actual signing — a surprise 4-year, $92M deal with Tennessee, $50M
  fully guaranteed, putting his APY in the [[Deebo Samuel]]/[[Terry
  McLaurin]] range. Both hosts call it "way out of market" relative to his
  down 2023 season, but Harmon defends the underlying player using the same
  71.9%-vs.-man/80.4%-vs.-zone numbers from the 03-07 profile — "still a
  high-quality separator." Likes the fit: Tennessee can use him as a
  flanker/slot/motion piece rather than parking him as a static X the way
  Jacksonville did, especially with [[DeAndre Hopkins]] able to play X.
  Justifies the price given Tennessee's WR2 last year was Chris Moore (not a
  tracked page) with 424 yards, and given Levis-focused logic: "when you have
  a rookie quarterback contract... spend money with reckless abandon" to get
  a real read on [[Will Levis]]. Notes the deal may look cheap in hindsight
  once 2024's expected top-receiver extensions (Justin Jefferson, CeeDee
  Lamb, Tee Higgins, Ja'Marr Chase, Amon-Ra St. Brown, Brandon Aiyuk, Nico
  Collins) land. Also flags a 2024 Draft knock-on effect: Tennessee signing
  Ridley makes it unlikely they take a receiver at pick 7, "freeing up"
  [[Rome Odunze]] or [[Malik Nabers]] for another landing spot.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: calls the Tennessee contract "a joke" relative to the player — a quality starting NFL receiver, but not a true No. 1, more a "high-end [[Diontae Johnson]]" who needs to be couched among actual leaders of a receiving corps rather than being the corps leader himself. Doubts he plays into his mid-30s at a high level. Floats a theory connecting the deal to Tennessee's earlier decision to move on from [[A.J. Brown]] under a previous coordinator — reads the Ridley splurge as a "people-pleasing," "two steps forward, one step back" overcorrection typical of the organization. Separately agrees [[Treylon Burks]] is not yet a leader of that receiving corps, while granting [[DeAndre Hopkins]] already is one.
- 2024-06-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-20]]): Ridley's 2023 usage in Jacksonville under passing-game coordinator Press Taylor was very static/outside-only (go routes, comebacks, curls per his RP profile), and Ridley himself said upon signing with Tennessee that he was pitched on being deployed more diversely across alignments. Harmon thinks new OC Nick Holtz — who was Jacksonville's passing-game coordinator last year but reported directly to Taylor — likely wasn't the one pigeonholing Ridley, and expects Tennessee (with DeAndre Hopkins locked in as the true X, freeing Ridley to move around) to deliver on that promised versatility. Notes Ridley is being paid like a high-end WR2 even though his profile output has trended more toward that tier than true WR1.
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: Raised as context for [[Trevor Lawrence]]'s rough prior season, Waldman calls Ridley 'just not a primary receiver' -- he has the athletic ability but has lacked conceptual consistency at big moments since his time in Atlanta. He cites Jacksonville letting Ridley walk to Tennessee in free agency as evidence the Jaguars themselves didn't view him as a true WR1 worth matching Tennessee's contract offer for.
- 2024-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-11]]) [Dynasty]: Ridley signed a 4-year, $92M deal with Tennessee that both hosts called an overpay, but Harmon still views him as a 'high-end number two' -- his 2023 RP success rates (69th percentile vs. man, 65th vs. zone, 47th vs. press) were down from his pre-suspension peak, when he was trending toward 'superstar number one' territory. Harmon noted that in his explosive Week 1 with Buffalo, 48.6% of Ridley's charted routes were nine/comeback/curl against a weak Colts secondary and only 9.8% were slants, a lean he says shouldn't be repeated with Tennessee's less favorable matchups. He projects Ridley as a Z/flanker in Tennessee, a role Harmon thinks suits him better than the heavier X usage Jacksonville had him running. James Koh (untracked) called the signing a smart move for a franchise still repairing the fallout of the A.J. Brown trade.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon argued Ridley's route diet needs to change to be 'more maximized' in Tennessee -- he charted almost 50% of Ridley's routes last year (with Jacksonville) as go/curl/comeback routes, with slants on fewer than 10%, and said moving him off the X position and into more in-breaking work would help. That projection depends on a Titans offensive line guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn (not tracked) still called a below-average, rebuilding unit -- 2nd-youngest and 29th-most-experienced in the league -- even after drafting LT J.C. Latham 7th overall; Thorn expects the line to be merely 'better, maybe in the 20s' league-wide this year rather than a finished product, with real growth more likely in 2025-26 under OL coach Bill Callahan.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): Waldman thinks Tennessee paid for the wrong player. Ridley is a good receiver but his big Atlanta year is not who he is — Waldman cites catch-point issues, route issues, and coming up short in the big moments you rely on from a true WR1, adding that Jacksonville would have kept him if they saw him that way. He still projects roughly 140 targets, around 80 catches and close to 1,100 yards, but says the market treats him like a peak [[Stefon Diggs]] and buyers will get 'broken parts' instead: what you pay for is about the best you'll get. Guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) is slightly under market on all Titans pass catchers and calls the roster the 'discount Bengals' — cheap, shine-off acquisitions.
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with [[DeAndre Hopkins]] hurt, Harmon is now 'inching Ridley up' to be the more productive receiver in Tennessee, having previously thought Hopkins' verticality might make it neck and neck. He likes the [[Will Levis]] connection and calls Ridley a decent value pick going in a fair range rather than badly underpriced. The caveat is alignment: in Jacksonville in 2023 Ridley ran a go, comeback or curl on over 40% of his charted routes (nearly 19% curls, 13% digs), and Harmon wants him off the line running more posts, digs and out routes from the slot, where he was successful. If Hopkins misses time and Ridley is pushed back to X, Harmon warns they will 'make a good player worse.' Levis threw digs on only 5% of throws in 2023 but graded best on in-breakers and nine routes.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Harmon thinks the market may have under-discussed Ridley this year after talking about him constantly a season ago, and that he has a chance to have a pretty big year in Tennessee. The reasoning is scheme: as Cincinnati's OC, Brian Callahan's Bengals had the second-highest pass-to-run ratio and second-highest pass rate over expected in the NFL even with [[Jake Browning]] starting, while Tennessee had the third-fewest pass attempts last year. Harmon expects a molding of Callahan's spread philosophy with his father Bill Callahan's run-game and offensive-line background, meaning some condensed formations and physical football rather than pure finesse 11 personnel — but a far more pass-happy team than the Mike Vrabel era.
