# D.J. Moore

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): called a "stone cold baller" coming off an "all-pro level" 2023 season, cited as the reason Chicago is sorted into the "critical need" wide receiver bucket despite Moore's own play — the Bears "ain't got nothing else going on at wide receiver" behind him. Floated as the ideal WR1 running mate for a rookie receiver the Bears could pair with [[Caleb Williams]] at first overall, with speculation Chicago could even trade up from the 9th pick to grab one.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]): pairing with newly-acquired [[Keenan Allen]] praised as an ideal duo for a rookie quarterback (presumed Caleb Williams) — played on the outside 80% of his snaps in 2023 and graded as a true vertical separator and man-coverage beater, complementary to Allen's underneath, slot-heavy game. Harmon says Moore is versatile enough to line up at all three receiver spots (X, flanker, slot) and is "the most deadly version of himself" when moved around rather than pinned to X full-time, crediting more downfield/YAC opportunities under Chicago's staff than he got during his Carolina tenure (cites a Thursday-night game vs. Washington where he beat man coverage repeatedly on curls and comebacks). Notes Shane Waldron's scheme leaned heavily on isolation/11-personnel route concepts in 2023, and expects the addition of Allen to activate the middle of the field more.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): a direct,
  on-record disagreement between the hosts over target competition if Chicago
  drafts a receiver at 9 alongside Moore and [[Keenan Allen]] — Koh argues
  [[Keenan Allen]] would be the leading target-getter for a rookie [[Caleb Williams]]
  over the first six games specifically, citing Allen's ability to get open
  underneath; Harmon disagrees flatly, "there's no scenario where Keenan Allen
  gets more targets than D.J. Moore," and separately argues that if Allen
  somehow did lead the team in targets it would mean the offense itself was
  struggling. Both agree the Odunze/Moore/Allen trio would "easily" catapult
  Chicago into the conversation for best receiver room in the NFL.
- 2024-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-30]]): Moore has graduated from tier three to tier two — in Harmon's language, from 'a passable number one' to 'a superstar number one receiver' — after playing like a superstar number one in 2023, and he is a clear-cut top-20 receiver with a fair argument for top-15 over names like [[Stefon Diggs]] and [[Cooper Kupp]]. He was awesome against press and on downfield routes last year, and Harmon says a genuinely good passing game can flow through him as the top target. Harmon and Koh partly disagree on deployment: Harmon argues Moore is one of the rare receivers with no bad alignment — he can win as an iso X and 'put bad DBs in a blender', as in the Thursday night Washington game — while Koh insists Moore is maximized only as a movable chess piece, because sticking him at X takes away his inside work and he is not so dominant outside that you win every snap there. Harmon largely lands with Koh in practice: with [[Rome Odunze]] arriving as a big true X, Chicago can finally put Moore off the line of scrimmage and get him on YAC routes and in space. He also expects Moore to remain the heavy favourite to lead the team in receiving yards and in first-three-read targets, and projects him for the most targets, receptions and yardage on the season.
- 2024-09-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-17]]): a long-running frustration — Harmon says he will 'go to my grave' wondering why no offense uses Moore as a layup route player who gets the ball in space on the quarterback's back foot as a first read. He argues Moore has never played for a good offensive coordinator; he rates Joe Brady but says Brady used him as a vertical X and was scapegoated by Matt Rhule. Harmon is explicit about the archetype: Moore is not [[Deebo Samuel]] and should not get running back carries, but he is unusually instinctual after the catch and consistently makes the first man miss. His complaint about Chicago is that Shane Waldron is instead designing targets for [[D'Andre Swift]], DeAndre Carter and Gerald Everett — players Harmon says are not needle movers or even helpful on the field right now.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): citing JJ Zachariason's Monday data dump (Zachariason is not a tracked expert), the Bears have not produced a top-20 weekly wide receiver performance since Week 6, and Moore has just one top-20 game this year after six in 2023. Waldman frames this as the ripple effect of [[Caleb Williams]] being under constant pressure, and as further evidence that the right coach-GM combination matters more to a receiver's fantasy outcome than his own talent — the Bears, he argues, may be stable in ownership tenure but unstable in how they approach running the organization. Practical read: anyone relying on a Bears wide receiver is fighting the environment. Contrast with the [[Resilience and Coach-GM Fit]] case he makes for Detroit.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): the worst personnel decision in Chicago's offense is playing Moore at X receiver. At 5-10, 210 he is far closer to [[Deebo Samuel]] than to [[DK Metcalf]], and Angelo wants him moved to Z with [[Rome Odunze]] taking more outside X snaps. Beyond alignment, Angelo says Moore looks like a shell of himself and describes watching him walk off the field mid-play to sit on the bench, supposedly hurt, while [[Caleb Williams]] scrambled for his life — something Angelo says he has never seen in a long time around football, and exactly the kind of thing Shane Waldron failed to hold veterans accountable for. He expects incoming coordinator Thomas Brown to be more direct about it.
- 2024-11-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-18]]): relaying what he had been told by people who had studied the tape more closely than he had to that point, Waldman said Moore is a very good route runner on physical technique but conceptually inconsistent — the depth he takes and the timing on certain concepts is 'like a box of chocolates', which he called maddening for a rookie quarterback to work with. He read Thomas Brown's week-11 plan as an acknowledgement of exactly that: get Moore the ball, but keep him on RPOs, screens and short routes where he cannot break the play for [[Caleb Williams]], while the two get on the same page. Waldman flagged this as second-hand and said he intended to watch the tape himself.
- 2024-12-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-03]]): 'real-ish' that D.J. Moore is rejuvenated after Chicago fired Matt Eberflus. Harmon read Moore's quote that 'as the season was going, you just kind of figured this was going to happen' as evidence there was little confidence in Eberflus in the building, and credits interim coach Thomas Brown with stripping the offense down — quick game, screens, short routes and yards-after-catch work, which Harmon says he has wanted for Moore on this show for a long time. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) supplied the split: over the last three games Moore is averaging 10 targets, 7.3 receptions and 88 yards per game with two touchdowns and a 73.3% catch rate, versus six targets, 3.3 receptions and 26 yards per game on a 54% catch rate with no touchdowns in the prior four.
- 2024-12-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-24]]): Harmon's Christmas 'gift' for Moore is a vacation, and he means it as a real read — he thinks Moore is burned out. He points to Moore answering a question about next season with 'I'm looking forward to a vacation' and a Bears social clip where he walks past the camera visibly unenthusiastic about being there, and says he would be burned out too in that building, needing time away from [[Caleb Williams]], Matt Eberflus, Shane Waldron and [[Keenan Allen]]. He is careful not to excuse it: 'I'm not excusing some of the lackluster things that have happened for DJ Moore this season.' Co-host James Koh (not tracked) agrees Moore reads as burnt out and needing a break. Harmon separately ties Chicago into his 'too many good players can be a problem' argument, noting the offense seemed to click more when Allen or Moore was off the field.
- 2025-01-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-13]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman says Chicago misaligned Moore, playing a flanker as a split end, and that looking at his route charting you did not know what you would get play to play — Waldman's read is that Moore did not understand the role he was in. [[Matt Harmon]] agrees the film was not pretty at times, but weights heavily that Moore, who has played in some disgusting environments in Carolina, was the one publicly raising that plays were not being scripted under Shane Waldron. Harmon's conclusion is that nobody in Chicago was confident in what they were being asked to do.
- 2025-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-19]]): Harmon puts Moore and [[Rome Odunze]] at 50/50 to lead Chicago in production and calls Moore a screaming bounce-back candidate if he takes to Ben Johnson's offense and [[Caleb Williams]] takes a step forward. He wants Moore used as an in-space weapon — full-speed motion into the slot at the snap running runway routes, with a boundary [[Sacrificial X Receiver]] outside — and says he told Moore at the Super Bowl that no coordinator has ever maximized him that way, to which Moore smiled and said it takes a certain level of creativeness before deferring to coaches. Moore ran about 20% of snaps in the slot in 2024 and Harmon floats roughly 45% going forward; James Koh (not tracked) would rather see him as a flanker off the line or moving around. Harmon's stated risk is price, not player: the Ben Johnson hype will inflate the Bears' ADPs, and he warns against mapping Detroit's clearly defined roles onto Chicago because Williams and [[Jared Goff]] could not be more different.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]) [Dynasty]: a fine player whose situation is quietly getting worse. He has one year left on his deal, and he did not look good last season when Chicago used him as a split end rather than the flanker he needs to be, and when his route depths were unpredictable; he played better once they triaged the offense and gave him specific routes. Waldman notes Moore and Cole Kmet both got significant extensions and then complained to the media, and repeats a theory from Phil Ottochian and Shane Marshaw on the TNL Network Bears podcast that drafting [[Luther Burden III]] and Colston Loveland may be Ben Johnson's message that both can be replaced. He would not be surprised if Burden takes a bigger chunk of this offense than people expect as a rookie.
- 2025-04-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: Chicago spending its first two picks on [[Colston Loveland]] and [[Luther Burden III]] is a message — Harmon says Cole Kmet and Moore are both on extensions and 'might not be long for this roster', and that he would be shocked if Ben Johnson watched Moore's 2024 tape and did not react badly to the effort and consistency. He says this as a self-described admirer of Moore's game dating to Maryland, and notes he spoke with Moore and [[Rome Odunze]] at the Super Bowl, where Moore seemed excited about the new staff. Harmon also sees a real skill-set overlap between Moore and Burden, since Burden profiles as a create-a-touch player early the way Moore did. James Koh (not tracked) adds that the whole Bears team, not just Moore, gave up on 2024, and that staples of the previous regime are on notice.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]) [Best Ball]: WR18 is the right price on Moore and possibly still light — Waldman says Moore has room to be higher and could 'wind up being wide receiver 2' at a WR18 cost. His reasoning is projection-driven: if he is right about [[Caleb Williams]], two and probably three Chicago pass catchers deliver fantasy starter value. His preferred distribution has [[Luther Burden III]] as the main option and a possible top-10 receiver, with [[Rome Odunze]] roughly WR25–30 and [[Colston Loveland]] between TE8 and TE14; Moore holds WR18 value in that world. If instead Burden's role is limited and Waldman is wrong about him, Moore lands somewhere between a top-five and top-10 receiver. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) pushed back on whether Moore is still the true lead receiver with Odunze developing and Burden arriving; Waldman's answer was that the volume and Moore's fit under Ben Johnson make WR18 very reasonable either way.
- 2025-05-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-14]]): Harmon says Moore's visible frustration in 2024 was understandable — he was coming off the best season of his career as Chicago's X receiver and then had that role diluted when the Bears drafted [[Rome Odunze]] ninth overall while also carrying slot-only [[Keenan Allen]], leaving nobody in the room willing to clear routes or chip on the edge. Both hosts argue the fix is not to give Moore the X job back: Harmon and Koh agree he is best as a flanker/slot player, because from a skill-set and physicality standpoint the offense is 'cooking with gas' when Moore gets the ball in space rather than working the boundary. Koh notes that Moore had always been a workmanlike player through bad situations in Carolina, and 2024 was the first year his effort level and body language visibly slipped — which he found surprising. Harmon expects Ben Johnson to reset the hierarchy and roles, with Odunze at X and [[Luther Burden III]] a candidate for slot work if Moore does not want it.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: drafting [[Luther Burden III]] 'spells kind of the end of' Moore in Chicago. His argument is fit rather than talent: the [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] role in a Ben Johnson offense is an effort role — blocking and dirty work in the run game while being the primary short-to-intermediate target — and effort is exactly what Moore did not give in 2024. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees. Angelo says Johnson will not deploy Moore as his Amon-Ra, leaving Burden and [[Rome Odunze]] as the two focal points.
- 2025-06-05 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-05]]) [Dynasty]: he would trade Moore plus a pick for [[Breece Hall]] without hesitation. Angelo calls the [[Luther Burden III]] selection a writing-on-the-wall moment — Ben Johnson wants his own guys he can develop and trust, and he saw Moore's antics in 2024: walking off the field mid-play and sitting alone on the bench while a rookie quarterback struggled, from a highly paid player who is supposed to be a captain, in front of a bottom-three offensive line. Drafting [[Colston Loveland]] and Burden tells Angelo that Moore is not going to be the guy. He also argues [[Rome Odunze]] profiles as more trustworthy on a down-to-down basis from a route-acumen standpoint, whereas Moore has to be schemed touches. Both hosts agree Hall would move the needle in that offense more than Moore does.
- 2025-06-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-27]]): Moore's poor 2024 was substantially role-driven — people inside the organization told Waldman he was playing split end rather than his natural flanker role for the first time, and that his route depths were 'like a box of chocolates' under a broken Shane Waldron install. [[Matt Harmon]] agreed and added that there was visible 'quiet quitting' on Moore's film, which Moore himself hinted at in an interview. Waldman still projects ~120 targets in 2025 with Moore and [[Luther Burden III]] alternating flanker and slot under Ben Johnson, but warns of long-term risk: Moore and Cole Kmet both took extensions and both aired issues to the media, which can make them expendable to a new coach. Harmon framed the Chicago room as a lever — the more Burden struggles the more targets Moore sees, and the better Burden looks the lower Moore's ceiling.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo does not think Moore fits what Ben Johnson wants because he is not a details player — closer to a [[Deebo Samuel]] type than a [[Davante Adams]] type, better as an extension of the run game than as the focal point of a passing attack. In his view Johnson drafted a more evolved version of that same archetype in [[Luther Burden III]], and by 2026 the big three are [[Colston Loveland]], [[Rome Odunze]] and Burden rather than Moore. [[Matt Waldman]] does not dispute the fit read but pushes back on the framing that it is a knock: he compares the two archetypes to fine-dining cooking versus a South Alabama soul-food kitchen — both good, you just cannot predict how heavy-handed grandma will be on a given day — i.e. one player is good when schemed, the other is good however you deploy him.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]): Waldman flags Moore's usage change as a genuine positive — under Ben Johnson he goes back to flanker instead of being miscast at the X, which he lists among the concrete reasons [[Caleb Williams]] should improve. He notes conflicting camp reports (some saying Moore does not look great, others saying he does and is being moved around the formation) and treats that as more evidence camp chatter is noise. Because Chicago likes to throw the short stuff and [[Rome Odunze]] absorbs the sacrificial vertical X work, Waldman thinks Moore and [[Luther Burden III]] may be better than people expect, though he cautions that with this many mouths to feed none of the Bears' pass catchers may finish top-20 at the position.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Moore is the weapon back of the Bears offense — a secondary rusher who can play slot, take cheap targets and create after the catch, already being used by Ben Johnson in the red zone and out of the backfield. Angelo has long argued Moore is not a primary passing-game option: a very good first- and second-level target with intermediate value, not a deep threat, and not a high-volume receiver. He named [[Rome Odunze]] and [[Luther Burden III]] as the two Bears who can handle three-level volume and [[Colston Loveland]] as a two-level volume player. [[Matt Waldman]] went further, calling Moore 'a better Deebo than Deebo' — more productive, more versatile, more resilient — while [[Deebo Samuel]] plays closer to [[Isiah Pacheco]] with little nuance as a runner. Both went over/under 30 carries and said over, contingent on Swift's efficiency [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-14 — According to Sigmund Bloom of Footballguys (not a tracked expert) with [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Moore has only fallen a round or two despite a miserable 2024, which Bloom finds odd given that [[Rome Odunze]] now looks like Ben Johnson's preferred receiver and the target tree also includes [[Luther Burden III]] and [[Colston Loveland]]. His broader worry is that everyone is assuming a bounce-back and pricing it in, when Detroit's own turnaround under Johnson took time and Bears observers expect the first month of the season to look like an extension of preseason — meaning no Bears pass catcher may be trustworthy early. Bloom's actionable framing: the buying window is late September or early October when the market gets disappointed.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon calls Moore's 2024 usage the weirdest X-receiver role of all time — 76% of sampled snaps on the line of scrimmage, an 'Andre Johnson alignment' paired with a '[[Kadarius Toney]] route tree' of 10.5% screens and a pile of flat routes, leading the league in screen targets and catches while stuck out on the boundary with no blockers. He relays Moore's own words from a Super Bowl interview: it is scary getting the ball with nobody out there blocking for you. Harmon wants him used like [[Deebo Samuel]] moving around the formation into space; The Athletic's Robert Mays (not tracked) argued for keeping him in the Andre Johnson alignment. Harmon says Moore is a very good player but not consistently detail-oriented, and projects a hierarchy shift: base projection just over 1,000 yards, adjusted down toward 900 as [[Rome Odunze]] rises. He also wonders long term whether Moore and [[Luther Burden III]] can coexist, since Burden's mid-range outcome is essentially a D.J. Moore-type player.
- 2025-08-22 — According to Cousin Josh, a recurring guest on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast]] and not a tracked expert ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Moore is his Biggest Trash Bag of 2025 — the headline award he hit on with [[Travis Kelce]] the previous year. His reasoning is dislike of the unknown: Moore caught 98 balls last season but at a low average depth of target, and the way he was used will be completely different this year. He thinks people are buying back into [[Caleb Williams]] off a preseason game against a fourth-string Bills defense, calls the offense a work in progress, and is unsure how Moore is used with [[Rome Odunze]] and an elite tight end also needing targets. His projection is roughly 930 yards and four touchdowns, or the familiar 900-1000 and five, which he calls a bad return on a mid-fourth-round pick. He describes the upside as capped rather than the floor as terrible, so he expects to be only slightly burned if wrong. [[Chris Harris]] pushes back on the framing — a trash bag should usually be someone with a truly bad floor, and Josh is describing a capped ceiling instead — and separately says he would have expected [[Garrett Wilson]], who costs more, to be the biggest trash bag with Moore as the stealth pick; Josh disagrees, saying he genuinely thinks Moore's floor is lower.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Going Deep Rookie Receivers and RB Depth Charts)]]): the tea leaves say Moore is what we thought he was — a [[Deebo Samuel]] type, not a Stefon Diggs, [[Davante Adams]] or [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] — so under Ben Johnson he projects as more of a factor in the run game and as an extension of the run game than as the primary targeted option, with [[Rome Odunze]], [[Olamide Zaccheaus]] and [[Colston Loveland]] carrying the passing attack. Angelo believes Johnson understands that about him. [[Matt Waldman]] used the same premise in his yardage model, granting Moore only 70-80% of the [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] role production Detroit generated in 2024 rather than 100%, which is what frees up 200-plus yards for the third-receiver group including [[Luther Burden III]]. Neither host expects Moore to function as an alpha target earner in 2025.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-18]]): Moore is one of the two most likely mid-season trade candidates in the league alongside [[Tyreek Hill]]. Harmon is not 'concerned' about the usage — Moore has 11 targets in two games and is being used creatively as a move-around piece including backfield reps — but [[Rome Odunze]] is deservedly getting more, and Harmon believes [[Luther Burden III]], not [[Olamide Zaccheaus]], was drafted to eventually take Moore's spot, calling the Zaccheaus-is-blocking-Burden read a false flag. His bigger point is buy-in: from body language and public comments he does not think Moore has bought into [[Caleb Williams]] at all ('like 0%'), which he calls a massive problem for both, comparable to [[Diontae Johnson]] in Carolina and an argument for addition by subtraction. Still, he would happily trade for Moore — the cheese has not gone bad — floating Pittsburgh, Kansas City or a Carolina reunion.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Film & Theory)]]): Moore has been a good NFL receiver but may not be the right receiver for Ben Johnson's system, and he is one of the expensive Ryan Poles-era holdovers Johnson inherited rather than chose. Waldman notes Moore publicly protested to [[Matt Harmon]] around the Super Bowl that Twitter critics were wrong about his route running, and then last week adjusted his route at the last second as [[Caleb Williams]] was releasing, forcing Williams to change the throw and spray it over him — the exact 'box of chocolates as a route runner' complaint levied against him a year ago. Waldman cites Ryan Fitzpatrick's on-air breakdown assigning the play to Moore rather than Williams. His read: if Moore and [[DeAndre Swift]] do not prove Johnson wrong within a couple of weeks, expect noticeably less work for both.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-30]]): Harmon floats Moore as a veteran trade candidate and says the Giants — in job-saving mode after the [[Malik Nabers]] injury, and having not spent much capital moving up for [[Jackson Dart]] — are not the worst fit, since Moore can win on quick hitters, designed plays, RPOs and screens while also working downfield. He does not think Moore is playing badly (he notes ESPN analytics have Moore as the NFL's top-ranked receiver, while saying he does not agree with that and does not know their methodology); rather, Moore has simply been bypassed by [[Rome Odunze]]'s second-year leap, and Harmon is happy for someone to sacrifice for that. The caveat is that the Bears are competitive, so they may not move him.
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-09]]): Moore is still 'an adventure as a route runner,' and Waldman took his on-air protest to [[Matt Harmon]] last year as a response to Waldman's own criticism. He notes Moore then continued to produce routes that drew public complaints from former NFL quarterbacks on social media -- he attributed a remark about receivers changing routes up mid-throw to Ryan Fitzpatrick -- so 'Moore got a little found out there.' Waldman uses this, along with [[Olamide Zaccheaus]] being assignment-sound but not dynamic, as the reason he expects [[Luther Burden III]] to take over snaps in Chicago eventually.
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-27]]) [Dynasty]: In a hypothetical trade scenario, Waldman said he'd take [[D.J. Moore]] for [[Breece Hall]] if he ran the Bears, noting Moore would benefit from not having to run Ben Johnson's disciplined route tree anymore and would reunite with his college quarterback Justin Fields -- though Moore might not welcome leaving a Chicago team currently in playoff contention. Waldman flagged Moore's recently signed extension (through age 29) as a complicating factor for any real deal.
- 2025-10-31 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-31]]): Harris's first surprise is how right Cousin Josh (co-host, not tracked) was calling Moore 'the biggest trash bag in the league' back in August. Harris expected Ben Johnson's reinvented Chicago offense to make Moore its do-everything focal weapon the way Detroit used [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], but says 'there isn't an Amon-Ra St. Brown on that offense' -- Johnson instead scheme-designs to the X receiver and has fed [[Rome Odunze]] early and often as the offense's best weapon, ranking Odunze well ahead of preseason market price. Harris had Moore 17th in standard/14th in PPR preseason and calls him 'relatively unplayable' now, compounded by injury (a hospital stay after the Commanders game) and non-production from the rest of Chicago's receiving corps. Harris says he isn't dropping Moore outright but won't trust him again 'until you see it,' even in a good matchup like this week's Bengals game.
- 2026-01-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (untracked co-host) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-20]]: DJ Moore's lack of full effort on the in-breaking route on Caleb Williams' overtime interception in the Divisional Round loss to the Rams directly cost Chicago the game -- Harmon called the play from the end-zone angle a clear dagger-concept target for Moore and said Moore did not run it to the ball. Both hosts framed it as fitting a theme of this postseason: teams 'died as they lived,' and while it's forgivable for a rookie (Luther Burden, Rome Odunze) to miss a route detail, Moore is the veteran on this roster and was, in Koh's words, 'the most loosey-goosey with the details' as a route runner in a moment that mattered.
