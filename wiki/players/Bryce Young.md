# Bryce Young

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): a bad rookie year that Harmon declines to treat as a settled verdict, on the grounds that none of the three prerequisites for a fair evaluation were in place in Carolina — functional offensive line play, functional skill-position talent, or functional offensive play-calling (Frank Reich's scheme, in Harmon's view, "wasn't the right offense for Bryce Young," and Reich was fired mid-season with play-calling handed to Thomas Brown and then back). The physical-tools case is explicitly limited: below-average arm strength, undersized, not especially elusive — "how many different ways can you succeed as a quarterback if you don't have either a cannon or elite elusiveness." Harmon's conclusion is that Young's only real path to success is a hyper-accurate, high-processing game — great timing, command of the offense, sharp pre- and post-snap reads — because the physical margin for error is thin. New Carolina HC Dave Canales (ex-Tampa Bay OC) is graded as "the right guy" to get a fair, functional evaluation of Young in year two — reaching a level Harmon compares to "the Jared Goff to Canales's Sean McVay" would be "a huge win" for both, but is explicitly called a lot to ask. Open question, unresolved: whether Carolina "pulls the ripcord" on Young before giving Canales's system a real chance.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: on the incoming [[Diontae Johnson]] trade, says Young needs weapons who are leaders in their own right and can model that for a young quarterback — cites [[Brock Purdy]] and, to a degree, Jared Goff (coach/system context, not a tracked page) as examples of a strong surrounding cast accelerating a QB's growth — but doesn't think Johnson is that type of leader, so Young himself will need to be the stabilizing "duct tape" for a mercurial receiver room, uncertain he's there yet.

- 2024-04-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-15]] [Dynasty]: doing heavy Superflex drafting this cycle and finding more "hive mind" consensus at quarterback than in past years, which pushes him to value Young's job security on the way down draft boards. Feeling real upside: Carolina revamped the interior offensive line with two new starting guards, added veteran [[Diontae Johnson]], and hired Dave Canales, whose track record (most recently rehabilitating [[Baker Mayfield]] in Tampa Bay) has been kind to quarterbacks. Frames 2023 as effectively a lost rookie year that shouldn't be held against him — "if you put Joe Burrow in that situation with that offensive line, we would have been talking about" the same criticisms — and argues for giving Young at least two years before judging. Takes him later in single-QB drafts.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman ranks Young 24th among QBs for now given no 'proof of concept' yet, but places him in a tier with Geno Smith, Caleb Williams, Aaron Rodgers, Kirk Cousins, Russell Wilson and Derek Carr, at the bottom of that group with room to ascend on Underdog ADP. He cites new OC Dave Canales' track record turning around Russell Wilson in Seattle, Geno Smith's Comeback Player of the Year season, and Baker Mayfield's 2023 breakout in Tampa Bay, plus a bolstered interior offensive line and the Diontae Johnson trade, as reasons for optimism. Waldman compares Young favorably to Mayfield as a pocket mover with more anticipation in his game, though a weaker arm than Russell Wilson, and projects a realistic 2024 outcome of roughly 3,500-3,700 yards and 20-25 touchdowns. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) is using Young as a floor-play QB 'firewall' in two-QB/superflex leagues given his job security, and ranks Young ahead of Jayden Daniels.
- 2024-06-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-20]]), citing charter Derek Klassen's Reception Perception QB profile of Young: 'there's not a single green mark' in the profile, with particular weakness on anything deep downfield (nine-route, post and corner routes all below average or merely acceptable). Harmon flags real concern that defenses will start bringing safeties down against Carolina since the receiver corps historically hasn't threatened vertically. However, both hosts caution the 2023 sample is confounded — no pass protection, no receivers who could separate downfield, and reported staff infighting (the 'David Tepper of it all') — so isolating Young's true ability from a historically bad environment is much harder than isolating receiver talent via charting. Harmon expects Young's accuracy numbers to look meaningfully better with Diontae Johnson (a proven downfield separator) on the other end of throws and a more competent scheme under Canales/Idzik, though he's not calling the profile flattering by any stretch. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): both hosts defend Young against his rookie stat line. Waldman says the Carolina tape shows receivers not open and pressure arriving immediately — it was a bad-surrounding-talent problem, not a Young problem — and that Young was amazing at Alabama against disguised coverage and pressure and off-script outside the pocket. He moves the pocket and anticipates well without a big arm; Waldman comps him as more Goff-like in arm strength but more dynamic than Carson Wentz at maneuvering in the pocket. Waldman projects roughly 3,600-3,700 yards and more than 20 touchdowns, a matchup-based streamer, and says he would draft Young over [[Will Levis]] every day; guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) thinks the ceiling is higher still. McFarland's supporting study: rookie-year busts whose teams added weapons ([[Trevor Lawrence]] 13.1 to 18.6 PPG, [[Derek Carr]] 13.3 to 18, [[Jared Goff]] 9.3 to 17.9, Carson Wentz 14.2 to 22.1) — Carolina added [[Diontae Johnson]], [[Xavier Legette]], Robert Hunt, Damien Lewis and Dave Canales.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-12]]): Harmon says he cannot get how bad Young looked out of his head — 13-of-30 for 161 yards, zero passing touchdowns, two interceptions, a 32.8 rating and a 43.3% completion rate, with pressure on 39.5% of dropbacks (a top-seven rate) producing 2-of-10 for 41 yards. He stresses Carolina did the right things around him — [[Dave Canales]], a genuine number one in [[Diontae Johnson]], [[Adam Thielen]] in the slot, [[Xavier Legette]]'s athleticism — and it still failed. His structural verdict is blunt: Young is 'just not physically talented enough to be a creative thrower in the pocket' or to play off platform. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) adds the trait comp that if you described an undersized quarterback who struggles to escape the pocket with average arm strength, you could not tell Young from [[Gardner Minshew]], while conceding Young is far more accurate. Harmon also notes Carolina gutted its defense (trading Brian Burns, losing Derrick Brown) to fund the offense, so Young will live in bad game scripts.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): 'as bad as he looks, the situation is as bad as it looks' — and Young is playing down to it badly. The pile-up: a second offense in as many NFL years, which Waldman calls a bad sign; an overwrought owner; a coaching staff he doubts is genuinely granting line-of-scrimmage freedom, since a quarterback still learning the playbook cannot be given it; and an offensive line that was supposed to be better and is not. He acknowledges the standard critiques — too short, not stepping up in the pocket enough to deliver the ball, arm strength questions — while granting that Young has moments of good anticipation. Verdict: for fantasy it is as bad as it looks; in dynasty you are holding out hope for adjustments. On whether Carolina takes a quarterback with a likely top pick, Waldman says yes, because the staff is not invested in Young — if he keeps playing this way it is a slam dunk they draft another.
- 2024-09-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-17]]): Carolina benching Young for Andy Dalton two games in was the right call and unavoidable, because reporting suggested the alternative was losing the locker room — 'you can't lie to the guys about what's happening on Sunday.' Harmon cites an 18.0 passer rating on any attempt over 10 air yards and zero attempts beyond 15 air yards in Week 2, calling it 1970s football, and says the trade up to No. 1 will compete for one of the worst in NFL history. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) argues Young still has the physical tools to be a low-level starter and the problem is between the ears — he sees it, but cannot pull the trigger, like early-career [[Tua Tagovailoa]]. Harmon disagrees on the physical side: 5'10, sub-200, no live arm, throws off his tippy toes, and the profile is indistinguishable from [[Gardner Minshew]]. Both agree nobody would take Young over Minshew today. Harmon sees a real chance Young is off the roster by season's end via a deadline trade.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-19]]) [Dynasty]: after Young's benching for Andy Dalton, co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) said he does not see Young reclaiming the job in 2024 — Carolina is one of the oldest teams in the NFL and cannot afford to go back to him this year — and that Dalton is likely a QB20-to-QB24 rest-of-season option who will be fine for the Panthers. Harstad went further in practice, dropping Young in a dynasty league to claim [[Skylar Thompson]]: he does not think the book is necessarily closed on Young, 'but it's not looking great at this moment,' and he does not want to hold the roster spot for another year or more just to find out. Waldman did not contest the assessment. The bear case here is opportunity cost on a bench spot rather than a talent reversal.
- 2024-09-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-20]]): Young belongs in the bucket of QBs handed a brand-new offense in consecutive years, so his development should be assumed delayed. He looks frenetic and overwhelmed by the information in front of him, playing too fast — overlooking chances to change protections or communicate with receivers, missing pre- and early-post-snap tells, taking sacks and hits behind an offensive line Waldman calls a sieve. His verdict: the new scheme has disrupted his ability to handle the speed of the game, it is not a good match, and 'Bryce Young needs to be on the bench for a while' — though he expects owners and media to keep him on a short leash instead. He explicitly disputes Greg Cosell (not a tracked expert), who has argued Young is too short and fits neither the explosive nor the hyper-accurate ([[Baker Mayfield]] / Drew Brees) archetype: Waldman counters that Young has been in a worse situation than Mayfield, [[Russell Wilson]] or [[Kyler Murray]], all of whom had better lines or schemes, and that the tippy-toe issue is deteriorating technique under speed, not height. See [[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]].
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): Waldman used a listener comparison as an argument for patience rather than a verdict on Young. The numbers Bob Harris (untracked) read out: Young's first 18 starts produced a 2-16 record, 11 touchdowns, 13 interceptions and a 59.3 percent completion rate; [[Trevor Lawrence]]'s first 18 starts produced 3-15, 13 touchdowns, 18 interceptions and 59.5 percent — and both played in new schemes two years running. Waldman's reading is that this is further evidence teams need to be patient with young quarterbacks, and that the media-and-owner fad of expecting instant success because [[C.J. Stroud]] or Ben Roethlisberger had it is faulty. He noted separately that Andy Dalton's three first-half touchdowns were the most by a Carolina quarterback since Cam Newton in 2015.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31 (Tapping Out, Archetypes and Modeling)]]): co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) described the archetype-sorting exercise he ran on Young after his rookie year. The raw list of quarterbacks as inefficient as Young as rookies produced only about a 10% rate of good careers; filtering that same list to very high draft picks only moved it to roughly 33-40%, with Donovan McNabb, John Elway, Troy Aikman and Terry Bradshaw as examples of high picks who started early, struggled and turned it around. Harstad was careful to caveat it in both directions: he is not saying Young's bad play was good, and JaMarcus Russell is the reminder that plenty of high picks struggled early because they were simply not good. His takeaway was that getting the comp list right is the hardest and most easily botched part of the exercise. See [[Draft Capital as Quarterback Hit Rate Proxy]].
- 2024-11-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]]): Young is the counter-example of a benching that worked — the player who came back is 'way better', plays with more confidence and bravado and is more willing to take risks, and looks more confident in what he is seeing. Harmon stops short of declaring him the long-term answer, saying only that he has come out the other end a better player and that the environment around him improved: the first few weeks he had [[Diontae Johnson]] and [[Adam Thielen]] rolling their eyes at the young quarterback, and now he has [[Chuba Hubbard]] plus three rookie pass catchers in [[Ja'Tavion Sanders]], [[Xavier Legette]] and [[Jalen Coker]] behind a legitimately good offensive line. Koh (not a tracked expert) offers a psychological read: Young now plays like a man who is not scared of being benched because it already happened, so he may as well cut it loose. Harmon uses the Carolina case as the model Indianapolis could follow with [[Anthony Richardson]].
- 2024-11-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-25]]): the Chiefs game was a watershed. Asked whether Young has forestalled Carolina taking a quarterback in the 2025 draft, Waldman said he has shut the door and is nailing boards across the frame. Young looked like he did at Alabama and at Cathedral High — anticipation throws, tight-window throws, timing routes — and read Steve Spagnuolo's blitzes exceptionally well, finding quick solutions; Bob Harris (not tracked) supplied the numbers, 11-of-13 for a season-high 135 yards and a touchdown against five or more rushers on 38 percent of dropbacks. Waldman also credited a 17-play scoring drive where Young was changing run calls pre-snap, and stressed the supporting cast is thin: [[Diontae Johnson]] gone, [[Jalen Coker]] out, a geriatric [[Adam Thielen]], journeyman [[David Moore]], a mediocre line. He credited Dave Canales, the quarterback-turnaround coach behind [[Geno Smith]] and [[Baker Mayfield]].
- 2024-11-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-26]]): Young's game against Kansas City was not just the most impressive of his career (a short list) but one of the more impressive quarterback performances Harmon has seen recently — responsive against the blitz and looking to attack downfield, with balls landing right on receivers' hands. He credits the whole Carolina passing-game step forward to Young and to Canales' scheme becoming less bland than his Tampa work, helped by paid guard play in Robert Hunt and Damien Lewis. Harmon's caveat is explicit: he does not know whether Young will ever have the ceiling of a number-one-overall-pick quarterback, but he now looks like a starting quarterback they can run a functional offense with. Koh ties the offense's development curve directly to Young's.
- 2024-12-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-03]]): an explicit walk-back. Harmon says he was among the many who wrote a course that Bryce Young 'has done' and 'it's just not the case' — Young 'continues to every single week put plays on film that just did not exist for him as a rookie', specifically comfort under pressure, working muddy pockets and keeping his eyes downfield. Harmon's own read of the first two weeks was that Young looked like a non-functional player standing on his tiptoes to see over the line; he now points to the response to adversity — benched for a 36-year-old [[Andy Dalton]] by a coach two games into his tenure — as the impressive part, and credits confidence-driven momentum plus finally having functional NFL receivers. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) argued Young is playing free because the worst has already happened, and both credited Dave Canales's culture and development record, Koh naming [[Xavier Legette]], [[Jalen Coker]] and [[Chuba Hubbard]] as further evidence Carolina nailed that hire. Harmon cautioned nobody yet knows whether Young or [[Anthony Richardson]] becomes a long-term answer.
- 2024-12-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-09]]) — asked 'future star, feel it or fuck it' — Waldman says feel it. Five games since the benching, including a solid three-game run keeping Carolina in games to the end (his first interception in 125 attempts came against Philadelphia, and a [[Xavier Legette]] drop cost a touchdown). Waldman says the superpowers that made him a good prospect are back: he had looked confused in the pocket, unwilling to stand in, off on timing and accuracy and not reading the field, and now 'he ate the can of spinach.' He gives Dave Canales only begrudging credit — mostly for not getting in Young's way, which many coaches fail at — and notes the tell that Canales's prior turnarounds had real weapons ([[Geno Smith]] with [[DK Metcalf]] and [[Tyler Lockett]], [[Baker Mayfield]] with [[Mike Evans]] and [[Chris Godwin]]) while Young has an old [[Adam Thielen]]. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed, admitting he had let a training-camp photo of Young looking like a small child behind his line shade his whole evaluation.
- 2024-12-12 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-12]]) [Dynasty]: both are emphatic buyers after Young's post-benching run — Angelo says 'the arrow is pointing way up', citing Young taking two top-five defenses (Kansas City and Philadelphia) deep into games with almost no receiver help, and calls him a trade target: 'I'm pushing the chips in.' Waldman: 'I'm totally sold' — Young is far more comfortable in the pocket, reads blitz disguises and now looks confident in his answer, which is the 'Alabama Bryce' anticipation Waldman used to doubt was repeatable. Angelo credits growing confidence as a playmaker with his legs, not as a runner: both compare him to [[Patrick Mahomes]] and [[Russell Wilson]] as a mover who buys time, but better than Wilson at on-schedule play. Both want a weapon added — [[Tetairoa McMillan]] or a veteran like [[Tee Higgins]].
- 2024-12-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-17]]): Young's improvement is real and should be celebrated — 'the bar was in hell' and he has cleared it by a lot, going from the worst quarterback in the NFL in years to roughly league-average play, which Harmon says you can walk into 2025 with and feel okay about. He is explicit that this is far from franchise-cornerstone play, and he and James Koh (not a tracked expert) both push back hard on takes that Young has a higher ceiling than [[Caleb Williams]] or that Carolina fleeced Chicago. Harmon flags the second-contract trap ('we don't want to Daniel Jones ourself'), notes Young still took too many sacks, fumbled and threw picks against Dallas under heavy Micah Parsons pressure, but credits his confidence. He also argues Young took off once [[Diontae Johnson]] was traded, admitting he got that offseason fit wrong — the skill set was right, the personality was the opposite of what Young needed.
- 2024-12-19 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys co-host on RSP Film and Theory, NOT a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-19 (NFL QB Lunacy)]]): Young is the live counter-example to the claim that benching ruins a young quarterback — 'how has Bryce Young looked ever since his career was ended by his benching?' The dude needed a break and it was good for him; Harstad still isn't sure Young is the long-term answer, but says the bet looks a lot more alive now than it did before Carolina sat him down. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees and later uses Young as his benchmark for [[Michael Penix Jr.]]: if Penix shows the kind of line-of-scrimmage adjustments Young made post-benching against Steve Spagnuolo's defense, that plus Penix's physical tools would be a genuinely exciting sign.
- 2024-12-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-23]]): he 'feels it' on Young becoming one of the smartest pre-snap quarterbacks in the NFL within two years. Dave Canales committed to Young after a four-turnover game against Dallas, and Waldman points anyone skeptical to the Kansas City tape, where Young was ahead of Steve Spagnuolo's defense — one of the trickiest in the league — at almost every turn, and to the Arizona game, including a touchdown to [[David Moore]] against a blitz where Young ferreted out the pressure, decided quickly and threw with pinpoint accuracy. Waldman says he makes pre-snap and post-snap decisions look easy and will only get better. Bob Harris (untracked co-host) countered that until the Chiefs game all he saw was the training-camp photo of Young looking like a six-year-old behind that line, scared for a season and a quarter. Waldman's read: Young stopped overthinking and started trusting himself, and Canales — after [[Geno Smith]] and [[Baker Mayfield]] — plus a fortified interior line and a real ground game deserve credit.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'feel' Young over [[Anthony Richardson]] at Underdog QB22 — the Chiefs game was the turning point that convinced Waldman 'this dude's for real' and that he has gotten back to being for real. Untracked co-host Bob Harris agreed completely on the diagnosis, saying in that game Young handled pressure and was the fast processor making quick, smart decisions and getting the ball out that Carolina claimed it drafted — everything you heard about him coming into the draft — but Harris nonetheless 'fucked' Young to stay in on Richardson. Both said they would be fine pairing Young and Richardson as QB1/QB2 at these prices, which Harris used as an argument for late-round quarterback.
- 2025-06-25 — According to guest Cory Kinnan (Reception Perception QB charting, NOT a tracked expert) with [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-25]]): Kinnan grants clear improvement to passable quarterback play by the end of 2024 but will not bet on Young because the arm talent remains a serious question — he hit some honey-hole shots to [[Ja'Tavion Sanders]] but cannot drive the football. He does not see Young as a top-15 NFL quarterback, and while an offense can be constructed around deficiencies the way San Francisco did for [[Brock Purdy]], Carolina lacks the [[Deebo Samuel]] layup piece and the [[Brandon Aiyuk]] efficiency, so they must ride rookie [[Tetairoa McMillan]] — a big ask. Kinnan says Young must be near-perfect mentally, closer to a Joe Burrow standard, and must play anticipatory. Both hosts credit the benching as the right move and like year two with Dave Canales; Harmon praises Young's post-benching arrogance, rip-cord timing and off-script creation, and argues Young works the middle of the field better than any short quarterback in the league — processing and literally seeing it — a view Kinnan endorses, contrasting it with the Russell Wilson middle-of-field meme. Harmon's framing: the floor of non-functional is cleared, the ceiling is now the open question.
