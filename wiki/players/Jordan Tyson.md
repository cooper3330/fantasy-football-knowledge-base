# Jordan Tyson

- 2026-02-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-24]]): Harmon wouldn't personally take Tyson in the top 10 of the 2026 class even though some public rankings have him as the top WR prospect. He notes some evaluators compare Tyson to Jerry Jeudy as a route runner (oversells routes, lacks physicality), but Harmon says Tyson tracks and plays the ball in the air much better than Jeudy does. Harmon places him in his second tier of prospects, alongside Denzel Boston and KC Concepcion, behind top-tier Carnell Tate and Makai Lemon.
- 2026-03-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-03]]): Harmon flagged that Jordan Tyson's publicized bench press reps looked technically questionable (his backside was visibly off the bench on several reps, which should void the count), and noted he picked up 'interesting injury information' on Tyson over combine week without detailing specifics; Tyson otherwise did not test further. He's still among the names some evaluators slot near the top of the receiver class alongside Carnell Tate and Makai Lemon.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-05]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman named [[Jordan Tyson]] one of his riskiest 2026 receiver prospects, detailing a multi-ligament ACL/MCL/PCL knee injury in 2022 that limited him to 9 games, a 2023 season cut to 3 games by surgery complications, 2024 clavicle surgery, and a 2025 hamstring strain that also kept him from working out at the Combine -- missing over a third of his college games. He also flagged a tendency for defensive backs to out-physical Tyson at the top of routes despite a strong 25-plus-rep bench press, and praised his above-the-rim, contested-catch ability, comping him to Allen Robinson II with inside-out versatility. But Waldman was surprised Tyson graded 'a near zero' after the catch given his gaudy college numbers and in-air athleticism, and placed him in his third tier, below top-tier receivers [[Carnell Tate]] and Makai Lemon, rather than the elite-starter class, noting stars are almost always at least above-serviceable after the catch (citing Ja'Marr Chase, [[Puka Nacua]], [[Justin Jefferson]], [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] and [[Cooper Kupp]] as the bar Tyson doesn't clear). He's currently Waldman's WR3-4, projected as an eventual NFL starter, but with a lower floor than the tape alone suggests because of durability and route-running 'wonkiness'; Waldman compared his likely NFL outcome to a [[Garrett Wilson]]-type -- good for stretches, not consistently dominant.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Tyson projects as an aspiring Allen Robinson-type talent, but his injury history is a real red flag for a first-round rookie investment -- an ACL tear around age 19-20, plus a separate non-contact collarbone injury and a Grade 2 hamstring strain this past year that took 12-plus weeks to heal versus a normal 6-to-8-week recovery. Waldman said if he were an NFL team spending a mid-first-round pick, he'd want confidence the player is durable enough to be with him for at least two contracts, and Tyson doesn't yet give him that certainty the way [[Makai Lemon]] does. Angelo agreed the ceiling is real but conditional on health, saying 'if he stays injury-free and develops, yeah, he's going to be dummy-proof too.'
- 2026-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-26]]): Jordan Tyson (6'2', 203 lbs, Arizona State) enters the draft having done zero on-field testing because of a hamstring injury suffered in November 2025 that still hadn't resolved by late March; he has also never finished a full college season, with a torn ACL/MCL/PCL in 2022 that bled into 2023, a broken clavicle in 2025 and recurring hamstring issues. Harmon compares the no-testing scenario to Marvin Harrison Jr. and Corey Davis (both went top-5 without workouts) but says the hamstring worries him more than the clavicle, which could be a freak accident. RP charting was largely poor: 65% success on slant routes, 67.7% vs. man coverage (lowest of any 2026 first-round-projected WR charted), 60% success vs. press (30th percentile) - but an elite ~86% success rate vs. zone coverage, trailing since 2021 only Travis Hunter, Garrett Wilson, Jahan Dotson and Drake London. Harmon rates his 2024 film clearly better than 2025 (partly attributing the drop-off to the hamstring) and flags 11th-percentile arm length as a factor in his press-coverage struggles. He disagrees with unnamed 'talent evaluators' who call Tyson better than [[Carnell Tate]] if healthy, and rejects a Terry McLaurin comp raised on the show, instead landing on Amari Cooper: loose, fluid, capable of spectacular peak routes but inconsistent, projecting best as a movement Z/intermediate route-runner (digs, outs, crossers, posts) rather than a true X. He names three teams to avoid for Tyson given injury-management track records: 49ers, Ravens, Chargers. JJ Zachariason (not tracked) reportedly ranks Tyson his #1 WR prospect in his 2026 draft guide on strong age-adjusted production; Harmon calls Tyson a boom-or-bust profile given the medical red flags, with real questions about physicality beyond the injuries.
- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Tyson has all the release and separation tools of a true route runner and, long-term, has a case as the best prospect in this class -- but too often delivers the right move so fast it has zero effect on the defender, lacking the 'patience and suddenness' contrast (a slower setup followed by a sudden break) that bait-and-react technicians like Devante Adams, [[Ja'Marr Chase]], [[Justin Jefferson]] and [[Malik Nabers]] use. He does show it at times and succeeds when he does, which is promising, but Waldman says mastering it consistently typically takes a prospect two to three years in the league (it took Adams that long). Compared directly to [[Carnell Tate]] as a route runner today, Tyson 'is just not going to score as high, at least not at this time.'
- 2026-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-30]]) [Dynasty]: asked if a healthy Jordan Tyson (currently sitting out on-field pre-draft workouts) would still be overrated, Waldman says yes -- he's feeling it. Waldman thinks Tyson could eventually become a very good WR1 in the league, but he's not in the RSP's tier one right now because his tier-one value 'suffered a death by a thousand paper cuts': he shows an excessive number of flashy releases and moves, but consistently delivers them too quickly for a defender's brain to actually register and react to, so the move doesn't functionally count even though it looks good on tape -- a common young-receiver mistake of lacking contrast between patience and suddenness. Catching technique is also inconsistent. Waldman says fixing that sequencing issue would unlock the ceiling that makes people excited about him.
- 2026-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: both hosts arrived independently at nearly identical conclusions -- there's 'not enough depth of talent yet' for a WR1 grade, but the ceiling is exactly that. Waldman: Tyson's setup moves are so sudden the defender never registers them (so he isn't actually baiting anyone), and he's slow to locate/track the ball downfield, showing up as late, off-balance high-point attempts NFL corners will punish more than college ones did. He's coming off an ACL tear plus lingering hamstring issues, and his 2024 tape graded out cleaner than 2025, raising real durability questions. Waldman's ceiling comp is an Amari Cooper-level hot-and-cold player, or best case a rawer, more athletically gifted Chris Godwin; Harmon bucket-compares him to Luther Burden as a high-ceiling, detail-needy prospect who needs a demanding coaching staff (Waldman noted his final Missouri tape got 'loosey-goosey'). Both say they'd only draft him after Concepcion among this class's WRs, and both expect their views to firm up once he lands with a team.
- 2026-04-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-20]]) [Dynasty]: Reacting to Jordan Tyson's viral pro-day workout clips (dubbed 'human joystick'), Waldman said the tape shows real movement skill but is uncovered practice tape, not game tape, so it mostly confirms he is healthy and has refined his moves rather than revealing anything new about how he processes tight man coverage. He raised a specific injury-mechanism concern: watching Tyson land flat-footed and load onto his heel, citing movement-coach and injury-specialist framing that some of what he's doing tests the physical limits of tendons and ligaments regardless of conditioning. Waldman said he tries to stay 'injury agnostic' but still flags this as real risk, and that he remains lower on Tyson than the field, which he feels is going 'gaga' over the workout videos. He compared Tyson to a Ford race car before Ken Miles rebuilt it -- huge talent, potential WR1, but 'more flash than substance right now,' with the swing factor being off-field work to change how he absorbs contact forces. He also called some of the leaping/hurdle plays on tape unnecessary 'Hollywood' that don't reflect real evaluation.
- 2026-04-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-28]]): surprised Tyson went top-10 at pick 8 to New Orleans given his injury history (2022 ACL/MCL/PCL tear, 2024 broken clavicle, 2025 hamstring injury that kept him out of all pre-draft testing) and an imperfect scouting profile -- he had a couple of receivers rated ahead of Tyson pre-draft. Harmon charted a blended 2024/2025 sample because of the injuries; 2024 results vs. man/press were better than 2025, leaving open whether Tyson regresses or was simply banged up -- 'no idea from a medical standpoint.' Despite the risk, Harmon is bumping Tyson 'much higher' in his post-draft dynasty rankings than his pre-draft scouting rank because of scheme fit: New Orleans posted a 30-percentage-point year-over-year increase in 3+ WR snaps (3rd in the NFL), ranked 8th in motion rate and 1st in pre-snap motion rate last season, which should mask Tyson's weak press-coverage scores (30th percentile) via free releases rather than lining him up as an X. His real strength: 85.6% success rate vs. zone (best in this class, 89th percentile, one of the best marks Harmon has ever charted for a Power Five receiver, trailing only Travis Hunter, Garrett Wilson, Jahan Dotson and Drake London since 2021), plus strong digs (78%) and out routes (86% on an 11% route share). Comps him to Amari Cooper under Kellen Moore, projecting Tyson as a Z who thrives off motion rather than press-beating X reps, with [[Chris Olave]] handling man coverage and Tyson zone coverage. Also flags [[Bryce Lance]] and Jalen Polk as adding to a deep, high-upside young receiver room behind Olave.
- 2026-07-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and untracked guest Jim McCormick ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: McCormick, who studied Tyson closely as an Eagles draft follower, calls him the 'Humpty Dumpty of prospects' given a lengthy injury history -- a hamstring issue that kept him from testing at all pre-draft, plus a prior knee reconstruction, broken collarbone, and ankle problem -- yet he still went in the first round, and McCormick notes recent early-round rookie skill players have generally paid off. Both agree the only camp signal worth tracking is pure availability, not opportunity, since Tyson's draft capital already answers the opportunity question; a clean attendance report is bullish, while any soft-tissue news (hamstring, hip, knee) should be treated as a real red flag given his history, which McCormick likens to Alshon Jeffery's hamstring-plagued career. Harris personally won't rank Tyson in range to draft him, preferring a safer known WR2/3 with more proven floor over a rookie's theoretical ceiling, stressing that's a philosophy difference, not a claim Tyson can't hit.
- 2026-07-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-10]]): Harris says he's 'scared to death' of Jordan Tyson given a lengthy injury history and admits he doesn't know much about him, but doesn't think it's unreasonable for someone to take a late flier on Tyson over a [[Jakobi Meyers]]-type floor option, calling it low-risk as long as he stays healthy -- unlike reaching for him ahead of a clearly established player like [[A.J. Brown]].
- 2026-08-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-03 (Feel It or Fk It with Ian Hartitz)]]) [Dynasty]: he graded Tyson as 'this close' to an instant-starter grade coming out, and if put in the right situation projects strong production; his top-end comp for Tyson is [[Chris Godwin]] ('a twitchier Chris Godwin,' one of Waldman's favorite players of the past 15 years). The caveat is recurring small technical leaks -- route details, catch-point issues -- that separate a quickly-established starter from a talent teams wait three or four years on, plus durability risk given how silky and high-twitch his movement is; Waldman compared the risk profile (not the skill set) to Ricky Pearsall and, more pointedly, to Christian Watson's 'big paws, leaky game' path. Ian Hartitz (untracked co-host) had Tyson as his WR1 in this class over comps like Deion Burks and Amari Cooper, citing standout route feel despite acceleration questions.
- 2026-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-06]]): Harmon flagged rookie Tyson as having posted the best success rate versus zone coverage in this year's wide receiver class, and one of the top five marks among Power Four prospects charted since 2021, along with strong grades on out and dig routes. He floated a Tyson/[[Chris Olave]] split where Olave -- a strong man-coverage performer -- is the 'man coverage answer' while Tyson becomes the zone-beating complement in New Orleans's 2026 offense with QB Tyler Shough.
- 2026-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-14]]): a day after a mock draft in which Harris and mock-draft partner Jake Trowbridge (not tracked) both admitted 'we don't really know what he is,' Tyson left Saints practice on a Thursday with a hamstring injury -- a continuation of the hamstring problems that kept him from working out through the pre-draft process. The team called it not significant and has not ruled him out multiple weeks, and Harris says he's waiting a day or two for more clarity, but expects to lower Tyson somewhat in his rankings given the injury is a repeat issue rather than a new one.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-17]]): Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) called Tyson's lingering hamstring injury, which could carry into the regular season, a 'landmine' this year. Waldman agreed emphatically, saying he's 'already exploded' and is 'dodging the shrapnel.' He explained Tyson's dynamic movement is rooted in erratic, non-uniform mechanics rather than sound technique -- the distance his legs and shoulders move away from his frame, and the violence he generates without being balanced through it, both create injury risk and cause him to blow route timing or miss simpler plays he should make. Waldman said Tyson can look like the best receiver in this class moment-to-moment because the movement is genuinely impressive and creative, but called it unsustainable as currently built and said he needs real technical refinement, not just more flash.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]): Tyson pulled a hamstring and is now seeing specialists, dropping him in Harris's ranks with more news still expected. Per [[Matt Waldman]] in the same episode [Dynasty]: Tyson is 'a landmine' -- unbelievably talented with dynamic but aggressive movement, and that same aggressive style is what keeps getting him hurt and makes his route-running and catching process unpredictable. Waldman says some reps look like the best receiver in the class and others look like he needs to fix fundamentals or he'll never stay on the field. Both hosts agree the hope of Tyson stepping into a fantasy-starter WR2 role for New Orleans is 'out the window' to start the year given the hamstring and a crowded receiver room.
- 2026-08-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: after the Saints downplayed a hamstring injury as 'not super serious' and insisted it had nothing to do with Tyson's history of recurring hamstring trouble, Harris says the injury is in fact serious -- Tyson is out for at least two months and is now 'borderline undraftable' in redraft leagues given the pattern of recurring hamstring issues.
