# Austin Ekeler

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: sees a possible 2024 rebound after a down year but is explicit he wouldn't personally invest in it — "I'm not sure I want to invest in it" — with price being the deciding factor either way. Notes Ekeler himself has publicly acknowledged (on his own show, alongside Matt Harmon) that his window is closing, which Waldman reads as a sign he may be closer to the end of his relevance than the industry fully appreciates.
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): floated as a potential 2024 free agent, cited (with a joking aside about asking [[Matt Harmon]] for a raise) as the running back at the very top of the list of backs who understand how the league is undervaluing the position — logged here as market/leverage commentary rather than a fresh skill grade.

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]): grouped with [[Tony Pollard]] and [[Travis Kelce]] — reads the trio's down 2023 as more likely injury-related than skill-related, though for Ekeler (and Kelce) specifically flags it may also be age-related, given the cumulative toll of NFL workload, rather than purely a scheme or opportunity issue.

- 2024-03-01 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host on *RSP Film and Theory* — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-01]] [Dynasty]: ranks Ekeler second of the "Big Four" 2024 free-agent backs (behind [[Josh Jacobs]]), reasoning that receiving work is so disproportionately valuable in fantasy that "he doesn't need to be" 2022 Ekeler to still be valuable, both for an NFL offense and for fantasy purposes.
- 2024-03-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode [Dynasty]: groups Ekeler with [[Tony Pollard]] as "Column A" — the receiving-back tier of the free-agent class, as distinct from the bell-cow "Column B" of [[Josh Jacobs]] and [[Derrick Henry]] — and says an ideal roster build takes one back from each column.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: "hates" Ekeler's move to Washington from a fantasy standpoint given how good [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] looked in 2023 — reads Ekeler's cheap two-year, $11M deal ($5M year one, up to $1.5M in likely-unreachable incentives) as a market signal he's viewed as a complementary piece (a McKissick-type role) rather than a true lead back, and expects the split to be "disappointing for both players unless one gets hurt."
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: Waldman takes Ekeler over [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] in a feel-it-or-f-it pairing despite Ekeler's price having 'plummeted' to RB40 -- earlier in the best ball season he was going well ahead of Robinson -- arguing he hasn't fully declined and may never have recovered from an early ankle injury that masked his real level last year. Bob disagrees, taking Robinson instead, calling this the pairing he 'feels like I'm making the wrong pick on' even as he sticks with Ekeler.
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Debating who's underrated between Austin Ekeler and [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] in Washington, Bob Harris (untracked) took the pro-Ekeler side, arguing there's still plenty left in the tank after an injury-limited season and admitting his own bias toward older backs. Waldman disagreed, saying he does not think Ekeler can reclaim the lead-back role over Robinson, though his own rankings still have Ekeler slightly ahead overall -- a gap that has closed sharply since the signing, when Ekeler was priced well above Robinson early in best ball drafts. Both expect Ekeler to keep passing-down and two-minute duties. Waldman also raised lingering doubt about whether Ekeler is fully himself after last year's high-ankle sprain, grouping him with [[Kyle Pitts]] and [[Tony Pollard]] as players who may not have fully healed from a significant injury.
- 2024-07-16 — According to James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-16]]): Koh called Washington's signing of Ekeler 'a smart decision' to bolster the passing-down group, expecting him to work as a receiving-back complement to early-down runner [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] and create matchup problems out of the backfield. [[Matt Harmon]] did not push back on the take. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): the injuries have neutered Ekeler's ability as an accelerator — early in his career he separated from backers and safeties without trouble, and Angelo does not believe those gears are there anymore. [[Matt Waldman]] had floated Ekeler as a possible slot weapon if [[Terry McLaurin]]'s holdout drags, with [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] or [[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]] in the backfield, on the grounds that Ekeler is a good intermediate route runner who tracks the ball well; Angelo's speed objection is the counterpoint. Angelo also said Croskey-Merritt is the most talented back in the room and it is not close at this stage of Ekeler's career [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]]): Ekeler is Harris's number one late-round player he keeps drafting, around pick 133, explicitly as the counter-thought to the [[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]] hype. Harris concedes concussion-related risk after how Ekeler's season ended and admits Washington may hand everything to Croskey-Merritt, in which case he will look foolish and survive; his base case is a mishmash with fairly obvious roles for Ekeler, Croskey-Merritt and [[Chris Rodriguez Jr.]]. He notes that in the limited healthy time last year Ekeler still made big plays and looked roughly like himself, and the appeal is largest in full PPR. Guest JJ Zachariason (not tracked) called him the quintessential value play, citing previous-season yards per route run as a predictor of future target share and saying Ekeler graded impressively there, with neither Rodriguez nor Croskey-Merritt able to catch passes.
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]): Waldman says Ekeler is 'not dead yet' and that his role in Washington will be consistent, pointing to double-digit fantasy points in eight of the 12 games he appeared in last year as evidence there is still upside in the profile — even as he expects the broader Washington backfield, including [[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]] and [[Chris Rodriguez Jr.]], to shift seismically before the season settles.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Going Deep Rookie Receivers and RB Depth Charts)]]): Ekeler is the Washington back he expects to see most of the work in 2025, with [[Chris Rodriguez Jr.]] taking short yardage and the low red zone and [[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]] behind them — despite Croskey-Merritt's much higher draft price. [[Matt Waldman]] treated Ekeler's health as the hinge of the whole backfield, saying it is very easy for Croskey-Merritt to leapfrog Rodriguez if Ekeler gets hurt, and adding that [[Deebo Samuel]] will siphon some rushing work regardless. Neither host discussed Ekeler's own efficiency or receiving projection in detail.
