# Michael Carter

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]: calls Carter "a pretty damn good back" who hasn't gotten the opportunities he deserves behind [[James Connor]], expecting him to cut into Connor's 2024 workload — though both hosts agree probably not as much as some expect.
- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode: notes Carter shared a college backfield with Javonte Williams at North Carolina, where both were 1,000-yard rushers, and argues Carter's receiving and route-running skills specifically have not been leveraged at the level they deserve in Arizona. Expects a real timeshare with Connor in 2024, with standalone fantasy value possible even in a Connor-healthy scenario.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman argued [[Michael Carter]] -- an excellent receiving back who can block and is 'better between the tackles than you'd expect' -- may have more real value than [[Trey Benson]] behind [[James Conner]] in Arizona, since Benson's weaknesses (route running, pass protection, decision-making) are areas Carter already handles well. He pointed to Carter's track record as a productive complement in a shared backfield with Javonte Williams (each topping 1,000 receiving yards in a final college season) and called Carter 'basically free money' as a late-round hedge or an early-season waiver pickup once his role clarifies, predicting he'd out-snap Benson if Benson struggles in pass protection.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris is openly skeptical of Carter as the [[Trey Benson]] replacement — he has watched Carter get chances to become a regular NFL back since 2021 and has never seen him come close to winning over a coaching staff, invoking the 2022 Jets pairing of Carter and Zonovan Knight after [[Breece Hall]]'s knee injury as 'the worst duo I can remember from the last five years.' He ranked Carter and [[Emari Demercado]] side by side in pure emergency territory and would prefer to wait a week to see the split in a good matchup against the Titans, while acknowledging bye weeks deny many managers that luxury. He allowed that Carter could start performing like a starter, but said he has his doubts.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-06]]): Waldman is 'feeling it' on Carter as the lead back in Arizona, even though [[Emari Demercado]] was millimeters from outscoring him on the day. Carter had the most touches and was clearly the primary back — Waldman rates him the better receiver of the two and the more creative runner. He had wondered during the week whether Demercado would be the guy, since Carter had been inactive early in the year while Demercado handled special teams, but concluded the first-team practice reps were about accelerating Carter's timing in the offense after a long layoff. Waldman would bank on Carter first while accepting occasional hot-hand usage, and still sees Demercado as a desperation-flex body with enough touches to matter in an emergency. Bob Harris (not tracked) noted Carter was the clear early-down back and got two-minute work per Nathan Jahnke of PFF, and that Carter's practice-squad status last year probably owed more to roster construction and special-teams needs than to the staff's opinion of him.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with [[James Conner]] out for the year and [[Trey Benson]] out for a chunk of games after a knee scope, the Cardinals backfield 'turned out not really even to be much of a contest' — no hot hand, Carter was the starter, played okay, powered in the goal-line touchdown after [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] was ruled down at the one, appeared shaken up in the fourth quarter but returned. Harris: 'we know that Carter comes first in the pecking order.' Guest Brandon Funston of The Athletic (not tracked) preferred Carter (39% rostered) to [[Kenneth Gainwell]] (37%) despite disliking the archetype — both are small guys who run like big guys, 'minds write checks their bodies can't cash', not really explosive. Harris agreed Carter is the add while joking about his Jets-era resistance to ranking him.
