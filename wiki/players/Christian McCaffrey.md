# Christian McCaffrey

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]): unqualified RB1 overall for 2024 — asked whether there was any other answer, both hosts said no. Waldman explicitly dismissed the age concern: "I don't care if he's going to turn 28. He's the top one." See [[Aging Curves and Career Longevity]] for the contrasting age-curve argument from four days earlier.
- 2023-12-18 — **Matt Waldman**, same episode, on why: re-read his own 2022 write-up on McCaffrey's arrival in San Francisco. Before the trade (2022, six games in, 3–3) the 49ers ranked 19th in points per game (20.3), 18th in yards per game (340.2), with EPA of −0.05. After his arrival they ranked 2nd in scoring (29.8), 4th in yards (379.6), and 2nd in EPA. Final six weeks: 101 carries for 505 yards and 4 TDs plus 27 catches for 262 yards and 3 TDs; 22.2 PPR points per game over his final 10 games including five outings of 25+. Waldman's framing of the running-backs-don't-matter debate: "what are you looking for when you're drafting early? Leverage over the field. That's leverage." His conclusion — nothing about that profile has changed.
- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]] (recorded before the *Film and Theory* episode below, which cites it): the origin of the whole framework. What McCaffrey shares with [[Lamar Jackson]], [[James Cook]] and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] is control of his own pace. "He's not overly big, he's not blazingly fast — he's a very fast guy but he's not Chris Johnson. What he can do is control the pace and tempo of his runs to set up blocks and get an advantage on the defender's angle, and do that down in and down out, because he's trying to make the given play successful." Angelo calls him already "the engine of the 49ers," and argues that an NFL linebacker asked who is harder to tackle — [[Derrick Henry]] in his prime or McCaffrey — would not obviously say Henry: "I have to deal with Christian McCaffrey's feet." Framework: [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]].
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): supplies the film mechanism behind the valuation. McCaffrey heads the group of backs who navigate "really tight creases at a high rate of speed" and change direction in ways bigger backs cannot — a trait Waldman argues has a weight ceiling ("if you made them 205, 210, 215 pounds they probably wouldn't be able to sustain that kind of movement"). Contrast class is the space-expensive jump-cut runner ([[Saquon Barkley]]).
- 2023-12-21 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode: places McCaffrey (with [[Austin Ekeler]]) as the archetype created by a decade of rising passing efficiency to running backs, rather than as an individual anomaly — demand for the type has been proliferating and, as of December 2023, is not receding. See [[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]].
- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: reaffirms McCaffrey as his 2024 #1-overall pick in a three-way debate against [[CeeDee Lamb]] and [[Tyreek Hill]] — "the workload, the role, the supporting cast" — and repeats that McCaffrey has not had many 200-plus-carry seasons, i.e., has not yet been overused. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) takes CeeDee Lamb instead, with Tyreek Hill as his personal #2 behind McCaffrey.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): expects the Divisional Round game at Green Bay to hinge on the run game — the Packers are a bottom-five rushing-success-rate defense and were "begging" Dallas to run in the Wild Card round — a clean matchup for McCaffrey (and [[Aaron Jones]] on the other side of the ball).
- 2024-01-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-25]]): even against a genuinely strong Detroit run defense (one of the toughest in the NFL since Week 10), "you're not stopping that train" — expects a big chunk-play run in the NFC Championship regardless, on the reasoning that the mere threat of McCaffrey should pull an extra defender into the box and open up Brandon Aiyuk over the top. Separately, both hosts frame him as a sentimental Super Bowl pick — an all-time-great, future Hall of Famer who has never reached one.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): the prediction plays out — 130+ yards from scrimmage against Detroit's tough run defense in the NFC Championship win. Framed as evidence the McCaffrey-driven offense reduces pressure on [[Brock Purdy]] regardless of matchup difficulty: "it doesn't matter what the defense does, Christian McCaffrey is going to get his."
- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]), on Super Bowl LVIII: pushes back on postgame "Shanahan abandoned the run" criticism as results-driven hindsight — McCaffrey still touched the ball 30 times (22 carries for 80 yards, plus 8-for-8 receiving for 80 yards and a touchdown), and Kansas City's blitz-heavy game plan was designed specifically to make Brock Purdy throw rather than to bait San Francisco away from the run. Separately, in the offseason-outlook segment, flags McCaffrey as "entering the era of, the kind of window of running backs where you start to get really nervous" once the calendar turns to 2025 — the first hint of an age-related caveat on him from this show. See [[Aging Curves and Career Longevity]].
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): reacting to McCaffrey's new contract, Waldman said if any back deserves to be locked in atop the ranks it's him, crediting the Shanahan-tree usage (22 opportunities/game in 2023, leading the league in yards from scrimmage) and projects him near 2,000 total yards again. Comparing him talent-wise to [[Bijan Robinson]], Waldman called McCaffrey's footwork and processing 'slightly better' but framed the gap as 99.9th vs. 99th percentile rather than a real talent gulf, with Bijan's physicality compensating for the difference. Asked if anyone would go ahead of McCaffrey with a true first pick, Waldman said 'maybe' and named [[CeeDee Lamb]] specifically, estimating he'd take Lamb over McCaffrey roughly 40% of the time across hypothetical drafts.
- 2024-07-01 — According to Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Best Ball]: Bob reports McCaffrey has gone RB1 overall in nearly every one of the 'couple hundred' best ball drafts he's done since February, drawing a cautionary parallel to Jonathan Taylor's consensus RB1 status a few years back that didn't hold. He flags Kyle Shanahan's comment about wanting to 'protect McCaffrey from himself' with other backs getting run, but is skeptical the 49ers will actually cut his role once games matter. Waldman agrees McCaffrey is still worth the price even in a reduced role -- 'player and a half' value at replacement cost even down to five-eighths or seven-eighths of a workload -- because San Francisco's backup pecking order is unresolved: Elijah Mitchell can't stay healthy, rookie Isaac Guerendo may not pass-block or know the system well enough, and Jordan Mason also has injury history. Waldman says if McCaffrey's role shrinks, he'd expect Mitchell over Guerendo or Trey Sermon to be the primary beneficiary.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman rejected the idea that the 49ers offense is in trouble, but agreed the red zone is the real problem and that McCaffrey's absence is a plausible cause — the compressed-field passing avenues they used to score with are gone. He does think a Week 10 return could turn the red zone around, because defenses have to respect McCaffrey in a compressed area. The heavy caveat is the ramp-up: 'I don't know that he's going to be back Week 10 — nobody knows that... we don't know what his role will be when he comes back or how up to speed he'll be. Remember, we've seen players at wide receiver who didn't get a full month of August take a little bit of time to get up to speed. Christian McCaffrey hasn't been on a field since July.' He warned against hoping he is an immediate league-winning asset. He also tied San Francisco's red-zone struggles to personnel churn — [[Brandon Aiyuk]] lost, [[Deebo Samuel]] limited to three snaps, Jauan Jennings due back — since the red zone is the hardest area to be on the same page in.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): McCaffrey took 13 of 15 carries in his first game back, which Waldman says settles the split question — 'the toothpaste is already out of the tube.' He would have preferred an easier ramp but concedes McCaffrey was ready and looked good, and as an old-school running back guy he says give it to McCaffrey and let him work back in rather than rotate two players who then never get warm or into a rhythm. His historical framing: if Marshall Faulk came back from injury and Steven Jackson were a year or two into the league, you give the ball to Faulk. Waldman notes the Buccaneers bottled McCaffrey up as a runner but not as a receiver, and that the receiving work is what makes him 'a player and a half' for fantasy purposes — last year he was roughly the RB5 point total above that line. Bob Harris (not tracked) notes Kyle Shanahan keeps saying he wants to use him less and then doesn't, en route to 338 touches.
- 2025-01-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-27]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'fuck it' on a McCaffrey rebound season — and Waldman says it pains him because of how hard McCaffrey has worked. The reasoning is not simply age 29: it is that the cliff comes fast once a nagging Achilles issue is involved (he nods to [[Adam Harstad]], not a tracked expert, on aging generally), and that NFL teams prefer to move on a year early rather than a year late. He expects San Francisco to consider using him more sparingly, to add a back from what he calls a rich [[2025 NFL Draft]] class, and notes [[Jordan Mason]] is a free agent while [[Isaac Guerendo]] is unproven. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) noted Dave Ploogie's way-too-early rankings have McCaffrey in the second round; both said they would pass there but Harris would take the upside shot in the third where the risk can be mitigated. Waldman's summary: 'I don't think he's ever going to be that same guy' — a dragster only goes up and down the strip so many times.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]) [Best Ball]: feeling it on McCaffrey at RB5, going at the tail end of the first round after a four-game 2024 (202 rushing yards, no touchdowns) versus the 24-plus points per game season before it. Waldman's case is that McCaffrey is practicing without restriction, which suggests the Achilles is not much of a problem, and that he has rebounded from difficult injuries before and performed. He remains the main cog in the San Francisco offense, especially with [[Jordan Mason]] traded away and only [[Jordan James]] and [[Isaac Guerendo]] behind him. The caveat Waldman names is age: the cliff can come very fast. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) and Waldman both said they would rather have [[Derrick Henry]], who is going about a pick later, but neither would feel bad landing McCaffrey; Harris added that even a managed workload in that offense is still a massive workload.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): McCaffrey is his overall RB1 and he is not scared at a 12th-overall ADP, conditional on the Achilles being genuinely behind him — no calf, ankle or Achilles talk out of minicamp. He projects close to 260 attempts for about 1,300 yards and 10 to 11 touchdowns plus close to 70 receptions for 900 yards and five scores, which he considers enough to be the best back in fantasy at age 29. The supporting case is workload by elimination: [[Isaac Guerendo]] and Jordan James can take some plays, but San Francisco loses Deebo Samuel, expects to be without [[Brandon Aiyuk]] for a good part of the year or ineffective while he works back from the ACL, and has only [[Jauan Jennings]] and DeMarcus Robinson proven outside him — behind a still-good offensive line. Waldman frames the bet as being a year early last time rather than wrong.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): McCaffrey's usage has to be up, because [[Brandon Aiyuk]] is out an extended stretch coming back from the ACL, [[Jauan Jennings]] is dealing with a calf and [[Ricky Pearsall]] has been perpetually banged up with hamstrings dating to college — the offense severely needs his presence and he will simply touch the ball a lot. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed and argued the market is fading him on age when he is no more likely to be injured than anyone else absorbing that touch load. Waldman put him in his own tier below [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and above everyone else, and said he would not argue with anyone taking McCaffrey over Chase. Both said a late-first pairing of McCaffrey and [[Derrick Henry]] earns a stamp of approval regardless of the next several picks, though Waldman expects that window to close this month [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-18 — According to guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert), on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: McCaffrey was the name Doherty wanted for his No. 1 bust but deliberately did not use, because the case is pure injury and therefore boring — over the past five years McCaffrey has been injured in three and finished as the RB1 or RB2 in the two he was not. Everyone already understands the pick is unbelievably risky and everyone understands exactly why, so discussing it on a podcast adds nothing: he either gets hurt or he does not. Doherty went with [[Kaleb Johnson]] instead.
- 2025-08-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: reading the 2025 Almanac profile, Harris says he will not gloat about 2024 — he had 'given in' and called McCaffrey the no-doubt, no-brainer 1.01 in all non-superflex leagues for the first time in CMC's eight-year career, bought the 49ers' line that the training-camp calf was no big deal 'hook, line and sinker', and had loudly proclaimed in the prior Almanac that McCaffrey had passed the durability test. Instead the pulled calf lasted two months, he returned in Week 10 for three underwhelming fuller-workload games, then sprained the PCL in his knee in the Week 13 Buffalo snow game. Harris says his beef has never been with the ability — 'he's a monster when he's healthy' — but with how coaches use him: McCaffrey has missed 36 games since 2020, is listed at 210 pounds but weighed 202 at the combine, and since 2017 there have been only 25 individual games in which a back other than McCaffrey weighing 202 or less touched it 25+ times, while McCaffrey alone has 26 such games despite missing more than two seasons. He had wondered if CMC was the exception proving the rule about protecting smaller backs from massive workloads; a season later, 'we have our answer. No!' The bull case he grants: a body fresh off only 65 touches, [[Kyle Shanahan]] emboldened to use him like [[Derrick Henry]] again, and a league winner available in the late first or early second — 'if this guy gets hurt, you're screwed, otherwise you'll be thrilled.'
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Dr. Deepak Chona of SportsMedAnalytics (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]]: McCaffrey is a high-risk, high-reward pick his model prices below his top-12-to-15 ADP. On the bilateral Achilles tendinitis, Chona said bilateral presentation implies a compensation pattern, and chronically inflamed tendon can change internally and be more tear-prone — but McCaffrey's case did not look long-standing, the biologic injections (probably in Germany) suggest the tendon remodeled in a relatively healthy way, and so he sees no long-term bump in rupture risk, only a medium added risk. Supporting evidence: in the four games after returning he looked like himself at roughly 19 touches and four receptions per game and four yards per carry with no in-season ramp-up, before slipping on a snowy field and injuring the PCL. Moderate non-surgical PCLs recover well ([[Puka Nacua]], [[Lamar Jackson]] as comps), so Chona thinks McCaffrey can return to his late-2023 performance level. The problem is the summation — Carolina-era soft-tissue history, the tendinitis, plus age about 29 and position group, the two biggest risk factors — which his algorithm puts at twice the average running back injury risk, against a baseline of roughly two games missed. Koh (not tracked) pushed the bear case hard and pegged two to four games missed; Chona agreed the mentality of swinging big is defensible but said at that cost there are too many other good options.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]]): Harris did not move McCaffrey's rank after San Francisco traded for [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] and named Robinson the RB2, while openly conceding that could be a mistake. His base case is that Shanahan's past behavior with McCaffrey points to heavy usage rather than restraint, so he is not pricing in a committee. But he allows the other branch explicitly: it is 'definitely, definitely possible' the 49ers use McCaffrey more judiciously and let Robinson sledgehammer straight up the middle, in which case Robinson steals touchdowns and frustrates McCaffrey drafters. Harris files the question as category two — unknowable coaching intent — rather than pretending to a read.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): Harris took McCaffrey at 11-1 as his bet for the NFL's leading rusher, reasoning that a healthy McCaffrey in the Shanahan system would be priced alongside [[Saquon Barkley]] at roughly plus 375, so 11-1 is pure injury discount — he won the category easily two years ago, and this August he got through camp without a new injury. Harris is explicit that he does not trust him to stay healthy either, which is why McCaffrey is no longer the 1.01 in fantasy drafts, but he remains a first-rounder for the upside. Harris says the [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] acquisition did not lower his McCaffrey projection at all, noting Shanahan just 'beats the hell out of' McCaffrey. Guest Chris Raybon of the Action Network (not a tracked expert) disagreed, passing on McCaffrey on health grounds and taking [[Ashton Jeanty]] at 18-1 instead.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): the sharpest disagreement of the episode. Harris rates McCaffrey an 8 out of 10 on risk — he has been severely injured in half his NFL seasons, so treating his injury risk as equal to [[Ja'Marr Chase]]'s because 'anybody can get hurt playing football' is not good enough, and he thinks McCaffrey is probably the riskiest pick in the whole draft. Guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates him a 3, saying he is thrilled to get McCaffrey at pick seven or later given the steep fall-off after him, that reports say he is 100%, and that you have to trust the medical professionals at some level. Under pushback Bell concedes McCaffrey is his RB3 rather than RB1 precisely because he is a scarier injury risk than [[Saquon Barkley]] or [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], and that with guaranteed health for 17 games McCaffrey would be his overall 1 based on how San Francisco used him in 2023.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: McCaffrey missing Thursday's 49ers practice with a calf strain is 'either the funniest troll job in injury report history or a nightmare from which all of us will never wake up,' and both Harris and guest JEFF ERICKSON (RotoWire, NOT tracked) ranked it their top Week 1 terror. Harris notes the setup was the opposite of last year — all camp reports said McCaffrey was as healthy as he's ever been — so nobody gets to say the camp-injury signal was ignored. He reiterates the position he took with Jeff Bell a week earlier, that McCaffrey's risk factor should still be very high while Bell argued all running backs get hurt and McCaffrey is no riskier. Practical advice: if he's active you have no choice, you start him. Erickson said he moved McCaffrey into the first round but not the top five where a trusted-healthy McCaffrey would sit, and would trade him in a heartbeat for [[Josh Jacobs]] or [[Brian Thomas Jr.]], but would say no on [[Ladd McConkey]]; Harris said he'd do that Jacobs deal too. Neither trusts San Francisco's injury communication — 'why do you have to say calf?'
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): disaster averted. McCaffrey played 54 snaps to 18 for [[Brian Robinson Jr.]], the calf seemed fine with no injury issues, and he produced 142 scrimmage yards, which Harris says you will take. He describes him as a check-down machine and does not think the Niner offense looked super healthy, noting it is not going to be the Niner offense of a few years ago, but McCaffrey can still be really good. Guest **Sam Gold** (not tracked) notes McCaffrey drew around 10 underneath targets largely because injuries left the 49ers able to double [[Ricky Pearsall]].
- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-11]]): McCaffrey is somehow one of the healthiest 49ers, but Harmon called the Week 1 workload 'injury malpractice' given a player coming off multiple injuries and a calf issue that lingered from last season, and said Shanahan 'cannot be trusted, he cannot help himself'. Harmon had said on Twitter before Week 1 that you almost want to sit McCaffrey just to remove the temptation. At the same time he concedes McCaffrey is San Francisco's best offensive player even when everyone is healthy, is critical to both the run and pass game, and with the receiver room decimated should be catching a lot of passes going forward.
- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: McCaffrey looked fine in Week 1, but is already being given midweek days off because everyone, presumably the 49ers included, is terrified of his calf. Harris says that is not a start/sit question at all: McCaffrey was your first-round pick, you are starting him, and the only real Week 2 quandaries in San Francisco are McCaffrey's supporting cast and [[Ricky Pearsall]]. He adds a DFS aside that he benched McCaffrey in the Week 1 contest out of nervousness about the calf and it cost him, since McCaffrey outscored the back he pivoted to. Behind him, Harris thought [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] looked juiceless as the change-of-pace runner.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with Mac Jones filling in, the primary San Francisco plan was to get the ball out fast — throw it two yards to McCaffrey and let him run — and Harris said CMC was tough and good, racking up big yardage and taking the Niners into field-goal range on the winning drive with a longer run. The frustration was touchdown equity: he was stuffed twice at the goal line at 6-6, blown up on a fourth-down goal-line screen, tackled at the one on a fourth-quarter drive, and the one-yard touchdown went to Kyle Juszczyk instead. Harris wants [[Brock Purdy]] back — 'it'd be much better if Brock Purdy returns.'
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-29]]): in a head-to-head with [[Bijan Robinson]], Waldman keeps McCaffrey ahead for [[Redraft (Standard)]] despite a bad game with a fumble and drops and a struggling offensive line — he is still the elite performer, San Francisco has its quarterback returning, and Waldman expects the line to improve rather than deteriorate. He believes the way the 49ers use him and his talents still gives him an edge in-season. For [[Dynasty]] he flips to Bijan on age, giving Bijan four to five more years of runway. Bob Harris (not tracked) thinks Bijan currently looks the more dangerous runner, with McCaffrey still the more heavily used receiver.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: McCaffrey is the only 49ers skill player anyone expected to be available Thursday night in Los Angeles, with [[Brock Purdy]], [[George Kittle]], [[Ricky Pearsall]] and [[Jauan Jennings]] all sitting. Harris joked the 49ers went to a warlock and begged for McCaffrey's health at the cost of cursing every other skill player, and expects a very heavy McCaffrey workload as a result. He is an automatic start; DraftKings listed him at -120 for an anytime touchdown.
- 2025-10-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-03]]): McCaffrey was 'the only actual dude' the 49ers had on Thursday but is not achieving much on the ground — 10 carries for 29 yards in the first quarter and a half, 22 for 57 overall, with a few of those unfairly counted because he was burning the Rams' timeouts deep in 49ers territory when everyone knew the run was coming. Harris cautions that yards per carry is not a predictive stat and should not be treated as one, and blames the struggles with Shanahan's automatic-style runs on the offensive line more than on McCaffrey. The receiving work continues to save him: eight catches under five air yards as a check-down machine, plus a one-yard touchdown catch. Harris's framing is that CMC's only real question is health, 'the warlock's curse has protected him and hurt everyone else,' and for as long as you think he stays upright you just take it.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): CMC had 27 total yards on his first 10 touches and was 'really not looking great for fantasy' before a big screen and run after catch, and he was stuffed on a fourth-and-one pitch left in the red zone. Harris then flags a goal-line sequence for the same Yacht Club audience worrying about [[Bijan Robinson]]: [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] took four straight carries into the red zone, McCaffrey came in for one play and caught an arrow route inside the five, then left immediately and Brian Robinson scored from the one. Harris's verdict — 'is it a trend? It is not... there's nothing actionable in that,' but if you are worried about [[Tyler Allgeier]] you should be worried about this too.
