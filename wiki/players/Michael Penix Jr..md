# Michael Penix Jr.

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Going Deep - Draft Prospects)]]) [Dynasty]: real talent, but both hosts frame his outlook as dominated by his injury history (two ACL tears) rather than his throwing. On tape against Michigan in the national championship, Angelo reads him as "a step too slow" evading pressure off-platform — he anticipates the rush correctly but can no longer plant and redirect his feet in time, a movement cost from the injuries rather than a mental one. Waldman raises Trent Edwards and Carson Strong as the downside case — talented passers whose careers were derailed after taking a physical beating behind bad offensive lines, never regaining what they'd lost. The hosts' preferred outcome comp is a left-handed Geno Smith: less flashy arm talent than his college tape suggested but a "coach on the field" who makes the right read and the right decision, following a Jordan Love-style redshirt runway (see [[Player Development and Coachability]]) rather than starting as a rookie. Both are explicit that sitting behind a veteran for a year or more is the outcome they'd want for him, given the physical toll already on his ledger.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: revises down from the 2024-01-11 framing. "I wanted him to be in a much higher boat," and still has real accuracy and health concerns, though he no longer expects the total-flameout outcome he'd floated as a live risk two weeks earlier (his own Carson Strong comp) — Penix has stopped needing a leg wrap and appears to be moving normally. Lands him at "journeyman starter" tier for now, a downgrade from the earlier Geno-Smith-with-a-Jordan-Love-runway framing, though still describes him as having a real opportunity to prove people wrong.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-01]]) [Dynasty]: still "among my top five" quarterbacks in the class despite the January 22 downgrade, and wants to revisit his decision-making and pocket play specifically. States plainly that the medical evaluation, not the tape, will be the deciding factor on where he actually goes — "if he checks out medically, he's probably a top-20, top-25 pick... I think he'll go in the first round." Notes he didn't look meaningfully worse at Washington than he had at Indiana despite "getting the crap kicked out of him" there.
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]) [Dynasty]: a real upgrade from the February 1 read — now "still stands out to me," explicitly Waldman's top outlier candidate among the 2024 quarterbacks. Directly pushes back on outside evaluators he otherwise respects who say Penix "isn't good in the pocket" or "isn't great under pressure" — says he doesn't yet see what they're seeing, and wants a closer look specifically at his middle-of-field throws and pocket management before finalizing that view. Also floats a landing-spot connection: new Seattle OC Ryan Grubb was Penix's offensive coordinator at Washington, which Waldman flags as a name to watch alongside the already-logged Cliff Kingsbury-to-Washington/Caleb-Williams connection.

- 2024-02-29 — According to **Kevin Knight** (Falcons reporter, SB Nation's Falcoholic — guest, not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]) [Dynasty]: says he "loves" Penix and would be happy to see Atlanta take him if he falls to the second round, calling him a scheme fit for the incoming Rams-style offense, but cautions medical concerns could scare teams off well before Atlanta picks again, and he can't be Atlanta's Plan A at quarterback.

- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]) [Dynasty]: names Minnesota as his preferred landing-spot scenario among those discussed, pairing him with [[Justin Jefferson]], [[Jordan Addison]] and [[T.J. Hockenson]] plus a run game that could let him ease in behind play-action. Says he'd generally rather see Penix sit as a rookie, but Minnesota is the one situation where he'd take the chance on playing him early.

- 2024-03-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-08]]) [Dynasty], solo cast on the full QB class: an efficient mover under pressure who can sidestep or climb and resets his feet well, finding the open man in the middle of the field better than "people characterize" — adds that if Baker Mayfield or [[Kyler Murray]] did this better, both would have had better careers than they've had to this point. Still has concerns about specific middle-of-field throws he got away with at Washington that Waldman doesn't think will work in the NFL, but reads the year-over-year trend as improving — the lapses have gotten "less and less" over time. Floor: "at worst," a low-end starter who can keep his team competitive if the surrounding talent and scheme are strong enough to maximize his strengths and minimize his flaws.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: in a Washington-picks-second discussion, restates him as one of two true top quarterbacks in the class alongside [[Caleb Williams]] — "probably the best thrower, pure thrower," with more pocket toughness, movement and field vision than he's given credit for, pushing back again on the "not a hard worker" / "not pro-style" rumor-based criticisms that dog quarterbacks like him (draws the same pattern to [[Lamar Jackson]]'s pre-draft treatment). Doesn't expect Washington to trade down to take him — skeptical the front office's "new and improved" media narrative reflects real organizational change yet — but floats him as realistically available "around later in the second round" if Washington instead takes a quarterback at 1.02. Separately, in a hypothetical-GM exercise, says he'd rather build around Penix than reach for [[J.J. - 2024-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-26]]), discussing [[Rome Odunze]]'s contested-catch numbers: calls Penix a "1995, perfect quarterback" archetype — a pure pocket thrower who can sit back and launch it if you build a wall in front of him — but worries that modern NFL defenses demand off-platform, move-the-pocket throwing, and that Penix's accuracy and ball placement "fall apart" when asked for it. Cites the Michigan national-title game as the swing example, reading Odunze as open or facing only half-separation rather than genuinely well covered on the contested throws in that game.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]), discussing Justin Herbert/Chargers draft scenarios: passingly reaffirms he's "a big Penix fan" and believes a lot of the nagging concerns raised about him are overblown, if he's given time.

- 2024-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Feel It or F@#k It* ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-01]]): "a lot" feeling it — wouldn't want him starting as a rookie, but if he can get a half-to-three-quarters-year runway on the bench before playing, thinks "he'd wind up being one of the best quarterbacks in this class." The two ACL tears in the same knee plus a throwing-shoulder injury will scare some teams off, and Waldman doesn't expect him to get "the Carson [Wentz] treatment" despite strong combine buzz. Pushes back on criticism of his pocket comfort — on tape he was efficient moving away from pressure, sidestepping or climbing and resetting his feet rather than drifting into it, and is a good decision-maker and leverage reader; calls him maybe the best vertical/sideline pinpoint thrower in this and recent classes. Names a team with heavy draft capital but needs beyond just quarterback (floats New England, without claiming inside information) as an ideal fit to let him sit, and separately flags Minnesota — with [[Justin Jefferson]] and teammate [[Jordan Addison]] — as the landing spot Waldman would most want to see, while doubting the Vikings will actually do it.

- 2024-04-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-23 (Evaluation vs. Valuation)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman says Penix's talent isn't the real question — if his injury history were a single incident from three years ago, he'd be a top-three-or-four pick — but four straight seasons ending early with shoulder and knee issues is a lot to overlook for a high first-round selection. Penix wore a brace all of 2023 despite no new injury and wasn't the same player after Michigan's pressure got to him in the national title game. Waldman believes good protection and situation would let him succeed given his quick release, framing the pick as a risk/valuation question rather than an evaluation one, and projects Penix as a second-round talent, liking the Raiders as a fit or a 'surprise team' trading up in round two. He also floated a QB-backup-as-template theory with Reuter: Gardner Minshew's known, proven floor makes him a poor stylistic comp for the unproven Penix, with [[Aidan O'Connell]] the more apt 'we don't know yet' comparison; Waldman doesn't expect the Giants to draft Penix given [[Daniel Jones]]'s extension, seeing them as more likely targeting Drake Maye or J.J. McCarthy if they trade up.
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]): defended the Falcons' 8th-overall selection of Penix Jr. against fan/media backlash over Kirk Cousins' surprise, calling the criticism backwards. He noted most of Cousins' $180M deal is guaranteed only through year two or three with team outs, making the Penix pick a hedge given Cousins turns 36 in August and is still unable to break the pocket in April coming off an Achilles tear. He cited James Palmer's report that the Raiders, Seahawks and Saints were all trying to trade up for Penix, so trading back further risked losing him entirely. Waldman compared the setup to Green Bay's Jordan Love plan — sit a rookie behind a veteran to inherit a talented young receiver room — while noting Love had three full years of ramp-up before inheriting Green Bay's offense, a bigger runway than Penix would get if Cousins were hurt. He frames this as a strong long-term plan, not a 2024 competitiveness referendum.

Angelo likes Penix as a player and argues Kirk Cousins's achilles rupture (on his plant, not push, foot) won't hold up biomechanically at 36, meaning Penix's window arrives sooner than the Falcons' stated four-year timeline — Angelo predicted closer to two years and said GM Terry Fontenot's 'four years' framing is wrong. Waldman separately defended Atlanta's decision to draft a QB at 8 over a defensive piece, arguing the 2023 Falcons roster (strong run game, O-line, Pro Bowl-caliber weapons including Bijan Robinson) was a playoff-caliber roster undone specifically by Desmond Ridder's play, and that with roughly 12 teams needing a QB next offseason, adding one now while Cousins bridges is a market-inefficiency play; both agreed Penix projects well in Atlanta's infrastructure.

Waldman projects Penix could take over by roughly Week 7-8 if not earlier, expects him to show more as a runner than he did at Washington, and calls him a 'throw-first, run-second' QB whose pocket mobility already exceeds what Atlanta had last year. Angelo's case rests on [[Kirk Cousins]]' limited runway — turning 36 coming off an Achilles rupture, with maybe two years left — making a QB2/insurance pick unusually valuable in Superflex and 6-point passing-TD leagues; he expects Penix to be a starter within two years, likely not as a rookie unless Cousins is hurt, but possibly starting by the end of next season. Waldman is comfortable backing Cousins up with Penix in dynasty formats but explicitly not in redraft. Waldman rejects the 'rookies need reps' framing outright — there are many quarterbacks who got the reps and never amounted to anything, so reps are a factor but not the factor in development, especially in a preseason of vanilla coverages and constant personnel churn. If you already like what you see on tape, you save him for when you actually need him. He reads the Falcons' other moves (adding an edge rusher and a safety) as a team pushing to win now while insuring an aging QB coming off an Achilles, and predicts we will look back and wonder why anyone was upset.

Waldman reads this as plausible speculation that Penix has been lighting it up in camp and that football people are saying he will be a problem in the league; he compares the tenor of that talk to John Elway watching [[Patrick Mahomes]] destroy the Broncos late in his rookie season, while explicitly saying he does not think Penix will be that good. - 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]) [Dynasty]: not feeling it but not fully fading it either — a superflex-only speculative stash. Waldman thinks Atlanta only benches [[Kirk Cousins]] once they are out of the playoffs, so betting on a starter who has never played a full week as one is a stretch in single-QB leagues, though the weapons are good and he is a good player. What he saw in limited Broncos snaps: Penix changed plays at the line, threw a couple of pretty balls, and does three things Cousins cannot — avoid defenders in the pocket, create past the line of scrimmage as a runner, and throw vertically with zip. He expects rookie coverage misreads that draw 'he's a bust' reactions, the same ones aimed at Andrew Luck, but believes the Penix era may come sooner than later. He would help all three receivers, especially the height of [[Drake London]] on back-shoulder and boundary throws, and [[Darnell Mooney]] on timing routes.
- 2024-12-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-09]]): Waldman thinks Penix is 'going to wind up being very good,' but argues Atlanta is right not to hand him the job mid-playoff-race. His reasoning is the on-ramp: nearly every rookie and several second-year quarterbacks this season needed a long runway, and Penix would be asked to have a short one in the most important games of the year. The predicted cost is evaluative — people will fold the playoff outcome into their judgment of him and overreact that he is not good enough, even though the team started him in the hardest possible spot. Waldman expects Atlanta to go to Penix only in a desperation scenario where [[Kirk Cousins]] physically cannot play. [Dynasty]
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-19]]): taking over in Atlanta with three soft matchups left (Giants, Washington, Carolina) — Harmon called the Giants a possible 'perfect marriage' since they give up production over the top and Penix likes big-game hunting. Reception Perception's prospect charting, written up by Derrik Klassen, has him below prospect average on the in-breaking dig, curl and slant, but with an 88% success rate on out routes and 63% on corners; 15% of his college attempts were nine routes against only 8.5% slants. Klassen was not impressed by his downfield accuracy and labeled him a classic Bruce Arians quarterback. Harmon agrees he has a live arm — Charles McDonald's line that he'd have been the 1990 first overall pick — but notes he was never a rushing threat despite a good 40 time. The bet, Harmon says, is that Atlanta hopes he becomes their [[Matthew Stafford]] and unlocks play-action off the outside zone that [[Kirk Cousins]] could not run.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-19 (NFL QB Lunacy)]]): the early book on Penix taking over from [[Kirk Cousins]] is excellent arm talent that 'probably rivals' and on specific throws beats [[Caleb Williams]] — fades, sideline timing routes, any boundary throw a receiver can win with timing — delivered with velocity and placement from the pocket and on the move, plus 4.45 speed that lets him gain yards when he breaks the pocket. Both are things Cousins could not do post-Achilles, which contracted Atlanta's playbook; Penix immediately restores the width and depth of the field. Waldman expects more completions, more big plays, more touchdowns, but also continued turnovers and more fumbles as he learns his NFL pocket clock, and predicts a soft close (Giants, Commanders, Panthers) with vanilla cover 3 / cover 1 looks and defenses that have no book on him yet. Verdict: a boom-bust QB2 in flex leagues, and a desperation streaming starter. Co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, not a tracked expert) noted Penix at roughly $5M/yr is cheaper than a Gardner Minshew-tier backup a contender needs anyway.
- 2024-12-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-23]]): after Penix's first career start, anyone expecting more than 'standard rookie up-and-down performance' is taking the wrong approach — it is his first game against defenses in end-of-season form that can switch looks faster than he can process. Waldman says the film mostly matched his pre-start expectations from his Gut Check column, with two surprises: far more work to [[Darnell Mooney]] and better timing-route chemistry than he anticipated, and less [[Kyle Pitts]] involvement than expected (Pitts dropped a ball that would have changed that). He praised Penix's blitz recognition, his handling of blitz-and-drop disguises, and a lot of good third-down work. Box-score-wise Penix looks similar to [[Kirk Cousins]], but on film he took more downfield chances, drew pass interference by placing the ball where the defender had to trail the receiver, and made velocity-dependent throws Cousins could not. Waldman expects that to stretch the field.
- 2024-12-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-24]]): Penix was really good in his first start versus the Giants and anyone dismissing it on the box score (no touchdowns, one interception) is not watching the games — the pick was a [[Kyle Pitts]] drop that popped into the air on an accurately placed ball. Per Next Gen, Penix was pressured on 14 dropbacks, roughly 50% of his snaps, and took zero sacks, with a 2.36-second average time to throw; 43.8% of his attempts traveled 10-plus air yards versus sub-35% for [[Kirk Cousins]] this year. Harmon says the game did not look too big for him, he immediately activated the run game (Atlanta ran boots they never called for Cousins), and he has the arm to drive deep out-breakers from the far hash to [[Darnell Mooney]] while still working the middle to [[Drake London]]. The value to [[Drake London]] is that Penix will simply throw it up to a big X receiver on corner and out routes, exactly what he did with [[Rome Odunze]] at Washington and what Harmon says Odunze's current quarterback [[Caleb Williams]] will not do.
