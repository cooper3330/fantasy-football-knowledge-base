# Aaron Jones

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: hanging on for 2024 — "he can still play" — but price-sensitive given Jones's extensive injury history. Coins the qualifier explicitly: "injury agnostic, not stupid" — willing to invest at the right (discounted) price, not high draft capital, expecting his role to stay robust under Matt LaFleur whenever he's healthy and available.
- 2024-01-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: still feeling it — "I'm never gonna quit on Aaron Jones and Matt LaFleur's system," calling for top-15 running back value in 2024 if he's a bargain on draft day. Reaffirms age as the only real concern, unchanged from the January 1 take.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): flags a favorable Divisional Round run-game matchup at San Francisco, a bottom-five rushing-success-rate defense — paired with the same read for [[Christian McCaffrey]] on the other side of the ball.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: still on both hosts even after the Divisional Round loss — the read is that people "wrote him off too early," missing that his strong finish came on fresher legs after an in-season injury absence. Expects the market to overrate his age and undervalue him relative to that late-season form, a gap Waldman intends to exploit in drafts. A.J. Dillon expected to leave via free agency, which would strengthen Jones's role if he's re-signed.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): independent, strongly positive corroboration — "criminally underrated" for his entire career, closing the season with five straight 100-yard games where "every bit of the production was reflected in the play" behind a good offensive line. Calls Green Bay's offense dependent on him specifically — "I don't think Green Bay's offense works nearly as well" without a good running back driving it. The real caution: age (30s) and a 2023 season that again showed he can't be relied on to stay healthy, with the team's insertion of A.J. Dillon into the lead role during his absence having failed outright. Recommends Green Bay address the RB2 spot in the draft rather than lean on Dillon again.

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: reacting to Green Bay releasing him after signing [[Josh Jacobs]] — "it sucks for Aaron Jones," still clearly good when healthy, and reads the Packers' offense as specifically "not the same" without him (contrasted with a non-functional A.J. Dillon-led stretch). But flags the swings in his 2023 game log — five straight 100-yard finishing games sandwiched around stretches of inactivity/low usage — and that at age 30 with his injury history, "you don't typically trend back the other direction" as a runner. Names likely pay-cut friction as the practical cause of the split, and is a little surprised Green Bay didn't push harder to keep him.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: on signing with divisional-rival Minnesota, still calls him "a legit awesome running back" who "can do it all" — floats the Packers "getting a taste of their own medicine" watching Jones run against them. Reads it as bad news for Ty Chandler's (not a tracked page) redraft value beyond a complementary/injury-insurance role.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: still a terrific back going around RB18 in early best balls, but wary — floats a scenario where Jones lands in a heavy-checkdown-volume role similar to [[DeAndre Swift]]'s 2022 Detroit year, becoming a streaky RB1 whose value depends heavily on timing (whether the volume surge comes early and gets taken away, or comes late after a slow start). Would still take the chance in some leagues given his versatility, but is wary of the boom/bust volume pattern specifically.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): asked whether Jones is the most underrated established back in football, Waldman said yes, and called him his favorite back in football outside of [[Nick Chubb]]. The evidence he keeps returning to is the receiving: on Nick Mullens' first play after [[Sam Darnold]] got hurt, a third-and-14 ball thrown up over a linebacker, Jones caught it around the linebacker's outstretched arm with technique Waldman said is exactly what you would want from a receiver — the same play he remembers Jones making 40 yards downfield at UTEP, including against a much better Texas defense. His scouting summary: tough, great feel for unanticipated obstacles, great moves, good burst, terrific vision, and real receiving upside.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): Green Bay misses Jones even with [[Josh Jacobs]] in the building, because few backs give you both the inside toughness and experience Jones has and the outside space game. Waldman points to how Minnesota uses him — runs off tackle and around end, stretching the defense both vertically and horizontally with him on the field — and describes him as [[Bucky Irving]] and [[David Montgomery]] wrapped into one. The burst is the differentiator: it makes him a good inside runner who is also a big-play threat and a downfield receiving option in a way Jacobs and Montgomery are not.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): an emphatic 'feel it' on Jones returning to Minnesota being great news for whoever plays quarterback — great receiver, excellent runner, still has plenty of life in his legs, and he already understands the scheme. Waldman argued Green Bay underestimated both Jones and [[Josh Jacobs]]: the Packers likely remembered his fifth-round draft capital, expected a hometown discount, had lingering savior complex, and may have known something from the training room, and Jones simply moved on. He noted Jones played all 17 games in 2024 with career highs despite three separate injuries. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) flagged his early Underdog best ball ADP at RB28 and said to go get him; Waldman said RB18 would already have been a bargain.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]): Minnesota re-signed Jones for two years at legitimate starter money and then traded for [[Jordan Mason]], which Waldman reads as most likely a surface-level loser for both but a good NFL move. He expects Jones to still lead the backfield in 2025 with Mason taking over the following year, contingent on whether Jones's deal has an out. Bob Harris (untracked) pushes the durability point in Jones's favor: the standing concern when Minnesota signed him was that he isn't durable enough, yet he played all 17 games through three injuries and set career highs — 306 touches, 700-plus offensive snaps, 255 carries — while being 30-plus. Waldman describes Jones as shiftier than Mason, with Mason the more physical of two similar versatile, receiving-and-blocking backs.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Jones is the least obvious weapon back on the board — arguably one of the best pass-catching backs in the league, whom Waldman puts top three at contested catches down the field and ball tracking, and who has delivered top-15 fantasy seasons even in split situations because of his efficiency. With [[Jordan Mason]] added and Kevin O'Connell describing a possible 1A/1B split, Waldman sees Jones drawing slot work, especially while [[Jordan Addison]] is out and if [[T.J. Hockenson]] and [[Justin Jefferson]] are working back — 'sneaky good' in that role. [[Brandon Angelo]] agreed, noting it was a bigger part of Jones's earlier career and that a veteran in the slot is a coverage dictator, which takes pressure off a young quarterback in [[J.J. McCarthy]] [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): Jones's receiving usage is the interesting part of his Week 1. On the first play of the second quarter Minnesota split him out wide on a go ball and he drew a deep pass interference call — Harris calls that one of the seeds of the comeback — and later he ran a go route straight out of the backfield past a linebacker in coverage for a bomb-shot touchdown. Snaps went 28 [[Jordan Mason]] to 23 Jones, which Harris reads as a real split job whose weekly winner will change, with both usable while the Vikings offense functions.
- 2025-10-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-23]]): Jones's Week 8 availability against the Chargers was still unresolved as of recording, expected to be settled in pregame warmups; if activated and dressed, Harris expects the team wouldn't do so unless he were in line for roughly 8-12 touches, which would eat into [[Jordan Mason]]'s workload.
- 2025-10-30 — According to Jeff Bell (Football Guys, not tracked) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-30]]): both have Jones right around RB30 (Harris: RB30 standard, RB28 PPR; Bell: RB30) and see him in a near-even committee with [[Jordan Mason]]. Coming off injury and playing a Lions defense that has historically stopped the run, with Minnesota's offensive line struggling and J.J. McCarthy back at QB, both frame Jones as a shaky last flex option -- Harris says he'd rather trust a receiver he's unsure about than either of these two backs this week, though there's a scenario where Minnesota's offense turns on and Jones benefits.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): Jones was 'absolutely dealing' early — good plays in the run game and a couple of terrific screens — with [[Jordan Mason]] not appearing at all in the first quarter, which Harris reads as the big Aaron Jones commitment fantasy managers had wanted finally arriving. Then he hurt his shoulder to start the third quarter and did not return; the team called it a minor AC joint injury. Harris is openly skeptical — 'it smells fishy to me, especially because it's Aaron Jones, it's just what he does' — and says 'I would be expecting to not have Aaron Jones for a while'. Guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert) agrees: 'if it's so minor, then why didn't he just finish the game yesterday? There are running backs who finish games with a sprained AC joint.'
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): the Minnesota backfield is decided for as long as Jones is healthy — 44 snaps, with the first half almost entirely to himself, the second straight week of that pattern since he returned from the shoulder injury suffered against Detroit. Guest Jamie Eisenberg of CBS Fantasy (not tracked) agrees he is the lead and clearly the passing-downs back, while noting O'Connell said in the offseason that Jones is better in a tandem, so a fuller turn loose may never come; Eisenberg calls him a good back-end RB2, maybe better as a flex, with only two teams on bye.
- 2025-11-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-11-18]]): Jones played the bulk of the Vikings backfield snaps (36 to 9 for [[Jordan Mason]]) and started the game well with roughly two drops charged against him (one officially) for 80 scrimmage yards. Harris says that workload share and production should earn Jones another fantasy start in Week 12 against the Packers, while he wouldn't be chasing Mason.
- 2025-12-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Jones was slammed to the turf on a screen against Seattle, fumbled, and immediately clutched at his shoulder, initially refusing the medical tent before eventually going in and being ruled out for the game. Harris noted Jones's long history of shoulder injuries and said that while the team is calling it minor and he may play in the next week or two, 'I kind of feel like I'd be surprised' and isn't sure why anyone would take the chance on him.
- 2026-01-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Ben Gretsch (untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-15]]): Jones turns 32 in December 2026 with one year and $9M cash left, only $2M guaranteed -- cutting him saves the Vikings $7.7M against a $7M cap hit. Harris says Jones wasn't healthy or productive enough down the stretch and expects Minnesota to look for a new backfield answer entirely. Gretsch is more measured: the Vikings likely still want a space/outside runner to pair with Jordan Mason's inside work, and Jones may keep that role, but at 32 with recurring injuries a straight cut is 'pretty likely.' Both float a pay-cut renegotiation as the more probable outcome given his standing as a longtime respected teammate, similar to how Pollard could be retained on a restructured deal rather than released outright.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Andy Behrens (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-05]]): Jones is widely rumored to be a cap-driven cut, and Harris notes he flagged this back in January when discussing likely salary casualties -- Jones has zero guaranteed money remaining. Harris says he's 'not that interested' anymore and would only project about a dozen healthy games, treating a roughly four-game hamstring absence as baked-in. His outlook: fine as a committee piece, not a lead back, worth a late-round (round nine) speculative flex stash to carry through bye weeks.
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Dave Kluge (Footballguys.com, untracked guest) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-12]]): Jones re-signed with Minnesota after a season where he 'looked okay amid a disastrous situation' but kept getting hurt. Kluge notes the range of outcomes is already known -- two years ago, healthy, he was a top-six back getting targets and goal-line work; last year he was hurt again -- and expects him to be faded heavily in drafts given he's entering his age-31/32 season on a run of injury-marred years, likely falling to the 5th-6th round, where Kluge thinks the risk is priced out. Harris is more skeptical, unsure he'd rank Jones that high given the accumulated injury history even acknowledging the two-years-ago upside; Kluge counters that Harris may be underselling that ceiling.
- 2026-03-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-17]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon noted Minnesota had to restructure [[Aaron Jones]]'s contract just to keep him on the roster, calling him 'on the decline for sure.'
- 2026-07-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Jeff Erickson (Rotowire, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-28]]): Jones took a pay cut to stay in Minnesota after it looked like he might leave, and both hosts still like his tape and talent ('Aaron Jones still looked really good'), but both agree he's simply not trustable health-wise given his age and injury history -- 'how many years in a row do we need this.' Harris ranks him RB38, the lowest of the Vikings backfield trio relative to expectation, purely on durability risk rather than talent.
