# Josh Jacobs

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: names Jacobs, as a free agent, "easily" the best current candidate to become "the next James Connor" — a good back who becomes a bargain the moment his name value drops — and would be a strong fit for the Cowboys or Ravens. Directly compares him to [[Zamir White]]: still the better back of the two, and would be a top-five running back landing almost anywhere with a real opportunity, versus a low-end RB2 ceiling for White if Jacobs leaves Las Vegas.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: floats Baltimore as a landing spot alongside [[Derrick Henry]] — either back joining [[Lamar Jackson]]'s Ravens offense would be "massive." Separately, discussing Houston's backfield need, names Jacobs (with Henry) as a plausible fit if he doesn't land in Baltimore; also name-checks Saquon Barkley as the closest thing to a [[Christian McCaffrey]]-style free-agent fit for that Houston offense, while doubting Barkley actually leaves the Giants.
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): grouped with [[Derrick Henry]] as the two free-agent backs Waldman is most confident land real starting jobs somewhere this offseason — "they're getting starting gigs somewhere, and if they don't, somebody's stupid."

- 2024-03-01 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host on *RSP Film and Theory* — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-01]] [Dynasty]: names Jacobs his top bet of the "Big Four" 2024 free-agent backs (ahead of [[Austin Ekeler]], [[Tony Pollard]] and [[Derrick Henry]]) — "I'm a big Josh Jacobs fan," crediting him for doing things "casual fans" undervalue but that NFL franchises pay for. Argues Jacobs over the last three years is about as good at Henry's core skills as Henry himself was over that same span, with more receiving upside and youth on top of it, making true decline less likely.
- 2024-03-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode [Dynasty]: agrees Jacobs is the best bet of the four. Adds a skills note beyond the numbers: thinks Jacobs is likely a better receiver/route runner than Pollard and comparable to Ekeler if he lands somewhere that uses him that way — he "was fantastic at Alabama" and a good route runner there, but the Raiders' play-action-heavy, Jacobs-as-focal-point scheme never called on him to run a full route tree. Groups him with Henry in the "Column B" bell-cow tier of the free-agent class, versus the receiving-back "Column A" of Ekeler and Pollard.

- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: predicts Jacobs re-signs and stays with the Las Vegas Raiders — "feeling that," calling it "a good" outcome — while noting the Cowboys would be another strong fit given his all-around skill set, and that a [[Derrick Henry]] arrival in Vegas would push his preference toward wanting Jacobs to land with the Ravens instead.

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: on his move to Green Bay — likes it for the Packers given Jacobs's relative youth (turning 26) versus the since-released [[Aaron Jones]] (turning 30), while flagging his 2023 workload/production collapse (340 attempts/1,650 yards in 2022 down to 233 attempts/805 yards in 2023) as largely a product of the Raiders' non-competitive, post-McDaniels context rather than a talent decline. Says it's still unclear whether he'll be a good scheme fit in Matt LaFleur's system.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: both fully "feel" the finalized move to Green Bay, calling it one of the best fits of this free-agent cycle — helped further by the Packers releasing [[Aaron Jones]] and bringing back only complementary A.J. Dillon (not a tracked page). Waldman: Jacobs is "a pro's pro," a do-it-all back who can run gap or zone and is a much better receiver than his box-score totals suggest to anyone who "never watched Alabama tape" — expects the receiving numbers to climb now that he isn't sharing touches with Jones.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]): expects Jacobs to move well inside the top 10 of his RB rankings now that he's a Packer, even with A.J. Dillon (not tracked) still on the roster — "such a good pass receiver," an all-around back on an offense that will open things up for him, facing NFC North defenses Waldman doesn't rate highly at this point. Cites Matt LaFleur's scheme and stated plan to use Jacobs as a receiver out of the backfield as what "sealed the deal."
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): feels good about Jacobs hitting roughly 20 touches/game in Green Bay, projecting about 1,500 yards and 8-10 touchdowns, and floated a career-year scenario (1,600-1,800 yards, callback to his 2022 season as fantasy's top RB) if the Packers' line performs. He downplayed the AJ Dillon and Marshawn Lloyd threats as '3 big ifs' -- Dillon lacks explosive juice, Lloyd's ball security and pass protection are unproven -- though he noted the Packers hold a contract out that could open the door for Lloyd in 2025 if Jacobs falters. Bob Harris compared Jacobs to a 'rental car' the team will run hard and discard if he wears down, but said he doesn't count Jacobs out given his life story.
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]): he's 'fearful [Jacobs] is going to be pretty overdrafted this year.' He leans on Matt LaFleur's history as a split-backfield coach (Dion Lewis/Derrick Henry in Tennessee, Aaron Jones' receiving-heavy role in Green Bay), plus an Evan Silva report that LaFleur wanted to pair Jones and Jacobs before Jones' release -- suggesting Jacobs is more a receiving-down replacement for what Green Bay hoped AJ Dylan would be than a true bell cow. Jacobs hasn't shown receiving juice since Alabama. JJ wants the ADP gap between Jacobs and rookie Marshawn Lloyd (a likely Aaron Jones-role change-of-pace back) shrunk -- Jacobs should go lower, Lloyd higher -- while stopping short of recommending drafting Lloyd over Jacobs outright.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said Jacobs' refusal to sign with the Chiefs out of Raiders rivalry makes him like the player more — a guy who lived in a car for stretches in high school and still turned down money because he wanted a frontline role, which Waldman read as evidence the money has not clouded his love of the game. Untracked co-host Bob Harris made the fantasy point: Jacobs has been slightly disrespected in this draft season and has gone way later than he should at points. With [[A.J. Dillon]] on injured reserve and [[Marshawn Lloyd]] expected back sooner than feared, early on it is 'the Josh Jacobs show.' Waldman added he thinks Jacobs is a better receiver than he gets credit for.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): he loves Jacobs as a runner but says the Packers genuinely miss [[Aaron Jones]], because Jacobs is not as well-rounded a threat. His comp is [[David Montgomery]] — both are very good interior runners with excellent movement who catch the ball extraordinarily well, both have been underused in the passing game at points in their careers, and Waldman's explanation for the underuse is that neither has great speed. That is the specific gap versus Jones, who has the burst to be both a good inside runner and a big-play threat and a downfield receiver in ways Montgomery and Jacobs are not.
- 2024-12-05 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-05]]) [Dynasty]: the 'old 24' argument used to fade Jacobs was backwards. Two years earlier the dynasty market moved [[Rhamondre Stevenson]] ahead of Jacobs on mileage grounds even though Jacobs had outproduced him every year and is only twelve days older, with the extra mileage itself the reason for the discount. Harstad's reading: Jacobs accumulated those touches because he is Josh Jacobs and is built for that role, and he was still leading the NFL in touches at the time of the episode. He used the case as the concrete example of why an age-uncontrolled mileage adjustment produces a systematically mispriced dynasty asset — the volume is the coaches' testimony to durability, not a warning about it.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-15]]): Jacobs is his number 1 flag player for 2025, his third appearance on the list after 2020 and 2021. The praise is physical — not a runaway athlete, but incredible contact balance, explosiveness in close quarters, nearly impossible to tackle from the side once he turns the corner, nails on the goal line, a power back with enough speed and elusiveness to transcend the label. His real irritation is with the analytical argument against Jacobs, the 'they would not have drafted [[Marshawn Lloyd]] if they were not going to use him' school, which he says is contradicted every year by [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], [[Bijan Robinson]] and [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]; LaFleur spreading carries without an elite back says nothing about what he does with one. The actionable twist: even though Harris ranks him in the first, he wants you not to draft him there — take the other first-round name and Jacobs should still be there mid-second, which he calls like getting two first-round picks. CBS is the exception, where ADP has caught up to his rank.
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Jacobs is his real round-two answer and his number one flag player for 2025 — effectively a first-round pick being drafted in the middle of the second, so landing him there feels like getting two first-rounders. Harris described shooting the moon on [[Christian McCaffrey]] at pick seven in a full-PPR draft specifically because he expected Jacobs to fall back to him, and it worked; he would prefer [[Derrick Henry]] if Henry ever lasted as long, but in his experience he does not. Guest Scott Pianowski (Yahoo, not tracked) also lists Jacobs as a round-two safe pick: [[Matt LaFleur]] is an excellent coach, Green Bay wins 10 or 11 games as usual, and Jacobs took control of the offense last year even accounting for the week-one [[Jordan Love]] injury changing things. Pianowski took [[Jonathan Taylor]] over him in the Kings Classic and second-guessed it, distrusting Indianapolis's quarterback room.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates Jacobs a 1 out of 10 at ADP 21 — not much risk at all. Harris is irritated Jacobs is not a first-round pick and gave him a 2 only because he felt ashamed to write a 1, admitting he is in the bag for him. Harris took Jacobs in the second round of a DraftKings best ball draft from the seven slot the night before, behind [[Derrick Henry]] [Best Ball].
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Jacobs is his single most-drafted player of 2025, landing on four of the roughly seven or eight teams he cares about. He has a first-round grade on Jacobs but deliberately skipped him in round one because, outside of a top-three pick, Jacobs was usually getting back to him in the second — he calls the market putting Jacobs around 23rd overall 'woefully wrong' and the pick 'easy money.' In La Liga Lebowski, an auction where [[Saquon Barkley]] and [[Christian McCaffrey]] had aged out of their contracts, Jacobs was the back he paid up for despite limited budget. He notes Jacobs is only 5-foot-10 but shifty and powerful enough to hit the hole, and that he rates [[Derrick Henry]] only narrowly ahead of him — in the charity league he took Henry first overall-ish and Jacobs was sniped at pick 13.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-04]]): Jacobs is Harris's 2025 preseason fantasy MVP — the same award he gave him the previous summer, making it a repeat pick. His reasoning is price against production: Jacobs was available in the middle of the second round in the season after finishing as the RB5, which Harris says seemed crazy to him all summer. He explicitly logs the downside rather than hiding it — 2021 was underwhelming, 2023 he got hurt, so this is not an invulnerable, foolproof scheme and no player is invulnerable. Granted health, though, Harris thinks Jacobs does it again. He frames the MVP category itself as ambiguous (is value the late-round steal who delivers, or simply the highest overall scorer?), and picks Jacobs under the value-versus-cost reading.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Harris, who loves Jacobs and got him relatively cheap, says he was prepared to come on this segment saying uh-oh. It was rough for a long stretch as Detroit sold out to stop the run, but the Lions wore down late, Jacobs found room and got the touchdown at the end. Guest **Sam Gold** (not tracked) was definitely freaked out in the first half but found the second half very encouraging, saying Green Bay just kept feeding him and he looked like he got stronger as the game went on.
- 2025-09-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-12]]): For a second straight week an opposing defense entered clearly trying not to let Green Bay's run game beat them, and for a second straight week the interior of the Packers offensive line was not getting the push it did in 2024, so Jacobs had tough sledding early (he also false-started on the opening drive). Harris says whatever the efficiency metrics say — and he can imagine what the metrics bros are saying — Jacobs has looked really good, like himself, consistently grinding out yards you would not expect even when stuffed. The gravity is real: defenses remember last year, so when Green Bay goes heavy and imbalanced near the goal line and fakes to Jacobs, everybody jumps, which produced a slant touchdown to [[Romeo Doubs]] and a red zone touchdown to [[Tucker Kraft]]. Jacobs still got two carries inside the 10 and scored his second touchdown of the season, but Harris wants to see better interior blocking.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 74 scrimmage yards against Cleveland with a clear commitment to getting him rolling — a third-and-two conversion split out wide and a 31-yard screen in the third quarter — but hard sledding otherwise. Harris's structural note is now three games in a row where Green Bay has not gotten much push up the middle for the run game; Jacobs is consistently making guys miss in the backfield to turn negative runs into vaguely positive ones, which is good football and bad fantasy.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): a maddening but ultimately productive night in the 40-40 tie. Green Bay repeatedly got to the goal line and did not feed him — a [[Tucker Kraft]] catch to the one turned into a false start and a fade touchdown to [[Romeo Doubs]] instead, and from the one they faked it to Jacobs for a second Doubs score, which Harris frames as the football gods punishing them (a blocked extra point gave Dallas its first points). He nearly scored on a lateral-ish forward pass on a [[Jordan Love]] scramble. Eventually the Packers understood the assignment: three straight Jacobs runs from the six for a touchdown, then a second score on a great fourth-quarter run where Trevon Diggs made a visible business decision to avoid him. Harris stresses his sturdiness. Risk flag: trainers appeared to look at his shin or knee in the fourth and he did not play at all on Green Bay's overtime drive; Packers are on bye in Week 5.
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-13]]): Jacobs may have to be discussed as a receiving asset again — he had five catches for 57 yards in this game. Waldman said Alabama leveraged Jacobs as a receiver better than any NFL team had until the Packers, and that he never understood what the Raiders were doing with him in the passing game. On the ground Waldman called him springy and alive this season, noting that while Jacobs is not old, he has been playing in the league since he was 20; he also sounds happy in interviews.
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-13]]): Jacobs bullied Cincinnati for over 140 scrimmage yards and two touchdowns, including a 15-yard interior burst to open the fourth quarter that Harris called 'undeniable,' plus an earlier score off a big middle screen. Harris said to 'just play the man's music' -- Jacobs was a workhorse in a slow, field-position-heavy game and helped set up a late Tucker Kraft score by driving Green Bay deep into Bengals territory.
- 2025-10-17 — According to co-host Josh (not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-17]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Grouped in Green Bay's 'wagon premium, if only it came family size' tier, Josh called Jacobs a near-automatic weekly starter behind [[Jordan Love]], describing him as the one settled, reliable piece in an otherwise chaotic Packers offense where the receiver room -- [[Matthew Golden]], [[Dontayvion Wicks]], [[Romeo Doubs]], [[Christian Watson]], [[Jayden Reed]] -- is too injury-scrambled to project on a weekly basis.
- 2025-10-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-21 (Week 8 Waivers & W7 Reviews)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Jacobs was a late game-time decision (illness plus a calf injury) and was benched for a series early for [[Emanuel Wilson]] with little to show for it, prompting real worry from Harris mid-game. Coach Matt LaFleur explained at halftime it was a planned pitch count to save Jacobs for a bigger second-half role, which played out -- Jacobs won a foot race to the pylon to tie the game at 13, then scored again on three straight carries to win it late, plus a play-action score to [[Tucker Kraft]] came off a Jacobs look. Harris's takeaway: keep watching his activity status closely, but 'when he is [active], buddy he's in your lineup.'
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-10-27]]) [Redraft]: Jacobs scored the go-ahead touchdown bouncing a run outside; his snaps were managed (26 to Emmanuel Wilson's 34, an untracked backup) due to a calf injury for the second straight week. Harris says this is deliberate touch management, not an alarm bell -- 'Green Bay is not getting to a Super Bowl without him.'
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): Jacobs was 'basically the only Packer who came through for you' on Monday night, taking six of the first seven touches and 13 in the first half, and accounting for 58 of Green Bay's 83 first-half yards. Harris's read on Green Bay is that 'they want to maul you for as long as the scoreboard lets them' - he cites Greg Olsen calling the approach frustrating to watch on the Panthers game because explosive plays correlate with winning and the Packers' run game does not generate them, and concedes Olsen has been proven right two weeks running even if the take is 'a little dogmatic for me'. Jacobs finished the Packers' only touchdown drive on back-to-back plays from the 13 after a [[Luke Musgrave]] pass-interference call, and Harris absolves him of the fourth-and-one fumble at midfield because the Eagles defense was audibly screaming 'inside zone this way' and he was not getting the first down regardless.
- 2025-11-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-11-18]]): Jacobs banged his knee on the MetLife Stadium turf on a tight-roped screen reception, went to the blue medical tent, and did not return after a brief second-half appearance; he ultimately exited for good after a play-action snap where Malik Willis (in for an injured Jordan Love) found Christian Watson. Harris says the team is calling it not season-ending but is cautious given it's a knee injury on bad turf -- could be one week, could be multiple. Harris says flatly he doesn't think there's a league where a Jacobs owner shouldn't already have handcuffed [[Emmanuel Wilson]], and repeats that recommendation strongly for Week 12: 'if you have Jacobs, I want to get Wilson -- that's the clearest path.' Guest Jake Seeley (The Athletic, not tracked) agrees, framing Wilson as the safest FAAB spend of the week for Jacobs owners.
- 2025-11-21 — According to Chris Harris on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-21]]: Jacobs was limited in practice all week and told reporters his knee issue is swelling and range-of-motion related rather than structural. Harris said that's bad news in that they wanted him to play the past two games, but good news in that it likely isn't a multi-week issue. Green Bay's Thanksgiving game against Detroit raises the chance of an [[Emmanuel Wilson]] spot-start opportunity this week if Jacobs sits.
- 2025-12-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-12]]): Jacobs has not practiced this week and is dealing with a knee injury he called 'day to day,' after visibly getting hurt on a hit against the Bears and needing Emmanuel Wilson as handcuff insurance; Harris said he's assuming Jacobs plays but is monitoring closely. Separately, in the 2026 first-round exercise, Harris put Jacobs at his RB6, ahead of Josh's placement of him at 11, noting Jacobs is on every one of Harris's fantasy-playoff teams this year and predicting Jacobs will fall to a mid-second-round ADP next year the same way he was undervalued this preseason -- 'people are going to be wrong, and that's fun.' - 2025-12-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-23]]): Jacobs fumbled inside the Bears' five-yard line in the second half and then played only two more snaps early in the fourth quarter before disappearing entirely; Harris says he personally doesn't think it was the reported knee injury that limited him -- he suspects Jacobs was benched after the fumble -- but says the team's messaging on the knee for championship week bears watching. Harris notes with some pain that Jacobs was the only common roster piece across his four semifinal teams, three of which he still won.
- 2025-12-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-26]]): Jacobs was removed from the Packers' injury report and will play Saturday, but Harris said he obviously isn't getting the same work share he had earlier in the year, with [[Emmanuel Wilson]] getting an 'uncomfortable amount' of work behind him. He called Jacobs's lost fumble near the goal line the prior week 'brutal' and said it certainly contributed to the collapse against the Bears -- he even wondered if it amounted to a benching -- but concluded most managers have no better option and will still have to start him.
- 2026-01-13 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-13]]) [Dynasty]: criticized Green Bay for continuing to feed Jacobs as a bell-cow even when inefficient, pointing to the wild-card loss to Chicago where Jacobs ran 19 times for 2.9 yards per carry and nobody else touched the ball -- part of a season-long pattern behind a disappointing offensive line. Harmon said the Packers 'have to move on from that' approach to the run game this offseason -- not necessarily cutting Jacobs, but stopping the practice of 'gutting him out' through injuries as a low-efficiency early-down hammer -- and named it as the biggest fix Matt LaFleur needs to make heading into 2026, more so than personnel.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris ranks [[Josh Jacobs]] RB8, defending the pick against the read that he 'just likes these guys' by pointing to Jacobs still being only 28 with a thin depth chart behind him (Marshawn Lloyd, Chris Brooks and 'a bunch of garbage'). Harris cited Jacobs' strong second half of last season as the case for him at end-of-round-one/early-round-two, while flagging the concern that will follow him into summer -- whether he was simply fatigued down the stretch.
- 2026-05-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-28 (Going Deep)]]) [Dynasty]: citing legal reporting (including Drew at FootballGuys), Waldman said an active investigation into an off-field legal issue involving Jacobs -- rather than an on-scene arrest -- suggests there may be real substance to it, though Jacobs has reportedly claimed to have exculpatory evidence. Waldman called the situation roughly 50/50 and said the range of outcomes spans two to eight missed games up to a full season if Jacobs is charged with a felony. He and Angelo used that worst-case framing to game out the Packers backfield: [[Marshawn Lloyd]], [[Christopher Brooks]], [[Damien Martinez]] and [[Savion Williams]] as the internal options, with both hosts agreeing Green Bay would likely need to add a veteran ([[James Connor]] was their top external pick, with [[Nick Chubb]] preferred over [[Joe Mixon]] as alternates) if Jacobs misses extended time, turning the offense heavily into a [[Jordan Love]]-driven passing attack.
- 2026-06-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-01]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman flagged that Josh Jacobs was charged with a domestic-violence offense and released, with charges later dropped but the matter still under investigation, introducing real roster-spot uncertainty behind him and elevating [[Chris Brooks]]' outlook as the Packers' backup plan.
- 2026-07-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-28]]): Jacobs has already dropped in Harris's ranks because of the looming threat of an NFL suspension tied to an alleged assault, even though county officials have not yet filed charges. Harris says if charges are filed before Labor Day, Jacobs drops further because suspension odds rise; there is also a scenario where any suspension doesn't land until 2027, sparing this season entirely, but that can't be known now. Harris frames it as a 'Sword of Damocles' hanging over Jacobs heading into drafts -- unresolved by anything training camp reveals -- and notes it raises the stakes on backups Marshawn Lloyd and Chris Brooks, since Green Bay could still trade for a veteran like James Conner or Alvin Kamara if the legal situation worsens.
- 2026-08-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-06]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman won't draft Jacobs this year -- currently RB11/23rd overall, which he thinks is too high given offseason legal speculation and worsening durability (he flags late-season 'old man creaky knees' signs last year that he doesn't expect to improve, only get worse). In the same range he prefers [[Chase Brown]], calling his profile much better for a high-end RB1 finish, and [[Javonte Williams]], who has no competition in his backfield in an offense he expects to score a lot.
- 2026-08-07 — According to Cousin Josh (untracked recurring guest co-host, [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-07]]): Jacobs is his acid-reflux #4 purely because of looming suspension risk tied to off-field misconduct allegations that both hosts say they believe (Chris: 'I tend to believe women, and so I tend to believe that he did them'). Both project the actual NFL discipline to land at the start of next season rather than mid-year, reasoning that the league office moves only when public outrage forces its hand and otherwise lets things drag. Talent-wise, neither is worried -- last year he was an RB1 with rushing yards down but touchdowns intact, and Green Bay hasn't changed anything about his role -- and Josh ranks him RB13 and calls him an ideal RB2 pairing if you land [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] or [[Bijan Robinson]] with an early pick, expecting him to go in the round 3-4 range since the public dislike of him is already priced into ADP rather than driving it lower.
- 2026-08-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Josh Jacobs is dealing with a groin injury that will cost him a week of training camp, and Adam Schefter has specifically named him -- alongside [[Puka Nacua]] -- among players facing possible NFL discipline, with Harris noting Schefter doesn't float suspension talk unless he believes it's actually coming. Harris said Jacobs hasn't been convicted of anything and the exact charge or timeline is unclear to him, but a suspension in the range of two to four games could land before or during the season, which would significantly boost [[Marshawn Lloyd]]'s value. Guest Jamie Eisenberg (not tracked) separately floated that as Jacobs ages and Green Bay eyes a Super Bowl-contending season, the team may proactively lighten his workload regardless of how the legal situation resolves.
- 2026-08-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-13]]): Harmon ranks Josh Jacobs notably below consensus among his running back tiers, calling him a player who 'runs the gamut of red flags' this year -- heavy workload (one of the league's most-used backs over the last four years), a real efficiency dip last season (4.4 to 4.0 yards per carry), an ongoing NFL investigation into an alleged domestic incident with possible suspension exposure, and an offensive line and defense he expects to be worse in 2026. James Koh (untracked co-host) independently says he wants no part of that risk profile either, calling Jacobs someone with 'five possible trap doors' this season.
- 2026-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Cousin Josh (untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-14]]): Harris gave Jacobs a 7, Josh an 8. Both cite suspension risk (Harris earlier speculated Jacobs, not [[Puka Nacua]], is the more likely subject of the Schefter chatter) plus a fresh groin injury suffered in camp that Harris expects will cost him a significant chunk of camp/preseason time -- he says he was overworked into injury last year and may simply not be trusted with the same workload going forward. Harris says he's comfortable fading Jacobs at his current ADP (roughly pick 32 standard, 35 PPR) despite acknowledging it feels strange to be behind the market on a player of his talent.
