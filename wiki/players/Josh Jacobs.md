# Josh Jacobs

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: names Jacobs, as a free agent, "easily" the best current candidate to become "the next James Connor" — a good back who becomes a bargain the moment his name value drops — and would be a strong fit for the Cowboys or Ravens. Directly compares him to [[Zamir White]]: still the better back of the two, and would be a top-five running back landing almost anywhere with a real opportunity, versus a low-end RB2 ceiling for White if Jacobs leaves Las Vegas.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: floats Baltimore as a landing spot alongside [[Derrick Henry]] — either back joining [[Lamar Jackson]]'s Ravens offense would be "massive." Separately, discussing Houston's backfield need, names Jacobs (with Henry) as a plausible fit if he doesn't land in Baltimore; also name-checks Saquon Barkley as the closest thing to a [[Christian McCaffrey]]-style free-agent fit for that Houston offense, while doubting Barkley actually leaves the Giants.
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): grouped with [[Derrick Henry]] as the two free-agent backs Waldman is most confident land real starting jobs somewhere this offseason — "they're getting starting gigs somewhere, and if they don't, somebody's stupid."

- 2024-03-01 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host on *RSP Film and Theory* — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-01]] [Dynasty]: names Jacobs his top bet of the "Big Four" 2024 free-agent backs (ahead of [[Austin Ekeler]], [[Tony Pollard]] and [[Derrick Henry]]) — "I'm a big Josh Jacobs fan," crediting him for doing things "casual fans" undervalue but that NFL franchises pay for. Argues Jacobs over the last three years is about as good at Henry's core skills as Henry himself was over that same span, with more receiving upside and youth on top of it, making true decline less likely.
- 2024-03-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode [Dynasty]: agrees Jacobs is the best bet of the four. Adds a skills note beyond the numbers: thinks Jacobs is likely a better receiver/route runner than Pollard and comparable to Ekeler if he lands somewhere that uses him that way — he "was fantastic at Alabama" and a good route runner there, but the Raiders' play-action-heavy, Jacobs-as-focal-point scheme never called on him to run a full route tree. Groups him with Henry in the "Column B" bell-cow tier of the free-agent class, versus the receiving-back "Column A" of Ekeler and Pollard.

- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: predicts Jacobs re-signs and stays with the Las Vegas Raiders — "feeling that," calling it "a good" outcome — while noting the Cowboys would be another strong fit given his all-around skill set, and that a [[Derrick Henry]] arrival in Vegas would push his preference toward wanting Jacobs to land with the Ravens instead.

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: on his move to Green Bay — likes it for the Packers given Jacobs's relative youth (turning 26) versus the since-released [[Aaron Jones]] (turning 30), while flagging his 2023 workload/production collapse (340 attempts/1,650 yards in 2022 down to 233 attempts/805 yards in 2023) as largely a product of the Raiders' non-competitive, post-McDaniels context rather than a talent decline. Says it's still unclear whether he'll be a good scheme fit in Matt LaFleur's system.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: both fully "feel" the finalized move to Green Bay, calling it one of the best fits of this free-agent cycle — helped further by the Packers releasing [[Aaron Jones]] and bringing back only complementary A.J. Dillon (not a tracked page). Waldman: Jacobs is "a pro's pro," a do-it-all back who can run gap or zone and is a much better receiver than his box-score totals suggest to anyone who "never watched Alabama tape" — expects the receiving numbers to climb now that he isn't sharing touches with Jones.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]): expects Jacobs to move well inside the top 10 of his RB rankings now that he's a Packer, even with A.J. Dillon (not tracked) still on the roster — "such a good pass receiver," an all-around back on an offense that will open things up for him, facing NFC North defenses Waldman doesn't rate highly at this point. Cites Matt LaFleur's scheme and stated plan to use Jacobs as a receiver out of the backfield as what "sealed the deal."
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): feels good about Jacobs hitting roughly 20 touches/game in Green Bay, projecting about 1,500 yards and 8-10 touchdowns, and floated a career-year scenario (1,600-1,800 yards, callback to his 2022 season as fantasy's top RB) if the Packers' line performs. He downplayed the AJ Dillon and Marshawn Lloyd threats as '3 big ifs' -- Dillon lacks explosive juice, Lloyd's ball security and pass protection are unproven -- though he noted the Packers hold a contract out that could open the door for Lloyd in 2025 if Jacobs falters. Bob Harris compared Jacobs to a 'rental car' the team will run hard and discard if he wears down, but said he doesn't count Jacobs out given his life story.
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]): he's 'fearful [Jacobs] is going to be pretty overdrafted this year.' He leans on Matt LaFleur's history as a split-backfield coach (Dion Lewis/Derrick Henry in Tennessee, Aaron Jones' receiving-heavy role in Green Bay), plus an Evan Silva report that LaFleur wanted to pair Jones and Jacobs before Jones' release -- suggesting Jacobs is more a receiving-down replacement for what Green Bay hoped AJ Dylan would be than a true bell cow. Jacobs hasn't shown receiving juice since Alabama. JJ wants the ADP gap between Jacobs and rookie Marshawn Lloyd (a likely Aaron Jones-role change-of-pace back) shrunk -- Jacobs should go lower, Lloyd higher -- while stopping short of recommending drafting Lloyd over Jacobs outright.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said Jacobs' refusal to sign with the Chiefs out of Raiders rivalry makes him like the player more — a guy who lived in a car for stretches in high school and still turned down money because he wanted a frontline role, which Waldman read as evidence the money has not clouded his love of the game. Untracked co-host Bob Harris made the fantasy point: Jacobs has been slightly disrespected in this draft season and has gone way later than he should at points. With [[A.J. Dillon]] on injured reserve and [[Marshawn Lloyd]] expected back sooner than feared, early on it is 'the Josh Jacobs show.' Waldman added he thinks Jacobs is a better receiver than he gets credit for.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): he loves Jacobs as a runner but says the Packers genuinely miss [[Aaron Jones]], because Jacobs is not as well-rounded a threat. His comp is [[David Montgomery]] — both are very good interior runners with excellent movement who catch the ball extraordinarily well, both have been underused in the passing game at points in their careers, and Waldman's explanation for the underuse is that neither has great speed. That is the specific gap versus Jones, who has the burst to be both a good inside runner and a big-play threat and a downfield receiver in ways Montgomery and Jacobs are not.
- 2024-12-05 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-05]]) [Dynasty]: the 'old 24' argument used to fade Jacobs was backwards. Two years earlier the dynasty market moved [[Rhamondre Stevenson]] ahead of Jacobs on mileage grounds even though Jacobs had outproduced him every year and is only twelve days older, with the extra mileage itself the reason for the discount. Harstad's reading: Jacobs accumulated those touches because he is Josh Jacobs and is built for that role, and he was still leading the NFL in touches at the time of the episode. He used the case as the concrete example of why an age-uncontrolled mileage adjustment produces a systematically mispriced dynasty asset — the volume is the coaches' testimony to durability, not a warning about it.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-15]]): Jacobs is his number 1 flag player for 2025, his third appearance on the list after 2020 and 2021. The praise is physical — not a runaway athlete, but incredible contact balance, explosiveness in close quarters, nearly impossible to tackle from the side once he turns the corner, nails on the goal line, a power back with enough speed and elusiveness to transcend the label. His real irritation is with the analytical argument against Jacobs, the 'they would not have drafted [[Marshawn Lloyd]] if they were not going to use him' school, which he says is contradicted every year by [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], [[Bijan Robinson]] and [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]; LaFleur spreading carries without an elite back says nothing about what he does with one. The actionable twist: even though Harris ranks him in the first, he wants you not to draft him there — take the other first-round name and Jacobs should still be there mid-second, which he calls like getting two first-round picks. CBS is the exception, where ADP has caught up to his rank.
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Jacobs is his real round-two answer and his number one flag player for 2025 — effectively a first-round pick being drafted in the middle of the second, so landing him there feels like getting two first-rounders. Harris described shooting the moon on [[Christian McCaffrey]] at pick seven in a full-PPR draft specifically because he expected Jacobs to fall back to him, and it worked; he would prefer [[Derrick Henry]] if Henry ever lasted as long, but in his experience he does not. Guest Scott Pianowski (Yahoo, not tracked) also lists Jacobs as a round-two safe pick: [[Matt LaFleur]] is an excellent coach, Green Bay wins 10 or 11 games as usual, and Jacobs took control of the offense last year even accounting for the week-one [[Jordan Love]] injury changing things. Pianowski took [[Jonathan Taylor]] over him in the Kings Classic and second-guessed it, distrusting Indianapolis's quarterback room.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates Jacobs a 1 out of 10 at ADP 21 — not much risk at all. Harris is irritated Jacobs is not a first-round pick and gave him a 2 only because he felt ashamed to write a 1, admitting he is in the bag for him. Harris took Jacobs in the second round of a DraftKings best ball draft from the seven slot the night before, behind [[Derrick Henry]] [Best Ball].
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Jacobs is his single most-drafted player of 2025, landing on four of the roughly seven or eight teams he cares about. He has a first-round grade on Jacobs but deliberately skipped him in round one because, outside of a top-three pick, Jacobs was usually getting back to him in the second — he calls the market putting Jacobs around 23rd overall 'woefully wrong' and the pick 'easy money.' In La Liga Lebowski, an auction where [[Saquon Barkley]] and [[Christian McCaffrey]] had aged out of their contracts, Jacobs was the back he paid up for despite limited budget. He notes Jacobs is only 5-foot-10 but shifty and powerful enough to hit the hole, and that he rates [[Derrick Henry]] only narrowly ahead of him — in the charity league he took Henry first overall-ish and Jacobs was sniped at pick 13.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-04]]): Jacobs is Harris's 2025 preseason fantasy MVP — the same award he gave him the previous summer, making it a repeat pick. His reasoning is price against production: Jacobs was available in the middle of the second round in the season after finishing as the RB5, which Harris says seemed crazy to him all summer. He explicitly logs the downside rather than hiding it — 2021 was underwhelming, 2023 he got hurt, so this is not an invulnerable, foolproof scheme and no player is invulnerable. Granted health, though, Harris thinks Jacobs does it again. He frames the MVP category itself as ambiguous (is value the late-round steal who delivers, or simply the highest overall scorer?), and picks Jacobs under the value-versus-cost reading.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Harris, who loves Jacobs and got him relatively cheap, says he was prepared to come on this segment saying uh-oh. It was rough for a long stretch as Detroit sold out to stop the run, but the Lions wore down late, Jacobs found room and got the touchdown at the end. Guest **Sam Gold** (not tracked) was definitely freaked out in the first half but found the second half very encouraging, saying Green Bay just kept feeding him and he looked like he got stronger as the game went on.
