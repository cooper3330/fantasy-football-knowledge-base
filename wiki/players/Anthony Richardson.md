# Anthony Richardson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-01 — Both hosts on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]] [Redraft (Standard)]: unqualified top-12 quarterback call for 2024 — "100%." The underlying case is largely visual rather than a stats argument: Waldman's shorthand example is a Richardson play against the Rams in which he "bewildered" Aaron Donald. Both frame it as a value they were already selling on the show throughout the prior season, ahead of a full season of tape existing.
- 2024-01-01 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode, on durability rather than talent: hopes teams follow the Miami Dolphins' lead with Tua Tagovailoa, who reportedly trained with a jiu-jitsu coach in the offseason specifically to learn how to fall safely and reduce head-impact risk, and wants a similar investment made in protecting Richardson given the money teams put into a starting quarterback.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]): used as the explicit contrast case for grading [[Caleb Williams]] — Waldman confirms he sees Richardson as closer to "all boom" than Williams, because he rates Richardson's mental processing/decision-making as more settled, even though Williams carries the higher pure ceiling grade of the two.

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]): floated as a best ball value-stack pairing with [[Justin Fields]] — "more of a stretch" given the durability/sample-size uncertainty than Fields is, but if his rookie-year flashes (before the injury that ended his season) hold up over a full season, Waldman thinks he could be one of fantasy's top-five quarterbacks, in the [[Jalen Hurts]] mold of scoring at a high rate without needing to lead the league in passing yardage.

- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]): calls him the class's "lottery ticket" and draws a direct parallel to how hard he pushed [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] in 2023 — expects to pursue Richardson aggressively as an "affordable" late-round QB upside target. Relays that Colts GM Chris Ballard has now publicly corroborated Waldman's own pre-draft read that Richardson was a better passer than his rookie box score suggested, crediting acclimation to the pro offense rather than arm talent as the missing piece. Wants the Colts to actively coach him on absorbing and avoiding hits the way Miami did with Tua Tagovailoa, given how much of his rookie season was lost to injury.
- 2024-04-23 — According to guest QB trainer Will Hewlett ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-23]]): Richardson has 'unbreakable' raw athleticism and is one of the smartest, most detail-oriented QBs Hewlett has trained (color-coded notes, elite recall of coaching points), calling him his favorite prospect since Patrick Mahomes and worth the #1 overall pick on traits alone even if other evaluation misses; [[Matt Waldman]] separately pushed back on the 'raw' label, arguing Richardson's pocket manipulation and spatial awareness already exceeded many lauded passers and reflected inexperience rather than a lack of processing ability.
- 2024-05-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (untracked co-host) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-23]]): despite a rough, up-and-down rookie season described as an 'incomplete grade' as a passer, Harmon believes Richardson processes well and gets through progressions quickly -- praise he attributes also to Colts OC Shane Steichen, who has 'gushed' about Richardson's fast transition to the pro playbook. Koh, citing analyst Derrick Klassen (untracked), notes Richardson is more accurate on a down-by-down basis than his highlight-reel misses suggest, though it's unclear that trait fully translates from his college profile. Both hosts flag health as the number-one swing factor, noting he has not stayed healthy in high school, college or the pros. Harmon expects Richardson's tendency to work off-script and through his reads to pair especially well with [[Josh Downs]]' skill set.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman pushed back hard on the media narrative that Richardson needs to 'evolve' his style, calling him a strong, natural pocket manager comparable to [[Lamar Jackson]] rather than a reckless Cam Newton-style runner. He drew a double standard: Matthew Stafford absorbed similar hits and was called 'brittle,' not told to become 'more intellectual' — a framing he says only gets applied to dual-threat QBs. He noted Richardson posted two top-5 QB finishes across only four healthy starts last year, ranks him personal QB4/5 (behind only Josh Allen, Jalen Hurts and Lamar Jackson, ahead of Patrick Mahomes, Joe Burrow, Dak Prescott, C.J. Stroud, Kyler Murray and Jordan Love), and says a top-3 fantasy QB finish this year is 'very well within the realm of possibility.' Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) agreed, citing coach Shane Steichen's staff calling Richardson 'probably the best athlete in football,' and noted he traded a 1.01 startup pick to acquire Richardson in a dynasty league.
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: Waldman says he's 'all in' on Richardson as his preferred upside QB this year, admitting he's 'basing that on a very narrow band of evidence' but buying into it anyway given a deep field of viable QB2-range starters this season means the downside risk of chasing upside at the position is mitigated. He separately argues Richardson's rushing threat should open up run creases for [[Jonathan Taylor]] by forcing defenses to account for the quarterback as a runner.
- 2024-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-11]]): Harmon says Richardson's rookie season was too incomplete to draw firm conclusions -- evaluators who liked him as a prospect still like him, skeptics still see the 'can't throw, not accurate' read confirmed. He cites Derek Klassen's Reception Perception prospect profile finding that Richardson's misses are so visually bad they create a false impression of overall inaccuracy, when down-to-down he actually graded out fairly accurate. Both hosts flag durability, dating back to high school, as the real swing risk. Harmon is bullish on offensive coordinator Shane Steichen's scheme fit (heavy use of repeated route concepts once something is working) and on the surrounding WR room -- [[Michael Pittman Jr.]], [[Josh Downs]], [[Adonai Mitchell]] -- giving Richardson a high ceiling and, in his view, a higher floor than the league currently credits him with, even though the group's overall floor stays uncertain given Richardson's own unknowns.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'I'm all about Anthony Richardson.' Agreeing with Football Guys colleague Dave Kluge, he names Richardson (rounds 5-7), [[Kyler Murray]] and [[Jayden Daniels]] (rounds 9-10) as the three quarterbacks offering league-winning upside at various price points. The supporting number is Tristan Cockcroft's 2023 figure of 0.45 fantasy points per snap for Richardson versus 0.36 for [[Josh Allen]]. Waldman's roster-construction point is that quarterback is deep enough in non-superflex leagues to take a swing on one of these upside arms while mitigating the risk with a steady veteran or a known week-to-week producer — the risk is real and it will cost you, but it is manageable.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): the overwrought reaction to Richardson's bad joint practice and uneven exhibition against Cincinnati is a false negative, and Waldman is happy about it because it lets him get Richardson later or reach comfortably. He explains joint practices are scripted — coaches ask each other for third-and-long reps and deliberately uncomfortable situations to test a quarterback — and that Richardson processed well enough and did not get rattled after mistakes. He dismisses Geno Stone's 'all he does is run' comments as trash talk amplified by beat writers who only know the Tim Tebow / Terrelle Pryor / Kordell Stewart scouting caricature, which Waldman says could not be further from the truth. His statistical case: in four starts last year Richardson posted two top-five finishes at 0.45 fantasy points per snap, with [[Josh Allen]] next at 0.36. He doubts that is repeatable but is willing to burn a pick to find out, because the double-digit-round QB pool covers the downside. He is drafting Richardson 'like last year was the tip of the iceberg.'
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Film and Theory)]]) [Dynasty]: he pushed back on calling Richardson merely 'revolutionary' — his view is that if Richardson hits the way he can hit, there will not be anyone like him for the next twenty years, and the right frame is a Barry Sanders, Jim Brown or Bo Jackson type of player where you say they do not make them like that in this era of football. It is explicitly a conditional upside take rather than a projection, framed against Harstad's Andrew Luck versus Robert Griffin III analogy for evolutionary versus revolutionary prospects.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman fully endorses the read that Richardson played week one at roughly 60 percent of his potential, with another 40 percent to come in reading the field. He nearly threw two interceptions and missed two shots to [[Adonai Mitchell]] — though one of those was really on a receiver being held up — yet still generated four or five big plays plus his running. The misses are inexperience with certain reps and looks rather than a skill ceiling, and Waldman quoted the coaching line that this is a superhero still learning to use his superpowers. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) said he already regretted his [[Kyler Murray]] and [[Jayden Daniels]] shares and wished all of them were Richardson.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): every Richardson game 'is going to be a Rorschach test.' He was not consistently accurate — egregious misses on two would-be long touchdowns to [[Adonai Mitchell]] — yet the opening-drive touchdown to [[Alec Pierce]] is 'a one-of-one throw that might be the best throw we see all year.' Harmon's point is that the variance is the product, not a bug to be resolved after one week: Richardson will attempt throws [[Gardner Minshew]] couldn't physically contemplate, which raises the ceiling for the whole receiver room. He and Koh (not tracked) agree the priority is health and development — 'every game doesn't need to be a referendum on Anthony Richardson' — and that the honest accounting is a season-long tally of highs versus lows.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): after a 9.9-point disaster, Waldman says keep starting him and do not drop him. He contextualizes the game — one egregious interception plus a lesser one, but also repeated drops from running backs, receivers and tight ends, a route miscommunication with [[Adonai Mitchell]] that Waldman puts on Mitchell, and multiple big run and pass plays called back by offensive line penalties. Richardson is the figurehead because he is the quarterback, but 'there was a lot more going on.' Crucially, this is still effectively his rookie year — he played four games in 2023, so he is only entering the second quarter of a rookie season and will swing between highs and lows. Waldman notes he spent up on Richardson and [[Jayden Daniels]] precisely because he believed there was a reservoir of capable streaming QBs later, while conceding not all of them proved as capable (naming [[Kirk Cousins]]). Separately he warns the Colts' run defense is bad enough to keep the offense off the field.
- 2024-09-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-17]]): Richardson 'is what he is right now' and Harmon declines to do hyper-aggressive week-to-week analysis on him — he is not a consistently accurate quarterback, period, and everyone knew that coming in. What we know is that he will produce great throws and awful throws; what we do not know is whether he ends up good or bad, and Harmon says the whole season is a development year. The fantasy consequence is that he lowers expectations for every Indianapolis pass catcher, and he makes the counterfactual explicit: if [[Derek Carr]] — a quarterback with a far lower ceiling — were the Colts starter, Harmon says he would have been drafting all of these Colts receivers. The offsetting positive is arm talent: Richardson's live, huge arm is what has lifted the veil on [[Alec Pierce]] as a downfield threat after years of dead-arm quarterbacks in Indianapolis.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Harmon affectionately calls Richardson a lunatic at quarterback — a developmental, frustrating player who will not be perfect, but who carries a 12.8 average depth of target against zone coverage (about 17 in Week 1), a figure Harmon calls wild. That is why [[Alec Pierce]]'s nines and posts are producing: almost any other quarterback takes the normal throw instead. The caveat Harmon raises is pressure — Richardson throws to his first read on only 33 percent of pressured dropbacks, a bottom-half figure, which is precisely why he thinks this team needs [[Josh Downs]] back as the second-read outlet, a role Downs already proved out in Richardson's brief rookie action.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-19]]): Waldman restated a stylistic comp he had recently made publicly — that Richardson reminds him of Otto Graham in style of play — and noted he was surprised the comp drew agreement rather than confusion. Co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) added supporting context that Graham held the pro football record for quarterback rushing touchdowns (roughly 50) until Steve Young broke it in the 1990s, was a genuine multi-sport athlete who also played professional basketball, and was as big as some of the linemen he played with. Waldman's point is about archetype rather than projection: a large, athletic passer whose designed and improvised running is a real part of the offense, which is a historically rare but not unprecedented profile.
- 2024-09-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-20]]): with only four rookie starts, Richardson's second season is still a continuation of the rookie evaluation — six career games in. The positives are real: he moves the pocket, makes pre-snap adjustments, changes plays, finds hot receivers, and can throw with great accuracy. But he swings — good management decisions in one moment, something reckless the next, and games where accuracy deserts him. Waldman has not seen a game totally fall apart, and calls the highs and lows exactly what you expect from six NFL games. He used the 2023 audible to [[Josh Downs]] up the seam — praised at the time as advanced for his years — against a throw this year delivered straight to a linebacker whose drop he appeared to stare down, as the illustration that development is not linear: when bandwidth gets consumed, things a player had down can temporarily unravel.
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): Richardson went 10 of 20 for 167 yards with zero touchdowns and two interceptions, one of which Koh calls maybe the worst throw he has ever seen — floated into the end zone to nobody. Koh's structural concern is volume: 20 attempts plus eight rushes is only about 28 chances to make a play, and Indianapolis has gone from 12th in plays per game last year at 64.4 to dead last at 50. He expected a low completion percentage but around 61 percent, not the roughly 50 percent Richardson is running. Harmon says Richardson has never been a consistently accurate quarterback, that some of the misses look worse because [[Adonai Mitchell]] has not always been in the right place, and that he does not want to render a verdict until there is a full season's catalog rather than hyper-focusing on small moments. He expects more bad games, with Pittsburgh next.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]): 'all the criticism is justified right now — he's a work in progress,' but Waldman calls giving up on Richardson from a fantasy perspective unwise: 'he still has the same upside that we saw last year. That hasn't evaporated.' Two supports: the rushing upside is the actual reason to roster him, and he has job security because 'the Colts believe in him and are working to get him to the future.' Waldman explicitly separates the evaluations — 'I don't care if he's a good NFL quarterback' — as part of his broader soapbox that fantasy quarterback value and NFL quarterback quality are different questions. - 2024-10-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-23]]) [Dynasty] [Redraft (Standard)]: previewing a Footballguys Gut Check column he calls one of the easiest he has written in a decade, Waldman says he watched the film and deliberately used none of it — instead comparing Richardson's first nine career games statistically to three unnamed quarterbacks who between them hold multiple MVPs and conference championship appearances across about 25 years of play. His conclusion: you would not know the difference between Richardson and those three at the same point, and the ways their careers went will surprise people. Advice: in redraft, do NOT drop him — he has faced defenses that are among the stingiest to fantasy quarterbacks and the schedule should ease; in dynasty, if anyone is foolish enough to sell him, take him every chance you get. The only real worry is injury, and all three comps drew the same injury hand-wringing yet stayed largely healthy. Waldman insists Richardson is inexperienced, not raw — a distinction he says critics blur — and that people (including former quarterbacks) declaring film-based development red flags are often chasing clicks or lack a long-term evaluation track record; he compares the dynamic to people calling [[Lamar Jackson]] raw when Waldman graded him the most advanced pocket passer in his class.
- 2024-10-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-24]]) [Dynasty]: his Gut Check article was titled 'enjoy the panic on Anthony Richardson' — buy Richardson now if you can. Waldman compared Richardson's first nine games (within about 100 snaps) to three quarterbacks with three MVPs and a Super Bowl appearance between them, one of whom is [[Lamar Jackson]]; touchdown rate, interception rate, rushing touchdown rate, completion percentage and yards per attempt all came out very similar. He rejects the Dan Orlovsky footwork critique as alarmist — Orlovsky watches film but has no developed scouting process for prioritizing what does and does not develop, and his frame of reference is a career backup rather than a starter juggling weekly game plans. Waldman notes [[Jameis Winston]], [[Josh Allen]], Lamar Jackson and Cam Newton all lacked refined feet early. The things that would actually worry him — misreading coverage, bad situational decisions in the red zone or on third down, reckless throws not caused by disguise, the profile of Kenny Pickett, Trubisky, Paxton Lynch, Drew Lock and Desmond Ridder — he does not see; Richardson's misses are him trying to make throws that worked at Florida and learning his NFL limits, the same curve [[Matthew Stafford]] rode. Caveat: the managers who bought Lamar and [[Jalen Hurts]] will be patient too, so the discount only exists in leagues where owners are panicking.
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): after a 10-of-32 game, Waldman charted the tape and said Twitter's list of eight mishandled passes was generous — but five of the eight should have been caught, either dropped or the receiver mispositioned on a well-placed ball. At 15-of-32 Richardson is closer to 250–275 yards with three missed touchdown opportunities, including back-to-back plays to [[Tyler Goodson]] and [[Michael Pittman Jr.]], plus a pass-interference call Waldman thought was incidental contact off the route break. Verdict: Richardson 'did enough to win this game.' Bob Harris (not tracked) countered with the box score: a 31.3% completion rate, the second worst by any passer with 30-plus attempts in ten seasons, behind only [[Josh Allen]]'s 30% game. Both agreed it is a fantasy bust right now; Harris said he will not drop him but will not start him until he sees a turnaround, accepting he may miss the breakout. Waldman's development case: judge unusual athletic archetypes — [[Josh Allen]], Cam Newton, [[Lamar Jackson]], John Elway, Brett Favre — not Bubby Brister, and on that basis Richardson is on par through nine or ten games. He also flagged the in-game tapping out as something that will bother people.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31]]): the benching for Joe Flacco is an organizational failure, not a verdict on Richardson. Angelo counted five clearly catchable drops in the Texans loss plus a Pittman pass-interference no-call; Waldman said Richardson could legitimately have finished 17-of-32 for 275 and five touchdowns, was pressured 64% of the time, and did it behind a receiver room in the bottom 10% of the league in drops and bottom 5% in average separation. Angelo faults Chris Ballard and Shane Steichen for benching a 22-year-old with under 250 attempts without telling Michael Pittman Jr., Josh Downs or Zaire Franklin, and pins the call on owner Jim Irsay's win-now impulsivity; he expects both to be fired. Waldman rejects the 'guys with these numbers after 10 games mostly busted' data lists as apples-to-oranges, arguing you compare by archetype instead — Elway and Bradshaw, alongside Josh Allen, Cam Newton and Lamar Jackson, whose statistical profiles after nine games he found on par with Richardson's. The 'tap out' both call overblown (third-and-23, third quarter); Waldman says he is a meticulous worker and that Bradshaw-style intermittent benching, communicated honestly, was the correct development plan.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31 (Tapping Out, Archetypes and Modeling)]]): the 'tap-out' furor is farcical — Waldman noted teammates like Zaire Franklin essentially said Richardson should have lied and claimed the wind was knocked out of him, and finds it absurd that an industry that markets players as heroes is coaching them to lie; Richardson takes an outsized hit rate shrugging off 300-pound linemen and asked for a breather on a third-and-23 that was not decisive. On the benching, co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) said he is not losing sleep over it: the question is not whether Richardson plays but whether the Colts develop him, and Philip Rivers, [[Aaron Rodgers]] and [[Patrick Mahomes]] all developed on the bench — the in-game-only development belief is an artifact of the rookie wage scale. Waldman rejected a StatHead comp list Jason Wood posted (whose Occam's-razor read was simply that Richardson is not good), calling it 'apples, kumquats, raisins and anvils'; he would model Richardson only against the physical-freak archetype — Terry Bradshaw, John Elway, Cam Newton, [[Lamar Jackson]], [[Josh Allen]] — players who need extra latitude to test one-of-one plays and make conceptual mistakes finding the limit, with JaMarcus Russell and Michael Vick as the necessary misses on that list. See [[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]].
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-31]]): Shane Steichen benching Richardson for [[Joe Flacco]] is 'a perfectly defensible decision' — the production simply is not good enough, full stop. Harmon acknowledged the film-versus-box-score argument (Dan Orlovsky's point) but dismissed it as trivially true given how bad the box score was, and noted the receiving corps deserves some blame. He said he was initially skeptical, then changed his mind after hearing Dominique Foxworth on Mina Kimes's show say the people in the building know more; Harmon's reading is that the helmet tap-out is 'a symptom, not a disease,' with the underlying issues around preparation and professionalism. He also flagged a structural problem: the Jalen Hurts-style design Steichen wants requires running Richardson, but Richardson gets hurt constantly, so the offense drifted to deep shots that suit nobody. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) went further and thinks the benching will actively help Richardson's development, citing Steve Young, Patrick Mahomes behind Alex Smith, and Aaron Rodgers as sit-and-learn precedents, David Carr as the counterexample of a QB ruined by playing too early behind a bad line, and [[Bryce Young]] as a QB who needed a mental break last year and never got one.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): the benching for [[Joe Flacco]] was almost certainly owner-driven rather than a Shane Steichen call — Waldman's read is that Jim Irsay got tired of national-media noise and forced the change, and that the staff did not even tell the offensive players in advance. He argues this contradicts everything Chris Ballard said at the draft: Ballard independently says the same things about Richardson that Waldman does, that it is not that he cannot throw but that he has not thrown, and that he needed reps to develop. Taking him off the field guarantees a second offense in three years or a new staff that argues for a new quarterback. For fantasy, Waldman separates the two questions entirely — see [[Fantasy Quarterback Value vs NFL Quarterback Quality]] — pointing to the first four games of 2023 and the 2024 opener as proof Richardson can be a phenomenal fantasy QB while still being a work in progress as an NFL passer. He also expects the benching to be reversed.
- 2024-11-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-07]]): Waldman used Richardson as his example of analysts over-reading a single mechanical blip — the reaction to Richardson bouncing and sinking deep at the end of a drop on one play and missing the throw, when he has thrown that same ball accurately hundreds of times going back to high school. Waldman's point is that observers fixate on 'the grammar' rather than the story arc of a development path, and that the real question is whether those things can be cleaned up, which typically takes longer to integrate into a player's game than people expect. Co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) added that nobody knows whether Richardson develops or not, and the only way to find out is to keep putting him in situations where he succeeds more often than he fails but fails often enough to stretch — arguing intermittent usage, benching him when the failure rate runs past the band and pushing him when he handles it, may be the best compromise for QB development.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): asked whether [[Joe Flacco]] gives the Colts a better chance to win, Waldman flatly rejects it — the benching was 'all about optics.' Flacco delivers a prettier stat line with a higher completion percentage; Richardson delivers the bigger plays that actually give you a chance at winning, and he will probably get better and better. Bob Harris (not tracked) notes the Colts are 1-3 with Flacco starting and 0-2 since the switch, and that Flacco threw a pick-six on the team's first offensive play and interceptions on each of the first two possessions. Waldman's one charitable reading is that the break lets Richardson watch what Flacco does well, the way [[Bryce Young]] learned from [[Andy Dalton]]. He also punctures the premise that the pre-benching situation was working: it was making people suffer too. Bob concedes the rushing equity has not come through as expected.
- 2024-11-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]]): whether the Colts are cooked depends entirely on the unknowable reason for the benching. If it was a developmental breather — let [[Joe Flacco]] play himself out of the job, get [[Josh Downs]] and [[Adonai Mitchell]] activated, then bring Richardson back to grow with them — Harmon calls that totally defensible and thinks Indianapolis has the best ecosystem of the four cooked-or-not offenses. If it is what Shane Steichen keeps saying out loud, that Flacco gives them a better chance to win, they are cooked, because Flacco has been worse by efficiency and EPA per drop-back. Harmon says he was 100 percent behind the benching: the film was not good, Richardson has not played well, and the body language pulling himself out of that last game was terrible. Koh (not tracked) warns about sequencing — once you put Richardson back in you cannot take him out again without it becoming 'we benched him because he stinks', which is the damaging version, and notes Richardson is getting hammered on social media, a confidence killer. Both expect Indianapolis to wait until Flacco is unambiguously toast.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): Richardson's issue is not physical but the what, why and when of the position, plus habits away from the building. Angelo argues the athletic gap between the most and least talented player on a 53-man roster is narrower than people think, since all of them are in the top quarter percent of the population; what is wide is what they do with the 20-plus hours a day they are not in meetings or practice. He believes Richardson has realized his habits were those of a 21-or-22-year-old rather than someone who wants to be a champion, and notes that being a famously good note-taker is only one aspect — the separator is working when nobody is watching. He groups Richardson with [[Malik Willis]] and Geno Smith as players who stared down the career fork in the road.
- 2024-11-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-18]]): on 'was the benching good for him' Waldman said that is what the Colts want you to believe, and treated Richardson's talk of 'the little things' as a scripted answer — he is not worried about the work ethic, having heard Richardson is a meticulous note-taker and strong in the classroom. What the benching really taught him is that he has to leverage every opportunity because he plays for an impulsive owner and the timeline will be short wherever he went. On the return start Waldman was emphatic: his receivers finally caught the ball, he threw bullets downfield, he manipulated defenders well in the middle of the field, and he completed a third-and-five to [[Josh Downs]] with a defender wrapped around him 'like a coat' that most quarterbacks do not make. Waldman criticized the unimaginative up-the-middle designed runs. He expects continued ups and downs but has Richardson back in every lineup, calling him the swing-for-the-fences play down the stretch given the schedule.
