# Ja'Lynn Polk

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name as "Jalen Polk"; normalized here to his real name, Ja'Lynn Polk.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-29 — According to guest **Eric Froton** (NBC Sports college football analyst — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, hosted solo by James Koh with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]) [Dynasty]: grades him NFL-caliber alongside Washington teammates [[Rome Odunze]] and Jalen McMillan (not otherwise profiled on this wiki), praising his tenacity as a route runner and competitiveness at all three levels of the field — "he's attacking the ball like he just wants it."
- 2024-02-29 — According to **Kevin Knight** (Falcons reporter, SB Nation's Falcoholic — guest, not a tracked expert), same episode ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]): names him a realistic Atlanta second-round target if the team doesn't land [[Malik Nabers]] or [[Rome Odunze]] at 8th overall — a receiver who "checks all the boxes" and would complement Atlanta's existing pieces well.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): the strength of his game is pass catching and positioning — high-points and pulls the ball away from a defender's reach into his chest, is adept working the boundary, and has the awareness and technique to get in bounds multiple ways. Sometimes allows targets into his chest with underhand hand position when overhand at the earliest point would be optimal, but shows on tape that he's capable of attacking at the earliest point when he squares the target — he just doesn't do it enough yet.
- 2024-04-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-23]]) [Dynasty]: Polk tested at 6'1"/203, ran a 4.52 (slower than expected), and posted a 37.5-inch vertical, but Harmon came away impressed with the tape — a smooth deep route runner with a huge catch radius, full extension in every direction, and zero drops over his sampled snaps. Alignment was versatile (60.6% outside, 35.8% slot, 3.6% backfield; 47.1% off-line/52.9% on-line), pointing to a flanker/slot hybrid rather than a true X. He posted a 58th-percentile success rate vs. press and 68.1% vs. man, with his best work on intermediate routes (digs, posts, curls) where Harmon said he generates late separation via buildup speed rather than initial burst. Contested-catch rate was 84.6% on 30.2% contested targets, nearly matching teammate Rome Odunze. Co-host James Koh (untracked) floated a Puka Nacua family comp — not literally the next Nacua, but sharing his rugged, tough playing style and deceptive long speed; Harmon separately comped him to a grittier Jordan Addison, projecting a very good No. 2 rather than a dominant No. 1. Harmon called him Washington's clear No. 2 in 2023 (69-1,159-9) and said he may rank him as high as a Day 2/priority-Round-2 prospect once stacked rankings update, ahead of teammate [[Jalen McMillan]].
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]) [Dynasty]: gave Ja'Lynn Polk the "best lunch-pail receiver" superlative, comparing his willingness to block in the run game and work the middle of the field to early [[Puka Nacua]] before the Rams knew what they had. Praised elite hands and ball tracking, with a timing/technique comp to [[Rome Odunze]]. Measurables: 6'1"/203, 4.52 forty, 37.5" vertical -- not top-end twitch, but Harmon says the catch radius is "four days," fully extending in every direction, and predicts quarterbacks will love him. Finished second to Odunze in class contested-catch rate. Harmon also ranked Polk over [[Ladd McConkey]] in his personal stack, citing better physicality, a wider release-move arsenal versus press (Polk 67.7% success vs. McConkey's class-low 44% vs. press), and better contested-catch ability -- arguing McConkey's press struggles functionally cap him from playing X.
