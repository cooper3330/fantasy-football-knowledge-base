# Ja'Lynn Polk

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name as "Jalen Polk"; normalized here to his real name, Ja'Lynn Polk.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-29 — According to guest **Eric Froton** (NBC Sports college football analyst — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, hosted solo by James Koh with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]) [Dynasty]: grades him NFL-caliber alongside Washington teammates [[Rome Odunze]] and Jalen McMillan (not otherwise profiled on this wiki), praising his tenacity as a route runner and competitiveness at all three levels of the field — "he's attacking the ball like he just wants it."
- 2024-02-29 — According to **Kevin Knight** (Falcons reporter, SB Nation's Falcoholic — guest, not a tracked expert), same episode ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]): names him a realistic Atlanta second-round target if the team doesn't land [[Malik Nabers]] or [[Rome Odunze]] at 8th overall — a receiver who "checks all the boxes" and would complement Atlanta's existing pieces well.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): the strength of his game is pass catching and positioning — high-points and pulls the ball away from a defender's reach into his chest, is adept working the boundary, and has the awareness and technique to get in bounds multiple ways. Sometimes allows targets into his chest with underhand hand position when overhand at the earliest point would be optimal, but shows on tape that he's capable of attacking at the earliest point when he squares the target — he just doesn't do it enough yet.
- 2024-04-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-23]]) [Dynasty]: Polk tested at 6'1"/203, ran a 4.52 (slower than expected), and posted a 37.5-inch vertical, but Harmon came away impressed with the tape — a smooth deep route runner with a huge catch radius, full extension in every direction, and zero drops over his sampled snaps. Alignment was versatile (60.6% outside, 35.8% slot, 3.6% backfield; 47.1% off-line/52.9% on-line), pointing to a flanker/slot hybrid rather than a true X. He posted a 58th-percentile success rate vs. press and 68.1% vs. man, with his best work on intermediate routes (digs, posts, curls) where Harmon said he generates late separation via buildup speed rather than initial burst. Contested-catch rate was 84.6% on 30.2% contested targets, nearly matching teammate Rome Odunze. Co-host James Koh (untracked) floated a Puka Nacua family comp — not literally the next Nacua, but sharing his rugged, tough playing style and deceptive long speed; Harmon separately comped him to a grittier Jordan Addison, projecting a very good No. 2 rather than a dominant No. 1. Harmon called him Washington's clear No. 2 in 2023 (69-1,159-9) and said he may rank him as high as a Day 2/priority-Round-2 prospect once stacked rankings update, ahead of teammate [[Jalen McMillan]].
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]) [Dynasty]: gave Ja'Lynn Polk the "best lunch-pail receiver" superlative, comparing his willingness to block in the run game and work the middle of the field to early [[Puka Nacua]] before the Rams knew what they had. Praised elite hands and ball tracking, with a timing/technique comp to [[Rome Odunze]]. Measurables: 6'1"/203, 4.52 forty, 37.5" vertical -- not top-end twitch, but Harmon says the catch radius is "four days," fully extending in every direction, and predicts quarterbacks will love him. Finished second to Odunze in class contested-catch rate. Harmon also ranked Polk over [[Ladd McConkey]] in his personal stack, citing better physicality, a wider release-move arsenal versus press (Polk 67.7% success vs. McConkey's class-low 44% vs. press), and better contested-catch ability -- arguing McConkey's press struggles functionally cap him from playing X.
- 2024-05-02 — According to Brandon Angelo ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): Polk is one of his favorite underrated landing spots, graded higher than teammate [[Jalen McMillan]] and [[Ricky Pearsall]] on his board. Angelo compared him to [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] as a 'safety blanket' type — physical in the run game as a blocker, more versatile than given credit for (especially in the red zone), and a high-leverage-situation player. He projects a long, reliable Tyler Boyd-type career and a natural fit alongside [[Javon Baker]] to make [[Drake Maye]]'s life easier. Angelo also noted Polk was the first non-Maye offensive skill player drafted under new GM Eliot Wolf/coach Jerod Mayo's regime, calling that meaningful.
- 2024-05-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-02]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon and Koh disagreed sharply on New England taking Polk at pick 2.5. Koh called it too early for a player who is 'just a guy' adding depth, not a true X. Harmon loved it, saying Polk sat in his 'priority round two' bucket on the Reception Perception stack rankings — the same tier he'd have graded a No. 32 overall selection — and defended the value versus alternatives he ranked higher (Coleman, Xavier Legette, Adonai Mitchell, Jermaine Burton), arguing those players had higher ceilings but lower floors. Polk graded 2nd in the class in zone-coverage success rate (83.5%, behind only Rome Odunze), 68.1% versus man, and 69.2% versus press, winning primarily on intermediate and buildup-speed vertical routes rather than through elite top-end speed. Harmon projects him as a flanker/slot Z (roughly 60/40 outside-inside) rather than an X, giving rookie QB [[Drake Maye]] a reliable, tight-coverage-beating target — a needed reset after Maye's college receivers. Harmon says the Polk pick combined with drafting [[Javon Baker]] in round four is what ultimately sold him on New England's overall receiver draft.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is 'feeling it' on Ja'Lynn Polk as a good inside-out slot receiver capable of winning above the rim on fade/seam routes similar to undersized tight ends, but with more after-the-catch ability than a pure fade-and-seam specialist. He comps Polk's early role to the Detroit Lions' Kenny Golladay-type usage, projecting he could eventually kick outside to X/split end. With [[Jacoby Brissett]] likely at QB, Waldman puts Polk in a 500-700 yard range for 2024, calling both Polk and [[Javon Baker]] dynasty-interesting but redraft bye-week options at best right now.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo names Polk as a quiet-camp player who sizzles once games start, projecting he could lead the Patriots in receptions this year. He's not the sexiest athlete but profiles like other long-term, down-to-down reliable producers — citing [[Noah Brown]], [[Puka Nacua]], Jacoby Meyers, [[Chris Godwin]] and [[Khalil Shakir]] as the archetype — because coaches can build an offense around a receiver whose role is always known. Angelo praises Polk's blocking, catch-point strength, and physical after-the-catch play, comparing his sneaky pre-draft profile to [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]'s, whose rookie-year competence wasn't obvious on tape but showed up in games through chemistry with the quarterback and situational awareness.
- 2024-07-04 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-04]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo said he told his subscribers Polk profiles as a lighter version of [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] -- an extension of the run game who rarely makes mistakes and does the little things right -- which should let him carve out early usage in a Patriots passing game with almost nothing established beyond [[Rhamondre Stevenson]]. Compared directly with fellow rookie [[Javon Baker]], Waldman argued Baker has the higher ceiling and more explosive plays, while Angelo countered that Polk is the more technically sound route runner even though Baker is cleaner at the catch point. Both expect Polk to keep ascending into a larger role as the Patriots offense, under new head coach Jerod Mayo, works through a season of rookie growing pains.
- 2024-07-05 — James Koh (Reception Perception co-host, not a tracked expert) said in [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-05]] [Dynasty] that he views Ja'Lynn Polk as an underrated Patriots second-round pick whose skill set -- bigger, more physical, better able to play on the line than fellow rookie-era slot piece Demario Douglas -- fits well with new QB Drake Maye's need for a receiver who can win over the middle in the intermediate area and take shots off play action. Koh said he wants Polk to lead the Patriots in targets as a rookie and sees him working the more traditional boundary/intermediate role while Douglas stays a pre-snap motion/man-beating slot option, with the two working in concert on third downs. This is a scouting projection rather than Reception Perception charted data, since Polk has no NFL sample yet.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman said [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] has 'nice developmental upside' and should be able to contribute immediately in certain sets given his ability to win the ball in the air and play the boundary, with some after-the-catch skill, but rated teammate [[Javon Baker]] as the more refined overall route runner of the Patriots' two rookie receivers.
- 2024-07-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-09]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon is 'actually pretty high' on Ja'Lynn Polk, citing an 81st-percentile success rate vs. zone (83.5%, top-3 in his sampled class), no charted drops, and above-prospect-average success on digs, posts and curls, calling him one of the best contested-catch receivers in the class alongside teammate [[Rome Odunze]]. His weakness is the deep/vertical route tree (subpar on nines, corners, outs; solid-not-elite 68.1% vs. man, 69.2% vs. press). Harmon thinks Polk profiles as a flanker who fits an offense working the middle of the field -- drawing a comparison to the Joe Flacco-era Cleveland Browns passing scheme new OC Alex Van Pelt may lean on with [[Drake Maye]] -- and floated using Polk the way the Rams used [[Puka Nacua]] as a rookie, in the dirty-work middle-of-field role, without predicting Nacua-level volume, just a similar usage pattern.
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said Ja'Lynn Polk projects as the Patriots' WR1 by target share -- with no clear pecking order behind [[Demario Douglas]] in the slot, and competition from [[K.J. Osborne]], an unhealthy [[Kendrick Bourne]], a longshot Juju Smith-Schuster roster bid, and unproven Tyquan Thornton -- but cautioned that being New England's top receiver doesn't make him a fantasy starter. He praised Polk's route running, contested-catch ability and after-the-catch skill as giving him a real shot to lead the team in receiving, but said to draft him as WR3-caliber value with a WR3 ceiling, not higher.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) calls Polk his favorite late-round bet in New England, arguing the Fantasy Life wide receiver supermodel underrates him because it can't fully calibrate for having shared a Washington offense with [[Rome Odunze]] and [[Jalen McMillan]]. Polk isn't a vertical stretcher and isn't elite anywhere, but he is above average at every level of the field and good with the ball in his hands, which gives him broad application in an offense with almost no competition — JuJu Smith-Schuster is a shell, [[Kendrick Bourne]] is coming off an ACL and never an elite player, Tyquan Thornton and Jalen Reagor are non-factors. McFarland says Polk is going far too late at Sleeper 161, ESPN 169 and FFPC 182. Waldman agrees with the read: good at a lot of things, great at none.
- 2024-08-27 — According to guest Derek Klassen (not a tracked expert) and host James Koh (not tracked) citing Reception Perception charting ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): Polk profiles at an 85-plus percent success rate on digs, curls and comebacks, which Koh offers as direct support for Klassen's claim that Polk 'absolutely eats' in the intermediate area. Klassen said on draft night that drafting Polk proved New England knew what kind of quarterback it had taken in [[Drake Maye]] — a passer who wants to throw dig and deep curl routes into tight windows and needs a receiver who will pin the ball on his body, find it and take a hit to make the catch. He believes New England now has enough of that archetype, alongside [[Kendrick Bourne]] and [[Demario Douglas]], to shore up Maye's intermediate accuracy numbers.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): both Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) argue New England's answer is more Polk on the field. Koh notes the Washington rookie has just 18 yards on three catches while Hunter Henry (127 yards) and Austin Hooper (36) lead the team; in the Week 2 loss to Seattle, Brissett's 149 passing yards were 109 to Henry and 18 to Polk. Harmon calls Polk a good prospect and says he is picture perfect for a scheme that wants to run under center off a strong run game and take intermediate shots off play action — exactly what the current top two route runners, [[Demario Douglas]] (3.0 average depth of target, slant-flat merchant) and [[Tyquan Thornton]], cannot provide.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-10]]): Polk has looked 'exactly like the player I imagined him to be' but is being misused. Over the two games Harmon charted he has been on the line of scrimmage on 73.9% of routes and in the slot on only 12.2% — essentially the X — and Harmon does not love that: Polk's RP profile is a significantly better zone beater than man beater (college 83.5% vs zone, 81st percentile; 68.1% vs man, only 42nd percentile), and his 2024 charting mirrors it almost exactly at 83.9% vs zone and 64.7% vs man. He is a crafty over-the-middle worker — 17.4% of his routes are digs at an 83% success rate, matching his 84.6% college dig mark, his best route. Harmon wants him as a flanker who kicks into the slot, and likes Koh's suggestion that a returning Kendrick Bourne could take the X so Polk can move off the ball; the current alternative, [[Kayshon Boutte]], he calls not a good player. He notes Polk spent the offseason throwing with [[Drake Maye]] on the second team.
- 2024-10-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-15]]): another very quiet game — three targets, two drops, with head coach Jerod Mayo calling it out afterward. Harmon's line is blunt: the book on Polk is that he is supposed to be the reliable one, so when the ball comes his way he has to catch it, and he cannot have crushing drops. Separately he dislikes the deployment, repeating a take from the prior week that Polk has been given X receiver usage when he would be best served off the ball, rotating between flanker and slot. Harmon would like to see Polk and [[Demario Douglas]] alternate roles, since their strengths differ. New England, in his view, is a room of WR3s that still needs a real X, and Polk has not changed that.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]): Polk has ceded the big-play role in New England to [[Kayshon Boutte]] because 'Ja'Lynn Polk can't hold onto the ball right now' — repeated drops. Waldman offered one mitigating factor, that 'some of those throws aren't well-placed for him' given [[Drake Maye]]'s placement issues, and Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) called that generous.
- 2024-11-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-20]]): Polk's rookie year 'makes me feel like I am a crazy person.' In college he was a flanker/slot who played 35-40% of sampled snaps inside; in New England the rookie report has him outside on 84.9% of sampled snaps and on the line of scrimmage 68.2%, running a nine route on 20.6% of sampled routes with 6.9% corner routes — vertical, contested usage that nothing in his profile supports. Harmon calls it the same set-up-to-fail deployment he flagged for [[Quentin Johnston]], and comps the trajectory to [[Jonathan Mingo]], misdeployed as a rookie and traded midway through year two. The counterweight: Polk has not helped himself — 15 routes and no catches on Sunday plus a false start Jerod Mayo said cost a touchdown, a stretch of drops and contested-catch errors that got him benched for weeks, then complaining about it on Instagram. Harmon: the two things he loved as a prospect were the hands and the dirty-work blocking, and 'his blocking sucks and he can't catch in the pros.' He is not completely willing to give up — the zone success rate is solid, not special, and he could be a useful flanker/slot role player — but nothing in the rookie data says he has been unlocked. Koh (not tracked) noted Polk has no catch over 20 yards and only one over 15 all season.
- 2024-12-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-03]]): 'Polk has been a disaster' and 'an even bigger mess' than [[Jalen McMillan]] — one of the clearest data points for Harmon's view that the depth of the 2024 receiver class has been underwhelming below the top five. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) noted New England traded down out of the [[Ladd McConkey]] pick to take Polk, which Harmon said means you have to 'bang, bang, bang the Patriots for that one too'. Both expressed surprise at the Washington receiver group as a whole, given the three of them were widely treated as NFL players coming out.
- 2025-01-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-13]]) [Dynasty]: Polk is the most baffling rookie of the last couple of seasons and posted the worst rookie season Harstad's model has ever recorded. Harmon's read is role malpractice rather than pure talent failure: Polk was a classic flanker-slot hybrid and dirty-work player, and New England deployed him for long stretches as the vertical boundary X, a role for which there was zero evidence on his college film — a parallel to how [[Quentin Johnston]] was misused out of the 2023 class. Harmon concedes the counter, that a prospect he graded with wonderful hands and tenacious blocking could neither catch nor block as a pro, but attributes much of that to being out of position and out of confidence. He is going to be one of the few to leave the door slightly cracked — though far less emphatically than he does for [[Rashad Bateman]] or [[Curtis Samuel]] — and speculates Polk and [[Tetairoa McMillan]] swapping teams might have produced the same results. Both hosts agree everything in New England was poisoned, making [[Drake Maye]]'s rookie year more impressive.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo defends Polk's rookie year as an opportunity failure rather than a talent verdict. New England had no consistent target on the outside, in the middle of the field or in the short-to-intermediate areas besides Hunter Henry, and Angelo argues Jerod Mayo wanted rookies to grow up from game one instead of growing into a larger role — producing regression across the season when rookies should progress, the way [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], a similar style of player, did. Polk was also miscast: he is best inside as a chain mover and reliable third-down option who can be moved around in 12 personnel because he blocks and is tough, but was played at X alongside [[Kayshon Boutte]]. Both hosts call him a slot receiver in the [[Demario Douglas]] / [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] / [[Calvin Austin III]] mold. Angelo warns a coaching change plus a likely significant WR addition ([[Tee Higgins]] is in play) makes rebounding hard.
- 2025-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-05]]): Harmon still thinks Polk can be useful in the NFL, though he is careful to say he does not have the candle lit for him the way he has for a [[Rashad Bateman]] type. His argument is misuse rather than inability — Polk's best role is in the slot, and he points to Eliot Wolf's comment that the team thought Polk would be a plug-and-play guy as an admission the coaches played him at the wrong position. James Koh adds that giving up on a high pick that fast would be inadvisable and that New England already has slot bodies in [[Demario Douglas]] and [[Kendrick Bourne]], which is part of why he does not see a clean [[Cooper Kupp]] fit there.
- 2025-02-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-28]]): Polk's rookie year was 'nightmarish' — 0.35 yards per route run — but Harmon splits the blame. Polk quite literally dropped his opportunities and, more damningly, failed to win contested catches despite good college hands, and his blocking regressed from a prospect strength. But New England lined him up outside on about 80 percent of snaps (only 16.7 percent in the slot) and asked him to run vertical routes at X — a role he never played at Washington, where [[Rome Odunze]] was the X and Polk was the dirty-work flanker who kicked inside on 35-40 percent of snaps. Harmon notes Elliot Wolf's comments that Polk was 'overloaded' and reads the front office as blaming the coaching staff. Verdict: not a sunk cost, but you are effectively rebuilding him from scratch under a new staff, and co-host James Koh (not tracked) was more pessimistic, saying he saw nothing even on non-vertical routes.
- 2025-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-21]]): Harmon thinks Polk was partly set up to fail as a rookie — New England played him a lot at X and asked him to run go routes, things he never did at Washington, where his best work was as an over-the-middle threat. He believes the front office (Elliot Wolf still in place) still wants Polk to hit and will not give up on its own pick, so the odds are not zero, but they clearly do not favor him. James Koh (co-host, not tracked) is harsher: with [[Stefon Diggs]] visiting, [[Mack Hollins]] signed and a new coaching staff that did not draft him, Koh sees a world where Polk gets pushed to the side entirely, and notes his confidence looked shot last year. Both frame Polk as a slot-flanker rather than the perimeter player New England actually needs.
- 2025-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-28]]): Harmon repeats that he liked Polk as a prospect while conceding his rookie year 'went about as bad as it could possibly go.' Structurally, he does not see Polk as an answer to New England's real hole, because Polk is not a player who beats man coverage on the outside — he is more of a zone beater and a classic number-two type. That is why Harmon frames the [[Stefon Diggs]] signing as filling the man-beating role and expects Polk to compete in an open scramble for snaps behind Diggs, Demario Douglas and [[Mack Hollins]], with another rookie receiver likely added on top.
- 2025-04-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-29]]): the new New England regime looks to be stiff-arming Polk, in James Koh's phrasing (Koh is not a tracked expert), after adding [[Stefon Diggs]] and drafting [[Kyle Williams]]. Harmon notes Elliot Wolf's front office said publicly they expected Polk to be pro-ready and an instant impact, which to Harmon indicts the coaching, and he says he will never get over the fact that Polk blocked hard and caught everything in college and then could not block or catch as a rookie. Harmon frames the new additions as the team simply wanting more options after being forced to play [[Kayshon Boutte]] on nearly every snap.
- 2025-05-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-23]]): Polk's rookie Reception Perception profile was a disaster and Harmon explicitly copped to 'a major miss' on his pro-readiness — 15 games, 33 targets, 12 catches, 87 yards, a 36% catch rate and no 100-yard game. The collapse came exactly where Harmon graded him strongest: over 80% contested catch rate as a prospect (comparable to [[Rome Odunze]]) fell to 20%, drop rate went to 10.7% after essentially no drops in his prospect sample, and he was the worst blocking receiver Harmon charted from the class. Success rates: 55% vs man, 61.5% vs press, 75% vs zone. Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) argue New England set him up to fail — 81.3% of snaps outside, 61.8% on the line and a nine route on 22.5% of routes (41% success, non-functional) despite Polk running the nine only 12% of the time at Washington, poorly, from a 35.8% slot / 47% off-line alignment. Both cite Elliot Wolf calling him a 'plug and play' guy and later hinting the staff overloaded him, and the benching-and-sulking episode Harmon compares to [[Elijah Moore]]. Bright spots: 76% on digs at 14.5% volume and 71% on curls. Harmon says he is not a fast or sudden player — a 'locomotive' build-up mover who should be a detached flanker/slot dirty-worker (the [[Puka Nacua]] rookie-year role, not the talent), and that he cannot run slants (71.4%). With [[Stefon Diggs]], [[Mack Hollins]], [[Demario Douglas]] and third-rounder [[Kyle Williams]] added, Harmon sees little playing time left and would already take Williams over Polk. - 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]): Harmon concedes Polk 'is going to go down as a miss for me' — he liked him as a dirty-work number two or three prospect and Polk flopped to 'epic proportions' in year one. But Harmon argues he was set up to fail: in his prospect profile Polk took 35.8 percent of sampled snaps in the slot, 3.6 percent in the backfield and was off the line for 47 percent, profiling as a flanker/big slot who won over the middle; in New England he took 81.3 percent of snaps outside and 61.8 percent on the line, running nine routes on 22.5 percent and corner routes on 8.7 percent of his snaps — two of his worst college routes. He looked best on digs and needed a free release as a build-up-speed player. Harmon still charges him with his own failings: immaturity, drops, terrible contested-catch work despite good college hands. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) puts the miss on the Patriots' usage rather than Harmon's evaluation. Harmon now has Polk fourth at best and possibly not making the roster.
