# Mark Andrews

## Expert Takes

- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'completely fuck that' to the idea that Andrews is washed. Waldman says he went to multiple scouts and athletic trainers about the term after hearing it applied to running backs and receivers, and was told the claim that observers can see a player is washed is one of the most ridiculous assessments out there — after 21 years of scouting he says it's hard enough to judge speed and explosiveness at a player's peak, let alone judge loss. He cites past wrong calls on [[David Montgomery]] and [[Nick Chubb]]. On the tape, Andrews clicked his heels on an out route and fell, but was otherwise open on intermediate routes against zone; he has always been a zone player rather than a man-beater, and Kansas City has clamped him for five straight games (about 20 yards a game, no touchdowns) — that is not new. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) added that Andrews looked gassed after limited practice following his car accident and warned against broad conclusions from a single data point.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): stay patient with Andrews through Week 3 despite an ugly start. Waldman flagged genuinely concerning underlying data — snap share down to about 30 percent and Andrews off the field in some formations, with no targets — but contextualized it as a run-heavy script with Baltimore up 21-6 at halftime. He sees no sign of Andrews or [[Travis Kelce]] slowing down physically; the looks are simply not there because of how the offenses are flowing. His broader rule: hold marquee players through the first five to six weeks even at 0-4 or 0-5, because you can still find receiver help later on a first-come-first-serve basis without dumping a star. He rejected Mike Dempsey's 'ceremonial benching' device. The one concession: if he had to come off a position early, it would be tight end, since replacement production there is findable — while admitting that is exactly the way you end up cutting Andrews too soon.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10]]) [Dynasty]: not the Mark Andrews of old, and Baltimore's offense may simply not need that player anymore. Angelo raises a second factor others have mentioned — that the car crash Andrews was in may have been more significant, injury-wise, than was let on — and concludes he doubts we see the 1,000-yard Andrews again. He is happy at the prospect of rolling into next season with [[Charlie Kolar]] and [[Isaiah Likely]] as the tight end room instead, and rates Kolar the better blocker of the two.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): revisiting the Week 1-4 debate, Waldman defends the position that it was acceptable to drop Andrews if you had a pressing need at another producing position and could land that player, on the reasoning you could probably reacquire him. Bob Harris (not tracked) supplies the arc: Andrews was TE29 through Week 6, TE20 through Week 7, and is TE8 now, finally hitting a 20 percent target share for the first time all season this week. Waldman's diagnosis is usage, not decline — Andrews was the deeper-route runner drawing the coverage gravity while [[Isaiah Likely]] worked underneath for the yardage, and [[Zay Flowers]] has usurped Andrews as Baltimore's top receiving asset. He also credits [[Lamar Jackson]]'s expanded game under the coordinator he wanted, versus Greg Roman's middle-of-the-field offense in which Andrews was 'the only game in town.' Lesson: read the usage context before writing off a good player.
- 2025-01-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-21]]) [Dynasty]: Andrews had a 'game from hell' — the drop on the two-point conversion has to be caught, though Harmon thinks [[Lamar Jackson]] could have delivered a better ball. Harmon pushes back hard on the 'usually sure-handed Mark Andrews' framing: he says Andrews drops passes plenty and it is 'kind of an issue for him' in critical situations, so the drop was not surprising. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) notes Andrews turns 29 with a $17M cap hit and only $6M in dead money and calls him an absolute cut candidate, saying he would be surprised if Andrews is a Raven next year; Harmon largely agrees, while allowing that Jackson may cape up for him and a lesser deal is possible. Both doubt Andrews takes a hometown discount on what is his last chance at a big contract, with Baltimore holding just over $17M in cap space and needing room for Ronnie Stanley.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman took Andrews at Underdog TE6 over [[T.J. Hockenson]], on the strength of catching passes from [[Lamar Jackson]], and explicitly set aside the playoff drop. Untracked co-host Bob Harris called the moment this generation's Jackie Smith — the reliable tight end who misses the incomprehensible play — and said 'we remember the last thing we saw', so he is watching whether it drives Andrews' price down, though he thinks TE6 is around the right price. Harris also noted Baltimore is talking about other people's futures more than Andrews', which probably works in his favor.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]) [Best Ball]: the four-million-dollar roster bonus Baltimore paid makes it likely Andrews is back, and his Underdog ADP of TE6 is a 'feel it' — Waldman would take him around that spot. He acknowledges it may feel risky given the optics of how Andrews's season started and ended, but says the tape showed the player can still play and he expects a good rebound year. Bob Harris (not tracked) agrees, framing it as a barren-wasteland position where you should grab a piece capable of scoring touchdowns.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Angelo questions the assumption that Andrews is still one of Baltimore's primary options — at this age he asks what Andrews offers that is genuinely high-end, and answers 'probably not much': he is out there because he is consistent, a veteran and a Pro Bowler, while [[Isaiah Likely]] offers the higher-end capabilities. [[Matt Waldman]] treats Andrews' primacy as an assumption worth granting only for the sake of the segment, and raises the scenario where Baltimore keeps Andrews and Likely in the red zone but pulls a receiver so [[DeAndre Hopkins]] works inside.
- 2025-08-13 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-13]]: Andrews is 'done' and Baltimore continuing to run heavy 12 personnel around him is hurting the team — Koh would not remove him entirely, but would deploy him far more sparingly, in third-and-medium spots where [[Lamar Jackson]] trusts him, and open up more 11 personnel now that [[DeAndre Hopkins]] is in the building. [[Matt Harmon]] pushed back in the same segment, saying Koh was being 'a little harsh': Andrews is definitely in decline but Harmon would not call him done, and he noted [[Isaiah Likely]] was hurt entering the season and may miss games, which he believes is why Likely's reported extension never got finished. Both agreed the tight-end-heavy structure limits slot availability for [[Zay Flowers]] and [[Rashad Bateman]].
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): Andrews is Harris's number five 'might be wrong about' — he keeps winding up with him because Andrews does not go aggressively in drafts, but the fear is simply 'what if he's cooked'. His read on 2024: a horrible start, then a season saved almost entirely by touchdowns, so the year-end numbers look fine but 'you were vomiting the entire time'. Mitigating factors — Andrews may not have been himself coming off the severe injury at the end of the prior season, and the [[Isaiah Likely]] injury (early August, initially maybe-Week-1) is what rescued his outlook. Harris expects this to be his last year in Baltimore either way and half-jokes he could be cut on today's cutdown day. Ben Gretch (guest, not tracked) adds a low-key touchdown angle: Andrews took four tush-push rushes late in the regular season plus four more in two playoff games, and Harbaugh said this offseason that if they run it again Andrews is the guy. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): a discouraging opener. Harris saw Andrews early and then did not see him at all after that, which he says does not feel good after one week, and it is especially concerning because it came with [[Isaiah Likely]] out of the lineup, meaning the target competition at tight end was as thin as it will be. He also noted [[DeAndre Hopkins]] scoring an unbelievable one-handed touchdown in this game while saying he still would not want him.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Andrews apparently does still work here — his first and only catch came with two minutes left in the first half against Cleveland. Harris allows that [[Lamar Jackson]]'s instinct still appears to be to look for Andrews in the end zone, and there was a red-zone throw to him at the six that was in his hands before a defender yoinked it away, leading to a field goal; Andrews also drew an offensive pass interference that wiped a play out. But the verdict is blunt: Andrews has got to be a must-bench, maybe even a must-drop. Harris frames the comparison elsewhere in the show — even a one-legged [[Brock Bowers]] can outdo him.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): Baltimore made a visible effort to end the Andrews slump on Monday night — the very first play of the game was play-action to Andrews for 19 yards, he added another on that opening drive and two more on the first drive of the third quarter, then a pylon touchdown right after the ill-advised Derrick Henry halfback option pass, plus a long garbage-time touchdown once the game was out of reach. Harris admitted he sat Andrews in multiple leagues and does not regret it ('it was a sane thing to do'), noting the final line is a little artificially inflated and that any tight end can post back-to-back two-catch weeks. But 'Lamar hasn't lost Andrews's number,' so Harris will rank him more like a starter in Week 4 even with [[Isaiah Likely]] possibly returning.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): it is still Andrews's show in Baltimore, and Waldman does not believe the Ravens trade him — they will stick with him through the playoff contention window they have and ride it for all it is worth, which caps [[Isaiah Likely]]'s standalone value.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06]]): an admittedly easy buy with [[Lamar Jackson]] back — Waldman thinks Andrews is about to really start rolling. He pushes back on the [[Isaiah Likely]] hype for a structural reason: Likely is a free agent next year and Waldman thinks it more likely Likely commands a salary big enough to send him elsewhere, leaving Baltimore happy to roll with Andrews and possibly re-sign [[Charlie Kolar]] — a better receiver than people expect and a better blocker than Likely — as a one-two punch that is not especially fantasy-relevant beyond an occasional matchup flex.
