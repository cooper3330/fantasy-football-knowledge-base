# Breece Hall

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]: uses Hall as the counter-example to the episode's pace-control thread. Hall "tries to hit the home run every time — either you're getting a home run or you're striking out." His comp: "that old baseball player who's hitting .198 but he has 20 home runs. It's great when it works, but a lot of times you have to control the tempo of your own run and get what's blocked." He attributes the Jets giving Dalvin Cook more work partly to this, citing on-air comments from Robert Saleh.
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, the fuller and more sympathetic version — and an explicit bullish forecast attached to it. "With a better line, even without the skills you're mentioning, he's going to be a big-time yardage producer. It's going to be scary." The knock is specific: "he averts one disaster at a time." Hall processes what is immediately in front of him extremely fast, but "he's not maybe seeing two steps ahead, he's not seeing it in the full scope" the way [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and [[James Cook]] do. Waldman's framing is that this makes him *more* impressive, not less — "the fact that he's literally just going, okay, what do I do next, what do I do next, and he can get eight yards on that play is incredible" — "but it's not optimal for what he could be."

**Read:** both hosts are describing a ceiling-raiser, not a bust flag — the
projection is that improved processing (or an improved offensive line) unlocks a
volume producer. The near-term risk is the workload split that the boom/bust
style invites.
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): projects Hall around 300 touches, 1,600 total yards and double-digit touchdowns, calling him possibly a top-five back in pure talent -- noting he wasn't fully himself in the 2023 opener post-ACL but got his explosiveness back by season's end and 'makes it look easy,' the same way he did at Iowa State. Waldman said as a runner alone he could hit 12 TDs and 1,400-1,500 yards, with 2,000 total yards possible if health holds; the swing factor keeping him at 1,600 rather than higher is whether the Jets offensive line stays healthy. He floated Hall as an RB1 usurper especially if Aaron Rodgers has a strong year and weapons (Mike Williams, Allen Lazard, Malachi Corley, Xavier Gipson) develop, with Braelon Allen also mentioned as a nice late-round Jets stash. Bob Harris agreed on the touch range but called Hall RB1 upside a 'possibility, not expectation,' saying with a true first pick today it would still be [[Christian McCaffrey]].
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman names Hall as one of the strongest threats to unseat [[Christian McCaffrey]] for RB1 overall, saying he now has 'an eighth to a quarter of himself back' as an athlete a full year removed from his ACL injury, and that his system fit remains strong. He still says take McCaffrey ahead of Hall given the latter's ceiling is unproven at full health, but ranks Hall among the top three candidates in the field, alongside [[Bijan Robinson]], to reach the RB1-overall outcome.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): asked to take Hall over [[Bijan Robinson]], Waldman said he feels it but that the gap is closer than the framing suggests — he considers the two very close and would not be surprised if either finished as the overall RB1 over [[Christian McCaffrey]], naming them the two best-positioned in that order while refusing to claim he can divine the difference. The bull case is receiving work: [[Aaron Rodgers]] has complimented Hall's route running, went six-of-six in a red-zone session with two of those to Hall, and said afterward that his scouting report had not flagged those skills and that he had never been in an offense where the primary red-zone read was the running back. Waldman adds that the Jets offensive line has old and injured spots that are great in name, so Rodgers may check down often — especially if he and [[Garrett Wilson]] are still out of sync. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) was emphatically on Hall.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): Hall is his top running back not named [[Christian McCaffrey]], a little ahead of [[Bijan Robinson]], and picking inside the top five he is likelier to take Hall. But he refuses to make it emphatic: he calls the two close to even and says the safer player is actually Robinson because Atlanta's offensive line is younger, better and has played together for three years while the Jets line is only good on paper — 'the great part of the iceberg nobody wants to look at.' Hall's edge is straight upside via receiving work; Waldman projects 78 targets and 59 catches, arguing the Jets would need to dominate games for a big touch increase and that [[Garrett Wilson]], [[Mike Williams]], [[Allen Lazard]] and [[Tyler Conklin]] will take targets from a healthy [[Aaron Rodgers]]. Bob Harris (not tracked) leaned Hall harder, citing his league-leading ~90 running back targets last year and Rodgers calling Hall his primary red-zone read.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): off a 7-of-8, 52-yard, one-touchdown receiving line plus 14 carries for 62 yards, Waldman says Hall is not literally the Jets' best receiver but is 'the best weapon on the New York Jets without a doubt.' He singles out a split-out wheel route against a Titans linebacker as a technique showcase — the way Hall attacked and tracked the ball on a vertical route was, in Waldman's framing, exactly what [[Treylon Burks]] has failed to do, and Hall did it better than the Titans' own former first-round receiver. He understands why [[Aaron Rodgers]] has made Hall the first read on many passing plays and says Rodgers flagged Hall's red-zone receiving value in camp, something Waldman admits he had underrated.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): reacting to a headline built on Robert Saleh saying Hall had a bad day but still has plenty of juice, Waldman was incredulous the question was even being asked — 'fuck yeah Breece Hall still has more juice,' and he is still probably the best back in football at this stage, or at least a top three to four back. He dismissed the small sample of games in an offense that has not looked good, and drew a sharp usage distinction: far more is demanded of Hall than of [[Braelon Allen]]. He used the segment to warn against reading coach quotes as evaluations — go back and hear the question that prompted the answer, since coaches are more polite publicly and may simply be repeating an absurd question back to the room. He also took a shot at the New York media as the most insulated people he has met.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): Waldman still likes Hall a lot and calls him a genuinely instinctive runner — a word he says he normally rejects for backs — but the Jets offense in London was 'stupid', and specifically the play calling. Taking the ball out of Hall's hands as often as they are, while the offense goes nowhere, denies a rhythm runner the chance to get into a rhythm, and Waldman says the same problem applies to taking the ball out of [[Aaron Rodgers]]' hands on predictable, unsuccessful plays. He allows that [[Braelon Allen]] is playing well but does not accept that as the reason. Both hosts took a victory lap for having recommended [[Saquon Barkley]] and [[Derrick Henry]] over Hall as the cheaper one-two punch.
- 2024-10-10 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert), co-host with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): Hall is a buy as a positive-regression candidate. He averaged roughly 4.8 yards per carry across his first two seasons and currently sits second-worst in the league at 3.0, just ahead of [[Gus Edwards]]. Harstad's heuristic is that good players produce and bad players do not, so when a player he is confident in underperforms his ability he bets on the rebound; he concedes the reasoning is somewhat circular, since he often infers quality from production, but says this is not a hard case — nobody needs to question whether Hall is the best running back on the Jets. He acknowledges it has not looked that way for a month and attributes it to small-sample weirdness. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees Hall is the obvious answer on the positive-regression side.

- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Hall's Sunday night line — 103 receiving yards, 109 of them after the catch per James Koh (not tracked) — is the shape of his 2024 revival: Harmon says his fantasy season is 'back on track' because he is catching passes and hitting a couple of explosive plays, not because the ground game works. - 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]) [Dynasty]: Hall is a dynasty buy-low — Waldman would take that chance because he thinks Hall is one of the most talented backs in the league when healthy, and he would bet on it at any reasonable price. He is aware of the bear case and states it fairly: Aaron Glenn has floated a three-way split, [[Braelon Allen]] is being handed a [[David Montgomery]]-style role, and analysts at JetsX argue the data shows Hall slowed after his injury and has not recovered his feet. Bob Harris (not tracked) says Hall had been going a little high for his taste and that the split news should push the price down, which is exactly what makes him interesting; he also notes for context that in the 14 games [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and Montgomery both played last year, Gibbs was RB5 at 18 points per game and Montgomery RB9 at 19, but that the surrounding Jets pieces are nowhere near that level.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-18]]) [Dynasty]: an emphatic dynasty buy-low. Hall revealed this summer he played last season with a knee injury that did not require surgery but needed time to heal, and Waldman noted he had also come back early from an ACL two years ago because the Jets wanted him available for [[Aaron Rodgers]] — meaning the analytics sites showing downticks in his yards per carry and speed metrics are measuring a hurt player. Waldman expects the Jets to make 'another colossal mistake' and sell him at midseason, and thinks Hall thrives elsewhere: a better creator, with power, quickness, cutting ability and deep speed, and value as a receiver. His [[Braelon Allen]] framing was that Allen is a 55-degree day with a chill where Hall is a 70-75-degree day. Bob Harris (not tracked) said he is torn — he does not like Hall at his redraft cost, but agrees the dynasty value is there precisely because the market perceives him slowing down, and both hosts stressed that even superhuman athletes sometimes need a full year to recover.
