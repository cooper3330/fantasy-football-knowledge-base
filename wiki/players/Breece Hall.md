# Breece Hall

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]: uses Hall as the counter-example to the episode's pace-control thread. Hall "tries to hit the home run every time — either you're getting a home run or you're striking out." His comp: "that old baseball player who's hitting .198 but he has 20 home runs. It's great when it works, but a lot of times you have to control the tempo of your own run and get what's blocked." He attributes the Jets giving Dalvin Cook more work partly to this, citing on-air comments from Robert Saleh.
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, the fuller and more sympathetic version — and an explicit bullish forecast attached to it. "With a better line, even without the skills you're mentioning, he's going to be a big-time yardage producer. It's going to be scary." The knock is specific: "he averts one disaster at a time." Hall processes what is immediately in front of him extremely fast, but "he's not maybe seeing two steps ahead, he's not seeing it in the full scope" the way [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and [[James Cook]] do. Waldman's framing is that this makes him *more* impressive, not less — "the fact that he's literally just going, okay, what do I do next, what do I do next, and he can get eight yards on that play is incredible" — "but it's not optimal for what he could be."

**Read:** both hosts are describing a ceiling-raiser, not a bust flag — the
projection is that improved processing (or an improved offensive line) unlocks a
volume producer. The near-term risk is the workload split that the boom/bust
style invites.
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): projects Hall around 300 touches, 1,600 total yards and double-digit touchdowns, calling him possibly a top-five back in pure talent -- noting he wasn't fully himself in the 2023 opener post-ACL but got his explosiveness back by season's end and 'makes it look easy,' the same way he did at Iowa State. Waldman said as a runner alone he could hit 12 TDs and 1,400-1,500 yards, with 2,000 total yards possible if health holds; the swing factor keeping him at 1,600 rather than higher is whether the Jets offensive line stays healthy. He floated Hall as an RB1 usurper especially if Aaron Rodgers has a strong year and weapons (Mike Williams, Allen Lazard, Malachi Corley, Xavier Gipson) develop, with Braelon Allen also mentioned as a nice late-round Jets stash. Bob Harris agreed on the touch range but called Hall RB1 upside a 'possibility, not expectation,' saying with a true first pick today it would still be [[Christian McCaffrey]].
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman names Hall as one of the strongest threats to unseat [[Christian McCaffrey]] for RB1 overall, saying he now has 'an eighth to a quarter of himself back' as an athlete a full year removed from his ACL injury, and that his system fit remains strong. He still says take McCaffrey ahead of Hall given the latter's ceiling is unproven at full health, but ranks Hall among the top three candidates in the field, alongside [[Bijan Robinson]], to reach the RB1-overall outcome.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): asked to take Hall over [[Bijan Robinson]], Waldman said he feels it but that the gap is closer than the framing suggests — he considers the two very close and would not be surprised if either finished as the overall RB1 over [[Christian McCaffrey]], naming them the two best-positioned in that order while refusing to claim he can divine the difference. The bull case is receiving work: [[Aaron Rodgers]] has complimented Hall's route running, went six-of-six in a red-zone session with two of those to Hall, and said afterward that his scouting report had not flagged those skills and that he had never been in an offense where the primary red-zone read was the running back. Waldman adds that the Jets offensive line has old and injured spots that are great in name, so Rodgers may check down often — especially if he and [[Garrett Wilson]] are still out of sync. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) was emphatically on Hall.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): Hall is his top running back not named [[Christian McCaffrey]], a little ahead of [[Bijan Robinson]], and picking inside the top five he is likelier to take Hall. But he refuses to make it emphatic: he calls the two close to even and says the safer player is actually Robinson because Atlanta's offensive line is younger, better and has played together for three years while the Jets line is only good on paper — 'the great part of the iceberg nobody wants to look at.' Hall's edge is straight upside via receiving work; Waldman projects 78 targets and 59 catches, arguing the Jets would need to dominate games for a big touch increase and that [[Garrett Wilson]], [[Mike Williams]], [[Allen Lazard]] and [[Tyler Conklin]] will take targets from a healthy [[Aaron Rodgers]]. Bob Harris (not tracked) leaned Hall harder, citing his league-leading ~90 running back targets last year and Rodgers calling Hall his primary red-zone read.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): off a 7-of-8, 52-yard, one-touchdown receiving line plus 14 carries for 62 yards, Waldman says Hall is not literally the Jets' best receiver but is 'the best weapon on the New York Jets without a doubt.' He singles out a split-out wheel route against a Titans linebacker as a technique showcase — the way Hall attacked and tracked the ball on a vertical route was, in Waldman's framing, exactly what [[Treylon Burks]] has failed to do, and Hall did it better than the Titans' own former first-round receiver. He understands why [[Aaron Rodgers]] has made Hall the first read on many passing plays and says Rodgers flagged Hall's red-zone receiving value in camp, something Waldman admits he had underrated.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): reacting to a headline built on Robert Saleh saying Hall had a bad day but still has plenty of juice, Waldman was incredulous the question was even being asked — 'fuck yeah Breece Hall still has more juice,' and he is still probably the best back in football at this stage, or at least a top three to four back. He dismissed the small sample of games in an offense that has not looked good, and drew a sharp usage distinction: far more is demanded of Hall than of [[Braelon Allen]]. He used the segment to warn against reading coach quotes as evaluations — go back and hear the question that prompted the answer, since coaches are more polite publicly and may simply be repeating an absurd question back to the room. He also took a shot at the New York media as the most insulated people he has met.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): Waldman still likes Hall a lot and calls him a genuinely instinctive runner — a word he says he normally rejects for backs — but the Jets offense in London was 'stupid', and specifically the play calling. Taking the ball out of Hall's hands as often as they are, while the offense goes nowhere, denies a rhythm runner the chance to get into a rhythm, and Waldman says the same problem applies to taking the ball out of [[Aaron Rodgers]]' hands on predictable, unsuccessful plays. He allows that [[Braelon Allen]] is playing well but does not accept that as the reason. Both hosts took a victory lap for having recommended [[Saquon Barkley]] and [[Derrick Henry]] over Hall as the cheaper one-two punch.
- 2024-10-10 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert), co-host with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): Hall is a buy as a positive-regression candidate. He averaged roughly 4.8 yards per carry across his first two seasons and currently sits second-worst in the league at 3.0, just ahead of [[Gus Edwards]]. Harstad's heuristic is that good players produce and bad players do not, so when a player he is confident in underperforms his ability he bets on the rebound; he concedes the reasoning is somewhat circular, since he often infers quality from production, but says this is not a hard case — nobody needs to question whether Hall is the best running back on the Jets. He acknowledges it has not looked that way for a month and attributes it to small-sample weirdness. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees Hall is the obvious answer on the positive-regression side.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]): asked if Hall is the best open-field runner in football, Waldman said he feels it completely — 'the way he accelerates in and out of cuts is fantastic, the way he sees the field, the burst, the long speed.' He named [[Saquon Barkley]] and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] as legitimately in that range, then went further: 'I'd probably take Hall over all of those backs right now.' A pure talent evaluation, not a production or situation call. [Dynasty]
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Hall's Sunday night line — 103 receiving yards, 109 of them after the catch per James Koh (not tracked) — is the shape of his 2024 revival: Harmon says his fantasy season is 'back on track' because he is catching passes and hitting a couple of explosive plays, not because the ground game works. The Jets are 28th in rushing success rate, down among the Raiders, Texans, Patriots and Browns, and Harmon admits he can't separate how much of that is design versus Hall himself. He ties it back to the offense: because the Jets are never in second-and-three or second-and-four, they never get to the under-center play-action shots that would open the intermediate middle of the field.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): amid trade chatter around Hall, Waldman says that if Hall were moved to a good team — his hypothetical was San Francisco replacing [[Christian McCaffrey]] — he would put Hall right up there with [[Saquon Barkley]] at the top of the running back board. The take is conditional and about ecosystem rather than a change in his view of Hall's talent; Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) confirms there is a lot of talk about a Hall trade and says he is working an ongoing 'will they rebound or not' series on it, while still siding with Waldman on Barkley as RB1.
- 2025-06-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-05]]) [Dynasty]: Hall is 'rotting on the vine' — Waldman says a healthy Hall should be a top-five NFL back but he cannot even put him in his top 15–20 right now. Angelo notes new OC Tanner Engstrand brings the Detroit two-back system, with Hall as the [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and [[Braelon Allen]] competing for the [[David Montgomery]] role — but the Jets line is not the Lions line, so per-down efficiency drops and you get two backs in mediocre no-man's land rather than a top-three back plus a mid-teens back. Angelo also flags post-ACL Hall as a different player than pre-ACL Hall. Waldman adds the scheme problem: an inefficient offense creates predictable down-and-distance, and [[Justin Fields]] is not a check-down passer and will take low-red-zone carries, capping Hall's touchdowns and receptions — 'Breece Hall is in football prison.' The dynasty angle: if he looks good on limited touches and stays healthy, he is a buy-low, since he is young and will likely find a way out of New York; both agree Chicago should be poking around, and Waldman would trade [[D.J. Moore]] plus a pick for him.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]) [Dynasty]: Hall is a dynasty buy-low — Waldman would take that chance because he thinks Hall is one of the most talented backs in the league when healthy, and he would bet on it at any reasonable price. He is aware of the bear case and states it fairly: Aaron Glenn has floated a three-way split, [[Braelon Allen]] is being handed a [[David Montgomery]]-style role, and analysts at JetsX argue the data shows Hall slowed after his injury and has not recovered his feet. Waldman is not sure he believes it yet from what he has seen — the quickness may not be quite there, but the cutting ability was strong, the vision is great, and he is a great receiver. Bob Harris (not tracked) says Hall had been going a little high for his taste and that the split news should push the price down, which is exactly what makes him interesting; he also notes for context that in the 14 games [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and Montgomery both played last year, Gibbs was RB5 at 18 points per game and Montgomery RB9 at 19, but that the surrounding Jets pieces are nowhere near that level.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-18]]) [Dynasty]: an emphatic dynasty buy-low. Hall revealed this summer he played last season with a knee injury that did not require surgery but needed time to heal, and Waldman noted he had also come back early from an ACL two years ago because the Jets wanted him available for [[Aaron Rodgers]] — meaning the analytics sites showing downticks in his yards per carry and speed metrics are measuring a hurt player. Waldman expects the Jets to make 'another colossal mistake' and sell him at midseason, and thinks Hall thrives elsewhere: a better creator, with power, quickness, cutting ability and deep speed, and value as a receiver. His [[Braelon Allen]] framing was that Allen is a 55-degree day with a chill where Hall is a 70-75-degree day. Bob Harris (not tracked) said he is torn — he does not like Hall at his redraft cost, but agrees the dynasty value is there precisely because the market perceives him slowing down, and both hosts stressed that even superhuman athletes sometimes need a full year to recover.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): Hall is Harris's number one 'might be wrong about' — his own note reads 'what if Breece Hall is bad now? Which I don't think he is, but maybe he is. And maybe his coaches know it.' He still takes Hall in the third round all the time and calls it a fun roster construction to open RB-WR or WR-RB and land Hall third; he is buying the talent on a player who was a consensus top-three pick a year ago, trusting the tape, and dismisses the volume of [[Braelon Allen]] hype since two-thirds of a backfield is all he needs. He thinks [[Justin Fields]] is not a huge upgrade over the jabronis Hall has played with, but notes running backs can eat with running quarterbacks stressing defenses, and that Hall is exactly the speed back who breaks big plays with a little extra space. Ben Gretch (guest, NOT tracked) is lower, mainly on offense: Fields takes sacks and scrambles rather than checking down, so he converts far fewer dropbacks into pass attempts than a pocket passer like [[Joe Flacco]] would, hurting Hall's reception upside; Gretch also expects few touchdowns, heavy split carries, low play volume and a win-with-defense-and-clock approach. Both note the Lions staff sees Hall as their [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and Allen as their [[David Montgomery]], with talk of splitting Hall out and using him creatively. Harris adds it feels gross to keep going back to Jets players. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]): Waldman maintains Hall is the better back as long as he is healthy enough, but flags two risks. First, a political one — if the perception forms that Hall does not play hurt or plays poorly through injury, a staff might hang its hat on [[Braelon Allen]] and send Hall packing, something coaches do either ignorantly or knowingly. Second, a usage one: much of Hall's value comes from receiving, and he does not see [[Justin Fields]] as a dump-off quarterback — Fields is a duck-and-run guy, or a throw-to-the-tight-end guy. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) offered the counter that the Aaron Glenn and Tanner Engstrand staff want a Detroit-style split with Hall in the [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] role.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]) [Dynasty]: 'hell yes' Hall is a bell cow in dynasty. The juice is visibly back in his legs, he out-played [[Braelon Allen]] (whose touchdown Waldman waves off), and Waldman calls him a borderline special back if not already a special one — Aaron Glenn's staff has eyes. The caveat is the receiving role: with [[Justin Fields]] at quarterback, Waldman sees a tuck-and-run passer rather than a dump-down passer, and Hall's best seasons came on very high target shares (top-five, 80-plus targets), so those days may not return. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) said he was skeptical about Hall returning ADP value and is now 'more encouraged after one game than I have any right to be' — with both agreeing they are liking the player more than the situation.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Hall was the best player on the field for either team, and Harris stresses this is not take-commitment but simply what an eyeball saw. He was shot out of a cannon, going 15 and then 8 yards on the first two plays, and new head coach Aaron Glenn was making flex poses at him on the sideline. 145 scrimmage yards against a Steelers defense everyone spent August calling good, with a Jets offensive line nobody trusted, is a number Harris says we would throw a parade over if [[Christian McCaffrey]] had produced it. The snap count was 36 to 19 over [[Braelon Allen]], and Hall was in on a late fourth-and-one near the goal line, with the fake to him springing [[Justin Fields]]. Harris says the summer arguments against Hall, his expiring contract and Glenn's three-backs comment, all look circumstantial and dumb after one week. Caveats: no touchdown, Allen stole the first one, and [[Isaiah Davis]] got an odd red zone carry.
- 2025-09-26 — According to guest Cousin Josh (not a tracked expert) on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-26]]): Hall is his number one. Week 1 (21 touches, 145 yards) put the three-headed-monster story to bed, week 2 was a throwaway with the quarterback knocked out against Buffalo, and Tampa is never an easy place to run. He still gets the bulk of the work, and four of the next five opponents — Miami, Dallas, then Carolina and Cincinnati after Denver — are brutal, laughable run defenses, with the Miami game on Monday night. Josh's ponies-for-a-horse offer is [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] plus [[Keenan Allen]], which Harris calls a really fair, strong offer. Harris adds he wouldn't be shocked if you could get Hall for [[Emeka Egbuka]] straight up, but Josh disagrees on the one-for-one: positional scarcity means Egbuka alone isn't enough for a player still valued like a third-rounder, and Fantasy Pros has Egbuka 39 to Hall 42, which Josh reads as a reason to take the running back. Both note the 0-3 Jets being trashed in New York is what makes the window exist.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Hall 'was awesome and he was the only thing that worked' for the Jets against Dallas, with six carries of 10+ yards, all but the first up the middle on inside zone — the one play the much-hyped Jets offensive line can actually block. Harris is scathing about the rest of that line, which he says was a disaster of false starts and holdings otherwise: 'I'm old enough to remember when people would come on my show in August and just assume that the Jets offensive line was going to be a strength.' Caveats: Hall was stuffed at the goal line on the first drive, had one punched out at the Dallas 10 and appeared to hurt his hand on the play though he returned, and was tackled at the one on a near fourth-down receiving touchdown. On the Jets, 'you're looking at Hall, you're looking at [[Garrett Wilson]], you're not looking at anything else.'
- 2025-11-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Hall is one of Harris's five pivotal players and the same archetype as [[Chris Olave]] — the last good player on a stripped-down roster who has not yet had the lights turned out — but at a more premium position, and in a redraft re-draft today Hall would go higher than Olave. Harris would not be in the trade market to give Hall away right now because everybody already knows what the Jets are, so the price would be poor; he might wait for one good game and then reconsider. His reasons for optimism: guys have good seasons on bad teams all the time, the Jets did not trade him at the deadline, and there is an incentive for New York to feed him heavily ('give me a hundred carries') to keep him happy toward a re-signing. Clear high-variance hold, must be in your lineup if kept.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-10]]) [Dynasty]: riffing on Bob Harris's joke petition against the Jets trading him, Waldman said the opposite — he would love to see Hall moved to an offense where he can dominate, naming the Chiefs and the Bears as two teams where he could be the final piece of the puzzle. He does not expect New York to become that environment because components are missing and, above all, the quarterback question has to be answered and he doubts they can answer it; the coaching staff and front office have a lot of work to do.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): the Jets offense only cooked in a minor way, and it was Hall - five straight Hall runs to end the third quarter, then a 42-yard screen in pouring rain to open the fourth that gave New York a lead it never gave back, plus a 30-yard run later. Otherwise 'he was destined to thrash his body against the rocks', an experience Harris expects him to relive in New England two days later against an excellent front. Verdict unchanged: 'obviously he's always in your starting lineup, he's a hell of a player'.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-13]]): on a Pick 6 over/under of 58.5 rushing yards against New England, Waldman takes the under purely on game script — [[Justin Fields]] cannot throw, the Jets have no receivers with [[Garrett Wilson]] out, and the Patriots offense is likely to build a lead and force New York to throw. He notes Hall was under those totals against Buffalo and Tampa Bay for the same reason, whereas against Cleveland — a strong run defense but no quarterback or lead-padding offense — the Jets run game stayed a factor. Waldman still calls Hall one of the only two Jets pass-game options worth any chance, out of the backfield. [[Chris Harris]] takes the over, conceding the game-flow logic but betting Hall breaks one because he is awesome.
