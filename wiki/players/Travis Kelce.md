# Travis Kelce

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: his days as a first-round fantasy pick are over. Flags that a nagging injury from the start of the 2023 season may have quietly affected him all year — "he never seemed quite right" — and is non-committal on buying back in even at a discount: "he may not drop far enough" to be worth it.
- 2024-01-01 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode, floats a self-described "tinfoil hat" theory that Kelce's heavy media/marketing visibility late in the season is a deliberate cash-grab ahead of a decline he can see coming. Waldman's only response is a wry "I think we're all in that same boat" — neither endorsing nor rejecting it.
- 2024-01-15 — Guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Redraft (Standard)]: pushes back hard on the January 1 decline read, and it's recorded here as a live disagreement rather than resolved. His case: Kelce played through two significant injuries this season rather than declining on talent — a Week 1 hyperextension/bone bruise he returned from after a single week rather than the usual 3-4, and an ankle injury in Week 4 vs. Minnesota — and was still the only Chiefs offensive player defenses feared all season, evidenced by opponents finally dedicating a true double-team after Kansas City's blowout of the Chargers. Predicts "at least two more seasons" of real production once he's fully healthy and Kansas City gets any secondary receiving threat to pull attention away — directly contradicting Waldman's January 1 "his days as a first-round pick are over."
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]), after Kansas City's AFC Championship win: reaffirms the January 1 "value" read for 2024 drafts — expects him to go outside the first round, with many drafters taking [[Sam LaPorta]] ahead of him — but explicitly separates draft cost from play, noting Kelce "was not bad this year, he just wasn't Kelce" and still finished the season as fantasy's TE1 regardless. Points to the AFC Championship performance itself as proof his skill set hasn't diminished: "he'll be fine."
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): the strongest reinforcement yet — 11-for-11 on targets in the AFC Championship, "played an absolute whale of a game," despite averaging just 29 yards per game over his final three regular-season games. Explicit instruction to fantasy managers: don't just look at regular-season box scores — "if Kelce doesn't retire... this is one of those things that we should remember next season."

- 2024-02-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-12]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: after a modest Super Bowl LVIII box score, still calls him "a top-five tight end" but explicitly does not push him closer to TE1 off the game — "he's no longer the dominant force, and it's not because he's older, but because the Chiefs don't have another dominant force opposite him." Reframes his outlook as a supporting-cast question rather than a decline question: if Kansas City lands "a Ja'Marr Chase type... or a Justin Jefferson-like receiver... or another Tyreek Hill type," Kelce "could be a dominant player once again." Expects him to remain "a reliable part of the offense" and "a key component as the game wears on" purely on the strength of his rapport with [[Patrick Mahomes]].
- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]), on Super Bowl LVIII: quietest possible first half (1 target, 1 catch, 1 yard), then effectively carries Kansas City's passing game alone the rest of the way — 8 catches for 92 yards across the second half and overtime. Kelce and [[Rashee Rice]] combined for 18 of the game's targets (Isiah Pacheco next-closest with 6, Justin Watson with 5), a touch below but consistent with the ~20-plus combined mark the two of them drew all postseason. Framed as consistent with the entire postseason run — Kelce is "the reason Kansas City is not just hanging in these ball games but literally carrying the offense" — not as new information about his outlook.

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]): grouped with [[Tony Pollard]] and [[Austin Ekeler]] — reiterates that the trio's poor 2023 shows were more likely health-driven than talent-driven, though for Kelce specifically flags a possible age component layered on top, given the accumulated wear of his career workload.
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said 'fuck yes' Travis Kelce sustains top-three fantasy TE value in 2024, arguing people overweight declining athletic testing and underweight route-running skill -- elite route runners can keep producing well into their mid-30s (Kelce has said he wants to play three more years). He credited Kansas City's condensed, big-receiver formations and manufactured pick/rub routes for consistently freeing Kelce against mismatched coverage, plus scramble-drill chemistry with Patrick Mahomes, calling him the team's leading receiver over [[Rashee Rice]], [[Xavier Worthy]] and [[Marquise Brown]]. Bob Harris (untracked) noted Mahomes' ADOT hit its lowest point last year without [[Tyreek Hill]] in the mix, and both flagged that added speed from Worthy and Brown/Rice this year could open the middle of the field and lift Kelce's efficiency further.
- 2024-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-01]]): Harmon notes the Chiefs used to deploy Kelce as a backside boundary receiver on three-by-one sets and no longer build those concepts in or feed him there, and at 35 he does not expect that to return; the team has talked about limiting his regular-season snaps, especially on first down, to save him for pivotal moments, and he has significant off-field commitments. Harmon also observes that everything he said about [[Rashee Rice]]'s role still holds if you swap in Kelce's name, and that despite the room spending 30 seconds on him, Kelce is probably still the team's leading target, yardage and touchdown getter. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) has him as his TE1 with 124 targets and 971 yards, just shy of 1,000.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): Kelce is a hold and a roster lock — the production is 'steps away,' one play or two or three moments from a completely different conversation. Bob Harris (untracked) noted the process indicators are all intact: Kelce is running the routes and playing the snaps, and in Week 2 was a step from a touchdown with a four-yard play called back. Waldman pointed to [[Patrick Mahomes]]'s own comment that Kansas City is calling plays specifically for Kelce but two or three defenders are covering him, which is a real limiting factor and is part of what has opened things up for [[Rashee Rice]]. His view is that the Chiefs are working other components into the offense, it is working for them, and eventually defenses have to come off Kelce a little.
- 2024-09-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-26]]): Kelce 'has looked sluggish,' and Koh asks aloud whether he is actually in game shape or whether age is catching up at 35. The specific loss matters for Kansas City's structure — Kelce was the intermediate threat who crushed the middle of the field, and right now he is not getting open, with Koh adding that even when he is open he is not open by much. His after-the-catch production, another pillar of his game, is described as non-existent so far. The countervailing note from Harmon: Kelce started slow last season too and was cooking again by the time it was Super Bowl season, so this may be a repeat of that pattern rather than decline.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): with Rashee Rice reportedly lost for the season, Waldman's answer to who absorbs the target share is Kelce, 'who everybody wrote off.' He pushed back hard on the narrative that movies, TV and Taylor Swift left Kelce out of shape — 'he's not out of shape, he looked fine' — and framed the Chargers game as a course-correction spot given Kelce's strong history against them. Waldman expects Kelce to move back into being the focal point of the offense. He added a bigger-picture caveat that the more interesting question in Kansas City is Patrick Mahomes, who looks like 'everything's a half step off.'
- 2024-10-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-24]]): Kelce at this stage of his career is one of four Kansas City pass catchers who all need some degree of 'role catering' — you can no longer ask him to do the isolated-X iso work he used to, and he needs to be aligned off the line and moved in motion. Harmon also said Kelce is no longer someone who wins in contested-catch situations, which was part of why the [[DeAndre Hopkins]] addition mattered. The knock-on benefit: with Hopkins occupying the intermediate middle and [[Xavier Worthy]] threatening deep, defenses that already play two-high shells against Mahomes leave space over the middle for Kelce to work into.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]): asked whether Kelce has another high-end regular season in him after a top-five but underwhelming finish, Waldman's answer is conditional on price. He disputes the premise that few tight ends can produce at a high end, listing [[Sam LaPorta]], [[T.J. Hockenson]] (post-ACL), [[David Njoku]] (with a quarterback), [[Evan Engram]] and the perennial [[Kyle Pitts]] question as plausible rebound candidates. Given that depth, if Kelce is being drafted as a low-end TE1 or falls to a high-end TE2, Waldman says yes, he can deliver another high-end season and that is a safe investment; but if the cost is taking him over the top four to six players at the position — where you also fold youth into the formula — that is not where you want him.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): with Kelce undecided on his future, Waldman says he should not be overlooked if he returns — he was TE5 with 16 games, 97 catches, 823 yards and three scores, and an eight-touchdown version of that season is TE4 or knocking on TE3. What is gone is the dominant-wide-receiver-playing-tight-end era: Waldman projects 700-900 yards at best with four to six touchdowns, comping the current version to Antonio Gates, still a top tight end. He expects Kelce to be perennially underpriced because drafters keep waiting for the age cliff, and predicts he will fall outside experts' top 10 or even top 12 tight ends — a mistake. Waldman's tight ends over Kelce today: [[Brock Bowers]], [[Trey McBride]], [[George Kittle]], [[Mark Andrews]], with [[Sam LaPorta]], [[T.J. Hockenson]] and [[David Njoku]] under consideration.
- 2025-02-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-11]]): 'We're two years into the decline of Travis Kelce — this is what we've got.' Kelce was the big-play answer against zone in the divisional round, less so in the conference championship and a non-factor in Super Bowl LIX. Harmon says he honestly thinks Kelce should retire, framing it as a bad note to end on but noting most players don't get the storybook ending, and that Kelce passed one up chasing the three-peat. The business case reinforces it: Kelce has zero guaranteed money left, is the fifth-highest cap hit on the team, and cutting him costs roughly $2.6M in dead cap while saving about $17M the Chiefs badly need. Harmon calls extending him to spread the cap hit into an age-39 season 'absolutely out of control.'
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]): asked whether Kelce, who confirmed his return days after Brett Veach and Andy Reid floated it at the Combine podium, finishes 2025 as a top-12 fantasy tight end, Waldman said he wants to say yes but lands on no — with the addendum that it is a price problem, not a talent problem. He thinks a top-12 finish is a real possibility, but everyone will have that take and Kelce will end up overpriced; the ideal buy would be somewhere in the TE13-16 range, which Waldman doesn't expect to be available. Realistically you'll have to take him as the sixth to ninth tight end off the board, meaning you pay likely value or slightly above. Bob Harris (not tracked) agreed and said he was surprised the retirement decision came this early, speculating it might have gone differently had Kansas City won the Super Bowl.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): after a down season that landed him at low-end TE1, Waldman thinks Kelce can be a high-end TE1 again and calls him a bargain 'finally for the first time since he was an ascending player.' The weight loss is the hook — it should make him lighter on his feet and improve stamina, though Waldman flags the accumulated pounding as the open question. His projection is roughly 800-900 yards and 8-10 touchdowns, which he says could put Kelce in the top five. The broader argument is about aging tight ends: he cites Tony Gonzalez, late-career Jeremy Shockey, a healthy late-career Todd Heap and Antonio Gates as elite tight ends who sustained production as athleticism faded because they understood how to get open in zone and became reliable check-down options, and expects Kelce to at minimum be that.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-22]]): 'feel it' — Kelce is done as an elite fantasy producer. Waldman said Kelce was for years his own example of what an elite tight end looks like as a route runner, and he still has the short-area quickness to work the underneath game and get open on crossers, but he is no longer the guy you can match up against a cornerback and automatically win downfield. He can win only in the short range now. Waldman's conclusion was that Kelce cannot be the lead guy in this offense and needs more weapons around him to be effective again — which ties to the returns of [[Xavier Worthy]] and [[Rashee Rice]]. He thinks the offense gets better with those pieces back but is unsure how much better, and that the group needs speed.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'Travis Kelce does look slow. I don't know, it's hard to feel like that's coming back into full force.' Harris noted Andy Reid laying into Kelce on the sideline against the Giants and read it as the frustration of what used to be a top offense leaking out. Broader Chiefs verdict: he retracted what he wrote in his summer almanac that a world-historic quarterback cannot lead a mediocre offense — with [[Patrick Mahomes]] they are mediocre anyway. He rejected the 'they wouldn't pay for weapons' argument, pointing to a second-round pick spent on a 'drag racer' and a first on 'a guy who currently has one shoulder' — the investment happened, the personnel just is not good or reliable.
- 2025-09-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris raised Kelce as the frustrated-manager case, asking whether a listener should consider starting [[Cade Otten]] over Kelce in Week 4 without dropping Kelce. Guest Ben Gretch (Stealing Signals, not tracked) said he thinks he would, noting Philadelphia has been good against tight ends in a small sample — Kelce managed four catches for 61 yards against them and [[Jake Ferguson]] five for 23 — and adding that Kelce is a top-12 tight end by draft cost who has not performed like it and 'does look pretty washed to me when I watch the games,' which he called concerning.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Kelce is the only Chiefs pass catcher Harris will call a must start, and even that comes with a shrug — a good first half with an early touchdown, then he 'kind of vaporizes' later in the game, 'but that's a stupid position, so I hear you.' The point is relative rather than absolute: with [[Xavier Worthy]] downgraded and the rest of the group a mishmash, Kelce plus [[Patrick Mahomes]] are the only Kansas City players Harris is confident starting until [[Rashee Rice]] returns in Week 7.
