# Justin Fields

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): with Chicago holding the 1st-overall pick and [[Caleb Williams]] available, frames Fields as a near-certain trade/release candidate this offseason and picks Atlanta or Pittsburgh as his best landing spots — "he needs to get out of Chicago," citing a real fan-base/organizational split that makes it hard for him to trust the building going forward, independent of talent. Prefers Atlanta specifically: a ready-made weapon set (Drake London, Kyle Pitts, Bijan Robinson), a strong offensive line, and a real running game, plus a new coaching staff (Raheem Morris) without Chicago's baggage. Draws an explicit processing-speed comparison to fellow Atlanta-adjacent quarterback [[Desmond Ridder]]: Fields "may be a little tick slow sometimes" processing the field but still understands when to throw and when not to throw against certain coverages better than Ridder did, projecting a more sophisticated decision-maker than what Atlanta had in 2023 under then-HC Arthur Smith (coach — out of scope for this wiki, since departed). Pittsburgh (Mike Tomlin) is graded as a solid second option — good and improving offensive line, but a receiver room "not quite as strong" as Atlanta's.

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]): despite the destination uncertainty, keeps Fields as a locked-in fantasy top-10 quarterback no matter where he lands, and says he'll draft Fields as his QB1 in a lot of best ball drafts this cycle given the discount his uncertain landing spot creates. Floats a value-stack idea: pairing Fields with [[Anthony Richardson]] in the mid-to-late rounds, arguing that if Richardson's rookie-year flashes (before his injury) hold up over a full season, he could end up rostering two of fantasy's top-five quarterbacks — likening both to a [[Jalen Hurts]]-style profile that scores at a high rate without needing to lead the league in passing yardage. Relays sourcing from Pro Football Weekly's Hub Arkush (not tracked): Fields is universally well-regarded as a person and teammate, with the only real question being whether he's proven himself an elite passer yet. Also relays that Atlanta remains the most-discussed speculated destination, with [[Kirk Cousins]] also in the mix there and most quarterback-seeking teams likely weighing both options.

- 2024-02-29 — According to **Kevin Knight** (Falcons reporter, SB Nation's Falcoholic — guest, not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]): reports Falcons buzz has shifted toward pouncing on a Fields trade if Atlanta can't move into the top three of the draft, likely for a modest package (a 2024 day-two pick plus a conditional 2025 day-two pick) — low financial/draft risk given Fields is only guaranteed for two years even if his fifth-year option is exercised. Says the front office wants a quarterback with real long-term-starter upside rather than a short-term bridge, and views Fields as having that chance even if it isn't a certainty.

- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]): weighing Pittsburgh's veteran quarterback options (Fields, [[Russell Wilson]], Kenny Pickett, Mason Rudolph), picks Fields as the "ideal" fit — his athletic ability offsets the Steelers' still-unresolved offensive-line problems, and he "fits very well within that culture" as "a worker" and "a young player to grow with," with Pickett kept as a developmental backup. Separately, on Atlanta: still rates Fields the best long-term-upside option there over [[Kirk Cousins]] and [[Baker Mayfield]] — comparing his ceiling to what Sean McVay (coach — out of scope) extracted from Jared Goff — while conceding Cousins offers a more immediate path to contention.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: reacting to the finalized trade (Chicago to Pittsburgh for a conditional 2025 sixth-round pick), both "feel it" — Waldman calls Pittsburgh's return "far better" value than what other teams paid at quarterback this cycle (e.g. Minnesota/[[Sam Darnold]]), and expects either a full sit-and-develop year behind [[Russell Wilson]] or an "intermittent benching" competition rather than a clean QB1 handoff. Reframes the "hasn't proven himself as a passer" critique relayed from Hub Arkush (Pro Football Weekly, not tracked) on 2024-02-26: attributes much of Fields's Chicago struggles to a poor offensive coordinator and an offensive line that didn't protect him, not talent — repeats the "needed a Josh Allen-style reset" framing, adding that Chicago still "did right by him" at the end by prioritizing his long-term development situation even at the cost of immediate playing time.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]), reacting to the finalized Bears-to-Steelers trade: the hosts disagree on the return. One host calls trading Fields for only a conditional sixth-round pick (upgrading to a fourth if he plays 51%+ of Pittsburgh's snaps) "a flat-out bomb" given his rookie-scale contract and said he would rather have kept Fields as a cheap, high-quality backup than take that little back. The other argues Chicago had essentially no leverage — the trade was common knowledge across the league for months — and that keeping Fields in the building behind Caleb Williams was untenable given how much the roster loves him, framing Chicago as having "genuinely wanted to do right by him" by sending him somewhere (Pittsburgh) he can compete to start amid uncertainty over [[Russell Wilson]]'s recent play.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: on the same Pittsburgh pole-position report, frames Fields as being given real time to win the job rather than facing an all-or-nothing competition — losing the competition early wouldn't mean the team is "jettisoning" him, since he's competing against a proven, quality NFL quarterback in [[Russell Wilson]] regardless of Wilson's recent performances.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]), cited retrospectively as an example while discussing "coach killer" scenarios: pairing Fields with then-Bears HC Matt Nagy's Andy Reid-style passing scheme "made no sense" for a quarterback who needed a more run-driven, intricate development plan — Mitch Trubisky (not tracked) similarly struggled under Nagy's system until OC Bill Lazor shifted to 12-personnel, run-heavy football built around Dave Montgomery (not tracked) and the offense clicked. See [[Coach Killer Prospects]].
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]): Waldman is firmly against the idea of using Justin Fields as a Steelers kick returner under the new kickoff rules, calling it a needless risk to a player he still hopes wins the starting QB job -- and says Calvin Austin III should handle returns instead.
- 2024-05-14 — According to Adam Rank ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-14]]), Bears fan and NFL Network personality, not a tracked expert: Rank argues [[Justin Fields]]' Bears tenure failure was 'operator error' by the organization, not by Fields — the team signed Andy Dalton as starter over him as a rookie, never gave him first-team preseason reps, played him behind a patched-together backup offensive line, and surrounded him with a receiver room led by an aging Allen Robinson and Darnell Mooney under a lame-duck coaching staff trying to save its own job. Fields still flashed real ability (one of three QBs ever to rush for 1,000 yards in a season), but under new GM Ryan Poles the team fully committed to a rebuild, trading Fields to Pittsburgh this offseason for a conditional Day 3 pick (as low as a sixth-rounder) to hand the offense to [[Caleb Williams]] with zero veteran competition.
- 2024-07-12 — Discussing dynasty risk with 'straight-line' mobile QBs, JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]) [Dynasty] used Fields as the cautionary example of a QB whose fantasy value was propped up almost entirely by rushing amid questionable passing, and said he expects Russell Wilson to simply start the entire 2024 season in Pittsburgh over Fields -- more pessimistic on Fields' outlook there than the current market.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): Waldman does not believe Fields is winning the Pittsburgh job — he keeps the possibility open but expects [[Russell Wilson]] to start — while projecting that Fields will carry a package of plays every week, likely as a red-zone runner and in situational looks that leverage his skill set, which gets him on the field regardless. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) made the [Best Ball] case: at QB31 versus Wilson at QB29, if you are going three quarterbacks deep, Fields is worth the stab because his 18.3 points per game the prior season beat essentially every quarterback being drafted ahead of him in that range, including Wilson. Both agreed the reported competition is somewhat overblown camp speculation.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): the Steelers should and probably will name [[Russell Wilson]] the starter, so Fields is a fantasy asset without a job. Waldman concedes Fields has genuine top-10 weekly QB upside because he runs the football — he has produced top-10 weeks in each of his last two seasons as a starter — but frames the tension bluntly: if Fields is playing real games, it means the Steelers are losing. His recommendation is a last-round flyer in leagues whose roster configuration allows it, purely so he does not have to fight the waiver wire if the job turns over. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed that Fields is the pure-fantasy pick and Wilson the football pick.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Fields should stay the Steelers starter. He offers a running element [[Russell Wilson]] doesn't and can throw downfield; he may not read the field as well as Wilson but should be given time to improve and get comfortable, and the fact that Wilson was a game-time decision after re-injuring his calf argues for resting him — if Fields works you never need Wilson, and if not Wilson can come off the bench later. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) noted the original argument for playing Fields early was familiarity with the AFC West opponents including Denver, and framed the situation as ideal either way for Pittsburgh; from a fantasy view he called Fields a Konami-code quarterback and said hell yes.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-12]]): Harmon thinks Arthur Smith did a good job building the Pittsburgh passing game around Fields' preference to avoid throws down the middle, feeding [[George Pickens]] deep and outside the numbers instead. His line for the offense's ceiling is 'a very 2022 Falcons type of way' — that unit was 11th in success rate and top 15 in EPA per play, which the hosts note came with six field goals and zero touchdowns in this particular Week 1. Fields put up 14 rush attempts on 17-of-20-ish passing volume. Harmon expects him to keep the job as long as he avoids turnovers, accepts he will always be a sack eater, and argues that [[Russell Wilson]] taking a vet-minimum deal removed any financial incentive for Pittsburgh to force a change.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): 'feel it' — as long as Fields keeps winning games he stays the Steelers' starter, and 'Fields will have to completely fall apart for him to lose his job.' He is showing rapport with [[George Pickens]], and the rushing element he provides is 'a tier higher than what [[Russell Wilson]] could ever provide' — Wilson retains some mobility but never at Fields's level. Waldman's caveat is the reverse: expect mistakes Wilson would avoid, since Wilson is savvier working the pocket to find a second or third read. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) framed Pittsburgh as the inverse of Las Vegas — the veteran started, and a fortuitous injury handed the job to the young quarterback the team may have wanted to play anyway.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Pittsburgh's pass game is more interesting than the raw yardage suggests — 25th in dropback success rate but 15th in dropback EPA, which Harmon credits to scrambles and some big plays, notably against Atlanta in Week 1. He argues the Steelers have not made full use of Fields as a designed rusher and that he should have more freedom to take off and run; he would also like far more option looks with [[Jaylen Warren]] in the backfield. Harmon praises Arthur Smith's designs for getting Fields and [[George Pickens]] the throws each wants, which happen to overlap, and expects the whole offense to improve once they exploit the quarterback run game. He is baffled that [[Russell Wilson]] is suiting up while inactive.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]): the case against Fields in Pittsburgh is not the big plays — those are real — but the cost. Waldman said Fields 'loses yardage and creates bad down and distance situations,' pointing to his own buy/sell/hold and Gut Check film work from the prior week showing Fields is less predictable and puts the offense behind schedule. [[Russell Wilson]] gives the Steelers 'down and distance play script stability,' which lets one of the league's best defenses play aggressively, blitz and dictate terms rather than defending short fields. Waldman added that Fields 'can sometimes be a little bit more about Justin Fields, unintentionally — not like [[Kyler Murray]], who's all about Kyler Murray.' Tomlin's willingness to sit with uncertainty rather than be decisive for its own sake was framed as good leadership, not indecision.
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Harmon says he never had a strong take on the Steelers' QB choice — he assumed both would play and thinks Pittsburgh 'fell ass backwards' into the pair after busting on [[Kenny Pickett]]. On film, though, the split is real: per Pro Football Focus, Fields is actually worse using play action than on non-play-action dropbacks and averaged 5.2 yards per attempt in Weeks 1-6, and [[Russell Wilson]] objectively throws a better go ball and fade than he does — which is what [[George Pickens]] eats on. Harmon notes Fields did connect with Pickens on outside breaking patterns in Week 1 versus Atlanta and had similar success with [[D.J. Moore]] on curls in Chicago. He handled the benching well, Harmon is personally rooting for him, and doesn't think we have seen the last of him in 2024.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]): asked whether Fields will be Pittsburgh's 2025 starter, Waldman joked 'fuck it, Skylar Thompson' — the only quarterback actually under contract — before saying he does expect Fields to get his chance, but that he would retain both Fields and [[Russell Wilson]] and would prefer Wilson to start. His reasoning is a comparison of difficulty: he thinks it will be harder for Fields to improve what he needs to improve conceptually as a player than it will be for the Steelers to improve what they need to improve around Wilson. So the take is not a talent dismissal so much as a bet on which gap closes faster if Pittsburgh wants to compete in 2025.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): ranking the free-agent quarterbacks, Waldman puts Fields above Kirk Cousins ('noodle arm') and clearly above [[Daniel Jones]], but below [[Sam Darnold]] and [[Jameis Winston]]. He grants that Fields will be better for fantasy than for real football because he can run and drafters like that. The evaluation is unchanged from Chicago: as a Pittsburgh starter he showed the same behaviors that gave the team good reason to switch to [[Russell Wilson]], and the pattern is that it falls apart once defenses adjust. Waldman does not think he is a starting quarterback right now — close, capable of good games, and possibly in need of a Geno Smith / Sam Darnold style journey before he gets there.
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]): asked about Fields as a Raiders option alongside [[Russell Wilson]] and [[Sam Darnold]], Waldman was the least enthusiastic of the three — 'you saw what Fields was about,' and he does not think Fields is any more than two or three years away from growing into something more than what he currently is, which he treats as too long a wait relative to the alternatives.
- 2025-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-05]]): it sounds like Pittsburgh will re-sign Fields and start him, which Harmon notes would be an oddity — a quarterback traded for, benched by that team, then brought back on a new deal as the starter. He says he is not the biggest Fields guy in the world but does not hate walking into the season with him: 'an acceptable solution to a team that doesn't have very many good solutions.' His warning is about deployment — the fundamentalist read on a runner like Fields is to lean harder on the run game, and Harmon says instead you do what Philadelphia does with [[Jalen Hurts]] (while stressing Fields is not Hurts) and give a plus runner real options in the route concepts. Pittsburgh's problem is that with [[George Pickens]] a trade candidate they could go from one option to none.
- 2025-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-12]]): Fields signs a two-year, $40M deal with the Jets, making him the lowest-paid non-rookie-contract starter by annual average at $20M — which both hosts read as a bridge-quarterback number and probably the best deal Fields could have gotten. James Koh (not tracked) wondered whether it signals a front-office/coaching-staff disconnect, since Fields does not look like the quarterback Aaron Glenn would want after watching [[Jared Goff]]; Harmon reads it the opposite way, arguing a defensive-coach hire loves a mobile quarterback because man-coverage defensive coordinators hate facing scramblers, and expects the Jets to lean hard on a run game with three interesting backs plus Fields himself. Harmon is candid that Fields has real, serious holes in his game and his initial read was that Fields would not be the quarterback to unlock [[Garrett Wilson]] — while acknowledging Fields pumped volume to [[D.J. Moore]] for the best season of Moore's career. Koh emphasizes the human side: Fields has been through coordinator churn in Chicago and Pittsburgh, and reuniting with a college teammate could restore confidence, with the ceiling being a symbiotic lift for both. Koh's own caveat is that the high-side outcome most likely does not happen.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]): Waldman agrees with Sigmund Bloom (untracked) that Fields signing with the Jets and reuniting with [[Garrett Wilson]] is a win, while noting Bloom is 'the cult leader of the land of the bright and shiny toy' and is drawn to every fast, agile player. The case is that Fields runs a first-read offense, so the most reliable read available is what matters, and Wilson is that read from their Ohio State rapport. Bob Harris (untracked) adds that OC Tanner Engstrand was groomed under Ben Johnson in Detroit and that with [[Aaron Jones]]... rather, the Jets' additions, they should cook up designed usage. Both endorse Bloom's caveat that Fields and Wilson will likely be underrated again once ADP absorbs the reunion bounce — Waldman warns Bloom's Kool-Aid has a hangover effect.
- 2025-06-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-05]]): Fields is worth owning as a QB2 if he is not overvalued, purely for rushing production — Angelo notes his rushing touchdown line sits around nine and a half and expects roughly 10 if he starts all 17. But Waldman is blunt about the underlying quarterback: the benching in Chicago was not just fumbles, it was mismanagement on third-and-short, inside the 50, in two-minute, and in the black and red zones, plus bouncing runs he should not bounce and missing wide-open receivers in the first window while trying to do 'the [[Kyler Murray]] thing' without Murray's quickness. Both note he is not a check-down passer — unlike a Jared Goff offense, backs do not get hook routes and flats — which is why they downgrade [[Breece Hall]] and upgrade [[Garrett Wilson]] and [[Mason Taylor]] on target concentration.
- 2025-06-25 — According to guest Cory Kinnan (Reception Perception QB charting, NOT a tracked expert) with [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-25]]): Kinnan says Fields reads pre-snap well but does not adjust his eyes post-snap — safety rotations, simulated pressure, even stick concepts make him double-think — and has never gotten the ball out on time despite the big arm and 4.4 speed. He holds the ball forever, which at least pairs oddly well with [[Garrett Wilson]]'s methodical, blind-spot-creating stems and their Ohio State rapport. Kinnan frames the Jets as the last real chance: if a Ben Johnson disciple in Tanner Engstrand, an offensive line with back-to-back first-round investments and Tyrod Taylor as a mentor cannot make it work, nobody can. He does not trust Fields' eyes over the middle and would not draft him in fantasy or bet money on him in 2025. Harmon likes the move for the Jets at $30M guaranteed — the cheapest non-rookie-deal starter — but is 90% convinced Fields is there because he used to gash Aaron Glenn as a runner, and does not expect a [[Sam Darnold]] or [[Baker Mayfield]] style revival.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]): a blunt evaluation prompted by [[Garrett Wilson]]'s extension — Fields is not a good starting quarterback but a low-end borderline starter who happens to run really well and cannot make full-field reads on as good or as quick a basis as he should. Waldman does not expect improvement now that he is in his third offense and third scheme in three years. He flags that this will annoy Ohio State partisans (including much of the Football Guys staff) and says he genuinely hopes to be wrong — that Fields becomes a top-12 quarterback even without the rushing bump and that New York builds something — but he is not projecting it.
- 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Fields has been rough in training camp with poor completion counting stats, though Harmon flags camp stats as largely stupid and notes Aaron Glenn's point that the offense is still being installed. Fields is going as a top-10 to top-12 quarterback off the board on rushing upside; the one part of the consensus Harmon disagrees with is the claim he cannot be benched because of the two-year deal — with a new staff wanting credibility and Tyrod Taylor as a safe metronome backup, Harmon says there is every chance he gets yanked if he plays poorly enough, pointing to [[Bryce Young]] and [[Anthony Richardson]] as proof anyone can be benched. Both hosts put his odds of starting all 17 games at roughly 75-80 percent if healthy; co-host James Koh (not tracked) adds that Fields was a late signing rather than a day-one plan and that Glenn comes from a Detroit culture willing to move on quickly.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): Fields is back to what he was because he never went away — and, per Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked), never climbed anywhere either. He is what he is. For fantasy purposes Waldman will start him weekly, but calls it a more volatile weekly start, and takes Fields over [[Daniel Jones]] without hesitation. He also flags the knock-on effect for [[Breece Hall]]: Fields reads as a tuck-and-run quarterback rather than a dump-down passer, so there will be some checkdowns but not a high-target-share receiving back.
