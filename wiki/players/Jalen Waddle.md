# Jalen Waddle

## Expert Takes

- 2025-10-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-21]]): Jalen Waddle ran 24 routes but drew only 4 targets for 1 catch and 15 yards in Miami's 31-6 loss to Cleveland, and got hurt on a Quinn Ewers throw that trailed him into contact late in the game; Malik Washington (8 targets, 5 catches, 30 yards) and Nick Westbrook-Ikhine (30 routes run) both saw more involvement. Harmon flagged this as a real problem given Miami has no other established WR1 with [[Tyreek Hill]] out, arguing the offense should be force-feeding Waddle the ball regardless of how the scheme is trending.
- 2025-10-31 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-31]]): Waddle was the head of Miami's passing offense versus Baltimore -- an opening jumbo-package play-action slant for a big gain, a 36-yard bomb wrongly called back for a phantom leg-whip penalty, and back-to-back explosive plays on a third-and-long conversion and a 24-yard timing slant late in the first half. Harris says the offensive environment 'feels good enough right now' for Waddle given how functional Miami looked despite Tua Tagovailoa's mistakes, reversing his more pessimistic read from a couple weeks earlier.
- 2025-12-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-16]]): [[Jalen Waddle]] appeared in and out of the lineup Monday night with a minor injury and didn't get either of Miami's red-zone touchdowns, which instead went to [[Darren Waller]]. Harris frames Waddle's fantasy outlook as entirely subject to [[Tua Tagovailoa]]'s play -- he's a legitimate speed threat but 'not the Tyreek kind of player,' and Miami still lacks a second good receiver with [[Tyreek Hill]] out since Week 4.
- 2026-02-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-17]]): Waddle had a solid statistical season for Miami last year despite the offense struggling and nagging injuries, but Harmon is frustrated he's been deployed almost purely as an X receiver under Mike McDaniel without much movement, and is unsure whether new OC Bobby Slowik will use him differently. Harmon floats a 'darkest timeline' for Miami: keep Tua as a bridge, run him into the ground, and trade Waddle -- their most tradable asset -- for a 2nd-round pick to fully tank into a strong 2027 QB class. He and co-host James Koh (untracked) both call Waddle a legitimate 'dark horse trade candidate' this offseason, noting Buffalo already tried to trade for Waddle at last year's deadline, which signals Miami is at least willing to listen on offers.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-19]]) [Dynasty] [Best Ball]: breaking down Denver's trade of a late first, late third and fourth-round pick for Waddle, Harmon says the public reaction ('overpaid,' 'not that good') misreads what RP charting shows. Waddle finished 8th in the NFL in first downs per route run and top-12 in both successful targets per route and yards per route last year despite playing in a Miami offense that ranked 25th-26th in EPA/play, 32nd in plays run, 31st in seconds per play and 30th in neutral drop-back rate. He's also been dead last in Miami's 'role-catering' pecking order behind Tyreek Hill and [[De'Von Achane]], running an outside/on-ball alignment 77% of snaps (2022-24) and on the line 81-84% the last two years -- against press man and without scheme help. His success rates: 73.5% vs. man (career-best, 5th straight year above 70%), ~77% vs. press (career-best), and 81.3% vs. zone (3rd time clearing 81%+), plus an 83% contested-catch rate in his charted 2025 games -- nearly double his contested-target rate from his first four seasons, attributed to Tua Tagovailoa's chaotic pocket play. Denver ranks 5th in plays/game, 12th in seconds/play and 6th in neutral drop-back rate, and pairs him with [[Courtland Sutton]] as the outside/X anchor, freeing Waddle to move around under [[Sean Payton]] for the first time in his career. Harmon comps the trade (compensation, age-28, production arc) to the Stefon Diggs trade from Minnesota to Buffalo, though he says he rated Diggs more highly than he rates Waddle at the time of trade. He projects Waddle as a 2nd-round redraft pick, outside the top 12 receivers but not top 10, and expects him to out-target Sutton given Denver's 'layup' role for him; James Koh (untracked co-host) independently guessed the Diggs comp and agreed the trade is a win for both sides given Miami's non-competitive 2026 outlook.
- 2026-06-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman is 'feeling it' on Jalen Waddle as Denver's true WR1 alongside [[Courtland Sutton]], projecting Sutton (209 fantasy points) and Waddle (201) almost dead even -- Sutton benefiting from Bo Nix's familiarity and getting peppered more often, Waddle more efficient in yardage per target despite moving around more. He said it 'wouldn't surprise' him if Waddle becomes the outright top option, and pushed back hard on Sutton's current WR35 ADP (down from WR14 and WR11 finishes the last two years), saying he'll buy heavily at that price -- 'fuck that' to a WR35 projection for a player of Sutton's caliber.
- 2026-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-23]]) [Best Ball]: Harmon called Jalen Waddle one of his biggest 2026 'flag plants,' ranking him a top-12/13 fantasy receiver despite an ADP in the 20s. He compared Waddle's likely role change under Sean Payton to Brandon Cooks' 2016 Saints season, when Cooks dropped from ~53-54% of snaps on the line of scrimmage (2014-15) to just 31% as Payton moved him around Michael Thomas and Willie Snead; Harmon expects a similar shift for Waddle, who has been an on-ball X receiver in Miami (77-84% outside, 79-81% on-line from 2022-2025) because Tyreek Hill occupied the movement role even after Hill's injury last year (Malik Washington, not Waddle, took the motion duties then). He cited Waddle's 2025 route splits -- almost 20% slant rate at ~87% success, plus a strong 2024 nine-route success rate near 58% -- as evidence he'll be Denver's best 'quick separator' against man and press, better than Courtland Sutton on breaking routes. Caveats: Bo Nix threw checkdowns/flats on a charted-high ~29% of dropbacks and only 5.6% slants (84% success when he did), so Harmon wants that mix pushed into double digits; Nix was also weak on posts (33.3% success on 11% of routes) and had an ankle injury requiring a second cleanup surgery this offseason (which Nix insists is not a concern); and new play-caller Davis Webb is unproven after 20-plus years of Payton calling his own plays. Harmon still expects Waddle to 'go crazy' after the catch as Denver's primary detached Z receiver.
- 2026-08-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-18]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon is 'sky high' on Waddle, saying he's been well ahead of consensus in both dynasty and redraft since the trade to Denver, and pushes back on the idea that Sean Payton's history of rotating WRs (Troy Franklin, Marvin Mims) will limit Waddle the way it limited lesser players. He projects Waddle as the offense's off-ball movement Z-receiver who soaks up the 'easy button' layup targets given Bo Nix's tendency to play within chaos, while [[Courtland Sutton]] keeps handling the tougher outside X-receiver routes against press coverage. Guest Jeff Bell (not tracked) has Waddle as his WR20 and agrees the offense should be pass-heavy enough to support a clean three-WR rotation (Sutton X, [[Pat Bryant]] power slot, Waddle Z).
- 2026-08-28 — According to cousin Josh (Chris Harris's untracked co-host) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-28]]) [a DraftKings player-prop pick]: with Waddle now traded from Miami to Denver, Josh takes the over on his season receiving-yards prop (924.5, up from an opening line near 899), citing that Denver threw the ball the most of any team last year and that 19 players cleared 1,000 yards league-wide -- Josh expects Waddle to be one of the players who clears 1,000 this year in that role, and downplays Courtland Sutton as target competition, saying there's enough volume for both even though he's not against Sutton either. Harris says he likes the pick and knows how much Josh values Waddle, but notes 1,000 yards has generally been hard to hit given how spread out passing volume was across the league last season.
- 2026-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-09-03]]) [Dynasty]: doesn't expect Waddle to age well long-term because his game is built entirely around speed, and once that speed declines Harris doesn't see another skill for him to fall back on (see [[Aging Curves and Career Longevity]]).
