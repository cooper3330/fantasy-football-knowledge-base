# Xavier Worthy

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-29 — According to guest **Eric Froton** (NBC Sports college football analyst — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, hosted solo by James Koh with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]) [Dynasty]: flagged, alongside Texas teammate [[Adonai Mitchell]], as having real jets — expects Worthy could lead the entire 2024 receiver class in pure speed at the combine, calling it a genuine special trait NFL evaluators are hunting for in this draft.

- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]) [Dynasty]: on running the fastest 40 time ever recorded at the combine — thinks it will elevate his stock more with decision-makers above the scouting staff (who tend to get "seduced by speed") than with scouts who watch tape closely. Was already a good player pre-combine, but flags a real technical flaw obscured by the highlight-reel speed: he "fights the football," with contested-catch/attack-point issues visible only on close viewing, not from tracking route patterns alone. Projects him as a speed-complement piece rather than a true No. 1 — a "Jordan Addison" or "DeVonta Smith" role, not an every-down X — and draws his career-shape range between Ted Ginn (speed without translating to a great career) and DeSean Jackson (speed plus real receiving skill), saying Worthy "has a chance to be closer to DeSean Jackson than he does Ted Ginn" if he "makes friends with" the football instead of fighting it.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): fights the ball at the catch point more than some realize, even though he tracks it well with his back to the quarterback — his tracking ability is comparable to [[Rome Odunze]]'s. Makes more difficult catches than he gets credit for. By weight he falls in the range of Isaac Bruce and Desean Jackson, and like those two, his toughness at the catch point belies his size — fearless over the middle, wins targets bigger receivers lose, and takes hard contact (including getting his legs cut and taking ping-pong hits) at the boundary and over the middle.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: calls Miami "a great fit" for Worthy post-pro-day, comping his long-term potential to Isaac Bruce/DeSean Jackson — not just a gadget piece, since he runs quality routes and makes tough plays, though he could be started as a gadget weapon early and developed outward. Frames [[Tyreek Hill]] as an ideal same-room mentor specifically because Hill can credibly tell Worthy he can't lean on speed alone.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on *Reception Perception: The Show* ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-11]]): charted back-to-back with Texas teammate [[Adonai Mitchell]] in the same disliked, gimmicky Texas offense — a "not fun" experience given shaky QB play and half-field-read route jogging he declined to chart as legitimate routes. Expects Worthy's finished route chart will not be skewed downfield the way Mitchell's is — calls him more of a "layup receiver" by comparison, run underneath more often as a proven, well-regarded college player rather than because he's viewed as the better prospect.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]), naming a high-end career-outcome comp: DeSean Jackson (not tracked) — grades Worthy high enough to be a game-wrecking No. 1 option who operates on all three levels, offering more after the catch and as a middle-of-the-field crosser than his speed-only reputation suggests, plus real punt-return value; sees him fitting the modern receiver mold ("more Devonta Smith than Julio Jones") if he lands in a vertical passing offense. Flags Texas QB Quinn Ewers's slow, limited multi-read processing as directly costing Worthy easy separation looks on tape. [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, independently lands on the same DeSean Jackson-tier ceiling and adds a second comp, Isaac Bruce (not tracked), for the contested-catch/physicality-despite-size profile — consistent with his earlier Bruce/Jackson framing on this page.
- 2024-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: grades [[Xavier Worthy]] a "niche player" and priority-second-round talent, not a Round 1 receiver -- his RP profile shows solid man-coverage (68.4%) and zone (59th percentile, against a small elite sample) success and a strong stop/curl/slant game, but a 28th-percentile success rate vs. press, 18% contested-catch rate, and 6.8% drop rate reflect his sub-170lb frame showing up on tape; his outcome depends heavily on scheme fit (a pre-snap-motion offense like Green Bay's, comping to [[Jayden Reed]]'s rookie role), Marquise Brown is a realistic ceiling comp, and Harmon pushes back on Deshaun Jackson/Tank Dell/Zay Flowers comps since Worthy's college press-coverage rate (59.5%) trails all of them. James Koh (untracked co-host) largely agreed, adding Worthy is much better on one-cut "go-and-stop" routes than on multi-move routes.
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: said he 'fucking loves' Worthy's landing spot with the Chiefs despite Buffalo trading out of the pick, comparing his reachable upper-range comp to DeSean Jackson and Isaac Bruce rather than gadget players like Mecole Hartman, calling him 'a legitimate outside receiver who can work over the middle.' He projected 60-80 catches as a reasonable 2024 range, noting the ceiling depends on the health of others — if Rashee Rice plays a full season near 100 catches and Marquise Brown also nears 100, Worthy's target share compresses toward 45-60 catches, with Travis Kelce still absorbing volume too. Waldman cited Kadarius Toney's immaturity, Marquise Brown's injury history, and Skyy Moore's failure to develop as reasons Worthy could become one of the class's best impact players quickly.
- 2024-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-30]]): Harmon had Worthy as a priority round-two player, not a first-rounder, citing a sub-20th-percentile contested-catch rate and only 59.5% success rate vs. press, despite good marks vs. man (68.4%) and zone (80.7%). He calls it a bad pick for Kansas City specifically -- a team he says needed a true contested-catch, press-beating X receiver (naming Ricky Pearsall, Xavier Legette or Keon Coleman as better fits) rather than another speed piece, given the Chiefs finished fourth-fewest outside-WR yards with 5 TD:7 INT to outside receivers in 2023 despite Patrick Mahomes. Harmon worries 'dynasty bros' will overrate Worthy given the fastest-recorded combine speed (4.21). Co-host James Koh (untracked) is more sympathetic, arguing Andy Reid's staff will scheme him open and that his speed alone will open the field for [[Rashee Rice]], [[Marquise Brown]] and [[Travis Kelce]] even without a true No. 1 receiver in the room -- both agree the Chiefs likely never crown a true WR1 behind Kelce, spreading targets across several twos/threes.
- 2024-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (untracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-07]]) [Best Ball]: both take the under, emphatically, on Vegas's 750.5-yard line. Koh's median projection is just 47 receptions, 612 yards and 5.1 TDs, high case 918 yards/7.2 TDs -- more than 100 yards under Vegas even in the high scenario. They reject Andy Reid's public Xavier Worthy-to-DeSean Jackson comp: Jackson's 120-target, 912-yard rookie season came behind a barren Eagles WR room (Kevin Curtis 63 targets, Jason Avant 57, Hank Baskett 52), unlike Kansas City's deeper group, and Harmon says flatly 'there ain't no way' Worthy sees 120 targets in that offense. Koh ranks the Chiefs' non-Kelce WR talent tiers with [[Marquise Brown]] #1 (though he calls much of Brown's production 'manufactured' via screens), [[Rashee Rice]] and Worthy both graded 'high-quality WR2,' with Worthy possibly outkicking Rice's talent ceiling if he hits his 'solid speed-based two' prospect profile -- and notes even a Rice suspension (he floats a scenario of Rice missing six games) still wouldn't be enough to push Worthy's projected number over the line given rookie usage patterns in Reid's complex offense.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Best Ball]: Worthy has become the first Kansas City receiver off the board in best ball drafts, ahead of Marquise Brown, and Waldman calls the price 'ballsy' given the historical failure rate of rookie receivers in Kansas City's tricky route tree — but says his gut overrides that logic ('hell yes I'm feeling this even though the logic would tell you fuck no'), citing Worthy's big-play, high-variance ceiling as ideal for the best ball format specifically, plus [[Rashee Rice]]'s legal uncertainty (four to eight games, possibly a full season) opening target share. He comps the profile/landing-spot excitement to DeSean Jackson's rookie year with Andy Reid in Philadelphia (WR29 that season), while noting Patrick Mahomes is a better real-life fit than Donovan McNabb was for Jackson. Bob Harris (untracked, RSP Cast co-host) called it 'pretty aggressive' and said he's sticking to cheaper shares rather than paying the current price.
- 2024-07-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-02]]): Harmon says Worthy profiles as a flanker/slot rather than X and will stretch the field for Kansas City, but flags real concern about contested-catch ability and hands -- 'shaky,' though not as bad as Troy Franklin -- attributing some of it to his 165-pound frame. Co-host James Koh (untracked) says he was lower on Worthy than consensus during the draft class relative to receivers like Zay Flowers or Tank Dell, who proved at the collegiate level they could beat press coverage. Both expect Worthy to produce a highlight deep touchdown early in the season and also get 'buried' on a trick-play reverse at some point.
