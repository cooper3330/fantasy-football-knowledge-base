# Xavier Worthy

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-29 — According to guest **Eric Froton** (NBC Sports college football analyst — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, hosted solo by James Koh with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]) [Dynasty]: flagged, alongside Texas teammate [[Adonai Mitchell]], as having real jets — expects Worthy could lead the entire 2024 receiver class in pure speed at the combine, calling it a genuine special trait NFL evaluators are hunting for in this draft.

- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]) [Dynasty]: on running the fastest 40 time ever recorded at the combine — thinks it will elevate his stock more with decision-makers above the scouting staff (who tend to get "seduced by speed") than with scouts who watch tape closely. Was already a good player pre-combine, but flags a real technical flaw obscured by the highlight-reel speed: he "fights the football," with contested-catch/attack-point issues visible only on close viewing, not from tracking route patterns alone. Projects him as a speed-complement piece rather than a true No. 1 — a "Jordan Addison" or "DeVonta Smith" role, not an every-down X — and draws his career-shape range between Ted Ginn (speed without translating to a great career) and DeSean Jackson (speed plus real receiving skill), saying Worthy "has a chance to be closer to DeSean Jackson than he does Ted Ginn" if he "makes friends with" the football instead of fighting it.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): fights the ball at the catch point more than some realize, even though he tracks it well with his back to the quarterback — his tracking ability is comparable to [[Rome Odunze]]'s. Makes more difficult catches than he gets credit for. By weight he falls in the range of Isaac Bruce and Desean Jackson, and like those two, his toughness at the catch point belies his size — fearless over the middle, wins targets bigger receivers lose, and takes hard contact (including getting his legs cut and taking ping-pong hits) at the boundary and over the middle.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: calls Miami "a great fit" for Worthy post-pro-day, comping his long-term potential to Isaac Bruce/DeSean Jackson — not just a gadget piece, since he runs quality routes and makes tough plays, though he could be started as a gadget weapon early and developed outward. Frames [[Tyreek Hill]] as an ideal same-room mentor specifically because Hill can credibly tell Worthy he can't lean on speed alone.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on *Reception Perception: The Show* ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-11]]): charted back-to-back with Texas teammate [[Adonai Mitchell]] in the same disliked, gimmicky Texas offense — a "not fun" experience given shaky QB play and half-field-read route jogging he declined to chart as legitimate routes. Expects Worthy's finished route chart will not be skewed downfield the way Mitchell's is — calls him more of a "layup receiver" by comparison, run underneath more often as a proven, well-regarded college player rather than because he's viewed as the better prospect.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]), naming a high-end career-outcome comp: DeSean Jackson (not tracked) — grades Worthy high enough to be a game-wrecking No. 1 option who operates on all three levels, offering more after the catch and as a middle-of-the-field crosser than his speed-only reputation suggests, plus real punt-return value; sees him fitting the modern receiver mold ("more Devonta Smith than Julio Jones") if he lands in a vertical passing offense. Flags Texas QB Quinn Ewers's slow, limited multi-read processing as directly costing Worthy easy separation looks on tape. [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, independently lands on the same DeSean Jackson-tier ceiling and adds a second comp, Isaac Bruce (not tracked), for the contested-catch/physicality-despite-size profile — consistent with his earlier Bruce/Jackson framing on this page.
- 2024-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: grades [[Xavier Worthy]] a "niche player" and priority-second-round talent, not a Round 1 receiver -- his RP profile shows solid man-coverage (68.4%) and zone (59th percentile, against a small elite sample) success and a strong stop/curl/slant game, but a 28th-percentile success rate vs. press, 18% contested-catch rate, and 6.8% drop rate reflect his sub-170lb frame showing up on tape; his outcome depends heavily on scheme fit (a pre-snap-motion offense like Green Bay's, comping to [[Jayden Reed]]'s rookie role), Marquise Brown is a realistic ceiling comp, and Harmon pushes back on Deshaun Jackson/Tank Dell/Zay Flowers comps since Worthy's college press-coverage rate (59.5%) trails all of them. James Koh (untracked co-host) largely agreed, adding Worthy is much better on one-cut "go-and-stop" routes than on multi-move routes.
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: said he 'fucking loves' Worthy's landing spot with the Chiefs despite Buffalo trading out of the pick, comparing his reachable upper-range comp to DeSean Jackson and Isaac Bruce rather than gadget players like Mecole Hartman, calling him 'a legitimate outside receiver who can work over the middle.' He projected 60-80 catches as a reasonable 2024 range, noting the ceiling depends on the health of others — if Rashee Rice plays a full season near 100 catches and Marquise Brown also nears 100, Worthy's target share compresses toward 45-60 catches, with Travis Kelce still absorbing volume too. Waldman cited Kadarius Toney's immaturity, Marquise Brown's injury history, and Skyy Moore's failure to develop as reasons Worthy could become one of the class's best impact players quickly.
- 2024-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-30]]): Harmon had Worthy as a priority round-two player, not a first-rounder, citing a sub-20th-percentile contested-catch rate and only 59.5% success rate vs. press, despite good marks vs. man (68.4%) and zone (80.7%). He calls it a bad pick for Kansas City specifically -- a team he says needed a true contested-catch, press-beating X receiver (naming Ricky Pearsall, Xavier Legette or Keon Coleman as better fits) rather than another speed piece, given the Chiefs finished fourth-fewest outside-WR yards with 5 TD:7 INT to outside receivers in 2023 despite Patrick Mahomes. Harmon worries 'dynasty bros' will overrate Worthy given the fastest-recorded combine speed (4.21). Co-host James Koh (untracked) is more sympathetic, arguing Andy Reid's staff will scheme him open and that his speed alone will open the field for [[Rashee Rice]], [[Marquise Brown]] and [[Travis Kelce]] even without a true No. 1 receiver in the room -- both agree the Chiefs likely never crown a true WR1 behind Kelce, spreading targets across several twos/threes.
- 2024-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (untracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-07]]) [Best Ball]: both take the under, emphatically, on Vegas's 750.5-yard line. Koh's median projection is just 47 receptions, 612 yards and 5.1 TDs, high case 918 yards/7.2 TDs -- more than 100 yards under Vegas even in the high scenario. They reject Andy Reid's public Xavier Worthy-to-DeSean Jackson comp: Jackson's 120-target, 912-yard rookie season came behind a barren Eagles WR room (Kevin Curtis 63 targets, Jason Avant 57, Hank Baskett 52), unlike Kansas City's deeper group, and Harmon says flatly 'there ain't no way' Worthy sees 120 targets in that offense. Koh ranks the Chiefs' non-Kelce WR talent tiers with [[Marquise Brown]] #1 (though he calls much of Brown's production 'manufactured' via screens), [[Rashee Rice]] and Worthy both graded 'high-quality WR2,' with Worthy possibly outkicking Rice's talent ceiling if he hits his 'solid speed-based two' prospect profile -- and notes even a Rice suspension (he floats a scenario of Rice missing six games) still wouldn't be enough to push Worthy's projected number over the line given rookie usage patterns in Reid's complex offense.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Best Ball]: Worthy has become the first Kansas City receiver off the board in best ball drafts, ahead of Marquise Brown, and Waldman calls the price 'ballsy' given the historical failure rate of rookie receivers in Kansas City's tricky route tree — but says his gut overrides that logic ('hell yes I'm feeling this even though the logic would tell you fuck no'), citing Worthy's big-play, high-variance ceiling as ideal for the best ball format specifically, plus [[Rashee Rice]]'s legal uncertainty (four to eight games, possibly a full season) opening target share. He comps the profile/landing-spot excitement to DeSean Jackson's rookie year with Andy Reid in Philadelphia (WR29 that season), while noting Patrick Mahomes is a better real-life fit than Donovan McNabb was for Jackson. Bob Harris (untracked, RSP Cast co-host) called it 'pretty aggressive' and said he's sticking to cheaper shares rather than paying the current price.
- 2024-07-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-02]]): Harmon says Worthy profiles as a flanker/slot rather than X and will stretch the field for Kansas City, but flags real concern about contested-catch ability and hands -- 'shaky,' though not as bad as Troy Franklin -- attributing some of it to his 165-pound frame. Co-host James Koh (untracked) says he was lower on Worthy than consensus during the draft class relative to receivers like Zay Flowers or Tank Dell, who proved at the collegiate level they could beat press coverage. Both expect Worthy to produce a highlight deep touchdown early in the season and also get 'buried' on a trick-play reverse at some point.
- 2024-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-01]]): Worthy's college charting shows a legitimate downfield separator — 73.7% success on post routes while running them on 14.5% of sampled routes, 62.5% on nines, and 81% on outs at a high relative rate. Harmon projects him as Kansas City's stretch player both vertically and horizontally, and says the shots are now available to someone who can actually separate on them. The weaknesses are press coverage and contested catches: Harmon almost does not care about the press score because Worthy will rarely see press in that offense, but warns the tight-coverage drops will produce a few frustrating moments Chiefs fans should be hardened to. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) leads with the 4.2 speed and notes Harmon does not yet know what Worthy is as an NFL player.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]): he is holding firm in a good way, and more so with [[Marquise Brown]] hurt — Worthy should get enough underneath touches to be valuable. But he pushes back on the field-stretching narrative: it is less about raw speed than about what coverages defenses play against Kansas City. The Chiefs have had speed that could win even without [[Tyreek Hill]], and against a high-enough Cover 2 even Hill does not win regularly. So he expects big plays and a Worthy–[[Patrick Mahomes]] connection without the wholesale change people hype. Projection: a fantasy starter in three-receiver lineups, maybe a WR2 this year, eventually a WR1 with DeSean Jackson-like talent — the open question is whether it blossoms right away.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]) [Best Ball]: on delivering as a massive deep threat — 'from a football point of view, totally feel it; from a fantasy point of view, fuck it.' Worthy will be a big enough vertical threat that two or three defenders react to him running across the field, opening the middle for others, and he will produce huge weeks in best ball. But Waldman points to how defenses shut down Kansas City's deep game with two-high safety looks — the same coverage answer that ended the deep production of [[Tyreek Hill]] in that offense for two to three years before the trade. No matter how fast you are, you rarely beat two-deep consistently unless you are Randy Moss with a great run game around you. He projects a solid WR3/flex with spike weeks, not a WR2, unless Kansas City feeds him underneath volume — if the volume is there early, get on the train; if it is only a big-play fest, no. Beneficiaries of the two-high look include [[Rashee Rice]] and [[Travis Kelce]].
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): Worthy is his pick for the rookie who makes the biggest splash, and he is not as far from the big three as people realize. Waldman gives two readings. Conventionally, his biggest impact is stretching defenses to open underneath routes for Kansas City — cover two is cover two, and unless you are Randy Moss you do not beat two defenders deep. But he will not rule out the other reading: if anyone can outrun this league it is Worthy and [[Tyreek Hill]], if anyone can get it there it is [[Patrick Mahomes]], and if any coach has found the wrinkles it is Andy Reid — noting that if the cover-two clamps could be beaten by speed alone they would have done it with Hill years ago. Crucially Waldman argues Worthy is more than a deep threat: he is a complete player, and with [[Rashee Rice]]'s legal situation unresolved and no other established number two on the depth chart, Kansas City will use him enough for it to show early.
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): Worthy looked great in the preseason and 'the speed is real' — Harmon is very excited about what he looks like in this offense, because he trusts Kansas City to do the role catering necessary to free him from press coverage and from X-receiver route responsibilities, the same accommodation they made for [[Rashee Rice]] last year. Drafting Worthy is also what made [[Kadarius Toney]] redundant in his view: the roster can only carry so many designed-touch players. The implicit caveat is that Worthy's value here is conditional on deployment rather than on beating press himself, and Harmon notes Kansas City is again carrying seven receivers, a crowded mix of old and new faces.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman is 'totally feeling' that Worthy is the rookie receiver people will wish they had drafted more of. He acknowledged the risk directly — in Andy Reid's system [[Rashee Rice]] worked and [[Travis Kelce]] works, but essentially every other receiver has failed, so the market defaults to a Mecole Hardman comparison. Against that he cited the special rapport already showing between [[Patrick Mahomes]] and Worthy early in camp, particularly hitting routes downfield, how well Worthy accelerates downhill, and his effectiveness in the middle of the field. It would not shock Waldman if Worthy finishes as Kansas City's top fantasy receiver. Untracked co-host Bob Harris was cooler — he saw some nice plays, wants to see what happens with [[Marquise Brown]], and thinks Rice is the receiver to own, but is not against Worthy.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Going Deep)]]): Worthy is his headline 'hot start, cold finish' rookie. Once [[Marquise Brown]] returns in two-to-three weeks, Kansas City has four viable mouths to feed alongside [[Travis Kelce]], [[Rashee Rice]] and [[Isiah Pacheco]], and Angelo expects [[Patrick Mahomes]] to spread it around week-to-week rather than feed a rookie. He also expects defenses to play more two-high against Worthy's and Brown's speed, which should push volume back to the backfield. Angelo's bet is 2025, not 2024: he calls Worthy his pick to lead Kansas City in receiving yards next season while 2024 produces a couple of boom games and a lot of volatility. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees on scheme grounds, noting Andy Reid's offenses have historically won without multiple receivers dominating the scoreboard, and questions whether Worthy gets true WR1 work beyond shallow-route usage.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): Worthy is not a one-dimensional player overall, but Kansas City is currently using him as a Mecole Hardman type, and the expansion of his game may take time. Waldman pointed to a concrete failure: a fourth-quarter crosser where Mahomes threw and Worthy stopped his route, reading zone and settling when it was a man-to-man route that required him to keep running — a drive-changing detail. His larger thesis is that details like this pile up on all rookie receivers, and specifically that this offense demands far too much of its wide receivers: he cited the long history of highly touted young Chiefs receivers by draft capital or scouting report who did not work out, and the same pattern under Andy Reid in Philadelphia. His prescription is either patience with young receivers before they fail, or simply signing veteran free agents. Bob Harris (untracked) suggested Brett Veach is better at drafting running backs than receivers; Waldman countered that Reid may simply be a worse offense for a wide receiver than for a back.
- 2024-09-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-26]]): 'kind of a gadget player right now,' with the caveat that it is very early in his career. After the Week 1 explosion he managed eight targets and four catches for 34 yards across Weeks 2 and 3. Harmon says he is not big enough to win against press man on the outside — 'I don't need to see another go route to Xavier Worthy against man coverage as an outside receiver again' — yet he has run about 69% of his routes outside and 31% from the slot, because Kansas City is catering the inside role to [[Rashee Rice]]. Koh cites the prospect profile: 68% vs man (44th percentile), 59.5% vs press (28th percentile), nearly 81% vs zone. Harmon calls the next-[[Tyreek Hill]] comparison one of the least founded takes he can name — Hill is physically rocked up and does not go down to arm tackles, while Worthy was dragged down by one on a Week 3 screen. He expects genuine spike weeks off post routes from the slot, but a volatile profile.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): asked by co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) whether Worthy is the next man up with Rashee Rice reportedly facing an ACL tear, Waldman said 'fuck it' — he would like to feel it but does not think it happens. Worthy will get more targets and Kansas City may try him in some of what Rice did well, but he is 'not as rugged as Rice,' so you are leaning on speed and movement rather than the physical in-traffic work. Waldman expects a mishmash instead: Andy Reid will split Rice's routes by type across Worthy, JuJu Smith-Schuster and Skyy Moore, and Worthy is not yet there on route adjustments and scheme knowledge — as most young receivers in Reid's system are not. - 2024-10-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-01]]): Worthy hit a free-release go-route touchdown from [[Patrick Mahomes]] that both hosts celebrated as a rare deep connection, but Harmon's evaluation is unchanged — at 165 pounds Worthy is not a player you can line up on the boundary and let win, and he showed on film in weeks two and three that he cannot get off physical press man coverage, no matter his speed. With [[Rashee Rice]] hurt, Kansas City now has to scheme for Worthy the way it schemed for Rice and for [[Travis Kelce]] (46.7% first-read target share in this game), and Harmon's open question is what else Worthy can offer and how to design it. He said he would rather the Chiefs had drafted [[Adonai Mitchell]] from that same team over Worthy, and repeatedly wished Mahomes had a true X-receiver ball winner — 'even [[Keon Coleman]]' — instead of a room of gadget and speed pieces.
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Harmon charted Worthy again (not a one-game sample) and says the season is 'basically the meme' of the bear case on him. Versus San Francisco: 8 targets, 3 catches, 19 yards, a 45.5% success rate versus man and 25% versus press. James Koh (not tracked) notes 45% against man would sit just outside the bottom five in Reception Perception history — 'Justin Hunter, Terrace Marshall, Jalen Reagor range'. Harmon is careful to be fair on context: nobody planned for Worthy to lead the WR room at 81.3% route participation and a 32% target share in Week 7. He can rip posts against zone on a free release and left a long touchdown on the field to a Mahomes overthrow, but any press man is a non-factor, and being small means he goes down the moment anyone squares him up. He also isn't the crisp separator the class's better route runners were, so Harmon doubts a post-bye bump: if he were Andy Reid he'd be designing touches, not making him the first read.
- 2024-10-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-24]]): Worthy 'is not ready' to be an every-down receiver and may never be, given his size — quoting analyst Derrick Klassen approvingly that 'you put your hands on Xavier Worthy, plays over'. Harmon was careful to say Kansas City never drafted him to be the number one; he needs to be an ancillary piece. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) cited NextGenStats: pressed on 27 routes this year, targeted on just three of them, with zero receptions and one interception on those targets — arguing even [[Patrick Mahomes]] has to stop looking that way. Both saw the [[DeAndre Hopkins]] trade as the fix: with Hopkins as the on-line X, Worthy can move to the slot and off the line, where he will face less press and get more of the deep posts he wins on. Harmon said Worthy has been open on almost every post route he has charted this year, with a versus-zone success rate right around average.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2024-11-14]]): the returns on Worthy have not been good and Reception Perception offers no counter-argument. Charted at 55.6% success vs man (poor) and 40% vs press, which would be among the worst marks charted in three seasons — not quite [[Quentin Johnston]] territory but in the Rishard Matthews / Geronimo Allison / Justin Hunter range. He is aligned all over: 60.5% outside, 31.5% slot, 8% backfield, and Harmon notes Mecole Hardman would still be getting the gadget looks regardless. Contested catch 33% (bad in college too), 38.5% of catches in space but brought down on first contact 100% of the time, and only screens, posts, corners and flat routes come in at or above NFL-average success rate. Harmon's key point is that Worthy was drafted as a field-stretcher to open space for [[Rashee Rice]] and [[Marquise Brown]], not as a volume receiver, and he simply lacks the skills to step up when those players were lost — a straight continuation of his college profile against bigger, faster, smarter defenses. The optimistic comp is early-career [[Brandin Cooks]], but Harmon stresses Cooks was 5'10 and 189 pounds versus Worthy's 165 at the combine, and Cooks was brought down on first contact only about 53% in 2016; he rejects the pre-draft DeSean Jackson comparisons on both size and hands. Koh (not tracked) adds that over weeks three to eight Worthy averaged six targets for three catches and 34 yards a game — barely five yards per target with [[Patrick Mahomes]] throwing — and both hosts agree [[Keon Coleman]] has been the better NFL player so far, while noting none of it matters to an undefeated Chiefs team.
