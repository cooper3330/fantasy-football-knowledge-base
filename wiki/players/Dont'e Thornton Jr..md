# Dont'e Thornton Jr.

## Expert Takes

- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): the Tennessee receiver has to develop a better release game or he will keep playing slower on the field than he looks running sprints — his range of motion with his footwork is not believable right now, whether releasing against press or attacking off coverage at the top of the stem. Waldman likes his pacing to set up defenders, but wants complementary short and intermediate skills so defenses cannot simply overcompensate to take away the vertical shots. He also does not drop his weight nearly enough to win breaks back to the quarterback; unfixed, opponents will play off him and still cut off the target.
- 2025-04-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-29]]): Thornton to the Raiders is a classic Al Davis-spirit pick — a 6-4, 200-plus perimeter receiver running in the 4.3s — and it fills the size-speed hole on the outside that Harmon thinks the room lacked. But he is emphatic that Thornton is a pure project coming out of Tennessee's offense with almost no production, and his Reception Perception rookie route tree is comically narrow: 43.5% curls with zero percent corner, zero percent out and zero percent comeback route participation. Harmon is not saying he is ready to contribute in year one.
- 2025-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-09]]): a pure project receiver taken early in the fourth round, but a skill set the Raiders genuinely lack — 6-5, 205 with 4.3-flat speed in a room otherwise full of smaller vertical types like [[Tre Tucker]], [[Jack Bech]] and [[Jakobi Meyers]]. Harmon wants him as a big body who lid lifts on the outside, plays a bit sacrificial and clears intermediate and underneath space for Bech, [[Brock Bowers]] and Meyers. The charting shows something to develop: a 70% success rate versus man in the Rookie Roundup sample, and 43.5% of his routes were curls at an 85% success rate, with Harmon praising how he dips and sinks his hips to control his speed on stop routes. Caveats are real — tiny college production (26 catches, 661 yards, six TDs), a Looney Tunes Tennessee offense, everything north-south and linear, and James Koh (not tracked) adds that he is stiff at the catch point and is confused by the pick, though Koh still expects Thornton to break loose for a few plays given how much attention Vegas draws underneath.
- 2025-06-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-04]]): Harmon identifies the missing ingredient in an amorphous Raiders offense as a lid lifter with size and speed on the outside, and says Thornton may be that guy — [[Jakobi Meyers]] talked him up in OTAs as a real wide receiver rather than just a speed guy. Harmon liked what he saw in a brief rookie-roundup sample on the Reception Perception site but hedges that he is probably just a speed guy for now, with development needed coming out of that Tennessee offense. His point is that even so, size-speed on the outside is precisely what this team lacks, so if Thornton can earn a year-one role it fits exactly. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) calls him a freak show and one of the athletes making the Raiders fun again.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]) [Dynasty]: of the three Raiders dynasty darts run past him, Thornton is the one he is 'feeling all day long' — the size-plus-speed profile is worth a bet at his cost. The caveat is explicit: he does not expect anything great this year, but Thornton has skills to build on, so at that price, in a league with expanded rosters or a deep waiver wire, take the shot and see what develops. Waldman ranks him ahead of [[Tre Tucker]] and [[Jack Bech]] on this list.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): reports via Levi Edwards that Thornton has become a top target for [[Geno Smith]] deserve a 'let's slow down.' Waldman grants the tools — fast, big enough, strong enough — and accepts that Thornton will be among the Raiders' top four pass-game options, which technically makes him a 'top target' out of six to eight names on the depth chart. But he projects Thornton as the low-percentage target player and is skeptical he holds the job once he faces defenses that demand things from him he is not seeing in practice. His summary line: optimistic about Thornton contributing, pessimistic about him being a weekly fantasy start, and listeners should be too. Price guidance is explicit — fine to draft after the 15th round, an overpay anywhere inside it.
- 2025-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-25]]): the fourth-round Tennessee rookie is getting camp pop for running with the starters, and Harmon says archetype-wise he is exactly what the Raiders lack — a vertical X receiver, with [[Brock Bowers]] and [[Michael Mayer]] at tight end and [[Jack Bech]] and [[Jakobi Meyers]] as flanker-slot types. In three charted games he took 91.8 percent of sampled snaps outside and was on the line for 88.7 percent, without being locked to one side. Marks: 70 percent versus man (encouraging), 61.5 percent versus zone (not as good), a bizarre route tree with 43.5 percent curls and 2.2 percent slants, but an 85 percent curl success rate showing he can threaten deep and throttle down. Decent hands. Harmon stresses he is very raw and developmental out of a Looney Tunes Tennessee offense. He is 6-5, 205 with a 4.30 forty, which Koh (not tracked) calls extraordinary at that height, but a 33-inch vertical is a 20th-percentile mark and his MockDraftable athletic comp is [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]] — a comp both hosts accept, while agreeing Thornton has better hands.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]] (ASR: 'Dante Thornton'): Thornton is starting outside for Las Vegas despite being a very raw player because the offense specifically needs receivers who can lift the top off defenses and open interior space for its many slot types. Nguyen cited his 6-foot-5 frame and 4.3 speed as the reason the Raiders are pushing him into the starting lineup so soon. The implication is a role built on the vertical threat rather than route-running polish.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): the hosts split. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) said 'fuck yes' he delivers on the preseason buzz — 6-5, 4.3 speed, beat writers calling his camp standing undeniable, [[Jack Bech]] slipping to WR4 while Thornton climbs, and [[Geno Smith]] making a concerted effort to involve him; Harris is taking him in the 18th-19th round and says he belongs on the [[Dynasty]] radar. Waldman said 'fuck it', he is not believing in him: the plays are preseason-like, and he cited Vic Tafur of The Athletic noting Thornton came down with contested catches but struggled to create separation against backups. With [[Brock Bowers]] dominating targets, Waldman expects Thornton to be a field-stretching, run-blocking [[Sacrificial X Receiver]] in year one out of a limited college role. Both agreed he is excellent in [[Best Ball]]; Harris conceded his enthusiasm is only for a late-round flyer, not a startable weekly piece.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-27]]): the camp hype is real but overcooked. Harmon likes the 6'5, 205, 4.3-running Thornton and notes he is playing early only because he is the single player on the Raiders roster with an X-receiver skill set — [[Tre Tucker]], [[Jack Bech]] and [[Jakobi Meyers]] cannot fill that role — and because Pete Carroll's competition ethos does not care about draft slot (Harmon cites Russell Wilson beating out Matt Flynn as a third-rounder). But he thinks people are 'jumping the shark': the buzz comes from a handful of deep shots against vanilla preseason looks with second and third string defenders, and in Week 1 a fourth-round rookie starting at X is a warning sign, which Harmon believes is precisely why Las Vegas signed [[Amari Cooper]]. Koh (not tracked) is harsher, calling Thornton strictly developmental and pointing to the Tennessee/Josh Heupel receiver pipeline needing seasoning. Harmon expects a stripped-down over-the-top role in 2025 and a season or two before every-down impact. Thornton plays regardless of whether Meyers is traded.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-05]]): he is selling Thornton for the first month, largely because everyone else seems to be buying after [[Amari Cooper]]'s retirement. Harmon agreed Thornton is clearly the favorite to start at X and will play a role, but urged reasonable expectations — he is not about to be the next [[Puka Nacua]] day-three-receiver-turned-immediate-starter. His stated ceiling comp has always been a [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]] with better hands, achievable in time, and even then the target volume question remains with [[Brock Bowers]], [[Michael Mayer]], [[Jakobi Meyers]] and [[Ashton Jeanty]] in a Pete Carroll / Chip Kelly run-leaning offense. Harmon also read the Raiders signing Cooper at all — with Thornton already in the starting lineup through camp and preseason — as a signal about how they view their outside receivers; the room is now down to four players.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-23]]): the Thornton experiment is going 'exactly as I expected it to go' — the idea of a fourth-round size-speed freak starting at X is intriguing in the offseason, and then the games start and you realize you have a fourth-rounder taking near-every-snap X reps, 'and that's not good.' Harmon says Thornton is not getting open because he was a raw rookie coming out of Tennessee. He tied it to a broader criticism that Chip Kelly has galaxy-brained the Raiders offense by stacking speed on the outside when the team needs singles-and-doubles hitters to matriculate the ball, which is why he had tweeted that Las Vegas should play Jack Bech.
- 2025-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-25]]): Thornton has settled in as the Raiders' X receiver, but Harmon does not think he is ready to be a real target earner or a player they can trust running routes consistently on the boundary — 'it's nice that he's gotten to a point where they feel comfortable playing him,' and he has not been horrific, but this is an area of weakness on the roster. The structural criticism is that Las Vegas is 'galaxy braining' the offense: the thesis needs someone to lift the lid and clear space for the run game and for [[Brock Bowers]] and [[Jakobi Meyers]] underneath, and they are already getting exactly that from [[Tre Tucker]]'s speed, so doubling up on vertical archetypes leaves nobody hitting singles and doubles in the passing game. Harmon would rather see more [[Jack Bech]] in that outside rotation.
- 2025-12-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-01 (Feel It or Fk It)]]): Waldman said Dont'e Thornton Jr. is a real reason [[Lamar Jackson]] hasn't thrown a touchdown in three games, citing two dropped, catchable touchdown passes (an accurate deep shot in the fourth quarter and one at the end of the half that 'clanked right off his hands'). Waldman called it the same unreliable-hands profile Thornton showed at UNC, but noted [[Romeo Doubs]] cleaned up a similar issue over time at Nevada and floated Thornton could follow a similar developmental path.
- 2026-01-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-05]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is not buying in on Thornton. He classifies him as a big-play speed guy who isn't good at separating, and argues that combination doesn't survive in the NFL -- he didn't see it on Thornton's college tape and views his strong training camp as more a product of unrealistic one-on-one speed reps than translatable skill. Waldman admits he was drawn in by the speed himself ('maybe me more than most') but says this was always going to be a slow burn if it happened at all, and now leans toward thinking Thornton was never NFL-starter material, while allowing Bob Harris's suggestion to hold him one more year to see if he's proven wrong.
- 2026-01-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-26]]) [Dynasty]: 'fuck no.' Waldman says Thornton's elite speed has not translated into separation, which he holds up as evidence that pure speed is an overrated quantity for evaluating NFL separation -- pointing to Stevie Johnson as a counter-example, an intelligent route runner without elite speed whose fantasy value was still real for stretches of his career.
- 2026-03-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-10]]): Harmon said the Raiders mishandled [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]] as a rookie by throwing the size-speed X-receiver prospect 'to the wolves' before he was ready -- he came from Tennessee's spread offense and 'wasn't a guy that was ready to contribute right away,' and 'for the most part he sunk.' Harmon called him 'a pure developmental pick' and said he does not consider it over for Thornton, just that he needs 'a fresh start' under the new Clint Kubiak coaching staff rather than being written off.
- 2026-07-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-06]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls a 2026 breakout 'possible' but leans toward a third-year (2027) leap instead, citing Thornton's offseason work with route consultant Drew Lieberman -- whose past clients (Wan'Dale Robinson, Evan Engram, Dontayvion Wicks, Julian Edelman, Jakobi Meyers) have typically shown on-field improvement by year two of the program. Waldman's own tape evaluation says Thornton 'has a ways to go' on route refinement and separation, though camp reports suggest early strides. He currently slots Thornton behind [[Jack Bech]] in the Raiders' WR pecking order and expects him to make the roster without necessarily moving up in 2026. Bob Harris (untracked) added the rookie efficiency detail -- 13.5 yards per catch on 10 catches/135 yards -- and said he's drafted Thornton in dynasty as a patient, low-cost stash given the lack of a crowded depth chart ahead of him, while keeping expectations modest.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-17]]): Waldman said Thornton, in his second year, hasn't separated himself from [[Jack Bech]] or rookie [[Malik Benson]] in a three-way, effectively dead-heat competition for a Raiders receiver role.
