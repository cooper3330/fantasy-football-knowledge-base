# Ty Simpson

- 2026-02-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-16]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is skeptical of the early-first-round buzz on Simpson, calling it 'rich' and noting the traditional 'Alabama bump' has faded post-Nick Saban. Simpson (6'2', 208) shows pinpoint deep accuracy, blitz adjustments, fluid/fast footwork and a quick release, but velocity to the far side of the field may be a touch light. Waldman's bigger concern is process: Simpson is 'impatient cycling through progressions,' takes unnecessary sacks because he can't filter what matters, and mismanages blitzes and broken plays in the red zone -- comping him to Kenny Pickett and Desmond Ridder as a 'whiteboard' quarterback who will be more impressive on film study than in live reps. Waldman frames it as 'theory smarts' (Simpson, currently) versus 'life smarts' (Mahomes, Caleb Williams), using Steve Young's development into a life-smarts QB as the aspirational path. In a direct 'feel it or f**k it' against [[Garrett Nussmeier]], Waldman took Nussmeier over Simpson.
- 2026-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-30]]): despite Dan Orlovsky ranking him the class's true QB1, Waldman says 'fuck it' -- he does not see Ty Simpson that way and prefers Fernando Mendoza (an untracked comparison Waldman has praised on prior shows). His core knock: Simpson's game 'falls apart against pressure' and he compounds mistakes -- likened to taking a sack-turned-safety instead of throwing the ball away -- retreating from pressure rather than taking the correct available option, and showing streakiness across only one year as a starter. Waldman and Bob Harris (untracked co-host) also discuss whether Simpson gets an 'Alabama accelerator' in media evaluation (logo scouting), with Waldman arguing NFL scouts generally don't logo-scout unless the same coaching regime produces a durable pattern (e.g. a Clemson QB-development track record), which he says doesn't cleanly apply here.
- 2026-04-16 — According to Dalton Wasserman (guest draft analyst, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: [[Ty Simpson]] is clearly Wasserman's QB2, ranked 36th on his top-100 big board (a borderline 1st/2nd-round grade), with a big gap to the class's QB3. He pushed back on a 'less-mobile Daniel Jones' comp circulating for Simpson, arguing his arm talent and ability to stretch the field downfield are underrated. When protected and confident -- and Alabama's talent around him meant he was rarely playing at a disadvantage -- Simpson reads the field like a coach's son (his father coaches), attacks the middle, and threw 30 'big-time throws' last season. The flag is mental: against elite SEC pressure (Georgia, Oklahoma, South Carolina) he struggled to find quick answers to the blitz and forced turnovers by predetermining throws. Wasserman's preferred outcome is a landing spot where Simpson can sit a year to settle his pocket footwork against NFL-caliber pressure before starting.
- 2026-04-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-20]]) [Dynasty]: In a pre-NFL-Draft superflex startup with Scott Barrett and other industry names, Waldman drafted Ty Simpson at pick 1.08 and took heat from Sigmund Bloom ('four nopes, all caps'). Waldman conceded he doesn't love Simpson as a pure prospect but graded him slightly higher than he had Cam Ward the year before, arguing draft capital (likely Round 1) plus a plausible landing spot in Arizona give him a real shot to start within a year or two behind existing skill talent -- comparing the situation to J.J. McCarthy inheriting Minnesota's setup. Like Ward, Waldman said Simpson needs a strong surrounding cast to thrive. He acknowledged he was knowingly 'rolling the dice' on an unknown landing spot, but with his other roster slots (Jahmyr Gibbs, Jaxon Smith-Njigba) already high-floor, he was comfortable reaching for quarterback upside.
- 2026-06-02 — According to James Koh (untracked co-host) and Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-06-02]]) [Dynasty]: both hosts say the Rams' 13th-overall pick of Ty Simpson made little sense at the time and makes even less sense now. Koh: 'I don't understand the Rams' pick of Ty Simpson... it made less sense when they extended Stafford, and now this move (the Myles Garrett trade) makes even less sense' -- arguing a win-now roster that just gave up a 2027 first-round pick for Garrett shouldn't also have burned a first-rounder on a developmental QB instead of a weapon like Makai Lemon or Casey Concepcion. Harmon notes the Rams justified it as a rare shot at a QB while picking unusually high (13th, via Atlanta), comparing it to the Falcons' Michael Penix reasoning, but says that wasn't a consensus opinion around the league and Simpson isn't viewed as a toolsy prospect. Both agree the swing only pays off if Simpson eventually proves out as heir to Matthew Stafford.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-17]]) [Dynasty]: Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) noted his own rooting interest -- he'd publicly said pre-draft he'd take Simpson 8th overall in a Superflex rookie draft, then actually did exactly that. Waldman confirmed he has Simpson rated highly in Dynasty as his QB2 overall and inside his top 15 players regardless of position, but pushed back hard on reading too much into a strong preseason debut -- comparing it to Tyler Huntley and, per Bob, Bruce Gradkowski, both of whom led their league in passer rating during early rookie action without it meaning much long-term. Waldman said Simpson is 'in the first date stage' of being an NFL quarterback -- he hasn't yet been tested on game management and processing against real complexity, since preseason defenses aren't testing quarterbacks the way regular-season ones will.
