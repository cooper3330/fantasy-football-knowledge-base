# Marshawn Lloyd

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo's clearest "not as excited as the market" name in the class. Explicit bust-risk framing — comped to Stevan Ridley (not tracked) rather than Sony Michel (not tracked), i.e. a talented back whose flaws actually sink him rather than one who gets away with them. Concerns, in order of weight: ball security (the single biggest one, with a poor Senior Bowl showing cited as a live data point); a habit of bouncing too many runs outside rather than taking what's blocked; third-down/passing-game role questions; and decision-making Angelo grades as "pretty good but not star level," despite a public perception he thinks is inflated by a handful of highlight Caleb Williams option-pitch plays in open space rather than a full assessment of the tape — see [[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]] for the general pattern this instance illustrates. [[Matt Waldman]], same conversation: agrees his 99th-percentile physical ceiling makes him one of the better backs in the class, but grades his 50th-percentile outcome, given the mistake level shown so far, as "out of the league in a couple years" — citing Chuba Hubbard's swap-in for an ineffective Miles Sanders (neither tracked) in Philadelphia's 2023 backfield as the cautionary NFL comp for what happens once a team stops carrying a back through his mistakes. Both hosts' shared prescription: he needs to land in a room built around veterans and a strong offensive line that "makes him better," rather than one banking on him to be the stabilizing presence — the inverse of what they want from [[Blake Corum]] or [[Jonathan Brooks]].
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: extends his skepticism with a direct stylistic comp — Lloyd's game "makes me miss D'Angelo Williams" (not tracked), but grades him "at least a tier away" in talent even at Lloyd's best-case outcome, including fixing a "pretty big flaw": inconsistent judgment on when to bounce runs versus stay the course, versus Williams's excellent decision-making and equal mastery of gap and zone schemes. Concedes Lloyd may be the better outside-zone runner, but says Williams had more functional power.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]), contrasting him with [[Kimani Vidal]]: acknowledges Lloyd is a more dynamic runner than Vidal today but says Vidal could do in four years, if he develops, what Lloyd already does now — and that Lloyd will likely be drafted (and graded) in round two versus Vidal's projected round five-six.

- 2024-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Feel It or F@#k It* ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-01]]), with co-host **Bob Harris** (not a tracked expert): restates him as a boom-bust prospect built like a feature back, comping his talent to D'Angelo Williams (not tracked) but worrying "I miss D'Angelo Williams" rather than seeing the next one. His fumble rate is "awful... free agent tier level" — the specific flaw that dropped him from a possible RB2 in the class to outside Waldman's top 10, despite otherwise-strong score. Frames the range of outcomes as Stefon Ridley (not tracked) — career derailed by fumbling — versus Sony Michel (not tracked), who had just as bad a fumble rate but improved and whose real problem turned out to be health, not ball security; says Lloyd "can go the Michel track" and is "worth the hype" if he does. Bob Harris (not tracked) separately raises Tiki Barber's Tom Coughlin-coached turnaround as a hopeful precedent for Lloyd getting coached out of the habit.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]), answering "who are you afraid you're too low on": names Lloyd, currently his RB14 with a 78.9-of-100 score — a contributor with real starter-level strengths whose ranking is capped entirely by ball security. Says if his ball security were merely competent he'd be well inside the top 10; if good, inside the top five; if great, he could be Waldman's RB2 overall. Frames the open question as whether this specific type of college ball-security issue tends to carry over and derail NFL careers or gets coached out cleanly — precedent cited both ways (Adrian Peterson corrected his early fumbling issues; some players never do). If Lloyd fixes it, Waldman thinks he could be a top-five back and one of the two-to-three most productive players in this class.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]): his favorite write-up of the cycle alongside [[Javon Baker]]. Restates the process-coupling concern in new comp terms — Lloyd's odd rhythm before contact looks like [[DeAndre Swift]] at Georgia or Miles Sanders (not tracked) at Penn State, plays that "look funky" on rewatch with no clear decision-making process behind them, just a gap between instinct and the internal clock he needs to operate on. The open question is whether he corrects it (a Dave Montgomery, not tracked, outcome) or never does (a Ronald Jones, not tracked, outcome) — physical talent alone won't answer it; it comes down to marrying his physical ability with better process coupling. [[Brandon Angelo]], same episode, restates the market-gap framing against [[Kimani Vidal]]: "pound for pound, Vidal is a better running back," but Lloyd's larger draft capital buys him a longer NFL leash regardless.
