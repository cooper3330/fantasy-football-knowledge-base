# Evan Engram

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-07-04 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-04]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the hosts noted Engram, another Drew Lieberman reclamation project per Waldman, topped 115 targets last season and has developed into a legitimately fantastic tight end with real staying power, making him -- alongside [[Christian Kirk]] -- one of the two locked-in pieces of Jacksonville's passing game that the rest of the receiver room is competing around.
- 2024-07-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]): Engram is a possible 'arbitrage' on Dalton Kincaid -- a similarly athletic, similarly used tight end coming off two strong seasons -- and Engram's own turnaround also traces to working with trainer Drew Lieberman, the same trainer Waldman credits for Chase Brown's route-running improvement.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): Engram is the signal flare at tight end — he is not truly one of the system-safe group, but he is good enough, and once he is the sixth or seventh tight end off your board everyone after him becomes a big question mark on system or ability. Waldman's illustrations of the drop-off: [[David Njoku]]'s huge share of production came with [[Joe Flacco]] rather than with the quarterback now tied to a large contract who Waldman suspects would rather disappear than play, and Dallas Goedert is talented in a possibly better system but may be third in the pecking order regardless.
- 2025-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-19]]): Engram is one of Jacksonville's two largest cap charges alongside [[Christian Kirk]] and Harmon expects him to be cut. His reasoning is scheme rather than decline — Engram is a hyper-specific player indicative of the old Jaguars, while the Liam Coen tree generally wants tight ends who contribute as blockers in the run game, the way [[Cade Otten]] was useful for Tampa Bay. Harmon also thinks Jacksonville is a candidate to draft a tight end for those traditional in-line duties, and notes [[Brenton Strange]] showed a little something in 2024.
- 2025-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) named Engram his tight end value, saying he is ahead of consensus rankings on him. His framing: after [[Trey McBride]], [[Brock Bowers]] and [[George Kittle]] separated into a big three (Bowers in his own tier), 2025 is back to a world of taking a tight end early or waiting a long time. He is not fully buying the joker-role narrative, but likes that [[Bo Nix]] is good on short-area throws — Engram's game — and that Denver's target competition beyond [[Courtland Sutton]] is a pile of interchangeable 11-to-12-yards-per-catch options ([[Marvin Mims Jr.]], [[Troy Franklin]], [[Pat Bryant]], [[Devaughn Vele]]) with no stable second option. At a low-end TE1 price he will take that. Waldman's own tight end preference was late-round rookies — [[Colston Loveland]] and [[Mason Taylor]].
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-29]]): pushing back on people telling him Engram would occupy Denver's power slot role, Harmon insisted a move tight end and a big slot receiver are not the same role and can coexist — the proof being the 2023 Lions, where [[Sam LaPorta]] at tight end and [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] in the big slot worked in concert, so Sean Payton could do the same with Engram and [[Pat Bryant]]. James Koh (not tracked) went further and challenged the premise that Engram is good at all: he was run out of New York onto the NFL trash heap, and the industry's transitive property is backwards — [[Trevor Lawrence]] elevated Engram into a 'good' player rather than the reverse. Harmon countered that [[Bo Nix]]'s development could elevate him again.
- 2026-01-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and untracked co-host James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-27]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Engram was 'a nothing burger as a signing' for Denver, finishing the AFC Championship with 13 routes run, three targets, two catches and 19 yards. Harmon reiterated a stance he's held for multiple years that Engram's Jacksonville production was really [[Trevor Lawrence]] elevating him rather than Engram being an inherently good player, saying that watching Engram over the last two seasons, 'he doesn't have anything left.' The hosts also joked that fellow Denver tight end [[Adam Trautman]] logged 14 routes and zero targets as the de facto TE1 in this game, underscoring how thin the position group is for the Broncos.
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-22 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: expects Engram to benefit from the low-red-zone spacing created by [[Jaylen Waddle]] and [[Courtland Sutton]] after a disappointing prior season Waldman calls 'the joker was on us again.' Both hosts note Engram is currently going for free in drafts.
- 2026-07-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with [[Jaylen Waddle]] and [[Courtland Sutton]] forming a co-WR1 look that should create defensive spacing problems, Waldman thinks Engram is capable of a bounce-back year and, given how cheap he is in drafts, is worth rostering as a TE2 or as a high-risk TE1 for anyone willing to stream the position early in the season.
