# Marquise Brown

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]), floated by co-host James Koh as a hypothetical Kansas City free-agent fit at outside receiver: Harmon rejects the idea on archetype grounds. "That just doesn't do it for me. I don't need a speed guy here... I like Hollywood Brown for the player he is, but he's another zone-beating type." His explicit read of what Kansas City actually needs is a receiver who can beat man coverage and win contested-catch situations — "Hollywood Brown is a nice player, but he's neither of those things." See [[Zone vs Man Route Running]].

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): "I kind of tend to think he's not back in Arizona" — expects Brown to leave in free agency, which would leave the Cardinals with "a gaping hole" at wide receiver even with him still nominally on the roster; sorted Arizona into the "critical need" wide receiver bucket regardless of how his free agency resolves.

- 2024-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-07]]): full RP
  charting on a career-worst 2023 (101 targets, 51/571/1, career-low 50.5%
  catch rate) that Harmon partly excuses as an injury year (a heel/foot
  injury, worse in the back half of the season) but not entirely — career-low
  success rates vs. man (59%) and zone (78.8%, 51st percentile). He's "never
  even crossed 65%" success rate vs. press in his career and is not a
  high-quality press-coverage beater at his size, yet Arizona deployed him on
  the line as an X receiver on 77.4% of snaps (only 16.2% from the slot) — a
  scheme mismatch Harmon says the Cardinals only got "50 right": correctly
  targeting him on in-breaking routes vs. zone, but wrong to line him up
  outside on the line so often given a sub-75% slant success rate (down from
  84-87% in 2020/2022) and almost no out-breaking route usage. Also poor after
  the catch and in contested catches due to size — broken down on first
  contact on 82.4% of in-space attempts, a number trending worse year over
  year. Harmon's read: a complementary piece best used at flanker/slot on
  intermediate crossers and dig/post/out routes in a play-action offense, not
  a true WR1, but still enters his age-27 season with "a little something
  something" if he stays healthy. See [[Reception Perception Methodology]] and
  [[Scheme vs Talent]].


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: a plus move to Kansas City — for the first time he has both a plausible quarterback and scheme together (having had the QB in Baltimore, more of the scheme in Arizona, but never both). Comps him to "75 to 80%" of [[Tyreek Hill]] — has the speed and better-than-advertised hands (a clear step up from a Marquez Valdes-Scantling type, not a tracked page) but lacks Hill's physicality and contact balance — stopping short of calling him a system-made star.

- 2024-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-21]]): reacts to the finalized Kansas City signing (one-year, $7M, up to $11M) as "a big upgrade" over Marquez Valdes-Scantling and over any Chiefs receiver not named [[Rashee Rice]] — calls it the "Patrick Mahomes discount," a prove-it deal a still-young player takes to cash in next offseason off a big season with Mahomes. Lays out the man-coverage decline year by year — 73.6% success rate as a rookie down to 62.6% in 2022 and an all-time-low 59% in 2023 — calling the rookie year and 2023 both outliers (bracketed by injury in 2023) and the 62-63% range more representative of his real level; flags the 2023 zone number (51st percentile) as also a career low against a prior baseline of 80%+ every season, and notes a surprisingly average 74% success rate on slants given his short-area quickness, which Harmon attributes to a lack of separation quality in tight coverage rather than a lack of twitchiness. Projects him as a flanker/slot hybrid rather than a true X, which fits Kansas City's defensive-coverage diet — Chiefs opponents play man on only 25.7% of pass snaps, so a zone-beater on dig/out/post/curl routes matters more than a man-beater, though the room still lacks a receiver who wins man coverage in those higher-leverage situations. Notes his career-high 19.7% go-route rate in Baltimore was inefficient and should shrink in a more varied route tree; projects him as Kansas City's likely fourth-most-targeted player behind [[Travis Kelce]] and [[Rashee Rice]]. Distinguishes his route profile from Rice's (Rice heavy slant/dig, Brown more dig/curl/post) as a case there isn't pure positional overlap, and voices confidence in Andy Reid/Matt Nagy to find the right role for him as they did for Rice.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on *Reception Perception: The Show* ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-11]]): in a free-agent buy/sell discussion of [[Hunter Renfrow]] potentially joining Kansas City, flags concern about the Chiefs getting "too slotty" — pegs Brown as most likely deployed as a flanker-type receiver who can also play some outside with his speed, rather than a true X, continuing his prior flanker/slot-hybrid read on the signing.
- 2024-05-07 — According to James Koh (untracked co-host) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-07]]), in discussion with [[Matt Harmon]]: setting Travis Kelce aside as a tight end, Koh ranks Marquise Brown as the most talented wide receiver on Kansas City's roster ahead of [[Rashee Rice]] and [[Xavier Worthy]], though he cautions that a lot of Brown's production is 'manufactured touches' via screens and quick-game concepts rather than true separation. Koh grades Brown a 'high-quality WR2' in his internal tiers, on par with Rice, and both hosts project all three non-Kelce Chiefs receivers bunched in a 600-700 yard range for 2024 with Kelce clearing 900.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]): Waldman is 'totally feeling' Marquise Brown as the de facto Chiefs WR1 with Rashee Rice suspended, and says even once Rice returns, Brown may remain the more refined route-runner who wins downfield and in the intermediate game while Rice is still developing scheme-based (RPO/screen) usage. His caveat is durability - can Brown play 11-13-plus games - but if healthy he expects Brown to hold the top role in the Chiefs' offense, with rookie [[Xavier Worthy]] (4.21 40) providing a vertical complement that stretches the field for both. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) cites Chiefs insider Nick Athan characterizing Brown as the de facto WR1.
- 2024-07-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-02]]): Harmon notes Marquise Brown ran a real outside route tree in Arizona in 2022 (19.2% nine routes, 11.7% posts, 5.8% corners) that [[Rashee Rice]] never ran, and profiles as a better zone-beater than man-beater but not a strong press-beater; Harmon is mildly surprised Brown has played X the last two years rather than flanker/slot, where he thinks Brown fits better. His addition, along with [[Xavier Worthy]], is meant to clear out space underneath for Rice's slot-heavy role in the Chiefs' 2024 offense.
- 2024-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-01]]): Brown last year with Arizona lined up on the line of scrimmage for 77.4% of sampled snaps and in the slot on just 16.2% — usage Harmon does not love — but he guesses Brown ends up taking most of the Chiefs' X snaps by default, since [[Xavier Worthy]] will not do it and [[Rashee Rice]] probably will not either. The routes Harmon finds most interesting are posts and digs, because the intermediate and deep middle is the area no Chief filled last season; if Brown opens that up it is critical to the offense. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is more bullish, citing glowing camp chemistry reports with [[Patrick Mahomes]] and pegging Brown to lead Kansas City in both targets and yards, though his own projections have nobody on the team clearing 900 receiving yards. Koh argues Brown wins deep and intermediate better than Rice and fits a return to layered Andy Reid route concepts.
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): Brown is dealing with a scary shoulder injury from early in the preseason and may miss Week 1, which matters structurally for Kansas City because very few of their receivers are true outside players. Harmon says Brown can play that outside role — it is probably not his most optimal alignment, but there is proof of concept from Arizona, where he was primarily an on-the-line receiver, plus some earlier work there and in Baltimore; he has never been a true exclusive slot. If Brown misses time, that is the scenario in which Harmon could see Kansas City sliding [[Rashee Rice]] outside to do some of what he did at SMU.
