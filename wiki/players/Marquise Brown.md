# Marquise Brown

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]), floated by co-host James Koh as a hypothetical Kansas City free-agent fit at outside receiver: Harmon rejects the idea on archetype grounds. "That just doesn't do it for me. I don't need a speed guy here... I like Hollywood Brown for the player he is, but he's another zone-beating type." His explicit read of what Kansas City actually needs is a receiver who can beat man coverage and win contested-catch situations — "Hollywood Brown is a nice player, but he's neither of those things." See [[Zone vs Man Route Running]].

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): "I kind of tend to think he's not back in Arizona" — expects Brown to leave in free agency, which would leave the Cardinals with "a gaping hole" at wide receiver even with him still nominally on the roster; sorted Arizona into the "critical need" wide receiver bucket regardless of how his free agency resolves.

- 2024-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-07]]): full RP
  charting on a career-worst 2023 (101 targets, 51/571/1, career-low 50.5%
  catch rate) that Harmon partly excuses as an injury year (a heel/foot
  injury, worse in the back half of the season) but not entirely — career-low
  success rates vs. man (59%) and zone (78.8%, 51st percentile). He's "never
  even crossed 65%" success rate vs. press in his career and is not a
  high-quality press-coverage beater at his size, yet Arizona deployed him on
  the line as an X receiver on 77.4% of snaps (only 16.2% from the slot) — a
  scheme mismatch Harmon says the Cardinals only got "50 right": correctly
  targeting him on in-breaking routes vs. zone, but wrong to line him up
  outside on the line so often given a sub-75% slant success rate (down from
  84-87% in 2020/2022) and almost no out-breaking route usage. Also poor after
  the catch and in contested catches due to size — broken down on first
  contact on 82.4% of in-space attempts, a number trending worse year over
  year. Harmon's read: a complementary piece best used at flanker/slot on
  intermediate crossers and dig/post/out routes in a play-action offense, not
  a true WR1, but still enters his age-27 season with "a little something
  something" if he stays healthy. See [[Reception Perception Methodology]] and
  [[Scheme vs Talent]].


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: a plus move to Kansas City — for the first time he has both a plausible quarterback and scheme together (having had the QB in Baltimore, more of the scheme in Arizona, but never both). Comps him to "75 to 80%" of [[Tyreek Hill]] — has the speed and better-than-advertised hands (a clear step up from a Marquez Valdes-Scantling type, not a tracked page) but lacks Hill's physicality and contact balance — stopping short of calling him a system-made star.

- 2024-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-21]]): reacts to the finalized Kansas City signing (one-year, $7M, up to $11M) as "a big upgrade" over Marquez Valdes-Scantling and over any Chiefs receiver not named [[Rashee Rice]] — calls it the "Patrick Mahomes discount," a prove-it deal a still-young player takes to cash in next offseason off a big season with Mahomes. Lays out the man-coverage decline year by year — 73.6% success rate as a rookie down to 62.6% in 2022 and an all-time-low 59% in 2023 — calling the rookie year and 2023 both outliers (bracketed by injury in 2023) and the 62-63% range more representative of his real level; flags the 2023 zone number (51st percentile) as also a career low against a prior baseline of 80%+ every season, and notes a surprisingly average 74% success rate on slants given his short-area quickness, which Harmon attributes to a lack of separation quality in tight coverage rather than a lack of twitchiness. Projects him as a flanker/slot hybrid rather than a true X, which fits Kansas City's defensive-coverage diet — Chiefs opponents play man on only 25.7% of pass snaps, so a zone-beater on dig/out/post/curl routes matters more than a man-beater, though the room still lacks a receiver who wins man coverage in those higher-leverage situations. Notes his career-high 19.7% go-route rate in Baltimore was inefficient and should shrink in a more varied route tree; projects him as Kansas City's likely fourth-most-targeted player behind [[Travis Kelce]] and [[Rashee Rice]]. Distinguishes his route profile from Rice's (Rice heavy slant/dig, Brown more dig/curl/post) as a case there isn't pure positional overlap, and voices confidence in Andy Reid/Matt Nagy to find the right role for him as they did for Rice.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on *Reception Perception: The Show* ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-11]]): in a free-agent buy/sell discussion of [[Hunter Renfrow]] potentially joining Kansas City, flags concern about the Chiefs getting "too slotty" — pegs Brown as most likely deployed as a flanker-type receiver who can also play some outside with his speed, rather than a true X, continuing his prior flanker/slot-hybrid read on the signing.
- 2024-05-07 — According to James Koh (untracked co-host) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-07]]), in discussion with [[Matt Harmon]]: setting Travis Kelce aside as a tight end, Koh ranks Marquise Brown as the most talented wide receiver on Kansas City's roster ahead of [[Rashee Rice]] and [[Xavier Worthy]], though he cautions that a lot of Brown's production is 'manufactured touches' via screens and quick-game concepts rather than true separation. Koh grades Brown a 'high-quality WR2' in his internal tiers, on par with Rice, and both hosts project all three non-Kelce Chiefs receivers bunched in a 600-700 yard range for 2024 with Kelce clearing 900.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]): Waldman is 'totally feeling' Marquise Brown as the de facto Chiefs WR1 with Rashee Rice suspended, and says even once Rice returns, Brown may remain the more refined route-runner who wins downfield and in the intermediate game while Rice is still developing scheme-based (RPO/screen) usage. His caveat is durability - can Brown play 11-13-plus games - but if healthy he expects Brown to hold the top role in the Chiefs' offense, with rookie [[Xavier Worthy]] (4.21 40) providing a vertical complement that stretches the field for both. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) cites Chiefs insider Nick Athan characterizing Brown as the de facto WR1.
- 2024-07-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-02]]): Harmon notes Marquise Brown ran a real outside route tree in Arizona in 2022 (19.2% nine routes, 11.7% posts, 5.8% corners) that [[Rashee Rice]] never ran, and profiles as a better zone-beater than man-beater but not a strong press-beater; Harmon is mildly surprised Brown has played X the last two years rather than flanker/slot, where he thinks Brown fits better. His addition, along with [[Xavier Worthy]], is meant to clear out space underneath for Rice's slot-heavy role in the Chiefs' 2024 offense.
- 2024-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-01]]): Brown last year with Arizona lined up on the line of scrimmage for 77.4% of sampled snaps and in the slot on just 16.2% — usage Harmon does not love — but he guesses Brown ends up taking most of the Chiefs' X snaps by default, since [[Xavier Worthy]] will not do it and [[Rashee Rice]] probably will not either. The routes Harmon finds most interesting are posts and digs, because the intermediate and deep middle is the area no Chief filled last season; if Brown opens that up it is critical to the offense. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is more bullish, citing glowing camp chemistry reports with [[Patrick Mahomes]] and pegging Brown to lead Kansas City in both targets and yards, though his own projections have nobody on the team clearing 900 receiving yards. Koh argues Brown wins deep and intermediate better than Rice and fits a return to layered Andy Reid route concepts.
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): Brown is dealing with a scary shoulder injury from early in the preseason and may miss Week 1, which matters structurally for Kansas City because very few of their receivers are true outside players. Harmon says Brown can play that outside role — it is probably not his most optimal alignment, but there is proof of concept from Arizona, where he was primarily an on-the-line receiver, plus some earlier work there and in Baltimore; he has never been a true exclusive slot. If Brown misses time, that is the scenario in which Harmon could see Kansas City sliding [[Rashee Rice]] outside to do some of what he did at SMU.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]): Brown is interesting for the Chiefs' playoff run but Harmon expects a rotation, not a role — nobody in the Kansas City receiver room ran a route on even 70 percent of dropbacks last week. His offseason projection had been Brown at X, [[Xavier Worthy]] at flanker and [[Rashee Rice]] as a 110-plus catch big slot; with [[DeAndre Hopkins]] now on the roster playing mostly outside, he would rather see Brown moved off the line and given slot reps. The real need he identifies is the intermediate curl/dig area: [[Patrick Mahomes]] and Worthy cannot connect deep, Worthy is weak in the intermediate zone and Hopkins is a short-area slants threat now. Both hosts flagged the injury caveat — the shoulder/clavicle injury is upper body, but sending him on deep shots invites big contact and his health history is poor — so Harmon expects only another short-term deal next offseason. James Koh (co-host, not tracked) still expects [[Andy Reid]] to draw something up for him in the postseason.
- 2024-12-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-24]]): Brown's debut for Kansas City mattered structurally more than statistically — he profiled as roughly a 55/45 outside-to-slot player, and giving the Chiefs real slot reps let [[DeAndre Hopkins]] and [[Xavier Worthy]] move around from a formation standpoint rather than being locked into fixed alignments. Harmon calls him 'just like an actual NFL receiver,' which he means as a genuine compliment relative to what else Kansas City had been running out there. Co-host James Koh (not tracked), noting Brown had not played football in a long time, thought he looked pretty darn good on return and argues the Chiefs' 'death star' is now humming and being underrated because they keep winning close games.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Harmon likes Brown over 39.5 receiving yards at -115 against Houston, noting he has cleared the number in both games he has played since returning and that he is a big factor in the Kansas City offense right now — Kansas City targeted him for a reason and Harmon thinks that reason has shown up. The mechanism is alignment: Brown gets a little slot and a little outside, which is typical for a Chiefs receiver, and Derek Stingley will almost certainly travel with [[DeAndre Hopkins]] as the pure perimeter player, so a few slot reps should get Brown to the number on roughly three catches. Harmon also credited Brown's return plus a more comfortable [[Xavier Worthy]] with making the Chiefs offense look dangerous late in the season.
- 2025-02-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-04]]): scanning the free agent market for the speed perimeter receiver a Chip Kelly Raiders offense needs, Harmon lands on Brown as arguably the best available option and says that is not a bad thing — while noting the tier above him is uncertain ([[Tee Higgins]] situation unresolved) and the alternatives ([[Stefon Diggs]], [[DeAndre Hopkins]], [[Davante Adams]]) either lack speed or are implausible fits. The framing is a comment on a thin market as much as on Brown.
- 2025-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-05]]): Brown matches up well in Super Bowl LIX as Kansas City's field-stretcher — third-highest route participation on the team in the postseason at 72.7%, with a huge 18.3 air yards per target. Harmon does not expect high volume but does expect at least one big play against the zone coverage he thinks Vic Fangio will lean on, and he liked the over on Brown's 18.5-yard longest-reception line, reasoning that a target profile that deep plus any yards after the catch gets there. The caveat Harmon raises elsewhere in the segment: Brown, along with [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]], reads to him as a zone beater who has struggled against man at various times, which is why he wants [[DeAndre Hopkins]] more involved if Philadelphia plays man.
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-13]]): [[Marquise Brown]] is about the only true lid lifter in the free agent market and a pretty decent fit for the Giants next to [[Malik Nabers]] — he can play inside or outside and has taken primary X snaps, though Harmon does not think X is his best use. The point is not that Harmon values Brown himself: he says he is 'mostly looking for someone here that I don't really care about', whose job is to clear space and let Nabers work underneath and after the catch.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): on Brown returning to Kansas City and being a legitimate weekly fantasy play — in theory absolutely, in practice he has to prove he can stay healthy, which Waldman calls his major problem. His practical rule is to apply an injury-risk discount to Brown's ADP, and to buy when the market has not yet bid him up. Context in the Kansas City room: [[Xavier Worthy]] had a domestic-violence incident with no charges filed but worth monitoring, and [[Rashee Rice]] has off-field issues plus knee-injury recovery.
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]) on Brown re-signing in Kansas City for one year, $11M — a number Koh calls shockingly low and a bargain for the Chiefs: Harmon reads it as Brown wanting a mulligan on a lost 2024, essentially the same prove-it deal after the preseason chest injury cost him until roughly Week 16. Brown has positional flexibility — he once played almost exclusively as an X in Arizona (82.9% of sampled snaps outside, 77.4% on the line) and took a career-high-adjacent 35.2% of sampled snaps from the slot last year — and Harmon likes him a lot more inside. He is good in the intermediate area on digs and outs, and the career-long pattern holds: weak against man, good against zone, at 79.2% success vs zone last season. But Harmon calls it 'a diminishing version of the same product', with everything declining amid a long history of lower-body and foot injuries. In a room with [[Rashee Rice]] and [[Xavier Worthy]] he expects everyone to move around.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Brown is not the answer to Kansas City's standing X problem — his success rate versus press last year was in the 7th percentile, albeit on a small sample after an injury-shortened season, which Harmon says is exactly not what you want from a starting X. He profiles better as a slot/flanker. Harmon does grant that Brown has outside reps that worked: on the 2023 Cardinals he took 82.9% of sampled snaps outside and 77.4% on the line of scrimmage and had real moments. His projected Chiefs alignment is Brown at X, [[Xavier Worthy]] at flanker and [[Rashee Rice]] as the big slot, with the usual caveat that in Kansas City everybody plays everywhere. Koh (not tracked) again pleads for a big-bodied X.
- 2025-06-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: at WR58 in roughly round 12 Waldman would generally pass — if Brown is genuinely the best of that range then fine, but he expects to find more appealing options, saying yes to [[Christian Kirk]] without a doubt and an emphatic yes to [[Luther Burden III]] over him, while also passing on [[Rashid Shaheed]]. His objection is availability rather than injury prediction: Brown's career history says he does not stay healthy for a full season and he has been 'predictably unavailable'. Waldman also sees him now as the over-the-middle player Kansas City wanted [[Skyy Moore]] to be, projecting 40-50 catches for 500-700 yards and five to six touchdowns as a genuinely good outcome. He thinks the market is still anchored to Brown's rookie production with [[Lamar Jackson]] and that those days are not coming back.
- 2025-08-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Brown is back at practice for Kansas City and enters the six-game [[Rashee Rice]] suspension window as a live candidate for targets. Harris recalls being fairly high on him during his Ravens career — a 1,000-yard season plus campaigns of six, seven and eight touchdowns — and says the raw materials are still good, but that as the years have passed Brown has seemed to lack precision or something in his game. He also flags the 2024 wipeout: Brown lost the entire regular season to injury, returned for the playoffs and looked really bad, though Harris allows that coming back cold without any regular-season reps is a hard thing to do. His framing is that if Brown were to outproduce [[Xavier Worthy]] over the opening six weeks, all would be forgiven and it would be a genuine story.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): Brown is the joint number one waiver add for both Harris and guest Jake Ciely of The Athletic (not a tracked expert), and both 'feel terrible about it' — 26% rostered, and Harris says he did not even play that well, taking 16 targets and still finishing under 100 yards, with the stat line propped up by one miracle late catch from [[Patrick Mahomes]]. The case is pure volume: no [[Jalen Royals]], [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]] as the nominal number two, and roughly 30% or more of the targets in a Mahomes offense, with [[Rashee Rice]] about five weeks from returning. Ciely thinks he lands top-30 weekly until Rice is back; Harris agrees for Week 2, calls him the only name on the list who will be ranked as a starter in every ranking, and adds he could change his mind next week.
- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris expects Brown to be the ultimate DFS chalk in Week 2, citing a projected 57 percent ownership at 5,200 dollars off a shockingly large 19-target Week 1 that produced far less than it should have. He understands the appeal — how many more targets can one player get — but refuses to play him, saying he has been ground down by Hollywood Brown before and will not get back on that ride. Guest Jake Trowbridge (not a tracked expert) is likewise off him.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-02]]): Brown ran 61% of Kansas City's routes in Week 4, third among their receivers, and scored a touchdown, but Harmon does not think there is much pop left. He describes him now as a reliable zone beater and a smart receiver who will be in the right place, though a little mistake-prone — essentially an intermediate-only threat, because he is not a ball winner, he is small, and he offers little after the catch, so trying to generate big plays through him is a stretch. Harmon would like to see Brown and [[Tyquan Thornton]] rotate, reasoning that Thornton's speed better serves the goal of funneling targets to [[Xavier Worthy]] and [[Rashee Rice]]. He was explicit that this does not make Brown unplayable. Koh added that Kansas City tried Brown in the slot early in the season, abandoned it in Week 4, and played him primarily outside on in-breaking routes.
