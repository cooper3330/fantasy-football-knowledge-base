# Ainias Smith

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name inconsistently as "Aniah Smith" / "Anais Smith" / "Elias Smith";
> normalized here to the real name of the Texas A&M receiver/running back,
> Ainias Smith.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Going Deep - Draft Prospects)]]) [Dynasty]: the episode's title subject ("the sleeper miscast as a gadget"), and both hosts are unusually high on him. Waldman calls him "my sleeper in this class" and expects him to outplay his current public perception once he gets an NFL camp. The evaluation rests on fine catch-point technique that a true gadget/gimmick player typically lacks — turning the body away from contact before hitting the ground, tracking the ball on difficult across-the-middle and back-shoulder throws, hands technique on contested catches — plus efficient (not just dynamic) movement in the open field, an explicit contrast with Kadarius Toney, whom Angelo describes as dynamic but inefficient, "moving in circles around himself." Both flag the same risk regardless of talent: getting typecast early as a gadget/gimmick piece (comps: Lynn Bowden Jr., LaViska Shenault) and never escaping a marginal role — the preferred career shape instead compared to Danny Woodhead, whose value was largely unlocked outside his original drafting team. Both expect a Day 2-or-3 pick, discounted further by a lingering injury, and call him a diamond in the rough at that price. See [[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]].
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): weighs the off-field record — a two-year-old ACL tear, plus a Texas arrest for unlawful carrying of a weapon, DUI, and marijuana possession. Waldman largely waves off the weapon and marijuana charges (calls the NFL's drug-testing structure tacitly permissive of marijuana use, and says having a permit matters more than the weapon charge itself), but flags the DUI as the real issue — Smith has to show through his actions that he's matured, and a good team will interview around him to confirm it. On tape: "one of the most consistent pass catchers in his class," technically strong at every phase of route running, wins targets high, low, over the middle and at the boundary, and earns the ball with a notably tight catch-point attack even on full-extension grabs — praised by explicit contrast with [[Courtland Sutton]]'s looser hand technique in the same spot.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: reaffirms him as an "off the radar" rookie name, noting he entered college football as a running back before converting to receiver; restates him as one of the best after-the-catch runners and smoothest route runners in the class with the most consistent pass-catching technique Waldman has seen from this group, with the same March 16 caveat that his off-field record (marijuana and unregistered-firearm charges, a DUI) will discount his draft price if teams aren't convinced he's matured.
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon's real answer for most intriguing day-three sleeper -- Texas A&M's Ainias Smith (5'9"/190, small 8.5" hands), who ran 81.5% of his sample snaps from the slot with solid success rates vs. man (74%) and zone (77.8%), good hands, and wins in tight coverage on digs and posts. Flagged an 85.7% success rate on out routes (12.6% of his sample) as a sign he could be a vertical slot option, and floated a [[Curtis Samuel]]-style gadget/YAC comp given his tackle-breaking after the catch. Harmon admitted he watched little Texas A&M tape and leaned more heavily than usual on the charted data here.
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon ranks Ainias Smith as the favorite to win Philadelphia's WR3/slot job over Devante Parker, Johnny Wilson and Parris Campbell, citing a 74% success rate versus man coverage on his rookie-sample RP charting -- the best of that group. He compares Smith's profile to [[Curtis Samuel]] rather than Parris Campbell as a 'gadgety' college player who can develop into a real route runner from the slot, noting Smith is slot-only while Parker is X-only, which likely dictates the pecking order (Smith, Parker, Wilson, then Campbell in Harmon's ranking). He also floated that if Smith wins the slot job, it raises scheme questions about whether A.J. Brown or DeVonta Smith slide inside more often in Kellen Moore's 11-personnel-heavy offense, pointing to what Moore previously got from Keenan Allen and CeeDee Lamb as interior receivers.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman likes Smith as Philadelphia's likely third receiver over Parris Campbell and DeVonta Parker, noting Smith already earned reps quickly in a new role and that [[A.J. Brown]] has publicly talked him up. He argues Smith offers more juice than Kenny Gainwell in a Danny Woodhead-style backfield role — useful in Philly's frequent empty formations, where a limited receiver like a boundary-stationed [[DeAndre Swift]] added nothing last year, whereas Smith can be rotated into the backfield or motioned. The new kickoff/return rules are also a tailwind for Smith's value, per Waldman.
- 2024-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-16]]): Harmon called Smith 'a sleeper prospect' out of Texas A&M whom the Eagles drafted to play exclusively slot, and said he's a 'player to watch day one' who should help unlock more varied alignments for [[A.J. Brown]] and [[DeVonta Smith]]. He noted GM Howie Roseman downplayed the pick's uniqueness (available in the sixth round, so they took him), and that incumbent slot options Parris Campbell and John Ross are unproven -- Harmon flatly called Campbell 'not good' despite annual camp hype. [Dynasty]
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]) [Dynasty]: a downgrade in the short term. Waldman really liked the Texas A&M tape, but Smith has started slow in Eagles camp while [[Johnny Wilson]] has been more intriguing as an athlete with Parris Campbell hurt again. He now thinks Smith needs a year or two; the path is hanging onto a roster spot or the practice squad without being snapped up by another team, and growing into the contributor Philadelphia anticipated.
