# Jared Goff

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]): the direct comparison point for grading [[Tua Tagovailoa]]'s ceiling. Harmon self-identifies as "the biggest Goff homer in the world" but still places him outside a top-12 quarterback conversation — "somewhere" in the 11-14 range; James Koh independently lands at "11, 12-ish." Credits a "significantly... noticeably stronger arm" than Tua's as the separating physical trait. Frames the open long-term question as what Detroit's offense looks like once OC Ben Johnson eventually leaves for a head-coaching job, and what extension Goff is paid on. Wild Card performance cited as context: 22/27, 277 yards, TD, INT, 121.4 rating in a win over the Rams.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): additional context on his season ahead of the Divisional Round at Tampa Bay — Baltimore "stuffed Jared Goff in a locker" earlier in the year (a bad regular-season loss, cited as evidence of how good Baltimore's defense can be), but he answered well against pressure the one other time he saw Tampa Bay this season, going 10/19 for 171 yards and 2 TD against the blitz in a Week 6 matchup — cited as a positive indicator for the rematch. Notes both Goff and Baker Mayfield are among the league's most pressure-susceptible quarterbacks, making the Lions' pass protection the swing factor in the game.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]): picks the 49ers over the Lions in the NFC Championship, but calls Goff's road performance a mitigating factor for Detroit's chances rather than a red flag — notes Goff actually posted his best game of the season on the road, in Tampa Bay, this year.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): used as the optimistic development comp while discussing [[Brock Purdy]]'s ceiling — Goff "is so much more advanced mentally now" than he was during his earlier Rams (Sean McVay) years, offered as evidence that quarterbacks who develop can take years to arrive rather than being static from day one.
- 2024-01-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-25]]): calls the NFC Championship at San Francisco "a big moment" for Goff specifically, citing Kyle Shanahan's history against him from their shared Rams years — "he just boxed up Goff bad" — and flags Goff as "a little hit or miss" against the blitz, which 49ers DC Steve Wilks is more willing to send than most. Both hosts favor San Francisco to win comfortably. Separately, notes unconfirmed league-circle chatter that a Ben Johnson-to-Washington head-coaching move is close to "a done deal," comparing the certainty to when everyone knew Kyle Shanahan was headed to San Francisco before it was official — explicitly flagged in this wiki as an unconfirmed rumor, not a fact, but directly relevant to the already-open question of what Detroit's offense looks like once Johnson leaves.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]), after Detroit's NFC Championship loss: Waldman is willing to go further than "serviceable" — "more than serviceable" — pointing to a great offensive line and a full supporting cast (David Montgomery/Jahmyr Gibbs, Amon-Ra St. Brown, Sam LaPorta) as evidence Detroit is set up for continued success regardless of coordinator departures. The Ben Johnson rumor firms up further — "the Commanders seem to be heading towards hiring Johnson," with Waldman noting it was "baked into the cake" once Washington hired Adam Peters (ex-49ers) as GM — while a competing report has Ravens DC Mike Macdonald also drawing head-coaching interest elsewhere. Waldman's explicit framing: he's "not so worried about coordinators" in general — "we overrate coordinators, it's players that actually make the difference more often than not" — unless a new coordinator misuses a player's core skill set (his example: forcing a spread-concept player under center).
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): the Ben Johnson-to-Washington rumor firms further still — "the worst kept secret in the league," possibly official "by the end of this week" — with a new wrinkle: offensive line coach Hank Fraley is rumored to follow Johnson out the door too, which would pull run-game infrastructure along with the play-caller. Reaffirms the standing "top-15, not top-10" quarterback grade in passing (Detroit's Super Bowl reach credited more to Brad Holmes's overall drafting than to Goff individually).
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): **the standing rumor is resolved, not just complicated — Ben Johnson turned down every head-coaching opportunity, including Washington, and is staying in Detroit as OC.** Harmon calls the football world "absolutely shocked." With continuity secured, the read on Goff himself sharpens rather than softens: "I don't want to be the bag on Jared Goff guy... but there is a level where Jared Goff cannot take you much further" — the offense (8th in EPA/play, 7th in success rate, a top-10 unit) is credited to a full supporting cast Detroit will need to keep paying (Amon-Ra St. Brown due for a market-rate extension soon, Goff's own next contract looming), with a clear remaining team need at X receiver — Josh Reynolds "gave admirable play" but "probably cost them the game at times" in the NFC Championship. Floats a long-shot scenario where Detroit trades for a franchise-tagged [[Tee Higgins]] if Cincinnati goes that route, while conceding it's unlikely.
- 2024-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-20]]): Waldman gave an extended retrospective on the Goff/Sean McVay arc. McVay inherited a slow-to-develop Goff coming out of a "high school"-level Jeff Fisher offense and an air-raid college scheme, and chose to heavily script and micromanage him to win immediately with a first-round investment; that worked until it didn't, because Goff was never allowed to develop independent in-game processing and struggled once disguised, blitz-heavy pressure (notably from Miami/Brian Flores) forced him to think for himself. Waldman argued McVay effectively scapegoated Goff to justify pivoting to [[Matthew Stafford]], who had the standing to tune out McVay's constant in-ear direction. He credited Dan Campbell and Detroit for throwing "everything" at Goff with nothing to lose and finding he handled pressure fine — something Waldman said was also true of Goff at Cal — concluding all three parties (McVay, Goff, Campbell) were ultimately "vindicated" by how it played out.
