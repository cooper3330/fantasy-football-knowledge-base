# Jared Goff

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]): the direct comparison point for grading [[Tua Tagovailoa]]'s ceiling. Harmon self-identifies as "the biggest Goff homer in the world" but still places him outside a top-12 quarterback conversation — "somewhere" in the 11-14 range; James Koh independently lands at "11, 12-ish." Credits a "significantly... noticeably stronger arm" than Tua's as the separating physical trait. Frames the open long-term question as what Detroit's offense looks like once OC Ben Johnson eventually leaves for a head-coaching job, and what extension Goff is paid on. Wild Card performance cited as context: 22/27, 277 yards, TD, INT, 121.4 rating in a win over the Rams.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): additional context on his season ahead of the Divisional Round at Tampa Bay — Baltimore "stuffed Jared Goff in a locker" earlier in the year (a bad regular-season loss, cited as evidence of how good Baltimore's defense can be), but he answered well against pressure the one other time he saw Tampa Bay this season, going 10/19 for 171 yards and 2 TD against the blitz in a Week 6 matchup — cited as a positive indicator for the rematch. Notes both Goff and Baker Mayfield are among the league's most pressure-susceptible quarterbacks, making the Lions' pass protection the swing factor in the game.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]): picks the 49ers over the Lions in the NFC Championship, but calls Goff's road performance a mitigating factor for Detroit's chances rather than a red flag — notes Goff actually posted his best game of the season on the road, in Tampa Bay, this year.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): used as the optimistic development comp while discussing [[Brock Purdy]]'s ceiling — Goff "is so much more advanced mentally now" than he was during his earlier Rams (Sean McVay) years, offered as evidence that quarterbacks who develop can take years to arrive rather than being static from day one.
- 2024-01-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-25]]): calls the NFC Championship at San Francisco "a big moment" for Goff specifically, citing Kyle Shanahan's history against him from their shared Rams years — "he just boxed up Goff bad" — and flags Goff as "a little hit or miss" against the blitz, which 49ers DC Steve Wilks is more willing to send than most. Both hosts favor San Francisco to win comfortably. Separately, notes unconfirmed league-circle chatter that a Ben Johnson-to-Washington head-coaching move is close to "a done deal," comparing the certainty to when everyone knew Kyle Shanahan was headed to San Francisco before it was official — explicitly flagged in this wiki as an unconfirmed rumor, not a fact, but directly relevant to the already-open question of what Detroit's offense looks like once Johnson leaves.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]), after Detroit's NFC Championship loss: Waldman is willing to go further than "serviceable" — "more than serviceable" — pointing to a great offensive line and a full supporting cast (David Montgomery/Jahmyr Gibbs, Amon-Ra St. Brown, Sam LaPorta) as evidence Detroit is set up for continued success regardless of coordinator departures. The Ben Johnson rumor firms up further — "the Commanders seem to be heading towards hiring Johnson," with Waldman noting it was "baked into the cake" once Washington hired Adam Peters (ex-49ers) as GM — while a competing report has Ravens DC Mike Macdonald also drawing head-coaching interest elsewhere. Waldman's explicit framing: he's "not so worried about coordinators" in general — "we overrate coordinators, it's players that actually make the difference more often than not" — unless a new coordinator misuses a player's core skill set (his example: forcing a spread-concept player under center).
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): the Ben Johnson-to-Washington rumor firms further still — "the worst kept secret in the league," possibly official "by the end of this week" — with a new wrinkle: offensive line coach Hank Fraley is rumored to follow Johnson out the door too, which would pull run-game infrastructure along with the play-caller. Reaffirms the standing "top-15, not top-10" quarterback grade in passing (Detroit's Super Bowl reach credited more to Brad Holmes's overall drafting than to Goff individually).
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): **the standing rumor is resolved, not just complicated — Ben Johnson turned down every head-coaching opportunity, including Washington, and is staying in Detroit as OC.** Harmon calls the football world "absolutely shocked." With continuity secured, the read on Goff himself sharpens rather than softens: "I don't want to be the bag on Jared Goff guy... but there is a level where Jared Goff cannot take you much further" — the offense (8th in EPA/play, 7th in success rate, a top-10 unit) is credited to a full supporting cast Detroit will need to keep paying (Amon-Ra St. Brown due for a market-rate extension soon, Goff's own next contract looming), with a clear remaining team need at X receiver — Josh Reynolds "gave admirable play" but "probably cost them the game at times" in the NFC Championship. Floats a long-shot scenario where Detroit trades for a franchise-tagged [[Tee Higgins]] if Cincinnati goes that route, while conceding it's unlikely.
- 2024-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-20]]): Waldman gave an extended retrospective on the Goff/Sean McVay arc. McVay inherited a slow-to-develop Goff coming out of a "high school"-level Jeff Fisher offense and an air-raid college scheme, and chose to heavily script and micromanage him to win immediately with a first-round investment; that worked until it didn't, because Goff was never allowed to develop independent in-game processing and struggled once disguised, blitz-heavy pressure (notably from Miami/Brian Flores) forced him to think for himself. Waldman argued McVay effectively scapegoated Goff to justify pivoting to [[Matthew Stafford]], who had the standing to tune out McVay's constant in-ear direction. He credited Dan Campbell and Detroit for throwing "everything" at Goff with nothing to lose and finding he handled pressure fine — something Waldman said was also true of Goff at Cal — concluding all three parties (McVay, Goff, Campbell) were ultimately "vindicated" by how it played out.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]): given the proposition that Goff is better against pressure in Detroit because he became a better NFL quarterback, Waldman rejected the premise — 'fuck that, that's not true.' His argument is that Goff was always good under pressure: 'if you watch Jared Goff at Cal, he was fantastic versus pressure, unbelievable versus pressure.' What changed is that 'Jared Goff wasn't being micromanaged in his ear by Sean McVay,' plus a much better offensive line. He was careful not to make McVay the villain outright — McVay is a good coach who was told Goff couldn't drive, so he drove for him, a learning curve — and said Goff was the scapegoat for the Super Bowl loss. Dan Campbell's approach was to throw everything at him and find out. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) supplied the stats: Goff is the only quarterback this season with a perfect completion percentage in a half, done three times including 12-of-12 against Minnesota, and over the Lions' past four games they have scored more touchdowns (18) than Goff has incompletions (15).
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): Goff is playing excellent football and just erased the last remaining narrative against him — a division road game, outdoors, in bad weather, in which he completed his first 11 passes. Waldman gives Ben Johnson credit for the game plan but leans on Tom Brady's broadcast point that playing in weather is a trial-and-error skill that simply takes reps: a quarterback in sunny Los Angeles never accumulates them, whereas New England, Pittsburgh, Cleveland or the NFC North force you to figure it out fast. Now that Goff has been in the North long enough to have played Chicago and Green Bay in those conditions, Waldman's read is that he has figured it out.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): Goff is his central case study in how long development takes and how much setting matters. Labeled a colossal bust as a third-stringer in Los Angeles, Goff had gone from a 1-11 Cal team to the Air Raid to a West Coast offense, which Waldman's colleague Will Hewlett described as being like learning Chinese. Waldman's theory is that Sean McVay, hired to salvage the number one pick, put strings on Goff's helmet and coached him play to play — helpful early, diminishing returns later, because it never let Goff learn to process alone; once Todd Gurley and the offensive line eroded and Belichick and Vic Fangio-style pressure schemes arrived, McVay used his Super Bowl equity to lobby for a veteran. Waldman says McVay may have enabled him or may have scapegoated him. In Detroit, Dan Campbell threw everything at him sink-or-swim and found a resilient quarterback who understood football.
- 2025-02-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-03]]): 'oh fuck no, I'm not worried about that offense.' Asked whether Detroit's brain drain — Ben Johnson gone, passing game coordinator Tanner Engstrand off to the Jets — threatens Goff after a season with 203 play-action attempts, a career high and 52 more than second-place [[Matthew Stafford]], Waldman argued the guts of the team are in the trenches, and the trenches are more than fine, with good skill talent alongside. Dan Campbell has not left, and while Campbell may not be the schematic genius, he is one of the reasons the roster looks the way it does, along with GM Brad Holmes. In Waldman's framing, that is the 'brain' worth worrying about, and it is still intact.
- 2025-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) said he plans to spend the summer yelling about Goff as the quintessential pocket-passer trap — going around QB8, he asks what the actual difference is between Goff and [[Dak Prescott]], and says he might even prefer Prescott, calling Goff a clear-cut mid-to-high-end QB1 by ADP who looks really, really overrated. Waldman drew the distinction explicitly: from an NFL evaluation standpoint Goff is not overrated at all, but from a fantasy price standpoint he absolutely is, and separating those two judgments is what playing the value game means.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): Goff at plus 1200 is Harris's single bet for the NFL's leading passer — he came close last year, and Harris is 'sort of just buying the Lions', pushing back on the outdoor-games knock by noting Goff performed well outdoors in 2024 even if that had not been true before. Guest Chris Raybon (Action Network, not tracked) liked the bet, expecting Detroit in more close games and fewer blowouts than last year (when [[Sione Vaki]] and Dan Skipper were seeing late snaps), and adding that moving on from [[Tim Patrick]] for [[Isaac TeSlaa]] adds receiving upside at no run-blocking cost, with [[Jameson Williams]] likely to have another gear.
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]) — both hosts land on 'fuck that' for the stat that Goff had the second-largest QBR decline when pressured versus a clean pocket. Waldman's objection is that the decline is large partly because his baseline was the sixth-highest QBR overall; digging into the pressure and blitz splits, he found Goff 'really freaking good', completing 51.7% against pressure (third highest of the top quarterbacks he sampled this summer) with eight touchdowns against pressure, bettered by only three passers, and a 70% completion rate plus more yardage against the blitz than [[Sam Darnold]], [[Kyler Murray]], [[Patrick Mahomes]] and [[Brock Purdy]], with only [[Lamar Jackson]] ahead. He concedes QBR includes fumbles and Goff had seven. Bob Harris (not tracked) cited Week 6 versus Dallas (6-of-7, 170 yards, two TDs under pressure) and the following week versus Minnesota's Brian Flores blitz (a season-high 51% of dropbacks pressured; 10-of-11 for 164 yards and two TDs). Waldman blames the narrative on Sean McVay and a media that flatters him, and notes Goff finished the last three years as QB7, QB10 and QB7.
- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-11]]): on the FanDuel prop, Harmon takes the under on Goff's 249.5 passing yards while expecting him to throw a lot of touchdowns at home against Chicago. His reasoning is the Bears defense, which impressed him a lot on Monday night — they played well on the defensive line and 'smelled blood in the water' with J.J. McCarthy before letting it slip late — against a Detroit interior offensive line he calls a unit in transition. He notes Penei Sewell had issues with Micah Parsons in Week 1 but expects Sewell to be the player we know; it is the interior he thinks Chicago can pressure to keep the game and the yardage total low.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): Goff posted only about 150 passing yards in Detroit's comfortable win in Baltimore because the run game did the work — even removing [[David Montgomery]]'s 72-yard third-quarter run, the Lions still had 152 rushing yards. Harris's evaluation is unchanged and blunt: 'he is what he is — you get pressure on Goff, you get it in his face, you get him throwing on the move off his spot, it's usually not going to look that good, but when you don't it can.' Twice in this game it did: the stutter-go fade touchdown to [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] against Marlon Humphrey in tight man coverage, and a feathered fourth-down play-action shot near midfield. Harris's broader point is that the Detroit offensive environment is one of the few you can trust week to week even when individual pieces go quiet.
