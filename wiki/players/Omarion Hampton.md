# Omarion Hampton

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: one of his two 'lunch pail' backs in the class — what you see is what you get, with little hidden upside but a very high floor. Angelo expects him to go in the early second round and projects an 8-10 year career because he simply does not make mistakes as a runner and does the things you want at his size through contact. His stated calling card is low red zone work: Angelo says he is very good at recognizing defender leverage and playing off it, and should score a boatload of touchdowns. The caveat attached is that he is not an exciting watch unless you enjoy the small details.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'criminally overpriced until you find a fit' at Underdog RB19. Waldman mocked the online contingent that treats Hampton as the resolution to all the world's problems, and gave his own read: a fine, nice running back who can probably be a borderline A-back/B-back type able to do both roles, but who might end up stuck in a B-back role. He does not think year one is that time unless Hampton lands behind a strong starter with a great offensive line and that starter gets hurt. He rated the fade harder than his fades of [[Quinshon Judkins]] and roughly alongside [[TreVeyon Henderson]], and said he would take [[Kenneth Walker III]] or [[David Montgomery]] over him.
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: both agree with 'good but overhyped'. Waldman calls him a B-back who could be an A-back if called upon, landing somewhere between Jamaal Williams and [[David Montgomery]] — good, but not the player you stake the argument that this is a great running back class on. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) expects him to be the second or third back off the board: 48 production grade, 56th percentile strength of schedule and 49th percentile program quality at North Carolina dinging him relative to [[TreVeyon Henderson]], but 77th percentile PFF run grade, 63rd percentile combined PFF grade and a 6.33 Zierlein grade push him over the 60th percentile pre-draft threshold, where hit rates are good and 13 comparable players became a top-six fantasy back within three years. He loves watching Hampton at the second level — 'a loose freight train' — but worries about durability given how violently he runs. Waldman notes Hampton was the social-media flavor two weeks ago and [[Kaleb Johnson]] is now.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]) [Best Ball]: Hampton at RB17, 51st overall, is a 'fuck it' — too pricey. Waldman grants he is a good back with power, top-end speed, reasonable hands and the potential to become a good blocker, but says he gets moved and ragdolled around more than he should for his size, and that while the timed speed excites people relative to his frame, he needs a long runway to reach it and his overall athletic profile is not as strong as the speed implies. Waldman's conclusion is that Hampton profiles as the B-back in an A-back/B-back committee, so he would rather take [[Ashton Jeanty]] at RB4 than Hampton at RB17. Bob Harris (not tracked) says Waldman crushed his dreams having gone heavy on rookie backs in that draft.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: Hampton is dependable down to down and rarely loses yardage, but he cannot play as fast as he times because he cannot process as fast — his second-level decision making against linebackers is the question, and Angelo said his acceleration capability does not match his combine. Fix it and he is a Pro Bowl-caliber back; do not, and a top-25-to-32 pick carries real bust risk, because drafting him in the first round forces him to be serviceable from day one. He partly credited coaching and a weaker 2024 North Carolina offensive line, and doubted Sean Payton would take him in Denver — expecting a [[TreVeyon Henderson]], a third-round stab at [[Brashard Smith]], or continued faith in [[Audric Estime]] — because Payton wants the pass-game weapon of that offense and that is not Hampton. [[Matt Waldman]] framed it as high-end committee-level acceleration with low-end elite top speed: his big runs come when he has a long runway, and without clean space he could not beat second-level defenders. Both would rather have [[Damien Martinez]] two-plus rounds later.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): Hampton fits either the Steelers or Giants and Waldman likes the Pittsburgh fit better, since both want to pound the football — but his core read is that Hampton is 'a volume runner disguised as a big play runner,' and a cheap grocery-store disguise rather than a Spencer's Gifts one. He notes it takes Hampton a while to reach top speed, which will cost him, but calls him a powerful back who should improve at pass protection and cut his fumble rate; do those and he complements a [[Jaylen Warren]] type and grows into the [[Najee Harris]] role. Waldman explicitly rejects the Jim Brown framing others use and would take Harris over Hampton, expecting Hampton in Pittsburgh to look like the Harris people criticized.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): Hampton is his pick to go LOWER, and he rejects the media framing of him as 1B to [[Ashton Jeanty]]'s 1A — 'there is a grand canyon of difference' — placing him as roughly 3C, a top-ten back in the class but at the lower end. Scouts he trusts told him they do not get the hype; he gets pushed around and falls to the ground as a blocker, is big but does not run to his size, and his pads and hips misalign on contact ([[Isaiah Spiller]] comparison) so his power base disappears. Top speed is good, acceleration below starter level. Comps: [[James Robinson]] but a lesser version now, or Latavius Murray. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees and has him RB4-5, well below consensus: no plan down to down through contact, takes unnecessary hits for his size, and critically does not process moving pictures — if the gap is not clean he will not clean it with his eyes. Both doubt Sean Payton would tolerate him in Denver.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]): after weeks of everyone expecting him at pick 20 to Denver, he fell to the Chargers at 22, and Waldman frames him as a threat to split time with [[Najee Harris]], not to take the job outright. His comp is blunt — Hampton is more Gus Edwards than [[Joe Mixon]] — and he thinks Jim Harbaugh wanted a big bulky backfield (praising the Hassan Haskins waiver claim, shuttling off [[J.K. Dobbins]] after he played well) and is a meddlesome, weaker drafter than his brother John. Bob Harris (not tracked) agrees it is a share at least initially and says he is lower on Hampton than the field, noting Hampton went earlier than he would have taken him in a SiriusXM post-draft mock where he got [[Quinshon Judkins]] afterward.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]): if [[Najee Harris]]' eye injury is truly superficial, then on Hampton it is 'fuck this noise.' Waldman argues people want Hampton to be a thing because he is the latest shiny early pick and because it would prove beat writers and draftniks right that he was 1B to [[Ashton Jeanty]]'s 1A. Hampton is a physical specimen — so was Najee Harris — but Waldman says he does not have Harris' pass-protection chops and does not quite have his between-the-tackles chops either, and he distrusts the 'Harris sucked behind that Pittsburgh line' narrative, comparing it to Steelers fans insisting [[James Conner]] was the problem and Benny Snell the answer, when Conner is still holding off top prospects in Arizona. He notes backs cannot prove much in camp anyway, so Hampton could be proclaimed the bell cow in Week 1 and Harris could be taking 60% of the touches by Week 3. At RB16 before the injury news Waldman was already unwilling to pay, preferring [[Kenneth Walker III]], [[Chuba Hubbard]] and Conner at better prices with roles he is more comfortable with; the news only raises Hampton's price. He stays open to the eye injury being more serious. Bob Harris (not tracked) disagreed at the margin, buying a Hampton share in best ball at the still-RB16 range as a hedge.
- 2025-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-30]]): Hampton was the surprise of Harmon's running back charting project — he grades better in zone but only average in man/gap, and the Chargers under Greg Roman will run a lot of man/gap, making it an awkward fit at a time when people are crazy high on him. Harmon is curious whether it works out as well as the market expects, and notes co-host James Koh (not tracked) relayed that [[Matt Waldman]] shares similar concerns about Hampton as a gap runner. Koh remains gassed up on him post-[[Najee Harris]] injury but concedes the same shape of concern: Hampton creates big plays on gap runs but may not be the steady grinder, which is part of why Harris was signed. Harmon describes him as a firecracker in a soda can — genuinely explosive, with the charting also tracking explosive and run-stuff rates.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]): asked whether Hampton 'did not look quick enough' on his Hall of Fame Game red-zone carry, Waldman said 'fuck that noise' — on his one viewing Hampton had to bounce it outside, and burst should never be judged off a single carry or off preseason work (he cites early-career misreads of [[James Conner]] and [[David Montgomery]]). But he adds a real caveat from his own charting: across dozens of clips, getting through the box clean and beating box defenders was not as easy for Hampton as his reputation suggested, and he needs a longer track to reach top speed than the NFL usually gives — so he is probably not the game-breaker people expect from a back that size, though that is not failure. Waldman points instead to 1,500 scrimmage yards and 15 touchdowns in each of his last two college years, Greg Roman's historical usage of backs, and the fact that Hampton currently projects to the preponderance of the workload.
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman agrees Hampton gets the first shot at the lead role but calls himself the goofball offering alternative facts on the split — he argues the 'Najee missed install' angle is overstated, because [[Najee Harris]] has already learned multiple NFL offenses while Hampton is learning both a new offense and a new league, including defensive disguises and how fast NFL defensive tackles close. On the eye injury, Waldman consulted his father, who worked with Olympic athletes on eye injuries: most likely a corneal abrasion healing in five or six days, possibly blood forced into the anterior chamber, which needs rest from anything that raises ocular pressure — his father called the Chargers' 'superficial' label BS but still projected Hampton ready around week one or two if eased in. Waldman therefore holds a 50-50 split projection even with Hampton starting, and says if Hampton struggles in pass protection or hot-read recognition, Harris takes half. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked) moved Hampton up from a fifth-round redraft pick to as early as the top of round three, because the week-12-or-13 takeover thesis is now a week-one one. Both flag that the bigger backfield story is the [[Rashawn Slater]] injury, worth shaving 10-15% off Chargers rushing projections.
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-13]]): Hampton is his number one ADP surprise — a first-round rookie running back whose price never got going despite [[Najee Harris]]'s shocking eye injury, sitting in the fourth round rather than the second where he expected the market to push him. He would take Hampton in the fourth and expects to end up with shares, while explicitly disclaiming knowledge of the player: 'I don't really believe any of it until we see them live,' and he notes [[Matt Waldman]] had real questions about Hampton. Dave Kluge of Footballguys (guest, not a tracked expert) took Hampton in the fourth of their mock for the same reason, ranks him one RB and about eight overall spots above ADP, and explains the drift — Hampton spiked into round two while Najee Harris was absent, then fell back with Harris returning and the Rashawn Slater injury; he expects late third to early fourth to be the settling point.
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: expects a near-even split with [[Najee Harris]] by the end of the year rather than a Hampton takeover, even with Harris's eye injury opening the door. His reasoning is that Hampton needs a year to adjust to box defenders being much quicker than what he faced in the ACC — a lot of his college big runs came off long runways he will not get in the NFL — and that there are gaps in his decision making and in his pass protection, which is promising but not there yet. Waldman projects the Chargers backfield collectively at 1,400-1,500 yards and 10-12 touchdowns split fairly evenly, with Harris's blocking and receiving keeping him on the field. [[Chris Harris]] says he is surprised Hampton is lasting to the late third round and expected a second-rounder; Waldman attributes the calm to overreaction-free reading of a preseason bounce-outside run.
- 2025-08-20 — According to guest Scott Pianowski (Yahoo Fantasy, not a tracked expert) on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Hampton is a round-three safe pick — he should get the workload he wanted now that [[Najee Harris]] has had a lost summer, and the Chargers want to pound the rock even after losing Rashawn Slater. Pianowski flags that Hampton has been a helium player all summer with rising ADP, so season to taste. Harris raises [[Matt Waldman]]'s doubts from the previous day's show — big plays came against Wake Forest, the wide running lanes will not exist in the NFL, and Hampton is always trying to hit home runs rather than creating space for himself. Pianowski does not watch college film and defers to Waldman's expertise, but leans on trusting the coach and the quarterback: he trusts [[Jim Harbaugh]], who has run the ball successfully at Stanford, San Francisco and Michigan, and trusts [[Justin Herbert]], while allowing Hampton is also a good talent in his own right.
- 2025-08-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: on the [[Najee Harris]] injury leak he bumped Hampton again, to one spot ahead of [[James Conner]] among running backs and near the top of the fourth round overall — while predicting that from here he will go a lot higher than that in most drafts. Harris flagged his own hesitation: he has [[Matt Waldman]]'s words ringing in his ears, since Waldman does not love Hampton as a prospect, and said if Hampton starts going in the second round he certainly will not pay that price. He expects the Week 1 Chargers-Chiefs opener in Brazil to be a featured showcase for Hampton and the first real evidence.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): yes, Hampton is going too high, because [[Najee Harris]] is a better back than people realize and Waldman does not trust Pittsburgh media or fans on running back judgment. He expects an even split once Harris is available Week 1. On Hampton the player: a bruiser who drags defenders and runs through contact, with open questions about whether he consistently finds the right creases and how he handles lower-leg wraps. Waldman rejected the widely held view that Hampton is the 1B to [[Ashton Jeanty]]'s 1A on talent. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) agreed the price moved from about RB16 to RB14 on camp enthusiasm he never joined, noting Greg Roman has said they will pair him with someone regardless; Harris said he would rather have [[Kenneth Walker III]], where he has heard from beat writers that no split is coming, accepting the risk that behind Walker and [[Kyren Williams]] he has no fallback.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates Hampton a 4 out of 10, wanting to see the workload and noting [[Najee Harris]] is a professional running back who will be on the field if healthy, so the split is unknown. Harris rates him a 6, adding a blunter question on top of the committee risk — 'is he good?', is he ready to be a third-round fantasy pick. Harris nonetheless says he is much less offended by Hampton in the third than by [[Ashton Jeanty]] in the first.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): Hampton got the Week 1 workload only because [[Najee Harris]] had no time to get conditioned after the eye injury, and Waldman expects 'more of an even split than people anticipate' — he explicitly warns that citing Week 1 volume here has no context. Hampton is 'the hammer': get him on the perimeter with a runway and nobody catches him, and downhill he carries defenders for a 'lower middle class Earl Campbell impression', but when gaps are squeezed as Kansas City did and he must create in the backfield, that is not his game. He comps him to Rashard Mendenhall. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) is more skeptical — he has not seen anything special yet — and notes Josh Norris cited the RSP's Latavius Murray comp, which Waldman accepts as a good-enough starter-level back.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): it was all Hampton in the Chargers backfield, which is the important part. He was fairly well stuffed, but Harris says he cannot watch that and claim any other back would have had more success, because there was no room to run against a Chiefs front that is really stout against the run. He wants a better read in Week 2. Relatedly, Harris pushes back hard on a note he saw suggesting you drop [[Najee Harris]], calling that patently incorrect and saying he is pretty sure Najee was not in game shape and that the workload split will get closer as games arrive.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-09]]): Hampton got every opportunity in the Week 1 win over Kansas City — co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) noted roughly an 80% snap share and plenty of carries — but neither host thought he looked good running between the tackles in the man-gap scheme Jim Harbaugh and Greg Roman prefer. Koh had flagged the fit concern in the offseason off Hampton's charting profile, and said it is not that Hampton is bad in man-gap, it is that it is not a strength: the way to configure the run game around him is outside, letting him use his big body and long speed to punish defenders. Harmon added that Hampton creates big plays when given a crease on a gap run but struggles to read it out and be a sustaining rusher, so expect a low success rate with a deceivingly high yards per carry as he rips occasional long runs.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Hampton's Monday night against the Raiders was a dud — 8 carries for 24 yards and 1 catch for 1 yard despite a 37-9 snap edge over [[Najee Harris]] — and it ended badly. He was stuffed three straight times from the two-yard line in the third quarter, then had a handoff hit him in the bread basket and go on the ground while the Chargers ran clock; it was scored a [[Justin Herbert]] fumble, but Harris says it is a running back fumble all day long and notes the coaches clearly agreed because that was Hampton's final snap of the night. Harris: he does not really make anybody miss, and I don't know if he's good or not — not enough film yet. Guest John Daigle (Establish The Run, not tracked) countered that opportunity is trending up. Harris cautions it is way too early to call a late-third-round pick a bust, though he owns none himself and thought the price too rich; the market usually makes first-round rookie backs a top-15 overall pick and did not here. Broncos next, a hard matchup.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Going Deep Listener Q&A)]]): used as the worked example for why raw per-carry efficiency misleads on rookies. Against the Raiders, [[Najee Harris]] was the more efficient of the two backs, but Angelo says that does not mean Harris is the better player — Hampton's process coupling, the timing of reads and footwork, simply is not there yet, and he adds it is not fully there for [[Jaydon Blue]] either. His point is that this takes a lot of time, and the right way to read the numbers is to check the offensive line's down-to-down blocking efficiency first and then ask whether the back is at, above or below expectation given that blocking.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with [[Najee Harris]] lost for the season to an Achilles rupture, Hampton is set up to consolidate the backfield of a 3-0 Chargers team — he took 43 of the 48 offensive snaps after the injury. Harris called it Hampton's best of three games, though not an incredible performance: a well-designed 22-yard screen on the tying fourth-quarter drive where the design did the work, and a three-yard inside draw for the game's first touchdown before halftime. He defended Hampton's 'bad runs' as pure stuffs no back would have salvaged, and noted Hampton had held the starting job even through two poor weeks. Caveat: [[Kimani Vidal]] is on the practice squad and a promotion likely follows, and the Chargers are a pass-first offense that throws to three or four targets regularly.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-29]]): Waldman clarifies and partly softens his pre-season stance. He says his view was never 'I hate Hampton' — it was that he did not like him as much as most did, rejecting the consensus that Hampton was equal in talent to [[Ashton Jeanty]], and that with [[Najee Harris]] present Hampton would not be a featured bell cow. Two weeks of heavy usage have not changed his evaluation: Hampton is a good starting back if you avoid asking him to change direction behind the line or in traffic, and instead give him a crease so he can be a battering ram with speed who can outrun a safety with a runway — which is exactly what has happened. He notes Najee Harris was outplaying Hampton before getting hurt. Because the Chargers now have no choice, this is Hampton's best case, so Waldman calls him a [[Dynasty]] sell-high as production accumulates over the coming weeks.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): the first game without [[Najee Harris]] and the Chargers threw the rookie into the deep end — Hampton was the only back to get any carries at all, playing 49 offensive snaps to Hassan Haskins's five and [[Kimani Vidal]]'s two. First half looked like more of the same: moving the pile rather than creating, a couple of check-down screens. Then a late third-quarter shotgun draw sprung him and Harris notes Andy Behrens's Yacht Club point that you just want to see Hampton make one guy miss on his own — he did not have to here, but at 220 pounds he could house it, which he did, and in the fourth he ripped another big gain around the left edge on a surprise third-and-short handoff. Harris's honest ledger: we still have not seen Hampton make anybody miss with a move, but he is definitely strong, can be physical, and we have now officially seen the speed translate in the clear. Caveat: they all need better blocking.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Hampton was catching screens and 'kind of maybe the only thing that was working a little bit' once the Chargers' offensive line was decimated, before suffering an ankle injury and going to the locker room. Harris was baffled that with Hampton out on the goal line they gave the carry to [[Kimani Vidal]] when Hassan Haskins 'is like twice his size,' and neither replacement played well. Guest **Sam Gold** (NOT a tracked expert) says he'll have a hard time buying Haskins or Vidal even in Hampton's absence: behind backup-to-the-backup tackles they don't generate enough on their own to offset the blocking, and this is 'not a [[Rachaad White]] situation' — you'd only play them out of desperation for three points.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: since Monday's show Hampton has gone on IR with an ankle injury and is out at least four weeks, 'and sometimes it's longer than that with an ankle injury', leaving the Chargers down two starting running backs and creating the week's top waiver situation ([[Kimani Vidal]] and Hassan Haskins). Guest Brandon Funston of The Athletic (not tracked) adds a note against overpaying for the vacated role: 'it took a while to even to get Omarion Hampton going there'. Harris flags the offensive environment too — three tackles lost, an offense that looked awesome for three weeks now unable to protect the quarterback.
