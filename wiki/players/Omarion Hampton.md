- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: one of his two 'lunch pail' backs in the class — what you see is what you get, with little hidden upside but a very high floor. Angelo expects him to go in the early second round and projects an 8-10 year career because he simply does not make mistakes as a runner and does the things you want at his size through contact. His stated calling card is low red zone work: Angelo says he is very good at recognizing defender leverage and playing off it, and should score a boatload of touchdowns. The caveat attached is that he is not an exciting watch unless you enjoy the small details.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'criminally overpriced until you find a fit' at Underdog RB19. Waldman mocked the online contingent that treats Hampton as the resolution to all the world's problems, and gave his own read: a fine, nice running back who can probably be a borderline A-back/B-back type able to do both roles, but who might end up stuck in a B-back role. He does not think year one is that time unless Hampton lands behind a strong starter with a great offensive line and that starter gets hurt. He rated the fade harder than his fades of [[Quinshon Judkins]] and roughly alongside [[TreVeyon Henderson]], and said he would take [[Kenneth Walker III]] or [[David Montgomery]] over him.
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: both agree with 'good but overhyped'. Waldman calls him a B-back who could be an A-back if called upon, landing somewhere between Jamaal Williams and [[David Montgomery]] — good, but not the player you stake the argument that this is a great running back class on. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) expects him to be the second or third back off the board: 48 production grade, 56th percentile strength of schedule and 49th percentile program quality at North Carolina dinging him relative to [[TreVeyon Henderson]], but 77th percentile PFF run grade, 63rd percentile combined PFF grade and a 6.33 Zierlein grade push him over the 60th percentile pre-draft threshold, where hit rates are good and 13 comparable players became a top-six fantasy back within three years. He loves watching Hampton at the second level — 'a loose freight train' — but worries about durability given how violently he runs. Waldman notes Hampton was the social-media flavor two weeks ago and [[Kaleb Johnson]] is now.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]) [Best Ball]: Hampton at RB17, 51st overall, is a 'fuck it' — too pricey. Waldman grants he is a good back with power, top-end speed, reasonable hands and the potential to become a good blocker, but says he gets moved and ragdolled around more than he should for his size, and that while the timed speed excites people relative to his frame, he needs a long runway to reach it and his overall athletic profile is not as strong as the speed implies. Waldman's conclusion is that Hampton profiles as the B-back in an A-back/B-back committee, so he would rather take [[Ashton Jeanty]] at RB4 than Hampton at RB17. Bob Harris (not tracked) says Waldman crushed his dreams having gone heavy on rookie backs in that draft.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: Hampton is dependable down to down and rarely loses yardage, but he cannot play as fast as he times because he cannot process as fast — his second-level decision making against linebackers is the question, and Angelo said his acceleration capability does not match his combine. Fix it and he is a Pro Bowl-caliber back; do not, and a top-25-to-32 pick carries real bust risk, because drafting him in the first round forces him to be serviceable from day one. He partly credited coaching and a weaker 2024 North Carolina offensive line, and doubted Sean Payton would take him in Denver — expecting a [[TreVeyon Henderson]], a third-round stab at [[Brashard Smith]], or continued faith in [[Audric Estime]] — because Payton wants the pass-game weapon of that offense and that is not Hampton. [[Matt Waldman]] framed it as high-end committee-level acceleration with low-end elite top speed: his big runs come when he has a long runway, and without clean space he could not beat second-level defenders. Both would rather have [[Damien Martinez]] two-plus rounds later.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): Hampton fits either the Steelers or Giants and Waldman likes the Pittsburgh fit better, since both want to pound the football — but his core read is that Hampton is 'a volume runner disguised as a big play runner,' and a cheap grocery-store disguise rather than a Spencer's Gifts one. He notes it takes Hampton a while to reach top speed, which will cost him, but calls him a powerful back who should improve at pass protection and cut his fumble rate; do those and he complements a [[Jaylen Warren]] type and grows into the [[Najee Harris]] role. Waldman explicitly rejects the Jim Brown framing others use and would take Harris over Hampton, expecting Hampton in Pittsburgh to look like the Harris people criticized.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): Hampton is his pick to go LOWER, and he rejects the media framing of him as 1B to [[Ashton Jeanty]]'s 1A — 'there is a grand canyon of difference' — placing him as roughly 3C, a top-ten back in the class but at the lower end. Scouts he trusts told him they do not get the hype; he gets pushed around and falls to the ground as a blocker, is big but does not run to his size, and his pads and hips misalign on contact ([[Isaiah Spiller]] comparison) so his power base disappears. Top speed is good, acceleration below starter level. Comps: [[James Robinson]] but a lesser version now, or Latavius Murray. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees and has him RB4-5, well below consensus: no plan down to down through contact, takes unnecessary hits for his size, and critically does not process moving pictures — if the gap is not clean he will not clean it with his eyes. Both doubt Sean Payton would tolerate him in Denver.

- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]): asked whether Hampton 'did not look quick enough' on his Hall of Fame Game red-zone carry, Waldman said 'fuck that noise' — on his one viewing Hampton had to bounce it outside, and burst should never be judged off a single carry or off preseason work (he cites early-career misreads of [[James Conner]] and [[David Montgomery]]).
