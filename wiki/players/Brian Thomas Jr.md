- 2026-10-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-10-15]]): Harmon is torn on a BTJ trade -- Jacksonville has said he's more valuable to them than as a trade asset, and Harmon agrees given injury risk elsewhere at WR (Parker Washington, Jacoby Meyers). But BTJ has lost snaps to Josh Cameron (39 snaps the last two weeks vs. BTJ's 69) and trails Washington (99 routes) and Meyers (102) in target priority. Harmon stresses BTJ is not a pure sacrificial X -- he's run 26% of his routes from the slot this season -- he's simply not getting the ball (targeted on just 11% of his routes the last two weeks). On tape, Harmon doesn't think BTJ is playing at a level that justifies taking volume from Washington, Meyers or Brenton Strange; he's better than last year's injury-marred tape but not a standout. Co-host James Koh (untracked) pushed back, arguing BTJ's downfield skill set gives Jacksonville more strategic value than a 3rd-round pick would. Harmon floated San Francisco (Shanahan/Liam Cohen scheme, a Z-receiver/slasher role opposite Mike Evans) and a half-joking Giants reunion with former LSU teammate Malik Nabers (Jameis Winston's deep-ball offense) as landing spots if traded, plus Kansas City as a 'fancy Tyquan Thornton' replacement; Bills/Chiefs interest is unlikely to be consummated since AFC rivals rarely trade with Jacksonville.
- 2026-10-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-10-20]]): [[Brian Thomas Jr]] played zero snaps in the second half of a game where [[Travis Hunter]] expanded his offensive role, and had another drop after a difficult start to the season. Harmon contrasted this with [[Jordan Love]]'s public vote of confidence in [[Matthew Golden]] despite his drops, saying he hasn't seen that same trust and resiliency dynamic built between Thomas and the Jaguars. Harmon said Thomas is 'not just a sacrificial X receiver' running blocking-type routes -- the actual receiving usage simply 'isn't working' -- and called this an inflection point: it's now 'a matter of when, not if' Jacksonville trades him, especially with [[Josh Cameron]] emerging as a blocking-first X and Hunter's receiving ceiling rising. Both hosts floated potential landing spots -- San Francisco, the Rams, or an AFC team like the Chargers -- while noting Jacksonville likely won't help a direct conference rival; James Koh also raised a reunion angle with former LSU teammates [[Malik Nabers]] and [[Jayden Daniels]] in Washington, or a fit with the Giants.
