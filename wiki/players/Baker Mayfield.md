# Baker Mayfield

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]): **feeling it** on Mayfield being Tampa Bay's starting quarterback in 2024. Reasoning: the Buccaneers are likely to re-sign him based on how well he held the offense up behind an offensive line Waldman describes as not good. Raises a Geno Smith-style year-over-year leap as a live possibility. Waldman's own framing is measured rather than enthusiastic — "I don't love Baker, but I like what he's doing this year within his capabilities."
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the tone shifts from measured to genuinely bullish. Both hosts now expect Tampa Bay to re-sign Mayfield "assuming a reasonable enough price tag"; Harris grades the season "Pro Bowl level Jeff Garcia," crediting a more mature package around the same traits (gritty, downfield arm, offense command) that read as flaws during his Cleveland rookie year. [[Mike Evans]] has publicly said he hopes to stay in Tampa too, which both hosts read as the offense wanting to keep "the band together."
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): a more skeptical, "hard to pin down" read than Waldman's — calls his career "streaky," citing genuinely bad stretches (the worst quarterback in the league during his Carolina run) alongside real high points (the Raiders/Broncos wins). Explicit ceiling: "always be somewhere lower than the 20th-best quarterback in the league, but still" a viable starter. On contract: floats something in a 2-year, $50M range as fair value, doubts he'd actually sign for that little, but strongly recommends he stay in Tampa Bay rather than face what would be his 7th head coach (including interims) if he changed teams again — especially with [[Mike Evans]] and OC Dave Canales likely also staying.
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): the "likely also staying" Canales assumption from the January 23 episode is now overtaken — **Dave Canales is leaving Tampa Bay to become Carolina's head coach.** Harmon credits Canales's scheme design directly for getting "the best out of Baker Mayfield that they possibly could" in 2023 (leaning into air-raid tendencies while keeping him disciplined within Todd Bowles's run-heavy first-and-10 approach), which makes the OC departure a fresh, ungraded 2024 variable on Mayfield's outlook rather than a settled Tampa re-sign story.
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): the OC question from January 30 gets a resolution — Tampa Bay hires Liam Coen (a former Rams assistant Mayfield reportedly signed off on and wanted, from their brief prior time together) as OC. Waldman is "totally feeling it" on Mayfield repeating his 2023 performance under Coen, but explicitly conditions that on the receiver room staying intact — "if Mike Evans leaves, then no, f\*\*k that," and says losing [[Chris Godwin]] too would likely be enough on its own to knock Mayfield off his draft board at whatever price the market sets, even while still projecting him as a viable high-end streaming/QB2 option regardless.

- 2024-02-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-27]]): points to personnel/scheme reasons behind his strong 2023 beyond his own play — Tristan Wirfs's move from right to left tackle and a strong year from the right tackle spot gave him a much-improved offensive line versus 2022, and [[Mike Evans]]'s expanded route tree (fewer go routes, more quick-developing slants/outs) suited Mayfield's get-the-ball-out-fast style far better than the prior Byron Leftwich/Tom Brady scheme.

- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: rates staying in Tampa Bay "the most likely outcome," bolstered by the Bucs hiring Liam Coen (ASR garbled in the audio as "Lean Cohen"; normalized here) — a Mayfield-approved former Rams assistant from their prior time together. Projects a returning Mayfield as a fringe top-10 fantasy quarterback but warns against drafting him at that price: "you want to draft him in the bottom of the top 15" or lower for value. Comps him as an "upper-end Jeff Garcia" and a Derek Carr/David Carr type who won't take the same sack volume but will still take unnecessary hits by breaking the pocket when he shouldn't have to.

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): argues [[Mike Evans]]'s re-signing directly benefits Mayfield's own leverage calculus by removing his incentive to test the market — floats hypothetical suitors (Atlanta, Minnesota if Kirk Cousins leaves, Denver under Sean Payton) that Harmon says now "negate" themselves with Evans staying, given how much of Mayfield's 2023 success traced to Evans's expanded route tree. Separately credits new OC Dave Canales as an emerging "quarterback whisperer" for back-to-back reclamation jobs, first Geno Smith and now Mayfield.
- 2024-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-25]]): Harmon says he has let go of his skepticism from Mayfield's disastrous 2022 Carolina efficiency, arguing quarterbacks should be divorced from rotten situations — Matt Rhule's Panthers fired Joe Brady to hire Ben McAdoo. What Dave Canales did was ask what Mayfield actually does well and build to it: grip it and rip it, push the ball outside the numbers and deep, get it out fast on slants and out routes, and avoid asking him to read the middle of the field where he drifts into chaos mode. He cites EJ Snyder and Brett Coleman of the Bootleg Football Podcast (not tracked) noting Mayfield threw out routes at elite volume and efficiency. Koh is more bearish, expecting an efficiency slide post-Canales like [[Geno Smith]]'s in Seattle.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Mayfield has absolutely turned a corner, and the specific mechanical change is pocket movement — in Cleveland his first reaction to interior pressure or a tightening pocket was to turn his back and try to roll out, which was awful for his game; now he climbs the pocket as his first reaction in difficult situations, the same skill Waldman says [[Jayden Daniels]] still has to learn. He also has freedom to make line calls he didn't have last year. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) asked whether Cleveland bailed a year early; Waldman allowed it was possible but argued the situation was dysfunctional and that getting cut loose forced Mayfield's reassessment of himself as a person and professional.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-12]]): a genuine mechanical change, not just production. In Cleveland Mayfield would not climb a compromised pocket unless it was a scripted hitch-and-throw; when the pocket broke down in two areas he turned his back and ran outside like [[Kyler Murray]], which resets nothing and kills his options. Week 1 he climbed difficult pockets and found receivers, and per the national broadcast crew he told them Dave Canales would not let him check out of dead plays while the new staff gives him that freedom — Waldman saw him changing something every two or three plays, for the better. With [[Chris Godwin]] healthy and [[Mike Evans]] still Mike Evans, Waldman calls him a good bet for top-12 QB; in Cleveland he would have said a top-five ceiling was 'hell no.' Harstad (NOT tracked) frames it as floor near top-15, likely ceiling top-10 to top-12, top-five remote, and notes 2023 Tampa production was dismissed as fluky but now looks commensurate with the play.
- 2024-09-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-20]]): Mayfield is Waldman's case study in the games 6-20 window, when opposing coordinators finally have enough pro tape to build a book. He looked all-world early in Cleveland and then went downhill, and Waldman splits the blame — some Baker, some the team, teammates and coaching staff, multiple systems, plus what he attributes to Mayfield's own attitude and approach to the game at the time (citing what Mayfield's wife has said). His read now is that Mayfield has learned a lot since being forced out of Cleveland and deserves credit for how he has played in Tampa across last season and this one. Separately, Waldman uses Mayfield alongside Drew Brees as the 'hyper-accurate' archetype in the [[Bryce Young]] discussion, noting Mayfield had a good offensive line and a better scheme around him than Young does.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): like [[Sam Darnold]], Mayfield deserves most of the credit for his own turnaround rather than Dave Canales — Waldman puts the coaching share at roughly 25 to 30 percent. He credits Mayfield with getting genuinely better in the pocket and better at managing aspects of the game, and specifically with owning up to his part in his own immaturity as a quarterback and as a person. The take sits inside his standing argument that NFL coaches do not develop players, especially quarterbacks, so the credit assigned to [[Kevin O'Connell]] and Canales over Kyle Shanahan, Liam Coen or Sean McVay is misplaced.
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]): Mayfield was better than his line looked in Tampa Bay's 51-27 win over New Orleans (325 yards, four touchdowns, three interceptions on 594 team yards) — one pick was genuinely bad, one was tipped and one bounced off [[Jalen McMillan]]'s hands. Waldman says interceptions are rarely the right lens on a quarterback; what matters is how he responds and creates second and third chances. Mayfield is 'better than ever in the pocket,' now climbing it to generate quick opportunities rather than bailing backwards as he did in Cleveland, where he would turn and run instead of stepping up one or two steps to find the open man. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) adds the leadership and toughness angle, noting Mayfield has a better receiver duo — maybe the best one-two in the NFL — but a worse offensive line and run game than he had in Cleveland.
- 2024-12-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-30]]): 'fuck that revisionist history' — Cleveland moving on was not a mistake. Waldman praises the last two years in Tampa as genuinely different football from what he saw in Cleveland, where Mayfield could not manage a pocket, broke it too readily and invited pressure rather than creating second-chance opportunities from within it. He had three-and-a-half to four years as a starter there and did not fix it; it took being a journeyman across three teams in two years, and what Mayfield's wife described as a come-to-Jesus moment about what he had to own, before he did. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) pushes back that this argues for teams being more patient with first-round talent and investing more in development — Waldman agrees with the principle in the abstract but notes that relative to the rest of the league Cleveland was among the most patient with a struggling quarterback, alongside the Giants with [[Daniel Jones]], so he will not pile on Cleveland for this specific decision.
- 2025-01-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-14]]): Mayfield went 15 of 18 for 185 yards, two touchdowns and a 146 rating (83% completions) in the 23-20 loss to Washington — Harmon's highest single-game success rate for him since joining Tampa Bay — and both hosts blame Todd Bowles, not the quarterback, for a game plan that had him throwing only 18 times against what Koh calls a very mid Washington defense. Harmon's criticisms are narrow: Mayfield got to his scrambles a bit quickly and ran himself into pressures and sacks on seven carries, and he fumbled again, with turnovers a continuing problem. Both hosts loved the spinning first-down run. Harmon's headline is that Tampa's offense remains the best part of the team, while the defense and Bowles' fourth-down and timeout management held them back all season.
- 2025-01-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-27]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: asked whether Mayfield is about to make another offensive coordinator a lot of money, Waldman says there is a good chance — he sees Mayfield as sitting on 'that meridian line between franchise starter and not franchise starter'. Statistically 2024 was fantastic for a stretch, but his question is what happens without some of the weapons: [[Chris Godwin]] potentially gone and [[Mike Evans]] a year older, including the effort spent chasing Evans's 1,000-yard streak. Bob Harris (not tracked) pushed back jokingly against Evans slander. Waldman lands on yes.
- 2025-02-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the right price on Mayfield after a QB5 finish is somewhere in the QB9 to QB12 range, and he may go even lower because of noise around [[Chris Godwin]]'s departure and coaching-staff turnover — 'anything lower than that is just a gift.' Waldman is 'feeling' Tampa Bay's promotion of passing game coordinator Josh Grizzard, who called the team's third-down plays, to offensive coordinator over Liam Coen: if the offense is working, do not mess with it, and making [[Mike Evans]] and Mayfield learn a new system for a year would be a dumb idea. He would rather Tampa build the offensive line and add weaponry, with [[Tetairoa McMillan]] or a newcomer replacing Godwin.
- 2025-02-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-21]]): Mayfield leads the NFL over the last two seasons with 69 touchdown passes and sits second in passing yards at 8,544, behind only [[Jared Goff]] — whose 67 touchdowns and 2.8% interception rate against Mayfield's 2.7% make the two eerily similar, with the trade-off being that Mayfield takes more sacks and scrambles more. Harmon groups them as the poster boys for first-overall reclamation projects, and notes Detroit had Ben Johnson through the whole run while Tampa has churned coordinators. James Koh (not tracked) went further, saying he changed his mind: last year he saw Mayfield as a product of Dave Canales, [[Mike Evans]] and [[Chris Godwin]], but in 2024 he was convinced Mayfield genuinely elevated the talent around him. Koh's caveat is that nobody should be surprised if he comes back to earth, and if he is only along for the ride the Bucs are in trouble.
- 2025-05-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-30]]): Harmon says Mayfield 'still doesn't get enough credit for how he's played the last couple of years' and that he played at a top-ten quarterback level last season — while carefully declining to call him a top-ten quarterback. He names Mayfield as one of two quarterback rehabilitation projects he was wrong about, along with [[Sam Darnold]], and says that history is why he is forcing himself to stay open-minded on [[Justin Fields]]. Koh (not tracked) supplies the numbers: roughly 4,500 passing yards and 41 touchdowns, third in the NFL in passing yardage and second in touchdowns behind only [[Joe Burrow]]'s 43, and 69 passing touchdowns over the last two seasons to lead the league — up from 28 in 2023, which Koh thinks is closer to the true baseline heading into Mayfield's age-30 season with Liam Cohen gone.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): Mayfield is the case study for what [[Kyler Murray]] has not done. Waldman pushes back on the 'whitewashing' of the Cleveland years — the idea that the Browns mishandled a franchise player — and says the fix was on Mayfield himself, as even his wife described leaving Cleveland as forcing a hard look at himself. The single change that moved him closer to a Drew Brees-type profile is that he finally learned to step up in the pocket. Waldman traces the flaw to his scouting report at Oklahoma and Texas A&M and to the Rose Bowl against Georgia: Mayfield scored well in the first half against zone with a weak rush, then his game fell apart once Georgia went man-to-man and forced precise, on-time throws from inside the pocket; even his completions in that half were late throw after late throw, and he spun away and rolled out rather than climbing. He repeated that in Cleveland. Waldman is clear that Mayfield will never be confused with Murray athletically.
- 2025-08-15 — According to Cousin Josh (untracked co-host of the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast]]) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-15]]): Mayfield is his number 2 frag player at ADP 69 — a sixth-round quarterback outside the super-tier of [[Josh Allen]], [[Lamar Jackson]], [[Jayden Daniels]], [[Joe Burrow]] and [[Jalen Hurts]]. He loved watching him (the Top Gun of quarterbacks, pure popcorn) but distrusts the efficiency: QB3 last year on a touchdown rate that jumped from 4.9 to 7.2 percent and rushing yards that doubled to 378 with 41 total touchdowns, plus Harris's own Almanac research that players who post a first top-10 finish after year six average an 18th-place finish the next season — the [[Geno Smith]] pattern. He adds no [[Chris Godwin]] to start, an older [[Mike Evans]], no left tackle and no offensive coordinator. [[Chris Harris]] agreed but framed it as positional rather than personal: with [[Justin Herbert]] at 109, and Dak, Purdy and Drake Maye later still, if the prices were reversed we would be drafting Mayfield. He also worries about the interception-heavy 'he just wins games' quarterback who Blake Bortles the following year.
- 2025-08-18 — According to guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert), on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Mayfield is his No. 3 bust, and the sixth-round ADP is indefensible — for a while he was going ahead of [[Patrick Mahomes]], which Doherty calls a gut-level mistake. The core number is a 7.2% touchdown rate against a 4.6% career mark before last year, in the best offense and best supporting cast he has had; 40 passing touchdowns is not repeatable, and he doubled his previous career high in rushing without being a true dual threat. Tristan Wirfs and [[Chris Godwin]] are injured, [[Emeka Egbuka]] is an unproven rookie, and [[Bucky Irving]] could pull the offense toward the run. His advice: do not bet on a repeat, go find this year's Baker Mayfield. [[Chris Harris]] agrees and adds that Mayfield threw 13-plus interceptions in four of five starting seasons, had never finished better than seventh in passing touchdowns nor topped 28 before last year, and that taking any non-top-six quarterback in the sixth round is inexcusable.
