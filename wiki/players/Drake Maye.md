# Drake Maye

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name as "Drake May"; normalized here to his real name, Drake Maye.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — Guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Dynasty]: strongly feeling it — explicitly rejects a "Mitchell Trubisky 2.0" comparison some had floated for a fellow North Carolina passer. His case rests on lower-body mechanics rather than arm talent (which he also grades highly): Maye has what he calls a "spidey sense" for pressure — the same trait he credits to Tom Brady, Patrick Mahomes, and Josh Allen to a degree — that lets him make small in-pocket adjustments to extend plays and avoid sacks, unlike a prospect such as Will Levis, whose lower body he calls "almost completely detached" from his reads. Flags one specific mechanical fix needed: his back foot comes up early on some outside-the-hash throws, causing accuracy misses. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees the feet are "the window into what a quarterback sees" as a general evaluation principle, and reframes some of Maye's sack/pressure numbers as more a product of surrounding talent and offensive circumstances than a pocket-processing problem.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: his own first detailed grade, delivered more skeptically than guest Daniel Harms's bullish 2024-01-15 take — a live disagreement, not a resolution of it. Flags Maye as a "high-end accuracy in the box score, but not on the film" case, comparable to Baker Mayfield's college profile: pocket movement grades well, but decision-making and footwork-driven accuracy grade below what the box score suggests. Explicitly floats him as "my Desmond Ridder of this class" (ASR renders this "Desmond Ritter"; see [[Desmond Ridder]]) — a player who may be overhyped relative to what the tape shows — while flagging more film left to re-watch before finalizing a grade over the following six weeks.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-01]]) [Dynasty]: the promised re-watch, and the skepticism holds. Admits he'd worried he "sleepwalked" through the first evaluation and had avoided saying much publicly until rewatching — but the rewatch "didn't change my feeling." Specific diagnosis: can extend plays and take hits, but his ability to diagnose coverage and know what to do with it "consistently at a higher level" is missing. New comp, replacing the earlier Baker Mayfield reference: Jake Locker — a quarterback with all the physical tools (size, arm) but accuracy that was never there — with a stated rule of thumb that a starting-caliber quarterback needs to solve roughly 3-5 of a game's 5-7 "defense has you cornered" moments, and Maye "strikes me as that player" who can't yet do it that consistently. Flags a systemic risk on top of the individual one: the archetype of quarterback North Carolina's system produces (Mitch Trubisky, Sam Howell, Drake Maye — described as "not too far apart on the same wavelength") has not translated to sustained NFL success. Grade if it holds: "my Will Levis, Desmond Ridder" of the class — an explicit downgrade in company from the January 22 Desmond-Ridder-only comp.

- 2024-02-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-05]]) [Dynasty]: reinforces the skepticism a third time, now framed explicitly as a rapid-fire "f\*\*k it." Says he initially avoided forming an opinion because industry consensus ("everybody's talking about Drake Maye... he's the automatic number two") made him second-guess an independent read, then found his own tape conclusion held anyway: "he's like my Desmond Ritter this year" (see [[Desmond Ridder]]) — draftable, a great athlete capable of huge plays, but with "some Mitchell Trubisky to his game" on decision-making, accuracy and mechanics. Explicitly staying away. Notes he isn't surprised some evaluators are privately leaking similar concerns pre-combine, and flags a market signal to watch: whether Maye's consensus draft slot starts sliding out of the top three in the following weeks.
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]) [Dynasty]: the market-slide signal from February 5 hasn't materialized yet — most mock drafts Waldman is seeing still slot Maye in the top three alongside [[Caleb Williams]] and [[Jayden Daniels]] — but he flags Maye specifically (more than Williams or Daniels) as the one name whose spot could still realistically be disrupted, without further elaboration this episode. Also groups Maye with Daniels as needing real bench/development time before starting — not primarily a physical-readiness question but a processing one: rookies have to learn to read NFL-caliber disguised coverages faster than they ever had to in college, and Waldman's explicit read is that Maye "has seen" more complex looks than Daniels but hasn't "necessarily done it well" against them yet. States a two-year runway (rather than Daniels's one) is what he'd want for Maye specifically.

- 2024-03-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-08]]) [Dynasty], solo cast on the full QB class, the most detailed critique yet: gaps identifying and acting on favorable leverage between receivers and coverage; difficulty spotting linebackers dropping post-snap; squeezes targets into tight man-coverage windows where the defender already has the advantage before the ball is thrown; and is one to two beats late delivering to wide-open receivers because he keeps double-checking other options against flooded zones. Illustrates with a charting detail: it took six full games of charting before Waldman saw Maye's first intentional throwaway. On the plus side: decision-making qualities during his initial drop, works progressions sideline-to-sideline with some timeliness, flashes leverage-advantage recognition and can layer the ball well. Verdict, framed through [[Quarterback Processing and Confidence]] — the identification is often there, but the confidence to close the gap between identification and action isn't, the same failure mode Waldman's flagged before in Kenny Pickett and [[Desmond Ridder]]: "I don't think he's the number two quarterback in this draft, and I don't think he is remotely the next safest quarterback in this class." Could still become a competent-to-good decision-maker paired with good physical tools, but carries real bust risk if it doesn't develop.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: in the same Minnesota-fit conversation, grants he's "tough" against pressure but otherwise reads closer to [[Sam Darnold]] than to a true difference-maker — floats that if a team is betting Maye becomes what Darnold could have been, that's the real signal of the plan, while reiterating he sees Maye as much closer to what Darnold has actually shown than to his ceiling.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: discussing Washington's No. 2 pick, says flatly "I don't think Drake May is the best rookie fit for any team that wants to take someone in the first round" — reads Washington's best fit as [[Michael Penix Jr.]] instead, someone he sees as more accurate and better at finding the open man even if graded as less mobile/tough on paper. On New England at No. 3, floats a smokescreen scenario where the Patriots trade back rather than take Maye, preferring a more mobile pocket passer like [[Jayden Daniels]] if it means re-tooling their offense around a different quarterback archetype entirely.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): qualifies his own do-not-draft list — if he's a GM whose bosses want the rookie quarterback starting this year or next, "there is no way I am putting Drake May on my draft board." Calls him "the quintessential robo quarterback" — checks every physical/pedigree box (prominent program, prolific yardage, requisite completion percentage, big frame, big arm) but is missing the harder-to-coach situational skills: pinpoint placement/accuracy, confidently and accurately processing coverage, late pre-snap/early post-snap diagnosis, sensing edge pressure specifically (distinct from handling hits, which he's fine at), and efficient pocket management — drifts outside and loses his linemen's leverage. Reiterates the Jordan Love developmental comp: can see a path to him being a good starter, but only with real bench time to get acclimated to the NFL before those weaknesses get exposed. [[Brandon Angelo]], same episode, agrees and adds the explicit contrast with [[Caleb Williams]]: Williams can carry a game's tempo and flow on day one; Maye can't yet, which Angelo calls the biggest difference between the two prospects.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]): reacting to ESPN's Merrill Hodge (not a tracked expert) calling Maye a potential "coach killer" — reframes it as a systemic critique of the NFL's rookie-QB development pipeline rather than a knock on Maye's talent or character: Maye is talented enough that a team will start him before he's ready, and the coach absorbs the blame. Directly prefers [[J.J. McCarthy]] over Maye as a Day 1 starter — "I'd rather go with the unproven vertical guy who's good in the pocket... than the guy who's [proven] that he's not very good in the pocket" — even while calling Maye the more athletic of the two.
- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, contrasting Maye's pocket movement directly with [[Caleb Williams]]'s: when Williams leaves the pocket it's purposeful — pressure is already there, and he stays efficient, keeps his eyes downfield and takes small steps to climb, the way Baker Mayfield climbs a pocket. Maye, "much more frequently," either stands in and takes the hit or turns his back to pressure and rolls/flushes — reactionary, inefficient escapes that force blockers to reset angles and receivers to reroute, costing time. Restates this as the core swing factor in the two prospects' pocket management, consistent with his prior [[Quarterback Processing and Confidence]] critique of Maye.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]), naming Maye their lead "coach killer" candidate — see [[Coach Killer Prospects]] for the definition — needs "a year or two of seasoning" (Waldman's explicit word choice over "developing") because North Carolina, like fellow UNC alums Mitch Trubisky and Sam Howell before him, never asked its quarterbacks to process the field on an NFL timeline. Waldman: "the timing element of playing quarterback isn't there with them or wasn't there at UNC." See [[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]] and [[Quarterback Processing and Confidence]].
- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]) [Dynasty]: speculates that if Maye lands with Minnesota, it would be his best chance at rookie success playing behind [[Justin Jefferson]], though still a likely-bumpy rookie transition; warns that if it doesn't work out, Jefferson could push for a trade before his contract is up.
- 2024-05-02 — According to Brandon Angelo and Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): both compared Maye stylistically to [[Jordan Love]] as a big-armed quarterback who ideally needs time to sit and develop, but worried he won't get that runway the way Love did behind Aaron Rodgers — instead he's likely to be thrown in immediately with a mostly rookie/inexperienced supporting cast. Waldman praised new coach Jerod Mayo for prioritizing receivers who can help Maye (Ja'Lynn Polk, Javon Baker) and noted Mayo reportedly gave Maye input on which receiver prospects he liked, choosing Baker over Devontae Walker at Maye's preference — a level of rookie autonomy Waldman said Bill Belichick would never have granted, and one he argued could have saved both Belichick's and Mac Jones's jobs had it existed earlier.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman remains largely on the 'fuck it' side for Drake Maye as a Year 1 starter, reiterating previously-discussed hero-ball concerns, and expects [[Jacoby Brissett]] to open camp as the more likely Week 1 starter — 'a pretty serviceable piece, not a winning piece' for a Patriots team with no real expectation of a big season. Bob Harris (untracked, RSP Cast co-host) went further, floating renaming the podcast 'The Fuck Drake Maye Podcast,' framing his skepticism as being about the system pushing a young QB into a starting role prematurely rather than about Maye's talent specifically.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo says he wasn't uber-high on Maye pre-draft — three-level accuracy was spotty — but believes New England will bring him along correctly under Jerod Mayo, likely sitting him his entire rookie year behind Jacoby Brissett given the roster is thin (Rhamondre Stevenson graded only average-to-slightly-above as a lead-back weapon). Angelo credits Maye's physical toolset — big, strong, big arm, able to throw to all three levels — and says the gap is translating collegiate improvisation into NFL structure. Waldman agrees this is a deliberate, patient development plan and says he'd be pleased to see Maye not play meaningful snaps until Week 13-14 or later.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]) [Best Ball]: guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) expects [[Jacoby Brissett]] on the bench by Week 4 or 5 given a brutal opening schedule and fan/owner pressure. He acknowledges major holes in Maye's game but likes the rushing profile for fantasy: 14% designed rush share in college and an 11% scramble rate, both second only to [[Jayden Daniels]] in the class. McFarland's framing is Mitchell Trubisky-range value — roughly 25 rushing yards a game, the odd two-touchdown rushing week, which turns a 15-point passing week into 20. Not a home-league draft pick, a waiver-wire add, though best ball drafters will take him. Waldman also prefers Maye to Brissett because he is likelier to uncork the big downfield plays for [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] and [[Javon Baker]].
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): reacting to Ben Volin's report — corroborated to Bob Harris by Tom King of the Nashua Telegraph — that [[Joe Milton III]] was outperforming Maye, Waldman said the alarming part is not Milton but the fit. Maye's feet were a problem when he entered college, and Alex Van Pelt's offense is rigid about where the quarterback's feet must be and when the throw comes out, which Waldman called a scary year-one setup. He expects a lot of [[Jacoby Brissett]] and thinks Maye needs time, though he believes feet can be cleaned up inside a season with work. Waldman cautioned that camp rep reports lack context, citing Ed Valentine's account of Brian Daboll putting [[Daniel Jones]] in nothing but third-and-long against Wink Martindale's most devastating blitzes to test how he handled it, and noting staffs sometimes give first-team reps purely to confirm a player cannot do something before cutting him.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]): should Maye start in September? 'Oh, fuck that.' He had good moments in preseason, but against vanilla defenses with heavy personnel churn, and preseason data is not gospel. Maye needs time to sit, observe and learn defensive looks and disguises, and above all to clean up his footwork — people will show isolated plays where the footwork is fine, but it must be automatic enough to survive looks that boggle his mind. Waldman's mechanism: when a quarterback has to think about what he is seeing, the baseline skills slow down and get sloppy, and that ruins the whole game. He endorses Phil Perry's point that Alex Van Pelt's system demands the feet be in a specific spot at release with strict timing, which is not the offense Maye played in college — while [[Jacoby Brissett]] started 11 games in Van Pelt's Cleveland offense and was QB15 over that span. Great hope long term, but not a fantasy asset, and few Patriots should be.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): asked if Maye is ready to start as a rookie, Waldman said no — but expects New England to play him anyway. His projection at the time was roughly eight games of [[Jacoby Brissett]] and nine of Maye, with a high interception count and a high sack rate, offset by decent rushing yardage and occasional big plays that make him a streamer at best. He debunked the viral 'Herbert-like' clips: rolling left with nobody bearing down and hitting a receiver with no defender within 15 yards tells you nothing outside a high school game. His structural worry is that Maye is up and down in the pocket, up and down reading the field, and up and down on accuracy — and that facing disguised NFL coverages with young receivers who may not share his read is the [[Sam Darnold]] formula for seeing ghosts, which could force a face-saving benching. Bob Harris (not tracked) countered that the drumbeat has grown and Maye may start the whole year, noting New England's week 14 bye is late for a convenient switch and that Brissett was QB15 over 11 games in Cleveland under now-coordinator Alex Van Pelt.
- 2024-08-27 — According to guest Derek Klassen (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): Klassen admits this may be copium because he loved Maye as a prospect, but attributes the poor intermediate-area numbers in his charting to two things — Maye learning a new offense, and a UNC receiving corps made up of fast, quick players not built to catch tough passes in tight windows over the middle. That is precisely how Maye wants to play: he wants a receiver who will run a dig and take a shot to the ribs. Klassen said on draft night that drafting [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] showed New England understood the quarterback they took, and expects a shoring up of Maye's accuracy numbers now that he has bodies who can hold up that end. Host James Koh (not tracked) notes Maye has outplayed [[Jacoby Brissett]] this preseason without being named starter and expects him playing by midseason, while questioning whether New England's flanker/slot mid-game types truly match the charting.
