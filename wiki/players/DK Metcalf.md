# DK Metcalf

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman calls DK Metcalf, who per Next Gen Stats was the fastest player in the NFL last season, currently underpriced at roughly WR22 on Underdog ADP (a third-round pick), and expects a pass-happier offense under new OC Ryan Grubb (drawing on Grubb's University of Washington system) to boost his opportunity. Waldman says he likes all three Seattle receivers (Metcalf, Lockett, Smith-Njigba) and views the whole group as underpriced relative to the passing volume he expects this season.
- 2024-06-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-20]]): new OC Ryan Grubb's Washington Huskies offense was an extreme, downfield-heavy vertical passing attack, and Harmon views that as close to a perfect scheme fit for Metcalf, who 'hasn't had one of his most productive seasons in a minute.' Caveats that the fit depends heavily on pass protection holding up, and that quarterback play (whether Geno Smith can push the ball downfield effectively) matters as much as scheme.
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: Waldman says Metcalf's WR20 ADP is correctly priced and expects him to outperform it, projecting him closer to WR1 territory than WR3 territory. The driver is new OC Ryan Grubb running a far more pass-heavy offense than Seattle has used previously; Waldman calls 'production' -- not talent or scheme fit -- the swing factor, i.e. the plus/minus on where Metcalf actually lands.
- 2024-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-06]]): Metcalf may have leveled off. Harmon does not consider him a top-15 receiver league-wide anymore, naming [[Brandon Aiyuk]], [[Garrett Wilson]], [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[Nico Collins]] and [[Jaylen Waddle]] as better, and saying [[Drake London]] and [[DeVonta Smith]] have shown him more in isolation — while contrasting him with [[DJ Moore]], who Harmon says has jumped from tier three to tier two. Metcalf's best man and press scores came as a rookie in 2020 and he has never been an above-average zone beater, which Harmon partly attributes to near-exclusive X alignment: citing the [[Rashad Bateman]] profile he released the same day, he argues pure X receivers almost never clear 80% vs zone unless they are elite route runners, as [[Davante Adams]] is. Harmon still calls him a clear number one you can run an offense through, just maybe not a true superstar. James Koh (not tracked) notes that verdict would have been offensive two years ago.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]): he is getting excited about Metcalf and calls him a change of mind. The driver is Ryan Grubb's offense — the talk is about moving Metcalf around the formation to manufacture ways to get him open and feed him high-volume looks, rather than leaving him as a fixed outside piece. Waldman's stated concern is Metcalf's body type and injury risk, but he notes Metcalf has been healthy throughout his career, so he is willing to take the chance on a career year at what he considers a reasonable current price.
- 2024-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): Metcalf has probably peaked — still a really good, true number one NFL receiver in the 10–15 range of the league, but Harmon doubts he ever jumps to a tier-one level because he is not a full-field separator and not the route runner [[Rome Odunze]] is. That is fine as a career outcome, he says; the issue is that other receivers have jumped past him while Metcalf stayed the same, which is why he prefers [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] to rise. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) disagrees on the near term, arguing on gut instinct that Ryan Grubb will treat Metcalf as his Odunze-equivalent and feed him deep shots, and notes Metcalf's development stalled over the last couple of years after a fast early trajectory under Pete Carroll.
