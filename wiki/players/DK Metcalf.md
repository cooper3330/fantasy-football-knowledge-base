- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: Waldman says Metcalf's WR20 ADP is correctly priced and expects him to outperform it, projecting him closer to WR1 territory than WR3 territory. - 2024-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-06]]): Metcalf may have leveled off. Harmon does not consider him a top-15 receiver league-wide anymore, naming [[Brandon Aiyuk]], [[Garrett Wilson]], [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[Nico Collins]] and [[Jaylen Waddle]] as better, and saying [[Drake London]] and [[DeVonta Smith]] have shown him more in isolation — while contrasting him with [[DJ Moore]], who Harmon says has jumped from tier three to tier two. Metcalf's best man and press scores came as a rookie in 2020 and he has never been an above-average zone beater, which Harmon partly attributes to near-exclusive X alignment: citing the [[Rashad Bateman]] profile he released the same day, he argues pure X receivers almost never clear 80% vs zone unless they are elite route runners, as [[Davante Adams]] is. Harmon still calls him a clear number one you can run an offense through, just maybe not a true superstar. James Koh (not tracked) notes that verdict would have been offensive two years ago.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]): he is getting excited about Metcalf and calls him a change of mind. The driver is Ryan Grubb's offense — the talk is about moving Metcalf around the formation to manufacture ways to get him open and feed him high-volume looks, rather than leaving him as a fixed outside piece. Waldman's stated concern is Metcalf's body type and injury risk, but he notes Metcalf has been healthy throughout his career, so he is willing to take the chance on a career year at what he considers a reasonable current price.
- 2024-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): Metcalf has probably peaked — still a really good, true number one NFL receiver in the 10–15 range of the league, but Harmon doubts he ever jumps to a tier-one level because he is not a full-field separator and not the route runner [[Rome Odunze]] is. That is fine as a career outcome, he says; the issue is that other receivers have jumped past him while Metcalf stayed the same, which is why he prefers [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] to rise. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) disagrees on the near term, arguing on gut instinct that Ryan Grubb will treat Metcalf as his Odunze-equivalent and feed him deep shots, and notes Metcalf's development stalled over the last couple of years after a fast early trajectory under Pete Carroll.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): the notable change in Seattle is that Ryan Grubb has Metcalf working in-breaking routes over the middle of the field in a way Harmon says we have never really seen from him. Historically Metcalf has been an iso route player — boundary X, running goes and curls with the occasional over. Harmon likes how it works in concert with [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]]: Metcalf over the middle drags safeties and opens space on the other side of the field, and Geno Smith is now sixth in the league in attempts over the middle with a much lower deep-throw rate.
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]): Metcalf is 'the most frustrating player I've watched this year,' and Waldman is fully feeling the idea that he is not fulfilling his potential. Bob Harris (not tracked) framed it with usage — roughly a 21% target share and 31 catches, versus [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] at 19% and 34 catches and [[Tyler Lockett]] at 14% and 26 catches. Waldman's issue traces back to Ole Miss: a physical freak with size, speed and length who 'plays like he has T-rex arms' and will not attack the football, including three plays against San Francisco (two on the same drive, at least one costing a score). Waldman calls him a finesse receiver in a big man's body whose first reaction on plays is technically unsound; if he played to his height the way short receivers with 'short man syndrome' do, he would be a wrecking machine. Very good receiver who should be great.

- 2024-12-12 — According to guest Patrick Peterson (former NFL cornerback, not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-12]]: a rookie-year Metcalf was the moment Peterson knew he was aging — a night game in Seattle in 2019, third and two, Metcalf as the single receiver off a plus-two split setting up a fade before coming back across his face so fast that Peterson was relieved Russell Wilson looked elsewhere. His conclusion was that you cannot categorize receivers by frame: Metcalf has legitimate releases and lateral quickness that most big receivers do not, clearly honed deliberately because getting off the line is the big receiver's problem. [[Matt Harmon]] used it to indict combine-driven evaluation, recalling the pre-draft consensus that Metcalf's three-cone time made him a stiff who could not move off the line of scrimmage — Metcalf has always brought that quickness against press.

- 2025-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-12]]): after charting Metcalf's full 2024 season following the trade to Pittsburgh for a second-round pick and a five-year, $150M deal (~$50M APY through his age-33 season), Harmon calls it probably the worst season of Metcalf's career and says he has plateaued rather than ascended. The alarming marks: a career-low 71.3% success rate vs zone (10th percentile — Harmon's benchmark for a good receiver is 79-80%), a career-low 40% contested catch rate (he was over 80% his first two years and has not cracked 70% since), and a 69.2% success rate vs man (54th percentile) that dips below the 70% bar for the first time in his career. Zone comps at that level: 2020 Jalen Reagor, 2019 DeVante Parker, two [[Allen Lazard]] seasons. Harmon notes the numbers were the same before and after his injury, so injury does not explain the drop. He partly blames Ryan Grubb's scheme — no protection plan, a siloed run/pass game, and a go-heavy route tree with almost 13% curls, where Metcalf collapsed to a 47.2% success rate after multiple seasons over 87%. Koh agrees Metcalf has never been a stop-down player. Strengths remain: 62.7% on nine routes, 83.6% on slants, 78.4% on digs, ~79% on posts, over 75% vs press. Harmon likes the Arthur Smith fit for exactly that reason — [[A.J. Brown]] ran almost 45% slants/digs/posts in his 2019-20 samples and [[Drake London]] 49.5% in 2022 — and sees Metcalf as a very good, not elite, WR he ranks around WR28-30 rather than top five.
