# DeAndre Hopkins

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-14]]) [Dynasty]: uses Hopkins as *the* current exemplar of "old man game" — the player most likely to stay productive deep into his 30s on skill rather than athleticism. Credits advanced positioning at the catch point, peerless technique, route-running against zone, quarterback rapport, and — approvingly — knowing how to draw contact without drawing a flag ("the wrestling rule breaker... he knew how to cut all the corners"). Context given: 898 yards and 6 TDs through 13 games on a weak Titans team with no other receiver drawing coverage away.
- 2023-12-14 — **Adam Harstad** (guest — not a tracked expert), same episode: more pessimistic. Grants that old-man game retains value but argues it now compensates for less athletic decline than it used to, because opposing cornerbacks are markedly faster. Expects Hopkins to get "maybe two, three more years" rather than a Larry Fitzgerald / Andre Johnson-length tail.

- 2024-03-01 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-01]]: used, alongside a retired Andre Johnson, as the worked example of his fantasy-value "age cliff" model. By that model Hopkins "would still show up as... still viable" — still producing, unlike Andre Johnson's final (still-active-at-the-time) season, which the model reads as already over the cliff. Expects Hopkins to keep fluctuating up and down until something (an injury or a step lost) actually pushes him out of viable-producer status, consistent with his 2023-12-14 "maybe two, three more years" estimate on this page.

- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): notes he led all Titans receivers in 2023 by a wide margin
  (1,057 yards; the WR2, Chris Moore, not a tracked page, had just 424), but
  describes his career as "certainly on the back nine" at this point —
  part of the case for why Tennessee needed to add [[Calvin Ridley]] rather
  than run the offense through Hopkins and not much else.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: unlike [[Diontae Johnson]] or [[Calvin Ridley]], agrees Hopkins "is for sure" already a leader of a receiving corps.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on *Reception Perception: The Show* ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-11]]): cited (as his prime self) as the aggressive player comp for 2024 WR prospect [[Adonai Mitchell]] — both share a "lull you to sleep" tempo-change route-running style that produces a late, sharp separation break against man coverage, plus elite ball-tracking/body-control downfield.
- 2024-06-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-20]]): pushes back on the narrative that Hopkins was 'washed' last year — his RP profile shows he was still good in Arizona and remained really good after being traded to Tennessee, primarily deployed as a pure outside receiver with occasional slot movement. Harmon frames him as the true X in Tennessee's 2024 receiver room, which is what frees Calvin Ridley to move around the formation rather than being pigeonholed outside.
- 2024-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-11]]) [Dynasty]: says he was 'more impressed with Hopkins last year than I think most people were' -- his 2023 success rates with Arizona (72.2% vs. man, 78.5% vs. zone, 72.9% vs. press) are down from his 2019-2020 peak (75.8/83.2/76.4) but still 'fringe number one, high-end number two' level, a plateau Harmon compares to how Keenan Allen has aged. Paired with [[Calvin Ridley]] atop Tennessee's WR room, Harmon called it 'a nice setup for a young quarterback' in [[Will Levis]] even accounting for both receivers' age and the overpay for Ridley.
- 2024-07-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he's buying Hopkins in the WR40 range as a value play in a Tennessee offense he likes this year (Brian Callahan, Calvin Ridley, Will Levis). Two data points support it: Hopkins posted a strong 2.09 yards per route run last year despite bad situational factors (run-heavy scheme, poor QB play), and Waldman cited a trend that older WRs (year 10+) drafted in the middle rounds are actually good value due to survivorship bias -- if they're still going as a WR3/4 that late in their career, they're probably still good. On film, Waldman says Hopkins' game is built on technique, leverage, hand usage and positioning rather than speed -- citing his own past film study of Hopkins beating a prime Jalen Ramsey -- which is why his game should age well ('old man game'), comparing him to Anquan Boldin rather than speed-dependent receivers who fall off a cliff like Dez Bryant or Kenny Golladay-type players.
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]): Hopkins is out 4-6 weeks with a knee injury entering his age-32 season, and Harmon is bummed on two counts. First, he thinks Hopkins has been better the last two years than credited — his 2022 Reception Perception results were down from his peak but still solid, and partial charting of 2023 Tennessee games showed him still playing at a high level. His game should age well because he wins on the line of scrimmage, beats press man, and separates through nuance, technique and savvy rather than athleticism. But an aging receiver picking up injuries is 'usually just the first sign that things are going the wrong direction.' Second, Hopkins is the player who unlocks the room by holding down X so [[Calvin Ridley]] can work off the line. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) called Hopkins the linchpin of the whole Tennessee passing attack.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Hopkins is back practicing after a knee injury with Week 1 availability still up in the air, and Harmon calls getting him back important because Tennessee projects to run a fair amount of 12 personnel — neither [[Jared Wiley]] nor [[Chigoziem Okonkwo]] profiles as a big-time target getter, which pushes targets out to the perimeter receivers. He expects Brian Callahan's offense to throw far more than the old regime and to give [[Will Levis]] early-career Josh Allen-style checkdowns rather than letting him go wild horse every snap.
