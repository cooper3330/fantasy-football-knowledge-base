# DeAndre Hopkins

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-14]]) [Dynasty]: uses Hopkins as *the* current exemplar of "old man game" — the player most likely to stay productive deep into his 30s on skill rather than athleticism. Credits advanced positioning at the catch point, peerless technique, route-running against zone, quarterback rapport, and — approvingly — knowing how to draw contact without drawing a flag ("the wrestling rule breaker... he knew how to cut all the corners"). Context given: 898 yards and 6 TDs through 13 games on a weak Titans team with no other receiver drawing coverage away.
- 2023-12-14 — **Adam Harstad** (guest — not a tracked expert), same episode: more pessimistic. Grants that old-man game retains value but argues it now compensates for less athletic decline than it used to, because opposing cornerbacks are markedly faster. Expects Hopkins to get "maybe two, three more years" rather than a Larry Fitzgerald / Andre Johnson-length tail.

- 2024-03-01 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-01]]: used, alongside a retired Andre Johnson, as the worked example of his fantasy-value "age cliff" model. By that model Hopkins "would still show up as... still viable" — still producing, unlike Andre Johnson's final (still-active-at-the-time) season, which the model reads as already over the cliff. Expects Hopkins to keep fluctuating up and down until something (an injury or a step lost) actually pushes him out of viable-producer status, consistent with his 2023-12-14 "maybe two, three more years" estimate on this page.

- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): notes he led all Titans receivers in 2023 by a wide margin
  (1,057 yards; the WR2, Chris Moore, not a tracked page, had just 424), but
  describes his career as "certainly on the back nine" at this point —
  part of the case for why Tennessee needed to add [[Calvin Ridley]] rather
  than run the offense through Hopkins and not much else.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: unlike [[Diontae Johnson]] or [[Calvin Ridley]], agrees Hopkins "is for sure" already a leader of a receiving corps.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on *Reception Perception: The Show* ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-11]]): cited (as his prime self) as the aggressive player comp for 2024 WR prospect [[Adonai Mitchell]] — both share a "lull you to sleep" tempo-change route-running style that produces a late, sharp separation break against man coverage, plus elite ball-tracking/body-control downfield.
- 2024-06-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-20]]): pushes back on the narrative that Hopkins was 'washed' last year — his RP profile shows he was still good in Arizona and remained really good after being traded to Tennessee, primarily deployed as a pure outside receiver with occasional slot movement. Harmon frames him as the true X in Tennessee's 2024 receiver room, which is what frees Calvin Ridley to move around the formation rather than being pigeonholed outside.
- 2024-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-11]]) [Dynasty]: says he was 'more impressed with Hopkins last year than I think most people were' -- his 2023 success rates with Arizona (72.2% vs. man, 78.5% vs. zone, 72.9% vs. press) are down from his 2019-2020 peak (75.8/83.2/76.4) but still 'fringe number one, high-end number two' level, a plateau Harmon compares to how Keenan Allen has aged. Paired with [[Calvin Ridley]] atop Tennessee's WR room, Harmon called it 'a nice setup for a young quarterback' in [[Will Levis]] even accounting for both receivers' age and the overpay for Ridley.
- 2024-07-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he's buying Hopkins in the WR40 range as a value play in a Tennessee offense he likes this year (Brian Callahan, Calvin Ridley, Will Levis). Two data points support it: Hopkins posted a strong 2.09 yards per route run last year despite bad situational factors (run-heavy scheme, poor QB play), and Waldman cited a trend that older WRs (year 10+) drafted in the middle rounds are actually good value due to survivorship bias -- if they're still going as a WR3/4 that late in their career, they're probably still good. On film, Waldman says Hopkins' game is built on technique, leverage, hand usage and positioning rather than speed -- citing his own past film study of Hopkins beating a prime Jalen Ramsey -- which is why his game should age well ('old man game'), comparing him to Anquan Boldin rather than speed-dependent receivers who fall off a cliff like Dez Bryant or Kenny Golladay-type players.
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]): Hopkins is out 4-6 weeks with a knee injury entering his age-32 season, and Harmon is bummed on two counts. First, he thinks Hopkins has been better the last two years than credited — his 2022 Reception Perception results were down from his peak but still solid, and partial charting of 2023 Tennessee games showed him still playing at a high level. His game should age well because he wins on the line of scrimmage, beats press man, and separates through nuance, technique and savvy rather than athleticism. But an aging receiver picking up injuries is 'usually just the first sign that things are going the wrong direction.' Second, Hopkins is the player who unlocks the room by holding down X so [[Calvin Ridley]] can work off the line. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) called Hopkins the linchpin of the whole Tennessee passing attack.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Hopkins is back practicing after a knee injury with Week 1 availability still up in the air, and Harmon calls getting him back important because Tennessee projects to run a fair amount of 12 personnel — neither [[Jared Wiley]] nor [[Chigoziem Okonkwo]] profiles as a big-time target getter, which pushes targets out to the perimeter receivers. He expects Brian Callahan's offense to throw far more than the old regime and to give [[Will Levis]] early-career Josh Allen-style checkdowns rather than letting him go wild horse every snap.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Tennessee's passing game most needs a second pass catcher to emerge, and the obvious candidate is a healthy Hopkins — but Harmon says he is playing through some form of torn MCL and has no idea when that gets right, with only 25 routes run on the season to [[Treylon Burks]]'s 37. When healthy, Harmon sees Hopkins as the vertical X receiver this offense lacks: not a separator even before the injury, but a high-pointer whose strength is winning contested balls. That would in turn let [[Calvin Ridley]] be maximized off the line of scrimmage on intermediate routes, giving the Titans what Harmon calls meat and potatoes instead of only touchdowns and checkdowns.
- 2024-10-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-24]]): the trade sending Hopkins to Kansas City was an excellent deal and probably a steal. Waldman concedes Hopkins is likely not what he used to be, but says he still has more than enough left, and that Kansas City with [[Patrick Mahomes]] was probably the only place he could have landed that would be a great fit. Mentioned in passing while contrasting Kansas City's quarterback situation against the Raiders' offense around [[Brock Bowers]].
- 2024-10-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-24]]): after the trade to Kansas City, Harmon charted three Titans games (Bills, Packers, Colts) and found Hopkins still a genuine X receiver — 81.9% of snaps on the line of scrimmage, only just over 14% in the slot or backfield. The good: an 80% success rate on slants, strong curls and out routes with good timing, just under 80% success rate versus zone, and slightly above-average release against press on shorter routes. The bad: 59.3% success rate versus man, a far cry from even his final Arizona year, and nothing downfield — 22% of his charted routes were nine routes at a sub-50% success rate. Verdict: 'not a star player anymore' at 32, but a useful condensed-X and contested-catch presence ([[Sacrificial X Receiver]] discussion) who gives [[Patrick Mahomes]] a third-and-six answer the Chiefs lacked. Harmon noted Hopkins gives 'nothing' after the catch, so the [[Rashee Rice]] slot role is not a fit. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) was more bullish, calling it a perfect fit and predicting he 'unlocks this offense'.
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): Hopkins will be fantasy relevant in Kansas City before Thanksgiving. The bet is on workload rather than a specific route role — Waldman sees room in the passing attack for the eight-to-twelve-target job [[Rashee Rice]] held, with maybe seven catches wherever they come from. He acknowledged Andy Reid's offense is usually an obstacle to newcomers unless they are veterans, citing [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]]'s productive first year, and referenced Drew Brees' point that veteran quarterback plus veteran receiver rapport comes together quickly. Extra edge he named: Hopkins pushes off so well that he is rarely intercepted, handy alongside a [[Patrick Mahomes]] who entered the week tied for the most interceptions in the NFL. Both hosts agreed he will matter more in Kansas City than he ever would have in Tennessee.
- 2024-10-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-29]]): Hopkins's Kansas City debut was small — 14 routes, three targets, two catches, 29 yards — but he moved the chains on 14.3% of his routes, and Harmon expects that chain-moving role. He directly contradicts Andy Reid, who suggested Hopkins would play where [[Rashee Rice]] played: per PFF he lined up outside on 90.9% of snaps, and his route chart was vertical and intermediate in-breaking rather than the underneath horizontal-space stuff Rice ran. Harmon says Hopkins does not have the YAC juice for that job and Kansas City lacks a true slot anyway. Charting-wise Hopkins is now clearly better against zone than man, so Harmon sees occasional power-slot work over the middle versus zone but primarily outside X ball-winning. Koh (not tracked) called him arguably one of the best X receivers ever and the first true outside X the Chiefs have had in years. Harmon cites Bill Barnwell's framing: this is a December/January/February trade, not an October one, so expect a slow ramp while chemistry builds.
- 2024-11-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-25]]): asked whether Hopkins will emerge in the fantasy playoffs, Waldman went against his heart — Hopkins is probably his favorite receiver in the NFL and looked great — and said fuck it. His reasoning is role, not ability: Hopkins is a player who does not need the ball to affect the game, and Kansas City will not feed him at the level managers hope. Waldman does not see this growing into what [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] is doing week after week; the way the Chiefs spread the ball and run it means Hopkins is part of the machine rather than the standout cog. Worth rostering and startable in good matchups, but expect big games that are unpredictable and therefore hard to actually have in a lineup. Bob Harris (not tracked) agreed.
- 2024-12-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-24]]): Hopkins is 'very much a declined player' but still useful in specific ways, and Harmon rates him a more consistently impactful midseason addition than [[Amari Cooper]]. The usage is matchup-driven: he had mostly played pure X, which is why he draws the target when Kansas City sees the rare single-high look — Harmon cites over two yards per route run versus single high — and he ran in-breakers against the zone-heavy Bucs and won contested shots against man-heavy Las Vegas for 90 yards. Last week his slot rate jumped to 34% of snaps from 11.8% in the prior two weeks as [[Marquise Brown]]'s return let the Chiefs move everyone around. Harmon's explicit conclusion: there will never be a week where you can count on eight targets in this offense, so treat him as a week-by-week matchup play. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) notes the flat statistical line — 71% catch rate but 4.3 receptions and 47.8 yards per game over nine games.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-23]]): the Kansas City trade was framed at the time as 'not a September trade, not an October trade — a trade for January', and in January Hopkins produced zero. He played just 15 snaps in the divisional round with one target and no receptions, and Harmon concedes he expected a bigger role by now. His explanation is that Kansas City is up to its usual tricks at receiver — constant rotation with no clear-cut set of guys — leaving Hopkins and [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]] as rotational third options. Harmon groups him with [[Marquise Brown]] and [[Amari Cooper]] as three veteran deadline receivers who combined for four targets and zero receptions in the divisional round. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) called the collective outcome unbelievable given how impactful both trades felt at the time.
- 2025-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-05]]): Hopkins has been marginalized in Kansas City's postseason offense and Harmon finds it strange enough to wonder aloud whether there is an undisclosed injury — fifth in route participation at 36.4%, barely ahead of [[Noah Gray]] at 33.3%, with exactly one catch in the playoffs and only 3.8% of his postseason snaps in the slot, making him a pure outside player for them. Co-host James Koh argues for more Hopkins in this game specifically, because Philadelphia plays closer to league-average man/zone than the very zone-heavy Buffalo defense, and because [[Xavier Worthy]], [[Marquise Brown]] and [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]] are the zone beaters. Harmon accepts the matchup logic but notes any bet has to be one catch, since that is all Hopkins has managed. He calls Hopkins one of the best receivers of the era and would enjoy him finishing with a ring.
- 2025-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-19]]): discussing Detroit's veteran options, James Koh (not tracked) calls Hopkins the best culture fit available for the Lions but says the 2024 film was not good and that if there is a proverbial cliff, Hopkins may have fallen off it — while allowing two mitigations, a midseason trade into a complicated Kansas City playbook he may never have fully learned, and a Chiefs play-calling style that spreads the ball around rather than concentrating targets. Koh's bottom line is that Detroit needs reliable team speed and Hopkins does not check that box. Harmon is more dismissive of the whole veteran-perimeter route for Detroit, doubting they are a Hopkins or [[Amari Cooper]] organization on culture grounds and predicting they draft a big-bodied X instead.
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]) on Hopkins signing a one-year, $6M deal with Baltimore: he charted the Chiefs sample that morning and 'he looked better than I expected', though from very low expectations — 65.3% success rate vs man and 66.7% vs press, both below the 50th percentile and far off his peak, with zone at just under 74%. He gets open on a narrow route menu: nothing on nines, posts or digs, but curls, slants and especially out routes (13.2% of the sample, 77.8% success) where he sells inside and works back out using leverage. He is slow now and a long-developing separator, which Harmon theorizes is why Kansas City barely played him (28.5% of postseason snaps) behind bad pass protection. In Baltimore he sees a useful third receiver behind [[Zay Flowers]] and [[Rashad Bateman]] with great hands in tight coverage — 'not going to be a fantasy football factor' but a plausible chain-mover on third down. Koh adds the Ravens' history of late-career receivers.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): he takes the other side of the Footballguys read that has [[Zay Flowers]], [[Rashad Bateman]], [[Mark Andrews]] and [[Isaiah Likely]] all ahead of Hopkins in the pecking order and calls it hard to expect much from a 33-year-old. Waldman projects 600-800 yards and 8-12 touchdowns and says Hopkins will be a worthwhile fantasy play, explicitly taking that bet. His reasoning is red-zone specific: Hopkins has always won there whether one-on-one in the short area or against zone, making plays in tight windows, and tight-window throws — back shoulders, throws on a line, low balls — are exactly what [[Lamar Jackson]] is great at, even in an era with fewer fades. He argues Flowers and Bateman will not get those types of targets, and only Likely and Andrews will compete for them. He also dismisses the 'why didn't he emerge in Kansas City' objection as unfair given that was a midseason addition, whereas Hopkins is here from training camp.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman takes the deliberately 'unreasonable' side, noting Hopkins caught 75 balls for 1,057 yards and five touchdowns with rookie Will Levis two years ago, and scores on about 5% of targets — his career average — which extrapolated over a full Chiefs season would have been roughly 800 yards and seven touchdowns despite no camp with [[Patrick Mahomes]]. He wonders whether Baltimore kicks [[Rashad Bateman]] outside and uses Hopkins as a big slot in the high red zone, where the rate could climb to 8-10%; he leans on [[Lamar Jackson]]'s track record of meaning what he says after Jackson called Hopkins unlike any receiver he has played with. Upside without injuries: seven-to-eight touchdowns; with an injury elsewhere, 900-1,100 yards and double-digit scores, an [[Adam Thielen]]-style late-career resurgence. He concedes recommending a touchdown outlier is normally bad practice. [[Brandon Angelo]] disagrees: he takes [[Rashad Bateman]] and [[Isaiah Likely]] of the three, seeing Hopkins as a keep-them-honest X and de facto player-coach chasing a ring, elevating others rather than producing.
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-13]]): Hopkins arrives in Baltimore as a situational piece, not a full-time player, and Harmon does not expect a big target share. His 2024 charting across Tennessee and Kansas City had him outside on 75.4% of sampled snaps and on the line of scrimmage on 77.7%, the highest since his final Houston season in 2019 — Harmon expected the Chiefs to slide him inside against zone once [[Rashee Rice]] was hurt, but they could not because they lacked any normal outside receiver. His man-coverage separation is clearly in decline and he no longer threatens defenses vertically, but he still separates quickly on slants and curls at high volume with good success rates, and retains ball skills, tracking and hands. Harmon's ideal use is winning key third downs and critical moments. He is conflicted about the fit with [[Rashad Bateman]] since both are X types, but thinks they can complement each other and free Bateman for slot work; Todd Monken said Hopkins makes him want more 11 personnel while also calling third tight end Charlie Kolar a starter.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): still for real — it was one play in Week 1, but that play said it all. He is not a WR1 or necessarily a WR2, but in three- or four-receiver leagues Waldman says pick him up if you have not, because he still expects Hopkins to lead Baltimore in touchdown receptions. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) agrees but flags the run-heavy offense making him hit-or-miss week to week.
