# DeAndre Hopkins

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-14]]) [Dynasty]: uses Hopkins as *the* current exemplar of "old man game" — the player most likely to stay productive deep into his 30s on skill rather than athleticism. Credits advanced positioning at the catch point, peerless technique, route-running against zone, quarterback rapport, and — approvingly — knowing how to draw contact without drawing a flag ("the wrestling rule breaker... he knew how to cut all the corners"). Context given: 898 yards and 6 TDs through 13 games on a weak Titans team with no other receiver drawing coverage away.
- 2023-12-14 — **Adam Harstad** (guest — not a tracked expert), same episode: more pessimistic. Grants that old-man game retains value but argues it now compensates for less athletic decline than it used to, because opposing cornerbacks are markedly faster. Expects Hopkins to get "maybe two, three more years" rather than a Larry Fitzgerald / Andre Johnson-length tail.

- 2024-03-01 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-01]]: used, alongside a retired Andre Johnson, as the worked example of his fantasy-value "age cliff" model. By that model Hopkins "would still show up as... still viable" — still producing, unlike Andre Johnson's final (still-active-at-the-time) season, which the model reads as already over the cliff. Expects Hopkins to keep fluctuating up and down until something (an injury or a step lost) actually pushes him out of viable-producer status, consistent with his 2023-12-14 "maybe two, three more years" estimate on this page.

- 2024-03-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-14]]): notes he led all Titans receivers in 2023 by a wide margin
  (1,057 yards; the WR2, Chris Moore, not a tracked page, had just 424), but
  describes his career as "certainly on the back nine" at this point —
  part of the case for why Tennessee needed to add [[Calvin Ridley]] rather
  than run the offense through Hopkins and not much else.
