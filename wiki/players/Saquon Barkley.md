# Saquon Barkley

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]] (recorded before the *Film and Theory* episode below): the physical version of the same objection. Barkley is granted as "great mover, super dynamic," but he and [[Derrick Henry]] "physically cannot get in and out of these positions as quickly" as [[James Cook]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] or [[Christian McCaffrey]] — the trait Angelo argues the league is moving toward.
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, the comp that frames his whole case: Barkley is "a more agile version of an Adrian Peterson type of runner" — "jump cuts or bust with him," but violent enough with it to make it work. Waldman's Peterson anecdote (a photo of cracked earth captioned as Peterson's jump-cut landing) is the origin of the "space-expensive" argument he restates in the *Film and Theory* episode below. He notes [[Nick Chubb]] as the one back of comparable size who has gotten away with that movement class efficiently.
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): a film-mechanics knock delivered while explicitly conceding the fantasy value. Barkley is one of the last backs still relying on the big jump cut — the Adrian Peterson move, a violent plant and a dramatic lateral cut — and Waldman's objection is that the move is space-expensive, because the back jumps two to three yards forward toward the line before cutting. His verdict: "while he's still a valuable fantasy commodity, an NFL commodity, he's not the most efficient runner on a play-to-play basis when it comes to certain changes of direction." The contrast class is the group that changes direction inside tight creases at speed — [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], [[James Cook]], [[Austin Ekeler]]. Full framework: [[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]].
- 2023-12-21 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode, tangentially: recounts trading Le'Veon Bell and Antonio Brown for the 1.01 in 2018 to take Barkley, and that Bell and Brown had "one more good season between them" afterward — told as a story about the dynasty trade market rather than as an evaluation of Barkley.

- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: predicts a free-agent landing spot with the Houston Texans, "feeling it" — pairing with [[C.J. Stroud]] on "a team on the come up" that could win a tough AFC South alongside Indianapolis, with Dallas also floated as a real possibility.

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]] [Redraft (Standard)]: reacting to his three-year, $46.75M Eagles deal ($26M guaranteed, beating the value of two franchise tags) — says the [[Christian McCaffrey]] and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] signings challenged his own prior that plugging any back into an elite ecosystem (49ers, Lions) is enough; instead, those teams sought premium talent to weaponize the ecosystem, and reads the Eagles' Barkley deal as their "dollar store" version of the same strategy after cycling through Miles Sanders and D'Andre Swift. Likes the fit given Philadelphia's line (even post-[[Jason Kelce]] retirement) and [[Jalen Hurts]]'s rushing lanes, but is skeptical of the pass-game upside some project opposite A.J. Brown/DeVonta Smith — "he's not, like, a difference-making pass catcher. He's not CMC" — drawing the same conclusion from [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]'s underwhelming receiving role in Detroit as a caution that most backs don't move the needle as receivers the way hoped. Still calls him "an absolute difference-maker" in a healthy offensive ecosystem when not battling injury.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: reacts to the actual signing with Philadelphia — not the Houston landing spot Waldman had predicted on 2024-03-11 — "feeling" it strongly regardless. Expects Barkley to get the vast majority of Philadelphia's backfield work given the money ($37-46M, 3 years, effectively closer to a 2-year deal in practice) and the volume the Eagles already funneled to running backs (roughly 90% of targets under Jalen Hurts). Calls [[DeAndre Swift]] "a good three tiers below" Barkley in ability, so an Eagles line that got real production out of Swift should elevate Barkley further. Floats a "vengeance tour" narrative — a great, if not always mature decision-maker on the field, playing an NFC East rival with something to prove — as the kind of story worth buying into this year specifically.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: revisits the Giants' choice to pay [[Daniel Jones]] rather than Barkley — "they should have paid Saquon Barkley," arguing a cheaper second- or third-round quarterback could have filled Jones's seat, and that New York "may regret" letting Barkley walk "a couple times a year."
- 2024-05-14 — According to Chris Long ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-14]]), former Eagles defensive end and Green Light Podcast host, not a tracked expert [Dynasty]: signing [[Saquon Barkley]] was 'outside the norm' for GM Howie Roseman, who typically avoids heavy investment at running back, but Long is excited about the move. He frames it as adding needed backfield 'firepower' to help balance the run game after Philadelphia lost its longtime starting center's blocking, and — more importantly — as protection for [[Jalen Hurts]]: 'you can't have him pounding the rock his whole career if you want to invest in this guy long term.'
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: projects roughly 313 touches (about 30-33% of the Eagles backfield), close to 1,700 yards and 16 touchdowns, saying he'll be on the field 'all day, every day' with only Kenneth Gainwell as red-zone competition and nobody else (Terion Davis-Price, Kendall Milton) established behind him. He argued the Eagles' offensive line plus Jalen Hurts drawing box defenders (the way DeAndre Swift benefited from in Philly) sets Barkley up for a monster year. Bob Harris said he's been 'acquiring more shares' and expects Barkley and Hurts to split short-yardage scoring rather than Hurts vulturing everything, calling round-one-turn/early-round-two ADP a great price as long as Barkley doesn't try to force extra yardage at the goal line to compete with Hurts for touchdowns.
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Best Ball]: Both expect a monster year for Barkley, arguing Philadelphia has arguably the best overall offensive personnel in the league (contrasted with a Giants roster near the other end of that spectrum) despite Barkley's individual production with New York never being in question. Waldman says he's not overly worried about Jalen Hurts vulturing goal-line touchdowns, believing Barkley will convert on roughly three times the scoring opportunities DeAndre Swift saw in Philadelphia. Both frame the Eagles paying a running back market rate -- unusual league-wide right now -- and Howie Roseman's stated intent to get Barkley 300-plus touches as a clear signal of organizational commitment that should be weighted alongside the on-field talent.
- 2024-08-27 — According to guest Derek Klassen (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): Barkley is the reason Klassen expects a course correction in Kellen Moore's neglected running-back passing game. Host James Koh (not tracked) argues Moore's offenses shy away from checkdowns and designed back touches — Ezekiel Elliott's targets plummeted late in Dallas, and even amid the Chargers' receiver apocalypse [[Austin Ekeler]], one of the best pass-catching backs in years, got few designed screens or checkdowns. Klassen partly blames personnel: last year's Chargers backfield had no juice and no play caller was fixing that run game. He sees Barkley as far closer to what Moore had in [[Tony Pollard]] years ago, and 'a million times better', with the juice and receiving potential to be a useful part of the offense again behind a line that gets to the second level. Koh insists Philadelphia must get backs involved to take load off [[Jalen Hurts]].
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he 'fucking feels' the Barkley vengeance tour — a top-five back in the league this year. Barkley looked mature in his decision-making and productive in the receiving game, and the surrounding context does the rest: the Eagles offensive line, a strong defense, and a weapon alignment that makes Philadelphia hard to pick a poison against even in games where [[Jalen Hurts]] is inefficient, which means Barkley gets nice creases. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) added the supporting number: [[DeAndre Swift]] had roughly 720 of his 1,000-plus yards before contact behind that line last year, and giving Barkley that same edge should be ugly for defenses.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): Barkley's revenge tour will not be stalled by the Philadelphia receiver injuries — 'he's white hot right now,' a man possessed. Waldman described watching him accelerate past two cornerbacks and a safety on a run to the left and then take it across the width and length of the field, concluding there is 'nothing washed' about his athletic ability. He also credited Howie Roseman for zigging while the rest of the league zagged on paying running backs, betting that Barkley could touch the ball 300 times and win games.
- 2024-10-10 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert), co-host with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): Barkley is genuinely great and also genuinely overproducing, and both can be true at once. Harstad singles out the run where Barkley hit the second level and accelerated away as evidence against the age-curve truthers who insisted he should have lost two steps, and says the straight-line speed he still has is exactly the trait that drives high yards per carry. But he is averaging six yards per carry against a career average nowhere near that, and Harstad expects a moderate decline, guessing around 5.0 by season's end — a number [[Matt Waldman]] agrees is more in line with his history. Harstad is explicit this is not a downgrade to scrub status: he still expects a top-three or top-four fantasy back barring injury, and thinks Barkley has a good shot at the Hall of Fame.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): the viral reverse hurdle was not planned — Waldman thinks Barkley intended a spin move on the safety, realized he had committed too early, and improvised the most incredible elusive maneuver he can remember. He is more interested in what led up to it: Barkley extended his forearm away from his frame, which is what good backs do to diffuse the force of a blow, and here converted it into a stiff arm; then split very wide on the spin and took a small step up with his right foot to create the illusion of forward momentum, making the defender think he was closer than he was before spinning away.
- 2024-11-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-18]]): asked whether the Eagles' Barkley is the Penn State Barkley, Waldman said 'f--k it' — and meant it as a compliment. People see the highlight runs and conclude nothing changed, but at Penn State he could out-athlete defenders while ignoring the design; he has since matured into a much better decision-maker who understands how plays develop and sticks with the play, then out-athletes people once it does. 'He's f---ing fantastic.' Waldman tied it to his drafting philosophy of anticipated volume in every round, citing Howie Roseman's one-sentence justification for paying a back while the league devalued the position — he is a guy who will touch the ball 300-plus times and is a very important part of the offense — and noted Roseman may have underestimated: Barkley is on track for roughly 370 touches and might hit 400.
- 2024-11-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-25]]): after Barkley's 300-plus-yard, multi-touchdown revenge game against the Giants, Waldman pulled out his 2018 Rookie Scouting Portfolio, where he had [[Nick Chubb]] ranked RB1 and Barkley RB2 — controversial then and now — and argued the honest framing is that Barkley is essentially Chubb in a good offense. He gives Barkley the edge as a receiver and on downfield usage plus the reverse hurdle nobody else has, but says pre-injury Chubb was better at decision-making, power, making the line look better, and equally able to break long runs; Barkley also benefits from a good quarterback and a line Chubb never had. Bob Harris (not tracked) added the volume angle: Howie Roseman paid real money, 13-plus million a year, precisely because Barkley would take 300-400 touches, and touches are what drive fantasy scoring.
- 2024-12-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-09]]): after 124 yards in Week 14 Barkley is on pace for 2,122 rushing yards, which would beat Eric Dickerson's 1984 single-season record, and Bob Harris (Footballguys, not a tracked expert) noted the Eagles appear to be scripting the record for Week 18 against the Giants. Waldman says 'fuck it' on him actually breaking it — the Pittsburgh run defense is next and he expects that game to be where Barkley falls short, though he allows Barkley is the type of back who could still post a big number there. On the asterisk question Waldman would not formally asterisk a 17-game mark (Jim Brown's was a 14-game era, Dickerson's a 16), but expects historians to argue it forever; either way he says Barkley deserves his flowers for a phenomenal season.
- 2024-12-12 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-12]], relayed alongside [[Matt Harmon]]: Barkley is masking real problems in the Philadelphia offense and is the reason the run game looks so good — 'players over plays'. Koh used him as the counterexample to the running-back-doesn't-matter argument: even granting that a replacement back like Kenneth Gainwell might produce 70% of the yardage, the missing 30% is special plays that lift the offense emotionally, force safeties into the box, and thereby prop up [[A.J. Brown]]'s efficiency and the team's first-down rate — take Barkley off the field and close wins get very tight. Harmon agreed the offense is players over plays and noted Barkley also contributes as a passing-game player, while both flagged that in the postseason, when the talent gap narrows, plays start to matter more than players.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]): Barkley took a knee shot from Minkah Fitzpatrick that slowed his pace, and Waldman had set a checkpoint — a 100-to-130-yard day would have made him say the 2,105-yard record was a legitimate shot. Pittsburgh having slowed him makes it tough; Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) pegged him on pace for about 2,050 and floated the Eagles force-feeding him roughly 250 yards in the Week 18 Giants game. Waldman would enjoy that against the Giants but says it would be unwise for the playoffs, citing Adam Harstad's workload research (not a tracked expert, work with Jason Lisk at Football Guys): the danger is not season-long volume but a single game far over a back's usual weekly number, which raises injury risk in the following week or two.
- 2024-12-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-23]]): he now believes Barkley can break Eric Dickerson's all-time single-season rushing record of 2,105 yards, which requires averaging 134 yards per game the rest of the way. What changed his mind: Barkley getting 60 on the Pittsburgh defense, his game against Washington, and the fact that he is openly talking about it on TV — Waldman thinks he has bought into the 'vengeance tour' and wants to break it against the Giants. He amplified a point from Steve Smith (untracked): it is not just Barkley, the offensive line wants to be part of history as much as he does, if not more. In the same segment Waldman argued the [[Jonathan Taylor]] talent comparison is genuinely close.
- 2024-12-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-30]]): all in on Barkley chasing the single-season rushing record against his former team after the Steelers game, and he jokes Barkley should wear a [[Daniel Jones]] nameplate on the back. Asked whether the record deserves an asterisk for coming in a 17-game season, Waldman says no — Eric Dickerson faced the same argument against O.J. Simpson's fewer games, they are all great backs, and if anything the level of difficulty is higher now given the speed and athleticism of the competition over a longer season, so the asterisk goes away. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) relays Steve Smith's point on NFL Network that the chase is as much about the offensive linemen, who were the ones celebrating hardest at 2,000 yards, and expects Philadelphia to go for it at ownership level for morale reasons despite [[Nick Sirianni]] facing playoff risk; Waldman agrees the morale case wins.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-23]]): the thing Harmon cannot get past in the NFC Championship is that he does not see how Washington stops Barkley. He cites Washington allowing the second-most yards before contact per rush, the seventh-lowest stuff rate in the league, the most yards per carry allowed on gap scheme runs, and the fifth-most explosive rushing yards allowed on runs off the left and right end and through the C-gap — asking how Barkley does not break two long bangers that end the game. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) supplied the head-to-head record: 150 yards and two touchdowns in Week 16, 146 yards and two touchdowns in Week 11, averaging roughly 148 rushing yards and two scores per game against Washington. Harmon adds Philadelphia's edge is depth rather than top-end talent.
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): Barkley is 'the story of the season' as the player who weaponized a great rushing ecosystem — but Harmon is careful to separate the two, noting Philadelphia's run environment is elite in its own right, evidenced by [[Will Shipley]] ripping 57 yards off the C-gap behind a Mekhi Becton pull. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) points out Barkley did not look 100 percent and still ran for 118 yards and three rushing touchdowns in a 55-23 win, part of a seven-rushing-touchdown team playoff record. Harmon also credits his blocking on Hurts' designed keepers, which is part of why defenses cannot simply sit on the read option. See [[Rushing Ecosystem and Running Back Weaponization]].
- 2025-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-05]]): Barkley breaks the math no matter the matchup, but Harmon expects his explosives in Super Bowl LIX to come on gap-scheme runs rather than zone — Kansas City allowed a 40.4% rushing success rate on zone-concept runs, third worst in the league, but is much stiffer on gap looks at 4.22 yards per carry and a 48.4% success rate, and overall gave up just 3.79 yards per carry to running backs, second best in the NFL. He pictures pulls from Mekhi Becton or Jordan Mailata springing them. Barkley cleared 110 rushing yards in eight of his last ten games, and while co-host James Koh flagged the tough matchup, Harmon refused to bet an under on him on principle: you never want to be holding that ticket when he rips a 65-yarder in the third quarter.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): Barkley is the odds-on RB1 for 2025 and safe to call the top of the heap at this point in the offseason, but Waldman explicitly leaves the door open — a long offseason of line injuries, holdouts or trades could elevate a back of commensurate talent. His named hypotheticals: [[Breece Hall]] landing somewhere strong (e.g. San Francisco if they moved on from [[Christian McCaffrey]]) would sit alongside Barkley, and [[Nick Chubb]] on a team with an excellent line would have an outside chance too; [[Derrick Henry]] should keep his role. Caveat from the Super Bowl: Waldman notes the Chiefs' plan did hold Barkley to 57 rushing yards and made him hesitant, and expects more teams to have a blueprint for limiting the Eagles' ground game — Kansas City's execution under Steve Spagnuolo just was not enough with [[Jalen Hurts]] running.
- 2025-02-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-11]]): Barkley was bottled up on the ground in Super Bowl LIX — 57 yards on 25 carries, 2.3 yards per carry — against what Harmon calls arguably the best rush defense in the NFL. The more useful signal is in the passing game: Barkley led the team in first downs per route run, and Harmon says there was 'meat left on the bone' with him as a pass catcher in this offense even after an Offensive Player of the Year season. Having him as a pre-snap outlet answer was, in Harmon's read, a key reason [[Jalen Hurts]] handled Kansas City's blitz-plus-zone looks so much better than he had during the regular season. Harmon also cites the Baltimore model — an elite back behind a rushing quarterback — as a template that wins games.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): he still thinks Barkley has the higher upside of the two, but prefers [[Bijan Robinson]] at third overall to Barkley at second. The reason is workload: he doubts the Eagles will need to lean on Barkley as heavily as they did, and reads the [[A.J. Dillon]] addition as a deliberate signal that Philadelphia wants a bigger back to take volume off him — still a high workload, just not overkill after a 345-plus carry season that blew past the old curse-of-370 threshold. Waldman is explicit that he loves Barkley and this is a marginal preference at the top of the draft, not a bearish call.
- 2025-06-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-30]]): he agrees with lowering expectations on volume — Philadelphia added depth-chart pieces who can contribute, changed offensive coordinators (not by choice), and he suspects the receiving corps privately pushed for more targets after the Super Bowl. Even so, Waldman still projects about 1,800 total yards and 15 touchdowns, which is a top-five running back season in many years, and he will not argue with anyone drafting Barkley as their RB1. He rejects the 370-touch curse framing directly, saying he does not believe in curses (naming Michael Fabiano as a proponent) though he does accept that heavy usage trends downward over time; his real objection is paying the historic premium that follows a historic season. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) would happily take Barkley in the second half of round one if others are scared off, and notes Philadelphia's offensive line remains very good.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): trending downward. The reasoning is roster intent rather than decline — after the season Philadelphia had, the priority is repeating, and that means having Barkley healthy for the playoffs. He read the [[A.J. Dillon]] signing and [[Will Shipley]]'s presence as a deliberate redundancy plan, and expects the Eagles to throw more often so [[A.J. Brown]] is not idle on the sideline. The implication is a managed regular-season workload [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): Barkley is Harris's number one overall player and he rates him a 2 out of 10 on risk — 'the bad dude in the planet', with no [[Tyler Allgeier]] equivalent on his roster. Guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) also has him as his RB1 but rates him a 4, trying hard not to buy the 400-touch voodoo while acknowledging it, and flagging the [[Jalen Hurts]] tush push as a touchdown vulture. Both dismiss the 400-touch cliff argument as a small-sample numerical dive — Harris asks whether the 400th touch is magic — and Bell refuses to rank him RB7 over it. Harris notes he considered the QB rushing-TD issue and concluded Saquon was fine anyway last year.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-04]]): previewing Cowboys at Eagles, Harris notes he rated Barkley as his number one overall fantasy player in the face of doubters after a dream, picture-of-health 2024 season — and pointedly adds that this doesn't mean Barkley can't get injured immediately, in which case the doubters will have their ammunition. He sees nothing about the Cowboys' defense that takes you off Barkley, [[A.J. Brown]], [[DeVonta Smith]] or [[Jalen Hurts]]: Dallas is without Micah Parsons, wasn't good on defense last year even with him, Trayvon Diggs is expected to play but on a pitch count, and DaRon Bland moves back to slot corner because he struggled outside. Guard Landon Dickerson being questionable is the one hole he can poke in Philadelphia's offense, which he otherwise calls one of the few situations beyond reproach that boosts its players sight unseen.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Barkley was 'the most electric player on the field for either team' in the opener and if you took him high in the first round 'you have no complaints.' Harris pointedly rebuts the PPR concern he'd heard all summer — Barkley caught four passes (with one drop), including a one-handed catch after which he made two defenders miss — and his touchdown was 'shot out of a cannon' for a 21-20 halftime lead. He notes Dallas sold out to stop him after the delay and stuffed him repeatedly on up-the-gut runs, aided by guard Landon Dickerson leaving hurt in the second half, and criticizes Philadelphia for refusing to answer with play action the way the Lions would. He also needles the crowd that ranked Barkley outside RB1 on injury risk.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 22 touches for 54 yards against a Rams defense that clearly decided Barkley would not beat them. Harris conceded that so far the argument he should not have been the 1.01 is 'probably correct,' but explicitly rejected the curse-of-370 and worn-out explanations — the mechanism he saw was no push up front, worsened by Philadelphia losing right tackle Lane Johnson, plus the Eagles being too stubborn with the run in the first half (35 total first-half yards for the offense). Barkley also dropped a third-quarter dump-off that could have sprung him. His conclusion: 'I would be trading for Barkley at a discount right now, of course I would.'
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-02]]): Barkley is 'a freaking MVP' and a phenomenal back who is being made to look average — he has nowhere to run because the Eagles' run game under Kevin Patullo is 'super lacking creativity' compared with Kellen Moore's 2024 design. Angelo's broader point was that Philadelphia has a bottom-tier offensive designer paired with top-tier talent ([[A.J. Brown]], [[DeVonta Smith]], [[Jalen Hurts]], Barkley), which nets out somewhere in the middle now but gets exposed against good teams when both the talent and the play-calling gap get magnified. [[Matt Waldman]] noted the Eagles cannot simply reinstall last year's offense midseason; the realistic fix is asking Hurts and the veterans what they run best and cutting the rest.
- 2025-10-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-10]]): Barkley opened the Giants game with 19- and 13-yard carries — the 19 was his longest run of the year — and then didn't get another real attempt until five minutes were left in the first quarter, because the Giants went up 13-3 and Philadelphia's play mix tilted hard toward the pass. Harris credits [[Matt Waldman]]'s Thursday-show assessment of new coordinator Kevin Patullo as looking accurate for the first half, where everyone got involved, but says the second half was 'nothing shy of a disaster': the Eagles didn't block anybody, were missing guard Landon Dickerson and may have a real interior offensive-line problem, and simply didn't stick with the run. His conclusion for rest-of-season: because the game flow wasn't as predicted, the Eagles' aerial weapons all paid off or had chances to, whereas Barkley much less so — and unless things change it may not be as lucrative to hang on to Eagles in the second half of the fantasy season. He also uses the game to restate his standing objection to game-flow-based start/sit reasoning.
