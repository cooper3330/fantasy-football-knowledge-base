# Justin Herbert

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): pushes back directly on the assumption that new HC Jim Harbaugh and OC Greg Roman will simply make the Chargers run-heavy and cap Herbert's upside. Builds the case from Harbaugh's and Roman's actual coaching history rather than public reputation: as a college QB coach under Jon Gruden, Harbaugh helped produce a Rich Gannon MVP season (though Waldman discounts that somewhat given Gannon had Jerry Rice and Tim Brown); as a college head coach he got 3,300+ yards and 34 touchdowns from journeyman Josh Johnson at San Diego, then developed Andrew Luck at Stanford (3,400 yards, mid-30s TDs); Roman got similar efficient, high-touchdown play from Colin Kaepernick and Alex Smith. Waldman's explicit 2024 projection for Herbert: not a return to a 4,500-5,000-yard ceiling, but a more efficient "dink and dunk" profile well-suited to the modern two-high-heavy defensive era — roughly 3,300-3,500 yards and 30-plus touchdowns, which he says is enough production "to say he's reaching his ceiling in this defensive era." Notes the same offense should specifically benefit [[Keenan Allen]] more than a pure vertical receiver like Mike Williams (not a tracked page).

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]): after the Keenan Allen trade, calls the Chargers' starting receiver duo of Josh Palmer (not a tracked page) and [[Quentin Johnston]] a candidate for the worst in the NFL, and worries the team is "wasting" Herbert's prime — an "extremely talented player who has not been unlocked in any way, shape, or form" — by heading into 2024 without meaningfully addressing the position. Floats that the front office may be prioritizing salary-cap health and offensive-line/running-game investment this year, with a real push around Herbert coming in 2025 and beyond.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]): Harmon pushes back on the popular comparison of new OC Greg Roman's scheme fit for Herbert to what Roman and Jim Harbaugh built around Colin Kaepernick, calling that projection overblown given how different the two quarterbacks are as passers.
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman called Justin Herbert one of the best pure pocket passers Jim Harbaugh has ever coordinated (alongside Lamar Jackson, trailing only Alex Smith), and pushed back on fears that Greg Roman's run-heavy reputation will suppress Herbert's volume -- the Chargers ran the ball just 36% of the time last year (about 38% the two years before that) and would need a dramatic philosophical shift to approach 50%+, so he expects only a modest bump into the low-40s. He said the offense will still need to throw enough to justify real passing-game investment, with Herbert historically excellent working the middle of the field -- part of why Waldman is so bullish on [[Ladd McConkey]] as the scheme's natural slot fit.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): asked whether Herbert has value as a low-end QB2 at his QB17 ADP with a foot issue expected to cost a couple of weeks, Waldman said he rejects the consensus that Los Angeles will not throw — he expects enough volume for a high-end QB2 to low-end QB1 finish. His reasoning: Jim Harbaugh cannot flip the roster into a Michigan-style ground offense immediately with this offensive line, the improved run game should generate more play-action, and Greg Roman has never had a quarterback like Herbert so the fear that Roman will simply run his specialized scheme regardless is overstated. Waldman also pushed back on the thin receiving corps concern, noting Herbert has produced with non-name targets like Jalen Guyton and [[Josh Palmer]] when [[Keenan Allen]] and [[Mike Williams]] were hurt. His stated tipping point: three or four bad games and Harbaugh will let Herbert throw more, because winning is the priority.
- 2025-01-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-14]]): Herbert threw four interceptions in the wild card loss and Harmon refuses to let him off the hook — inaccuracy, timing and decision-making issues he says will draw deserved criticism. But he splits the blame: per Next Gen Stats, Herbert faced 11 quick pressures on 30.6% of dropbacks (pressure inside 2.5 seconds), including five unblocked, much of it up the middle, and everybody around him seemed to be 'actively trying to hurt' him. Harmon still calls it a successful season that changed the narrative on Herbert's style — he had been too cautious and decision-safe, and played looser and faster with the ball in 2024. Koh adds that [[Greg Roman]]'s passing scheme did not put him in position to succeed. Harmon cautions against playoff-game referendums on quarterback rankings.
- 2025-06-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-05]]): Herbert is the 'quarterback rotting on the vine' — high-end talent in a low-ceiling situation. Waldman's projection: about 476 attempts, 67–68% completions, 3,775 yards (7.93 per attempt), 23 touchdowns, four interceptions, plus roughly 307 rushing yards and a couple of scores, on a 50.1% run to 49.5% pass split. He notes the Chargers ran 47% of the time last year, up seven points from the prior season, and that Jim Harbaugh achieved that with a post-rehab [[J.K. Dobbins]] and an aging Gus Edwards — then doubled down by adding [[Omarion Hampton]] to [[Najee Harris]]. Angelo: Herbert needs a higher-volume passing offense; with Greg Roman calling plays this will never be a McVay or Ben Johnson scoreboard-lighting attack, it will be clock control and 'winning football', so everything depends on efficiency rather than volume. Waldman's framing for fantasy: a good game manager who gives you occasional big plays, not an unleashed passer.
- 2025-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Herbert is the number 8 flag player and also a post-hype sleeper — the quarterback the market has 'mysteriously decided' to be completely out on, going in the 10th round as roughly QB15 and outside the top 100. Harris rejects the standard guest line that Jim Harbaugh and Greg Roman are too run-heavy: Herbert was 13th in attempts and completions and 9th in passing yards last year, led the league in interception avoidance with three all season before throwing four in one playoff game against Houston, and finished QB11 only because of an unlucky 23-touchdown season. He notes he nearly flagged Herbert last year but backed off over the plantar fascia foot injury. Caveats: Herbert missed throws he should not have on tape, and it is frustrating in year six to still be chasing his 5,000- and 4,700-yard seasons; Los Angeles drafted for offense, re-signed [[Keenan Allen]], and replaced Rashawn Slater with Joe Alt.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Herbert is a flag player he drafted twice — once as his QB2 in a superflex, once in the 10th round of a standard league where a switch would be painless given the cost. He warns that the Friday night opener in Brazil against a very good Chiefs defense could be rough, but says that over the sweep of the season he is still buying in on Herbert.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): a week one victory for the only quarterback on Harris's flag-player list this year. He is careful that it is one week and came against a Chiefs defense that is really stout up front against the run, so nobody can promise the Chargers throw that much every week, but the constant chatter about Greg Roman and Jim Harbaugh being run-focused felt real silly. His broader point is that when the argument against a player is run-pass mix, depth chart or coaching, it can be real but it is usually a lazy argument made by people who just do not want to like the player. Next test is Week 2 against the Raiders.
