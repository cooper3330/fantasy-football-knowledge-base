# Justin Herbert

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): pushes back directly on the assumption that new HC Jim Harbaugh and OC Greg Roman will simply make the Chargers run-heavy and cap Herbert's upside. Builds the case from Harbaugh's and Roman's actual coaching history rather than public reputation: as a college QB coach under Jon Gruden, Harbaugh helped produce a Rich Gannon MVP season (though Waldman discounts that somewhat given Gannon had Jerry Rice and Tim Brown); as a college head coach he got 3,300+ yards and 34 touchdowns from journeyman Josh Johnson at San Diego, then developed Andrew Luck at Stanford (3,400 yards, mid-30s TDs); Roman got similar efficient, high-touchdown play from Colin Kaepernick and Alex Smith. Waldman's explicit 2024 projection for Herbert: not a return to a 4,500-5,000-yard ceiling, but a more efficient "dink and dunk" profile well-suited to the modern two-high-heavy defensive era — roughly 3,300-3,500 yards and 30-plus touchdowns, which he says is enough production "to say he's reaching his ceiling in this defensive era." Notes the same offense should specifically benefit [[Keenan Allen]] more than a pure vertical receiver like Mike Williams (not a tracked page).

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]): after the Keenan Allen trade, calls the Chargers' starting receiver duo of Josh Palmer (not a tracked page) and [[Quentin Johnston]] a candidate for the worst in the NFL, and worries the team is "wasting" Herbert's prime — an "extremely talented player who has not been unlocked in any way, shape, or form" — by heading into 2024 without meaningfully addressing the position. Floats that the front office may be prioritizing salary-cap health and offensive-line/running-game investment this year, with a real push around Herbert coming in 2025 and beyond.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]): Harmon pushes back on the popular comparison of new OC Greg Roman's scheme fit for Herbert to what Roman and Jim Harbaugh built around Colin Kaepernick, calling that projection overblown given how different the two quarterbacks are as passers.
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman called Justin Herbert one of the best pure pocket passers Jim Harbaugh has ever coordinated (alongside Lamar Jackson, trailing only Alex Smith), and pushed back on fears that Greg Roman's run-heavy reputation will suppress Herbert's volume -- the Chargers ran the ball just 36% of the time last year (about 38% the two years before that) and would need a dramatic philosophical shift to approach 50%+, so he expects only a modest bump into the low-40s. He said the offense will still need to throw enough to justify real passing-game investment, with Herbert historically excellent working the middle of the field -- part of why Waldman is so bullish on [[Ladd McConkey]] as the scheme's natural slot fit.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): asked whether Herbert has value as a low-end QB2 at his QB17 ADP with a foot issue expected to cost a couple of weeks, Waldman said he rejects the consensus that Los Angeles will not throw — he expects enough volume for a high-end QB2 to low-end QB1 finish. His reasoning: Jim Harbaugh cannot flip the roster into a Michigan-style ground offense immediately with this offensive line, the improved run game should generate more play-action, and Greg Roman has never had a quarterback like Herbert so the fear that Roman will simply run his specialized scheme regardless is overstated. Waldman also pushed back on the thin receiving corps concern, noting Herbert has produced with non-name targets like Jalen Guyton and [[Josh Palmer]] when [[Keenan Allen]] and [[Mike Williams]] were hurt. His stated tipping point: three or four bad games and Harbaugh will let Herbert throw more, because winning is the priority.
- 2025-01-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-14]]): Herbert threw four interceptions in the wild card loss and Harmon refuses to let him off the hook — inaccuracy, timing and decision-making issues he says will draw deserved criticism. But he splits the blame: per Next Gen Stats, Herbert faced 11 quick pressures on 30.6% of dropbacks (pressure inside 2.5 seconds), including five unblocked, much of it up the middle, and everybody around him seemed to be 'actively trying to hurt' him. Harmon still calls it a successful season that changed the narrative on Herbert's style — he had been too cautious and decision-safe, and played looser and faster with the ball in 2024. Koh adds that [[Greg Roman]]'s passing scheme did not put him in position to succeed. Harmon cautions against playoff-game referendums on quarterback rankings.
- 2025-06-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-05]]): Herbert is the 'quarterback rotting on the vine' — high-end talent in a low-ceiling situation. Waldman's projection: about 476 attempts, 67–68% completions, 3,775 yards (7.93 per attempt), 23 touchdowns, four interceptions, plus roughly 307 rushing yards and a couple of scores, on a 50.1% run to 49.5% pass split. He notes the Chargers ran 47% of the time last year, up seven points from the prior season, and that Jim Harbaugh achieved that with a post-rehab [[J.K. Dobbins]] and an aging Gus Edwards — then doubled down by adding [[Omarion Hampton]] to [[Najee Harris]]. Angelo: Herbert needs a higher-volume passing offense; with Greg Roman calling plays this will never be a McVay or Ben Johnson scoreboard-lighting attack, it will be clock control and 'winning football', so everything depends on efficiency rather than volume. Waldman's framing for fantasy: a good game manager who gives you occasional big plays, not an unleashed passer.
- 2025-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Herbert is the number 8 flag player and also a post-hype sleeper — the quarterback the market has 'mysteriously decided' to be completely out on, going in the 10th round as roughly QB15 and outside the top 100. Harris rejects the standard guest line that Jim Harbaugh and Greg Roman are too run-heavy: Herbert was 13th in attempts and completions and 9th in passing yards last year, led the league in interception avoidance with three all season before throwing four in one playoff game against Houston, and finished QB11 only because of an unlucky 23-touchdown season. He notes he nearly flagged Herbert last year but backed off over the plantar fascia foot injury. Caveats: Herbert missed throws he should not have on tape, and it is frustrating in year six to still be chasing his 5,000- and 4,700-yard seasons; Los Angeles drafted for offense, re-signed [[Keenan Allen]], and replaced Rashawn Slater with Joe Alt.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Herbert is a flag player he drafted twice — once as his QB2 in a superflex, once in the 10th round of a standard league where a switch would be painless given the cost. He warns that the Friday night opener in Brazil against a very good Chiefs defense could be rough, but says that over the sweep of the season he is still buying in on Herbert.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): a week one victory for the only quarterback on Harris's flag-player list this year. He is careful that it is one week and came against a Chiefs defense that is really stout up front against the run, so nobody can promise the Chargers throw that much every week, but the constant chatter about Greg Roman and Jim Harbaugh being run-focused felt real silly. His broader point is that when the argument against a player is run-pass mix, depth chart or coaching, it can be real but it is usually a lazy argument made by people who just do not want to like the player. Next test is Week 2 against the Raiders.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon's second FanDuel pick is Herbert over 1.5 passing touchdowns (-106), and he is emphatic that Herbert is playing at a very high level — 'heat coming out of his hands,' essentially one bad throw all season. He places Herbert just outside the [[Josh Allen]]-[[Lamar Jackson]] tier but 'banging on that door' as roughly the fifth-best quarterback in the league, and says the Chargers are one of the few teams that can credibly concentrate targets across three receivers because of him. Structural support for the bet: Los Angeles is throwing at a rate Adam Levitan compared to the 1984 Dolphins, they barely target running backs or tight ends, and neither [[Omarion Hampton]] nor [[Najee Harris]] has gotten going on the ground. Caveat noted and dismissed — Denver's defense is good, but Pat Surtain may be dealing with an injury.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-23]]): Herbert is playing loose in a way he did not under Joe Lombardi, whose Drew Brees-derived system Harmon believes drilled a robotic, by-the-book, go-through-the-progressions style into a quarterback already inclined toward it. Under Greg Roman he is off-script more, and Harmon says the physical talent shows — ripping deep balls to [[Quentin Johnston]] and a touchdown throw to [[Keenan Allen]] that 'nobody should be able to make.' Harmon also defended Roman against his industry reputation, arguing coordinators get blamed because people love [[Lamar Jackson]] and Herbert. He credited Herbert for continuing to feed Johnston through two seasons in which those targets fueled unfair 'not clutch' TV narratives about Herbert himself.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): laid a big egg in a terrible loss for a 3-0 team, but Harris assigns the blame to protection rather than the quarterback. Herbert was hurried on 19 of his 41 attempts, which Harris calls a lot, and neither interception was his fault — the first a fluke where Dexter Lawrence deflected a pass into his own hands (Lawrence's first career interception, returned to the Charger one), the second a timing throw to [[Ladd McConkey]] who was knocked over by his own teammate's pick. Only one downfield shot worked all day. Harris, who had been crowing about the Charger passing game, now warns that if the blocking is going to look like this with both starting tackles out, September may wind up being its high-water mark.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris still considers Herbert 'a terrific player' but said he'll have a hard time being consistently great after losing his third-string left tackle to an ankle injury on the very first offensive play, leaving him under constant duress the rest of the game. His first interception was 'absolutely not his fault' (a deflection at the line landed with a Colts defender), but the second was a bad decision, forcing a red-zone throw into [[Quentin Johnston]] while already down big. Harris framed this as continuing a month-long pattern of the Chargers offense not being fully competitive with the current line.
- 2025-10-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Scott Pianowski (untracked Yahoo analyst, [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-23]]): with [[Joe Alt]] expected back at left tackle and [[Trey Pipkins]] possibly returning, Herbert remains the clear foundation of a pass-heavy Chargers offense (one of the league's highest pass-rate-over-expected teams), projected for 270-280 yards against Minnesota, making all four of the Chargers' pass catchers ([[Ladd McConkey]], [[Keenan Allen]], [[Quentin Johnston]], [[Oronde Gadsden II]]) startable with relative confidence.
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said 'fuck it' -- not quite elite -- on [[Justin Herbert]], praising elite arm talent, footwork, mobility and pocket-climbing ability (contrasted earlier with [[Jackson Dart]]'s inability to do the same) but noting he makes one genuinely bad decision almost every game, citing a red-zone interception against the Colts and a throw where he misjudged a linebacker's drift and threw across his body. Waldman said only four or five quarterbacks rank above him and called him legitimately fun to watch regardless of outcome, terming him a 'good fantasy player capable of excellent production' rather than an excellent fantasy player outright, since the weekly bad decision caps his ceiling against good teams.
- 2025-10-31 — According to Adam Harstad (untracked Film and Theory co-host, [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-31]]) [Dynasty]: Herbert 'might end up being that kind of guy' -- a Dak Prescott-style flat-value asset -- if he settles in as a very good but not truly elite quarterback rather than breaking into MVP-tier territory.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]) with guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert): the biggest development is the Chargers without Joe Alt, who appears to have re-injured his ankle. Tennessee did not even have Jeffery Simmons and still put huge heat on Herbert, which Harris says 'put the kibosh on a lot of the excitement for that Charger offense'. Doherty calls it 'a true survive and advance game' and says the ceiling remains for Herbert and all four big pass catchers, but the floor goes lower and he needs to lower them in his general ranks. Harris is blunter about the receivers: 'a nightmare, just a disaster — I can't tell you for sure who number 15 is versus number 35, they're all the same, but they all get scary downsides'. Both flag upcoming games against Pittsburgh's weak pass defense as telling.
- 2025-11-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Herbert has been a favorite of the show and a 'flag' player this year, currently QB3 or QB4 and sustaining a lot of fantasy rosters — but Harris now fears for his safety because the Chargers have no tackles ('unless you count Trevor Penning, which I don't think even the Chargers count'). He will not trade for Herbert, and if asked whether a bad week or two would drop him outside the top ten, his answer is yes: he remembers what the offense looked like without Joe Alt, 'like [[C.J. Stroud]] but West Coast,' pressure in a second and a half every dropback with no run game. He would not actively shop Herbert but would ask around if someone just lost [[Jayden Daniels]]. Jim McCormick (guest, not tracked) agrees the injuries have piled up too far — 'Keenan Allen's playing right tackle this week' — and calls Harbaugh's build a fragile one that depends on protection.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): Herbert's pressure rate was 'extremely high' — over 50% while the game was competitive — with the Chargers' tackles out for the year, yet the commitment to the pass and his willingness to take hits made it work out okay. Guest Jamie Eisenberg of CBS Fantasy (not tracked) calls him 'a set-it-and-forget-it quarterback,' unwilling to sit him over the tackle situation, though he flags the recurring ankle issue as a longer-term worry and likes the Jacksonville matchup because their secondary is 'an absolute disaster.' Both hedge explicitly: Harris says if Jacksonville gets eight sacks and he cannot get the ball off, that becomes a C.J. Stroud-type situation and 'we're allowed to change our minds.'
- 2025-11-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-17]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said the Chargers are functionally done in the AFC race, and while Herbert still makes some of the most beautiful throws in the league, he also reliably makes at least one 'dumbass' throw a game. He put most of the blame on left tackle Trevor Penning, calling him a massive pass-pro liability who allowed multiple pressures/sacks and tipped passes again in this loss -- the Jaguars generated a 50% pressure rate and three sacks, including a hit that sent Herbert to the injury tent. Waldman noted Herbert has been pressured/hit at some of the highest rates in the league this year and that the wear is showing on tape, even though he still has a strong receiving corps to work with.
- 2025-11-17 — According to Chris Rabon (Action Network, untracked guest), with [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-17 (Week 11 Game Film Review)]]) more alarmed: Herbert took an unblocked hit on the very first snap against Jacksonville, was flagged for intentional grounding, and was benched with 11 minutes left for Trey Lance as a decimated offensive line produced what Rabon estimated as a roughly 50% pressure rate. Rabon called that unsustainable but said he's seen shell-shocked road performances like this from teams visiting Jacksonville before and wasn't ready to panic, expecting the Chargers to regroup during their bye and lean on Herbert's arm since the running back room is entirely banged up. Harris was more concerned, saying he's 'very worried,' though he acknowledged pressure alone doesn't always sink a fantasy quarterback's box score.
- 2025-12-01 — According to Andy Behrens (untracked, Yacht Club/The Deep Shot co-host) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-01]]): Justin Herbert broke his left hand against the Raiders and had to leave for a series, with Trey Lance in; he returned but was stuck in shotgun the rest of the game since he couldn't take snaps under center. Behrens said the first drive -- 14 plays, nine-plus minutes, good run-game chunks from Kimani Vidal, play-action hits to everyone but Ladd McConkey -- was exactly what he wanted to see before the injury derailed it. He said he's genuinely worried about Herbert moving forward and doesn't think he'll be a top-10 fantasy quarterback the rest of the season, especially given the Chargers' brutal remaining defensive schedule: Eagles, Chiefs, Cowboys, Texans, then Denver. The offensive line, already unable to block Max Crosby in this game, feeds the pessimism, and Behrens said the receiving corps -- who mostly failed to produce useful fantasy lines despite some touchdowns -- 'trickles down' into the same concern.
- 2025-12-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest John Daigle ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Herbert is dealing with a broken hand and was not confirmed to play in the Chargers' Monday-night game against the Eagles as of this recording, a complicating factor in evaluating [[Omarion Hampton]]'s workload.
- 2025-12-09 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Herbert played through a broken hand, throwing for only 79 yards but scrambling for a fluky 60-yard gain off an emergency checkdown read and going into 'Josh Allen mode' late in the fourth quarter and overtime, scrambling and converting repeatedly to force and then win the game. Harris calls it gutsy and courageous but says none of it is remotely sustainable: Herbert was sacked seven times (N'Kobe Dean routinely beat Kimani Vidal in protection), completed under half his passes, and faced a pressure rate over 50%. With a Week 15 trip to Kansas City on a compromised offensive line, Harris reiterates a sit call he's been making for weeks: 'we should be running away from our Chargers in our fantasy playoffs.'
- 2025-12-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-23]]): this was 'way more reasonable' for Herbert -- he still took pressure but not constantly, and the Chargers used shorter passes and misdirection more effectively. He had a long rushing scramble, two passing touchdowns, and a QB sneak touchdown in a game that looked like a track meet before a defensive stand in the second half (with [[Omarion Hampton]] scoring the only second-half touchdown). Harris says he's 'never doubted Herbert himself,' the concern has always been the situation around him, and while this game makes it 'seem more possible,' he isn't ready to rank the Chargers as though they've solved their offensive-line problem going forward.
- 2026-01-13 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-13]]): in the Chargers' wild-card loss to New England, Herbert threw for just 159 yards, no touchdowns, and avoided a pick despite endangering the ball a few times -- an eerily similar line to his 14-of-32, 44% completion, 1 TD/4 INT playoff performance against Houston last year. Harmon said Herbert 'is not blameless' and has developed bad pocket habits under constant pressure, but put most of the blame on OC Greg Roman's game plan and an offensive line that whiffed on free agents Bradley Bozeman (center) and Mekhi Becton (guard); Herbert was pressured relentlessly and the staff did little to scheme players open or adjust protection against blitzes and stunts. Harmon expects Jim Harbaugh keeps Roman given their long history together, but flagged Harbaugh's noncommittal postgame answer on Roman's play-calling as a signal he could actually be let go.
- 2026-01-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-22]]): said Mike McDaniel joining the Chargers as offensive coordinator is good for Justin Herbert's outlook, but worried the hype and his ADP price will rise this summer since Harris already thought L.A.'s offense 'was about to be excellent' before injuries hit -- meaning a strong 2026 would get credited entirely to McDaniel rather than the existing talent already in place.
- 2026-06-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-08]]): Waldman thinks Justin Herbert's offseason footwork work under new coach Mike McDaniel is mostly scheme-driven rather than a fix for underlying issues -- McDaniel wants quick, decisive throws off misdirection/play-action to set up intermediate shots, and the retooled footwork supports handoffs and quick releases within that system. Waldman's real concern is Herbert's processing in the 'middle range' between the quick game and extended plays, comparing it to what he saw of Herbert in college: not the processor Brock Purdy or Tom Brady were, capable of a beautiful throw one snap and an inexplicable bad decision the next because his 'high-end processing is good but not great.' He noted he wrote up the specific supporting numbers in a recent Football Guys fantasy notebook.
- 2026-07-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-06]]) [Redraft]: Waldman dismisses concern over OTA/minicamp reports that Herbert looked 'uneven,' explaining it as the normal friction of learning a new system's short-drop, quick-game timing -- accuracy over distance isn't the issue for Herbert, timing is, and early-camp unevenness in that specific skill is expected. He frames Mike McDaniel's install as targeting a quicker release (the 'first couple seconds, two and a half seconds' window) where Herbert has already shown effectiveness, versus a mid-range where he's struggled, with deep extension-and-scramble plays remaining a strength. Bob Harris (untracked) agreed the report was seasonal 'look at me' content-chasing from beat writers rather than a real signal worth reacting to.
- 2026-07-24 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris notes Herbert led the NFL in pass attempts last year without reaching 4,000 yards, evidence the passing volume wasn't the problem so much as a collapsing offensive line, and gives Herbert a 'mulligan' for a down 2025. Harris believes 'the QB is mighty good' and expects Herbert to deliver if the Chargers' tackle duo stays healthy, which would also lift [[Ladd McConkey]].
