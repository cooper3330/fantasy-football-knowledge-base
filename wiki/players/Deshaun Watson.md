# Deshaun Watson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]): "open to the idea" that Watson can be a real difference-maker again after the Browns acquire [[Jerry Jeudy]] — cites "good quality play from him last year in spurts" that "got better as the season went along" as the basis for optimism, without a firm projection attached.

- 2024-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-21]]): flags Watson as the swing factor for the whole receiver-room gamble Cleveland has made — "quite frankly, I don't know if this is a team that has that kind of time" to develop [[Jerry Jeudy]]/[[Elijah Moore]]/[[Cedric Tillman]] into reliable options, framing Cleveland's timeline as needing to win in 2024, not a two-to-three-year plan, and calling him along with the receiver room's reliability the central swing question for the offense ("just don't set my watch to it" applied to the team overall, and to Watson specifically). Notes losing [[Nick Chubb]]'s complementary run-game impact removes another stabilizer, leaving the offense's identity resting on [[Amari Cooper]] and [[David Njoku]] regardless of Watson's play.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): asked whether Watson can ever regain the top-five fantasy form he had in Houston, Waldman is deeply pessimistic — and his reason is motivational rather than physical. He says he does not think Watson wants to play football, that he is stuck with a big contract, and that if he could disappear from the limelight he would, whether because the saga soured him on the public eye or because he feels guilty about the allegations. Waldman disclosed that in his next Rookie Scouting Portfolio dynasty rankings and projections update, due in two to three days, he has cut Watson from 14 games to nine and has [[Jameis Winston]] at eight, adding he would not be shocked if Watson finds a way to be hurt even longer. Bob Harris (not tracked) framed him as potentially 'the Josh Gordon of quarterbacks' — a name people keep chasing without ever getting paid.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman expects Watson to lose the job to [[Jameis Winston]] by midseason, pointing to Cleveland having stripped as much salary off Watson as it could that month to free cap space. Untracked co-host Bob Harris added reporting: he asked longtime Browns beat writer Steve King directly whether Watson wants to play football, and King said he did not know that he does — Harris framed it as one more data point in a series pointing the same direction, while allowing King may not be a fan. Waldman said he hopes Watson shakes off whatever is dragging him down and overcomes it, but is not betting on it.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Harmon calls Watson an absolute mess and says Cleveland will not have a normal passing game until he can be even functional, let alone average — the Browns are 31st in dropback success rate. Koh (not tracked) argues it is mental at this point and that the fit is broken at the root: Kevin Stefanski's system requires a quarterback who can operate from under center and Watson cannot, pointing out that [[Joe Flacco]] was balling in the same system for exactly that reason. Both agree the organization eventually has to choose between the quarterback and the two-time coach of the year, and that having both in the building means it never works.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]): Watson's Achilles injury was officially confirmed as season-ending, with a choice between surgery types including the faster-return procedure [[Aaron Rodgers]] had. Asked whether the injury is good for Browns morale, Waldman said yes and argued it organizationally, not personally: teammates had been playing amid a constant media 'shit storm', with the city and locker room fatigued by the situation, and now 'they don't have to wonder why Watson hasn't been the guy that he used to be.' He credited Watson with an efficient start to the game before the injury, and warned against the crowd-psychology habit of reading a player's face and declaring you always knew. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) added the framing that 'if the circus has come to town, the quality of the show better be worth the price of admission.'
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): Watson had not thrown for 200 yards in a game all season and last topped 300 in January 2021, and Waldman blamed Cleveland's brass for burying [[Jameis Winston]] as an emergency backup to avoid hearing fans chant his name. He drew a distinction on off-field history — Winston's problems were known and are behind him, whereas Watson's were unresolved when the Browns were courting him — and said the objective truth that it was not working was obvious to everyone including the players.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]): Watson is finished as a starter. Cleveland is dead last in the NFL in catchable air yards with him and first with [[Jameis Winston]], and while starting he led the NFL in shotgun empty dropbacks at roughly Daniel Jones-level efficiency — the exact inverse of the under-center, play-action, naked-bootleg offense [[Kevin Stefanski]] runs. Harmon's broader point is that the trade and contract were not only bad on the off-field grounds but were a scheme mismatch from day one: any quarterback uncomfortable under center is a poor Stefanski fit, and he wondered aloud whether even [[Joe Burrow]] would mesh with Stefanski for the same reason. With Watson now also coming off an Achilles injury, both hosts said Cleveland cannot walk into 2025 with him as the week one starter, though the dead money may tie the front office's hands. James Koh (co-host, not tracked) added that Stefanski's success without Watson proves the coach, not the scheme, was never the problem.
- 2026-02-03 — According to untracked co-host James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: reacting to Todd Monken's hire as Browns head coach, Koh predicted [[Deshaun Watson]] is '100% for sure' going to get a chance at the starting job in 2026, saying Monken's full shotgun-spread scheme fits Watson's skill set well, though he stopped short of predicting Watson would win the job outright. [[Matt Harmon]] countered in the same discussion that [[Shedeur Sanders]] is the Week 1 favorite as things stand. Both hosts agreed Cleveland's quarterback situation is unsettled and that the receiver room and offensive line need a full overhaul either way.
- 2026-02-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and untracked co-host James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-24]]): Cleveland is carrying a $131M dead-cap figure for Watson in 2026 and would owe roughly $50M in dead cap in 2027, and he'd hit free agency after 2027 -- financially, cutting him is close to impossible (Koh compares it to the Dolphins potentially eating $99M on Tua, saying Cleveland 'would be even more screwed'). Koh floats, half-jokingly, that the Browns could even consider extending Watson purely to help their cap situation; Harmon strongly disagrees, calling an extension 'football malpractice' and saying the team has to 'get off the train' on him. Both agree there is essentially a 100% chance Watson gets at least an opportunity to start in 2026, even though Shedeur Sanders will likely open the year as starter, with Watson possibly beginning the season on PUP. Harmon notes new HC Todd Monken's expected spread/shotgun, tempo-driven scheme is the same kind of system Watson thrived in during his Houston prime, giving him a real runway if given the chance -- though neither analyst expects him to still be good enough to capitalize on it.
- 2026-04-28 — According to James Koh (untracked co-host) and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-28]]): Cleveland is expected to start Watson in Week 1 2026, not on performance hope but because no team will trade for his remaining contract and the Browns keep restructuring it to kick the cap hit down the road. Harmon isn't convinced Watson recovers anything resembling his pre-injury form ('a million years ago'). Both view the Taylen Green pick as confusing given the timing -- a rookie who needs a full development year is now stuck behind Watson, [[Shedeur Sanders]] and [[Dylan Gabriel]] in a chaotic QB room.
- 2026-05-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-18]]) [Redraft]: Waldman said feel it that Watson will be the Browns' starter, noting Watson is a full year removed from his Achilles tear and appears healthier, with glowing reports from his own offseason workouts (Jerry Judy in attendance) and owner Jimmy Haslam -- who once called the signing a 'swing and a miss' -- now praising his throwing and reporting he's in lockstep with OC Todd Monken. Waldman expects Monken to lean on what worked for Watson in Houston, using [[Casey Concepcion]] as a run-game complement and receivers like [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]], [[Jerry Jeudy]] and [[Cedric Tillman]] filling out the passing game. He doesn't see Watson reaching QB1 status this year but projects him competing with [[Aaron Rodgers]] for low-end QB2 value, adding that whatever ceiling Watson's early career had never existed for [[Shedeur Sanders]].
- 2026-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-16]]): Harmon says Watson has been 'flat out one of the worst quarterbacks in the league' since arriving in Cleveland, noting his last win came September 15, 2024 and his last game played was October 20, 2024 -- nearly two years removed from football. He argues the Kevin Stefanski scheme was a poor fit for Watson's skill set and that new OC Todd Monken's more shotgun, spread-based system is a much better scheme fit, along with what Harmon calls a genuinely improved receiving corps (Casey Concepcion, Denzel Boston, Jerry Jeudy, plus Quinshon Judkins and Harold Fannin in the offense). Even so, Harmon views the Watson contract as complete sunk cost and says he would personally start [[Shedeur Sanders]] over Watson for the full season, while still expecting Cleveland to use multiple starting quarterbacks in 2026.
- 2026-07-31 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-31]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris calls Deshaun Watson's situation the last year of 'the biggest albatross contract ever in NFL history' and doesn't buy that there's a real quarterback battle -- Watson opened camp throwing back-to-back interceptions, consistent with a player who has now torn his Achilles twice. Harris expects head coach Todd Monken not to start him even though the team is publicly framing it as a competition, and says organizationally the team should 'just cut him' rather than risk putting him in a position to succeed and trigger another contract.
- 2026-08-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-10]]): Beat reporters are again suggesting Deshaun Watson could be ahead of [[Shedeur Sanders]] at quarterback in Cleveland, echoing a similar report from the spring. Harris is skeptical of the sourcing, suspecting the Browns are leaking this to prepare the league for an 'unthinkable decision,' and calls it a bad move given Watson's expiring, guaranteed-money-heavy contract -- but says he's only repeating what national reporters are repeating from front-office texts, not vouching for it himself.
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Dan Hanses]] and [[Mark Sessler]] (not tracked, Heed The Call, [[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-20]]): neither host is rooting for Deshaun Watson to keep the Browns' starting job. Sessler calls continuing to play him a 'sunk cost' exercise tied to the money and public-relations weight of his contract, and Hanses says he 'doesn't seem present or in the moment' relative to his pre-injury self; both expect Cleveland to eventually turn to Shedeur Sanders this season.
- 2026-08-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-24 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) and co-host Bob Harris (untracked, Feel It or F**k It), who disagreed: Harris predicted Deshaun Watson, now named Browns starter, would only start a couple of games before being benched in favor of [[Shedeur Sanders]] in a homecoming narrative, since Cleveland opens with tough road games before returning home. Waldman disagreed, saying he expects Watson to start six to eight games; he argued that if Watson is fully healthy, hostile crowd reaction 'won't matter' to him given years spent 'weaponizing himself' against booing since his college days facing rival programs. Waldman said the bigger open questions are the receiving corps and whether the offensive line and scheme fit Watson, not his psychology, and that the team will likely give the pairing six to seven games before considering a change. [Dynasty]
- 2026-08-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris calls Cleveland naming Watson the Week 1 starter 'an indefensible decision,' saying he predicted this back in July and that 'there just don't seem to be any adults in the room' to stop it. He notes Browns fans have booed Watson and that Watson responded 'as tone deaf as ever,' and says essentially no one thinks Watson is still good. Harris says the news doesn't change his ranks on Quinshon Judkins, Casey Concepcion or Denzel Boston, and calls even the good-case scenario -- Watson somehow playing well and Cleveland winning -- 'soul crushing,' since it risks the team re-signing what he calls the 'biggest boondoggle contract in NFL history' instead of turning to Shedeur Sanders or Dillon Gabriel.
- 2026-08-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-25]]): Watson was officially named the Browns' Week 1 starter, but Harmon doesn't expect it to last -- he calls Watson 'statistically one of the worst quarterbacks' at pushing the ball downfield since joining Cleveland, unchanged even under new OC Todd Monken, and points to a poor preseason showing against Buffalo's backups as further evidence. Harmon expects heavy 'designer,' short-area usage funneled to [[Casey Concepcion]], comparing it to how the Ravens used a rookie Zay Flowers before he was proven as a real route runner. James Koh (untracked co-host) frames the decision as a 'sunk cost fallacy' driven by ownership wanting to justify Watson's contract and by the fact it's easier to bench Watson for [[Shedeur Sanders]] than reverse course later; both hosts predict Watson struggles the first two weeks (road at Jacksonville, then at Tampa Bay) before Sanders takes over, with Koh guessing a Week 3 switch.
- 2026-08-27 — According to James Koh and Alfredo Brown, both not tracked experts ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-27]]): Watson has 'not looked good' in the Browns' quarterback competition against [[Shedeur Sanders]], though Sanders 'hasn't looked terrible' either -- neither has actually looked good. Koh floated a 'big brain conspiracy theory' that Todd Monken started Watson over Sanders to keep ownership happy, reasoning it is easier to bench a struggling Watson for Sanders than to bench Sanders after a public flop. Mapping Cleveland's brutal early schedule (Jaguars, Buccaneers, Panthers, Steelers, Jets, Ravens), both project the Browns to sit around 1-5 or 2-4 through six games, which they believe gives Monken 'all the ammo' he needs to bench Watson and hand the job to Sanders in Week 7 against Tennessee, with a bye week available as a second reset point if that stint struggles too.
