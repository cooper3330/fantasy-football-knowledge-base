# Deshaun Watson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]): "open to the idea" that Watson can be a real difference-maker again after the Browns acquire [[Jerry Jeudy]] — cites "good quality play from him last year in spurts" that "got better as the season went along" as the basis for optimism, without a firm projection attached.

- 2024-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-21]]): flags Watson as the swing factor for the whole receiver-room gamble Cleveland has made — "quite frankly, I don't know if this is a team that has that kind of time" to develop [[Jerry Jeudy]]/[[Elijah Moore]]/[[Cedric Tillman]] into reliable options, framing Cleveland's timeline as needing to win in 2024, not a two-to-three-year plan, and calling him along with the receiver room's reliability the central swing question for the offense ("just don't set my watch to it" applied to the team overall, and to Watson specifically). Notes losing [[Nick Chubb]]'s complementary run-game impact removes another stabilizer, leaving the offense's identity resting on [[Amari Cooper]] and [[David Njoku]] regardless of Watson's play.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): asked whether Watson can ever regain the top-five fantasy form he had in Houston, Waldman is deeply pessimistic — and his reason is motivational rather than physical. He says he does not think Watson wants to play football, that he is stuck with a big contract, and that if he could disappear from the limelight he would, whether because the saga soured him on the public eye or because he feels guilty about the allegations. Waldman disclosed that in his next Rookie Scouting Portfolio dynasty rankings and projections update, due in two to three days, he has cut Watson from 14 games to nine and has [[Jameis Winston]] at eight, adding he would not be shocked if Watson finds a way to be hurt even longer. Bob Harris (not tracked) framed him as potentially 'the Josh Gordon of quarterbacks' — a name people keep chasing without ever getting paid.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman expects Watson to lose the job to [[Jameis Winston]] by midseason, pointing to Cleveland having stripped as much salary off Watson as it could that month to free cap space. Untracked co-host Bob Harris added reporting: he asked longtime Browns beat writer Steve King directly whether Watson wants to play football, and King said he did not know that he does — Harris framed it as one more data point in a series pointing the same direction, while allowing King may not be a fan. Waldman said he hopes Watson shakes off whatever is dragging him down and overcomes it, but is not betting on it.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Harmon calls Watson an absolute mess and says Cleveland will not have a normal passing game until he can be even functional, let alone average — the Browns are 31st in dropback success rate. Koh (not tracked) argues it is mental at this point and that the fit is broken at the root: Kevin Stefanski's system requires a quarterback who can operate from under center and Watson cannot, pointing out that [[Joe Flacco]] was balling in the same system for exactly that reason. Both agree the organization eventually has to choose between the quarterback and the two-time coach of the year, and that having both in the building means it never works.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]): Watson's Achilles injury was officially confirmed as season-ending, with a choice between surgery types including the faster-return procedure [[Aaron Rodgers]] had. Asked whether the injury is good for Browns morale, Waldman said yes and argued it organizationally, not personally: teammates had been playing amid a constant media 'shit storm', with the city and locker room fatigued by the situation, and now 'they don't have to wonder why Watson hasn't been the guy that he used to be.' He credited Watson with an efficient start to the game before the injury, and warned against the crowd-psychology habit of reading a player's face and declaring you always knew. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) added the framing that 'if the circus has come to town, the quality of the show better be worth the price of admission.'
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): Watson had not thrown for 200 yards in a game all season and last topped 300 in January 2021, and Waldman blamed Cleveland's brass for burying [[Jameis Winston]] as an emergency backup to avoid hearing fans chant his name. He drew a distinction on off-field history — Winston's problems were known and are behind him, whereas Watson's were unresolved when the Browns were courting him — and said the objective truth that it was not working was obvious to everyone including the players.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]): Watson is finished as a starter. Cleveland is dead last in the NFL in catchable air yards with him and first with [[Jameis Winston]], and while starting he led the NFL in shotgun empty dropbacks at roughly Daniel Jones-level efficiency — the exact inverse of the under-center, play-action, naked-bootleg offense [[Kevin Stefanski]] runs. Harmon's broader point is that the trade and contract were not only bad on the off-field grounds but were a scheme mismatch from day one: any quarterback uncomfortable under center is a poor Stefanski fit, and he wondered aloud whether even [[Joe Burrow]] would mesh with Stefanski for the same reason. With Watson now also coming off an Achilles injury, both hosts said Cleveland cannot walk into 2025 with him as the week one starter, though the dead money may tie the front office's hands. James Koh (co-host, not tracked) added that Stefanski's success without Watson proves the coach, not the scheme, was never the problem.
