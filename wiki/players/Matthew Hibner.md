# Matthew Hibner

- 2026-03-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-02]]) [Dynasty]: some had ranked Hibner (SMU) low and questioned his strength, but he answered with 28 bench reps at 225 -- best among tight ends at the combine -- and Waldman said he was already impressed watching Hibner's tape the week before. At 6'5/252, Waldman thinks he's strong enough to play a move-tight-end role now and could add another ten pounds to handle an in-line role later; called him a solid blocker who delivers a real punch and takes good angles, though not a great stock blocker relative to his other traits. Gave him a 'contributor' grade currently, projecting he'll get targets and produce even if used as a limited second tight end.
- 2026-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-11]]) [Dynasty]: SMU's Hibner has good hands and wins the ball vertically and in contested situations, but isn't a dynamic athlete after the catch -- mostly a straight-line runner who might get one good cut. In space he plays like 'an oversized fullback' who's already a good blocker for his size and could still improve there, projecting best as a weak-side double-team in-line piece. Waldman thinks he has enough overall skill to carve out a long NFL career, potentially even a year or two as a bridge starter, as long as he stays healthy -- but doesn't see the ceiling of a true difference-making pass-game weapon.
- 2026-05-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-04]]) [Dynasty]: thinks it's plausible Matthew Hibner succeeds [[Mark Andrews]] as Baltimore's primary tight end weapon, echoing the team's own comments this week that they view him as a potential major piece. Waldman's strengths list: winning up the seam, making plays in the intermediate and vertical zones, tracking the ball well, and catching through contact; he's also not a bad blocker. Waldman expects an acclimation process similar to what [[Charlie Kolar]] went through behind Andrews, calling Hibner a 'sneaky good' mid-to-late pick depending on format and scoring.
