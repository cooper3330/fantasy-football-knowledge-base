# Andrei Iosivas

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-07-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-04]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman noted Iosivas (Princeton) sought out trainer Drew Lieberman, whose route-running framework Waldman says is the foundation of his own receiver-evaluation criteria, and per Lieberman's account in The Athletic, Iosivas and [[Chase Brown]] were among the fastest learners he's worked with, partly because neither had bad habits to unlearn. Angelo said Iosivas' size, speed, and raw athleticism could make him the tipping point receiver if [[Tee Higgins]] is traded mid-season, especially with [[Tyler Boyd]] gone and a big slot role open behind [[Ja'Marr Chase]]. Waldman agreed there is no clear 'next guy up' in Cincinnati beyond Iosivas, [[Jermaine Burton]], and [[Charlie Jones]], calling Iosivas a real dynasty name to watch.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]) [Dynasty]: totally feeling Iosivas as a dynasty asset — big, fast, strong, a great athlete who played well as a rookie without fully knowing what he could do. Waldman notes Iosivas was the one who called [[Chase Brown]] and told him to come train with WR coach Drew Lieberman, and relays Lieberman's line that the best thing about both was that they arrived knowing nothing about route running and therefore had no bad habits. If [[Tee Higgins]] is traded, Waldman expects the replacement to be a combination of Iosivas outside and [[Jermaine Burton]] in the [[Tyler Boyd]] role rotating with [[Ja'Marr Chase]]. Caveat: in a deep 2024 free draft he would still take Burton first as the better, more refined all-around player, since Iosivas can usually be had off waivers without overspending. Waldman disclosed he traded Iosivas away this week for [[Derrick Henry]] in a win-now dynasty build.
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]): Iosivas has been a camp standout, with the Athletic's Paul Dehner suggesting the second-year Princeton product could be the Bengals' WR3 and even absorb the full-time slot snaps vacated by [[Tyler Boyd]] — Harmon calls the reports of Iosivas running from the slot 'extremely consequential' and trusts Dehner as the best-connected Bengals beat writer, while dismissing Trenton Irwin's placement atop the initial depth chart. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) notes his measurables: 6-3, 205, 4.43 forty, 39-inch vertical, a genuine jump-ball and high-point player who would need to become a better zone beater. Harmon ties it to the bigger picture — Cincinnati has too often had no third answer beyond [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Tee Higgins]], and with Higgins likely gone in 2025, developing Iosivas and [[Jermaine Burton]] now is critical.
- 2024-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): Iosivas has been taking the first-team reps as Cincinnati transitions him into the slot with [[Ja'Marr Chase]] absent, and Harmon calls him a completely unproven player — we do not know who he is yet. His floor framing is generous rather than dismissive: at worst the Bengals have a good athlete who wins in contested-catch situations playing inside, which he says is not that bad a fallback. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) credits the team for at least getting him acclimated to the slot role before he was needed there.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Iosivas is the leading candidate to absorb the [[Tyler Boyd]] slot vacancy — Boyd saw nearly 100 targets in 2023 — and Cincinnati is one of the rare teams that runs almost every snap in 11 personnel because [[Joe Burrow]] wants to be in the gun and see everything, so they need a slot receiver to step up. Harmon notes he loves a big slot and that Boyd himself was 6'2, 203, so the Bengals are used to size at the position. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) says the camp hype is impossible to ignore: 6'3, a 4.43 runner and multi-sport athlete studying [[Keenan Allen]]'s inside releases on tape, with strong contested-catch and jump-ball ability. Both frame [[Ja'Marr Chase]]'s absence as a blessing in disguise that let Iosivas build first-team chemistry with Burrow.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-12]]): with [[Tee Higgins]] out (a hamstring that still had him not practicing on the Wednesday of Week 2, plus contract-related speculation the hosts flag but do not endorse), Iosivas and [[Ja'Marr Chase]] ran the most routes on the team and each drew six targets. Harmon's caveat is that both had an average depth of target under seven, which he says is not going to cut it in an offense where [[Joe Burrow]] was passing up downfield throws to check down.
- 2025-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-05]]): Harmon says Iosivas is 'fine' as Cincinnati's third receiver and that the team likes him a lot — Zac Taylor talked him up at his Combine availability. Koh's read was mixed: Iosivas established himself as the number three out of camp and was great and reliable in the red zone, catching a lot of touchdowns, but there was a lot left to be desired between the twenties. Harmon still expects the Bengals to take a couple of stabs at receiver in the draft, especially if [[Tee Higgins]] plays on the tag, though he does not think that pick would need to contribute right away in year one.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]) [Dynasty]: a third-year breakout is 'probably not' the base case on paper — [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and a re-signed [[Tee Higgins]] plus [[Chase Brown]] out of the backfield leave him third or fourth in the target order — but Iosivas is now working at about 220 pounds, improved measurably from year one to year two, and Waldman calls him a smart kid putting in the craft work that pays dividends. If Higgins gets hurt again or [[Jermaine Burton]] continues to implode, the breakout is very possible. Waldman's format split: in dynasty he is the guy you trade for and hold; in redraft only a bench stash in very deep leagues (25 roster spots). Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) notes Burton's absence from Waldman's list of hard workers is itself telling, and that the Bengals have been the league's pass-heaviest offense for three years.
- 2025-11-18 — According to Jake Seeley (The Athletic, not a tracked expert; [[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-11-18]]): with Ja'Marr Chase out, Iosivas steps in alongside Tee Higgins and Seeley wouldn't be surprised if he's a top-25 fantasy WR play for the week, ranking him ahead of [[Darnell Mooney]]; he notes the Patriots secondary hasn't actually been shutting receivers down this year and his real hesitation is about quarterback Joe Flacco, who looked lost against Pittsburgh's zone coverage on the prior night's film review. [[Chris Harris]] independently lands on Iosivas at the same spot -- No. 4 on his own list -- calling him 'always a pretty good player,' more of a possession receiver who has had usable weeks with Joe Burrow but is unpredictable week-to-week, and notes Tee Higgins isn't a true alpha, leaving room for Iosivas to soak up volume.
- 2025-11-20 — According to Chris Harris and guest Jake Trowbridge (untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-20]]) [Best Ball]: both hosts independently rostered Iosivas at $4,100 against New England, expecting a dramatic target-share bump with a suspended/unavailable Bengals teammate out of the lineup ('the expectorating one'); Trowbridge called him the most attractive cheap option over alternatives like Tee Higgins or Noah Fant, predicting he'll be extremely popular ('chalky') given the price, especially with word that Joe Burrow's practice window is open and he could conceivably play.
- 2025-11-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: ranked as Harris's #5 (and guest Scott Fish's #3) Week 13 waiver add, pitched as a one-week fill-in with Tee Higgins out (the short Thanksgiving week means Higgins won't be back in time) and Joe Burrow returning from injury. Iosivas picked up a stinger late in the Patriots game but Harris believes he's fine since he returned to finish that contest. Harris calls him 'the first receiver I want to grab' for Higgins owners needing a one-week solution, given it's Burrow throwing rather than Jake Browning/Joe Flacco.
- 2025-12-01 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-01]]): With [[Tee Higgins]] out against Baltimore, Andrei Iosivas was 'not nearly as involved' as [[Mitchell Tinsley]], leading Harris to say he'd rank Tinsley ahead of Iosivas going forward if Higgins remains out.
- 2025-12-09 — According to Jeff Bell (Football Guys, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-09]]): Bell flags that Iosivas's role didn't meaningfully change even in the one game Tee Higgins and Ja'Marr Chase were both out, which concerns him relative to Mitchell Tinsley and Mike Gesicki as Bengals pass-catching options for Week 15.
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-22 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: rejects Cincinnati OC Dan Pitcher's suggestion that a rookie receiver could beat out Iosivas for the WR3 job as what Waldman calls 'audentatitis' -- big, physical rookies who win contested catches and pop in OTAs but often can't read the field or beat man coverage once live reps start in August, and whose beat-reporter hype fades. Waldman argues Iosivas has developed into a legitimately usable WR3 who understands the route tree and makes the in-route adjustments Joe Burrow, a 'heady' quarterback, demands -- chemistry that rookies (including [[Isaiah Neyor]] and [[De'Zhaun Stribling]], both also praised in OTAs) haven't built yet.
