# Andrei Iosivas

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-07-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-04]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman noted Iosivas (Princeton) sought out trainer Drew Lieberman, whose route-running framework Waldman says is the foundation of his own receiver-evaluation criteria, and per Lieberman's account in The Athletic, Iosivas and [[Chase Brown]] were among the fastest learners he's worked with, partly because neither had bad habits to unlearn. Angelo said Iosivas' size, speed, and raw athleticism could make him the tipping point receiver if [[Tee Higgins]] is traded mid-season, especially with [[Tyler Boyd]] gone and a big slot role open behind [[Ja'Marr Chase]]. Waldman agreed there is no clear 'next guy up' in Cincinnati beyond Iosivas, [[Jermaine Burton]], and [[Charlie Jones]], calling Iosivas a real dynasty name to watch.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]) [Dynasty]: totally feeling Iosivas as a dynasty asset — big, fast, strong, a great athlete who played well as a rookie without fully knowing what he could do. Waldman notes Iosivas was the one who called [[Chase Brown]] and told him to come train with WR coach Drew Lieberman, and relays Lieberman's line that the best thing about both was that they arrived knowing nothing about route running and therefore had no bad habits. If [[Tee Higgins]] is traded, Waldman expects the replacement to be a combination of Iosivas outside and [[Jermaine Burton]] in the [[Tyler Boyd]] role rotating with [[Ja'Marr Chase]]. Caveat: in a deep 2024 free draft he would still take Burton first as the better, more refined all-around player, since Iosivas can usually be had off waivers without overspending. Waldman disclosed he traded Iosivas away this week for [[Derrick Henry]] in a win-now dynasty build.
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]): Iosivas has been a camp standout, with the Athletic's Paul Dehner suggesting the second-year Princeton product could be the Bengals' WR3 and even absorb the full-time slot snaps vacated by [[Tyler Boyd]] — Harmon calls the reports of Iosivas running from the slot 'extremely consequential' and trusts Dehner as the best-connected Bengals beat writer, while dismissing Trenton Irwin's placement atop the initial depth chart. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) notes his measurables: 6-3, 205, 4.43 forty, 39-inch vertical, a genuine jump-ball and high-point player who would need to become a better zone beater. Harmon ties it to the bigger picture — Cincinnati has too often had no third answer beyond [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Tee Higgins]], and with Higgins likely gone in 2025, developing Iosivas and [[Jermaine Burton]] now is critical.
- 2024-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): Iosivas has been taking the first-team reps as Cincinnati transitions him into the slot with [[Ja'Marr Chase]] absent, and Harmon calls him a completely unproven player — we do not know who he is yet. His floor framing is generous rather than dismissive: at worst the Bengals have a good athlete who wins in contested-catch situations playing inside, which he says is not that bad a fallback. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) credits the team for at least getting him acclimated to the slot role before he was needed there.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Iosivas is the leading candidate to absorb the [[Tyler Boyd]] slot vacancy — Boyd saw nearly 100 targets in 2023 — and Cincinnati is one of the rare teams that runs almost every snap in 11 personnel because [[Joe Burrow]] wants to be in the gun and see everything, so they need a slot receiver to step up. Harmon notes he loves a big slot and that Boyd himself was 6'2, 203, so the Bengals are used to size at the position. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) says the camp hype is impossible to ignore: 6'3, a 4.43 runner and multi-sport athlete studying [[Keenan Allen]]'s inside releases on tape, with strong contested-catch and jump-ball ability. Both frame [[Ja'Marr Chase]]'s absence as a blessing in disguise that let Iosivas build first-team chemistry with Burrow.
