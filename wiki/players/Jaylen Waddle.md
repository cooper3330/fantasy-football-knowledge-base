# Jaylen Waddle

## Expert Takes

- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]): considers Waddle 'frankly a better wide receiver' than [[DeVonta Smith]] — better at the catch point, more physical, better after the catch — but still gives Smith the slight overall value edge because of Philadelphia's stronger surrounding context.
- 2024-05-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-29]]), discussing Miami's addition of rookie [[Malik Washington]]: Waddle, used almost purely as an outside receiver in Mike McDaniel's offense, was frequently in and out of the lineup last season with nagging absences; Harmon floats that free-agent addition [[Odell Beckham Jr.]] could function as informal "injury insurance" for him, while cautioning the availability pattern could be random/fluky rather than a real durability concern.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: at WR17 ADP, Waldman called Waddle 'a great price' — he'd take Waddle as a top-15 receiver in almost any year, even coming off last year's WR32 finish, on talent and role alone, projecting 109 targets, 75 catches, just over 1,000 yards and 5 touchdowns. He sees real upside if Tyreek Hill's career winds down in the next year or two, at which point Waddle could post a 1,500-yard season. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) said he'd personally have more shares of [[Mike Evans]] and named [[DeVonta Smith]] and (with some hesitation) [[DJ Moore]] as players he prefers at a similar price. Waldman countered he'd rank Waddle above every comp Harris named except Moore and Evans on pure player evaluation, even if his actual draft shares skew toward those other names given the crowded Miami role with Hill and [[Odell Beckham Jr.]] still ahead of him.
- 2024-07-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-05]]) [Dynasty]: Waddle's 2023 Reception Perception profile is his best yet as a pro, even though it came in a down statistical season (1,014 yards, 4 TDs in 14 games missed to a high ankle sprain and a concussion; 14.1 YPR, 69% catch rate, down from 1,356 yards in year two). The key finding: Waddle, not Tyreek Hill, plays the harder boundary/X role for Miami, taking 75-81% of his snaps outside and on the line across the last two seasons, up both years. Overall he posted 72% success vs. man, 83% vs. zone, and 72% vs. press coverage -- winning on all three levels (69% on nine routes, 81.6% on digs, 80% on outs, 83% on slants, 100% on a small curl-route sample). The standout development is press-man beating: Waddle led the 2021 draft class in man-coverage success and ranked second in zone success coming out, but posted just a 33rd-percentile press score that season, which is why Harmon had him as WR3/top-of-tier-two behind Chase and Smith. He improved from 65.7% vs. press as a rookie to over 70% (72%) in 2023, which Harmon calls the single most important number for projecting him as a true X receiver. Despite the growth, Harmon stops short of ranking Waddle in the same tier as Stefon Diggs or Brandon Aiyuk -- he is not fully confident Waddle would be a clear WR1 if moved to a team without an established number one, calling him 'top of tier two, maybe bottom of tier three' rather than a lock. Both hosts note his box-score output (1,000 yards, 4 TDs) undersold the profile and initially made his top-five-money extension look surprising until you watch the tape. They also speculate that if Miami used Odell Beckham Jr. as more of a 'sacrificial X' to free Waddle for schemed touches like Tyreek Hill gets, his success rates could climb even further.
- 2024-07-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Contrary to perception, Jaylen Waddle has primarily lined up on the line of scrimmage outside more than [[Tyreek Hill]] in Miami's offense, and Harmon thinks Waddle -- like [[Keon Coleman]] in a different sense -- could actually be maximized by moving off the line as a move-around receiver rather than staying tied to the boundary, similar to how Miami already deploys Hill via motion. He floated a scheme where [[Odell Beckham Jr.]] takes more of the backside/boundary X reps to let Hill and Waddle roam the formation together.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): with [[Tua Tagovailoa]] out and Skylar Thompson under center, Waldman marks Waddle down a full tier to a WR3 — still startable, because Miami will feed him in the short passing game with designed plays, but no longer a set-and-forget option. He is the bigger casualty of the tier shift than [[Tyreek Hill]], who lands at low-WR1/high-WR2, while [[De'Von Achane]] benefits from the increased dump-off volume. Waldman would revisit if Thompson plays to his college flashes and the offense adjusts quickly from a left-handed to a right-handed quarterback.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14 (Age Cliffs and Dented Cans)]]) [Dynasty]: Waddle is his clearest 'dented can' of 2024 — three straight seasons of at least 1,000 yards including a 1,300-yard year two seasons ago, but only 367 yards through nine games and sitting around WR57-WR60 depending on format. Waldman sees nothing changed in what he is capable of. Adam Harstad (NOT tracked) agreed and added the strongest supporting argument: Waddle's first two seasons were the weirdest year-one/year-two split he can recall, going from a dink-and-dunk Danny Amendola role to nearly doubling his yards per reception on deep balls — 'you don't see a guy go from Wes Welker to Randy Moss from year one to year two.' That breadth of skill is value insulation, because situations change fast and you want receivers who adapt. Harstad puts him in the [[Chris Godwin]] archetype (Godwin more physical, less fast) and says he would take Waddle over [[DK Metcalf]] to start a team today; Waldman agreed.
- 2024-11-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-21]]): Harmon calls Waddle probably the most disappointing non-injury receiver of the year from a production standpoint precisely because he does not think there is anything wrong with the player — he still rates him a good player and thought 2023 was his best separation film. The problem is opportunity: Waddle is never the designed target, even back when it was only him and [[Tyreek Hill]], because Hill takes the screens and behind-the-line layups while Waddle plays the boundary X on the line. [[Jonnu Smith]] and the [[De'Von Achane]] passing-game usage have taken more off his plate in a passing game that is not voluminous. Miami made him the sixth-highest-paid receiver in the offseason, which Harmon calls insane against this usage; he stops short of saying they are scripting him out. Ranked second-most disappointing of the six, and Harmon's disappointment is with the designed opportunities, not the player. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) notes Waddle is on pace for 687 yards, 56 catches, two touchdowns and 40.4 yards per game — a career low, against 1,014 yards last year and a prior floor of 63 yards per game.
- 2024-11-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-25]]): Waldman warned that Waddle's big week will pull managers who had benched or dropped him back into lineups and 'only lead to future disappointments,' because before it he had exactly one viable fantasy week all season — week one. He put [[Tyreek Hill]] in nearly the same boat, noting week one was also Hill's most viable week until the last couple of games. The cause is that [[Jonnu Smith]] is Miami's leading pass-game weapon, leaving Waddle and Hill trading the co-star role week to week and often projecting third or lower in team receiving yardage. Waldman added that Miami has been a mess overall and that [[Tua Tagovailoa]] looks good when healthy.
- 2024-11-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-26]]): the 144-yard game (second most of his career) is real in the sense that Harmon never lost faith in the player — he has argued all offseason that 2023 was Waddle's best film season and his best Reception Perception season, beating man and press coverage consistently on the outside. Harmon says Waddle plays the hardest role in the Miami offense, which makes him a high-wire act as a producer, and that the low output has been role, not skill: [[Jonnu Smith]] leads the team in targets and is the only Dolphin above 20% first-read target share per Fantasy Points data. His prescription is more designed work and deeper targets for Waddle and less for [[Tyreek Hill]]. Koh 'buys' it.
- 2024-12-17 — According to James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-17]]: Waddle is functionally the third or fourth option in Miami's passing game behind [[Jonnu Smith]] and [[De'Von Achane]], which he calls a mistake given what has worked for this offense for multiple years. [[Matt Harmon]] added that Waddle has been banged up repeatedly, including again in this game, and that [[Malik Washington]] has given the team a little something in his absence. Neither host argued Waddle's talent had eroded — the framing throughout was that Miami's quick-game, motion-heavy structure and [[Tua Tagovailoa]]'s first-read tendency are suppressing the downfield opportunities that made Waddle productive.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-19]]): day-to-day with an injury and did not practice Wednesday of Week 16, with Thursday and Friday reports the instructive ones. The notable data point Harmon raised is that against man coverage Waddle, not [[Tyreek Hill]], has been Miami's more productive receiver at 2.18 yards per route run versus Hill's 1.93 — that is where Miami has generated a lot of its big plays for him.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-23]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon placed Waddle third in the complementary-two tier and admitted his opinion 'goes up, down, up, down' — at times he thought Waddle was just a two, after 2023 he thought Waddle looked much better on film than his results indicated, and this year was a clear disappointment with constant injuries. Despite ranking him third, Harmon said Waddle is the player in this group he is most curious to see promoted into a genuine number-one role, conceding that does not totally square with the ranking. He went further: if he were Miami and a team offered a second- or third-round pick for [[Tyreek Hill]], he would 'get out of the Tyreek Hill business' and find out what Waddle can do as the focal point.
- 2025-02-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-28]]): Waddle's 1.56 yards per route run was a career low after clearing 2.5 in each of the previous two seasons — a drop mirroring [[Tyreek Hill]]'s, which Harmon reads as an ecosystem problem in Miami rather than purely individual decline. Both hosts found it weird that Waddle was visibly deprioritized right after being paid 28.3M a year, roughly the seventh-highest-paid receiver in the league behind his own teammate. Harmon's structural point: Waddle is the on-the-ball player, effectively the X of this offense, so he gets the fewest layups while everyone else is schemed open — and he is more sympathetic to the argument that [[Jonnu Smith]]'s usage came at Waddle's expense than to the same argument about Hill. He also said Waddle's best film came in 2023, even though 2022 was the better efficiency and fantasy season.
- 2025-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) is buying Waddle again after being burned in 2024, framing last season as a clear outlier — yards per route run by year of 1.75, 2.59, 2.63 then 1.53, and points per game of 15.4, 15.3, 14.2 then 10.0. He concedes a 'Diontae Johnson syndrome' availability problem where Waddle seems to tweak an ankle most weeks, but argues Miami scoring two offensive touchdowns per game was reactive to Tua Tagovailoa's absence rather than the offense's true identity, and that the absurd low-aDOT target shares for [[De'Von Achane]] and [[Jonnu Smith]] are unlikely to repeat. Waldman agreed and added that Waddle has been held back by early slot usage and a de facto number-two role — he believes Waddle could run a Tyreek Hill role if asked, and noted he and Bob Harris (not tracked) discussed on their show that the market has become summarily out on the Miami passing game.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Harmon's explanation for Waddle's sagging production is role, not ability. In Mike McDaniel's condensed, motion-heavy offense the traditional designations blur, but if anyone in Miami is the X receiver it is Waddle, not [[Tyreek Hill]] — meaning Waddle runs the harder routes and gets essentially none of the layups, which instead go to Hill, De'Von Achane and Jonnu Smith, and that gap widened last year when the line could not pass protect. Harmon says nobody has a good answer to the question of where Waddle really stands, agrees he is a good player, and wants to see him given more of the Hill-style manufactured usage. He would find a Hill-less Dolphins receiving room genuinely interesting on the strength of his faith in Waddle.
- 2025-05-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-30]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon says he has waffled on Waddle at times but keeps coming back to the [[DeVonta Smith]] framing — a player he believes would be a clear number one on another team. He wants more Waddle and less [[Tyreek Hill]] in 2025, specifically the priority layup looks, and argues Miami should want the same because they paid Waddle heavily and he is both much younger than Hill and in the prime of his career. Koh (not tracked) notes Waddle has generated no offseason headlines of any kind beyond rehab work.
- 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]): Harmon argues Waddle, not [[Tyreek Hill]], plays the harder role in Miami and is the one Miami should be pouring into in 2025. Since Hill arrived Waddle has taken 75.1, 78.4 and 76.6 percent of sampled snaps outside and 77.9, 81.2 and 82.4 percent on the line of scrimmage — a rising trend and a flip from his rookie year (47.3 percent slot, 4.7 percent backfield, 65 percent off the line). He faces more press than Hill, who moves across the formation, runs the longer-developing routes and is fourth in line for designed targets behind [[De'Von Achane]], [[Jonnu Smith]] and Hill, while [[Tua Tagovailoa]] has released the ball under 2.5 seconds in each of the last two years — so the progression rarely reaches him. His 2024 marks were 74.5 percent versus press (up), around 80 percent versus zone (down a couple of points) and 71 percent versus man (flat). Harmon: a certified good player and a strong number two alongside [[DeVonta Smith]], but not elite and maybe not a true number one; he thinks the community has over-corrected downward. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) counters that Waddle has cracked 72 percent versus man only once in his career, which elite number ones clear year in and year out.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Waddle is the biggest beneficiary of [[De'Von Achane]] being used more as a spacing weapon than as a target hog, and Angelo's stated big prediction is that Waddle overtakes [[Tyreek Hill]] this season. He called Waddle 'a steal right now' at his current cost. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed on the value but framed it differently, saying he expects both Miami receivers to rebound rather than one overtaking the other [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-20]]): Waddle ranks fourth on the best-number-two list, and Harmon explicitly wants to buy low on 'the Jaylen Waddle experience'. 2024 charting: 71% success versus man, 79.3% versus zone (his first dip below 80% since his rookie year), and a career-best 74.5% versus press; he plays far more boundary and on-the-line snaps than people assume. Harmon thinks 2023 was his best film year even though 2022 was the more bonkers efficiency and production season, and that last year was the worst season of the top four here. The step-forward case is opportunity: [[Tyreek Hill]] doing Tyreek Hill stuff, [[Jonnu Smith]] gone, and [[De'Von Achane]] dealing with a calf injury Mike McDaniel vaguely called 'days to weeks'. The caveat is that he has not seen it yet in a healthy passing environment, so Harmon keeps him behind [[Tee Higgins]], [[Chris Godwin]] and [[DeVonta Smith]].
- 2025-08-22 — According to Cousin Josh, a recurring guest on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast]] and not a tracked expert ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waddle wins the 2025 Chipotle Award — the player you do not hate but who keeps making you sick and you cannot quit. Josh drafted him with an early third-round pick last year and cannot stop owning shares. He is still chasing the season Waddle finished as a top-seven receiver (2022) and has seen him catch 100 balls; he is telling himself a story about 140-150 targets on a two-step drop now that he is less warm on [[Tyreek Hill]], while admitting it probably will not happen. He compares it to still chasing season four of Dexter — the John Lithgow Trinity Killer year — through every later revival. He would turn down a reasonable trade offer for Waddle in his home league and will probably draft him again anyway, with the standing caveats that Waddle is always dinged up and the team is a mess.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-29]]): [[Malik Washington]] winning the third receiver job puts part of Harmon's Waddle thesis in doubt. He had expected [[Nick Westbrook-Ikhine]] to win the job — size and vertical production on his resume made him a plausible sacrificial X — which would have freed Waddle to move around the formation more. With Washington, a slot-flanker, in that role and [[Tyreek Hill]] still on the roster in his usual move-around role, Harmon now expects Waddle to remain a perimeter, on-the-line-of-scrimmage player. He was explicit that this is not a downgrade of the player: he still likes Waddle this year and would not tell anyone drafting this week to avoid him, but the alignment-change part of the bull case is weaker.
- 2025-09-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-12]]): Waddle is his number five change of mind, in the negative direction — Harris had him WR28 and has him WR44 this week, a big drop. He argues that if you believe [[Tyreek Hill]] now has a narrow path, Waddle has the same game and is the one who actually got hurt, returning to the game but leaving doubt. Guest cousin Josh (untracked) independently put Waddle on his own list, writing 'abandon ship' in his notes: he had him WR30 preseason, in the middle of his WR3 group, and now says he does not know how you can play him in any format after what Miami's offense looked like, calling their quarterback play the worst of anyone in Week 1 and predicting Mike McDaniel could be gone by Week 3 — while admitting he will still start Waddle in a deep half-PPR league just to take six points. Both note the pile-on caveat: it is one week, the media has no incentive to protect the Dolphins right now, and Harris says if Miami beats New England 51-3 they will both feel different.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest Scott Pianowski of Yahoo Fantasy (not a tracked expert) called Waddle 'a real question' at WR37, asking what the favorable path even is and whether he improves if [[Tyreek Hill]] is traded — odds he put at well under 50-50, maybe 15-25%. He had Waddle slightly ahead of [[Keon Coleman]] but moved Coleman above him during the conversation. Harris pushed the other way: Waddle is the player who produces 60-yard touchdowns, so a 4-104-1 line feels likelier from him than from Coleman, and he noted Waddle had a big game against New England, was on partial practice lists all week with a lingering week-1 shoulder issue, and was expected to play. Pianowski's broader objection is structural — he hates the Miami offensive line, does not trust Mike McDaniel and does not think McDaniel lasts the season.
- 2025-09-19 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-19]]): Waddle is a some-weeks flex you can use without feeling too terrible rather than a locked-in starter. He was invisible at Buffalo until his first target came in the final seconds of the first half — a touchdown from the three — and added a fourth-and-four crosser to open the fourth quarter. Harris's worry is decision-making upstream: on the game-ending interception from the Bills' 21, [[Tua Tagovailoa]] had predetermined the throw to Waddle, missed a linebacker leaking that way, and did not see [[De'Von Achane]] split wider and open. Harris does not think Miami has the league's worst offense and expects games where they are forced out of the short passing shell, as happened in the week two track meet with New England.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): with [[Tyreek Hill]] lost for the season, Harris is hiking Waddle in his Week 5 receiver ranks — he expects an uptick in volume but cautions that it comes with an equal uptick in defensive attention, since Waddle now becomes the coverage focus. He keeps a side-eye on the wider question of whether the Miami offense is actually good anymore, noting that the whole Monday night performance has to be graded on a curve because the Jets defense could not tackle and kept committing penalties (16 total). Later, discussing the waiver fallout with guest Jeff Erickson of RotoWire (not a tracked expert), Harris calls Waddle and [[Darren Waller]] the safer beneficiaries of Hill's injury, with [[Malik Washington]] the upside dart.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris expects Waddle to be very chalky in DFS at $5,400 because the pricing cannot account for [[Tyreek Hill]] not playing; he rostered Waddle himself. Guest Jake Trowbridge (not tracked) passed on him, which Harris took as evidence the lineup was not quite the chalkiest ever assembled.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with [[Tyreek Hill]] out for the year, Waddle is the Dolphins' No. 1 pass catcher. It was an uncomfortable watch — a third-down sideline conversion after which he limped off looking injured, back a couple of plays later with a catch to the five, a near-score in the second quarter, and a great up-the-ladder grab in the third wiped out by holding, leaving him stuck at four for 45 for a long stretch. Then Miami found something in the fourth quarter and Waddle was important on both drives, including a 46-yard bomb touchdown that Harris says offered 'a big exhale if you started him'.
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-10-09]]): Waddle's six catches for 110 yards and a touchdown without Tyreek Hill is the outcome Harmon's whole season thesis required. His argument is that Waddle's efficiency dip was structural, not talent — Miami is a heavy tight end offense, so usually only two receivers are on the field, and Waddle was almost always the outside-on-the-line boundary player who is not the first read, while Hill took the screens and layup targets off the line, [[De'Von Achane]] took the checkdowns and [[Jonnu Smith]] took the quick stuff. Tua wants the ball out fast, so that alignment starves him. With Hill out, Waddle hit a season-high 24% slot rate, which Harmon called a big deal for generating layups, and he can still win deep (the Carolina shot). He expects more intentional usage the rest of the season: Waddle is the 12th-highest-paid receiver on a per-year basis with a deal through 2029, so it is time to find out what he is as a lead receiver.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-13]]): Waddle has been one of the league's most productive receivers since Week 5, when [[Tyreek Hill]] went down — third in EPA on targets among receivers with 100-plus routes, third in yards per route run at 2.9, fifth in first downs per route run just behind [[Davante Adams]] and [[Puka Nacua]], and tenth in successful plays per route. Koh adds 29 catches for 485 yards and three touchdowns over six games, a 1,375-yard, 82-catch, 8.5-touchdown pace. Harmon's point is that Waddle was always this player and the circumstances changed — his charting last year still showed an 80% zone beater and 70% man beater, but a quick-throw quarterback, Hill's creative touches and 'Mickey Mouse' targets to Jonnu Smith and [[De'Von Achane]] buried him in the progression. He stops short of the [[DeVonta Smith]] tier but calls the role promotion overdue. Pick: over 78.5 receiving yards versus Washington, cleared in five of six.
