# Josh Downs

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): flags Downs as "certainly going to be worthwhile" specifically on the strength of [[Anthony Richardson]]'s return at quarterback, tying his outlook directly to Richardson's health and development rather than to anything new about Downs's own game.
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] score comes back almost identical to Sterling Shepard's rookie year, and every individual component (efficiency, touchdown rate, usage) lands within a tight band just above 100 — a flat, unremarkable profile with no standout trait. Read as "fair value" rather than a buy-low or sell-high: not a market inefficiency either direction independent of the [[Anthony Richardson]] health question already driving his outlook.
- 2024-05-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-23]]): Downs' rookie Reception Perception profile is outstanding despite playing hurt and catching passes from Gardner Minshew, whom Harmon calls a 'net negative' hard ceiling on any offense -- Downs still went for almost 800 yards. He posted a 93rd-percentile success rate vs. man coverage (77.5%), 82.5% vs. zone, and a 69th-percentile mark vs. press (72.7%), with green results across nearly the whole route tree except the nine route (only 5.4% of his routes, so a non-issue for a player who isn't a vertical threat). He lined up in the slot on 71.8% of snaps and off the line 78.8% of the time, but ran 25% of snaps outside and caught both of his 2023 touchdowns from an outside alignment, leading Harmon to argue he could be deployed as a flanker rather than a slot-only player. He carried an elite 72.7% contested-catch rate as a rookie (dropping only one pass all year) after posting a historic 93.8% contested-catch rate as a prospect. Per a stat from Jacob Gibbs (Fantasy Points Suite) that Harmon endorses, 331 of Downs' 771 receiving yards (43%) came on plays where he was not the first read or a designed target -- the highest rate in the NFL, comparable in target/yard share only to [[CeeDee Lamb]] and [[Puka Nacua]] -- which Harmon says projects well alongside [[Anthony Richardson]]'s tendency to work through progressions and off-script. Harmon expects [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] to remain the first-read/X option with Downs as the reliable off-script/second option. Harmon's pre-draft and current comp for Downs is Sterling Shepherd -- a good, productive player when healthy -- and he cautions dynasty managers against overreacting if Downs' box score lands around 800 yards again, noting the fantasy community over-hyped an unsustainable weeks 5-8 stretch (5.8 rec/78.8 yds per game, both TDs, an 98-catch/1,339-yard full-season pace) that faded down the stretch.
- 2024-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-11]]) [Dynasty]: Downs posted a 93rd-percentile success rate vs. man as a rookie, a good number vs. zone, and 69th percentile vs. press; Harmon dismisses the caveat that it came mostly from the slot, arguing the number stands regardless of alignment. He expects [[Anthony Richardson]] to lean on Downs as a reliable short-to-intermediate target and projects him as a slot-dominant player who can also line up as a flanker in two-receiver sets.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) picks Downs as talent held down by environment. Before his Week 9 knee injury Downs ran a 21% target share and averaged 12.5 PPR points per game without playing 85-90% of snaps, and McFarland thinks he may be the best receiver on the team over [[Michael Pittman Jr.]], whom he calls a good possession player. The problem is the Colts will run heavy multiple-tight-end sets and coaches point to [[Adonai Mitchell]] and [[Alec Pierce]] outside, so game scripts could cut Downs to 40-50% of snaps; add [[Anthony Richardson]]'s accuracy questions and pass plays turning into scrambles. McFarland says he would love Downs as a slot in a Rams/Stafford or Cousins-style pure dropback offense. Waldman expects Richardson to hit Downs for teasing big plays off pre-snap recognition.
- 2024-09-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-17]]): Indianapolis needs Downs back — Harmon says they 'capital N-E-E-D' him — calling him the best separator on the team and the receiver [[Anthony Richardson]] most requires, a right-place-right-time player who was on the same page with Richardson as a rookie in a way [[Adonai Mitchell]] has not yet been. Downs missed time with a preseason injury and Harmon flags his absence as a big deal for the whole room, since it has forced Mitchell into slot reps he is not ready for. In the projected healthy alignment Harmon sketches, Downs owns the underneath layup work, [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] takes anything intermediate, and [[Alec Pierce]] with possibly Mitchell handles over-the-top — a room he calls well constructed with four playable receivers.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Harmon says flatly that this Colts team needs Downs. The argument is mechanical rather than sentimental — [[Anthony Richardson]] goes to his first read on only 33 percent of pressured dropbacks, a bottom-half-of-the-league rate, and Downs is the designed second-read answer under pressure. Harmon points to proof of concept from Richardson's limited rookie action, where he repeatedly found Downs on those second-read plays when in trouble. Downs has not been healthy yet this season.
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): in his first game back from injury Downs played 57 percent of the snaps, behind [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] at 83 percent and [[Alec Pierce]] at 78 percent, and Harmon expects that share to rise. He led the team in first-read targets with 33 percent despite a low average depth of target, which Harmon reads as real evidence he will help the offense. Koh flags one thing to track: Downs took 25 percent of his snaps aligned as an outside receiver rather than purely in the slot. The ceiling on all of it is the environment — total opportunity is low for every Colts receiver with the team dead last in plays per game and [[Anthony Richardson]] not good enough yet for all of them to produce.
- 2024-10-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-29]]): Downs's nine-target, 69-yard touchdown game was not the fluke it looked like — Harmon shows the slot defender ([[Jalen Pitre]]) got collided with, but Downs had already won the route against man before the bust. Harmon says Downs was 'a problem' against man coverage all game: a contested slot fade off press where the 5'9, 170-pound Downs mossed a defensive back and was ruled down at the one, an uncovered post off a snap-back move Richardson never saw, and a curl against Derek Stingley Jr. He calls Downs a 'real deal downfield man-coverage-beating receiver,' not another pop-gun slot, comparing the archetype to [[Tyler Lockett]] rather than [[Wan'Dale Robinson]], and says he should be a 100-catch candidate in another world. Notable move: Harmon now says Downs is the best receiver on the Colts despite generally being a [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] fan. Caveat: the passing environment with [[Anthony Richardson]] means the numbers will not match the play.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-31]]): Downs is 'clearly their best wide receiver this year' and may be their best going forward — the only consistently positive, impactful force in the Indianapolis pass-catching corps all season. Harmon charted him for [[Reception Perception Methodology]] and said he is doing a great job getting open in the slot. He is the clearest beneficiary of the [[Joe Flacco]] change: Flacco makes quick decisions and has already shown he will get the ball out fast to him, and can also hit the intermediate throws that activate Downs as more than a 'pop gun slot receiver' — Harmon's comp is a [[Tyler Lockett]] type. He framed the second-year Downs, alongside [[Adonai Mitchell]], as the young player whose development window this Flacco stretch really matters for.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): Downs had a decent game against a Vikings defense that had been giving up the most fantasy points to wide receivers, but less than the room expected, and the tape showed a quarterback problem rather than a Downs problem — [[Joe Flacco]] underthrew him on a route that turned into an interception, and Waldman noted Downs was visibly upset with the play. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) framed the whole Colts pass game as having had too-high expectations for the Flacco change given Brian Flores's blitz packages and deep drops.
- 2024-12-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-24]]): Harmon's Christmas wish for Downs is purely durability — he jokes about giving him undetectable experimental peptides so he can stay healthy for 17 games plus the postseason. The substance underneath it is a comp revision: Harmon originally compared Downs to Sterling Shepard, and after watching Shepard pop a hamstring against the Cowboys he says he hopes he is not manifesting that injury future, adding that he actually thinks Downs has already been better in the NFL than Shepard was.
- 2025-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-07]]): 'I think he's the best receiver on this roster.' Harmon comps him to a [[Tyler Lockett]] type, notes he was targeted on almost 30% of his routes last season, led the Colts with 72 catches, and calls him incredible and super efficient — with the caveat that he has had injury issues, including one that opened the door for the [[Adonai Mitchell]] slot experiment in camp. The bigger constraint Harmon flags for the whole room is quarterback play: he does not want Indianapolis drafting another pass catcher like [[Colston Loveland]] or [[Tyler Warren]] because 'they need to get their quarterback to a point where he can throw accurate passes', not another option to throw to.
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Downs is the clearest beneficiary if [[Daniel Jones]] starts in Indianapolis — 'Josh Downs is going to eat this year for sure'. Harmon's reasoning is that Jones does not respond well to pressure, wants the ball out quick and plays frenetic, which makes Downs the layup and outlet receiver; he notes Jones made a PPR scam out of [[Wan'Dale Robinson]], and Downs is significantly better than Robinson, more explosive and can genuinely separate. Koh frames it as a PPR-scam ceiling — lots of little Mickey Mouse targets. Harmon is careful to say this is the one Colts pass catcher he feels good about in a Jones scenario: 'I don't feel great about basically anybody else if it's Daniel Jones.'
- 2025-04-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-08]]): used as the career comp for [[Jaylin Noel]] and, in Harmon's words, a player about five percent of his brain is always thinking about — one of the most efficient receivers in the league despite terrible quarterback play and a messy Colts offense. He cites Nate Tice's list of successful targets per route run among players with 300-plus routes in 2024 (TruMedia), where Downs ranked 11th, and his own charting: 75.2% success vs man as a prospect with a 93.8% contested catch rate, easily the best in the 2023 class. Harmon had him as WR4 in that class, in the same tier as [[Zay Flowers]] and [[Jordan Addison]] and ahead of [[Quentin Johnston]]; he actually went 79th overall, WR12, behind [[Jonathan Mingo]], [[Marvin Mims Jr.]], [[Tank Dell]], [[Jalen Hyatt]] and [[Cedric Tillman]] — the exact mis-pricing he expects to repeat with Noel. Comps Downs to a Tyler Lockett type who can work in-breakers and deep patterns from the slot; wonders repeatedly what he would be with a good quarterback.
- 2025-06-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-06]]): 'I like [[Michael Pittman Jr.]], I love Josh Downs' — Harmon straight up thinks Downs is the better player and the favorite to lead the Colts in catches and therefore yards, and describes him as a guy who should be on a clear upward trajectory toward a top-30 to top-35 receiver in the league. The caveat is the room: if Indianapolis successfully funnels targets to first-round tight end [[Tyler Warren]], Harmon says they are either eating off Pittman (recently extended) or kneecapping Downs, since all of them live in the same middle-of-the-field areas. He also flags that Downs is next up on his charting list, so a fuller Reception Perception read is coming.
- 2025-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-13]]): Downs 'might be the most underrated receiver in the NFL', inheriting that title from [[Tyler Lockett]], whom Harmon considers a close stylistic comp. On 107 targets in 14 games (72-803-5, 67% catch rate catching [[Anthony Richardson]] passes), his 2024 charting reads 76.7% vs man (90th percentile), 86% vs zone (92nd percentile and a top-30 mark all time, up from 82.5%) and 77% vs press (82nd percentile, up from 72.7%) — remarkable at a listed 5-9, 171 lbs, which Harmon attributes to hand-swiping technique and elite first-move quickness. His man/zone combination sits with 2018 Antonio Brown, 2016 Tyreek Hill, 2021 [[Deebo Samuel]] and 2016 Odell Beckham in the historical database. 94.4% on flat routes; the slant (76%) is the one score Harmon wants over 80, and Koh (not tracked) suspects defenses simply squatted on Richardson's first read. He is 10th in first downs per route run and 8th when lined up wide, near [[Ladd McConkey]], [[Mike Evans]], [[Nico Collins]] and [[Stefon Diggs]] — proof he can hack it outside. Only 16 routes in one- or two-receiver sets over two seasons. Sole criticism: went down on first contact on ~68% of plays in space, where McConkey breaks more tackles; that is what separates Downs from a true tier-two receiver.
- 2025-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-25]]): Harmon calls himself a huge Downs believer and a big-time Reception Perception player, and says a chunk of the RP subscriber base are 'Josh Downs bros.' Guest Cory Kinnan (not tracked) describes Downs as the Colts' gadget answer — if everything else breaks down, find where Downs is and throw it to him in the flat, on a drag or on a quick curl — and one of the two 'safety-est of safety valves' alongside rookie [[Tyler Warren]]. Both frame this as the overlap that makes him usable regardless of whether [[Daniel Jones]] or [[Anthony Richardson]] plays: Jones' game is getting the ball out quick, and Harmon says Downs is always going to be open. The stated ceiling cap is the offense itself — this is a 'Minshew minimum' projection, not a high-volume passing environment.
- 2025-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-01]]): Harmon calls Downs an ascending superstar player who gets open anywhere and everywhere on the field, and uses him as the benchmark comp for rookie [[Jaylin Noel]] — both undersized-profile receivers taken around 79th overall — saying Downs has already been a steal at that cost. James Koh, who calls himself the number one conductor of the Josh Downs hype train, agrees he is a great separator but argues [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] is the receiver who actually stirs the drink in this offense. Harmon's caveat for the entire Colts pass-catching group applies here: the personnel is genuinely good, but the quarterback situation between [[Anthony Richardson]] and [[Daniel Jones]] — with Harmon expecting both to start games — leaves the offense 'in the wilderness'.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-15]]): Downs apparently pulled a hamstring in a joint practice against the Packers. Severity was unknown at the time of recording, but Harris flagged a potential ranks lowering pending news.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon likes Downs but flagged a structural durability concern — he is already dealing with something in preseason, and at sub-180 pounds living on quick hitters over the middle, getting dinged up 'might always be the story with Josh Downs' career'. He said the [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] bold prediction can hit precisely on Downs missing time. James Koh (not tracked) also called Downs a natural separator he loves, but is out on him for 2025, wondering whether Daniel Jones will see him well and whether Shane Steichen will make him the primary read on enough concepts.
