# Shedeur Sanders

- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]): Angelo says up front he is not the biggest Sanders fan, but if Arizona traded [[Kyler Murray]] and went and got Sanders he would call it a clear upgrade of fit — Sanders can actually distribute the ball to playmakers, which Murray cannot. The caveat he names is pocket play: Sanders gets too skittish at times, and the upside is conditional on coaching him to calm down. [[Matt Waldman]] does not push back on the fit argument in the segment.
- 2025-02-27 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-27]]): [[Shedeur Sanders]] projects as a [[Ryan Tannehill]]-caliber player — good enough to win a playoff game with a really good team — and emphatically not [[Joe Burrow]] as some were claiming. Angelo poses the class-defining thought experiment: would GMs rather trade a third or fourth rounder for [[Joe Milton III]] than take Sanders in the top 10, and thinks the split is closer to even than people assume. He credits Sanders for not repeating the same in-game mistake twice, but says without upper-end arm talent — he cannot slither around and fit it in the way [[Cam Ward]] can — anything off schedule gets much harder. [[Matt Waldman]] is still deciding whether he sees Sanders as a future NFL starter; he wants to isolate decision-making plays where the design was good rather than Colorado sending everybody deep, and says his notes are leaning toward putting Sanders in the tier with Ward and [[Michael Penix Jr.]].
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]): Sanders is the more environment-dependent of the two top quarterbacks — he needs a really strong nucleus around him to play to the level he is capable of, and is less creative off structure than [[Cam Ward]]. Waldman therefore rated his best landing spot as New York if the Giants sign [[Aaron Rodgers]], where he could sit for a couple of years and develop, noting Sanders has played well with a ball winner in [[Travis Hunter]] and could do the same with [[Malik Nabers]]. If he goes to Las Vegas or New Orleans, Waldman expects him to be thrust into action way too early and to be worse for it. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) joked that Ward would agree with the assessment and Sanders would not.
- 2025-03-28 — According to Cory Kinnan (quarterback charter, not a tracked expert) with [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-28]]): Sanders' accuracy and placement 'pop off the page' — he charts 60% on deep balls over the last eight games, better than [[Cam Ward]]'s 42% — which Kinnan says rebuts the claim that he only throws at or behind the line of scrimmage. He grades the arm 'average plus,' meeting but not exceeding the NFL threshold, and notes Sanders plants and throws off his back foot rather than stepping into pressure, though his 2023-to-2024 pocket work improved markedly. He heavily contextualizes the Colorado situation: putrid offensive line, trips sets with wide splits, no bunch or condensed formations and no pre-snap motion, so almost nothing is diagnosable pre-snap. With a coordinator who gives him condensed sets and motion, Kinnan projects a Jared Goff or Geno Smith tier starter; he refuses Koh's Joe Burrow ceiling comp, calling Burrow untouchable. Harmon praises the anticipatory dig throws and says a positive starter is available in the right environment; Koh is more worried about tools and cites Greg Rosenthal's 'Andy Dalton with swag.' Kinnan also defends the perceived arrogance as what you want in a franchise quarterback. Separately, Koh believes the Giants' interest is gamesmanship to bait a trade-up, and Harmon likes him in Kellen Moore's New Orleans offense.
- 2025-03-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-31]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls Sanders an aspiring [[Geno Smith]] or [[Tua Tagovailoa]] style of passer — best as a distributor rather than a creator, though creating was all too necessary at Colorado. He has well-defined short drops, a good release, strong anticipation in the quick game, and pacing that is an asset when engineering an offense, but he is slower with quick-game concepts requiring a sideline-to-sideline read and needs to speed up same-side reads like double slants. The core flaw is leverage: he targets intermediate routes breaking back to him at the boundary when a defender is squatting over the top with the high ground, seemingly not registering the disadvantage, especially throwing from the far hash — and he compounds it by taking those risks in his own red zone, with black zone decision-making early in games that should be far more conservative than it is. Athletically he is not in the [[Josh Allen]] / [[Jalen Hurts]] / [[Lamar Jackson]] archetype; think Geno Smith and [[Baker Mayfield]], both of whom needed time to calibrate their athletic traits, and Waldman expects Sanders to arrive overconfident in his. The encouraging counterweight is adjustment speed: when coverage gets the better of him he often learns the lesson by the next half or the next week. Fit projection is a West Coast system in the Kubiak, Stefanski, McVay or Shanahan family.
- 2025-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: if forced to bet on the SAFEST 2025 rookie quarterback, Waldman would take Sanders over [[Cam Ward]] — the techniques, footwork, drop skills and timing routes are already there, making him the middle ground between Ward's low floor and [[Jalen Milroe]]'s high upside. The caveat is game management: like [[Will Levis]], there are certain coverage looks he throws into where you think 'don't do that,' and his red-zone and 'black zone' work early in games needs better management. Waldman speculates Colorado leaned on him so heavily to create that he may have skipped developing the conceptual management skills the NFL requires. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) called the read reasonable.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): the Rams at 26 are his favorite quarterback landing spot and Sanders has a chance to slide there, alongside [[Jalen Milroe]] and [[Jackson Dart]]. His reasoning is roster-based rather than about Sanders specifically: McVay and Les Snead need a successor to [[Matthew Stafford]], the 2026 class looks murky beyond a couple of names, and Los Angeles would struggle to move up for a passer without spending significant capital — so this is the year to bank one who can sit a year or two. Angelo says all three would benefit greatly from that runway. He also expects the Giants at three not to take Sanders, and thinks they should not.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]): Deion was not why he fell out of the first round — that was the lack of top-end athletic ability or a top-end arm. Falling past the second and third rounds is a combination of that, of documented studies showing Black quarterbacks get benched earlier, fall further and get fewer second chances (Waldman weights this as a smaller percentage but real), and above all of front-office fear of disruptive parents. He says teams avoid players whose families bug operations staff and create drama, and that Deion can call a press conference at any moment and command every outlet — a Tim Tebow-style clown car that only a truly elite player is worth. He calls Deion a great coach and player who did real good for HBCUs but is also a narcissist who unintentionally hurt his son; by all accounts Shedeur himself is poised, seasoned in the limelight, handled the slide publicly with total grace, and interviewed well per Waldman's non-media sources, contradicting the anonymous pre-draft reporting. Waldman graded him a second-round player by NFL norms and a third-rounder by his own, with potential to become a good starter — and would have preferred him backing up an unassailable starter rather than landing in Cleveland.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]): 'fuck it' on Sanders starting in 2025 — Waldman puts it at a 30% chance at most, and only that high because Jimmy Haslam might respond to media pressure and want a splash. The path he sketches: if Cleveland is out of contention early they may hand it to the rookie, since they are unlikely to give [[Kenny Pickett]] a look in a lost season; but if [[Joe Flacco]] gets hurt or underperforms while the team is still competitive, Pickett gets it as the more experienced option. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) pushed the odds up, arguing there is maybe a 60% chance the Browns are not contending, which would make it more like 80%; Waldman allowed it may be true but did not adopt the number. He added that from a pure fantasy perspective you should be rooting for Flacco, who showed in his 2023 comeback that he can make Cleveland's pieces relevant.
- 2025-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-16]]): Harmon thinks Sanders is a better quarterback than [[Dillon Gabriel]] and better than his fifth-round draft slot, saying he should have gone on day two — while also allowing that Sanders was overrated as a supposed first-round pick. His practical read is that draft capital still governs the roster decision: if Cleveland keeps only one rookie it is likely the third-rounder Gabriel rather than the fifth-rounder Sanders, and Harmon expects the four-way competition to look less equitable once camp starts, with [[Joe Flacco]] the likeliest starter.
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]): Sanders was his QB3 or QB4 in the RSP with a borderline starter grade — a contributor grade who could develop into a starter the way [[Kirk Cousins]] did early in his career, and Waldman says Sanders reminds him of a young Cousins, including a tendency to indulge in hero ball the way college team-leaders do. His preseason debut 'looked like a college game' with none of the coverage disguises he will face in the regular season, so Waldman discounts it, but says Sanders played the way a prepared player of his skill level should. Guest co-host Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not a tracked expert) is 'feeling it' that Sanders starts at some point in 2025, arguing all four Browns quarterbacks will start because [[Joe Flacco]] is a dead end on a non-contender and the fanbase will clamor for Sanders. Waldman calls the Cleveland QB room a disaster and hopes Sanders is the last to start and the one who sticks.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-02]]): Angelo had Sanders a full tier above [[Dillon Gabriel]] in his pre-draft tiers — essentially a starting-caliber quarterback with one or two developmental areas that will make or break his career, versus Gabriel's C-tier bridge-starter grade. The separator he named is creativity in short spaces: if Sanders cleans up his issues he can become a high enough level starter to hold a job for a long period, whereas Gabriel is an operation-based passer who suffers once the pocket gets muddy. Angelo added he does not think Gabriel is Cleveland's starter in 2026, implying the job is still open.

Harris connects it to the pre-draft scouting concern about Sanders holding the ball too long and trying to freelance, believing he's a better athlete than he is: 'in the NFL, he is most certainly not a better athlete,' and once Baltimore started blitzing him in the second half 'he had no answers.' Harris says Sanders is expected to start Week 12 against the Raiders and is skeptical he'll be ready any time soon, if ever.

- 2025-11-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman rejects the idea Sanders 'proved he should have been starting all along' or that there's a first-round-talent conspiracy -- he graded Sanders as a third-round pick, not a first-rounder, even while agreeing he was a better talent than [[Dylan Gabriel]]. He compared Sanders's movement and overall game to a young [[Kirk Cousins]], with real starter upside, and praised a cover-zero rollout throw to [[Isaiah Bond]], solid checkdown reads and clean deep-ball mechanics behind a leaky offensive line. Nothing on tape 'jumped off the page' to justify the surrounding hype, and Waldman flagged off-field concerns -- two speeding tickets in a month and comments implying the Browns are a 'shit show' -- as leadership red flags the team had hoped a redshirt year would let him mature past before he was forced into action early.
- 2025-11-25 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-25]]): in his first career start (a win over Las Vegas), Sanders posted a 9.2 adjusted yards per attempt, the highest by any Browns QB all season (no one else cleared 7.0), but the underlying efficiency numbers -- EPA per dropback and success rate -- were 'atrocious,' with much of the production coming from one excellent 50-yard bomb to [[Isaiah Bond]] and a touchdown that was a short catch-and-run behind the line to [[Dylan Sampson]]; Harmon notes a Jerry Jeudy fumble also dented the Browns' team EPA that isn't on Sanders. Harmon and co-host James Koh (untracked) both flag that Kevin Stefanski has repeatedly pulled the ball out of Sanders' hands in-game (wildcat with Quinshon Judkins even after Judkins was hurt) and question whether Stefanski simply doesn't trust him; they attribute it partly to Sanders being a third-string QB who got no camp reps with starters, and partly to Cleveland playing conservative, defense-first football with a shaky, aging offensive line. Harmon: he handled pressure well this game (unlike his preseason tendency to drift backward in the pocket) and deserves another look next week since 'none of these guys are your quarterback next year.'
- 2025-11-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-25]]): Harris had predicted the Raiders would show some pride against Cleveland but says it was 'quite the opposite' -- Sanders' limitations were too much to overcome. - 2025-12-01 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-01]]): Harris said Shedeur Sanders 'is no less limited than [[Dylan Gabriel]] was' in the loss to San Francisco, though Cleveland's offense had a plan and executed some of it -- a blown-coverage touchdown to Harold Fannin Jr. came off a shotgun play where Sanders calmly stepped up away from pressure. In the second half he got sped up and ran around the backfield more, and briefly left the game after getting fallen on, though he returned. Harris's bottom line on Cleveland's offense: 'this is [[Quinshon Judkins]]. This is nobody else. This is not a difficult equation.'
- 2025-12-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-04]]): Waldman said Sanders is playing about as well as he anticipated pre-draft, when he graded him as a 'bridge-type' quarterback who could start in spots but wasn't a long-term answer. Context matters: this is 'probably the worst receiving corps in the NFL' -- [[Jerry Jeudy]] 'absolutely horrendous,' [[Cedric Tillman]] a below-average X, [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] (praised, likened to old Marion Motley film for absorbing hits) as the de facto WR1, and undrafted Isaiah Bond effectively the best true receiver. Sanders has thrown the ball away smartly, had only one true 'hero ball' mistake (vs. the 49ers), and is clearly a level above [[Dylan Gabriel]], who 'shouldn't even be a backup' and can't threaten the third level at all -- making Sanders's play-action shots the offense's only real explosive-play equity. Both hosts compared Sanders's archetype to [[Brock Purdy]] and [[Kirk Cousins]]: a '95th-percentile' processor who can execute three-quarters of a play but lacks the top-shelf talent for the truly difficult final quarter of it, meaning he needs elite playmakers around him (like Purdy's Kittle/McCaffrey/Aiyuk nucleus) to function at a high level -- which Cleveland doesn't currently have. Verdict: not the outright 2026 starter, but he's clearly earned the right to keep figuring it out.

He did rally late with a rushing touchdown and a TD pass to Harold Fannin Jr., but Harris likens the overall meltdown to a 'Blake Bortles' performance and says Browns skill-position players should be avoided in the fantasy playoffs.

- 2026-06-25 — According to RP quarterback charter Alfredo Brown, with co-host James Koh (neither a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-06-25]]): Sanders posted a 54% success rate vs. man (well below the ~60% average) and roughly 73.6% vs. zone (still shy of 75%). Brown said Sanders tries to play the same backyard-improv style as Cam Ward but lacks Ward's arm strength and athletic ability -- he'd abandon clean pockets to run anyway, even sprinting toward his own screen-pass receiver to shorten the throw, which Brown likened to 'a teenager playing Madden.' Where Sanders excelled was taking what's given: strong success on slants and curls in structured quick-game concepts. Brown argued Kevin Stefanski's under-center, play-action-heavy scheme was 'oil and water' for him, and that the strain got worse whenever he had to sit and read through multiple progressions -- a big reason Harold Fannin Jr. became his security blanket. Brown is optimistic new OC Todd Monken's shotgun-heavy approach fits Sanders better, paired with an overhauled receiving corps (Casey Concepcion, Denzel Boston, Jerry Jeudy, Harold Fannin Jr.) Brown compared to Baltimore's Zay Flowers/Mark Andrews-Isaiah Likely setup. Bottom line: 'If I'm Cleveland, just go with Shedeur and see what you got.'
- 2026-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-16]]): Sanders' charting shows heavy reliance on in-breakers -- roughly 36% of attempts on post/dig/curl/slant routes -- plus an outlier 16% screen-pass rate (second-highest Harmon charted in 2025, behind only Aaron Rodgers; league average is ~9%). Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) both describe Sanders as a straightforward evaluation: accurate and effective when he plays on time and in rhythm, but prone to getting himself out of rhythm by trying to freelance/scramble like Josh Allen despite not having that caliber of improvisational talent ('his mouth is writing checks his body can't cash'). Koh projects him as probably a good NFL backup long-term. On the depth chart, Harmon notes reports shifted from Cleveland being committed to [[Deshaun Watson]] entering the draft to an 'even' QB battle by the end of OTAs once they saw Watson play again, and says he personally would 'start Sanders all year' given Watson is largely sunk cost, while still expecting Cleveland to use multiple starting quarterbacks this season.

The real flaw Harmon identifies is pace: Sanders holds the ball too long and can't scramble around to buy time the way he did at Colorado, so he has to speed up his decision-making, something camp won't reveal either way. Both hosts (Harris agreeing) have baked Sanders in as the Week 1 starter for their Browns rankings rather than Deshaun Watson, and Harmon says if Sanders can 'keep the boat afloat,' pass-catchers [[Casey Concepcion]] and [[Denzel Boston]] become plausible streaming options even without being true weekly starters.
