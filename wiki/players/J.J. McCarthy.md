# J.J. McCarthy

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. Team/role are as of that
> date and are almost certainly stale — treat as historical pre-draft
> scouting context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: grades him as at least a second-contract-starter prospect, with real upside if his decision-making sharpens. Flags a charting limitation up front — Michigan's offense simply didn't ask McCarthy to make many vertical/downfield throws, so there isn't much sample data on that part of his game. On tape: an "unbelievably coordinated athlete," not an electric scrambler but a competent, well-moving pocket presence with a whip-like arm; shows flashes of elite leverage reads and tight-window fits, undercut by occasional over-aggressiveness and inconsistent downfield accuracy. Draws an explicit distinction between a solid-starter outcome (his Baker Mayfield/Alex Smith comp) and a true "we're not letting you leave town" franchise outcome (his Patrick Mahomes comp), with the swing factor being whether McCarthy's confidence and decisiveness keep developing.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-01]]): Waldman flags an open re-grade in progress — needs to determine whether McCarthy's good reads of the field are "a happy accident" or intentional processing, and has real but unresolved lower-body-mechanics concerns. Fielding a direct "is he Zach Wilson 2.0" question from an outside quarterback coach, Waldman's answer was qualified: closer to the Zach Wilson people *expected* on draft night than the one who actually arrived in the NFL. Both hosts separately admit they graded Will Levis as a non-prospect pre-draft ("I did not think Will Levis was a draftable player" / "I thought he was terrible") and use Levis's rookie year as the explicit floor/median comp for McCarthy — Angelo grades McCarthy "a tier higher, talent-wise, than Levis," but still puts a Levis-like rookie season within the range of outcomes nobody should be upset about, landing his own overall grade "on that Mason-Dixon line between contributor and reserve."
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): the "happy accident vs. intentional processing" question from February 1 is still open — Waldman says he needs to watch more and revisit tape before deciding whether he sees McCarthy as a real outlier candidate in this class or as a repeat of the moderated Zach-Wilson-outcome comp already on this page.

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]): still in the "watching again" tier — the "happy accident vs. intentional processing" question about his decision-making from earlier episodes remains open. Directly answers an outside QB-connected contact's question, "is J.J. McCarthy Zach Wilson 2.0," with a qualified yes: a better iteration of what people thought they were getting with Wilson, but cautions the public's excitement is itself also "2.0" — the industry may be overrating him the same way it once overrated Wilson pre-draft. Grades him as having Wilson-like starter potential with fewer overall blind spots, but says the blind spots he does have are still real risk even as they drive his draft price up. Notes Jim Harbaugh's (his college coach — not tracked) public praise helped fuel the buzz, and separately mentions industry chatter comparing the reverse of McCarthy's polarization to how evaluators are now reassessing [[Brock Purdy]] — a player who looked replacement-level on paper but turned out better than his profile suggested.

- 2024-02-29 — According to **Kevin Knight** (Falcons reporter, SB Nation's Falcoholic — guest, not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]): says McCarthy is the only 2024 quarterback prospect likely to rise into the Falcons' range at 8th overall, but that taking him there would signal the team had already struck out on both [[Justin Fields]] and [[Kirk Cousins]] in free agency — a scenario he expects would leave Atlanta fans unhappy.

- 2024-03-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-04]]): the "Zach Wilson 2.0" hype cycle has kept intensifying, traced back to Jim Harbaugh (his college coach — not tracked) publicly calling him the draft's best quarterback. Still skeptical of the mania — feels "a whiff of desperation" from the buzz and distrusts the salesmanship framing college coaches bring to pre-draft praise ("he's the same guy who said chicken is a nervous bird... and then changed his mind"). Says McCarthy "could probably crack my top five" if his decision-making evaluation grades out as promising, but currently expects him to land outside it. Calls speculation the Chargers could draft him 5th overall "insane."

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: says he "really buys the smoke" that Minnesota has its sights set on McCarthy as [[Kirk Cousins]]'s eventual replacement now that Cousins has signed with Atlanta, seeing why Shanahan-tree offensive minds would like a QB profile like his (pointing to colleague Derek Klassen's — not tracked — scouting work on him), but expects it would require Minnesota to trade up the board to get him.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: on Minnesota's post-Cousins quarterback options, rates him merely "okay" and doubts he'd leverage [[Justin Jefferson]] vertically as well as [[Michael Penix Jr.]] or [[Jayden Daniels]] would.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: in a hypothetical-GM exercise on drafting quarterbacks, picks [[Michael Penix Jr.]] over McCarthy explicitly — "I would rather have Penix, who's less athletic at this point... who's more accurate" — arguing McCarthy's "nice footwork" and athleticism matter less than surrounding pass protection and receivers who can erase his catch-radius inaccuracies, neither of which McCarthy is guaranteed to get.

- 2024-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Feel It or F@#k It* ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-01]]), naming him his hardest player to evaluate this year: decision-making shows both high-end flashes (perfectly timed reads) and clear lows, concentrated specifically in the "black zone" (deep in his own territory) and "green zone" (inside the opponent's five) — the situational, low-margin-for-error areas coaches drill hardest. His biggest errors come there: holding the ball too long, forcing throws he shouldn't. Contrasts him with Zach Wilson and Mitchell Trubisky (not tracked), who had "invariably" low situational football with only easy highlight-reel throws as their highs — McCarthy at least shows some of the harder, more complex plays too. Open questions before the draft: can he throw accurately beyond the intermediate range, and how much of the limited deep-ball sample is Michigan's run-heavy scheme (40.4% run rate, 117th of 133 teams per Football Guys) versus the player himself — the deep-range accuracy he did show wasn't good even within that small sample.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]): asked directly whether McCarthy would have a better shot than [[Drake Maye]] at succeeding as a first-year starter, says yes — McCarthy's pocket movement and management are "a lot better in certain respects" than Maye's, even though Maye is the more athletic, tougher big-arm prospect. If forced to start one Day 1, "that's the guy I'd rather have," while reiterating he doesn't want either thrust into a starting role immediately.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]): also names him a "coach killer" candidate (see [[Coach Killer Prospects]]) despite liking him — thinks he can be a Brock Purdy-type quarterback with a bit more raw physical ability, but flags the risk as a repeat of the "Mitch Trubisky syndrome" (the well-liked, smiling prospect the fanbase falls for who doesn't pan out). Calls him "a pretty tight player that can be successful," but not a quarterback he'd trade up for.
- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]): expects McCarthy to be fine in the short-to-intermediate passing game for Denver but unproven in the deep game, projecting an 'upgraded Jake Plummer' outcome if he lands there; notes untracked analyst Cecil Lammey (via the Audible podcast) believes McCarthy is specifically the QB Sean Payton wants.
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: called Minnesota a 'super favorable' landing spot for McCarthy, citing Justin Jefferson, Jordan Addison, T.J. Hockenson and a year of Aaron Jones as supporting cast, plus Kevin O'Connell's proven scheme fit spreading the field with quick-game concepts. He said McCarthy's stock is up post-draft and ranked him among the top three or four quarterbacks in the class alongside Jayden Daniels, while acknowledging the real concern that McCarthy's limited college passing volume compares unfavorably to a high-volume passer like Bo Nix. His framing: for QBs in the 75-79 range of his RSP scoring system, landing spot and development matter more than the raw score, and Minnesota's cast gives McCarthy a strong chance to 'level up fast.'
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is buying McCarthy as Minnesota's Week 1 starter, putting the QB battle at roughly 60/40 in his favor over [[Sam Darnold]] — compared to only about 20/80 in Drake Maye's favor over Jacoby Brissett in New England. He expects McCarthy to show enough command of the short passing game and situational football (moving the chains, huddle command) in practice and preseason to win the job, with rougher moments expected in high-leverage down-and-distance situations early on. Waldman continues to call Minnesota McCarthy's ideal landing spot, reiterating [[Justin Jefferson]] finished third in the NFL in points per game last season playing with journeyman QBs — evidence a serviceable QB alone can keep Jefferson's fantasy value afloat.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): asked whether McCarthy is ready to start as he begins football drills ahead of OTAs, Waldman says 'f**k it' — not ready, though he is the type who could learn fast enough to prove that wrong. His specific reservations are a somewhat tangled game in terms of game management and open questions about him as a decision maker. On the claim that McCarthy would be QB1 in this year's class, Waldman says he would sit in about the same spot he already had him — the potential is there. The mitigating factor is the supporting cast: if [[Sam Darnold]] and his ghosts could have the season he had in that offense, Waldman errs toward McCarthy being capable of developing quickly.
- 2025-06-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-30]]): Waldman is starting to feel McCarthy as a top-12 fantasy quarterback, projecting 4,000-4,100 yards and close to 30 touchdowns, based on digging into [[Sam Darnold]]'s numbers and where Darnold's weaknesses were. The supporting case: [[Jordan Addison]], [[Justin Jefferson]] and [[T.J. Hockenson]] healthy, a run game with [[Aaron Jones]] or [[Jordan Mason]], and an offensive line now forecast as a top-10 to top-12 unit; Minnesota handled the blitz well last year, when Darnold had 12 touchdowns and zero interceptions against the blitz despite a sack rate bordering on [[Caleb Williams]] territory. Waldman expects McCarthy to play more hero ball and would not be surprised by an interception for every two touchdown passes, which matters only in penalizing scoring systems. Disagreement: co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) thinks top 12 is a bit reachy, but trusts Kevin O'Connell's offense and notes McCarthy's ADP is already rising.
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]): Waldman calls McCarthy an 'all over the place' evaluation — he makes occasional wow leverage reads, so the natural processing has high points, but he does not do it consistently and there are maturity issues in his decision-making. Waldman had him outside the top 20 early, moved him inside the top 15, and says the current reporting may push him back down. His specific worry is the red zone, where McCarthy was not a good decision-maker at Michigan and overindulged in reckless plays. He notes a split between national reporters saying McCarthy is raw and struggling and Minnesota coaches and local media saying development is going well — and says beat writers who watch the actual reps rather than quoting practice box scores are the ones worth weighting, so there is reason for concern. Waldman still leans on scheme, believing Kevin O'Connell and the surrounding talent get him to look like he has a future unless he falls apart early. His draft advice: never your first quarterback, and no longer cheap enough to be the late-round lottery ticket, so he may be off Waldman's boards. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked) says most people are running on blind faith in the O'Connell system after Nick Mullens and Josh Dobbs.
- 2025-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: from the joint-practice review, McCarthy mostly sounded good against New England but a common theme is that when he misses, he misses high — he threw an 11-on-11 interception missing his man high, which Harris says will be a real problem if it recurs in games. In the offenses segment, Harris frames Minnesota as: everyone assumes 'if [[Sam Darnold]] can do it anybody can,' so what if McCarthy can't? That roster is built to win now and the whole thing hinges on the quarterback. Guest Sigmund Bloom (Footballguys, not tracked) likes McCarthy long-term but notes he missed his entire rookie year, so the Vikings are rightly treating him as a rookie with training wheels and help, just as Michigan did — he can make the big throw, but you do not want to need him to that often.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not a tracked expert), speaking with James Koh on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]]: Minnesota's offense will not look the same with McCarthy as it did with Sam Darnold. Nguyen credited Kevin O'Connell with being one of the league's best play callers and with consistently getting the first or second read open, which helps a young quarterback, but noted Darnold's offense was not easy to execute — their air yards per target were among the highest in the league — and that Darnold had years of NFL reps plus his San Francisco tutelage while McCarthy has neither, having run a run-first offense at Michigan. With the Vikings beefing up the offensive line and adding [[Jordan Mason]], Nguyen expects a more run-oriented, [[Kyle Shanahan]]-[[Sean McVay]]-style 'training wheels' passing game — heavy play action, but fewer deep play-action shots and more bootlegs for easy completions on the edge, because you do not want to expose a first-year starter to a deep passing game.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: McCarthy is Harris' No. 2 Week 1 terror. The logical case says trust the Minnesota infrastructure — he thought the same about [[Sam Darnold]] last year and it worked — but the fear is McCarthy simply isn't ready to match what mid-career Darnold delivered, which would mean [[Justin Jefferson]] isn't automatic, no ancillary receiver becomes the next [[Jordan Addison]], and the run game gets less room. Worst case, 'it looks Zach Wilson-esque' and everyone who took Jefferson fifth overall regrets it. Erickson (RotoWire, NOT tracked) expects it to be rough early, noting Minnesota also subtracted receivers and McCarthy is 'essentially a rookie' after the knee injury, and both agreed that a year ago, given the choice, they'd have preferred McCarthy healthy over Darnold — a preference they now find funny. Both note the full redshirt year no longer exists in the modern NFL.
