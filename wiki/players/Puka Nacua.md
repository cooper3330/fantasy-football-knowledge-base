# Puka Nacua

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): after initially forgetting to place him mid-conversation, settles on Nacua as the #3 receiver in his 2023 rookie tier, ahead of [[Jayden Reed]] and [[Zay Flowers]] — "from a fantasy standpoint he's probably number one" by pure production, credits the fit with Matthew Stafford and the Rams using him flexibly as both a flanker and a slot option. Flags the longer-term dynasty question as being about quarterback timelines rather than Nacua's own play: whether the Rams have to replace Stafford, or the [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]]-topped Seahawks resolve their own quarterback situation, first — his estimate is Stafford has "two or three" years left, not five.
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: Harstad's [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] scores Nacua's rookie year at 126.1 — an entire tier above Mike Evans's 122.4 and the highest score of the whole 2023 class — prompting Harstad to expand the historic "big four" (Odell Beckham Jr., Ja'Marr Chase, Justin Jefferson, A.J. Brown) into a "big five." Waldman, for calibration, notes his own pre-draft ranking wasn't nearly that bold: he had Nacua 31st overall, at the bottom of his second tier, underrated by 14 spots relative to where he actually got drafted — a real hit, but not "the Nick Chubb of receivers," and a useful data point on how far even a well-regarded evaluator's ceiling call can lag actual outcome. Logged as a calibration item on [[Matt Waldman]]'s expert page.
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]): unqualified superstar framing after a 10/9/181/TD Wild Card game against Detroit — the 181 receiving yards set an all-time NFL rookie playoff-game record. "Tell me what he's bad at" — the only hedge either host offers is limited long speed and short-area quickness/twitchiness relative to his toughness and after-catch ability, flagged as the one trait a defense might eventually be able to game-plan around. Cited (see [[Reception Perception Methodology]]) as now separating better against man coverage than [[Cooper Kupp]].

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): the Rams graded only "justifiable pursuit," not a real need, at receiver — Nacua called a "superstar," with credit given to Sean McVay's pattern of building "clear vision" role designs for drafted receivers (and being willing to play a rookie immediately despite generally not trusting rookies in his complex offense) as the reason the fit worked so well.
- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]) [Best Ball]: skeptical Nacua repeats his rookie season, expecting defenses to now exploit his weaknesses and a [[Cooper Kupp]] rebound to cut into his role; projects both settling into similar round 5-6 best-ball value.
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon called Nacua unambiguously 'the real effing deal' and said anyone disagreeing 'is wrong' -- he's the same player fantasy managers saw last season, no caveats. He thinks Nacua is good enough and fits the offense well enough that the Rams could funnel the majority of their passing game through him if [[Cooper Kupp]] were ever out, given the lack of proven depth behind both.
- 2024-05-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-29]]): Nacua's rookie reception perception profile confirms he's a "devastating middle of the field receiver" -- 81.8% success on slants, 75.4% on digs (21.3% route participation, very high), and 86.7% on posts, with 60% of routes on the dig/curl/slant trio despite playing primarily X (50.4% of snaps on the line). Only 15% of routes were deep nine/post/corner combined, unusual for an outside X, making his profile hard to bucket -- Harmon comps him more to Hines Ward than to [[Justin Jefferson]] or [[Ja'Marr Chase]]. Overall success rates: 73% vs. man, 72.2% vs. press, but only 79.3% vs. zone (56th percentile), below the 80% benchmark and behind Jefferson's/Chase's rookie zone marks, with straight-line vertical speed a mild weakness. Co-host James Koh (untracked) frames 2024 around whether he can approach 1,400+ yards again; Harmon argues the profile itself already makes him a very good number one receiver, with a better zone score and more verticality the needed next step toward elite. - 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): called Nacua 'locked in' as the Rams' clear WR1 and the only Rams receiver worth paying up for, provided you don't overdraft him beyond the back of round one. He projects Nacua for roughly 1,300 yards, 90 catches and 6 touchdowns -- solid WR1 production -- while cautioning that [[Cooper Kupp]] (health-dependent) and [[DeMarcus Robinson]] (efficient in a complementary role) are both 'flies in his ointment' capping his target share rather than true threats to his WR1 status.
- 2024-06-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-27]]): Comparing Nacua's record-setting 2023 rookie profile (73% success vs. man, 79.3% vs. zone, 72.2% vs. press) to 2016 Emmanuel Sanders, a retired 'consummate flanker' who Harmon called one of his favorite players to watch (73.6% man, 82.4% zone, 76.3% press with the Broncos, playing opposite Demaryius Thomas), Harmon gave Nacua the overall edge despite Sanders posting better zone and press numbers. His reasoning: Nacua's route tree is far more unusual for a rookie -- 23% slants and 21.3% digs, almost no verticals (post/corner/nine routes all under 6%) -- yet he still produced special after-catch output, and Harmon said if he had a bad quarterback he'd rather have Nacua because of what he can create after short 'layup' throws. He noted Nacua's rookie numbers (105 catches, 1,486 yards) already closely mirror Sanders' best Denver season (101 catches, 1,404 yards, 9 TD in 2014), and flagged the open question of whether Nacua stays in this hyper-specific slot-heavy role or expands his route tree as the Rams' receiver room evolves.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon views Nacua as the clear WR1 in a Rams receiver room with a steep drop-off after him and [[Cooper Kupp]]; his route chart (dig and slant routes each over 20% of his charted routes) resembles a big slot receiver profile. Harmon and guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn (not tracked) agreed the Rams' retooled interior offensive line -- now the league's 3rd-heaviest (up from 23rd last year) after adding Jonah Jackson and moving Steve Avila to center alongside Kevin Dotson -- is built to dominate man-gap run concepts (LA ranked 2nd in man-gap run success rate last year), which should keep funneling Nacua into zone-beating, schemed-open looks off play-action rather than requiring heavy true drop-back volume, where Thorn rated the line only 'solid, middle of the pack' in pass protection.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): the knee tweak reported as week-to-week is not itself a five-alarm fire, but the market had already been overpricing Nacua and Waldman dropped him to WR17 on his Footballguys board — just behind a healthy [[Cooper Kupp]] at WR16 — while saying he would still take Nacua easily if he slid into WR2 range. His reasoning is volume context, not the injury: with Kupp out over the first four weeks Nacua earned 52 of his 159 targets, about 13 per game, and once Kupp returned he averaged 8.9 targets, a roughly 32 percent decline, though the two split targets nearly evenly (100 to 99) in games played together. Waldman also invoked healthy enough to play vs. healthy enough to perform, noting the returning Kupp may not have been fully himself. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) said he took Nacua over [[Garrett Wilson]] at the first-round turn in an FFPC Pros vs. Joes draft and instantly regretted it, calling Nacua's 105-catch, 1,486-yard rookie year a historic season being drafted at its ceiling.
- 2024-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-06]]): Nacua is rock solid everywhere and elite nowhere — 73% vs man, 72.2% vs press, 79.3% vs zone, all solid to very good but short of Harmon's benchmarks of 72% vs man and 80% vs zone for a great receiver, with the zone score a genuine surprise. His strengths are over the middle: 77% curl, 75% dig, 82% slant, with 49% of his routes being slants, posts and digs. Harmon calls him a tier-two receiver an offense can be run through, says the thing closest to elite is his physical toughness, and wants a more impactful full-field player on outs, corners and comebacks before pushing him higher. Both hosts note the knee injury in camp and that injuries are why he fell to day three. Harmon would not be shocked if 1,400 yards proves the apex rather than the starting point, though he could see 90 catches for 1,200 with better RP scores.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]): Nacua's injury is the reason he is raising [[Cooper Kupp]]. Waldman cautions that a Week 1 return would not mean a Week 1 Nacua — being healthy enough to be on the field and execute assignments is a different thing from being healthy enough to produce at your top level of athletic skill. He forecasts a minimum two-to-four-week stretch, possibly longer, where Nacua is not quite there, during which [[Matthew Stafford]]'s targets tilt toward Kupp and others. He does not question the talent; the take is about the shape of the ramp-up, not the player.
- 2024-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): part of the same tweeted take — neither Nacua nor [[Cooper Kupp]] finishes top-12 in fantasy scoring this year. His reasoning is situational rather than talent-based: the Rams are the most fragile offense in the league with offensive-line injuries and a suspension, and Nacua himself is an injury risk, having dealt with a training camp injury and having fallen to day three of the draft because of injuries. He projects Nacua as primarily an outside receiver in 2024 with Kupp inside.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): Nacua is going on short-term IR and will miss at least four games with what Harmon relays as a new, more significant injury to the same knee ligament (PCL) he hurt in the preseason — 'a re-aggravation or whatever, it's not great.' Harmon notes this was the exact worry from the preseason, when the knee bothered him all offseason, and connects it to the draft-day profile: not an overwhelming athlete, a very good college football player at BYU who 'never really established career momentum because of injuries,' which is much of why he fell. He also flags that Greg Rosenthal, who shares a building with Rams beat reporter Jordan Rodrigue, was pointedly saying only 'I'm fading Puka' on NFL Daily — which Harmon reads as a bad signal. Harmon's structural point: Nacua's absence removes the rugged press-man outside route runner McVay's offense requires, forcing that role back onto a 31-year-old [[Cooper Kupp]] or onto unproven bodies.
- 2024-11-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-21]]): Harmon believes he is watching another level jump from Nacua on film, pending full Reception Perception charting. Alignment is roughly 68% out wide and 28% in the slot, and the Rams are making him the purposeful number one read on concepts — visible in the motion usage they used to give [[Cooper Kupp]], where the motion man becomes the first read and gets fed. Harmon notes the raw volume is not at peak Kupp levels, but the intentional reads are. The specific improvement since his Week 8 return is ball tracking and catching away from his frame when tethered close to a coverage defender; Nacua told Harmon after the Super Bowl that he thought he led the league in drops and wanted better hands, while Harmon pushed back that his role — full speed over the middle — makes consistent catches genuinely hard. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) adds he remains a chore to bring down after the catch. Harmon also stresses Nacua's role is close to singular; the 'next Puka' comps only capture shades of it.
- 2024-12-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-10]]): after 12 catches on 14 targets for 162 yards, a receiving and a rushing touchdown against Buffalo, Harmon argues the berserk box score still undersells the tape. He asks whether five receivers in football are playing better right now and struggles to name anyone past [[Ja'Marr Chase]], saying that on pure on-field impact Nacua has been better than [[Justin Jefferson]] even while conceding Jefferson is the better player in skills and athleticism. He is second in the NFL in yards per route run against both man (4.31) and zone coverage. The leap Harmon points to is the catch point: Nacua told him at the Super Bowl he led the league in drops and wanted to improve his hands, and he is now finishing acrobatic sideline and boundary catches with elite ball tracking — the small details that move a receiver from tier two to tier one. Harmon calls him the most unique player in that group because he wins on deep overs, digs and daggers rather than the traditional single-high go route, and may chart him this week to cement the case. Koh insists heavy credit belongs to Sean McVay, whose purposeful pre-snap motion is why it works: Next Gen Stats has Nacua at 383 scrimmage yards after motion since returning in Week 8, more than double [[Jayden Reed]] at 168; Koh's comp is [[Cooper Kupp]], who he thinks 30 of 32 teams would have wasted.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-19]]): a good Week 16 spot against the Jets, who are seventh in man coverage rate and playing more single high this year under Jeff Ulbrich. Koh argued DJ Reed has been the better corner than Sauce Gardner this season, with Gardner grabby, penalty-prone and guessing wrong, plus tackling issues — not the player of the previous two years. Harmon's read: Gardner struggles with out-breaking routes if you can sell the break point, and while Nacua is a good separator and route runner, Harmon does not consider him the most deceptive route runner. If he can win at the break point — selling a go or a dig into a blaze out — Harmon expects big plays, since Nacua's biggest jumps have come at the catch point and on sideline out-breakers. Open question is what the Jets concede over the middle to a middle-of-field-based Rams offense.
- 2025-01-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-09]]): Nacua wins 2024 Best Receiver vs Zone Coverage and takes honorable mentions in both the underneath and man-coverage categories — Harmon says he 'honestly could have won' the underneath award too. Versus zone he was first in yards per route run, first in first downs per route run and first in targets per route run, with a 16.76% first-down rate per route against zone versus 12.7% for second-place teammate [[Tutu Atwell]]. He was also second only to [[A.J. Brown]] in yards per route run against man. Harmon's argument is that the mix shifted: as a rookie a large share of his production was plays created for him by Sean McVay's scheme, while in year two the balance tipped toward plays he created himself — downfield route running, a ball-winner mentality in tight coverage, the sideline shot [[Matthew Stafford]] likes to squeeze in. Harmon calls him clearly a tier-one NFL receiver. James Koh (co-host, not tracked) explicitly recants his prior worry that Nacua's rookie season might have been his ceiling, saying he made more iso plays in year two and that the concern was '100% wrong'.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): the Rams are an alpha-receiver-focused offense and the alpha is Nacua, not [[Cooper Kupp]]. In the earlier meeting with Philadelphia the Eagles were able to blot Kupp out but could not get as much done against Nacua, because Nacua is the pre-snap motion guy and moving him around defeats the coverage matchups Philadelphia wanted. Harmon's broader concern for Los Angeles is the offense around him: he doubts [[Kyren Williams]] can carry it or force Philadelphia out of base defense against a run defense allowing 4.08 yards per carry and top-ten in rush yards per game through 17 weeks, and the Eagles do not need to blitz to generate pressure on [[Matthew Stafford]].
- 2025-01-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-21]]): the Rams never felt the absence of [[Cooper Kupp]] in the playoffs because they have a bona fide number one in Nacua — Harmon says nobody was asking 'where's Cooper Kupp' with Nacua on the field. He still wants Los Angeles to add a second and third receiver around him, ideally a speed and man-beating type, and says a Rams wide receiver pick in the draft would rocket up dynasty draft boards.

The alignment case: per Next Gen Stats, Nacua lined up 51% outside and 46% as an inside receiver last year with the rest in the backfield or motion, and Harmon wants him moving — slot reps, motion, stacks and bunches to get him rolling after the catch — which Adams' ability to play the primary X enables. Koh (not tracked) counters that the same argument works with the names reversed, since you also want Adams working the slot; Harmon concedes the point. Remaining risks both flag: a shaky Rams offensive line after offseason turnover, and whether [[Matthew Stafford]]'s body holds up for a full season.

- 2025-06-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-11]]): after charting the full year-two profile, Harmon formally graduates Nacua into his six-man tier one of NFL receivers alongside [[Ja'Marr Chase]], [[Justin Jefferson]], [[CeeDee Lamb]], [[A.J. Brown]] and [[Nico Collins]] — sixth and last in that group, but in it. The 2024 line was 11 games, 106 targets, 79 catches, 990 yards, 3 TDs, down from his 1,486-yard rookie record but far better per game; Harmon says he led all 200-plus-route receivers in first downs, yards, targets and successful plays per route run. Charting: 76% success rate vs man, 85% vs zone (91st percentile), 80.5% vs press (92nd percentile) — up from 79% zone and 72% press as a rookie, and contested-catch rate up from ~64% to over 80%, exactly the improvement Nacua told him at the Super Bowl he was chasing. Harmon calls him arguably the most dominant intermediate-area receiver in football: digs/outs are 34% of routes at 80.5% and 87%, 100% on comebacks, 90% on curls, roughly 45% of his routes in that zone. He went down on first contact on only 43.2% of in-space plays and broke multiple tackles on 16%. The stated weakness is the vertical game — average on nines, below average on corners, though those are only 10.6% of charted routes, so Harmon says he is not the guy to hand a true X role to the way you can with Brown or Collins. James Koh (untracked co-host) offers a late-career [[Larry Fitzgerald]] comp; Harmon rejects any clean comp, says Nacua is not a [[Cooper Kupp]] replacement (Kupp's best man-coverage season is nowhere near 76%), and argues he makes [[Matthew Stafford]] better rather than merely being deployed well. Both expect the [[Davante Adams]] arrival to push Nacua's off-line-of-scrimmage and slot rates above the 60% / 27.1% marks in the year-three profile.

He is one of only two receivers targeted on 40%+ of routes with 4.0+ yards per route run and a first down on 20%+ of routes (the other is [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]]). Harmon has him WR3 in dynasty behind [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Justin Jefferson]] and openly wonders why he is not WR1 given he is younger and playing better; the caveat he raises is the [[Matthew Stafford]] back injury, since he doubts a Jimmy Garoppolo-level backup makes those throws. As an evaluation, he pushed back: most of Nacua's work comes against zone, where he is very good, plus reliability after the catch and usage as an extension of the run game. Waldman disputes the claim that Nacua has gotten better as a route runner — you can find isolated examples, but the way he is used does not put him in the same class of skills and concepts as Chase or [[Davante Adams]], and Waldman says Adams is helping Nacua more than the reverse right now.
