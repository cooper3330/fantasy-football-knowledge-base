# Kyren Williams

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: yes, he'd draft Williams as a running back one in 2024 alongside [[Christian McCaffrey]] as the top two backs — on the strength of scheme and volume rather than individual talent. Reviewed Brian Baldinger's line-play breakdowns and his own film: the Rams' offensive line and receivers open "huge creases" for Williams to exploit. His explicit split: "Is he a top-10 talent at the running back position? Hell no. Is he worthy of top-10 production with the run game that they have? Absolutely." See [[Scheme vs Talent]]. The read holds only "if nothing changes materially" — no marquee free-agent addition at running back, no line or coaching turnover.
- 2024-01-01 — **Waldman**, same episode, the stated risk to that outlook: if the Rams sign a power back in free agency — he names [[Derrick Henry]] as the hypothetical — his enthusiasm for Williams "would diminish noticeably." Also flags Sean McVay's history of abruptly pulling the plug on a hot hand, citing Cam Akers going from a top-three-or-four fantasy asset late in a prior season to benched by the start of the next, as precedent for how fast this outlook can flip.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman thinks concerns about rookie Blake Corum cutting into Kyren Williams's workload are overplayed for 2024, though he acknowledges longer-term concern as Corum develops. He reminds drafters Williams posted 25 touches a game and a 'league-winning' stretch after returning from injury last season, and frames his falling ADP as a buying opportunity — he's deliberately drafting teams without Williams early to get comfortable, planning to 'lean into' him as his price drops further into July and August rather than reaching for him at his current inflated cost.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman remains bullish on Kyren Williams as the lead Rams back for 2024 as long as he's healthy, noting his ADP has slipped from the tail of round one/early round two toward later value partly due to rookie Blake Corum's camp buzz and lingering foot-injury notes; he cautions not to overreact to offseason foot chatter since Williams practiced at mandatory minicamp. Waldman points to Williams' final five-to-six games last season (returning from an ankle injury) as evidence of his ceiling - roughly 25 touches per game, 'league-winning volume' - and calls any ADP dip a buying opportunity, while still expecting Corum to eventually take over as Sean McVay's next feature back by 2025.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Best Ball]: Discussing [[Kyren Williams]] going as RB8 in round three, the hosts called him overrated at that price -- his ceiling last year was only reached over his final five games on 25-carry volume, and a deep field of RBs (Derrick Henry, Travis Etienne Jr., Josh Jacobs, Isaiah Pacheco, Joe Mixon, Rachaad White) go later at a discount. Waldman compared the Williams/Corum dynamic to 'the Priest Holmes-Larry Johnson situation' -- a lead back with a talented, cheaply-drafted backup ([[Blake Corum]], RB35/round 9) who could get 80% snap share if Williams is hurt. He noted Sean McVay tends to lean on one back, but last year's 4-game Williams absence (3 losses, offense sputtered without him) shows real risk, and expects Williams used more as a receiver and less as a runner than in year one, with Corum taking change-of-pace work.
