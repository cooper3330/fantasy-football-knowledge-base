# Kyren Williams

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: yes, he'd draft Williams as a running back one in 2024 alongside [[Christian McCaffrey]] as the top two backs — on the strength of scheme and volume rather than individual talent. Reviewed Brian Baldinger's line-play breakdowns and his own film: the Rams' offensive line and receivers open "huge creases" for Williams to exploit. His explicit split: "Is he a top-10 talent at the running back position? Hell no. Is he worthy of top-10 production with the run game that they have? Absolutely." See [[Scheme vs Talent]]. The read holds only "if nothing changes materially" — no marquee free-agent addition at running back, no line or coaching turnover.
- 2024-01-01 — **Waldman**, same episode, the stated risk to that outlook: if the Rams sign a power back in free agency — he names [[Derrick Henry]] as the hypothetical — his enthusiasm for Williams "would diminish noticeably." Also flags Sean McVay's history of abruptly pulling the plug on a hot hand, citing Cam Akers going from a top-three-or-four fantasy asset late in a prior season to benched by the start of the next, as precedent for how fast this outlook can flip.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman thinks concerns about rookie Blake Corum cutting into Kyren Williams's workload are overplayed for 2024, though he acknowledges longer-term concern as Corum develops. He reminds drafters Williams posted 25 touches a game and a 'league-winning' stretch after returning from injury last season, and frames his falling ADP as a buying opportunity — he's deliberately drafting teams without Williams early to get comfortable, planning to 'lean into' him as his price drops further into July and August rather than reaching for him at his current inflated cost.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman remains bullish on Kyren Williams as the lead Rams back for 2024 as long as he's healthy, noting his ADP has slipped from the tail of round one/early round two toward later value partly due to rookie Blake Corum's camp buzz and lingering foot-injury notes; he cautions not to overreact to offseason foot chatter since Williams practiced at mandatory minicamp. Waldman points to Williams' final five-to-six games last season (returning from an ankle injury) as evidence of his ceiling - roughly 25 touches per game, 'league-winning volume' - and calls any ADP dip a buying opportunity, while still expecting Corum to eventually take over as Sean McVay's next feature back by 2025.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Best Ball]: Discussing [[Kyren Williams]] going as RB8 in round three, the hosts called him overrated at that price -- his ceiling last year was only reached over his final five games on 25-carry volume, and a deep field of RBs (Derrick Henry, Travis Etienne Jr., Josh Jacobs, Isaiah Pacheco, Joe Mixon, Rachaad White) go later at a discount. Waldman compared the Williams/Corum dynamic to 'the Priest Holmes-Larry Johnson situation' -- a lead back with a talented, cheaply-drafted backup ([[Blake Corum]], RB35/round 9) who could get 80% snap share if Williams is hurt. He noted Sean McVay tends to lean on one back, but last year's 4-game Williams absence (3 losses, offense sputtered without him) shows real risk, and expects Williams used more as a receiver and less as a runner than in year one, with Corum taking change-of-pace work.
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]): he's 'a little bit lower on Williams in the market this year' despite acknowledging Sean McVay's clear preference for him. Two market trends worry him: top-12 ADP RBs with poor prospect scores hit at a worse rate historically, and Williams scared him with a slow, poor combine; and top-12 RBs with a backfield teammate drafted inside the top 48 at the position also underperform more often -- the Rams used a Day 2 pick on Blake Corum, whom JJ called the closest statistical/size comp to Williams in this year's class. He'll roster Williams in some leagues but 'not everywhere.' Waldman countered that Williams played well but credited the Rams' offensive line even more, and argued Corum is actually the more talented between-the-tackles runner and decision-maker -- subtleties that won't matter as long as Williams remains the clear starter.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): Williams's big game was enabled by a real personnel shift, and Waldman loved it — the Rams used 12 personnel on 44.6 percent of snaps, their highest usage since 2022, for a team that is normally one of the highest 11-personnel clubs in the NFL, dusting off Hunter Long to do it. He read the change as Sean McVay openly admitting his receiver corps cannot be leaned on the way it was before, so the plan became two tight ends and win with Kyren Williams — done while down 14-0, in a game they came back to win behind [[Matthew Stafford]]. Waldman said he usually has McVay on his dartboard but owed him an apology here, and revised his standing 'a running back drafted in Los Angeles means nothing' beef: the problem may be less McVay than the GMs and scouts who keep drafting backs knowing McVay rolls with his starter until he cannot. He credits that loyalty rather than criticizing it.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): asked whether [[Blake Corum]]'s snap uptick (11 snaps, five carries for 25 yards and a catch for eight against Green Bay, after roughly eight snaps across the first four games) is the start of a decline for Williams investors, Waldman said no. Williams is being counted on for pass protection, and Waldman highlighted two plays in that game where Williams took on a defensive end and a defensive tackle in protection — 'really cool looking stuff' showing how reliable he is. He is also a smart runner playing with good technique. Waldman expects Corum to eventually cut into the workload but sees the Rams feeding Corum more in the Ronnie Rivers role, and notes he has found Sean McVay's running back usage baffling for six years.
- 2024-11-22 — According to Adam Harstad, untracked co-host ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]) [Dynasty]: Williams is his other canonical waiver-archetype hit — a late-drafted back with no rabid fanbase, whose profile everyone wanted to argue about. Harstad's response was that of course the profile has warts, that is why he fell; the thing to weigh instead was that the organization liked him, supported him publicly and behaved as if his roster spot was secure. He also volunteered the counter-example against himself: he cut Williams to hold [[Rashid Shaheed]], which bit him big time, and grouped Williams with [[Jaylen Warren]] and [[Jerome Ford]] as the same type of later-round, organization-loved back.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]): with Williams finishing RB7 on the year (a little lower in points per game but comfortably top ten), Waldman says he probably drafts him as an RB1 again and adds that he is 'starting to accept that he might be the Priest Holmes of this generation' — while noting he thinks Holmes was the better player. His argument is that Williams has demonstrated and proved his mettle after already fending off [[Blake Corum]] once. The caveat he volunteers himself: he loves Corum, and it is only a dinged-up game or two away from Corum getting the chance — if any back in that class can Wally Pip Williams it is Corum — but he is not holding his breath for that in 2025.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): Waldman and co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) treated the [[Davante Adams]] signing as probably a plus for Williams rather than a threat, with Waldman not expecting Adams to eat so far into the touchdown total that Williams owners should be dismayed. Bob Harris expected Williams to hold roughly his RB10 ADP, noting the parallel to the discount Williams got a year earlier on the arrival of [[Blake Corum]].
- 2025-06-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-02]]): he is feeling the RB12 price and endorses the RB8 projection as a good fit. The short list of backs he would take ahead of Williams is [[Saquon Barkley]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], [[Josh Jacobs]], [[De'Von Achane]], [[Bijan Robinson]], [[Derrick Henry]] and [[Christian McCaffrey]] — plus [[Nick Chubb]] if he were being unrealistic — and that is it. On the committee worry, Waldman acknowledges people are concerned about [[Jarquez Hunter]] and [[Blake Corum]] making the backfield changeable, but argues the fact that the Rams are even discussing a contract extension with Williams tells you what they think of him, and that he is going to be the guy this year.
- 2025-08-15 — According to Cousin Josh (untracked co-host of the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast]]) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-15]]): Williams was his number 1 frag player last year and is deliberately off the 2025 list — he says he loves Williams at ADP, will take the new contract and the organizational commitment, and calls himself way more bullish than bearish. [[Chris Harris]] said he is fine with Williams in round three and would consider him there in a standard league, ranking him somewhere in the early teens at the position. Josh kept a small hedge, saying he still thinks he gets his revenge eventually and that Williams could be a 1-1 for him after this season, but he will not put a player he can draft in round three on a frag list because it does not help anybody. Both noted Williams has been phenomenal near the goal line.
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Williams is his round-three safe pick — 'who's safer than Kyren Williams?' The last bear case was that the Rams treat backs as disposable razors and drafted [[Blake Corum]] and then [[Jarquez Hunter]], but the sizable new contract punctures it, because even if money should not matter to a team with infinite dollars, that is not how NFL teams actually behave. Harris says the setup is terrific: [[Sean McVay]] has pivoted to building the interior offensive line and running three receivers who can block, drawing light boxes and pounding the ball between the hashes, and Williams at 5-9, 194 somehow thrives there on vision and decisiveness while being hard to find on interior runs. Even shaving eight touchdowns off his 16 leaves a high-floor third-rounder. His own caveat: an injury is probably coming given seven games of 25-plus touches in two years, though he refuses to call any back high-risk purely for injury odds.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): Harris calls Kyren the back who embodies the weirdness of rating risk — you keep waiting for the beating to catch up with him and he keeps taking 25 touches and getting up — and rates him a 3 out of 10. Guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates him a 4, citing the [[Matthew Stafford]] injury, competent competition behind him for the first time ([[Blake Corum]] had a very good camp and Bell rates [[Jarquez Hunter]] highly), and a receiving-role ceiling that Sean McVay has hinted at expanding but has not — in PPR, Kyren is not catching as many balls as his peers. Bell's defense of the player: McVay loves him like a second son, and he does everything at the position flawlessly even though he is not the 'zoom zoom' sparkly package that [[De'Von Achane]] is.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): still Kyren's gig all the way. He had a carry down to the one late in the second quarter, and after the Rams inexplicably tried sneaking a sore-backed [[Matthew Stafford]] into the pile on third and goal and got stuffed, they went for it on fourth and one and pitched left to Williams, who scored easily. [[Blake Corum]] played 10 snaps with two touches and rookie [[Jarquez Hunter]] was a healthy scratch.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-22]]): asked whether the Rams backfield is becoming a committee after Williams' 20 carries for 94 yards against Philadelphia and [[Blake Corum]]'s 8 for 53, Waldman said he is 'slightly feeling that' — not ready to say no. Sean McVay has ridden Williams heavily for the past couple of years, but Waldman believes Corum has earned begrudging respect and will keep earning more work. He does not expect an even split, and if Williams cannot stay healthy the Rams may have to do gymnastics to figure out the lead role. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) said he asked Jake Ellenbogen of Downtown Rams the same question on his radio show and got the same read — still the Kyren Williams show, with side orders of Corum and red-zone tidbits.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): shared the load with [[Blake Corum]] early — the very good Rams interior offensive line was mashing with both backs — but when it got close it was a lot more Williams, who finished with 48 snaps to Corum's 20. He had a bad fumble on the first play after the Colts took the lead in the second half and was not punished for it; in fact Sean McVay went over after the game and made a special point of telling him he is a stud, so Harris would not expect any benching or committee punishment here. Harris rates the Rams as really good, a blocked field goal and a crazy Eagles comeback away from being undefeated.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris said that as games go along, when the Rams need it they still go to Kyren Williams, which is why [[Blake Corum]]'s rising touch count has not yet changed the hierarchy. Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) suggested a projected blowout against the depleted 49ers could mean a lot of both backs. Harris also mocked the fantasy-manager habit of insisting they would be smarter than Sean McVay about giving the ball to Corum instead of Kyren Williams — a preference that only exists because you started Corum.
- 2025-10-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-03]]): Harris lays a large share of the Rams' Thursday loss on Williams — a drop, then a fumble at the three-yard line on a power run he 'should be in' on, and then a third-and-six catch from the Rams' 11 that he had to come back for and went down short, setting up the fourth-and-one stuff that ended the game. Harris defends Sean McVay's fourth-down call despite McVay telling reporters afterward it was terrible, arguing McVay is taking the flak: for three-plus years the Rams have been able to line up and run power with an interior offensive line that simply beats you, and that identity is the core of how this team rose from 5-12 in 2022. They just didn't win the rep.
- 2025-10-17 — According to co-host Josh (not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-17]]) [Redraft (Standard), Dynasty]: As part of the Rams' concentrated-target-share argument, Josh said he 'wouldn't even mind' buying Williams cheap right now -- not calling him an RB1 outright, but noting that if something happened to [[Puka Nacua]], Williams becomes a set-it-and-forget-it fantasy start given how tightly McVay's offense funnels volume to its three core skill players, comparing the situation to [[Rachaad White]]'s plug-and-play role behind Tampa Bay's passing attack.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris called Williams 'the hard-luck loser' of the Rams' blowout of Jacksonville -- he was stuffed from the one-yard line on two separate occasions, including one where he appeared to get in but was ruled down and the Rams didn't bother challenging, opting instead to throw a touchdown to [[Davante Adams]] since the game was already in hand. His touch share and goal-line work got siphoned by the passing-game script rather than any change in his role.
- 2025-10-30 — According to Jake Trowbridge (not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-30]]): Trowbridge admits he 'caught himself forgetting about' Williams ($6,800 DFS) despite a consistent season outside of a rough Week 7 against Jacksonville. He frames the disappointment around Williams not as a talent issue but as Blake Corum eating into what fans expected to be a near-workhorse share -- 'why is Blake Corum playing so much? I'm supposed to get 89% of the carries here' -- calling it evidence that bell-cow roles are increasingly rare across the NFL.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): a pointed complaint about role. The Rams repeatedly drove to the one-yard line and then ran quarterback sneaks — 'jerk-face touchdowns' — with two more going to [[Davante Adams]] and one to Tyler Higbee on play action, so 'if you got Kyren Williams, you're just like, come on man, this is not used to be the plan, but it is the plan this year'. Williams did eventually pay off with a short touchdown in the fourth quarter, and Harris found it notable that [[Blake Corum]] handled an entire long drive in the third quarter. Context: [[Matthew Stafford]] is being pushed toward an MVP with four touchdowns of fewer than three yards and looked for a fifth on that same drive.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): the Rams essentially named their number on a depleted 49ers defense and scored 42, and Williams took two rushing touchdowns in easy spots. Harris notes the Rams seemed content taking rushing scores rather than padding [[Matthew Stafford]]'s MVP case, and that [[Blake Corum]] mixed in for most of the third-quarter opening drive, getting them into the red zone and drawing a stuffed carry at the one.
- 2025-11-24 — According to guest Sam Gold (Sam's Film Room, not a tracked expert) and Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Against Tampa Bay, all three of the Rams' first-half trips inside the five became play-action passing touchdowns (to Davante Adams and Colby Parkinson) rather than Williams runs, which Harris called Sean McVay chasing a Matthew Stafford MVP campaign at Williams's expense. Williams has 'made a living' fantasy-wise on one-yard touchdown runs the past two years, and Harris said some of those short scores are now clearly being scripted to receivers instead.
- 2025-12-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-01 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman called Kyren Williams a 'really solid player' and gave him the benefit of the doubt over [[Blake Corum]] in the Rams' backfield split, while acknowledging both offer similar short-area explosion and that Corum has earned an increasing share of the work.
- 2025-12-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-08]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said Williams 'looked fantastic' in the same game as [[Blake Corum]] and is quick and decisive, but rated him a slightly less dynamic and less creative athlete than Corum in how he uses his agility.
- 2025-12-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-08]]): Williams remains the clear lead back in the Rams' offense over [[Blake Corum]], scoring from the five in a blowout of Arizona as the Rams ran through, over, and around a defense that couldn't stop the ground game out of any personnel grouping. Harris said Williams keeps most of the workload even as Corum's role has grown enough to make the touch split feel closer than it used to.
- 2025-12-09 — According to Chris Harris and Jeff Bell (Football Guys, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-09]]): Williams will be out for Week 15, elevating Blake Corum into lead-back flex consideration against the Lions in what both hosts view as a potential shootout.
- 2025-12-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-12]]): Harris said that if forced to pick his 2026 RB12, he'd lean Williams over [[Ashton Jeanty]], citing Williams's new contract as proof the Rams are committed to him as the clear lead back -- 'they're not going to do disposable razors with running backs in LA' -- despite Blake Corum's big week. Harris framed Williams as a safer, more proven bet than Jeanty given how uncertain the Raiders' coaching and O-line situation is heading into 2026. [Dynasty]
- 2025-12-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-15]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman expects the Rams to continue splitting work between Williams and [[Blake Corum]] into 2026 rather than returning to a true Williams-led backfield -- both backs play at a similar intensity and don't need heavy volume given a strong offensive line and Matthew Stafford's game management, and Los Angeles is scoring well enough that there's little incentive to change course. He said only a hidden injury to Corum would push the touch share back toward Williams.
- 2025-12-15 — According to Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (untracked guest on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-15]]) with [[Chris Harris]]: Williams played 37 snaps to [[Blake Corum]]'s 32 in a high-scoring win over Detroit. Doherty says Williams remains in the RB12-15 range with a 'floor to ceiling' profile rather than spike-week upside, but that floor has stayed solid enough that he never considers benching him, even as Corum's role and touchdown equity grow with [[Davante Adams]] likely out.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris ranks [[Kyren Williams]] RB10 and called him a potential flag-plant target there, but flagged the recurring worry that he and [[Blake Corum]] were 'awfully interchangeable' for stretches of last season and that the Rams could shift the workload (as they once did toward DeAndre Swift over Cam Akers) without much warning. Harris noted the Rams gave Williams real money via a new deal, making a full change of plans less likely, and that the Rams' offense overall is one of the few he trusts. He said he's never been outright negative on Williams, just cautious, and expects both him and [[Blake Corum]] to be used and usable.
- 2026-07-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris says he's higher on Kyren than the broader market, attributing the gap to other analysts being more scared of [[Blake Corum]] eating into the split than he is. He argues Kyren is meaningfully more valuable for fantasy than Corum because of receiving work -- Kyren ran 350 routes last season (more than three times Corum's 116) and was 'meaningfully more efficient' as a receiver, and the Rams also value him as a pass blocker. Harris said nothing short of a heavy August drumbeat of Corum taking over passing-down work would change his read, and he doesn't expect camp noise to move his ranking of the two Rams backs relative to each other.
- 2026-07-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-27]]) [Dynasty]: despite reports the Rams will lean more on [[Blake Corum]], Waldman still favors Williams at his round-3/RB15 ADP given the Rams' strong offensive line (with Alaric Jackson healthy) and Matthew Stafford at QB, which he says gives Williams efficient, predictable volume compared to riskier backs like Javonte Williams or Cam Skattebo. Waldman projects Williams at ~230 of the backfield's 415 carries for ~1,075 yards and keeping ~84% of the RB receiving work (39 catches to Corum's 7). Bob Harris (not tracked) says the shifting Corum usage is 'enough to hesitate' on Williams at ADP, though he'd still take him in many draft settings -- the two hosts land in slightly different places on how much to discount him.
- 2026-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Cousin Josh (untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-14]]): Harris moved his risk score from a 2 last year up to a 3, and Josh gave a 4, both citing [[Blake Corum]] getting more involved as a mild week-to-week threat to Williams's workload. Josh, self-described as historically 'the Kyren hater,' says he's come around some -- Williams 'has a nose for the end zone' on a strong offense -- but both would still rank him well ahead of his ADP slot (27th overall) despite the added risk.
