# Calvin Austin III

## Expert Takes

- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): named Austin as what people thought Wan'Dale Robinson would be -- he called Austin the better of the two -- saying Austin has impressed every camp and could be the next Doug Baldwin for Russell Wilson: a small, explosive route-runner who wins contested catches and is the fastest player in Pittsburgh's receiving corps. If Wilson builds rapport with him, Waldman sees Austin as a cheap bet to lock down real playing time as Pittsburgh sorts out its WR2 spot behind [[Denzel Mims]] and Van Jefferson.
- 2024-07-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-04]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said Austin was the same physical archetype as [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] at Memphis but the better player -- more capable against man coverage, able to win on the outside, and slightly faster -- though injuries and a carousel of poor quarterback play limited him to flashes as a rookie and last season. Angelo compared Austin's size/speed/contested-catch profile to Tutu Atwell and argued the swing factor is scheme: if Arthur Smith borrows Sean McVay's motion-heavy usage of similar players, Austin could become a dynamic movement-Z threat, but Smith's run-first, low-creativity tendencies (cited: minimal passing-game use of Bijan Robinson) instead favor [[Roman Wilson]], a blocking-first 'dirty work' receiver, as the better scheme fit for now. Both flagged Austin's usage as a real swing factor for his 2024 outlook.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Asked to revisit a question from the Fantasy Football Expo, Waldman said he's 'feeling it' on [[Calvin Austin III]] at his current price, calling him a quicker, faster version of Wan'Dale Robinson who wins more consistently at the boundary in man coverage. He noted Austin has been hampered by injuries and poor QB play in Pittsburgh, and that new OC Arthur Smith rarely motioned receivers in Atlanta only because Desmond Ridder struggled to read the field -- with [[Russell Wilson]] under center, Waldman thinks Austin could be used more like a 'wannabe Doug Baldwin.' He flagged this as a change from his prior, more skeptical Expo answer, and noted the WR2 job behind Austin (Van Jefferson vs. others) is still unresolved.
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Reacting to a report (Mark Kaboly, The Athletic) that Calvin Austin III had an impressive camp, Waldman agreed it's legitimate and not just a small-pads mirage -- his one-on-one outside ability for a smaller receiver has shown up on film in pads before, both in Pittsburgh camps the past couple seasons when healthy and back at Memphis. He expects the staff to find him a role and believes [[Russell Wilson]] will like him enough to target him all over the field.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]) [Best Ball]: Austin is Waldman's late-round bet in Pittsburgh. He argues Austin and CFL standout Khalil Pimpleton were always better than [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] at the same physical profile — Austin more explosive, and unlike Robinson able to win one-on-one outside in college, beat press, go up for the ball and run vertical routes. With [[Van Jefferson]] unreliable at the catch point and [[Roman Wilson]] unready, Waldman thinks Pittsburgh may end up leaning on the guy they discount for being short and small; Russell Wilson himself compared Austin to [[Tyler Lockett]], and Waldman's own comp is Doug Baldwin. Arthur Smith's heavy motion usage, McFarland notes, is exactly the design that would unlock him. McFarland has Austin projected for essentially nothing and isn't recommending him, but says he may sprinkle him into last-round best ball picks.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): Austin is a permanent high, not a temporary one — Waldman's answer to the 'feel it or f**k it' prompt was emphatic. He liked the routes Pittsburgh featured him on in Week 3, specifically a lot of work over the middle plus a nice catch on the boundary. The upside case is quarterback-proof in his read: Austin is a good match for what [[Russell Wilson]] does well and Wilson's and [[Justin Fields]]'s skill sets have pretty good overlap, so a midseason switch — which Waldman still thinks is possible because Fields has had 'Justin Fields moments' alongside the good ones — would not break him. He pegged Austin as having a shot at WR4 fantasy value this year, which he says has real value. Bob Harris (untracked) argued Van Jefferson's absence benefited Scottie Miller more than Austin, and wondered when rookie [[Roman Wilson]], a healthy scratch this week, gets on the field.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: asked whether Austin becomes a [[Russell Wilson]] favorite, Waldman said 'feel it.' Austin was targeted early, had 'a really just bad drop' on third down, then finished with a 36-yard catch on 'a [[Tyler Lockett]]-like crossing route that you would see with this offense.' Waldman's structural argument is that Wilson's play-action volume, varied personnel sets and ability to execute the game plan will keep finding Austin in the open field: 'I would say that Austin is probably weapon number two among the wide receivers in this offense, and we're going to see that grow.' Van Jefferson scoring in the same game did not change that hierarchy for him.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31]]): Austin 'suddenly looks like the player he should have been' with [[Russell Wilson]] throwing to him. Waldman notes Wilson compared him to a young Tyler Lockett in camp and that Mitchell Trubisky flagged him as the guy to watch two years ago before a hamstring injury derailed him. The usage matters: Pittsburgh is deploying him in tight formations alongside [[George Pickens]], so defenders have to declare a shade of the field and the route breaks opposite it, generating good matchups — and Waldman notes Austin can go up and get Wilson's moon balls despite his size.
- 2024-11-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-25]]): Austin is right on the border of becoming a viable weekly option and is exactly the end-of-roster player to rotate on and off a fantasy bench, or to pick up off waivers in a good week. The bull case is trust — [[Russell Wilson]] keeps giving him meaningful deep targets and clearly believes in him, including a clean throw in heavy snow — versus [[Van Jefferson]], who has produced but mostly on wide-open looks and busted coverage. Waldman's caveat: this is Arthur Smith's offense, so the shorthand skepticism about volume is probably right, and Austin and Jefferson may keep splitting targets because Smith has rapport with Jefferson. A quality-of-target bet, not a volume bet.
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]): feeling him, and he has been starting Austin himself for the past few weeks in larger lineups. The case is [[Russell Wilson]]'s trust on shot plays — third-and-six deep looks, and vertical or red-zone targets inside the 30 on third down, including a roughly 24-yard slot post for a touchdown. Waldman sees him as the established shot player opposite [[George Pickens]] and working the middle of the field, and says that as long as the concussion he suffered clears in the next couple of weeks he is a roster and very possibly a lineup player. Bob Harris (not tracked) agreed depending on league depth, noting Austin is a listener favorite and that Ben Skowronek would fill in less capably during any absence, which is not expected to be extended.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]) — 'feeling it' that Austin leads Pittsburgh's receivers in 2025. The reason is usage: they play him inside and outside, and you can see why [[Russell Wilson]] compared him to [[Tyler Lockett]]. Waldman's projection is that if Wilson stays, [[George Pickens]] becomes the DK Metcalf of this offense to an extent and Austin emerges as the Lockett — 'people realize holy shit this guy's pretty good' — and he thinks they have only started to tap the potential. Risk noted: Pittsburgh may draft another receiver. He is not worried about [[Roman Wilson]] blocking him.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): pushing back on the assumption that [[Roman Wilson]] simply takes the job because of superior draft capital, Waldman argued Austin has also been limited by this offense, and that in the moments when Pittsburgh has actually fed him the ball he tends to show up. He is not conceding the role to Wilson on pedigree alone.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Harmon says he may chart Austin this season, having liked him a good bit as a day-three sleeper coming out with a solid showing in his rookie roundup. He stresses the size — listed 5-foot-9, 162, which Harmon and Koh both find remarkable for an NFL receiver — but notes the speed and that Austin actually played X receiver at Memphis, which was never going to translate to his NFL usage. Koh (not tracked) has him as a roughly 50/50 inside-outside player last year and sees Pittsburgh as a team likely to live in two-receiver sets with a real slot need; Harmon has heard the Steelers really like Austin. On [[Roman Wilson]] both agree there is almost no data after an injury-lost rookie year; Harmon saw a speed-slot profile good on in-breakers off play action, but was lower on him than the market, calling him a day-three rather than day-two prospect.
- 2025-06-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he is 'feeling' Austin as a fantasy-relevant player in 2025, because Austin joined Pittsburgh during a bleak quarterback era and has never played with a passer who works the middle of the field or makes multiple reads in structure — [[Russell Wilson]] was okay at it but his game was really one-on-ones outside and off-schedule improvisation. [[Aaron Rodgers]] reads and manipulates the middle and will throw into tight windows, all of which fits Austin. Waldman compared him to a young [[Tyler Lockett]] or a prime Randall Cobb — and said he is essentially what Giants fans thought they were getting in [[Wan'Dale Robinson]], who instead became a gadget player averaging about seven yards per catch on 120 targets. Conservative projection: 75-80 targets, near 50 catches, close to 800 yards and five scores — WR3 territory, with WR2 upside if [[Robert Woods]] has nothing left or [[Roman Wilson]]'s OTA hype does not convert.
- 2025-06-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the show's original question was whether [[Robert Woods]] would overtake Austin for Pittsburgh's number two receiver role, and the [[Jonnu Smith]] trade made Waldman answer 'fuck all that' — with Arthur Smith's tight-end-driven template plus [[DK Metcalf]], he sees Austin, Woods and [[Roman Wilson]] as the ones who lose. He points to 2023 Atlanta, where only Drake London cleared 30 targets among receivers. Disagreement: co-host Bob Harris (Footballguys, not a tracked expert) refused to write Austin off entirely, saying they get bumped down for sure but he would still take a late chance on Austin as he falls — a stance he admitted would surprise zero listeners. Waldman later said [[Malik Washington]] is his new Calvin Austin.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he pushed back on Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) writing Austin off after the [[Jonnu Smith]] trade. Waldman's math: if [[Aaron Rodgers]] throws for 4,000-4,100 yards and 30-32 touchdowns — which he came within about 100 yards of with the Jets — that is still outside top-15 quarterback production, but spread around it leaves room. [[DK Metcalf]] has never had more than about 120 targets and is a 100-120 quick-hitting-route player, [[Robert Woods]] is old and banged up, and nobody talking about [[Roman Wilson]] tells you he may not last. Waldman projects roughly 80 targets for Austin, a 60% catch rate, 48-50 catches, 750-800 yards and five touchdowns as the main deep threat — WR3 value. He pledged to buy a Steelers Austin jersey if he hits 1,000 yards.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Film & Theory Sequences, Signal, Steelers)]]): [[Calvin Austin III]] is one of the open questions in the Steelers passing game — Waldman asks whether he and [[DK Metcalf]] are conceptually advanced enough to handle what [[Aaron Rodgers]] demands at the line of scrimmage, since Rodgers historically asks more of receivers conceptually than they have experience with. Waldman is mildly optimistic: Austin is young, had his best season with an actual starting quarterback in Russell Wilson, and should feel he is getting an upgrade this year. The stated upside case is that if Austin or Metcalf shows he is more than what has been seen so far, the offense could be sneaky good; the risk is that neither does and Rodgers is left with [[Pat Freiermuth]] as his only reliable conceptual partner.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): yes, he remains a weekly starter as long as he is the WR2 in that pecking order. Pittsburgh has figured out how to use him — [[Aaron Rodgers]] hitting him on back-shoulder plays, exactly what he showed at Memphis as, in Waldman's phrase, a better version of [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] that nobody understood — and they schemed him out of the backfield against a linebacker and safety for a touchdown. Waldman expects them to keep finding ways to work him open. The caveat: the tight ends may have something to say week to week as their usage grows.
