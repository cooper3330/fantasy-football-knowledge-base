# Ashton Jeanty

## Expert Takes

- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Dynasty]: Naming his top two draft-eye-catchers from recent college tape study, Waldman singled out Boise State's Ashton Jeanty (5'8', 209) as an excellent receiver out of the backfield and an explosive, efficient runner who uses moves well both between the tackles and in the open field. With backfield mate [[George Holani]] (a UDFA trying to make the Seahawks' roster) gone, Jeanty is set to be the clear lead back this season, and Waldman said he has a real shot to be the top running back drafted out of this college class.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10]]): the only back in the 2025 class he considers a true day-one starter who should be on the field for 60%-plus of snaps — 'if [[David Montgomery]] and [[Kareem Hunt]] had a love child and gave that kid a jet pack,' an electric ball carrier with good pace and poise. [[Matt Waldman]] pushes back on how the trait is being described: what people are calling contact balance is mostly extraordinary quickness, 'BB-gun level,' though there is real indirect contact balance — getting swept at the legs, staying upright and re-accelerating — which Waldman values more than direct contact balance for a back who is not 225-230 pounds. He praises a stiff arm that works on timing rather than power, beating defenders to the punch. Waldman sees a little Barry Sanders in the lateral movement but says the comp he keeps returning to is Jahvid Best — speed, receiving ability, and enough strength for his size to work between the tackles. Jeanty has already cleared the 85 threshold on the RSP grading scale, which Waldman calls a good sign this early.
- 2024-10-23 — According to co-host Felix Sharpe of Campus2Canton (not a tracked expert), with [[Matt Waldman]] concurring ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-23]]) [Dynasty]: Jeanty is the flagship example of legacy recruiting services missing badly — a three-star prospect ranked roughly RB80-something by the services, whom Campus2Canton had as their RB16 and liked enough to interview. He played football in Italy before returning to Texas. Sharpe expects him to chase Barry Sanders' rushing record at Boise State, to be in New York for the Heisman ceremony, and to be the first running back taken in the NFL draft, if not one of the first couple — an extraordinary arc for someone treated as a recruiting afterthought.
- 2024-12-05 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-05]]) [Dynasty]: the widely circulated worry — 651 career carries entering a game, 21 more that day by halftime, and a near-certain 700-plus carries in three college seasons — should not be discounted at all. College workload is 'predictably useless' as a predictor even before controlling for age, and the volume itself is a coaches' preference reveal that Jeanty's body and running style tolerate it. Harstad grouped him with LaDainian Tomlinson, Ray Rice, Adrian Peterson and Chris Johnson rather than with hyper-efficient undersized backs like Andre Ellington, Tatum Bell or Felix Jones whose coaches deliberately capped them. Waldman added the same list of heavy-college-carry backs who went on to heavy NFL careers, and stressed that a back who has repeatedly recovered from a big load is the safer profile, not the riskier one.
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: the clear RB1 of the 2025 class and a likely top-12 NFL pick. Angelo projects 18-22 touches a game immediately and calls him the probable Offensive Rookie of the Year favorite, with a ceiling comp of Maurice Jones-Drew and a middle outcome of 'what we wanted [[J.K. Dobbins]] to be' — Waldman independently landed on the same Dobbins comp after first considering Jahvid Best. Waldman had him graded just above [[Jonathan Taylor]] as a prospect. The separating trait both cite is run stamina plus contact mitigation: like Jamaal Charles at a similar 206-pound size, Jeanty dictates contact, striking first into an open chest and spinning or accelerating off it so no one gets a clean hit. Angelo also thinks his receiving skill set (more visible in 2023 at Boise State than 2024) is broader than the public believes.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman says he 'really loves' Jeanty and that the dude 'has a chance to be special, and I don't say that lightly' — but 'fuck him' at Underdog RB6 until we know the landing spot and how he will be used, especially against a known quantity like [[Derrick Henry]]. Asked whether RB9-RB10, after [[Jonathan Taylor]], would be comfortable, Waldman said ability-wise yes, but landing spot is still the deciding variable. Untracked co-host Bob Harris agreed, invoking [[Clyde Edwards-Helaire]] — an ADP rocket strapped on after one Texans game where he ran through paper tackles, peaking around RB6 and finishing RB30. Jeanty is the one rookie back both hosts would take over veterans like [[J.K. Dobbins]].
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether sixth overall is too early for the Raiders to take Jeanty, Waldman says no. In a division where you may need to win 13-10 regularly, he expects Pete Carroll's team to lean defense, running the ball and controlled passing, and Jeanty fits — a back with the play stamina to finish a run with the same energy he started it even after navigating a maze of defenders. His comp is a less powerful, more electric [[Marshawn Lynch]], explicitly not one-to-one. He adds the roster logic: with a great tight end already the primary receiver and no true WR1 available in this class, taking the back makes sense. Bob Harris (untracked) 'feels this so fucking hard', citing Carroll's history of anointing a back — Chris Carson, Thomas Rawls, Reggie Bush — and riding him.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]) [Best Ball]: Jeanty rising to RB4 at pick 12 overall in early best balls draws a split verdict — Waldman says the ability is absolutely there for him to be an RB4 someday and it could even be this year, but on the price he says 'fuck it, you only live once', treating pre-draft best ball as speculative gambling money. Crucially, he still prefers Jeanty at RB4 to [[Omarion Hampton]] at RB17, and treats Jeanty as his standard for knowing how to run a football — the benchmark he measures [[TreVeyon Henderson]] against at roughly two-thirds of the way there. He praises not just speed but pace-setting ability. Bob Harris (not tracked) says he burned a mid-first to secure a single Jeanty share.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: Jeanty is his 1.01 in rookie drafts even in superflex, ahead of [[Travis Hunter]], because Hunter's receiver-versus-corner snap split will be unknowable until late summer and at 1.01 you want the surer thing; in superflex he would take [[Cam Ward]] at 1.02 and called 1.03 onward a hodgepodge. [[Matt Waldman]] separately named Jeanty to his all-time three-deep backfield alongside [[Nick Chubb]] and [[Saquon Barkley]], saying he was willing to take the chance on him despite the short evaluation history.
- 2025-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: on Bob Harris's (not a tracked expert) theory that the Giants — having let their superstar back go, having added two veteran quarterbacks, and facing a class with no great quarterbacks — should take Jeanty, Waldman is feeling it. He calls it an intelligent viewpoint: the Giants are pretty ready, have built in the trenches, and with [[Russell Wilson]] starting rather than [[Jameis Winston]] a star back gives them a strong play-action component and a real playmaker alongside [[Malik Nabers]], instantly legitimizing the offense. He would pair Jeanty with [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]], who showed something as a rookie with growth potential left. Waldman bets they will not go that direction, but says this is one of the rare spots where drafting a running back there would actually be a good idea.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): Jeanty is his favorite running back in the class and the clear 1A with 'a grand canyon of difference' to [[Omarion Hampton]] — but Jacksonville at five would be an awful landing spot, because the offensive line is putrid, [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] and [[Tank Bigsby]] are already there, and Jeanty would average something like 4.2 yards per carry while fantasy managers got upset. Waldman thinks the Jaguars-at-five talk is smoke and expects a trade-up, with Dallas an even worse outcome — he would be happier with the Raiders and thrilled with the Bears. [[Brandon Angelo]] partly disagrees on Jacksonville: Liam Coen could maximize Jeanty as a receiver, and he would be the offense's second option in the passing game, but agrees the line and low team scoring cap the upside and that a talented back needs the trenches rebuilt first.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): a top-10 ADP on Jeanty (currently RB4, seventh overall) is premature, though top-25 is unarguable — the price is 'the inflated price of the new running back who's the star.' On the Jordan Schultz 'beast mode 2.0' framing, Waldman says there is real Marshawn Lynch in him — the run stamina where he finishes a run with the same ferocity he began it — but with 'a little dash of Reggie Bush,' so the blend, not the pure Lynch comp. He projects 17 games, roughly 230 carries and 1,300–1,400 yards with double-digit touchdowns — rookie RB1, but not a lock for top-five production. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) notes Footballguys projects ~270 carries and Mike Clay slightly higher; Waldman deliberately goes lower because the defense may force more passing and he can see a two- or three-headed backfield with [[Raheem Mostert]], Sincere McCormick and Zamir White. Harris invokes Clyde Edwards-Helaire 2020 as the cautionary robust-role comp and says he is not investing at that price.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he takes Pete Carroll at his word about splitting the backfield with [[Raheem Mostert]] and therefore projects Jeanty closer to 1,000-1,100 rushing yards as a rookie rather than 1,300-1,400. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) disagreed directly, saying Carroll has always installed a feature back and stuck with him wherever he has gone, and citing Raiders beat writer Vinny Bonsignore as being higher on Jeanty still, with something like 275 to 300 carries as the desired game plan; Harris also pointed to Chip Kelly's offenses producing high-end workloads for even ordinary backs like Carlos Hyde and LeSean McCoy. Harris allowed the possibility Carroll is simply telling the truth, but read the comments as smoke.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): asked whether Jeanty joins the weapon-back group or the [[Christian McCaffrey]] tier, Angelo said no. He expects usage close to Jeanty's senior year at Boise State, because that is how Pete Carroll likes to play — a 12-personnel-heavy offense with [[Brock Bowers]] and [[Michael Mayer]], lots of run and play action, and a mandate to keep [[Geno Smith]] upright. In that structure there is little room to deploy the back as a detached receiver creating space for others [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-13]]): seeing Jeanty drafted ahead of [[Derrick Henry]], [[Josh Jacobs]], [[Jonathan Taylor]] and [[Breece Hall]] is 'galling' — he knew the price was coming but says the best case for Jeanty is simply the season you routinely get from those established backs, while his floor is far lower. Dave Kluge of Footballguys (guest, not a tracked expert) piles on with preseason observation: the Raiders offensive line is 'so, so, so bad' — they left it in after pulling the rest of the first team — and Jeanty was blown up in the backfield with almost no production. Kluge also worries about the jump from a small conference, and invokes peak [[Saquon Barkley]] on bad Giants teams as the environment risk. Harris declines to use surrounding cast as his reason, citing his own shtick that analysts are terrible at predicting situations in advance.
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: a great [[Dynasty]] buy but only 'good, not great' at his first-round redraft price. Waldman's projection is 1,100-1,200 rushing yards with eight to ten rushing touchdowns on under 250 carries, plus just 250-300 receiving yards on maybe 30-45 targets — because Pete Carroll's offense wants to run, but off play action it is not trying to get the ball to the back, so Jeanty's receiving work is check-down, leak and screen rather than 'McCaffrey level stuff'. He also expects the Raiders to supplement with someone from the veteran group ([[Raheem Mostert]], [[Zamir White]], [[Sincere McCormick]]). Waldman thinks Jeanty can be efficient despite a poor offensive line. [[Chris Harris]] adds that he will not draft any rookie in the first round and would only want Jeanty in the late second.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]]: Jeanty (ASR: 'Jinty'/'Gentry') is a feature piece of the Raiders offense alongside [[Brock Bowers]], with the wide receivers as complementary pieces — and notably he has been taking a lot of slot snaps, which is part of why the Raiders' slot is so congested and why [[Jack Bech]] is being pushed outside. Nguyen framed the whole offseason as an offensive rebuild (Jeanty, Bech, [[Geno Smith]]) under Pete Carroll and Chip Kelly, with Koh adding that Carroll will want to establish the run and that 12 personnel may be the vehicle.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): on the 7.5 rushing-touchdown prop Harris went under, but only weakly — he says he does not feel strongly either way and picked it because the number felt annoyingly close, with his stated fear being that [[Raheem Mostert]] takes half the job while Jeanty learns pass protection, the way [[Tyler Allgeier]] ate into [[Bijan Robinson]]'s rookie year. He still allows Jeanty 'might be the best back in the league this side of [[Saquon Barkley]]'. Guest Chris Raybon (Action Network, not tracked) took the opposite side on both counts: Jeanty at 18-1 was his single NFL-leading-rusher bet as the most underpriced name on the board, citing Pete Carroll and Chip Kelly wanting to run and an undervalued Raiders offensive line, and he took a strong over on touchdowns at about 8.5, saying Jeanty will challenge for double digits for the next five to ten years.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): Harris rates Jeanty a 7 out of 10 at ADP 12 and says flatly he would never draft him there — he does not want the rookie in the first round, and the risk is that everybody is misreading the situation and the Raiders bring him along slowly the way Atlanta did with [[Bijan Robinson]] and Detroit did with [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] as rookies. Guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates him a 4, calling him a little bit risky as a rookie in round one. Both note ADP is softer among experts than civilians: in mock drafts with the wise guys Jeanty sometimes falls to the second round, while in a Football Guys bowl leaning civilian in Columbus he went ahead of [[Saquon Barkley]] at 1.06 — 'too rich for my blood' for Bell.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris disclosed paying $72 for Jeanty in a 14-team keeper auction, believed to be the highest price any player went for in that league, calling it the one place he owns Jeanty. He framed it as a consequence of running the auction and not tracking the board — by the time he looked up there were no running backs left — and rationalized the price by noting he had cheap kept [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Joe Burrow]] stacked, so dollar-cost-averaging across the roster makes Jeanty reasonable. Behrens ribbed him that his public 'don't trust rookies, we don't know yet' stance is a ruse to smooth his own drafts; Harris did not retract the general rookie caution, only conceded he owns Jeanty here. Notable that the buy came in an auction where running back scarcity puts the whole league in range.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-04]]): Jeanty wins the 'player Chris can't wait to see play in Week 1' Huggy. Harris restates his strategic objection — he doesn't like taking a rookie in the first round — but says that doesn't mean he's dead on the inside, and he is eager to see what Jeanty has and how central the Raiders make him immediately. If Jeanty is amazing right away with a really big workload, Harris says that would be extremely fun. He frames his own August stances, especially about a player who has never played an NFL game that matters, as things that should be firm without being reality-denying, and commits to being 'just an eyeball' from here forward with as few axes to grind as possible: once it's too late to act on a take, who cares whether the take was correct. First look comes against New England.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Film & Theory Sequences, Signal, Steelers)]]): do not react to an early-season [[Ashton Jeanty]] fumble. Waldman's stated reason is talent plus historical precedent — Adrian Peterson fumbled into double digits as a rookie and Minnesota simply pulled him for a series or two to settle him down before putting him straight back in, which is what happens to an ungodly talent. Waldman's threshold for actually worrying is a cluster of signals rather than a single play: fumbling throughout camp, a documented college ball-security issue, coaches talking about it publicly, or a coach with a history of being a stickler. Harstad's complementary point is that the optimal fumble rate is never zero, so a back running with the aggression that produces extra yards will occasionally put the ball on the ground.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): 'fuck it' on Jeanty returning ADP value this year — but the framing is bullish long-term. He compares him to rookie-year 2001 LaDainian Tomlinson, who averaged roughly 3.8-3.9 yards per carry while the talent was plainly visible. On rewatch Jeanty looked powerful and balanced; not [[Omarion Hampton]]-powerful, but a quick scat back who uses his movement and low center of gravity extremely well, looking every bit the talent Waldman graded, in a bad situation. Asked with no explanation allowed whether you will wish you had taken [[Brock Bowers]] at Jeanty's draft slot, Waldman said he has felt that since July.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): give him a mulligan. Jeanty gashed for 10 on the Raiders' great opening drive and later scored on a nice power run to give Vegas a lead they never gave back, but Harris thought he did not show anything laterally, did not make a lot of cuts, and did not produce the explosion you were hoping for. His mitigation is that this was a bit of a rain game on a slippery surface, and if you want to argue Jeanty was not quite ready for that footing, maybe. If you spent a first-round pick on him you are still starting him; you just want to wipe this one away and see him in better conditions. He also notes New England obviously respected Jeanty, selling out to stop the run, which is precisely why play action started working for Vegas in the second half. In a bad game where you score a touchdown, Harris will take the bad game.
