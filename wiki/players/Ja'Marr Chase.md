# Ja'Marr Chase

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]): quarterback downgrade doesn't move him — "Ja'Marr Chase is still going to be Ja'Marr Chase" with [[Jake Browning]] throwing. Waldman's shorthand for where he ranks him: if asked to name the best receiver in the NFL, answering "that young veteran from LSU" gives you roughly a two-in-five chance of being right.
- 2023-12-18 — Injury note, same episode: Chase suffered an AC joint injury. Waldman flagged that AC joint injuries travel worse for receivers than for running backs — backs get strapped down and play hunched anyway, while receivers need to extend and reach — so his week-to-week availability was the live question, not his talent.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): #1 overall on Harmon's cross-class Reception Perception "stacked board" (2021-2023 classes) — one of only five receivers to ever earn Harmon's tier-one grade ("worth picking in the top 10... a ready-made star"), alongside Chris Olave, DeVonta Smith, [[Drake London]] and Garrett Wilson (Olave/Smith/Wilson not tracked). See [[Reception Perception Methodology]].
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): "feeling" him in almost any scenario, contingent only on Joe Burrow (or a comparable quarterback) staying under center — explicitly not contingent on whether [[Tee Higgins]] remains in Cincinnati. Restates the top-of-the-league grade in film terms: elite releases, route running, after-the-catch ability and catch-point physicality, on the short list of receivers with genuine 2,000-yard-season upside — "if you said he's the best receiver in the NFL, I wouldn't disagree with you," even allowing three or four others into that debate. Separately ties his dynasty valuation directly to quarterback security, ranking him above receivers whose own QB situations are less settled (Justin Jefferson's contract unresolved, [[Tyreek Hill]]'s self-described "shelf life").

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]): in a dynasty startup drafting today, says he'd take Chase "ever so slightly" ahead of [[Justin Jefferson]] — based on Chase's better quarterback certainty (Joe Burrow signed long-term vs. Jefferson's own extension still unresolved) rather than a talent gap. Explicitly calls the two "one-two" with the two players "not that far apart," and says he'd run to the podium for Jefferson if someone took Chase first. Separately argues [[Tee Higgins]]'s franchise tag is actually good for Chase's market, since it front-runs the wide receiver market reset both Jefferson and Chase are expected to trigger with their own new deals this summer.
- 2024-06-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-11]]): Chase's man-coverage success rate has been remarkably consistent across three seasons (75.3%, 75.3%, 75.4%), with press-coverage success at 80.2% in back-to-back years (81.6% as a rookie) and zone success climbing every year to 82% in 2023, a 79th-percentile mark. Harmon expects Chase's slot usage (23.9% of snaps in 2023) to jump further in 2024 now that [[Tyler Boyd]] is gone and the Bengals drafted outside receiver [[Jermaine Burton]] in round three -- not making him a 'pure slot receiver' but unlocking easier looks and after-catch chances, similar to how added slot work boosted [[Justin Jefferson]] and [[CeeDee Lamb]] into signature seasons. Harmon called out Chase's 91.4% success rate on out routes (his personal favorite route, per a Super Bowl interview) and 83% on the dig as areas to feed him more, while his nine-route success rate (63%) was 'good but not elite' relative to Jefferson's high-60s/70%+. Asked to compare him to [[Garrett Wilson]], Harmon said Wilson may be more physically talented but called Chase the more technically skilled, well-rounded route runner, and projected double-digit touchdowns and a shot at 1,600-1,700 yards in 2024 if [[Joe Burrow]] stays healthy -- Chase posted 87.2 yards/game and 7.4 catches/game in his injury-shortened 2023 (with [[Jake Browning]] starting), on pace matching Puka Nacua's and Amon-Ra St. Brown's per-game production.
- 2024-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-23]]): Chase belongs in any top-five receiver conversation and is 'ready to have that kind of dominant, dominant season' even though he has not yet matched 2022 [[Justin Jefferson]] or 2023 [[CeeDee Lamb]] statistically. The core of the case is a sustained 82nd-percentile success rate vs man, with year-over-year marks of 75.3%, 75.3% and 75.4% over three seasons while running a heavy vertical route diet — Harmon calls him a really underrated route runner and technician on top of the explosiveness. Deployment has trended his way: 84.8% of rookie-year charted snaps outside and 85.7% on the line, versus 2023 marks of 40.2% off the line, 23.9% in the slot and 3.8% in the backfield. Harmon expects a career-high slot rate in 2024 with [[Tyler Boyd]] gone and [[Jermaine Burton]] a pure outside player, and notes Chase told him at the Super Bowl he wants more slot work and is trying to earn the staff's trust to run routes from more alignments. Both hosts want Cincinnati to keep the [[Jake Browning]] under-center, motion and play-action ideas that manufactured touches for him — Harmon adds that [[Joe Burrow]] has been hesitant about turning his back to the defense and prefers the point-and-shoot shotgun game.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): Chase was a hold-in rather than a holdout and returned to practice, which is why Waldman is not worried about him — he has been working out and works out hard. Waldman adds that Chase has had poor preseasons before that people lost their minds about and then been excellent anyway. His evaluation is unqualified: he can catch, he runs routes, he is powerful, he is quick — 'honestly, I think he's the best wide receiver in the league,' and has been since roughly year two. Bob Harris (not tracked) supplied the contract context: Cincinnati has paid early only once ([[Joe Burrow]]), so Chase likely plays on the rookie deal this year and gets paid before the fifth-year option, mirroring [[Justin Jefferson]] a year earlier. Waldman lists Chase among the safe top-of-draft alternatives to a holdout receiver.
- 2024-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): Chase has practiced with the Bengals exactly zero times during his contract holdout and Harmon would be shocked if he plays Week 1. Harmon's offseason expectation that Chase would set a career high in slot snaps is now in doubt — Chase told him at the Super Bowl he wanted more slot work and was trying to earn the staff's trust to run the full route tree from inside, and he has had no chance to show it. Harmon notes his situation is unlike [[CeeDee Lamb]], [[Brandon Aiyuk]] or [[Justin Jefferson]], who were all in the final year of rookie deals; Chase has two years left, and the Bengals are more financially conservative than most, which makes the standoff harder to resolve. Harmon sees a clear gap between Chase and [[Tee Higgins]] as players.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Chase is back at practice in a real jersey after his hold-in, but Harmon says he would be surprised to see him play much if at all in Week 1 against New England, where Cincinnati is an eight-point favorite and simply does not need him; from a conditioning standpoint it would be tough to play a full complement of snaps after almost no practice time. Harmon expects him 'fully unleashed' in Week 2 against Kansas City. He adds that had Chase been present all offseason, the Bengals might have integrated him more as a slot receiver to replace [[Tyler Boyd]]; he will still see slot work and be moved around, but the missed time removed the runway to conceptualize a new role. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) agreed on the Week 1 sit.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-12]]): the one thing Harmon says the show got right is Chase's slot usage — 43% of his routes came from the slot in Week 1 despite him skipping most of the preseason, and Harmon thinks that is going to be critical going forward. The production was not there: six targets with an average depth of target under seven, matched by [[Andrei Iosivas]], which Harmon flatly says will not cut it. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) says he did not recognize the offense — Cincinnati previously played '1990s style football' with two dominant outside receivers winning downfield, and now Chase is running 'a bunch of little baby slot routes.' Both note Chase himself called the annual slow start embarrassing after the game, and that Cincinnati does this every September.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): buying Chase for rest of season despite an inconsistent, up-and-down start below his own standard. Harmon is not surprised by the quiet Week 2 — Chase has lined up in the slot far more this year, as Harmon predicted, and the worst possible team to do that against is Kansas City, whose best corner Trent McDuffie is a natural slot defender. He likes that other Bengals are emerging to take heat off Chase with [[Tee Higgins]] out: [[Andrei Iosivas]] on a couple of touchdown catches, Mike Gesicki with a role, Erick All making plays off motion, and [[Jermaine Burton]] with a long catch. Week 3 against Washington sets up well.
- 2024-10-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-01]]): Chase is a 'tier one and a half' receiver rather than a true tier-one, alongside [[Brandon Aiyuk]] — a distinction Koh said listeners would find surprising, since Chase is usually assumed into the best-in-league conversation. Harmon called himself a huge Chase fan who has gone to bat for him as an underrated route technician, but his most recent RP profile (82nd percentile vs man, 79th vs zone, 91st vs press; 2022: 81st vs man, 66th vs zone) sits below the highs reached by [[Justin Jefferson]], [[Tyreek Hill]], [[Davante Adams]], [[A.J. Brown]] and [[CeeDee Lamb]]. He noted all three of the 2024 holdout receivers — Chase, [[CeeDee Lamb]] and [[Brandon Aiyuk]] — have had poor starts, and allowed Chase may well be a tier-one player by season's end.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): asked point blank whether Chase is the best receiver in the world, Waldman said 'hell yes' and that it is not especially close, while acknowledging [[Justin Jefferson]] and [[Malik Nabers]] — the full LSU trio — plus [[Davante Adams]] at least short term and [[Tyreek Hill]] as legitimate members of the conversation. His stated criterion for 'best' is versatility: strong route running, yards after the catch, contested catches, and the ability to play all three receiver alignments. Chase looked awesome in the Ravens overtime game even against Marlon Humphrey, who cut him off on some snaps. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) noted the recency-bias pattern that people once said Chase 'couldn't catch' for a stretch — talent wins out in the end.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): Chase is his WR1 in the NFL. Bob Harris (not tracked) noted Chase owns three of the six best single-game fantasy WR performances since 2011 — 55 points in 2021, 55 last week in 2024 and 52 in 2023 — alongside [[Tyreek Hill]] (57 in 2020), Will Fuller (2019) and [[Tyler Lockett]] (2020). Waldman says there is a very clear top two, Chase and [[Justin Jefferson]], with everyone else in a tier below, and he gives it to Chase on scouting grounds: he liked Jefferson a lot pre-draft, but Chase was 'head and shoulders above everybody in that class' and one of the best he has ever scouted. Specifically he grades Chase the better athlete — more speed, more short-area burst — and more physical than Jefferson at the catch point, just as acrobatic and as good a route runner. He calls it splitting hairs but says the size-strength-speed combination makes it easy.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14 (Age Cliffs and Dented Cans)]]): Chase is his pick for best receiver in the NFL, with [[Justin Jefferson]] 1-2 in some order — he wrote as much the Monday before. His case: the most dangerous player in the league after the catch on size, speed, strength and the ability to accelerate past defenders who already have the angle; a route runner as good as or slightly better than Jefferson and the most technically crisp of the elite group; tough over the middle and in contested-catch areas; and able to play all three receiver positions. He adds that Chase has one of the three or four best quarterbacks in the league throwing to him. Adam Harstad (NOT tracked) noted Chase has the most 60-yard touchdowns of the last five years despite only being in the league for three of them — 'Lance Alworth kind of stuff' — and argued the big plays are demonstrated skill, not unsustainable variance: everyone regresses to a mean, but Chase's mean is not everyone else's.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]): Chase has been an absolute monster all season and Harmon gives him the edge in the week 14 Dallas matchup against a banged-up Trevon Diggs, whom he called an overrated cover corner regardless of health. He noted Dallas has genuinely improved — 10th in EPA allowed since week 8 with [[Micah Parsons]] back, and DaRon Bland returning toward full strength — but said he would still want Diggs shadowing him if he were Chase, and gave [[Tee Higgins]] the edge too, since Higgins on the field opens up more pre-snap alignment freedom for Chase. Contract note: Chase's extension is still undone and he is heading into a fifth-year option he will not want to play on, with Harmon citing [[Brandon Aiyuk]] as the cautionary example of what a lost season on that option can cost.
- 2024-12-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-12]]): Chase is the one genuinely elite producer in a down year at the position — leading the league with 93 receptions, 1,319 yards and 15 touchdowns, on pace for roughly 122 catches and 20 scores, with only two receivers past 1,000 yards through 14 weeks versus 11 last year. Harmon credits deployment as much as talent: after asking to play more slot, Chase is at 32% slot snaps (65.7% wide) against 14.3%, 20.6% and 24.8% in prior years, and his yards after catch per reception jumped to 6.7 from 5.4 and 4.8. He notes Chase's Reception Perception scores are good-not-gaudy (82nd percentile success rate vs man, 91st vs press, ~80th vs zone) below [[Justin Jefferson]] or [[A.J. Brown]], but that Chase runs a vertical tree and is 'almost an instant six points' at any moment; he posted a 91.4% success rate on out routes, the route Chase told him is his favorite because Zac Taylor lets him convert it to a go against press. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) added a 73% catch rate and called the old rookie-year hands concern dead.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]): Chase is the best player on the Bengals and the best player in the AFC North — Waldman puts him over [[Lamar Jackson]], [[Derrick Henry]], T.J. Watt and even Myles Garrett, calling those the only names with a real argument. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) floated a deliberately provocative hypothetical: a cheap Cincinnati front office trading Chase for a huge haul and paying [[Tee Higgins]] as the WR1 instead, on the theory that owners may not want to set a new positional record contract. Waldman rejected it flatly as the most incredibly stupid move the team could make — it would tear apart the offense and be a nightmare for [[Joe Burrow]] — while conceding the league-wide 'owners don't want to exceed a number at a position' idea is at least in circulation.
- 2025-01-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-09]]): Chase wins Best Overall Wide Receiver for 2024, plus an honorable mention versus zone coverage where he was second in yards per route run. Koh's raw zone numbers: 1,264 receiving yards and 10 touchdowns against zone, both far and away first in the NFL. Season line 127 receptions, 1,708 yards, 17 touchdowns — the receiving triple crown — and Harmon calls him far and away the best fantasy receiver of the season and probably the most valuable receiver to his team. The evaluative case is that he is good at everything: a dominant press-man beater, absurdly efficient against zone, and a route runner Harmon now puts in his top five in the league, a category he says Chase has 'graduated' into after being challenged by the coaching staff to win from multiple alignments — the preseason plan to move him around and get more slot reps against zone came to fruition. Also a contested-catch monster. Harmon says with [[Tyreek Hill]] having graduated off the category, Chase is close to one-of-one as an elite receiver who can go zero to sixty and house it. Harmon notes Chase is still one of the top two prospects of the RP era behind [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]; Koh called him a future Hall of Famer before he was drafted.
- 2025-02-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: no contract-year angst at all. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) cited Michael Fabiano's note that Chase posted 403 fantasy points in 2024 with career bests across the board, three 40-plus-point games including a 55-burger, and is unlikely to have a new deal before the end of training camp. Waldman would leverage the uncertainty only if it saves a pick in a trade — 'I'm still paying through the nose to get a hold of him' — and on taking him as a top-two 2025 pick said 'fuck that, I'm totally happy with taking him.' His reasoning: Chase has leverage from his own ability plus pressure from [[Joe Burrow]] on the organization, and Cincinnati has a long history (Burrow, A.J. Green, Willie Anderson) of signing players at the last minute anyway.
- 2025-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-05]]): reporting from the Combine has Zac Taylor and Duke Tobin, Cincinnati's de facto GM, both saying on the record that Chase will be the highest-paid non-quarterback in the NFL when he signs — and yet a same-day news alert said the two sides remain far apart on details. Harmon's read is that this is an ownership-cash problem rather than a valuation dispute, since guaranteed money has to be placed in escrow and the Bengals are a family-run business without the outside capital influx a team like the Raiders has. He expects Chase's extension to be completed before [[Tee Higgins]] can get a long-term deal, and dismisses the framing that a [[Joe Burrow]] restructure would be Burrow 'giving something up' — it is just money earlier.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]): Chase's extension makes him the highest-paid non-quarterback in the NFL at over $40 million a year with $112 million guaranteed, and paired with the [[Tee Higgins]] deal it commits Cincinnati to leading with offense. Waldman says the Bengals had to do it — without Higgins there is no commensurate replacement, and pivoting mid-stream to fund defense would only weaken the one thing that works, risking an offensive-coordinator change and scheme reset of the kind he has seen derail Chicago and Green Bay. He compares the roster shape to the Dan Fouts-era Chargers: all offense, no defense. Both hosts note the fantasy read is simple — Cincinnati was first in the league in passing yards per game (272) and passing touchdowns, and cap-wise the extensions actually lower 2025 numbers, possibly enough to retain Trey Hendrickson.
- 2025-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-19]]): Chase signed a four-year, $161M extension at $40.25M AAV, making him the highest-paid non-quarterback in NFL history after publicly vowing to clear [[Justin Jefferson]]'s $35M. Harmon credits the 2024 triple crown (175 targets, 127 receptions, ~1,700 yards, 17 TDs; 46 TDs in four years) partly to the slot usage he had predicted the prior offseason — Chase lined up roughly two-thirds outside and one-third inside, a versatility Cincinnati had not shown before. Asked to start a franchise with one receiver, Harmon says he would 'probably still take' Jefferson because he is such a great separator and so quarterback-friendly, though he calls Chase the leading candidate if you want one man to take a screen 60 yards, and thinks Chase is underrated as a technical route runner. With a quarterback he trusts at all three levels he 'might' flip to Chase. Harmon also theorizes Chase's 2024 holdout anger — showing up hours before the opener — fed the Bengals' slow start, and that a March deal removes that.
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-13]]): Chase is 'arguably one of the two if not the best' receiver in the NFL after a triple-crown 17-catch-leading, 1,700-yard, 17-touchdown season, and Harmon would take him slightly over [[A.J. Brown]] straight up. The structural story is alignment: Cincinnati finally weaponized him, with slot rate climbing 14.3% as a rookie, 20.6% in year two, 24.8% in 2023 and a career-high 32.3% in 2024 (Next Gen Stats: 66% wide, 32% slot, 2% backfield), enabled by [[Andrei Iosivas]] taking line-of-scrimmage snaps. Harmon cited Jacob Gibbs data: career 36% targets per route run in pre-snap motion versus 23% static, 3.06 yards per route run in motion versus 2.16 static, with motion routes doubling yearly (9, 20, 50, 100). Caveat — he played 1,001 snaps, never before over 900, partly from game script, so Harmon flags mild volume regression without projecting a big drop-off. He also noted Cincinnati schemes less for its receivers than almost any team, but that suits [[Joe Burrow]]'s under-center, Peyton Manning-style preferences.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): in the top-36 risk exercise, both Harris and guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not a tracked expert) rate Chase a 1 out of 10 — the safest player on the board 'by any measure' after the receiving triple crown, with [[Joe Burrow]] and a bad Bengals defense underwriting the volume. Bell has him as his overall 1 in all formats except standard scoring, where he would consider a running back. Harris does not have Chase as his own 1 overall — he prefers [[Saquon Barkley]] because running back is too scarce and the replacement cost too high — but concedes that anyone trying to weed out variance should head toward Chase. Both cite Andy Behrens' column arguing the platforms all defaulted to the wrong guy at 1.01, which Harris reads as the wrong position rather than the wrong player.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Chase being the consensus 1.01 on every fantasy platform is the wrong answer in a 10- or 12-team redraft league, full PPR included, because it is a value-based-drafting error — 'he doesn't play the right position to be the number one pick.' Harris's argument is replacement level: there are many receivers who are just fine and very few running backs who are, so the scarce commodity should cost the most. Guest Andy Behrens (not a tracked expert), who wrote the column that prompted the segment, attributed the 1.01 consensus to three factors — analysts moving in a herd once an opinion gains gravity, best-ball and high-stakes full-PPR habits (drafting Chase to stack with [[Joe Burrow]] and [[Tee Higgins]]) migrating into standard redraft, and pushback that simply assumes Chase repeats last year's roughly 1,800-yard, 17-touchdown line while assuming every running back regresses. Behrens noted no one has repeated as the overall WR1 since Antonio Brown about a decade ago. Neither host disputes Chase's talent; both dispute the price.
