# Terrance Ferguson

## Expert Takes

- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18]]) [Dynasty]: Ferguson is earning love for his speed and vertical leaping, but Waldman's caution is that he is a linear athlete and most of football is not played in straight lines. Given a well-blocked screen or open space to rumble to full speed, he produces chunk plays, and on well-devised play-action seam routes he tracks the ball over his shoulder, can fully turn to it, and even back-shoulder throws are reasonable for him. But his short-area quickness and bend do not match his linear ability, which puts him at a disadvantage on core tight end requirements: winning releases against tight coverage at the line, setting up tight man coverage during his stems, winning breaks against man, making defenders miss in the open field and redirecting against defenders as a run blocker. Waldman's verdict is that he will have to be great at what he does and at least competent at what is hard for a linear athlete — and right now he is not competent at many of those non-linear tasks.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21]]): asked whether he will be higher on the Oregon tight end after the draft following a strong combine, Waldman said 'f**k it' — he expects Ferguson may earn draft capital that pushes him up other people's boards, but he intends to keep him where he is unless the landing spot offers a genuinely good rationale for an offense that minimizes his weaknesses. His objection is archetypal: Ferguson is a high-end schemed-up player, someone a coordinator has to create offense for, and Waldman does not believe teams can manufacture enough of it to make him consistently fantasy productive.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: a fade — he calls Ferguson a mid-to-low-end linear athlete and had joked pre-draft that whoever took him too early in the NFL draft would be 'the sucker in the room'. His objection is archetypal rather than personal: the Rams keep collecting high-cut, linear tight ends who do not do much, what he calls 'Higbee syndrome', citing Higbee and Parkinson as precedent. Waldman is baffled that rookie drafters take Ferguson over [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] in the third or fourth round. [[Brandon Angelo]] says he was not high on Ferguson either and agrees with the reasoning.
- 2025-05-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-30]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon calls Ferguson 'a great sleeper for this year' and says he should be right up on the radar. His evidence is organizational intent rather than production: the Rams tried and failed to trade up for [[Brock Bowers]] in 2024, immediately started peppering [[Tyler Higbee]] with targets on his return — which Harmon says was his first indication Los Angeles was done with [[Cooper Kupp]] — and then spent a second-round pick on Ferguson, with the team's own draft-room video showing how obsessed McVay is with him. Harmon describes him as a high-caliber athlete who is 'very co-coded' and expects more 12-personnel usage, which he thinks blurs the line between tight end and slot receiver in this offense.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]): 'fuck it' on Ferguson as a 2025 fantasy weapon despite Sean McVay and the Rams seeming all-in on him. Waldman calls him a linear player in the mold of [[Noah Fant]] and [[David Njoku]] but not as talented as either as a pass catcher. He expects Los Angeles to scheme him into some big plays that excite highlight watchers, but on route running and how he can actually be used as an athlete, Waldman's ceiling comp is [[Mike Gesicki]] — which in a very good offense can translate to low-end starter production, so it is possible, just not something he is buying. His broader caveat is that McVay's staff is better at scheme than at personnel. Bob Harris (not tracked) agreed he is not buying either, but said he considers Ferguson viable in dynasty rookie drafts and startups.
- 2025-07-23 — According to James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]: Ferguson is a 'sleeper king' who has to be on the radar — super talented and very athletic, and the draft-room video showed [[Sean McVay]] insisting he had to have him. Koh frames this as part of a documented McVay obsession with tight ends, noting he had wanted to trade up for [[Brock Bowers]] the year before. [[Matt Harmon]] accepts the point that the Rams will play a lot of 12 personnel with Tyler Higbee still on the roster, which limits the team's overall slot snaps and, in his read, was the first indicator that [[Cooper Kupp]] was finished in Los Angeles — Kupp was not getting the ball while Higbee, who barely played until the end of the season, was suddenly spammed with targets.
- 2026-01-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: named Ferguson his #3 level-jumper, calling him unusually fast for a tight end and central to the Rams' 13-personnel packages -- his speed provides a credible vertical threat that clears space for Puka Nacua or Davante Adams as the single receiver, even though clear-out routes don't show up in a fantasy box score. Harmon noted real chemistry issues with Matthew Stafford and drops on contested catches down the field, chalking it up to a young player still growing into his body, but said the coaching staff's usage -- including stretches split out wide as a true X in three-tight-end sets -- shows real trust. [[Chris Harris]] disagreed sharply, saying Ferguson 'stunk' on an opportunity-to-production basis, looked uncoordinated and would trip over his own feet, and framed his path as binary: either he gets much better at football or he's out of the league.
- 2026-01-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and JJ Zachariason (not tracked) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-29]] [Dynasty]: the Rams' pick of Ferguson signaled their move away from a historically ~85%-11-personnel identity - Zachariason argues Ferguson's early value isn't as a pass catcher but as a fast, big-bodied clear-out threat who can be flexed out wide in 13 personnel to occupy two-high safety attention, letting the true WR1 ([[Puka Nacua]]) dominate a shrunken target pool. Harmon calls this the 'sacrificial X receiver of tight ends' role and notes Ferguson already looks like a physical freak even though his blocking still needs to develop; both hosts see his receiving role growing as that part of his game refines.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris said the Rams will 'always use three tight ends,' and floated [[Terrance Ferguson]] as the one from that group who 'exerts himself' enough that he still has to prove real ceiling -- someone worth watching for a bigger role jump rather than settling in as purely the third option.
- 2026-06-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-29]]) [Dynasty]: Currently has Terrance Ferguson ahead of [[Colby Parkinson]] in yardage heading into year two and calls him the room's 'big play guy' with 'all the magical talents you would want,' but says opportunity in a crowded target tree (also including [[Tyler Higbee]] and rookie [[Max Klare]]) is the key swing factor, and he isn't confident Ferguson gets enough volume yet to be exciting.
- 2026-07-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-21]]): despite only 12 catches as a rookie (11 regular season, 1 playoff), Ferguson 'looked like a stud playmaker' on tape and Harris now ranks him TE19, calling him part of his 'super deep sleeper' list for 2026. He cited three specific reps: a Week 10 slot snap where Ferguson shook 49ers linebacker Luke Gifford for a 32-yard gain, a Week 16 cut upfield past Seahawks LB Drake Thomas for 27 yards, and a Week 17 hitch-and-go past Falcons safety Xavier Watts for 27 more -- noting a full half his catches came on 20-plus air-yard throws, and that at times Ferguson's tape was hard to distinguish from Puka Nacua's. Harris floated that the Rams' heavy 13-personnel use (with Colby Parkinson and Tyler Higbee, plus 2nd-round rookie Max Klare) may not actually crowd Ferguson out if he's effectively a slot receiver. Guest Ben Gretsch (Stealing Signals newsletter, not a tracked expert) independently flagged Ferguson given his rookie-year aDOT and TD/yardage efficiency, comparing the setup to how [[Kyren Williams]] and [[Blake Corum]] each saw lightly-used rookie years balloon into real roles in year two. Both agreed August camp buzz on Ferguson is worth monitoring closely given the crowded depth chart, but neither is drafting him as a starter yet.
- 2026-08-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-28]]): Harris says he would have loved to put Ferguson on the Super Deep list and was glad Andy Behrens (untracked, Yacht Club show) talked him up as a dynasty buy on Wednesday, since it let Harris rave about how good Ferguson's limited rookie-year work actually was. But Harris says there's now too much hype and too many drafters taking him for Ferguson to still qualify as a deep sleeper -- his ADP has caught up to where Harris valued him when writing the Almanac, so he's off the list.
- 2026-09-08 — According to Scott Fish (not a tracked expert -- Fantasy Cares/Scott Fishbowl) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-09-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Fish's #5 Week 1 waiver add is rookie [[Terrance Ferguson]], whom Harris says he 'raved about ... in the almanac' as a low-sample-size player who 'made an impression on film' last year, but Harris admits he can't yet talk himself into starting Ferguson even on his own deeper-league rosters. Both hosts plan to watch the Rams-Niners Australia game to see whether the camp buzz around Ferguson translates to real usage; Harris took him twice this summer in deep, tight-end-scarce bench leagues purely as a speculative second TE, not a planned starter.
