# Joe Milton III

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-01]]): the episode's clearest boom/bust arm-talent case, and Waldman's explicit developmental pick. Arm strength described as "incredible" — 80-yard throws thrown flat-footed, a specific 64-65-yard sideline-to-sideline dart both hosts independently replayed multiple times to confirm what they'd seen, and a widely-shared clip of him throwing an orange 110 yards. The flip side: "he sometimes just can't hit the ocean." Direct Malik Willis comp — a player whose ceiling is a genuine Pro Bowl-caliber arm and whose floor is out of the league within a few years, which makes his eventual draft slot a real risk either way (a top-five pick at the ceiling outcome, "not draftable at all" at the floor). Waldman's explicit recommendation: the "Jordan Love treatment" — he "shouldn't see the field for two, three years," citing Malik Willis's stalled development as the cautionary comp for a talented arm rushed onto the field too early.

- 2024-03-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-08]]), solo cast on the full QB class, restating the boom/bust arm-talent case: "all the physical and athletic tools to succeed," with arm strength "among the best I've ever seen" in distance/velocity on vertical throws, plus viable legs as a runner. The conceptual side still needs work — too slow processing coverage in dense areas of the field, gets impatient and misses slow-developing routes just after abandoning the play's structure ("kind of Jayden Daniels-esque in that regard, but a little worse"). Best in a scheme with one or two reads before he's freed to create off-structure. Flags a common big-arm-QB problem: takes unnecessary hits trying to be a hero, leaning on size and arm strength rather than quick, efficient decisions. Expects him to get a training-camp look on physical tools alone, but "wouldn't be surprised if he's playing in a different league" before a second NFL look in a few years.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): the reports that Milton was outperforming [[Drake Maye]] in New England camp are, in Waldman's view, somewhat superficial — a big body with a big arm who throws deep and moves well will win early-camp practice reps, but the reasons Maye was a first-round pick and Milton was not are still the reasons. Waldman stressed the missing context: we do not know whether Milton got easy looks, whether he was on the field as a tool to stress-test the defense, or whether the staff deliberately put him in situations where they expected him to fail. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) noted Volin later reported Maye had been better over the previous four days and was clearly the number two, which Waldman accepted as reasonable.
- 2025-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-04]]), reacting to the trade sending Milton from New England to Dallas: he does not understand the Milton hype 'at all', calling him a random backup quarterback whose entire case is one Week 18 start and a good preseason. Harmon notes Milton was not a good or even productive college player in a 'Looney Tunes' Tennessee offense, and that his Week 18 line (22-of-29, 76% completion, 241 yards, one passing and one rushing touchdown, no interceptions) came against a Bills defense playing backups — he reads the snap counts to make the point. He also questions the destination: Dallas just paid [[Dak Prescott]] $60 million a year with a nine-figure dead-cap barrier, so there is nothing to compete for. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) disagrees, arguing the 6-5, 250-pound frame, huge arm and athleticism are a genuinely tantalizing toolkit and that unlike [[Trey Lance]], Milton has at least shown passing ability at the NFL level. Harmon's reply: 'maybe I get it, but I don't like it.'
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]): Waldman framed Milton as either a Daunte Culpepper-style year-two breakout or a case of New England selling Dallas 'a pool pass under the high school basketball court', and leans hard to the latter. He recalls former NFL scout Russ Lande telling him Milton was the type teams draft late hoping the arm and body either develop or flash enough to be flipped for profit — Waldman thinks the Patriots executed exactly that trade, comparing it to Carolina repeatedly clowning Dallas in deals. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked) is blunter: some of the worst throws he has seen on an NFL field, no touch — 'a jugs machine trying to throw a flat five yards out' — and says Milton truly cannot read a defense, adding that people think Milton is what [[Jalen Milroe]] actually is. Kluge allows a superflex spot start if [[Dak Prescott]] misses time, but sees no long-term NFL career.
