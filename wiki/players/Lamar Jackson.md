# Lamar Jackson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]] (recorded before the *Film and Theory* episode below, which cites it): Jackson is his purest case of pace control. "Lamar Jackson never reaches top gear" — the polar opposite of [[Isiah Pacheco]], where "you get zero or a hundred." With Jackson "you're getting what you need to do on a given down to be successful," dictating the pace and flow of the game and taking four or five rather than risking the negative-three play that puts the offense behind the sticks. Framework: [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]].
- 2023-12-21 — **Angelo**, same episode, on the structure argument — a direct challenge to the standard framing of Jackson: "people thought he was very good with no structure, which is true; people thought he's not very good when there is structure, which is false. He's very good when there's structure. They're so good without structure that they don't need structure — it's not that they're not good in structure." His mechanism: Jackson understands "the bandwidth of what the play can give them." Note this sits *alongside*, not against, Waldman's scheme-dependency call below — Angelo is arguing about Jackson's ability inside structure, Waldman about which throws a system must hide.
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, on pocket poise, and against a piece of contemporaneous analysis: a Darius Butler breakdown (retweeted by Greg Cosell) framed as Jackson "really improving from the pocket" drew a blunt response — "that's bullshit." Waldman's position is that Jackson "has always been great from the pocket, and scouts who didn't write him off as a wide receiver playing quarterback knew that a long time ago," operating in tighter confines than the highlights Butler used even showed. His image: "Lamar Jackson could have a bomb blow up in the pocket and I would bet he'd be one of the few quarterbacks that would dodge the shrapnel and still throw the ball downfield" — against [[Kyler Murray]], who "runs as if a bomb has blown up in the pocket" when anyone gets within three yards. Waldman calls it "inadvertent historical revisionism" rather than bad analysis by Butler.
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): the episode's deliberately contrarian call. On a Footballguys staff roundtable he asked which of Dak Prescott, Lamar Jackson, [[C.J. Stroud]] and [[Brock Purdy]] was **most** scheme-dependent; everyone answered Purdy and accused him of baiting them. Waldman answered **Lamar Jackson**. Two-part reasoning: (1) the popular case for Jackson's independence rests on off-structure creation, but "the percentage of off-structure plays that quarterbacks make is small relative to what they do on structure," so it can't carry the argument; (2) on structure Jackson "doesn't make those types of reads outside the numbers very well — he's not a good outside-the-numbers thrower beyond a certain distance," citing arm and placement limitations relative to what he does in the middle of the field. His evidence that this is a constraint rather than a preference: Todd Monken's offense looks very different from Greg Roman's, yet both are still built to keep him from making throws that a vast majority of NFL systems ask quarterbacks to make.
- 2023-12-21 — **Waldman**, same episode, the important qualifier: he has been "a huge proponent of Lamar Jackson as an NFL-caliber quarterback for a long time," and scheme dependence is not a knock — "he could be more desirable than anybody if you fit him to the right system, the same way that [[Deebo Samuel]] is... it shouldn't be considered a pejorative thing." The fantasy read is about *fragility to a coaching change*, not about talent.
- 2023-12-21 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode, in passing: lists Jackson among the modern-era players who would have been wasted in a scheme-centric decade — Colin Kaepernick and Pat White could do much of the same thing, and offenses of that era said "we're doing what we're doing, I don't care what you can do." Also cites Jackson's change of direction in tight space alongside the small-back group in [[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]].
- 2024-01-01 — Both hosts on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]] [Redraft (Standard)]: unqualified 2024 QB1 — "I have zero arguments for why he should not be." Waldman notes the era-wide two-high-shell defensive trend is suppressing quarterback touchdown counts league-wide, offered as context for critics citing Jackson's low passing-touchdown total in his MVP season rather than as a talent knock — consistent with his 2023-12-21 scheme-dependency read above.
- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): was "buying the hype big time" on the Ravens offense in the 2023 preseason and calls it good process even though he only ended up rostering Mark Andrews, Rashad Bateman and Gus Edwards from it rather than Jackson himself. Cites Odell Beckham Jr. — by Waldman's framing, enough of a well-traveled locker-room veteran to know — calling it "by far the best offense I've ever played on." Frames Jackson's whole career arc as "always proving the public wrong," most recently on whether the new scheme was actually different (it visibly was, on film, once the offense gelled by midseason).
- 2024-01-04 — **Waldman**, same episode, a qualifier: says he wouldn't personally hand Jackson 2023 NFL MVP despite ranking him the league's best quarterback from roughly Week 8 onward, on the grounds that MVP is a full-season award and Baltimore's offense hadn't gelled over the first half of the year — "I'm not going to be mad if he gets it," but the slow start matters to his own ballot.
