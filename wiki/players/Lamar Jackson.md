# Lamar Jackson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]] (recorded before the *Film and Theory* episode below, which cites it): Jackson is his purest case of pace control. "Lamar Jackson never reaches top gear" — the polar opposite of [[Isiah Pacheco]], where "you get zero or a hundred." With Jackson "you're getting what you need to do on a given down to be successful," dictating the pace and flow of the game and taking four or five rather than risking the negative-three play that puts the offense behind the sticks. Framework: [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]].
- 2023-12-21 — **Angelo**, same episode, on the structure argument — a direct challenge to the standard framing of Jackson: "people thought he was very good with no structure, which is true; people thought he's not very good when there is structure, which is false. He's very good when there's structure. They're so good without structure that they don't need structure — it's not that they're not good in structure." His mechanism: Jackson understands "the bandwidth of what the play can give them." Note this sits *alongside*, not against, Waldman's scheme-dependency call below — Angelo is arguing about Jackson's ability inside structure, Waldman about which throws a system must hide.
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, on pocket poise, and against a piece of contemporaneous analysis: a Darius Butler breakdown (retweeted by Greg Cosell) framed as Jackson "really improving from the pocket" drew a blunt response — "that's bullshit." Waldman's position is that Jackson "has always been great from the pocket, and scouts who didn't write him off as a wide receiver playing quarterback knew that a long time ago," operating in tighter confines than the highlights Butler used even showed. His image: "Lamar Jackson could have a bomb blow up in the pocket and I would bet he'd be one of the few quarterbacks that would dodge the shrapnel and still throw the ball downfield" — against [[Kyler Murray]], who "runs as if a bomb has blown up in the pocket" when anyone gets within three yards. Waldman calls it "inadvertent historical revisionism" rather than bad analysis by Butler.
- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): the episode's deliberately contrarian call. On a Footballguys staff roundtable he asked which of Dak Prescott, Lamar Jackson, [[C.J. Stroud]] and [[Brock Purdy]] was **most** scheme-dependent; everyone answered Purdy and accused him of baiting them. Waldman answered **Lamar Jackson**. Two-part reasoning: (1) the popular case for Jackson's independence rests on off-structure creation, but "the percentage of off-structure plays that quarterbacks make is small relative to what they do on structure," so it can't carry the argument; (2) on structure Jackson "doesn't make those types of reads outside the numbers very well — he's not a good outside-the-numbers thrower beyond a certain distance," citing arm and placement limitations relative to what he does in the middle of the field. His evidence that this is a constraint rather than a preference: Todd Monken's offense looks very different from Greg Roman's, yet both are still built to keep him from making throws that a vast majority of NFL systems ask quarterbacks to make.
- 2023-12-21 — **Waldman**, same episode, the important qualifier: he has been "a huge proponent of Lamar Jackson as an NFL-caliber quarterback for a long time," and scheme dependence is not a knock — "he could be more desirable than anybody if you fit him to the right system, the same way that [[Deebo Samuel]] is... it shouldn't be considered a pejorative thing." The fantasy read is about *fragility to a coaching change*, not about talent.
- 2023-12-21 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode, in passing: lists Jackson among the modern-era players who would have been wasted in a scheme-centric decade — Colin Kaepernick and Pat White could do much of the same thing, and offenses of that era said "we're doing what we're doing, I don't care what you can do." Also cites Jackson's change of direction in tight space alongside the small-back group in [[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]].
- 2024-01-01 — Both hosts on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]] [Redraft (Standard)]: unqualified 2024 QB1 — "I have zero arguments for why he should not be." Waldman notes the era-wide two-high-shell defensive trend is suppressing quarterback touchdown counts league-wide, offered as context for critics citing Jackson's low passing-touchdown total in his MVP season rather than as a talent knock — consistent with his 2023-12-21 scheme-dependency read above.
- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): was "buying the hype big time" on the Ravens offense in the 2023 preseason and calls it good process even though he only ended up rostering Mark Andrews, Rashad Bateman and Gus Edwards from it rather than Jackson himself. Cites Odell Beckham Jr. — by Waldman's framing, enough of a well-traveled locker-room veteran to know — calling it "by far the best offense I've ever played on." Frames Jackson's whole career arc as "always proving the public wrong," most recently on whether the new scheme was actually different (it visibly was, on film, once the offense gelled by midseason).
- 2024-01-04 — **Waldman**, same episode, a qualifier: says he wouldn't personally hand Jackson 2023 NFL MVP despite ranking him the league's best quarterback from roughly Week 8 onward, on the grounds that MVP is a full-season award and Baltimore's offense hadn't gelled over the first half of the year — "I'm not going to be mad if he gets it," but the slow start matters to his own ballot.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): "the top three quarterback in the NFL, I think, undisputedly," ahead of the Ravens' Divisional Round matchup with Houston — offered in passing while breaking down Baltimore's defense, not as new film analysis.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]): both hosts pick the Ravens over the Chiefs in the AFC Championship, crediting Baltimore's defense and a Jackson-led offense playing better football overall down the stretch than Kansas City's shorthanded receiver room.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]), after Baltimore's AFC Championship loss to Kansas City: an extended, personal defense of Jackson's postgame comments (angry with himself, defending his teammates) as evidence of genuine accountability rather than false modesty, tied explicitly to the racial double standard Waldman says Jackson has navigated throughout his career. The football case for real 2024 growth: this was Todd Monken's **first** year running this offense with Jackson, the receiver room was gutted all season (Rashad Bateman hurt, Odell Beckham Jr. "always going to be a part-time player," Isaiah Likely good but not Mark Andrews's level, Andrews himself hurt most of the year, and Jackson lost J.K. Dobbins — "easily could have been your best offensive skill player" — early), and an entire offseason of self-scouting plus a full season of tape on defenses now familiar with Monken's scheme should translate into real year-two gains. Explicit 2024 value call: "if he's not quarterback two, he's quarterback three," expected to supplant [[Patrick Mahomes]] at the top of redraft ranks — with the one live question being whether he passes Jalen Hurts, complicated by Philadelphia's new hire of Kellen Moore as offensive coordinator, which Waldman flags as a real variable nobody can grade yet ("people are going to have feelings about that without seeing any evidence of outcomes"). Separately faults Baltimore's offensive staff for not committing more to the run game with Justice Hill and Gus Edwards in the AFC Championship itself.
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]), a notably more critical read of the same AFC Championship loss than Waldman's: three turnover-worthy throws per PFF (6.4% of attempts, the highest rate of any quarterback that weekend), headlined by "an absolute head-scratcher" — a bad read, badly thrown, into triple coverage on Isaiah Likely (not a tracked page), underthrown by four or five yards. Ravens' designed-run rate that game ranked 563rd of 568 games played league-wide all season (including playoffs) — Gus Edwards and Justice Hill combined for six carries — which both hosts call a play-calling and structural failure, not just a Jackson issue, though free rushers getting home and Jackson holding the ball too long are logged as his share of it. Raises, without resolving, whether "playoff losses are starting to mount" for Jackson given a similarly tight first half a week earlier against Houston. The floor of the read stays very high regardless: "top three quarterback in the league, in my opinion" — full stop, no hedge.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]): Waldman calls Lamar Jackson the safest quarterback in the top five for fantasy, arguing counterintuitively that his rushing style (getting out of bounds, avoiding contact, forcing missed tackles) actually limits injury risk more than pocket exposure would. He credits OC Todd Monken with unlocking pre-snap read progress and diagnostic skill last year, noting Jackson was still a top-12 QB despite Mark Andrews missing time and a shaky offensive line. With Andrews back, Isaiah Likely capable of top-15 TE production even without him, Rashod Bateman better than his reputation, Zay Flowers coming off a strong rookie year, and Derrick Henry added at RB, Waldman sees enough weapon depth to keep pushing Jackson's ceiling as he pushes the ball downfield more.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: don't worry about Jackson because of the offensive line — Waldman issues a 'Chris Jones pass'. There are not many Chris Joneses in the NFL, and Kansas City's ability to line him up wide-nine and move him around confused a young line, compounded by Nick Bolton as a very good blitzer. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) accepted the logic but immediately demanded the same courtesy for [[Kirk Cousins]] against T.J. Watt; Waldman refused, arguing Cousins has already been exploited in ways future defenses can replicate, and the two agreed to revisit it.
- 2024-10-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-15]]): asked whether this is the best passing offense of the Jackson era, Harmon argues the process says yes even where the raw numbers do not — Koh (not tracked) counters that by EPA it clearly is not, citing Jackson's 2019 MVP season passing EPA of 155.5 against roughly 55.2 through six games in 2024, while granting that this unit is far more varied than the 2019 group where Marquise Brown and Willie Snead flanked Mark Andrews. Harmon credits three things: an offensive line that has stabilized over three weeks (Roger Rosengarten at right tackle, Ronnie Stanley playing his best ball since his injuries), the unlocked usage of [[Zay Flowers]], and the Todd Monken marriage of run and pass after the Greg Roman split. Jackson is fifth in PFF passer rating on play action; Harmon rejects the 'play action is divorced from the run game' finding on human grounds — defenders bite a quarter-second harder with [[Derrick Henry]] back there than with Gus Edwards. Matchup caveat: Cincinnati and Washington are two of the NFL's worst defenses.
- 2024-10-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-24]]): Waldman argues Jackson is a better quarterback than Troy Aikman ever was — Aikman was a good quarterback with one of the best offensive lines ever, one of the great running backs ever, an elite possession receiver, a terrific coach and a great defense — but the award standard is wins and Super Bowls, which colors Aikman's on-air commentary. He notes it took seven years for a major-stage analyst (Peyton Manning, on Monday night) to say what was apparent on the Louisville film: Jackson is a pocket-first quarterback who fights to stay in the pocket before he runs and happens to also be one of the great runners in the game. Waldman also uses Jackson's early career as the template for [[Anthony Richardson]] — unrefined feet early, small-picture criticism blown out of proportion, and the managers who bought Lamar are the ones who will stay patient now.
- 2025-01-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-21]]): blame for Baltimore's divisional-round loss to Buffalo splits roughly 50-50 between Jackson and his teammates. Harmon calls the fumble 'inexcusable' and notes those fumbles are still 'in his bag' even after he cut mistakes down dramatically in 2024, and reads the interception on the All-22 as a miscommunication with [[Rashad Bateman]] against blitz-and-man looks Baltimore did not expect — Bateman broke to the sideline expecting the safety to crash while Jackson threw as if he would keep working inside. Harmon refuses to litigate the 'playoff loser' discourse, comparing it to what was said about Peyton Manning before his first Super Bowl, and calls Jackson a surefire Hall of Famer who could go the Manning path or the Marino path. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) agrees the 50-50 split is fair this year specifically.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates Jackson a 6 out of 10, purely as a draft-cost objection rather than a player objection — Bell has been happiest this draft season being the last person in his league to take a quarterback, preferring to roll out a couple of [[Caleb Williams]], [[Justin Fields]] and [[Jordan Love]], and spending a second-round pick on a QB when [[Josh Jacobs]] is on the board is the risk. Harris rates Jackson a 3 for the same stated reason, having no question about the player and simply not taking him there — and immediately notes the two of them had the opposite reflex on [[Brock Bowers]]. Bell also gave [[Josh Allen]] a 6 and Harris a 3, on identical logic.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): Detroit opened in nothing but man coverage and it visibly flustered Jackson — Harris repeatedly saw him hold the ball waiting for someone to come open when nobody did, leading to seven sacks, tying a career high last reached in 2021 against Pittsburgh, and one scramble fumble at the goal line. Harris was sarcastic about the fantasy panic: Jackson still threw for 288 yards and three touchdowns and ran for 35, 'yeah, what a bum that guy is.' The caveat is real though — he looked confused far more often than usual, and Harris credited Detroit's man-coverage plan plus late zone mixes rather than any decline in Jackson.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): Jackson left the Chiefs game with an apparent hamstring problem and sat on the bench without getting treatment, which Harris finds telling — if it were bad enough to leave a game you would expect heat or ice quickly, or for him to go to the tent or locker room. He and guest Jamie Eisenberg (CBS, not a tracked expert) land on part pout, part hamstring; neither saw visible hobbling on his last play, and Eisenberg says he hates being a Zapruder analyst with injuries. Eisenberg's fantasy stance is unchanged on talent — healthy or limited, if Lamar starts an NFL game you start him in fantasy — but he calls the whole team broken right now: 1-3 against the hardest schedule, run game struggling, line banged up with Ronnie Stanley out, defense a joke. Baltimore's bye is Week 7, they get Houston next, and they may simply decide not to risk him.
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-09]]): Jackson should have been evaluated the way the industry evaluated [[Josh Allen]] — as a quarterback who was already good in the pocket, handled it well, and had shown accuracy and experience in a genuine pro-style offense under Bobby Petrino, who happened to also be a great athlete and needed time and opportunity like any other quarterback. Instead, Waldman argued, bias ran the other way: Jackson got 'training wheels he didn't need' while Allen got the ones he did need, and it took Peyton Manning saying it on air roughly four years later for mass media to accept a truth that he says was apparent on Louisville tape. He framed it bluntly as majority-view bias operating in favor of the people applying it.
