# Quentin Johnston

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]: calls Johnston "legitimately one of the top prospects in the NFL" pre-draft but sees "nothing" from his rookie year despite the Chargers giving him every opportunity to integrate into the offense — "I don't see the path to relevance." Draws the parallel to Davante Adams's poor first two seasons before he broke out, and hopes Johnston shows the same mental resilience, while not fully writing him off.
- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode: locates the problem more precisely than a talent gap. Johnston is "actually a better route runner coming in than he is a pass catcher" — his tape at TCU already showed he could get open, but flagged tracking and attack-position issues at the catch point (playing the ball with his hands at his chest rather than attacking it at its high point), the same category of flaw that sank prospects like Sammy Coates. Waldman had him rated lower than consensus pre-draft for exactly this reason, and projects his best long-term fit is an underneath/slot role (a Keenan Allen-style trait profile) rather than the outside "X" deep-threat role (a Mike Williams replacement) the Chargers actually drafted him to play.
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty], reacting to Adam Harstad's [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] score (bottom quintile of the class): labels him "the Tevin Coleman of wide receivers" — mostly unproductive plays punctuated by occasional huge ones, mirroring Coleman's Ohio State tape. Says he's selling for any reasonable return given the price is still propped up by first-round draft capital, but would still add him for free off waivers, with the emergence path running through the same Keenan Allen-style slot role rather than the outside role he was drafted for.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): revisited as the cautionary scheme-fit case study while grading the 2024 receiver prospects — his rookie season is now described as "one of the all-time worst Reception Perception rookie seasons ever." Both hosts explicitly separate talent from circumstance: the Chargers only needed him at X because Mike Williams got hurt, a role his college tape never really supported ("there was just really nothing on Quentin Johnston's film that indicated... this guy's going to be an X receiver"); had he landed somewhere that could play him in the slot or off the line more, "his career turns out, or at least starts out, completely differently." Not treated as a closed case — "his career is not over" — but used explicitly as the worked example for why landing spot/scheme usage can matter more than draft-class talent grade in year one.

- 2024-03-01 — **[[Brandon Angelo]]**, relayed by [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-01]]: used as the worked example of 40-time measurement error mattering financially — Angelo's estimation is Johnston is "probably a four five athlete all day long," but as a long-limbed three-point-stance starter he'd likely post a slow start and a materially slower measured time layered with the Combine's own timing-system error (est. 0.24–0.3s standard deviation), which Waldman says is enough of a gap to flip a receiver from a first-round grade to "a third day at best." Johnston's agent likely saved him real money by declining to run. See [[NFL Combine and Pro Day Skepticism]].

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): revisited while debating whether the Chargers belong in the "critical" or "substantial" wide receiver need bucket — called "a stone cold disaster" as a first-round pick, noting the Chargers are (for now) the NFL's highest spender at wide receiver ($76M) with arguably its worst receiver room, and pins the miss partly on the previous coaching regime: "you probably just lit that pick on fire."

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]), after the Keenan Allen trade left the Chargers with a Josh Palmer (not a tracked page)/Johnston starting duo: called it, off the top of the head, likely the worst starting receiver duo in the NFL — debated only against the Giants' Darius Slayton/Wan'Dale Robinson pairing before concluding the Chargers duo is still worse.

- 2024-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-26]]), while discussing [[Rome Odunze]]'s contested-catch profile: cites Johnston as the counterexample of a big receiver who wasn't good at the catch point — reads it as more of a technique problem (a "mess technically," not a real technician) than a soft-hands or physical issue.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): named
  (paired with [[Josh Palmer]]) as the single worst receiver room in the NFL,
  even ahead of the Giants, Patriots and Cardinals — "I don't know how you can
  have any optimism about Quentin Johnston based off his rookie season film,"
  though not ruled out entirely ("maybe he turns it around").
