# Quentin Johnston

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]: calls Johnston "legitimately one of the top prospects in the NFL" pre-draft but sees "nothing" from his rookie year despite the Chargers giving him every opportunity to integrate into the offense — "I don't see the path to relevance." Draws the parallel to Davante Adams's poor first two seasons before he broke out, and hopes Johnston shows the same mental resilience, while not fully writing him off.
- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode: locates the problem more precisely than a talent gap. Johnston is "actually a better route runner coming in than he is a pass catcher" — his tape at TCU already showed he could get open, but flagged tracking and attack-position issues at the catch point (playing the ball with his hands at his chest rather than attacking it at its high point), the same category of flaw that sank prospects like Sammy Coates. Waldman had him rated lower than consensus pre-draft for exactly this reason, and projects his best long-term fit is an underneath/slot role (a Keenan Allen-style trait profile) rather than the outside "X" deep-threat role (a Mike Williams replacement) the Chargers actually drafted him to play.
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty], reacting to Adam Harstad's [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] score (bottom quintile of the class): labels him "the Tevin Coleman of wide receivers" — mostly unproductive plays punctuated by occasional huge ones, mirroring Coleman's Ohio State tape. Says he's selling for any reasonable return given the price is still propped up by first-round draft capital, but would still add him for free off waivers, with the emergence path running through the same Keenan Allen-style slot role rather than the outside role he was drafted for.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): revisited as the cautionary scheme-fit case study while grading the 2024 receiver prospects — his rookie season is now described as "one of the all-time worst Reception Perception rookie seasons ever." Both hosts explicitly separate talent from circumstance: the Chargers only needed him at X because Mike Williams got hurt, a role his college tape never really supported ("there was just really nothing on Quentin Johnston's film that indicated... this guy's going to be an X receiver"); had he landed somewhere that could play him in the slot or off the line more, "his career turns out, or at least starts out, completely differently." Not treated as a closed case — "his career is not over" — but used explicitly as the worked example for why landing spot/scheme usage can matter more than draft-class talent grade in year one.

- 2024-03-01 — **[[Brandon Angelo]]**, relayed by [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-01]]: used as the worked example of 40-time measurement error mattering financially — Angelo's estimation is Johnston is "probably a four five athlete all day long," but as a long-limbed three-point-stance starter he'd likely post a slow start and a materially slower measured time layered with the Combine's own timing-system error (est. 0.24–0.3s standard deviation), which Waldman says is enough of a gap to flip a receiver from a first-round grade to "a third day at best." Johnston's agent likely saved him real money by declining to run. See [[NFL Combine and Pro Day Skepticism]].

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): revisited while debating whether the Chargers belong in the "critical" or "substantial" wide receiver need bucket — called "a stone cold disaster" as a first-round pick, noting the Chargers are (for now) the NFL's highest spender at wide receiver ($76M) with arguably its worst receiver room, and pins the miss partly on the previous coaching regime: "you probably just lit that pick on fire."

- 2024-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-20]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman called the Chargers' public praise for Quentin Johnston this spring "springtime" coach-speak, not a real plan — the priority is getting [[Justin Herbert]] and the offense competent, not developing a project receiver, so he expects Josh Palmer, Ladd McConkey and Gerald Everett to work underneath while Johnston's snaps stay inconsistent. He referenced Johnston's widely-circulated Reception Perception profile (poor catch technique, described as "claps") and said the team essentially has to rebuild him as a receiver from scratch rather than refine him. - 2024-05-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-23]]): Johnston's rookie season (38-431-2 on 17 games, 56.7% catch rate) produced one of the worst Reception Perception profiles ever charted -- an almost entirely red route tree, green only on the screen route. He lined up outside on 88% of snaps (91.3% of the sampled RP snaps) with essentially no slot or motion usage, running a nine route on 27% of his routes. His 44.1% success rate vs. man is the 5th-worst in RP history (behind Justin Hunter 31.9%, Terrace Marshall 39.5%, Jalen Ragin 40.8%, and Devante Parker 42.9%); his press mark is the 2nd-worst ever charted (32.3%, behind Terrace Marshall); and his 61.9% vs. zone ranks 9th-worst ever. He was also poor in contested catch (12.5% catch rate on a 22% contested-target rate), a flaw that dated back to his college profile. Harmon frames Johnston as 'set up to fail' -- the prior Chargers regime (Tom Telesco/Brandon Staley) plugged a rookie with a college profile showing he wasn't ready into a true X-receiver role, the hardest receiver role in the NFL, behind Keenan Allen and Mike Williams' injury -- but also says Johnston never forced the offense to manufacture him touches (only 3.1% of his routes came with him in space; he went down on first contact 87.5% of the time). Harmon rejects Davante Adams' 47%-to-79.6% turnaround as a realistic comp ('I don't think anybody's ever going to do that again'), instead pointing to Devante Parker (developed into a productive non-separator via contested-catch ability, which Johnston lacks) and Devin Funchess (bounced back to a solid 2017 season before moving to tight end) as more realistic outcomes. James Koh (untracked co-host) suggested simplifying Johnston's route tree to just nines and comebacks; Harmon disagreed, noting those are Johnston's worst routes technically, and instead advocated moving him off X duty into manufactured touches, play-action shots and big-over routes to use his athleticism without requiring route-running refinement, specifically citing his lack of hip-sink/throttle-down ability as the core fix needed. He hit 55+ yards only once and 35+ yards three times in 17 games despite a 70-86% snap share alongside Justin Herbert. For 2024, with Ladd McConkey drafted as the slot/flanker piece and Josh Palmer, DJ Chark and a healthy Mike Williams also in the mix under new coach Jim Harbaugh -- who Harmon says has zero loyalty to Johnston's draft status -- Harmon is doubtful Johnston secures a clear role and calls him 'a pure reclamation project.'
- 2024-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-13]]): Harmon reiterates a stance from a prior episode that Quentin Johnston shouldn't play X receiver based on both his college tape and his rookie-year charting, and would be better served as an outside flanker. But looking at the Chargers' thin depth chart, Harmon concedes Johnston is effectively the team's best X-receiver candidate by default unless D.J. Chark beats him out for the role. [Dynasty]
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]): Waldman is 'feeling' the idea that Quentin Johnston will be a significant disappointment relative to his draft slot, comparing the Chargers' plan to use him as a primary boundary receiver to how the Eagles misused Jalen Reagor by asking him to 'go up and win targets' when that isn't his real strength. He compares Johnston's college tape to Tevin Coleman's - a player whose mistakes were masked by superior college athleticism but don't fly in the NFL - and says Johnston's ball-tracking and hand-placement issues are difficult to unlearn this late in his career. Waldman's better long-term fit for Johnston is a slot/quick in-breaking role, comping it to Rashod Bateman's usage in Greg Roman's offense, but says that shift would make him a complementary piece rather than a true WR1 a defense fears in a game-deciding one-on-one situation. Waldman says he is not actively drafting Johnston, and Harris agrees he shouldn't be prioritized either.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon reiterated that Johnston 'had one of the worst rookie seasons I've ever charted' for separation, placing him in a 'pretty treacherous zone' historically. Guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn (not tracked) framed the Chargers' offensive line -- ranked 7th overall, anchored by LT Rashawn Slater and rookie RT Joe Alt -- as one of the more positive infrastructure situations in the league even amid skepticism about the receiver room, expecting a run-first, 60/40 split under Jim Harbaugh and Greg Roman that limits true drop-back exposure. - 2024-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-01]]): Johnston's rookie Reception Perception profile was so bad Harmon double-checked the math — 1st and 2nd percentile against man, zone and press, the worst of the in-season rookie class charted. The Chargers deployed him as a pure X (91.3% of sampled snaps outside, 74% on the line), which Harmon says was never his game: nothing at TCU showed a day-one X who could track the ball over his shoulder, and camp video still shows him struggling with it. Harmon thinks he should have been used the way [[Rashee Rice]] was — off the line, some slot, crossers over the middle. - 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]): Waldman uses Johnston as his prime coach-speak example and remains negative on the catching. Jim Harbaugh said in an interview that Johnston is really good at tracking and catching the football; Waldman's response is that if Harbaugh means it, he cannot be watching the player closely — it contradicts the drops and mishandled targets Johnston has had since college and early in the pros, particularly mistracking the ball on jump-ball plays in tight coverage that a quarterback like [[Justin Herbert]] should be able to trust him on. His explanation is not that Harbaugh is lying but that head coaches are scheme-obsessed front-facing managers who look at results and situations, not at receiver process and technique.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Film and Theory)]]) [Dynasty]: he treats Jim Harbaugh's camp praise — that Johnston catches the ball really well and has always been a good pass catcher — as a live test of whether the coaching staff actually studies the tape. Waldman's read is that Johnston catches certain targets cleanly but has been mixed at best on exactly the targets that would differentiate him athletically: contested-catch situations where he has to jump and track the ball, fades, and downfield vertical throws where his size and length should be the advantage. If Johnston has genuinely improved his ball tracking, Waldman will credit Harbaugh and WR coach Sanjay Lal (whose Buffalo reputation he called more hype than substance early on) and trust them on player development; if not, he files Harbaugh with the long list of coaches who should stick to talking about skiing. Harstad's gloss: coaches are surprisingly honest, they just often do not know what they are talking about.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman totally feels that the Chargers are finally using Johnston correctly. Last year's misuse was deploying him as a Courtland Sutton-style boundary ball-winner on deep vertical routes; what he actually does best is underneath — slants, hitches, little digs — where he can attack the ball without difficult tracking adjustments and get his hands into position. He still fought the ball on some looks in this game, but when he caught it he turned upfield fast, showed power and excellent explosion. Waldman wouldn't be surprised if Johnston finishes as one of the top two receivers in this passing game, behind [[Ladd McConkey]]. He also noted [[Alec Pierce]] out-demonstrated Johnston on contested catches even though Johnston had the better game.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): a mildly encouraging Week 1 — 38 yards on 22 routes, second on the team, with three missed tackles forced, 9.3 yards after the catch per reception and 6.3 yards after contact per reception per Fantasy Points. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) notices the Chargers stopped asking him to catch anything over his shoulder: essentially everything came back toward the quarterback on stop-type routes rather than overs or drags, and he looked more comfortable that way. Harmon agrees that's who Johnston was in college — get him working on quicker routes and let the explosiveness show — and notes he lined up outside on 92% of snaps, appearing 'locked and loaded' as the Chargers' X receiver. The framing is still qualified: Koh's own caveat is that 'Quentin Johnston is Quentin Johnston.'
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-12]]): Week 1 was the first time the Chargers used Johnston the way he should be used. The league mis-projected him by taking TCU's template — contested-catch vertical shots and ball-tracking — and assuming it would carry, when by an NFL standard he was actually bad at that on his college tape, and it did not work in Los Angeles. What does work: he is a good underneath route runner, quick and physical, and catches cleanly working across the field or squared up with time, rather than leaping back and hunting for his hands. Waldman saw a lot of clean catches and yards after the catch, and compares the ideal usage to a [[Rashad Bateman]]-type role under Greg Roman — over the middle, working across the defense, quick hitters. Honest caveat: Johnston still did not look fully confident to him, and Waldman is unsure how much of that is his own residual skepticism about the hands, since the plays looked smoother on rewatch.

That makes him a usage-fit story rather than a development story, and Waldman says he 'still had a little bit more upside' than [[Treylon Burks]], whose team is still asking him to do what he cannot do.

- 2024-10-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-01]]): the Chargers are using Johnston correctly — runway routes over the middle where he can get yards after the catch, plus the occasional shot when the look is there — and Harmon called it 'great coaching' to be extracting this level of production given what he showed as a rookie. - 2024-11-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-05]]): on a 4-of-5 for 118 yards and a touchdown game against Cleveland, Harmon calls the production a mirage but the improvement real — Johnston is now 'a useful NFL player,' a huge jump from a rookie year in which he was one of the worst-charted separators in Reception Perception history and dropped a lot of passes. He splits the blame: Johnston did nothing as a rookie to deserve role catering, but the previous staff left him out to dry asking him to run a real vertical route tree from X and track the ball over his shoulder, which Harmon says is simply not happening with this player. Under Harbaugh they get him off the line of scrimmage, on runway routes over the middle where he can build speed and beat press, and a run-heavy identity draws single-high looks. - 2024-11-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-18]]): Johnston 'made a play or two' in the Bengals game but Waldman does not believe he can become the big-bodied ball-winner the Chargers need opposite [[Ladd McConkey]], because he does not track the football well enough. Waldman rates him a terrific route runner, but argues ball tracking is close to uncoachable at this stage — knowing when to stop and go up for a vertical ball has to be natural and ingrained by the NFL, and Johnston waits too long because he does not get a beat on the flight early. If he does fix it, Waldman says it will take 'a shit ton' of solitary work he will likely have to repeat every day for the rest of his career. Comp given: 'Courtland Sutton light' as a pass catcher, which Waldman conceded is not a great thing to be.

Reframed as a flanker catching the ball uninhibited — slants, crossers, over routes, the occasional deep shot where he is clean behind the coverage — he is a functional starter and a very good runner after the catch: 'the second child who they projected to act like their first kid.'
