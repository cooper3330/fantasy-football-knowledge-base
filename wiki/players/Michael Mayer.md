# Michael Mayer

## Expert Takes

- 2024-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and guest Jagger May (Football Guys, untracked) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-20]]) [Dynasty]: both go "fuck it" on Michael Mayer's dynasty value after the Raiders drafted [[Brock Bowers]] in round one. May, who had Mayer as his TE4 in the 2023 class, still viewed him as a serviceable Dalton Schultz-level producer but doesn't see enough target volume for Las Vegas to feed Mayer, Bowers, Davante Adams and Jakobi Meyers under Gardner Minshew. Waldman compared Mayer's outlook to Harrison Bryant's on this same roster — projecting him as a short-yardage, schemed option good for 2-3 targets a game, with occasional spike weeks only off matchups or injuries to those ahead of him, and real value only as an injury-driven, Isaiah Likely-style handcuff in a two-TE look.
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said reports that new Raiders TE coach Luke Steckel (son of former hard-nosed Vikings OC Les Steckel) called tight ends 'blockers first' point toward Michael Mayer, not [[Brock Bowers]], as the primary in-line run blocker, which under 'rational coaching' should make Mayer fantasy's TE2 in the offense while Bowers stays the room's nominal TE1. He projected Mayer for only around 500-600 receiving yards with occasional red-zone value, since fully deploying Bowers' wide-receiver-caliber route skills would require the coaching staff to make what Waldman considers the smarter, less likely call.
- 2024-12-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-09]]): asked whether Mayer has the most confusing future of any fantasy tight end, Waldman says 'hell yeah.' At his draft range he expected a future Cole Kmet type — effective against zone coverage underneath, good on intermediate shots against zone, a solid red-zone player, maybe better than Kmet — but it is the [[Brock Bowers]] show in Las Vegas, and Waldman thinks you may be waiting years for Mayer to get out from under the Raiders for a true shot. The risk he names is label decay: by the time the opportunity comes he may be tagged as something different from what he was coming out. A trade or a rebirth elsewhere is the realistic path; the two-tight-end alternative runs straight into the Raiders' quarterback problem. [Dynasty]
- 2025-02-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-04]]): Mayer remains a real part of the Raiders offense rather than a throwaway. Harmon says he will 'go to his grave' arguing Scott and Norv Turner were doing genuinely cool things with this offense late in 2024, and that Mayer had moments and was being used to build off and open things up for [[Brock Bowers]] — i.e. his value is partly as a two-tight-end complement rather than as a target earner in his own right. Harmon adds that Scott Turner is now pass game coordinator for the Jets and that he likes the design work even if he doubts Turner's play sequencing. No projection given for Mayer's role under Chip Kelly.
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: both hosts argue the industry has written Mayer off far too early. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) notes Mayer graded out well in the model and that people are already giving up on him. Waldman agrees the evidence is thin — Mayer was not great as a rookie, then had [[Brock Bowers]] arrive and had personal issues that took him away from the team — so 'we don't even know if Michael Mayer is good or not yet'. Waldman says he does not love Mayer, but thinks Mayer could hold the same kind of role as [[David Njoku]] and be better at it than Njoku has been asked to be. They use him as the lead example of tight ends needing longer to develop and getting cut off by coaching and GM turnover before their normal trajectory plays out.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]]: Mayer's value is as the in-line blocking tight end that [[Brock Bowers]] is not, and Nguyen expects Las Vegas to use 12 personnel — not much in preseason, but regularly in practice — because it fits the personnel and the league trend of countering lighter, two-high defenses with heavier bodies. He noted every team this offseason is hunting a second tight end who can block. Development timeline: Mayer was not a good blocker as a rookie, showed improvement in year two but missed time with off-field issues, and year three is important for him to become not merely dependable but a really good blocker on the edge, which is what unlocks Bowers moving around.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): 'I don't think I've ever seen a team showcase their backup tight end quite so much in advance of a trade deadline.' Mayer, on the block for two-plus years, drew five first-half targets, none of them good — 'all just gross dump-off stuff in situations where he just makes nothing happen'. Las Vegas went back to him in the red zone for another non-play, and on the first throw of the third quarter [[Geno Smith]] telegraphed a stop route to Mayer that was intercepted. Harris reads the whole usage pattern as a trade-deadline advertisement rather than a real role.
