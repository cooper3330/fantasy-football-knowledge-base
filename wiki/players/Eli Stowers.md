# Eli Stowers

## Expert Takes

- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: his favorite 2026 tight end — a 6-4, 235 former quarterback (likely 240-245 by next season) who moved to tight end after a knee injury and has not played the position long. Waldman's notes: good suddenness in his movements suggesting the craft of releases can develop; he can beat high safeties at the near-side boundary with acceleration; he has the weight drop into whip routes and should improve his pop-and-drop breaks; good flat-drive and line steps produce sharp inside and outside breaks; he is a friendly target who attacks the ball, digs out low throws, high-points and has the hand-eye coordination for one-handed catches. After the catch he runs like the quarterback-runner he was — feet high, works over low shots, decisive between the tackles on screens, hits with authority but stays patient, and manages risk well as a ball carrier.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-05]]) [Dynasty]: opening a riskiest-prospects segment inspired by analyst Max Toscano's theory that 12/13-personnel-heavy NFL offenses will devalue 'move' tight ends in favor of blocking, zone-eating tight ends like [[A.J. Barner]], [[Tucker Kraft]] and [[Tyler Warren]], Waldman named [[Eli Stowers]] his top-ranked tight end among this year's riskier move-TE/slot hybrids -- closer to a slot receiver than a traditional inline tight end. He believes Stowers is 'close enough to being good enough' that a team could feel comfortable using him as a de facto wide receiver three in 12 personnel given his ball skills and after-the-catch ability, but flagged him as risky within the broader archetype concern that non-blocking tight ends may struggle to find early playing time as the league shifts toward heavier personnel groupings; Brandon Angelo agreed the theory 'has teeth' and said he is watching closely to see where Stowers and similar move-TE prospects get drafted, since there is little market buzz around that archetype in today's game.
- 2026-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-11]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls Stowers possibly 'the safest of the high-ceiling move tight ends' in the 2026 class -- explosive, reliable-handed, with 4.5 speed and good enough routes to be a top-two-or-three passing-game option early in his career. He wins in tight coverage as a big slot/possession threat in zone (corner routes, mismatches versus slower defenders) and also wins on slants, over routes, crossers, comebacks and outs against faster outside defenders thanks to his reach and reliable hands away from his frame. Waldman flags his unusual background: a former highly-recruited high school quarterback (12th nationally by some services) who tore his ACL his junior year after drawing offers from Georgia, LSU and Ole Miss, landed at New Mexico State, then suffered a throwing-arm labrum injury that pushed him to convert to tight end -- meaning he's newer to the position than most prospects but has risen quickly.
- 2026-04-09 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-09]]) [Dynasty]: Eli Stowers is his TE1 in the class, in the same tier as [[KC Concepcion]], [[Chris Brazzell]] and [[Omar Cooper Jr.]], and he comps him to Jordan Reed -- a versatile move tight end who wins in contested windows, gets late separation, and threatens after the catch, but isn't a true in-line/every-down blocker in the mold of [[Colston Loveland]]. Angelo pushed back on online criticism that Stowers 'can't block,' arguing he'll be adequate enough to stay on the field even though some evaluators are miscasting him as an outside X-receiver-type instead. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed he's his TE1 too, praising his ability to make plays in tight coverage, get open late in a route, and serve as a security blanket quarterbacks aren't afraid to target into a crowd, plus strong after-catch athleticism, mobility and strength. Both see him fitting the modern 'move TE' mold rather than the traditional blocking-first archetype of [[Trey McBride]] or [[Zach Ertz]].
- 2026-04-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-13]]) [Dynasty]: he isn't worried about questions over Stowers' experience level or whether he belongs in the top tier, noting most tight end prospects didn't play sophisticated tight-end roles in high school and that former college quarterbacks -- like [[Travis Kelce]] -- have had long, successful NFL tight end careers. Says the real question is whether Stowers can be schemed into an offense requiring a higher level of blocking than he currently offers, but disputes the idea he's an 'awful' blocker some teams reportedly use to justify moving him to a Mike Evans-style receiver role -- he sees real stalk-blocking competency on the backside of formations, with room to improve. Calls his route running very strong, says he reads zones well because of his quarterback background, rates his catch ability among the best in the class especially in contested situations, and says he might be one of the best after-the-catch runners at the position in this class.
- 2026-04-16 — According to Dalton Wasserman (guest draft analyst, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-16]]) [Dynasty]: Wasserman ranks [[Eli Stowers]] behind [[Kenyon Sadiq]] and [[Max Klare]] for now, mainly because Sadiq is currently the better blocker -- but this disagrees with [[Matt Waldman]], who Chris Harris says prefers Stowers over Sadiq. Stowers is unusually young at the position: a former four-star quarterback recruit at Texas A&M who transferred to New Mexico State, lost the starting job to Diego Pavia, and only then moved to tight end, meaning he has fewer than three full seasons at the position. He also turned in a historic combine. Evaluators are split on his ideal build going forward -- add ten pounds and stay at tight end, or drop ten and move to receiver. Wasserman wouldn't be shocked if in three or four years Stowers is judged the best tight end out of this class if he develops the right way, even though he's currently third of the Sadiq/Klare/Stowers trio on his board.
- 2026-04-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman says no tight end should have been drafted after Stowers this year and confirms Stowers is his TE1 in a deep rookie tight end class. He contrasts this with [[Kenyon Sadiq]], saying he can see the upside case for Sadiq but thinks the downside risk there is as strong, if not stronger.
