# Drew Lock

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]): the episode's long-form development case study, prompted by Lock's Monday-night start. Waldman's original evaluation ran against people comping Lock to Patrick Mahomes: "when you watch Drew Lock, his game was much more surface level than Patrick Mahomes' game" — his analogy is a pop musician who plays an instrument versus a jazz musician, where the popular one may not have the deeper skill. His diagnosis is developmental rather than physical: a high-school phenom at a mid-tier SEC school that needed him too much to enforce anything, so undisciplined technique and conceptual shortcuts were never corrected. Against Alabama, Georgia or LSU "you could see him trying to do things the right way and failing miserably — stumbling over his feet, throwing bad balls," like "the kid who was naturally gifted at math but now getting stretched and asked to do things he actually had to practice."
- 2023-12-21 — **Waldman**, same episode, the anecdote he treats as the crux: Peyton Manning called Lock early after the draft offering help; Lock said thanks and ended the call. Waldman's contrast is a Tom Brady-type response — "do you have a garage I can rent? I will wash your cars, I will babysit your kids, whatever, if we can watch film every day." Lock called Manning back in year three, when Teddy Bridgewater arrived and took the job, "and it was too late." Waldman sources a supporting account to **Cecil Lammey** (Broncos beat/media — not a tracked expert), who spoke to Lock's father: a good kid, but "I've been telling him since high school that the things coaches told him he needs to work on, he should apply himself more — and he's never done it."
- 2023-12-21 — **Waldman**, same episode, the fair reading: "you don't have to be a bad person — you just may not understand the gravity of the situation you're in... You don't realize you're great for where you are; you're not great for where you're going." [[Brandon Angelo]] labels it "being situationally naive" and offers Geno Smith as the counter-arc from a comparable starting point.
- 2023-12-21 — **Angelo**, same episode, the current-value call: "Is Drew Lock going to be a starter somewhere? I don't know. But he proved on Monday night that he can be a very solid long-term backup" — which both hosts treat as a genuinely good career outcome rather than a consolation. Both flag a follow-up segment on Lock and Zach Wilson for a future episode.
- 2024-04-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-23]]), revisiting his own draft-era scouting report: Lock looked like an accomplished thrower against weaker competition but got exposed against elite SEC defenses because he wasn't doing the 'daily work,' which Waldman frames as a maturity/coachability gap rather than a talent deficiency; per untracked reporter Cecil Lammey, Lock politely deflected an offer from Peyton Manning to mentor him right after being drafted, then sought Manning's help years later once Teddy Bridgewater arrived to compete for his job.
