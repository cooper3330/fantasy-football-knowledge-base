# DJ Moore

## Expert Takes

- 2024-05-14 — According to Adam Rank ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-14]]), Chicago Bears fan and NFL Network personality, not a tracked expert: Tyreek Hill, Justin Jefferson and Ja'Marr Chase are the 'undisputed' top three receivers, but Rank puts [[DJ Moore]] in his top five alongside [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], ahead of Cooper Kupp, [[Stefon Diggs]] and [[Davante Adams]] (his No. 6). Rank argues Moore is underrated because fans/media still view him through the lens of a tunnel-visioned Justin Fields throwing him the ball; in a hypothetical head-to-head 'rap battle,' Rank says Moore would beat a prime-era Stefon Diggs today, comparing Diggs to Drake — 'still good, just past his time.' Moore remains the Bears' clear target leader even with [[Keenan Allen]] and [[Rome Odunze]] added, but Rank does not project him for Tyreek Hill-level counting stats (Hill chased 2,000 yards) because Chicago's run-first approach with [[DeAndre Swift]] and a crowded three-man WR room will cap his target share below truly elite volume.
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): Moore is who he is, and the Week 3 usage was finally right — 53 routes, 10 targets with nine of them first reads, eight catches for 78 yards, and three designed targets per Fantasy Points Data. Harmon says he will go to his grave not understanding why teams do not scheme more designed touches to Moore to get the ball in his hands and let him work after the catch, and notes Chicago did more of that in this game. He draws the split clearly: Moore has the lower average depth of target and should be the manufactured-touch and after-catch player, while [[Rome Odunze]] at a 21.3 aDOT should be the one attacked down the field. Both Harmon and Koh want Moore and Odunze more involved going forward, ahead of [[Keenan Allen]] on his return.
- 2024-11-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-05]]): asked real or mirage on Moore's 4-33 line on nine targets against Arizona, Harmon says it is real. Moore has been under 35 yards in four of his last five games despite a prime matchup, and Harmon flags a pattern of odd behavior — walking off the field mid-play while [[Caleb Williams]] scrambled, public criticism of Shane Waldron over goal-line personnel, and a radio answer of 'I want to say no' when asked whether Matt Eberflus had lost the locker room. Harmon speculates cautiously that Moore may be unhappy after a career year with [[Justin Fields]] followed by an extension, a new quarterback and a crowded room, while stressing Chicago was right to move on from Fields. On the deep game he cites 11 targets 20+ yards downfield producing three catches, 101 yards, one touchdown, two interceptions, a 27% catch rate and a 56.0 passer rating — the chemistry with Williams downfield does not exist right now.
- 2024-11-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-21]]): Harmon ranks Moore the most disappointing of the six and the only one whose individual play he is genuinely raising an eyebrow at — a long-time Moore backer saying he has plainly not been pleased with what he is asked to do, and that he likely never bought into the offense. The core complaint is misuse: Shane Waldron imported the Seattle template of putting the best receiver at X, but Moore is a very different player from [[DK Metcalf]] — not an elite press-man beater and not a go-route/out-route X — and Harmon has long wanted him fed crossers, screens and quick hitters to cook after the catch, calling him potentially one of the NFL's most dangerous receivers with the ball in his hands. He cites Field Yates: under new OC Thomas Brown in Week 11 Moore's aDOT was 0.86 with 56 yards after catch, versus an 8.3-plus aDOT and 176 YAC total across Weeks 1-10. Harmon is not excusing the lack of effort or poor play, but is encouraged by Chicago's last game looking cohesive; he wants [[Rome Odunze]] at X and Moore moving around, with less [[Keenan Allen]] as a priority target. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) notes a career-low pace of 782 yards, 64% catch rate, 80 receptions, five touchdowns and a shockingly low 9.8 yards per reception after back-to-back 14-plus seasons.
- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Guest Dave Kluge (Football Guys, not a tracked expert) calls Moore, affectionately the little badass, his number four Week 2 quandary. Both [[Rome Odunze]] and Olamide Zaccheaus out-targeted him, and Moore is locked in as an outside receiver rather than the souped-up [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] slot role many hoped Ben Johnson would hand him — the passes are going across the middle while Moore and Odunze are pinned to the boundaries. Kluge moved Moore down and Odunze up to WR26 and WR27, back to back, and would start [[Ricky Pearsall]] over Moore this week, but is not panicking as much as with [[Tyreek Hill]] and would not bench him. Harris also has Moore in his top 20 and would not bench him under normal circumstances, praising a beautifully timed motion out route from Caleb Williams as the dream version. Both flag that Moore's fourth-round price makes the same stat line unacceptable from him where it is fine from Odunze. Kluge's silver lining: Ben Johnson knows the Detroit defense inside out and should game-plan a bounce-back.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Going Deep Listener Q&A)]]): a clear negative turn. Angelo says the tape is 'not good tape' — laziness in and out of routes, effort only when handling the ball — and reads Ben Johnson's post-Detroit comment about earning snaps and targets as aimed squarely at Moore, with the meaningful work already shifted to [[Rome Odunze]]. Waldman, who says he was one of the people Moore publicly dismissed on [[Matt Harmon]]'s show last year, credits Moore's Carolina route work (digs, crossers, improved fades) but says people who would know called him 'a box of chocolates as a route runner.' Both cite the Minnesota play where OC Declan Doyle confirmed Moore went off script on an out cut and raised his hand too late. Waldman notes that improvisation worked with Justin Fields but is fatal in a timing offense.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-22]]): Chicago may be moving Moore into more of a mini-[[Deebo Samuel]] type of role — he was given carries out of the backfield this week and Waldman expects a little more of that — while [[Luther Burden III]] absorbs more downfield receiving work. Waldman's rationale is that Burden may end up showing he is the more reliable route runner, which would push Moore toward manufactured touches rather than a pure alpha receiver role.
