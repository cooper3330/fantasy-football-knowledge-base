# Malik Washington

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]), on Senior Bowl prospects: "probably one of my favorite wide receivers in the draft class who isn't at the top of the conversation," a transfer from Northwestern to Virginia. Profile is a thickly-built slot receiver with big-play capability — "he runs like a running back, catches the ball like a top-end wide receiver," makes good adjustments, and is not a bad route runner. Projects him as an underrated NFL contributor, possibly even a starter within his first contract.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): a capable route runner trending toward having no notable weaknesses, but his true strength is his work after the catch point — a rugged catch-point receiver with the body control to adjust to the ball away from his frame, making full layouts or going airborne and taking hard contact to win it. After the catch he makes defenders miss with efficient movement, runs through arm-tackle attempts, and is a powerful, low-center-of-gravity runner who generates extra power from his own momentum.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): his high-floor "plant my flag" pick of the episode — projects him as a day-one starting slot receiver, pointing to a highly productive 2023 (110 receptions, 1,400+ yards, 9 TDs) and a PFF grade of 96 in the middle of the field, best in this class. Wins on leverage understanding, physicality and adjustment ability rather than top-end speed, with quick, efficient separation. Ceiling comp: Jarvis Landry crossed with Santana Moss; floor comp: Greg Dortch. Can play flanker or slot, do gadget work, and return punts — "eliminate two to three jobs from your depth chart" for one roster spot. [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, independently agrees strongly — has him as a top-12 receiver in the 2024 class, above a straight "instant starter" grade, and floats Demario Douglas/Mike Thomas (Jaguars) as his low-end outcome.
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]) [Dynasty]: named UVA's Malik Washington the class's best slot-only receiver, noting 84.5% of his charted snaps came from the slot and 91.2% behind the line of scrimmage. Success rates were strong across coverages -- 72.1% vs. man, 82.1% vs. zone -- with an 80% contested-catch rate and zero drops in his charted sample. Harmon loves the archetype (reliable route runner who also wins after the catch and in contested spots, running "like a rolling ball of butcher knives") and argues Washington is a clearly better prospect than [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] and [[Rondale Moore]] were as day-two picks, projecting him as a conservative day-three pick who could go as high as round three/top-100 for a team willing to build around the archetype.
- 2024-05-02 — According to Brandon Angelo (co-host of Going Deep on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): Washington lands in a spot where he can start in Miami's 11 personnel right away because he's simply better than incumbent slot options Braxton Berrios and River Cracraft. Angelo's case is specifically about YAC — those two are stable, reliable pass-catchers but don't create after the catch, while Washington can take a short throw 50-60 yards and force missed tackles and beat a safety deep at his size, a rarity among the receivers Mike McDaniel has typically deployed there. Waldman agreed, framing Washington as a post-catch dimension Miami's offense hasn't had and one that becomes more important as Tyreek Hill ages. Both hosts named him one of their two favorite day-three gambles in this class.
- 2024-05-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-02]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon 'fucking loved' Miami taking Washington in round six, calling it egregious he lasted that long given players like [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] and [[Rondale Moore]] went Day 2 — he singles out [[Luke McCaffrey]] going in the third round ahead of Washington as 'bananas despite the bloodline.' He cites GM Chris Grier telling Washington that HC Mike McDaniel 'has been bugging me to draft you,' with McDaniel adding he'd been 'seriously annoying' about it. Washington posted a 72.1% success rate versus man and 82.1% versus zone, zero drops across a three-game sample, and an 80% contested-catch rate, getting open at all three levels. He transferred from Northwestern (unproductive there) to UVA, where he broke multiple school records; Sharp Football's Rich Hribar comped his after-catch style to 'Maurice Jones-Drew playing receiver,' backed by Harmon's charted data (brought down on first contact only 45% of in-space attempts, forcing missed tackles on 20% of those). Harmon projects him stepping directly into Miami's open slot-receiver need behind [[Tyreek Hill]] and [[Jaylen Waddle]], beating out Braxton Berrios and River Cracraft, with real fantasy relevance if [[Tua Tagovailoa]] stays healthy.
