# Malik Washington

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]), on Senior Bowl prospects: "probably one of my favorite wide receivers in the draft class who isn't at the top of the conversation," a transfer from Northwestern to Virginia. Profile is a thickly-built slot receiver with big-play capability — "he runs like a running back, catches the ball like a top-end wide receiver," makes good adjustments, and is not a bad route runner. Projects him as an underrated NFL contributor, possibly even a starter within his first contract.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): a capable route runner trending toward having no notable weaknesses, but his true strength is his work after the catch point — a rugged catch-point receiver with the body control to adjust to the ball away from his frame, making full layouts or going airborne and taking hard contact to win it. After the catch he makes defenders miss with efficient movement, runs through arm-tackle attempts, and is a powerful, low-center-of-gravity runner who generates extra power from his own momentum.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): his high-floor "plant my flag" pick of the episode — projects him as a day-one starting slot receiver, pointing to a highly productive 2023 (110 receptions, 1,400+ yards, 9 TDs) and a PFF grade of 96 in the middle of the field, best in this class. Wins on leverage understanding, physicality and adjustment ability rather than top-end speed, with quick, efficient separation. Ceiling comp: Jarvis Landry crossed with Santana Moss; floor comp: Greg Dortch. Can play flanker or slot, do gadget work, and return punts — "eliminate two to three jobs from your depth chart" for one roster spot. [[Matt Waldman]], same episode, independently agrees strongly — has him as a top-12 receiver in the 2024 class, above a straight "instant starter" grade, and floats Demario Douglas/Mike Thomas (Jaguars) as his low-end outcome.
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]) [Dynasty]: named UVA's Malik Washington the class's best slot-only receiver, noting 84.5% of his charted snaps came from the slot and 91.2% behind the line of scrimmage. Success rates were strong across coverages -- 72.1% vs. man, 82.1% vs. zone -- with an 80% contested-catch rate and zero drops in his charted sample. Harmon loves the archetype (reliable route runner who also wins after the catch and in contested spots, running "like a rolling ball of butcher knives") and argues Washington is a clearly better prospect than [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] and [[Rondale Moore]] were as day-two picks, projecting him as a conservative day-three pick who could go as high as round three/top-100 for a team willing to build around the archetype.
- 2024-05-02 — According to Brandon Angelo (co-host of Going Deep on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): Washington lands in a spot where he can start in Miami's 11 personnel right away because he's simply better than incumbent slot options Braxton Berrios and River Cracraft. Angelo's case is specifically about YAC — those two are stable, reliable pass-catchers but don't create after the catch, while Washington can take a short throw 50-60 yards and force missed tackles and beat a safety deep at his size, a rarity among the receivers Mike McDaniel has typically deployed there. Waldman agreed, framing Washington as a post-catch dimension Miami's offense hasn't had and one that becomes more important as Tyreek Hill ages. Both hosts named him one of their two favorite day-three gambles in this class.
- 2024-05-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-02]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon 'fucking loved' Miami taking Washington in round six, calling it egregious he lasted that long given players like [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] and [[Rondale Moore]] went Day 2 — he singles out [[Luke McCaffrey]] going in the third round ahead of Washington as 'bananas despite the bloodline.' He cites GM Chris Grier telling Washington that HC Mike McDaniel 'has been bugging me to draft you,' with McDaniel adding he'd been 'seriously annoying' about it. Washington posted a 72.1% success rate versus man and 82.1% versus zone, zero drops across a three-game sample, and an 80% contested-catch rate, getting open at all three levels. He transferred from Northwestern (unproductive there) to UVA, where he broke multiple school records; Sharp Football's Rich Hribar comped his after-catch style to 'Maurice Jones-Drew playing receiver,' backed by Harmon's charted data (brought down on first contact only 45% of in-space attempts, forcing missed tackles on 20% of those). Harmon projects him stepping directly into Miami's open slot-receiver need behind [[Tyreek Hill]] and [[Jaylen Waddle]], beating out Braxton Berrios and River Cracraft, with real fantasy relevance if [[Tua Tagovailoa]] stays healthy.
- 2024-05-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-29]]) via the site's Rookie Roundup mini-sample (3 games): Washington (5'8", 191 lbs, UVA via Northwestern transfer, 4.47 forty, 42.5-inch vertical) posted a 72.1% success rate vs. man and 82.1% vs. zone -- both above the 70%/80% benchmarks Harmon and James Koh (untracked) use to flag prospects worth tracking. He broke multiple tackles on 20% of his 20 in-space attempts and profiles as a pre-snap-motion slot weapon. Both hosts were stunned Miami got him in round six -- Harmon heard a pre-draft hip medical flag as the likely reason -- and noted Dolphins GM Chris Grier said coach Mike McDaniel had been "seriously" lobbying for him. He projects as the Dolphins' third receiving option behind [[Tyreek Hill]] and [[Jaylen Waddle]], stepping into work previously split between Braxton Berrios and River Cracraft. College profile is a one-year wonder: a UVA single-season receiving record (1,426 yards) but no 700-yard season at Northwestern. [Dynasty]
- 2024-07-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-09]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon 'loved' Malik Washington's pre-draft profile out of UVA and thinks he should have gone well before the sixth round, calling him a likely value who will 'outkick that draft stock.' He projects Washington strictly as a slot specialist who needs schemed, designed touches underneath rather than a high-volume outside role, giving Miami's coaching staff another real slot option beyond [[Jaylen Waddle]] and [[Tyreek Hill]].
- 2024-11-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman flagged Washington as worth considering wherever managers cut him for doing nothing early and wrote him off as a draft-night name rather than a real player. His reasoning is comparative: Miami keeps talking up Erik Ezukanma's talent — Wes Welker called him a physical freak — but their actions say otherwise, because they keep bringing in other receivers, and it is Washington who is getting more of that work. Actions over words, applied to a receiver room.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]) — with [[Jaylen Waddle]] injured and possibly done for the year, Washington belongs on the waiver radar because he can play Waddle's inside-out role: heavy work after the catch and a good contested-catch player, though not as explosive as Waddle. Waldman's comp is 'an aspiring Santana Moss.' The caveat is opportunity ceiling — behind [[Jonnu Smith]], [[Tyreek Hill]] and [[De'Von Achane]] he is probably no better than the third option in the passing game each week, which Waldman prices at roughly three to five receptions and 50 to 70 yards per game. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) added that Jonnu Smith has 76 targets over the last 10 games to Hill's 73.
- 2025-01-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-13]]) [Dynasty]: Washington is Waldman's dark horse. If [[Tyreek Hill]] makes good on his exit threats, Waldman does not see a receiver in the 2024 class outside of maybe Luther Burden or [[Travis Hunter]] who could fill Hill's role, and he thinks Washington could be at least competent in it — not Tyreek, but an 'upper middle class Santana Moss' type. [[Matt Harmon]] agrees he was a big fan of the prospect, calls the sixth-round slide crazy and medical-driven (a neck issue), and notes his peripheral metrics like yards per route run were never going to look good playing across from two star receivers in a chaotic offense — but he popped with the ball in his hands and was awesome at UVA. Harmon adds a caution on the environment: with Tua Tagovailoa out, Mike McDaniel does not let backup quarterbacks play quarterback and shuts the offense down.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]) [Dynasty]: Washington is his top pick for a 2024 rookie receiver who adds 500-plus yards in 2025, and he calls him a phenomenal player he loved coming out of Virginia. The thesis is that [[Tyreek Hill]] is not a Dolphin anymore, that Washington complements what [[Jaylen Waddle]] does extremely well, and that he becomes a much higher-volume target in that offense. Angelo's actionable point is timing: for dynasty players Washington is the cheapest of his three breakout candidates right now, and his stock goes to the moon the moment the Hill news breaks, so the window to buy is before that. [[Matt Waldman]] had the same three names on his own list.
- 2025-02-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-28]]): Harmon liked Washington as a prospect, thought he went far too late in the draft — more of a day-two talent whose stock fell on injuries — and calls him a nice value for Miami. He ran 252 routes, third most among Dolphins receivers, and did well with limited opportunities; Harmon wants to see more for him in 2025, specifically more of the schemed layups Miami's offense generates. The caveat is that Washington is not a true perimeter receiver, so he does not solve the Dolphins' actual need, which Harmon frames as adding a sacrificial X to free up [[Jaylen Waddle]]. Both hosts noted that if [[Tyreek Hill]] stays, the top of the room is set and Washington's path is limited.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Harmon remains a fan, noting he had Washington ranked as a day-two player in his stack rankings before Miami took him on day three — the same profile mismatch as [[Khalil Shakir]], a fifth-rounder who worked out, and Harmon says Washington reminds him of Shakir. He waves off the poor yards-per-route-run figure as a function of playing behind two of the best receivers in the NFL. Beyond Washington, Miami has very little that can play slot: [[Nick Westbrook-Ikhine]] is a sacrificial-X type. Harmon's speculative version of the room without [[Tyreek Hill]] — Westbrook-Ikhine at X, [[Jaylen Waddle]] moving around like Hill, Washington in the slot — he concedes is sicko fascination stuff that Koh (not tracked) says 99% of the football world would reject.
- 2025-06-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-30]]): with [[Jonnu Smith]] traded and Miami's remaining tight end room (Julian Hill, Pharoah Brown, whose good year is about 30 catches) unproven, Waldman thinks the Dolphins may simply decide they do not need a tight end weapon and instead go more often to a third or fourth wide receiver. That makes Washington — or Nick Westbrook-Ikhine — the beneficiary he would watch, with a realistic upside of roughly 700-800 yards and four to five touchdowns, which he classifies as a low-end fantasy starter rather than a difference maker. He closed the episode calling Washington 'my new [[Calvin Austin III]]', the late-round flier he keeps believing in. Both hosts noted Miami's willingness to move a productive tight end rather than pay him raises questions about the organization's direction under Mike McDaniel.
- 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]): Washington is one of Harmon's two deep sleepers in the Dolphins room alongside [[Andrew Armstrong]]. He gave Washington a day-two grade in his stacked rankings and Washington fell to the sixth round because of a neck injury; Harmon says his profile popped at times in 2024 and that he fits the create-a-touch-but-can-also-run-routes mold — a poor man's [[Tyreek Hill]] or [[Jaylen Waddle]] himself.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): 'feeling it' on Washington as a contingency asset. If you drafted [[Tyreek Hill]] or [[Jaylen Waddle]], Waldman argues Washington is effectively a handcuff to both — he times 4.47, has real vertical explosion and leaping ability, and is quicker, more explosive and far more physical at the catch point and after the catch than [[Wan'Dale Robinson]]. He explicitly pushed back on a comparison he attributed to Sigmund Bloom that Washington is a 'middle-class Wan'Dale Robinson', offering instead a middle-class Santana Moss if either starter goes down. Waldman sees him usable in the slot now with a much larger ceiling on injury. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) said he would not draft him in redraft but has him on the waiver watch list and would take him cheaply in [[Dynasty]], citing the 'hinkiness' around Hill's situation.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-29]]): Washington has beaten out [[Nick Westbrook-Ikhine]] for Miami's third receiver job, which mildly surprised Harmon only because the team went out and pursued Westbrook-Ikhine in free agency. He had graded Washington as a day-two-caliber prospect despite a sixth-round selection caused by a neck medical red flag, and ranked him below [[Tory Horton]] within that group. Charting: 72.1% success rate vs man, 82.1% in his rookie roundup sample from last year's class — a proven separator who is tough, rugged and 'has some running back in him' with the ball in his hands. Harmon said Washington's skill set fits best alongside [[Tyreek Hill]] and [[Jaylen Waddle]], and recalled Mike McDaniel openly fiending for him on draft night.
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]): Waldman is 'starting to feel it' on Washington having fantasy value, noting [[Tua Tagovailoa]] praising how well Washington understands the offense while saying [[Tyreek Hill]] still does not quite have it, and Hill losing his captaincy. He repeats his Santana Moss-type upside comp, calls Washington a real pro worth holding, and says he is a more and more attractive late-round play because the Hill situation looks volatile: if Hill or [[Jaylen Waddle]] is hurt or traded, Washington could step into either role and be an every-week starter, with vacated targets available from [[Jonnu Smith]]. He is careful not to over-weight one preseason snap but says a nifty run-after-catch touchdown confirmed what people had been telling him. [Best Ball]
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Going Deep Listener Q&A)]]): a real contingent contributor if [[Tyreek Hill]] is moved. Angelo loved him at Virginia in a high-volume role and describes a good combination of playing the position well and being a genuinely good athlete in short spaces — plus availability, which he contrasts with [[Jaylen Waddle]], who in his words comes out of games more than anyone he has seen and is an Italian sports car you cannot count on. With Miami's defense, offensive line and run game all poor, Angelo expects Tua to lean on extensions of the run game, meaning more Washington and more back-field targets. He thinks Washington could handle and produce with an 18%-plus target share, and notes he has already been great on return duties. [[Matt Waldman]] says he agrees 100% and adds nothing.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): Washington is Harris's number-three Week 5 waiver add and the highest-ranked eligible receiver on his list at 4% rostered. Harris says he has repeatedly mistaken Washington for [[Tyreek Hill]] on film — both short, 'yoked' and fast — while allowing that Washington is probably not Hill fast and that he is not sure Washington knows how to run a route. The case is usage: Mike McDaniel has visibly worked to get him the ball as a gadget guy, so he plainly considers him a playmaker, and that temptation should grow now. On price Harris and guest Jeff Erickson (RotoWire, not tracked) agree the $59-60 FAAB Ben Gretsch spent in their stacked league is too much; Harris bid 11 in a 12-team league and would go to about 15.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-06]]): the expanded role did not arrive — [[Nick Westbrook-Ikhine]] moved into the Tyreek Hill-type role while Washington kept the one he had. Waldman hates it but expects it to continue: [[Jaylen Waddle]] is the primary, [[Darren Waller]] is the co-primary, and the remaining share splits three ways between Westbrook-Ikhine, [[De'Von Achane]] and Washington. He called the usage the most predictable thing he has seen — essentially every throw to Washington was stopped at or near the line of scrimmage, four times in the game. His evaluation is that Washington can do much more: he sees the speed and run-after-catch traits that made [[Jonnu Smith]] dangerous, describing him as a faster [[Khalil Shakir]] who can also go up and win the ball. The blocker is that Mike McDaniel is very conservative with young players.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'yeah, not so much.' Miami tried him a couple of times near the red zone — a screen and a jet pitch that technically counted as a pass — and got nothing: four catches for zero yards. Harris' conclusion is that even with [[Tyreek Hill]] out for the season, 'we're obviously holding off on starting him until we see something different'.
