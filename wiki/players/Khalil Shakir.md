# Khalil Shakir

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: "65% feeling it" as a 2024 fantasy starter — expects him to inherit [[Gabe Davis]]'s vacated role, offering a blend of what Davis provided at the catch point on deep shots plus better contested-catch ability and more after-the-catch production in the intermediate range. Frames the holdup as institutional trust rather than talent: "it's just a matter of this team finally trusting Shakir to be all that he can be," and calls next year a good bet that it happens. Flags his one open question as role overlap with [[Dalton Kincaid]] in an offense that may only support so many high-target players.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the strongest version of this call yet — Shakir "looks like a more consistently valuable piece of an offense" than both [[Gabe Davis]] and, "in this moment," [[Stefon Diggs]], on proven playmaking and the ability to get open consistently. Explicitly expects him to start in 2024, with the only real uncertainty being Buffalo's coaching staff (no head-coach firing expected, but OC Joe Brady has drawn head-coaching interest elsewhere).
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: independent corroboration — "reliable," a player Harmon says he was rooting for an opportunity for coming out of college off a strong Reception Perception profile, who looked like he might be getting iced out of the rotation (behind Deontay Harty and Trent Sherfield) before earning real playing time late in the year. Calls him "a good slot receiver," not a superstar, but already "a good football player" and part of Buffalo's offensive future alongside [[Dalton Kincaid]].

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]): reacting to Buffalo's signing of [[Curtis Samuel]] — pushes back on the idea the two overlap, calling Shakir more of a get-the-ball-in-space YAC slot piece versus Samuel's dig/out/corner route tree, and expects Shakir could see more outside reps to let Samuel work some slot snaps as well, giving Buffalo positional flexibility it lacked before.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]): thinks he could wind up as Buffalo's number two or three receiver, and that the team's presumed rookie WR pick "might not work out year one," leaving Shakir as a massive bargain. Describes him as a "less physical Heinz Ward with more downfield speed and dynamic movement" — a good route runner, excellent after the catch, tough at the catch point. Notes Buffalo has been slow to trust rookies (citing the same pattern with [[James Cook]] and [[Dalton Kincaid]]), but that whenever Shakir is on the field, Josh Allen targets him on difficult veteran-caliber throws (cites a tight third-down window against Pittsburgh) rather than the low-difficulty schemed-open looks a rookie like Quentin Johnston (not tracked) gets — reads that as a sign of quarterback trust outpacing coaching-staff trust. "I loved him and I'm still all over him because nothing on the field has shown me otherwise."
- 2024-07-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Khalil Shakir has shown himself to be 'at worst a very good role player' in the NFL -- a reliable short-to-intermediate slot target even if he's not funneled 120+ targets -- and factors into a crowded four-man rotation with [[Curtis Samuel]], [[Keon Coleman]] and a big-bodied X-type (MVS/Claypool) that Harmon expects to share reps in Buffalo's receiver room.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon called Shakir 'a good player' but projects him as 'probably a slot-mostly' receiver, part of a Bills room Harmon expects to spread roughly 80-90 targets across several complementary pieces ([[Keon Coleman]], [[Curtis Samuel]], [[Dalton Kincaid]]) rather than feature one clear dominant target -- a distribution he thinks fits stylistically with Buffalo's ascending, run-leaning offensive line (13th per guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn, not tracked) and new OC Joe Brady's scheme.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): Shakir is his first sleeper to hit — not that deep a name, but he expects a massive year and a 1,000-plus-yard season, with Shakir and [[Dalton Kincaid]] the motors of the Buffalo offense. The structural argument is that this offense will flow through throws close to Kincaid and the short-to-intermediate crew rather than through a rookie X, since Angelo does not think [[Keon Coleman]] is there yet. [[Matt Waldman]] says he has been on the Shakir train for a couple of years and has not left it; Angelo says he is trying to leave every draft with him.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Harmon says the real hipsters are still holding a candle for Shakir, who was awesome last year and whose Reception Perception prospect profile from three classes ago was really good; he has continued to check boxes. If forced to bet on Buffalo's most productive receiver in Week 1, Harmon takes Shakir over [[Curtis Samuel]], who is coming off turf toe, and describes Shakir as established and entrenched as the slot. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) argues instead for Samuel as the season-long target leader, so the two split on the pecking order.
- 2024-09-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-26]]): 'he's a stud,' a prospect Harmon liked, and his hot take is that dropped into [[Rashee Rice]]'s exact Kansas City role Shakir would put up the exact same numbers. Charting his last two games (Dolphins, Jaguars) Harmon has him over 70% in the slot — 'slot mostly, if not slot only' — at 84% success rate vs zone and 73% vs man, on the same short tree of slants, flats and curls he criticizes Rice for, though Shakir can also win in the intermediate area. Not a go-ball or deep receiver. At 6-0, 190 he is a physical power slot who catches everything, has great hands and makes defenders miss in space. Koh notes a 100% catch rate through three games: 14 of 14 for 168 yards and two touchdowns. The caveat is opportunity — Harmon doubts Buffalo ever gives him a Rice-sized target load with [[Keon Coleman]] and a returning [[Curtis Samuel]] around, though he thinks Shakir would answer it if they did.
- 2024-10-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon calls Shakir Buffalo's best separator by far and a genuinely talented player whose absence is a huge issue — with him out, [[Josh Allen]] went 9-of-30 against Houston because, in Harmon's read, nobody could get open. Through five weeks Shakir leads the Bills in route participation at 67.8% of Allen's dropbacks per Fantasy Points data, a figure Harmon compares to Kansas City's early-2024 rotation where [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]] led at 66.9% before the playoff version concentrated routes on [[Rashee Rice]] at 82%.
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Shakir is untouchable in Buffalo's slot — Harmon says the arrival of [[Amari Cooper]] won't push [[Keon Coleman]] into a power-slot role because 'you're not taking Khalil Shakir out of the slot, that guy's too good.' He caught seven of seven targets again in Week 7; James Koh (not tracked) says he keeps knocking Shakir's projected catch rate down below 90% and Shakir keeps beating it, so he may just leave the projection at 90%.
- 2024-11-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-20]]): Shakir is 'the best wide receiver in this room in my opinion, like the guy I would trust the most' — on a third down he is going to Shakir. He is one of Harmon's favorite slot receivers in the NFL, mostly a short-area threat from inside, and Harmon sees no reason to change a role that is working even though he thinks Shakir could play some outside. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) called him effectively a slot-only player and used that to argue the Buffalo room is jammed. Harmon allows that [[Amari Cooper]] could change his opinion once he is a fully activated player, but says they have not seen it yet given the wrist injury.
- 2024-12-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-10]]): true, Shakir is Buffalo's number one receiver — Harmon says he has been expecting this. Koh's numbers: since [[Amari Cooper]] arrived, Cooper has averaged 5.4 targets, 3.2 catches and 46 yards on a 59% catch rate over five games, while Shakir over seven games has exactly double Cooper's receptions per game with 69 yards per game on a 75% catch rate, and 81 targets in 12 games on the year. Harmon's caveat is that he is not sure Shakir is a true number one in the league-wide sense either; Buffalo simply uses a collection of good players more efficiently than Green Bay does, and having a math-flipping quarterback in [[Josh Allen]] means it does not really matter. He expects the room to get more interesting when [[Keon Coleman]] returns.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]): Shakir is being overlooked. He led the league in yards after the catch for much of the season, and Waldman insists that production is not a function of the offense — plenty of receivers get put in Shakir's short-passing role and cannot do what he does. Framed provocatively, Shakir 'is carrying a load that makes [[Josh Allen]] look good on a regular basis' on those short throws. Waldman adds that if Buffalo chose to expand the role downfield, Shakir can also win contested plays; they simply have not asked him to. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed and said there have been tastes of the expanded role, flagging an increasing role over time as something to remember heading into 2025 drafts.
- 2025-01-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-09]]): Shakir wins Harmon's 2024 Best Underneath Wide Receiver award. The case is efficiency plus reliability — zero drops on targets under 10 air yards all season, some of the best hands in the NFL, league-leading 3.14 yards per route run on throws under eight air yards versus zone coverage, second overall in yards per route run under 10 air yards behind only [[Puka Nacua]], a 63% success rate, and eight yards after catch per reception despite ranking only sixth in catches in that bucket. Full season: 100 targets, 76 receptions, 821 yards, four touchdowns. Harmon calls him Buffalo's most consistent passing-game contributor besides [[Josh Allen]], and notes Buffalo deliberately parses out narrow roles. The caveat is explicit: cut Shakir's profile off at 10 air yards and he looks elite, but production 'really drops off' beyond that. Harmon wants to chart him in the offseason to see whether there is more to activate, in an [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]-style continued development arc. He also flags Shakir as a test case against the assumption that efficiency collapses when volume rises — Shakir stayed efficient as volume grew. James Koh (co-host, not a tracked expert) agrees he was the chain-mover and an extension of the run game.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Shakir is Buffalo's meat-and-potatoes receiver — if you want to move the chains you throw it to Shakir. He led the Bills' pass catchers in the Denver game at 59% of dropbacks and six targets, ahead of [[Dalton Kincaid]] (53%), [[Keon Coleman]] (47%), [[Curtis Samuel]] and [[Mack Hollins]] (44% each) and [[Amari Cooper]] (41%), per fantasy points data. James Koh (not a tracked expert) argued Buffalo has found a rhythm over the last month by throwing a lot of bodies at defenses, and credited Joe Brady, whom Harmon would put near the top of his offensive head-coaching list alongside Liam Coen and Ben Johnson. Harmon's caveat: reliability is Shakir's role, not explosive perimeter production.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-23]]): Shakir is Buffalo's go-to guy underneath and, per Harmon's podcast framing, the best underneath receiver in the league, and the AFC Championship matchup sets up for him. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) laid out the Next Gen Stats case: Kansas City has allowed the second-most receptions and sixth-most yards per game to receivers lined up inside, while allowing the fewest receptions and second-fewest yards to outside receivers, so Buffalo should 'paint the middle' with Shakir and the tight ends. Koh notes Shakir went 12 targets, 8 catches, 70 yards in the Week 11 win over Kansas City. Harmon agreed the game should look like the divisional round, with Shakir and the tight ends carrying the passing volume rather than [[Amari Cooper]] or [[Keon Coleman]].
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): Shakir has been 'a great role player in his role all year' and was efficient in the AFC Championship — seven targets, six catches, 46 yards. But Harmon's structural point is that Shakir led the Bills with only 97 targets, which leaves 'a lot of room to get a 110-120 target player and still keep all of these other role players in the mix.' In other words Shakir's excellence is not a ceiling-raiser and does not preclude Buffalo adding a true alpha above him. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) counters that the week-to-week unpredictability of Shakir, [[Curtis Samuel]] and [[James Cook]] is what made the offense un-defendable in the regular season.
- 2025-02-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-28]]): Shakir is 'damn good' and a long-time Reception Perception favorite dating to his prospect days, so Harmon endorsed the contract extension — a man-coverage beater from the slot who is genuinely useful with the ball in his hands, named RP's best underneath receiver of the season at 7.9 yards after catch per reception. He led the Bills room with 100 targets, 76 receptions, 821 yards and four touchdowns. The hard ceiling is snaps: he ran only about 66 percent of the team's routes and takes 70.4 percent of his snaps in the slot, because Buffalo does not play him on the perimeter. Harmon's broader point is that Buffalo now has plenty of quality supporting players like Shakir but no true number one — the inverse of the [[Stefon Diggs]] era.
- 2025-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-18]]): Harmon liked Shakir as a Boise State prospect he thought should have gone on day two, and says Buffalo has found close to the perfect usage plan — 63.1% of sample snaps in the slot, 9.3% in the backfield, under 30% outside, almost 79% off the line of scrimmage, with 51.3% of charted routes slants, flats or screens and a 38% participation rate on slants and flats alone. He posted an 80.5% success rate vs zone (66th percentile), 75% on both digs and curls, and was in space on 65.2% of sample catches while going down on first contact only 46.7% of the time, retaining the contact balance that made him a fun prospect. Only 6% of routes were corners, outs or comebacks. His 63% success rate vs man is below average but not hopeless — Harmon lists 2021 [[Hunter Renfrow]] (63.7%), 2022 [[Christian Kirk]] (63%), 2023 [[Rashee Rice]] (63%), 2014 Jarvis Landry (62.6%) and 2016 Julian Edelman (64%) in the same band. Both hosts think dig/curl participation (15% combined) could rise; Harmon puts the odds of a full [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]-style leap at under 50% and suggests ~110 targets as the realistic ceiling, valuing him as the player who incentivizes [[Josh Allen]] to take layups.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]): Shakir's high ankle sprain should be monitored closely — Waldman expects him to be slowed for the first two to four games of the season even if he is technically ready for Week 1. He notes it is hard to have a genuinely 'mild' high ankle sprain, and that re-injury incidence is elevated, so even though Shakir may be Buffalo's most reliable receiver, the Bills may feel comfortable giving him extra rest to protect the rest of the season. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) jokes that this opens the door for [[Keon Coleman]] to be a dominant force, then immediately walks it back as wish-casting rather than a real projection.
- 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]): Shakir is dealing with a high ankle sprain and is week to week, which Harmon calls really tough for Buffalo because Shakir is 'definitely the best, most consistent player of this group' and clearly by far its best zone beater — an 80 percent success rate versus zone last year, good hands, wins after the catch, the guy who makes the whole thing go. With Shakir hurt, [[Josh Palmer]] and [[Curtis Samuel]] also banged up in camp, Harmon says [[Elijah Moore]] has a clear opening to make the roster.
