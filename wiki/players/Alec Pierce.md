# Alec Pierce

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2024-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-11]]): Pierce (6'3', roughly a 40-inch vertical) has been 'a little bit of a disappointment' so far in the NFL and hasn't developed as hoped since being drafted as a Day 2 prospect. Harmon calls him 'a fine number four' who can still play some, but says Indianapolis over-relied on him to run vertical routes last season -- a gap he expects [[Adonai Mitchell]]'s arrival to fill, likely pushing Pierce further down the depth chart.
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]): Harmon calls Pierce 'literally the poster boy of the sacrificial X' — and worse than the [[Romeo Doubs]] category, because Doubs at least occasionally gets open or wins a jump ball, whereas Pierce 'never gets open and never really wins contested catches.' Harmon says Pierce's route tree and RP success-rate data look like the bad pre-draft takes about [[DK Metcalf]] — straight-line, go routes and slants only — except with Pierce those criticisms were actually true, and he does not play as physically as his size suggests, calling him closer to 'a net negative X receiver.' Co-host James Koh (not tracked) pushes back partially: Pierce's strength is verticality and neither [[Gardner Minshew]] nor a healthy quarterback situation has been throwing it deep, though [[Anthony Richardson]] might get more out of him.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Harmon liked Pierce to a degree as a prospect and thinks he translated much of it in his first-year Reception Perception profile, but frames him as the player the bad [[DK Metcalf]] takes actually describe — a receiver who genuinely only runs go routes, post routes and slant routes. He calls Pierce maybe the most sacrificial X in the league last year, running deep routes and almost never getting the ball while clearing space for [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] and [[Josh Downs]]. With [[Adonai Mitchell]] looking more position-versatile, Harmon thinks there is actually more room for Pierce to keep doing that clear-out work, but he does not expect him to command many targets per game. Koh (not tracked) pushes back a little, noting Pierce was a 2022 second-round pick having a reportedly good camp.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Dynasty]: Pierce is a must-add in deeper leagues and definitely in dynasty, though not yet in shallow 12-team redraft — but he belongs on the short list. He ran by high-low coverage twice, made a catch over the middle showing the hands you want from a target, and won contested tight-target balls; Waldman said Pierce was effectively showing [[Quentin Johnston]] how ball-winning is supposed to be done even though Johnston had the better game. The upside case runs through [[Anthony Richardson]], who said pre-game that people wrote him off but he was hurt last year and is now healthy. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) said he is happy with his dynasty shares and is looking for redraft room.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): Pierce is 'definitely a sacrificial X receiver,' but Harmon is now skeptical of the analysis that he'll simply be a zero, because he plays for a quarterback willing to attempt throws [[Gardner Minshew]] could not physically contemplate — the opening touchdown from [[Anthony Richardson]] may be 'the best throw we see all year.' Koh (not tracked) argues Pierce's downfield ability finally has a QB to unlock it and that even the threat of it stretches the field for the whole offense, while conceding Pierce's primary role is eating up space and getting cardio in. Harmon also credits the Indianapolis beat writers here — after criticizing beat coverage of [[Tyler Johnson]] earlier — noting they consistently said Pierce had a good camp and would be part of the game plan even as fans chased [[Adonai Mitchell]]. Neither host expects this production weekly.
- 2024-09-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-17]]): the fantasy community overlooked that Pierce is not a worthless NFL player, but Harmon caps the ceiling hard. He points listeners to Pierce's 2022 Reception Perception profile — 57th percentile success rate versus man, 69th versus press, nothing special, and quite poor against zone — and repeats his draft-year comp that Pierce is 'the bad takes of [[DK Metcalf]]': he can sting you on go routes, big overs and posts, and slants, and that is exactly what has shown up. He is a viable role player who will not go away, but not the best receiver on the roster by season's end. Harmon and Koh (not tracked) agree the real change is quarterback arm strength — Pierce's downfield skill set never matched Indianapolis's previous dead-arm quarterbacks and now does with [[Anthony Richardson]] — while insisting the production cannot be consistent because he is not a consistent separator against zone and only above average against man. Second-round draft capital is noted as real.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): selling Pierce as the league's sixth-leading receiver in yards and doubting he finishes as the Colts' leading receiver, while buying that he stays a real part of the passing game. Koh (not tracked) cites 8 catches on 10 targets, 181 yards and two touchdowns and calls him a splash-play merchant; Harmon says the fantasy community wrongly threw him aside. His 2022 RP profile is limited but not useless: 9th percentile vs zone, but 72.4 percent vs press and 69.6 percent vs man, with nothing available in the intermediate middle — he can run nines, posts and slants and stretch a defense. Harmon's key point is fit: [[Anthony Richardson]] will actually throw the deep shots other quarterbacks decline. Long term he thinks Pierce is the fourth-best receiver on the roster if everyone hits their ceiling.
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): Pierce clearly won the Week 3 snap battle over [[Adonai Mitchell]], running 19 routes to Mitchell's two and playing 78 percent of the offensive snaps, second only to [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] at 83 percent. Harmon does not credit this to a step forward in Pierce's play so much as to Mitchell's inconsistency in being in the right place, and cautions that the whole Colts receiver room is capped by a team that has fallen to dead last in plays per game at 50 with [[Anthony Richardson]] completing around 50 percent.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): Pierce is not the lead piece in Indianapolis' receiving corps, but he may turn out to be what people expected [[Adonai Mitchell]] to be this year — a fantasy starter you ride out the bad weeks with because the good weeks are really good. Waldman's usage note is that the Colts are not using Pierce in a well-rounded way, but the way they are using him generates big plays. Practical advice: plug him in every week as a WR4; as a WR3 you want a matchup advantage. He credits [[Anthony Richardson]]'s week-one declaration that everyone forgot about Pierce and he would make sure they did not, and notes the added irony that [[Joe Flacco]] leads the NFL in passer rating while Richardson is last — though Waldman still wants Richardson back, having seen glimmers of hope.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): Pierce is the receiver he is most interested in, and his call is that Pierce regresses but does not fall out of relevance. The 28 yards per catch is obviously coming down, but Waldman argues Pierce may stay a viable three-wide-receiver-format starter because he is making few mistakes, is winning on a higher grade of difficulty on a regular basis, and has earned trust from two different quarterbacks in fourth-quarter and big-play moments. He pushes back on the argument that defenses will simply take away the one play Pierce makes — the wins come multiple ways, not off a single concept, even though he is heavily used in the deep game. Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) agrees, comparing him to [[Rashid Shaheed]], who has historically held that big-play profile in a sustainable way.
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]): still bullish on Pierce as a WR3 despite up-and-down weeks — he drew two key DPI calls in this game. The bigger reason to hold is role: with [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] rumored for IR and clearly not healthy, Waldman expects Pierce to end up in the Pittman role, which he calls potentially a big thing. Verdict: hang on to him. He separately notes Indianapolis beat writers still consider Pierce the better player than [[Adonai Mitchell]].
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]): absolutely worth a roster spot, and his single favorite candidate for a league-winning fluke week. Waldman calls Pierce the number one candidate to post something like a six-catch, 160-yard, three-touchdown playoff day that almost nobody had started. The reasoning is [[Anthony Richardson]]'s belief in him and frequency of deep targets — Richardson 'supports' Pierce — combined with Richardson looking better than his stats because Colts receivers drop footballs constantly, at least three or four bad drops a game plus a genuinely bad decision every other game. Pierce drops his share too. In four- or five-receiver lineups Waldman calls him an absolute start. Bob Harris (not tracked) framed the opportunity via snaps with [[Josh Downs]] out: [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] led at 57, Pierce next at 44, with more two-tight-end sets squeezing [[Adonai Mitchell]] in the slot.
- 2025-01-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-09]]): a notable on-air reversal. Koh nominates Pierce for a deep-ball honorable mention and Harmon concedes the case — Pierce led all wide receivers with 517 yards on throws of 20-plus air yards, on just 12 catches, from a league-leading 30 deep targets, with a slightly above-average 40% deep catch rate. He finished with 37 catches for 824 yards, meaning the majority of his season's yardage came on deep shots, and he led all Colts in receiving yards ahead of [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] at 808 and [[Josh Downs]] at 803 — an outcome Koh says nobody would have bet on preseason. Harmon's caveat is that Pierce had the lowest EPA or success rate of the deep group, so volume of opportunity is doing real work. He then walks back his own offseason position: he actually liked Pierce as a prospect in exactly this vertical X role (rookie RP data was disgusting against zone but solid against press man, which fits the archetype), but this offseason said Pierce was something worse than a [[Sacrificial X Receiver]] — and apologizes for turning on him.
- 2025-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-07]]): Pierce led the Colts with 824 receiving yards on just 37 catches. Harmon revisits his own history here — he liked Pierce as a day-two prospect and in his rookie profile, then wrote last offseason that he was 'something worse than a sacrificial X receiver', a throwaway line subscribers will not let him live down. His current view is in between: Pierce is 'kind of a weird player', purely straight-line — 'it's goes, it's posts, it's slants' — but not someone you can simply take off the field, because he clears out space and occasionally makes an impressive contested catch. He needs the right vertical-attacking quarterback to activate him, theoretically [[Anthony Richardson]]. Harmon likens the situation to [[Romeo Doubs]]: good enough that you cannot pluck him off the field, which ends up stunting [[Adonai Mitchell]] the way Doubs stunted [[Dontayvion Wicks]]. He is entering the last year of his rookie deal. Koh wants Indianapolis to shop him — a third-rounder or a defensive player back — to free snaps for Mitchell, and floats Kansas City or New England as speed-and-size-needy buyers.
- 2025-05-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-23]]): Harmon revisits his own past dismissal of Pierce as 'something worse than a sacrificial X receiver' — a line subscribers still remind him of — and publicly walks it back, noting Pierce had good press-man numbers as a prospect and as a rookie (57th percentile vs man, 9th percentile vs zone) and profiles as a straight-line, somewhat sacrificial X. He notes Pierce did little in his second season, which is why Indianapolis wanted a push at the position, but argues Pierce's imbalance is very similar in kind to [[Adonai Mitchell]]'s and that Pierce is roughly 10% better on nine routes. Harmon concedes Mitchell has more theoretical upside, but says he does not see a world where Mitchell fully pushes Pierce aside unless he is a dramatically better player in year two; a nominal start would not settle it. Both were second-round picks, so draft capital does not separate them.
- 2025-06-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-06]]): Pierce's 2024 was a quarterback-specific outcome. He led the league at 22.3 yards per reception with 824 yards on 37 catches, largely because [[Anthony Richardson]] would rip throws to him on clear-out routes that, in Harmon's words, you are not really supposed to throw and that Gardner Minshew 'quite literally never threw' in 2023 — Pierce outrunning zone windows no other quarterback would target. Harmon uses this as a general lesson that a receiver can grade well in Reception Perception and never pop because the quarterback and role don't mesh. He also thinks part of the staff's frustration with Richardson was that you can't make a living on those throws. Pierce is in the last year of his contract, and with [[Daniel Jones]] likely under center Harmon doubts the vertical volume repeats.
- 2025-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-13]]): Pierce is the clearest live example of Harmon's sacrificial X theory — Indianapolis needs someone to stretch the field so [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] (flanker) and [[Josh Downs]] (slot) can work underneath, and Harmon points back to the 2023 Gardner Minshew season when Pierce was barely getting the football but was clearing out enormous space for Pittman's production. He is on the last year of his contract, with [[Adonai Mitchell]] rotating in behind him at X. Pierce also finished top five in the NFL in post-route yards in 2024 alongside [[Justin Jefferson]], [[Jayden Reed]], [[Nico Collins]] and [[Tyreek Hill]].
- 2025-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-01]]): Pierce is a deep-threat, essentially sacrificial X receiver — a useful role player who does one thing over and over and does it well. Harmon credits his ~23 yards-per-catch 2024 (roughly 700-plus yards on around 40 catches) substantially to having a quarterback in [[Anthony Richardson]] willing to just launch the clear-out route, framing it as a role and quarterback-behavior outcome rather than a separation breakout. He later uses Pierce as the archetype comp for [[Dyami Brown]] in Jacksonville: a mostly-vertical, one-side-of-the-field route tree. Harmon still likes him as part of what he considers a very good, complementary Colts receiver group alongside [[Michael Pittman Jr.]], [[Josh Downs]] and [[Adonai Mitchell]].
