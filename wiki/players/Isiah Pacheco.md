# Isiah Pacheco

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]: Pacheco is the episode's named negative exemplar for pace control — "the polar opposite" of [[Lamar Jackson]]. "He's only running 100 miles an hour. You get zero or a hundred." The contrast is with backs who vary their gears play to play to set up blocks and defender angles ([[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], [[James Cook]]); Angelo's argument is that a single-speed runner is easier to corral in space because a defender only has one tempo to solve. Delivered in passing as an illustration, not as a ranking or start/sit call. Framework: [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]].
- 2024-01-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-25]]): running well in the postseason despite the single-speed criticism from a month earlier — "he just keeps kind of producing," an open question on whether he's Kansas City's long-term bell-cow back, credited as a tone-setter Andy Reid leans on once the calendar turns to the playoffs (contrasted favorably against gadget touches like jet sweeps/pop passes to non-Tyreek-Hill receivers, which both hosts want retired from the playbook). Flags a real offensive-line risk ahead of the AFC Championship at Baltimore — starting guard Joe Thuney considered likely to miss the game — as the swing factor on his workload, plus his own nagging in-week injury designation. Cites specific matchup context: Baltimore has allowed 4.68 yards/carry to opposing backs since Week 10 (5th-highest in the NFL), and Pacheco has cleared 65+ rushing yards in 6 of his last 8 games.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): grouped as one of the pieces (with [[Travis Kelce]] and [[Rashee Rice]]) that gives Kansas City enough offense to "play with anyone," part of a broader case for the Chiefs' continuity heading into the Super Bowl.

- 2024-02-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-12]] [Best Ball]: the Super Bowl LVIII goat card on the surface — two fumbles, one shaken off, one on the game's opening play of the second half — but both hosts read it as a 2024 buying opportunity rather than a real red flag. The key evidence for them wasn't the fumbles, it was what Kansas City did *after* them: the coaching staff never benched him for Clyde Edwards-Helaire or Jerick McKinnon, kept feeding him touches "before the fumble he looked great... they kept going to him," which both hosts read as the organization signaling continued belief in the job. Waldman: "he'll be fine next year as a fantasy prospect... probably should be a top-10 running back in your fantasy drafts." Concrete ADP data point: currently going tail-end of round 2 to the round-3 turn in Waldman's early mock drafts — a price he's "not averse to," predicting the market will let it slip further off recency bias even though "it shouldn't." Direct parallel drawn to [[Gabe Davis]]'s post-playoff overdraft the opposite way — this is the same last-thing-you-saw bias working in Pacheco's favor as an underrated buy instead.
- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]): Super Bowl LVIII box score (18 carries, 59 yards, 3.3 YPC) read as evidence of how inconsistent Kansas City's run-blocking was for the offense all season rather than as a talent read on Pacheco himself — "the fantasy stats for Isaiah Pacheco don't really tell the story of how inconsistent this run game was," with the offensive line specifically singled out as the problem, especially edge protection.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): asked whether the camp reporting that Pacheco will get an every-down role in 2024 is credible, Waldman said Pacheco is capable of it but that it will not happen, because Andy Reid's offense has always been a committee and the roster has enough talent to rotate bodies into specific roles and give Pacheco rest. He expects a higher share that does translate to RB1-level production, but not a dominant top-five season unless injuries force the staff's hand. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed the RB11 price is right, saying he likes both the pricing and the role even if Pacheco is not the primary receiving back.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: asked whether [[Samaje Perine]] drops Pacheco's value, Waldman said there is potential for it if you drafted Pacheco expecting him to ascend to a feature role with more red-zone touches and a big passing-game share. Andy Reid's blueprint is to use two and sometimes three backs in different roles, so Waldman pegged Pacheco as an RB2 with low-end RB1 upside who is 'not ascending into the elite realm' — you will get RB1 weeks when he finds the end zone, but Perine is effective both as a runner and as a receiver. His conclusion: Perine does not really drop Pacheco's value, but he will be 'an irritant' to managers who expected more RB1 weeks. Bob Harris (untracked) agreed Pacheco and Perine split the meaningful touches.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Going Deep)]]): the [[Samaje Perine]] signing eliminates the top-five upside some drafters had priced in, though Waldman says he never expected that ceiling anyway. He still has Pacheco as a top-15 to top-20 back on the strength of Andy Reid's red-zone usage. [[Brandon Angelo]] disagrees on trajectory, calling Pacheco a career-year candidate and floating a 1,210-yard type line, while agreeing Perine will take third-down and two-minute snaps off him — which Angelo frames as a positive for keeping Pacheco healthy into December and January. The disagreement is about ceiling, not about the committee shape.
