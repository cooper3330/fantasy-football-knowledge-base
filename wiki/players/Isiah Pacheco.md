# Isiah Pacheco

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]: Pacheco is the episode's named negative exemplar for pace control — "the polar opposite" of [[Lamar Jackson]]. "He's only running 100 miles an hour. You get zero or a hundred." The contrast is with backs who vary their gears play to play to set up blocks and defender angles ([[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], [[James Cook]]); Angelo's argument is that a single-speed runner is easier to corral in space because a defender only has one tempo to solve. Delivered in passing as an illustration, not as a ranking or start/sit call. Framework: [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]].
- 2024-01-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-25]]): running well in the postseason despite the single-speed criticism from a month earlier — "he just keeps kind of producing," an open question on whether he's Kansas City's long-term bell-cow back, credited as a tone-setter Andy Reid leans on once the calendar turns to the playoffs (contrasted favorably against gadget touches like jet sweeps/pop passes to non-Tyreek-Hill receivers, which both hosts want retired from the playbook). Flags a real offensive-line risk ahead of the AFC Championship at Baltimore — starting guard Joe Thuney considered likely to miss the game — as the swing factor on his workload, plus his own nagging in-week injury designation. Cites specific matchup context: Baltimore has allowed 4.68 yards/carry to opposing backs since Week 10 (5th-highest in the NFL), and Pacheco has cleared 65+ rushing yards in 6 of his last 8 games.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): grouped as one of the pieces (with [[Travis Kelce]] and [[Rashee Rice]]) that gives Kansas City enough offense to "play with anyone," part of a broader case for the Chiefs' continuity heading into the Super Bowl.

- 2024-02-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-12]] [Best Ball]: the Super Bowl LVIII goat card on the surface — two fumbles, one shaken off, one on the game's opening play of the second half — but both hosts read it as a 2024 buying opportunity rather than a real red flag. The key evidence for them wasn't the fumbles, it was what Kansas City did *after* them: the coaching staff never benched him for Clyde Edwards-Helaire or Jerick McKinnon, kept feeding him touches "before the fumble he looked great... they kept going to him," which both hosts read as the organization signaling continued belief in the job. Waldman: "he'll be fine next year as a fantasy prospect... probably should be a top-10 running back in your fantasy drafts." Concrete ADP data point: currently going tail-end of round 2 to the round-3 turn in Waldman's early mock drafts — a price he's "not averse to," predicting the market will let it slip further off recency bias even though "it shouldn't." Direct parallel drawn to [[Gabe Davis]]'s post-playoff overdraft the opposite way — this is the same last-thing-you-saw bias working in Pacheco's favor as an underrated buy instead.
- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]): Super Bowl LVIII box score (18 carries, 59 yards, 3.3 YPC) read as evidence of how inconsistent Kansas City's run-blocking was for the offense all season rather than as a talent read on Pacheco himself — "the fantasy stats for Isaiah Pacheco don't really tell the story of how inconsistent this run game was," with the offensive line specifically singled out as the problem, especially edge protection.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): asked whether the camp reporting that Pacheco will get an every-down role in 2024 is credible, Waldman said Pacheco is capable of it but that it will not happen, because Andy Reid's offense has always been a committee and the roster has enough talent to rotate bodies into specific roles and give Pacheco rest. He expects a higher share that does translate to RB1-level production, but not a dominant top-five season unless injuries force the staff's hand. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed the RB11 price is right, saying he likes both the pricing and the role even if Pacheco is not the primary receiving back.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: asked whether [[Samaje Perine]] drops Pacheco's value, Waldman said there is potential for it if you drafted Pacheco expecting him to ascend to a feature role with more red-zone touches and a big passing-game share. Andy Reid's blueprint is to use two and sometimes three backs in different roles, so Waldman pegged Pacheco as an RB2 with low-end RB1 upside who is 'not ascending into the elite realm' — you will get RB1 weeks when he finds the end zone, but Perine is effective both as a runner and as a receiver. His conclusion: Perine does not really drop Pacheco's value, but he will be 'an irritant' to managers who expected more RB1 weeks. Bob Harris (untracked) agreed Pacheco and Perine split the meaningful touches.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Going Deep)]]): the [[Samaje Perine]] signing eliminates the top-five upside some drafters had priced in, though Waldman says he never expected that ceiling anyway. He still has Pacheco as a top-15 to top-20 back on the strength of Andy Reid's red-zone usage. [[Brandon Angelo]] disagrees on trajectory, calling Pacheco a career-year candidate and floating a 1,210-yard type line, while agreeing Perine will take third-down and two-minute snaps off him — which Angelo frames as a positive for keeping Pacheco healthy into December and January. The disagreement is about ceiling, not about the committee shape.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): Pacheco landed on IR with a fractured fibula, leaving [[Carson Steele]] and [[Samaje Perine]] to carry the Chiefs' rushing attack. Waldman thinks Kansas City sees Steele and Perine as complementary rather than a full solution, and expects them to add either a more explosive back or someone they trust across multiple facets of the offense — Keaontay Ingram off their own practice squad if they are genuinely confident in the current two, a veteran if not. He is skeptical of a Kareem Hunt reunion given Hunt lied to the same regime about the incident that got him released, and notes Jerick McKinnon is available as a receiving asset. Bob Harris (co-host, not a tracked expert) agreed the room needs another body.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-19]]): asked in a Footballguys Twitter poll which Chiefs back would be the most valuable for fantasy in 2024, co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) answered that it is probably still the injured Pacheco, and Waldman did not dispute it. The reasoning is Harstad's playoff-weighting model rather than a health forecast: if Pacheco makes it back by around Week 12 — which Harstad called a reasonable timeline — he is starting for you in Weeks 15, 16 and 17, and those weeks are worth so much more than regular-season weeks that he is likely more valuable than whoever holds the job in his absence. Waldman's own caveat was durability, noting that the Chiefs' contingency planning only reaches Kareem Hunt if Pacheco cannot stay healthy.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]): a notably critical evaluation despite Waldman saying he likes the player. Pacheco 'moves with a kinetic energy, with a lot of shake and movement — a lot of that looks, not a lot of nuance. It's honestly a lot of flash without a lot of substance.' The runs people rave about, he argues, 'are not real power' — he compared them unfavorably to [[Nick Chubb]] bowling over a defensive back with a linebacker on top of him and a safety hitting him and still getting five, and to [[Saquon Barkley]] taking a slap on the thigh pad and going 70. Waldman put [[Kareem Hunt]] ahead of him as Kansas City's best back healthy or not, on reliability rather than explosiveness.
- 2024-11-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-25]]): asked to 'feel it or f**k it' on Pacheco bogarting [[Kareem Hunt]]'s role in the fantasy playoffs with his return imminent before the Black Friday game against Las Vegas, Waldman said fuck it — while conceding this is the call he most expects to be wrong about. His reasoning is what Kansas City should do rather than what fantasy managers want: with a wildly successful season and the postseason ahead, the smart move is to keep Pacheco's leg healing, give him sparing touches in something like the Carson Steele role, and let Hunt remain the lead. Bob Harris (not tracked) agreed completely, arguing you get healthier for the games that matter most rather than the ones that matter now.
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]): his return looked better than the raw seven carries suggest. Bob Harris (not tracked) noted [[Kareem Hunt]] had over 100 carries without a single 20-plus-yard run, while Pacheco ripped one off almost immediately. Waldman concedes he and Harris were wrong the week before to want Hunt kept in the role while Pacheco sat — the Chiefs knew what they had seen in practice. He frames the wider lesson as a time-out-of-the-game factor: nine weeks out is not nine months, the injury was less serious, the rehab did not need to be as long, so Pacheco's ramp is faster than the slow returns seen from [[Kirk Cousins]] or [[Tony Pollard]]. The open question is how quickly he recovers between games.
- 2025-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-05]]): Pacheco has been essentially phased out of Kansas City's offense from a touch perspective, which Harmon found jarring given Pacheco is one of only two Chiefs on the giant radio-row banner alongside [[Patrick Mahomes]] — he joked about who Pacheco's marketing agent is. The playing-time split behind it: [[Kareem Hunt]] ~55% of snaps, Pacheco 30%, [[Samaje Perine]] 16%, [[Carson Steele]] 11%, a full four-man committee Harmon calls maddening for fantasy and for betting. His Super Bowl line was 6.5 carries with the under priced at -145 and 22.5 rushing yards. Harmon wanted an angle to bet against the Chiefs run game but could not find a clean one, describing that run game as not good but oddly effective — efficient at keeping reasonable down and distance, never explosive.
- 2025-02-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-11]]): Harmon openly questions Pacheco's future — 'I don't know what the future of Pacheco is.' Pacheco, [[Kareem Hunt]] and [[Samaje Perine]] combined for 24 rushing yards on seven carries (3.4 YPC) in Super Bowl LIX, and Pacheco and Hunt managed three rushing yards combined in the first half. Harmon allows you can write the second half of the season off to injury, but notes Pacheco was losing real snaps to Hunt late and that Hunt was significantly better in the postseason. He is equally clear you can't walk into 2025 with Hunt as the starter — Hunt is a veteran getting exactly what's blocked — so running back belongs on a long Chiefs offseason needs list, alongside a run game that was top-six in zone and gap rushing success rate but under four yards per carry in both.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]) [Best Ball]: Pacheco has been going around RB35 pre-draft, and Waldman thinks that is worth a shot as your third or fourth back if that is how you build a roster. The bet is that he underwent the same procedure as [[Tony Pollard]], who needed until roughly week 10 of the following season to look himself again — so a full year removed, a return to form is not a bad bet in best ball. Risk noted in the same breath: Kansas City drafted [[Brashard Smith]] in the seventh and added [[Elijah Mitchell]] (Bob Harris, not tracked, quips Mitchell will have two good games and get hurt), and Waldman says Pacheco has perhaps a year left on his deal with no certainty of an extension.
- 2025-06-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he is completely feeling Pacheco at RB29 as the price to find out. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) set it up with the timeline — Pacheco opened 2024 with 19 carries then 15 before breaking his leg, and after returning never topped about five carries in any playoff game, which Waldman read as the Chiefs acknowledging he was not back to full speed. The reported return to his combine numbers, a sub-4.44 forty at 4.37 and 216 pounds, is the case for this year. Waldman projects him as no worse than an 800-yard rusher and probably no worse than 1,100 total yards with seven to eight touchdowns, which he called great value for the 29th running back off the board.
- 2025-07-07 — According to Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]] [Redraft (Standard)]: he is 'drafting heavily' because Pacheco is a cheap starter with a clear path to workload — 19 carries in the season opener and 15 in the game he broke his leg show there was a plan, and no reason it changes if he is healthy. Pacheco says he is healthy and his combine-level numbers (4.37 speed, 216 pounds) are reportedly back, but Harris flagged training camp as where that gets answered. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed the plan-plus-health case is the reason [[Brashard Smith]] is capped in redraft; he also cited Brett Veach's stated willingness to find backs in the seventh round — which is where Pacheco came from — as why the Smith lottery ticket is not unreasonable.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): the snap split was 28 Pacheco, 23 [[Kareem Hunt]], eight rookie [[Brashard Smith]], and Harris uses his typewriter-feet description for Pacheco. He allows the mitigating context that this was a hard matchup against another very good run defense, but the role news is what worries him: Hunt was clearly the third-down back, playing basically every third down, and clearly the short-yardage back too. At the very least there is no deference paid to Pacheco on the depth chart, and at worst Hunt looks like the one you would want to own. Harris does not think Hunt is that good, which he says makes the situation not great rather than a clean handoff of value.
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-10-27]]) [Redraft]: nobody is 'blown away' by the Chiefs backfield yet, but Harris says being attached to a more aggressive, more explosive Kansas City offense 'breathes new life' into Pacheco's outlook. He debunks a bad Rotoworld update claiming Kareem Hunt (untracked-relevant, also on the roster) wouldn't play -- Hunt wasn't even on the injury report -- and notes Prashard Smith is healthy too, so the committee remains crowded and frustrating for redraft managers.
- 2025-12-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-08]]): Harmon said Pacheco 'never flipped the switch' against Houston and 'has given me nothing all year' since returning from his prior injury, adding he may simply not be the same runner and that his downhill running style doesn't fit the Chiefs' current offensive line struggles. The snap count ran 38-21 in favor of [[Kareem Hunt]], including short-yardage work, with Kansas City reverting to 'the old ways' once the line collapsed.
