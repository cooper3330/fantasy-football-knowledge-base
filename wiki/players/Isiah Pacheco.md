# Isiah Pacheco

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]: Pacheco is the episode's named negative exemplar for pace control — "the polar opposite" of [[Lamar Jackson]]. "He's only running 100 miles an hour. You get zero or a hundred." The contrast is with backs who vary their gears play to play to set up blocks and defender angles ([[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], [[James Cook]]); Angelo's argument is that a single-speed runner is easier to corral in space because a defender only has one tempo to solve. Delivered in passing as an illustration, not as a ranking or start/sit call. Framework: [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]].
- 2024-01-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-25]]): running well in the postseason despite the single-speed criticism from a month earlier — "he just keeps kind of producing," an open question on whether he's Kansas City's long-term bell-cow back, credited as a tone-setter Andy Reid leans on once the calendar turns to the playoffs (contrasted favorably against gadget touches like jet sweeps/pop passes to non-Tyreek-Hill receivers, which both hosts want retired from the playbook). Flags a real offensive-line risk ahead of the AFC Championship at Baltimore — starting guard Joe Thuney considered likely to miss the game — as the swing factor on his workload, plus his own nagging in-week injury designation. Cites specific matchup context: Baltimore has allowed 4.68 yards/carry to opposing backs since Week 10 (5th-highest in the NFL), and Pacheco has cleared 65+ rushing yards in 6 of his last 8 games.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): grouped as one of the pieces (with [[Travis Kelce]] and [[Rashee Rice]]) that gives Kansas City enough offense to "play with anyone," part of a broader case for the Chiefs' continuity heading into the Super Bowl.
