# Emmett Johnson

## Expert Takes

- 2026-02-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-09]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is 'feeling it' on Johnson possibly being the best running back prospect in the class as a pure runner -- smooth, excellent agility, creative, with power and contact balance for his size, and what may be the best decision-making/game-management among the 58-59 backs he's graded this year. The one thing keeping him out of Waldman's top tier is pass protection: 'I need more proof of concept that he's gotten better' -- if that improves, Waldman says Johnson would be his RB1 on the board. As a receiver he's more checkdown/screen/wheel-route capable than a route-savvy [[Christian McCaffrey]] or [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] type, but he can make plays with the right matchup.
- 2026-03-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-02]]) [Dynasty]: walked through Johnson's full combine workout -- 4.56 40 (fine, within starter range), 4.29 20-shuttle (fine, low end of starter range), 35.5-inch vertical (fine) -- and said none of those raise concern about his ability to contribute or start in the league, though he's unlikely to be a home-run hitter. The real flag is his 7.32 three-cone drill; Waldman wants to see under 7.1, and Johnson's game is built heavily on change-of-direction ability, so the number is 'a step or two slower than what you would like to see.' Waldman said Johnson may have carried second-round buzz into the combine but now projects him more as an early Day 3 pick, and said he plans to re-watch tape to see whether Johnson still merits as high a ranking as he had pre-combine.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-05]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo said he really likes [[Emmett Johnson]] of Nebraska on tape -- strong through-contact skill and varied tools to break tackles, in a similar vein to how he once liked [[Bucky Irving]] and [[Devin Singletary]] -- but worried a lackluster Combine could push Johnson's draft capital down in a thin running back class; he noted Singletary was a third-round pick in an earlier era when that draft capital doesn't exist anymore for that archetype, and guessed Johnson goes around the sixth round rather than Day 2. Waldman agreed on the tape comp but voiced a bust-risk concern, worrying Johnson could turn into a '[[Trey Sermon]]-esque' situation -- flashes of vision and contact balance at the college level that don't translate because NFL gaps and closures open and close far faster than what he saw against Big Ten competition (Waldman said he may have overrated Sermon's explosiveness similarly at the time). Both said Johnson will need to land in a thin backfield -- they cited Chicago and Washington as examples -- to see meaningful early work given his projected mid-to-late Day 3 draft slot.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Johnson isn't talked about much partly because he didn't test well, but Angelo compares his profile to Devin Singletary and [[Bucky Irving]] -- not overly athletic but productive, in the vein of a back who can still stick in the league (Singletary was drafted in 2019 and remains an NFL contributor). Waldman used Johnson's roughly 4.55 40 as a case study for why raw testing is a poor decision tool in isolation: it doesn't show whether a player clears the minimum threshold his role actually requires. Waldman's personal rule of thumb is about a 4.59 40 as the floor for an average-size back to grade as a viable starter, and he evaluates Johnson more on compensatory skills -- vision, decision-making, quickness -- than pure measurables.
- 2026-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-01]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman called Johnson 'this year's candidate for the NFL Combine to throw a red herring into the collective draft analysis.' His 4.56 40 (matching Aaron Jones' comparable time) and 4.29 20-yard shuttle are starter-caliber by Waldman's own minimums, but Johnson's 7.32 three-cone (32nd-percentile RAS) drew pile-on criticism Waldman rejected at length, calling RAS-driven takes 'the pseudo-science church of oblong Moneyball.' He listed backs who started or contributed at a high level despite similar or worse three-cone marks -- Kenneth Gainwell (7.26), Dalvin Cook (7.27), Samaje Perine (7.25), Jamaal Williams (7.25), Chris Carson (7.53), Devin Singletary (7.32) -- arguing the drill measures lateral cuts specifically, while Cook's curvilinear (bending) movement style made it a poor proxy; Cook still posted a four-year peak averaging 1,300+ yards from scrimmage, topping out at 1,918 yards/17 TDs. On tape, Waldman said Johnson executes lateral cuts (unlike Cook) while also leaning on curvilinear movement, flips his hips for 90-degree turns without cutting, and processes fast like Devin Singletary, reading pre-snap tells for quick cutbacks and pressing gaps with precise footwork.
- 2026-04-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-06]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether Emmett Johnson's poor combine testing (lowest Relative Athletic Score of any RB who tested, ~7.32-7.37 in the three-cone) reflects his true ability, Waldman says feel it, not at all. He argues the three-cone is a difficult, learnable drill most backs refuse to run, and that he's seen backs like Chris Carson (7.53) and Kenneth Gainwell run similarly slow times. He draws a direct comp to [[Dalvin Cook]], who also tested poorly (bad three-cone and vertical) yet built his career on curvilinear movement -- bending through cuts rather than making sharp lateral cuts, a trait the three-cone doesn't capture well. Johnson shares that trait plus strong pre/post-snap box-reading and precise footwork where speed matters less than anticipation. Waldman's broader RAS critique: what matters isn't percentile rank but the minimum threshold needed to contribute in the NFL, and he believes Johnson clears that bar and 'is going to surprise people who wrote him off too soon.'
- 2026-04-09 — According to Paul Pertichese, guest analyst on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-09]] (not a tracked expert) [Dynasty]: Emmett Johnson grades out right around a top-100 prospect for Pertichese -- a good, shifty receiving back with quick feet for his size and a 'very slashing' style he compared aspirationally to Dalvin Cook, without Cook's overall talent level. Buzz cooled after a combine where his measured speed and athleticism scores undershot how fast he looked on tape, and he's viewed as a one-year-wonder since he wasn't on breakout-age radars entering the season. Pertichese projects him going late third or early fourth round and becoming a functional three-down option, with the gap between him and [[Mike Washington]] razor-thin -- Washington gets the nod only for flashier physical tools, while Johnson has fewer glaring weaknesses overall.
- 2026-04-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-09]]) [Dynasty]: has Emmett Johnson as his overall RB2 in the 2026 class, trailing [[DeMond Claiborne]] by roughly a tenth of a grading point (Claiborne was actually Waldman's RB1 until the fumble concerns surfaced). [[Brandon Angelo]] separately said Johnson is too reactive and lateral a mover to be a good weight-adding candidate -- unlike [[Nicholas Singleton]], added mass would blunt the change-of-direction quality that defines his game.
- 2026-05-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-21]]): Harmon profiled the Nebraska rookie (5'10', 202 lbs, ~4.5 40, 35.5-inch vertical -- 'plain Jane' athletic testing) and said his tape matched: better in man-gap than zone schemes, with real ability to make defenders miss once he reaches the second level but only average success getting there. On outside man-gap runs specifically he posted a 78.1% success rate, just behind Jadarian Price's 78.3% (a player Harmon calls one of the best in this class at running outside), and his overall man-gap success rate of 61.1% sat within range of Cam Skattebo's 61.5%. Harmon framed Johnson as a 'grinder' who reliably picks up 3-7 yards rather than an explosive playmaker, which he thinks pairs well next to explosive lead back [[Kenneth Walker III]] in new Kansas City OC Eric Bienemy's run scheme -- Bienemy is described as a strong running-back developer (cited for getting a career-best decisive season out of DeAndre Swift and a strong debut out of seventh-rounder Kyle Monangay in Chicago) who could carve out early-down burn for Johnson.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-17]]) [Dynasty]: Johnson led the Chiefs with 59 rushing yards in his exhibition debut (per Nate Taylor, ESPN), running through and around tackles, with a 4-yard touchdown erased by a holding penalty. Waldman said he 'totally' believes in Johnson, projecting that if his trajectory continues and his college tape keeps translating as the level of competition rises, he overtakes Amari Demercado as the backup option by midseason.
- 2026-08-31 — According to Scott Pianowski (guest, not a tracked expert; [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-31]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Pianowski's number five late-round favorite is Emmett Johnson, who appears to have won the No. 2 running back job behind an injury-prone starter (Kenneth Walker). He likes that Johnson had both a rushing and receiving profile in college, giving him potential dual-threat value if he ever gets a real role. Pianowski frames this as a lottery-ticket handcuff pick among a dozen-plus similar backups league-wide, saying Johnson is one of the few he's actively prioritizing because he trusts the player, even though the market currently lets him fall to almost any round you want. Harris agreed he has drafted Johnson himself in double-digit rounds and sees him as a low-cost, high-variance flier.
