# Antonio Williams

## Expert Takes

- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: one of his top two receivers for 2026 alongside [[Jordyn Tyson]], and 'really really interesting'. He tempers the production expectation because Clemson's receiver room is loaded — Angelo puts him nearer 900-1,000 yards than 1,200-1,300 — and reads him as primarily a slot receiver who can play outside, with great suddenness out of his breaks, real after-catch ability and value as a crafty punt returner on special teams. He also lists Williams among the day-two-or-better NFL receivers catching passes from [[Cade Klubnik]].
- 2026-03-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-02]]) [Dynasty]: an outright 'combine winner' -- at roughly 5'10/5'11 and under 200 pounds he needed to show speed and quickness, and did, running 4.41 in the 40 with a 7.03 three-cone and 39.5-inch vertical. Waldman said the profile matches his tape: he works the middle of the field heavily but can play outside, wins contested catches and has after-the-catch skill. Projects him as no worse than an early Day 3 pick who contributes right away, and thinks he'll actually go Day 2.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-05]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman likes [[Antonio Williams]] of Clemson as an inside-outside receiver, citing the play speed to threaten deep and the toughness at the catch point to win contested balls. He sees Williams as a more finished version of the catch-point traits people project onto [[KC Concepcion]], while noting Concepcion still grades higher overall on his board.
- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Williams looks like he could become very good against man coverage as an outside receiver, but that's a theoretical projection right now -- his release footwork, route setups and boundary work against tight coverage all still need refining. Waldman's fallback plan is the slot: Williams already handles underneath work well and offers a big-play element after the catch and downfield against zone, so a team could do a lot worse than stashing him there while his outside game develops.
- 2026-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: in a head-to-head 'boring glue guy' rapid-fire question, both hosts picked Williams over [[Germie Bernard]] -- Waldman cited cleaner hands and fewer catching issues, calling him 'a little more spicy,' and sees flanker/slot upside if he doesn't stay purely in the slot. Harmon agreed but hedged that Bernard may better fit the current NFL meta as an over-the-middle dig-route glue guy. Both project Williams as a likely NFL WR3.
- 2026-04-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-14]]): Williams is Harmon's 2026-class answer to his running joke comp of [[Sterling Shepard]] -- a reliable slot player who can beat man coverage better than a typical slot-only guy. At the combine he measured 5'11', 187 lbs, ran a 4.40 (88th-percentile vertical at 39.5', 80th-percentile broad at 10'4'), with wingspan at the 43rd percentile. In Harmon's Reception Perception sample (14.1 snaps tracked), Williams lined up in the slot on 82% of snaps, 4.3% in the backfield, and was off the ball on 86.1% of snaps. He posted a 75th-percentile success rate vs. man, nearly 82% vs. zone, and 73% vs. press -- clearing the 70-80% benchmarks Harmon likes from slot prospects -- plus an 80% contested-catch rate (on limited volume) that Harmon says reflects real improvement in ball skills after early-career drop issues. Harmon grades Williams a tier above fellow prospect [[Deion Burks]], but a tier below both [[Ladd McConkey]]-type players and Josh Downs as a ball-carrier, since he lacks great tackle-breaking or yards-after-catch ability; his translation to the NFL slot role is his central question given the era's shrinking three-WR usage.
- 2026-04-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman likes Washington's pick of Williams as one of the better landing spots among the early-round rookie receivers, since the Commanders need a flanker or slot option and haven't successfully drafted or signed a receiver who's worked out long-term. He says Williams currently profiles better in the slot than as a flanker but has the skill after the catch to grow into either, comping him to an aspiring [[Jayden Reed]]-type player.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-30 (Round 3 WRs)]]) [Dynasty]: Washington took Williams 7th in round 3, which Harmon called a great pick -- he had Williams as a tier-four receiver he would've taken early in round two, so this is value. Williams was in the slot on 82% of his charted 2025 snaps at Clemson (86% off-ball), a hyper-specific archetype Harmon admits he's overweighted in the past, but thinks Williams' skill set lets him play Z in two-receiver sets too, not just slot-only. He posted a 75th-percentile success rate vs. man coverage, flashed well on corner/post/dig routes, and caught roughly 80% of his (11.8%) contested targets. Harmon floated a Christian Kirk-type vertical-slot comp over the 'pop gun slot' label. He landed in an ideal spot: Terry McLaurin is essentially the only established option, with Brandon Aiyuk's return a 'very risky bet to write in pen' given he hasn't played since October 2024. New OC David Blough (a Ben Johnson protege but also Cliff Kingsbury's former assistant QB coach) is expected to blend more under-center/play-action verticals with QB Jayden Daniels, which Harmon thinks suits Williams well. Harmon says he's higher on Williams' year-one dynasty outlook than most, expecting instant impact given the opportunity.
- 2026-05-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-04]]) [Dynasty]: calls Antonio Williams an underrated fantasy asset, not an overrated one. He cites a longtime scout's Randall Cobb comparison (Waldman's own comp lands on Jaden Reed) and expects Williams to slot in as a slot flanker in an offense built to use that role -- likely the team's WR3, and possibly its second- or third-most productive receiver, even with [[Luke McCaffrey]] entering year three. Waldman flags that if the offense mirrors David Blough's Detroit scheme, Williams could play an enhanced Jaden Reed/[[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]-type role that drives a high target count, which he says is worth investing in. The RSP post-draft book has Williams with a shot to open the season ahead of [[Terry McLaurin]] on early-down snaps.
- 2026-05-11 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-11]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether Antonio Williams is slot-only in Washington, Waldman firmly said no -- he can play flanker too, and long-term projection aside, it might actually be easier for him to win the flanker role as a rookie since it requires reading less of the middle of the field than the slot does. He weighed Williams against the rest of Washington's depth (Luke McCaffrey as a possible ascending piece, and big-slot types Noah Brown and [[Dyami Brown]], neither of whom Waldman is excited about as a true number-two or -three), praising Williams' work as a runner after the catch and as a contested-catch player. His long-term outcome comp: a Jayden Reed/Randall Cobb-type role.
- 2026-06-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Williams is his surprise pick in Washington, comparing him to a [[Jayden Reed]]-type flanker (maybe not as good as Reed) who could become the team's third-most-targeted player depending on [[Chigoziem Okonkwo]] and [[Jayden Daniels]]'s development; [[Jayden Daniels]] is reportedly already cutting in front of backups in practice reps to build early rapport with Williams. Waldman projects 80-100 targets, 55-70 catches, 650-750 yards and 5 touchdowns as the rookie-year upside, and states Williams is 'the first player I draft in the second round' of current rookie drafts -- ahead of [[Luke McCaffrey]] and [[Traylon Burks]] -- reasoning that [[Terry McLaurin]] just turned 31 and is no longer 2022 form, the Commanders had no 800-yard receiver last year, and neither [[Brandon Aiyuk]] (health uncertain after two years out) nor a healthy [[Zach Ertz]]/[[Keenan Allen]]/[[Deebo Samuel|Debo]] signing is guaranteed, leaving an opening for a safety-blanket role.
- 2026-08-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-24 (Feel It or Fk It)]]): Waldman liked Antonio Williams coming out of the draft as a scheme-versatile back who could play inside and outside, and a beat writer covering the Commanders (David Harrison, untracked) told him Williams had one of the most impressive first days of training camp he'd seen in years. But Waldman said [[Stefon Diggs]]'s arrival and the team's shift toward more two-tight-end sets have squeezed the passing-game role Williams needed to become a factor, so despite the camp buzz being a legitimate 'attaboy' from the coaching staff, Waldman thinks it will take longer than this season for Williams to carve out a real role. Co-host Bob Harris agreed, adding that Diggs's presence has generally closed off opportunities other Commanders skill players might otherwise have earned. [Dynasty]
- 2026-09-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-09-01]]) [Dynasty]: he opened aggressive on Williams from a dynasty standpoint post-draft but had to knock him down after Washington signed [[Stefon Diggs]], removing his path to an immediate role -- though Harmon says reports indicate Williams has picked up steam as camp progressed and could see a role as the year goes on. He frames the bigger risk as the Commanders' offense itself: reporting from colleague Jori Epstein calling the WR room the team's deepest position struck Harmon as odd given the rest of the roster, which lost its top-tier left tackle and is installing a brand-new under-center/play-action scheme under first-time OC David Blough (only 31, promoted from assistant QB coach). Beat-reporter accounts of the offensive line have been uniformly horrific. Harmon says a slow Williams rookie year risks him getting lost in the shuffle unless he pops late, especially with a coaching change plausible for next season.
