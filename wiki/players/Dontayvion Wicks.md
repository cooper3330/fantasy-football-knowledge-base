# Dontayvion Wicks

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Dynasty]: "turned some heads" and specifically turned Waldman's — a strong performer down the stretch of 2023 who answered the call whenever [[Christian Watson]] was unavailable. Projects he could bump [[Romeo Doubs]] or an injury-prone Watson out of Green Bay's fantasy-relevant WR mix, offering catch-point toughness and ruggedness after the catch that neither Watson nor [[Jayden Reed]] provides, in exchange for less raw speed — an Antonio Freeman-type possession comp. Notes Wicks's position coach, Drew Lieberman — whom Waldman calls his own unofficial receiver-scouting mentor of the past 7-8 years — privately predicted a year earlier that Wicks "can actually be a starter one day."
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: Harstad's [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] has Wicks 5th in the entire class on pure per-route efficiency — ahead of [[Zay Flowers]] and [[Jordan Addison]] — despite one of the lowest usage/participation rates in the class, and calls him, in terms of model score vs. market cost, the single biggest buy in the whole rookie class (ahead even of [[Puka Nacua]], whose market has already caught up some). Waldman goes further than a hold: for the first time frames Wicks as a player he'd actively trade *for* as a target, not accept only as a throw-in — "he's not an afterthought to the trade, he's a part of it." Notable neighbors on the model's ranked list: Diontae Johnson directly above him, George Pickens directly below — three very differently-styled but similarly efficient receivers.
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]): singles out his touchdown catch in the Wild Card win over Dallas as "awesome," and separately calls him "darn near uncoverable" in man coverage — especially near the goal line — running "the crispiest routes of all time" for Green Bay. Speculates that under a more hubris-driven quarterback (his explicit example: Aaron Rodgers) Wicks would have been benched for his early-season mistakes rather than allowed to develop into this.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Best Ball]: reiterates his interest directly to Wicks on air — "I'm looking at you, young man" — as the receiver he's most focused on entering best-ball draft season, ahead of Green Bay's crowded WR room sorting itself out.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]) [Dynasty]: independent third-source corroboration of the buy thesis — the traits needed to be a true number-one receiver ("based on the way he ran routes this year from the outside") are present in a way Harmon explicitly says they aren't for [[Christian Watson]], making Wicks the second-best bet (behind [[Jayden Reed]]) to become Green Bay's number one. Also praised for blocking effort in the run game against San Francisco — "he was blocking his ass off."


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: going as roughly WR90 in early best balls, calling him "a free square" — reiterates plans for many shares given the price relative to his standing in [[Jordan Love]]'s crowded but cheap Green Bay receiver room.
- 2024-05-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-21]]): as a rookie (15 games, 6 starts, 39/58 targets, 581 yards, 4 TD, 67.2% catch rate, 14.9 YPR; 61% of snaps outside, 37% slot), Wicks posted a 72.2% success rate vs. man and 72.7% vs. press as a primarily outside receiver, with his charted success rates improving steadily from the in-season rookie report through year-end. His out route (11% of routes) hit a 90% success rate, and his corner route (6.9% of routes) hit 83.3%; he also graded well on the slant, curl, and post, with a heavy 19.4% dig-route rate at 70.6% success, suggesting a Puka Nacua-style middle-of-field role in this offense. His 41.2% success rate on go/nine routes was his clear weak spot. Combine measurables: 6'1", 206 lbs, a poor 4.62 forty but an excellent 39-inch vertical, consistent with a player who wins more via route craft/bounce than straight speed. He broke tackles on 25% of catches and went down on first contact only 41.7% of the time. Matt LaFleur has compared Wicks's route running to Davante Adams. Harmon says Wicks is within the range of outcomes to be the best receiver on Green Bay's roster -- more well-rounded than [[Romeo Doubs]] or [[Christian Watson]] -- though he still favors [[Jayden Reed]] as the odds-on favorite; he notes Wicks's May 2024 best-ball ADP (WR68, ~157th overall) undersells him relative to Reed (WR35), Watson (WR44), and Doubs (WR51). Playing-time competition and Green Bay's run-first LaFleur scheme are the stated risk to fantasy relevance.
- 2024-05-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-30]]): while breaking down [[Jayden Reed]]'s profile, Harmon said Wicks is "due for a role expansion" based on his own Reception Perception data and is the one Packers receiver who, if he takes a leap, could threaten Reed's status as the team's clear top target earner. Harmon noted he prefers extolling Wicks's profile over the more conventionally discussed Christian Watson/Romeo Doubs pairing.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman called Wicks 'a fun hot name right now' with a real path to being the Packers' WR2 and a 'close WR1,' grouping him with [[Christian Watson]] as having 'the most to gain' in Green Bay's receiver room, versus [[Jayden Reed]] whose role Waldman views as more fixed. In Waldman's touchdown split for the group he had Wicks around 7 touchdowns in one pass at the numbers, among the higher shares of the trio.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon graded Wicks as showing he 'could win inside outside breaking routes' with strong reps against man coverage last season, part of a crowded four-way Packers WR room (with [[Jayden Reed]], [[Romeo Doubs]], and Christian Watson) that Harmon compared structurally to Green Bay's unresolved offensive-line battles at left tackle and right guard. Harmon flagged QB Jordan Love's own public comment hyping Wicks for a 'massive' and 'monster' season, but cautioned that hype cycles have circulated for all four Packers receivers, and the real swing factor is which one ends up running the most routes -- something the offensive line's continuity (or lack of it) will help decide.
- 2024-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-30]]): Wicks is Harmon's bold pick to lead the Packers in receiving yards, and he calls him the best boundary receiver on the roster. Harmon admits he did not chart Wicks as a prospect and is 'not the originator' of the hype, but Wicks popped in efficiency metrics as a day-three rookie and earned a four-game in-season rookie report that held up across the full profile: 72.2% success rate vs man — above Harmon's 70% bar for an outside receiver worth betting on — and 69th percentile vs press, with 90% success on outs, 86.2% on slants and a high curl success rate, which Harmon reads as a three-level route runner. His frame is that Wicks is 'B, B-minus at everything': arguably the second best separator behind [[Jayden Reed]], first or second best ball winner alongside [[Romeo Doubs]], second best tackle breaker behind [[Christian Watson]]. He is neither a burner like Watson nor a pure outside stretch winner like Doubs, but plays both X and flanker. Harmon's caveat: in almost any other room he would demand 75% route participation, but here the three outside bodies eat into each other and someone has to be displaced or take a year-two route-running leap for the upside to land. Koh independently projects Wicks second in the room at 54-780-4.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Harmon calls Wicks probably the best true ISO route runner in the Green Bay room and more of an outside player than [[Jayden Reed]], who primarily runs from the slot. He saw more deep-down-the-field ISO route running and releases off the line against press from Wicks as a rookie than from Reed. Harmon says Wicks is too good to simply throw on the bench, and the open question is what that means for [[Christian Watson]] and [[Romeo Doubs]] — and whether the coaching staff shares his view of the two.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the film behind Wicks's quiet week one is better than the box score. He was targeted early and slipped on a downfield seam route — Waldman notes teammate Emmanuel Wilson slipped in the same region of the Brazil field on the next play, so it read as a turf issue rather than footwork — then had a drop where he should have reached and taken the safety's hit but which [[Jordan Love]] also shouldn't have thrown, and got wide open on an out route that Love threw behind him. Two of those came in the first half, after which the Packers went to the hot hands. Waldman says Wicks is third or fourth on the depth chart right now, expects a hot-hand rotation all year, and would hold him as people drop him. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) relayed ESPN's Rob Domoski framing: [[Romeo Doubs]] as the de facto go-to, [[Jayden Reed]] the jack-of-all-trades athlete, [[Christian Watson]] the big-play threat, everyone else situational.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): with [[Christian Watson]] reportedly headed for IR with a high ankle sprain and Wicks drawing 13 targets for five catches, Waldman said 'absolutely' he is a viable frontline fantasy option, noting he had flagged Wicks after Week 1 on the Football Guys top 10 as a near-term buy-low. He argued the ugly box scores were misleading: a turf-monster fall against Philadelphia, a drop, some bad throws, tipped passes, throws behind him, and a catch the officials simply missed. What Wicks does well is attack the football, work over the middle and produce after the catch. Waldman thinks losing Watson may actually help Green Bay by clarifying roles — deeper work for Jayden Reed, timing and tough middle routes for Romeo Doubs, with Wicks overlapping and differentiating in a more complementary trio than the Watson version. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) argued the roles were already well defined with Doubs as the go-to, Reed as the athletic jack-of-all-trades and Watson as the deep threat, but co-signed on Wicks.
- 2024-10-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-01]]): Wicks' Week 4 against Minnesota was 'the best worst game' — a team-high 13 targets, two red-zone touchdowns and 218 air yards but only 78 receiving yards, with fantasy points data crediting him three drops and a lost contested-catch attempt. Harmon said he wants to re-chart the game before deciding which were true drops, but stressed the key signal is that [[Jordan Love]] kept coming back to him in critical moments. He cited Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, not a tracked expert): Wicks has hit 70%-plus route participation in three career games, scoring 15.7, 24.0 and 23.8 fantasy points in them. Wicks' 2023 [[Reception Perception Methodology]] profile: 72.2% success vs man (71st percentile), 72.7% vs press, 49th percentile vs zone, 90% success on out routes; Harmon called him a great ISO man-coverage route runner and a pre-season sleeper who now gets a near full-time role.
- 2024-10-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Wicks has caught 7 of his last 22 targets (32%) and sits at a 34% catch rate on the season with a 28.6% drop rate — lower than the notorious [[Zay Jones]] rookie mark of 36.5%, and far worse than the next-highest 20-plus-target player, [[Adonai Mitchell]] at 25%. Harmon insists this is not a hands problem by profile: Wicks posted an over-70% contested catch rate and an ordinary drop rate in his rookie Reception Perception charting (PFF had him at 4.7%), and a 67% catch rate last year. In the in-season RP tracker Harmon gave him a 'wow' relative-performance designation on 83.3% success rate vs. man and 84.1% vs. zone across two games, while writing that he wanted to dock it because the hands were so bad — three drops and multiple contested-catch failures, with 0% contested catch rate per PFF, Fantasy Points and his own charting. His diagnosis is confidence and technique: looking upfield early, clapping at the ball instead of catching it, plus a crowded room ([[Christian Watson]], [[Romeo Doubs]], [[Jayden Reed]]) making him press to maximize scarce chances. Harmon is ~90% confident it is fixed by next season but unsure in-season, and argues Green Bay should keep feeding him to rebuild confidence. James Koh (not tracked) is more alarmed. Harmon jokes the [[Davante Adams]] comp (15.6% drop rate in year two) is being taken too literally and predicts Wicks gets rage-dropped then breaks out.
- 2024-11-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-26]]): Wicks has not taken the step, and the failure is not separation — Harmon says he still gets open but has 'literally dropped his opportunity' and truly fumbled it away this season. He maintains there was no mis-evaluating the rookie-year film, which was really good, and thinks Wicks has gotten into his own head; Green Bay does not want to give up on him and still wants to develop him, but is counting on him a lot less, which Harmon calls smart. Koh (not tracked) is harsher, saying the mental errors are everywhere and he does not know how the Packers can trust putting him on the field in Week 12. Harmon notes Wicks still made key blocks against San Francisco.
- 2024-12-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-31]]): Wicks 'definitely did not take the next step' in year two, going from a useful role player as a rookie to a receiver who dropped a lot of his opportunities. Harmon is careful not to write him off — he says Wicks is not a guy Green Bay should totally give up on and there is a scenario where he still develops, which is part of why the Packers keep rotating so many receivers through. He folds Wicks into his broader point that a bad or disappointing early season does not define a career, listing him alongside [[Michael Wilson]] and [[Demario Douglas]] as 2023-class receivers worth keeping an eye on into year three.
- 2025-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-19]]): Harmon will not give up on Wicks and thinks he still has a lot of potential, but says you cannot write him into the lineup in pen as a starter either. He is under a rookie deal through 2026 alongside [[Jayden Reed]], while [[Romeo Doubs]] and [[Christian Watson]] are in their final years. Harmon notes Green Bay has the fifth-lowest 2025 cap spend at wide receiver precisely because its four best receivers are all on rookie deals, giving them room to add a veteran perimeter man-coverage beater — the one thing he says the roster does not have at all.
- 2025-06-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-27]]): Wicks has one of the highest drop rates Harmon has ever charted — second highest in Reception Perception history behind only teammate [[Romeo Doubs]]'s rookie year — with a horrible contested-catch rate, and Harmon believes he also took a step back as a route runner in year two. He calls Wicks the most overrated separator in the league relative to the 'best separator in football' talk: a solidly above-average 64th-percentile success rate versus man, best over the middle on isolated slants, but below average versus zone in each of his first two seasons. [[Matt Waldman]] adds that Wicks was fighting the ball early despite good positioning at the catch point and good route progress working with coach Drew Lieberman, turning a potential breakout into a fight to be the WR3 unless [[Romeo Doubs]] is traded. In the closing would-you-rather, Waldman said if he had to develop a player and lean on him rather than plug him into a fixed role, he would take Wicks over [[Quentin Johnston]], since Wicks's catch problem reads as overthinking rather than an inability to track; Harmon disagreed and took the run-after-catch player as the easier fix.
- 2025-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-11]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon explicitly reverses his prior bullish call — he says he was in on Wicks and got that wrong. Every charting split went backwards in year two: 72.2% to 70.9% vs man, 78.6% to 78.3% vs zone, and 72.7% to 68% vs press. Worse than the drops, he says, is that Wicks regressed as a separator and technician when the whole point of year two is development. The catch issues are extreme: a 15.4% RP drop rate, second highest in RP history behind teammate [[Romeo Doubs]]' rookie season, and a contested catch rate collapsing from 71.4% to 25%, which Harmon calls complete failure at the catch point (he charts drops and failed contested catches separately). He pushes back on the industry framing of Wicks as one of the NFL's best separators — his good routes are a narrow band of slants, outs and corners, comparable to [[Adonai Mitchell]] though more balanced, while he ran the curl on 18% of routes at a poor 72.4% and the dig on 19.9% despite it never being a strength. His nine route did improve from 41% to 58%. Harmon's path forward: Wicks is not really an X or a flanker, and his do-a-bit-of-everything profile makes him an ideal WR3/WR4 injury fill-in behind Doubs, [[Jayden Reed]] and [[Matthew Golden]]. Koh adds that the drops likely compound mentally for a day-three pick fighting for snaps.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-05]]) [Best Ball]: Harmon is willing to 'ever so quietly' buy a little Wicks for the first month — at 10 or 25 cents on the dollar, not with his full wallet — as the next man up in the slot if [[Jayden Reed]]'s Jones fracture costs him time. He had written Wicks off with [[Matthew Golden]] and [[Romeo Doubs]] in the room, and took his one and only redraft share in a 22-round charity best ball draft as a joke. The case is not that Wicks is a secret elite separator — Harmon called him a good but somewhat overrated separator — it is that he has played roughly 30 percent of his snaps in the slot in each of his first two years and can credibly play all three receiver spots, winning on horizontal breaking routes (slants, digs, outs). The caveat is the hands, plus route-running regression from year one to year two that Harmon thinks was mentally tied to the drops. Koh (not tracked) predicted Wicks will surface in waiver-wire articles this season; Harmon noted [[Romeo Doubs]] as a precedent for a Packers receiver whose best year came in year three.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-13]]): Harris considered Wicks for DFS since he technically drew the most Packers targets last week, but ultimately would not play him, saying his hands are 'wooden ducks attached to his wrists'. He notes that in the Harris Football Almanac this summer he downgraded Wicks's hands grade to a D, and that his Yacht Club Discord thought even that was too generous. Harris grants the separation is real — the joke runs that ducks are great at separating from other ducks but bad at catching bread out of the air — but says the drops make him unplayable.
- 2025-12-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-01 (Feel It or Fk It)]]): Wicks had a strong game against Detroit, catching everything 'all over the place,' but Waldman noted the catch point hasn't been his calling card in recent years and expects him to keep having flashes rather than becoming a consistent lead target -- part of a Packers receiver corps Waldman called a 'whack-a-mole' group (Wicks sits at a 13% target share) with no established alpha.
- 2025-12-02 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-12-02]]) [Dynasty]: Wicks went for 7 targets, 6 catches, 94 yards and 2 touchdowns against Detroit, and Harmon credited a specific usage change -- 43% of his snaps from the slot this game -- for unlocking him on out-breaking and intermediate routes, calling him 'a really good route runner who gets himself open against man and press coverage,' though 'not necessarily the best vertical receiver.' Harmon noted he took a step back in his second season after being miscast more as a perimeter X-type, and flagged real improvement in his hands/catch technique this year. He complements [[Christian Watson]] (vertical) and [[Romeo Doubs]] (steady in-breaker X) in the room. Harmon and co-host James Koh (untracked) also argued Green Bay should stop rotating Doubs/Watson/Wicks off the field for lesser options (Malik Heath, etc.) given the trio combined for 81/69/69%-plus route participation. Long-term, Wicks is signed through this year with a contract year next, and Harmon said Green Bay could get proactive about extending or moving him; he ranked Wicks third of the four 'truthers' discussed (behind [[Adonai Mitchell]] and [[Jalen Coker]]) for theoretical ceiling but said his separation/route-running archetype is one Harmon likes to bet on long-term.
- 2026-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-19]]): when Jayden Reed went down, Wicks was actually the Packers receiver who gave the best slot performance -- Harmon says he looked notably better as a move/slot player in 2025 than he did playing X-receiver in 2024 while Watson and Doubs were banged up.
- 2026-04-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-14]]): Wicks was traded from Green Bay to Philadelphia along with a one-year, roughly $12 million contract extension -- Harmon compares the move directly to what Green Bay did with [[Christian Watson]] last offseason, and likes Wicks as a buy-low target. He notes Wicks had a genuinely strong rookie year (72.2% success rate vs. man, sub-79% vs. zone, just under 73% vs. press -- strong marks for an outside receiver) but took a real step back in Year 2 (sub-71% vs. man, sub-69% vs. press, still sub-79% vs. zone) and again in Year 3, disputing any narrative that Wicks is an elite separator; he also dropped too many passes and struggled in contested catch situations. Harmon thought Wicks was better in a part-time role and frames him now as part of a Wicks/Hollywood Brown/[[Elijah Moore]] trio meant to replace [[DeVonta Smith]]'s WR2 production in the aggregate as Smith takes over as the true #1, with new Eagles playcaller ties to Green Bay (he coached Wicks there) seen as a scheme-fit plus.
- 2026-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-16]]): discussing Green Bay's WR3 picture, Harmon says he expects Wicks -- not Hollywood Brown or Elijah Moore -- to be the third receiver, calling himself a 'closet Dontayvion Wicks truther' who thinks Wicks could be a genuinely useful receiver if he can consistently catch the ball. Harmon pointed to Green Bay using Wicks on explosive outbreaking routes from the slot last year as evidence he can profile similarly to how Makai Lemon projects in Philadelphia.
- 2026-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-27]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris countered Jake Seeley's pick of [[Makai Lemon]] by nominating newly-signed Eagles WR Dontayvion Wicks (formerly Packers) as the more overlooked name -- he never cracked the top two or three receivers in Green Bay, but Harris called him a 'clear number three at worst' in the Eagles room who could be the real number two if [[DeVonta Smith]] gets hurt, praising his after-the-catch vision and route feel while both hosts flagged unreliable hands ('duck hands') as the real limiter. Both agreed the appeal is that Wicks is a known NFL commodity, unlike a rookie, so he could be a fast, low-cost pivot the moment Eagles usage clarifies.
- 2026-09-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-09-01]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: expects Wicks, not the injury-slowed [[Makai Lemon]], to open the season as the Eagles' WR2 across from [[DeVonta Smith]], simply because Lemon hasn't been healthy enough to get ramped up. Co-host James Koh (untracked) floats it as potentially 'Wicks season' in Philadelphia, though Harmon cautions that if Wicks drops passes early it could accelerate Lemon's takeover of the role. Both hosts think Eagles WR2 usage overall will be inconsistent regardless of which of the two is out there, with Smith dominating the target share.
- 2026-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-09-03]]): [[Dontayvion Wicks]] showed moments of real developing talent 'before he got sent off' (a reduced role), cited as another piece of an unsettled Packers receiver corps behind [[Jayden Reed]] and [[Christian Watson]] that the hosts think has a chance to break out if [[Jordan Love]] leans on the pass game more with [[Josh Jacobs]] out.
