# Jaylen Warren

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his name as "Jalen
> Warren"; normalized here to the real name of the Steelers running back,
> Jaylen Warren.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): named alongside [[Najee Harris]] as an intriguing piece of the backfield under incoming OC Arthur Smith's run-heavy, volume-funneling scheme, but explicitly tempered — "if you were just waiting for a full-on Jalen Warren revival, I don't think we're going to get that." No specific 2024 role projection given beyond that caution.
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman floats Jaylen Warren as a 'Tyler Allgeier mini' -- a smaller-scale change-of-pace comp -- with the caveat that if new OC Arthur Smith leans on a similar role to what Allgeier had in Atlanta, it could complicate touch shares for the whole Steelers backfield, [[Najee Harris]] included.
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): projects Warren for roughly 1,000 total yards and 6-7 touchdowns as the price gap to [[Najee Harris]] (RB22 vs. Warren's RB25) narrows, but still prefers Harris, expecting Arthur Smith's downhill run-game scheme to favor Harris's style and widen the usage gap again. Bob Harris noted he'd lean toward the higher-upside option (implicitly Warren) if he already had solid volume elsewhere on a roster, but at current prices sided with Harris regardless.
- 2024-11-14 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14 (Age Cliffs and Dented Cans)]] [Dynasty]: Warren fits his favourite waiver-wire archetype — the unsexy late-round or undrafted back with genuine institutional buy-in, alongside [[Kyren Williams]], [[Chase Brown]], [[Chuba Hubbard]], [[Tyler Allgeier]] and Jerome Ford. His point is that the market has forgotten Warren simply because [[Najee Harris]] is having his moment: 'Warren's the same guy he was when we liked him a year or two ago,' and failing to unseat a good player is not evidence against him. Harstad is clear he does not expect Warren to become a star — the standard at the position is being useful enough that a team wants to use you.
- 2024-12-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-23]]): Warren had a big game, out-carrying [[Najee Harris]] 12 to 9, out-gaining him 48 to 42 rushing yards, and catching five passes for 44 yards. Asked whether Warren is what [[Clyde Edwards-Helaire]] was supposed to be, Waldman said yes — Warren is quicker, faster, and, most importantly, actually has contact balance, which Edwards-Helaire never showed. He expects Warren to land a nice free-agent contract with a chance to be a committee back or even a lead back on a good passing-down team; a Football Guys staffer had floated Kansas City as the fit that would make him the Edwards-Helaire-role back. Waldman also used Edwards-Helaire as his cautionary tale about drafting the most expensive rookie — enshrined at RB6 overall in ADP before ever playing a snap, a scenario he likens to [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] in 2024.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]): asked whether Warren can carry a fantasy team to consistent RB production now that [[Najee Harris]] has left, Waldman says he feels it — largely for a backhanded reason: Pittsburgh's quarterback situation is unsettled and whoever plays is probably not going to be good, so the offense will have to check down constantly, which feeds Warren. Kenneth Gainwell, the free-agent addition, is not a load-carrier, so Warren has to be. The caveat is that Warren may not be as complete a back as fantasy players want, and Waldman floats a [[Tony Pollard]] comparison in both its good and bad senses — a different style of player who may not be a true every-down workhorse. He jokes the Steelers, as with letting [[James Conner]] go for Benny Snell, may again have gotten excited about the wrong back.
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]): reacting to Warren's new two-year extension with roughly 12 million guaranteed, Waldman is 'feeling it' on the contract but frames Warren as a back you invest in rather than the back — his comp is Devonta Freeman from about a decade ago, a player who could carry an every-down load for a season or two and even give you Pro Bowl-caliber work behind a good line, but whom the team would rather pair with a complement for two-down, goal-line and closeout work. He believes Pittsburgh hopes that complement is [[Kaleb Johnson]], and notes a one-two punch is not disqualifying for fantasy: Detroit, Minnesota, Miami and Pittsburgh itself have all made committee backfields work for fantasy GMs. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed. [Dynasty]
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Film & Theory Sequences, Signal, Steelers)]]): [[Jaylen Warren]] being named the starter is a function of what [[Aaron Rodgers]] needs rather than pure rushing ability — Warren can block, can make adjustments out of the backfield, and Waldman believes Rodgers trusts him enough to execute the line-of-scrimmage changes Rodgers will make regardless of whose scheme it nominally is. Waldman characterized the fit with the comp that Warren is 'more of a Joseph Addai than a Donald Brown,' i.e. the back a demanding veteran quarterback keeps on the field for protection and option-route reliability. Harstad agreed with the framing.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Going Deep Rookie Receivers and RB Depth Charts)]]): after Warren's contract extension, Waldman said we know what he is — capable of a Devonta Freeman-type campaign as the man if needed — and that with an older quarterback in [[Aaron Rodgers]] you want a back you can trust on third down and in known down-and-distance situations, so 'Warren is the guy this year' with [[Kaleb Johnson]] getting situational work. Waldman called him a winning pick for fantasy GMs. Angelo went further: Warren is in a workhorse-type role early, is one of the better pass protectors at the position in the NFL, and even at worst he expects something like a 60-40 split in his favor into 2026. Angelo also floated a future 1,000-yard season built as roughly 750 rushing and 250 receiving, with the Pittsburgh backfield working like a lesser [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]/[[David Montgomery]] tandem where both are RB2-equivalent producers.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Warren is the better and definitely the more effective back, significantly better in the second half, and he caught the second of the jerk-face touchdowns from [[Aaron Rodgers]]. The problem is that he was not the main man in the first quarter, losing early snaps four to seven to [[Kenneth Gainwell]], who also handled the two-minute drill. Harris presents this as a genuine committee rather than a Warren backfield.
