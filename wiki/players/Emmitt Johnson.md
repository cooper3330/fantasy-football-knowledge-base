# Emmitt Johnson

## Expert Takes

- 2026-05-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-04]]) [Dynasty]: agrees with Andy Reid's public comparison of Emmitt Johnson's change-of-direction and agility to LeSean McCoy, saying Reid coached McCoy for years and knows what he's talking about. Waldman highlights this as validation of a pre-draft argument he made: Johnson was knocked heavily for a slow 3-cone time that didn't match what the tape actually showed, and Reid's framing is a real-world example of that measurable-versus-film gap playing out.
- 2026-07-09 — According to James Koh (co-host, not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-09]]): he 'really likes' Johnson as a backup piece behind [[Kenneth Walker III]] and thinks he projects as a good fit for Kansas City's revamped run-game approach under new hire Eric Bieniemi.
- 2026-08-13 — According to James Koh (untracked co-host) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-13]]): Koh's deepest sleeper this cycle is Emmitt Johnson, going 185th overall on FantasyPros ECR ADP -- a hard-charging, maximum-effort runner Koh thinks a coaching staff that values that trait will gravitate toward. He'll be watching to see whether Johnson claims a bigger role in Kansas City than currently anticipated, noting in the same breath that Kenneth Walker III has not always been the model of health.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman had Johnson as his RB2 in this rookie class pre-draft, ahead of teammate-comp [[Nick Singleton]] as a smaller, quicker, more receiving-oriented back. Landing with the Chiefs alongside [[Kenneth Walker III]] means less time to develop quietly, but Waldman says it's hard to imagine Walker staying healthy all season, which could open a path. Waldman calls Johnson a quick learner with excellent footwork, better contact balance and quickness than his profile suggests, and a smart, creative runner who had a strong preseason game -- if teams look past his 40 time and actually watch the tape, Waldman expects Johnson to 'shine,' and if the backfield ahead of him sees a half-season or full-season injury, Johnson has the skill to flip the team's fortunes. [[Emari Demercado]], also in the room, is a fine journeyman/pass-catching complement but not the same long-term caliber.
