# Bijan Robinson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]) [Best Ball]: plans to be "pounding the table for Bijan Robinson this summer like I was for [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]" in 2024 — explicitly a market call, not a talent reversal. His reasoning: both are very good, but managers will now pay the premium for Gibbs and dial back on Robinson, so the value flips to Robinson. Notes Robinson finished 2023 as roughly RB11 *despite* a season of usage frustration, which he treats as the floor case. Waldman had argued against Robinson at his 2023 first-round price.
- 2023-12-18 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode: expects Robinson to be a **top-5 running back in 2024** and says he's "willing to make the mistake of going too high" on him, having been on the other side in 2023 ("I was one of those that was like, no, don't draft him in the first round"). His read is that Arthur Smith split the backfield deliberately on a team that knew it wasn't good — "why am I going to run this guy into the ground when we need to get better at quarterback" — rather than because of anything Robinson did.
- 2023-12-18 — Caveat both hosts raised, same episode: the 2024 outlook for both Robinson and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] depends on coaching. Arthur Smith's return to Atlanta was uncertain, and Waldman said he'd be in on Robinson either way.
- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]: names a specific usage mechanism behind the 2023 frustration, and it argues for volume being the fix. Robinson is "a rhythm runner who gets better with the more touches he gets," and Atlanta under Arthur Smith is his headline example of a coach running "scheme over personnel, not personnel over scheme" — a top-eight pick splitting work with Cordarrelle Patterson. His framing is that this is a coach problem that "is going to bite you," not a player problem. See [[Player Development and Coachability]] and [[Scheme vs Talent]].

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: reaffirms the workload thesis now that Arthur Smith is confirmed gone from Atlanta — expects Robinson to be "run into the ground" (in the positive, bell-cow sense) by a new coaching staff, "with a dash of [[Tyler Allgeier]]" still mixed in as a complementary piece rather than a true committee split.
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): the first concrete scheme read since Atlanta's new hire — new OC Zach Robinson (from the Rams) is expected to bring a man-heavy, downhill gap-run scheme, which Harmon calls a good mechanical fit for Robinson specifically, translating the "well set up" framing usually applied to Kyren Williams's role in the same Rams system. Expects [[Tyler Allgeier]] to remain involved as a complementary piece rather than a full committee cut into Robinson's share.

- 2024-02-29 — According to **Kevin Knight** (Falcons reporter, SB Nation's Falcoholic — guest, not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]): says Atlanta's 2023 running game got worse, not better, after drafting Robinson to be a workhorse, and pins it on play-calling rather than the player, citing a specific game where the staff abandoned an early outside-zone run that had worked. Calls the offense "really, really close" with Bijan, [[Drake London]] and [[Kyle Pitts]] already in place if Atlanta fixes quarterback and receiver depth.
