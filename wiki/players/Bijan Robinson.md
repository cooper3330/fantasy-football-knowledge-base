# Bijan Robinson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]) [Best Ball]: plans to be "pounding the table for Bijan Robinson this summer like I was for [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]" in 2024 — explicitly a market call, not a talent reversal. His reasoning: both are very good, but managers will now pay the premium for Gibbs and dial back on Robinson, so the value flips to Robinson. Notes Robinson finished 2023 as roughly RB11 *despite* a season of usage frustration, which he treats as the floor case. Waldman had argued against Robinson at his 2023 first-round price.
- 2023-12-18 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode: expects Robinson to be a **top-5 running back in 2024** and says he's "willing to make the mistake of going too high" on him, having been on the other side in 2023 ("I was one of those that was like, no, don't draft him in the first round"). His read is that Arthur Smith split the backfield deliberately on a team that knew it wasn't good — "why am I going to run this guy into the ground when we need to get better at quarterback" — rather than because of anything Robinson did.
- 2023-12-18 — Caveat both hosts raised, same episode: the 2024 outlook for both Robinson and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] depends on coaching. Arthur Smith's return to Atlanta was uncertain, and Waldman said he'd be in on Robinson either way.
- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]: names a specific usage mechanism behind the 2023 frustration, and it argues for volume being the fix. Robinson is "a rhythm runner who gets better with the more touches he gets," and Atlanta under Arthur Smith is his headline example of a coach running "scheme over personnel, not personnel over scheme" — a top-eight pick splitting work with Cordarrelle Patterson. His framing is that this is a coach problem that "is going to bite you," not a player problem. See [[Player Development and Coachability]] and [[Scheme vs Talent]].

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: reaffirms the workload thesis now that Arthur Smith is confirmed gone from Atlanta — expects Robinson to be "run into the ground" (in the positive, bell-cow sense) by a new coaching staff, "with a dash of [[Tyler Allgeier]]" still mixed in as a complementary piece rather than a true committee split.
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]): the first concrete scheme read since Atlanta's new hire — new OC Zach Robinson (from the Rams) is expected to bring a man-heavy, downhill gap-run scheme, which Harmon calls a good mechanical fit for Robinson specifically, translating the "well set up" framing usually applied to Kyren Williams's role in the same Rams system. Expects [[Tyler Allgeier]] to remain involved as a complementary piece rather than a full committee cut into Robinson's share.

- 2024-02-29 — According to **Kevin Knight** (Falcons reporter, SB Nation's Falcoholic — guest, not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]): says Atlanta's 2023 running game got worse, not better, after drafting Robinson to be a workhorse, and pins it on play-calling rather than the player, citing a specific game where the staff abandoned an early outside-zone run that had worked. Calls the offense "really, really close" with Bijan, [[Drake London]] and [[Kyle Pitts]] already in place if Atlanta fixes quarterback and receiver depth.


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: calls him a "future All-Pro" while breaking down why the finalized [[Kirk Cousins]] signing helps the whole Atlanta offense, including the run game Robinson anchors.
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): projects Bijan for roughly 314 touches, 1,725 yards from scrimmage and 11 touchdowns, calling that 'easily' top-five running back production. He also flagged new Falcons OC Zac (B.) Robinson, formerly of the McVay/Rams tree, as explicitly targeting McCaffrey/Kyren Williams-style usage (Waldman noted Williams got 25 opportunities/game down the stretch last year on that same scheme tree), which is the structural reason for his bullish touch projection.
- 2024-06-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-20]]), citing Fantasy Points data: under Arthur Smith's zone-heavy scheme, Robinson ran 67.8% of his carries on zone concepts last year (40 of ~145 attempts by Harmon's read) versus 30.4% on man/gap runs, but was notably more efficient on the man/gap runs — 5.15 yards per carry and a 47.7% success rate. Harmon flags this as a reason to watch how new OC Zach Robinson (from the McVay/Rams tree, which ran more man/gap duo concepts last year in LA) balances the run scheme, suggesting Bijan could see big plays on man/gap runs even though the offense's identity is still expected to lean zone/outside stretch to set up London and Mooney downfield shots.
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman says Robinson 'easily' has RB1-overall upside and should have a 'LaDainian Tomlinson-like career,' expecting Atlanta's new offense to 'take off the governor' that limited him as a rookie under Arthur Smith's committee approach -- Tyler Allgeier will still get some work, but not the heavy split seen last year. He explicitly comps this to Sean McVay's Rams-tree history of feature-back workloads (Todd Gurley, Cam Akers, Kyren Williams), since Atlanta's new offensive staff comes from that lineage. Robinson is grouped among the top three RB1-overall candidates alongside [[Breece Hall]] and ahead of [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and [[Jonathan Taylor]] in this discussion.
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Discussing whether Bijan Robinson will be on the 'McCaffrey plan' in Atlanta, Waldman projected him as a top-five running back in touches -- comparable to [[Christian McCaffrey]]'s league-leading workload -- while doubting Robinson leads the league outright. He still ranks McCaffrey as the locked-in overall RB1 but names Robinson, alongside [[Breece Hall]] (and mentions [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] and [[Jonathan Taylor]] as other candidates), as one of a handful of backs with the workload path to push McCaffrey off that top spot. Bob Harris (untracked) agreed with the order McCaffrey, Hall, then Robinson, saying he has no argument with anyone who ranks Bijan ahead of Hall.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): Waldman said taking Robinson over [[Breece Hall]] is perfectly reasonable — he leans Hall but calls the two very close and would not be surprised if either finished as the overall RB1 ahead of [[Christian McCaffrey]]. On the committee worry, Waldman dismissed the reporting that [[Tyler Allgeier]] will get a significant role as mostly a pat on the back to keep Allgeier ready in case Robinson gets hurt, arguing it is not how this Shanahan-tree offense has historically distributed work.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): Waldman ranks Robinson just behind [[Breece Hall]] but calls him the safer of the two. The doubt is workload — in June, Robinson, Zac Robinson and Raheem Morris all talked about the full [[Christian McCaffrey]] treatment, and they are saying it less now, with reports of a possible 50-50 split with [[Tyler Allgeier]]. Waldman thinks the split talk is overplayed and projects Allgeier at roughly 10-11 touches per game with Robinson at 16-17, which he concedes may not be enough for the overall RB1-2 finish the market expects. Offsetting that: Atlanta's offensive line is younger and better than the Jets' and has been the strength of the team for three years, the defense has improved, and they play in a weaker division — all of which keeps opportunity stable. He calls the Hall-vs-Bijan debate close to a false narrative and says it depends which flavor of risk you want.
- 2024-09-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-12]]): Robinson opened with 18 carries for 68 yards, a 3.8 yards-per-carry average that co-host James Koh (not tracked) called disappointing on top of an already disappointing team performance. 72% of his carries were zone-concept runs and Atlanta led the NFL in Week 1 in outside run rate at roughly 81-82% of carries outside the tackles. Harmon's structural objection is that outside zone and a league-leading pistol rate do not belong together — Atlanta is trying to blend [[Zac Robinson]]'s McVay outside-zone concepts with a pistol look adopted to keep [[Kirk Cousins]] out from under center, and he argues they have to commit to one or the other. The volume is intact; the blocking scheme coherence is the concern.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]): an unprompted aside — 'I keep forgetting how good he can be, but then you see that balance he has at contact and you go, oh yeah, that's why we all like' him. Against Seattle, Waldman noted the defense over-pursued and got early penetration but had no backside support, which let Robinson 'put on a show' with the quick movements and agility to access cutback lanes. Framed as a reminder of his talent rather than a change of view.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-19 (NFL QB Lunacy)]]): brief mention inside the [[Michael Penix Jr.]] scouting report — expect a lot of designed scheme work to Robinson and Ray-Ray McCloud with the rookie under center, meaning RPOs, screens and throwouts. Waldman also notes Atlanta can still run the football, which is part of why opposing defenses will stay in base cover 3 / cover 1 looks rather than pressuring Penix constantly.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): given the choice between Robinson at third overall and [[Saquon Barkley]] at second, Waldman takes Robinson — while conceding Barkley still has the higher upside of the two. The reasoning is workload security, not talent: Robinson is an integral part of the Atlanta offense in both phases, and the Falcons are unlikely to run away with games the way Philadelphia can, so the volume holds. Bob Harris (not tracked) supplies the usage evidence — from week six on, Robinson averaged 23 touches, 120.9 yards and 22.7 fantasy points per game, the highest per-game average of any back over that span — and both note this resolves the exact usage worry they voiced a year earlier about Raheem Morris and Zac Robinson's deployment. The stated caution is to not repeat that mis-draft, and to not be talked off him by nice noises about Tyler Allgeier that the staff does not believe either.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): production a little higher than last year. [[Matt Waldman]] disagreed and called him neutral, reasoning that Atlanta will import more of the University of Washington offensive concepts and that Robinson's job in that system is heavily to set up play action for a downfield passing attack rather than to expand his own receiving role [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): Harris rates Bijan a 3 out of 10 on risk at ADP 2, calling him pretty safe and shrugging at the offensive line worry because 'I just don't think we're great at knowing what an offensive line even is'. Guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) rates him a 4, citing Atlanta being down two tackles with Kaleb McGary hurt and the swing tackle also injured, plus his belief that [[Tyler Allgeier]] plays more than the market wants to acknowledge, and the opportunity cost of the players you pass at pick two. Harris's counter is that every running back except about three of them has a backup who plays a lot, so the committee tax is not Bijan-specific.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon expects Robinson to catch a lot of passes in 2025, explicitly comparing the role to how Chase Brown became one of the most involved receivers for [[Joe Burrow]] from midseason on. The driver is that [[Michael Penix Jr.]] is not a scrambler and reads the checkdown out quickly, so the back becomes a volume outlet in what Harmon projects as a pass-heavy Atlanta script. Harmon also plugged his newly published Reception Perception profile on Robinson, saying readers will be pleasantly surprised and that 'this guy is the truth'.
- 2025-09-05 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-05]], relayed alongside [[Matt Harmon]]: after finishing his Reception Perception charting of Robinson, Koh said he is the best running back in the NFL, hands down, with no question in his mind, calling his 2024 absolutely insane. He allowed that you can make the case for [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], who had a really great season, but did not rate it above Robinson. Koh noted that the site's running back profiles include pass-blocking success rates, which he argued matter for fantasy because pass protection keeps a back on the field, alongside charting on [[Cam Skattebo]] and [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] and full examinations of the Jets, Jaguars and Giants backfields.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): the game started spectacularly, with Robinson split wide on the Falcons' first play for a slant and a nice gain, then a check-down screen on the third play that he took 50 yards for a touchdown. The other 16 touches went for 63 yards. Harris blames Atlanta rather than Robinson, citing bad play calling and frustrating side-to-side usage near the Tampa goal line, and says with better red zone calls Robinson gets at least one more touchdown; he also notes Tampa's run defense again looks good. His frustration is with Atlanta's over-cleverness in splitting Robinson wide with [[Tyler Allgeier]] in the backfield when he would simply rather Robinson be the running back, and he says that cleverness is part of what cost Atlanta the game.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest Jake Trowbridge (not a tracked expert) said Robinson was his 1.01 redraft pick before the season '1,000%' and would still be his 1.01 if redrafting today, having 'been really, really good' through two weeks. Harris had Robinson second behind [[Saquon Barkley]] preseason, with his only reason for the order being worry about Barkley's touch count after last season — a call Harris said looks a little foolish right now while defending it as reasonable. Trowbridge made Robinson the most expensive piece of his DraftKings week 3 lineup at $7,900 against Carolina, whose defense he said has not fixed a damn thing; he also considered a Robinson plus [[Jonathan Taylor]] double-up before deciding he could not make the rest of the salary work.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-29]]): asked to choose Bijan or [[Christian McCaffrey]], Waldman says you are fine either way but splits by format — for [[Redraft (Standard)]] he still gives McCaffrey a slight edge, while from a [[Dynasty]] perspective 'you take Bijan Robinson all day,' since Bijan has at least four or five more years than McCaffrey. He says Bijan is not far behind right now anyway: excellent on cuts, excellent as a receiver, and Atlanta's best offensive design this year has been finding him in wide open space downfield, with three or four long receiving gains where he was hit in stride and worked after the catch. Bob Harris (not tracked) says on the eye test Bijan looks the more dangerous runner right now, while conceding McCaffrey is still more utilized as a receiver even though Bijan is more than capable there.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): Robinson is the first overall pick with 'zero question' if a redraft could be held at this moment. Against Washington he broke a 70-yard wheel route when Bobby Wagner lost contain — he should have been gone but got run down from behind — and added a sick red zone touchdown carry in the second quarter, plus a red zone fumble on the first series that [[Michael Penix Jr.]] recovered. Harris's point is that the case is not production-driven and not garbage time: the Commanders were in the game throughout, and it is the way Robinson looks and the room he gets that sells it. He is also still ceding time to [[Tyler Allgeier]], who came in late third quarter when Robinson was tired and scored.
- 2025-10-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-14 (Week 7 Waiver Wire & More W6 Film)]]): Robinson opened the Falcons' upset of the Bills with an early fumble, then piled up 238 scrimmage yards and a touchdown, capped by an 81-yard TD run where he broke a leg tackle and outran the defense -- Harris compared the burst to peak [[Saquon Barkley]]. Harris declares Robinson 'not really debatable' as the number one player in fantasy right now.
- 2025-10-17 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-17]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Discussing Atlanta as a 'does this new-model wagon really work' tier team given quarterback uncertainty, Harris says there's no jury out on Bijan -- he and [[Drake London]] are, by any reckoning, top-five rest-of-season players regardless of who's throwing the ball. He cites Bijan touching the ball roughly seven times on one opening drive, getting tired, and handing off to [[Tyler Allgeier]] for an easy 25-yard score as evidence of a bell-cow role secure enough to ignore the quarterback question entirely.
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-27]]) [Redraft]: Waldman called Atlanta's offense overrated after [[Bijan Robinson]] managed just 48 yards on 13 carries against a bad Miami run defense, but pinned the blame on the offensive line and tight end play rather than Robinson himself -- Robinson missed a blitz pickup by choosing the wrong rusher, and the line was 'leaky' overall, getting him stuffed on multiple runs. He disagreed with the idea that [[Kirk Cousins]] was to blame, noting Cousins had 'no chance' behind that protection. Waldman noted Atlanta was also missing Drake London and starter Michael Penix Jr., compounding the offensive issues.
- 2025-10-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-28]]): against a Dolphins defense that was statistically among the worst run defenses in the league heading into the week, Robinson managed just 12 touches for 48 yards and lost a fumble on a hard hit deep in Dolphins territory after a Darnell Mooney pass-interference gain had set up the drive. Atlanta had five first downs total through three quarters and only 3 points until a garbage-time Tyler Allgeier score. Harris notes the Falcons were without Michael Penix and Drake London and that Kirk Cousins' play calling stayed 'appropriately stubborn' on the run out of disbelief it wasn't working, but the offensive line simply could not block Miami.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): Waldman is projecting Robinson to do 'the Roger Craig dance' — 1,000 yards rushing and receiving. He said the rushing half is the bigger challenge because of the split with [[Tyler Allgeier]], while the receiving half is safer because Robinson is a fixture in the passing offense and is used in a variety of ways Waldman likes. He also praised a circus catch in this game and said Robinson might have the best hands of the three Atlanta pass catchers he had just discussed, ahead of [[Drake London]] and [[Kyle Pitts]].
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): asked what is wrong with Robinson, Harris answers 'I don't think anything'. Atlanta kept feeding him even while trailing in the fourth quarter, and New England is simply one of the hardest teams in the league to run against. Robinson still finished with 20 touches and 96 scrimmage yards, added twin one-handed catches in the fourth quarter and a screen he took to the one before a jerk-face quarterback sneak touchdown. The one usage flag: at the goal line Atlanta went exclusively to [[Drake London]] rather than Robinson. Harris's bottom line — 'we're not doing anything cute with Bijan'.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): Robinson was 'okay' — 88 scrimmage yards, opening with a 16-yard carry into space, 'consistently explosive.' Harris then answers the Yacht Club chat's goal-line panic with the actual counts he looked up: Sunday's red zone snaps were 5 [[Tyler Allgeier]] to 4 Robinson with three of those together, and inside the five it was Allgeier 2, Robinson 1, again together on Robinson's. Over the last month the red zone snap split is 23-13 in Robinson's favour, and inside the five the Falcons have barely been there at all — three Robinson snaps in four games. The only two Falcons running back touches inside the five in the last month were both Allgeier's, both Sunday. Harris: 'the talk that Allgeier has been taking all the goal line work, it's actually not true' — there has been no goal-line work. You are allowed to be worried that both scored, but it is not an established trend.
- 2025-11-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-11-18]]): Robinson was unstoppable early against Carolina -- breaking two tackles for an early touchdown and later bashing in from close range for a second score -- but the Falcons faded badly in the second half, where Robinson managed just six carries for seven yards as Carolina controlled the ball. Harris notes Tyler Allgeier still got run near the goal line on at least one series and scored, showing the touchdown work isn't fully exclusive to Robinson even in a good game for him.
- 2025-11-24 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Robinson provided the explosive plays that kept Atlanta's win over New Orleans alive - a long screen carried down inside the 10, a bounced outside run into field-goal range, and a fourth-quarter screen where he shot through two defenders and nearly took it to the house. Harris continues to view him as Atlanta's clear offensive engine regardless of the passing-game struggles around him.
- 2025-12-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Robinson had a big game against the Jets, with a huge gain on a screen that drew a roughing penalty on Kirk Cousins' defender, then bounced outside for a touchdown to put Atlanta up 14-7, and later broke a tackle on another screen to help set up a score. He was stuffed on a goal-to-go carry from the one before halftime, with [[Tyler Allgeier]] scoring instead.
- 2025-12-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-08]]): Robinson piled up yards in the first half against Seattle but fumbled it away in the red zone on a third-quarter drive that Harris called 'a crusher' with the Falcons down seven. He got only three more carries the rest of the game and none at all in the fourth quarter, playing just five snaps in that final period as Atlanta pulled back on his workload. Harris said the drop-off made [[Tyler Allgeier]] look like a bigger threat than he actually was - Atlanta had essentially 'decided to pull the parachute' on Robinson rather than Allgeier earning real volume.
- 2025-12-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-12]]): Robinson fumbled deep in his own territory against Tampa Bay but was otherwise excellent. In the annual next-year top-12 exercise, Harris made Robinson his 1.01 for 2026 over [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], citing three straight years of RB1-caliber finishes (RB10 pace this year, RB4 last year, RB5 the year before), durability, 64 catches already this season on pace for 80+, and a good offensive line. Harris said touchdown scarcity (with Tyler Allgeier as red-zone competition) has been the only real knock, and that could ease if Allgeier isn't retained. Cousin Josh (untracked co-host), by contrast, put Robinson at 1.02 behind Gibbs, calling him 'slightly less heavy Saquon Barkley' -- someone defenders can't touch because he makes them miss, versus Gibbs, whom Josh compared to a Chris Johnson/Jamal Charles hybrid who is untouchable in a different way. Both hosts agreed no other player they discussed could realistically unseat the two of them from the top spots. [Dynasty] [Best Ball]
- 2026-01-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris flagged Bijan as an unusual goal-line outlier -- only 5 carries from inside the 3-yard line all season, scoring on just one -- but says he isn't worried about it, attributing it to Atlanta's poor red-zone environment and offensive line rather than any limitation in Bijan himself. He and guest Jim McCormick (untracked) note the Falcons instead leaned on backup [[Tyler Allgeier]] for several goal-line looks, calling that kind of role split volatile and not 'sticky' year to year -- Harris says he doesn't think there's anything about Bijan that prevents him scoring from the two, and a coaching/scheme change in Atlanta next year could fix the split entirely. Separately, discussing [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]'s second-half collapse, Harris said he now might prefer Bijan over Gibbs because Bijan keeps producing even when stuffed at the line, whereas Gibbs seemed more dependent on space.

He'd be 'a little surprised' if [[Tyler Allgeier]] carves out a Kareem Hunt-level role, and said if that did happen it would represent 'a blind spot,' though he doesn't expect the team to actually build it that way. He is more concerned about Atlanta leaning into two-tight-end personnel under Stefanski, which could cut into target volume for the wide receiver room rather than Robinson's touches.
