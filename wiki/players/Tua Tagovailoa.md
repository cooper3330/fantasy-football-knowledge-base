# Tua Tagovailoa

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: agreement that he can't get Miami to the Super Bowl as currently built, though both stop well short of calling him bad. Harms's case: he's a genuine anticipation-and-timing thrower who can hit tight windows between the numbers when the scheme (speed everywhere, heavy play-action) creates them for him, but he's not a strong post-snap reader of coverage and can be fooled — take away the scheme-manufactured windows and force him to go through progressions or throw outside the numbers, and he's unreliable. Structural problem layered on top: as a cold-weather-disadvantaged offense in the AFC East, Miami has to travel to Buffalo, New York and New England every year on the way to a title. Waldman agrees the defense needs to improve for Miami to get further, but both land on "good football player, not an elite quarterback" and don't see him as the long-term answer to get Miami to February.
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the sharpest negative take on Tua yet — "straight up bad," not just "bad for him," in a Wild Card loss at Kansas City (20/39, 199 yards, 0 TD, 1 INT in extreme cold). Cites Derrick Klassen's Reception Perception charting: Tua posted the 5th-lowest success-rate-against-pressure figure among 2022 starters (alongside Kyler Murray, [[Jared Goff]], Aaron Rodgers, [[Lamar Jackson]], Jalen Hurts) — but unlike the mobile passers on that list, he has no scrambling ability to bail him out. Diagnosis: not a plus athlete, no answer for second-reaction situations once the play breaks down, and a weaker arm than Jared Goff's. Harmon pegs his ceiling "just a touch under" Goff's — outside a top-12-to-15 quarterback conversation. See [[Reception Perception Methodology]].
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): a more optimistic redraft-relevant read than Harmon's January critique — reframes 2023 (4,624 yards, 29 TD, 69% completion, QB9) as not yet his statistical peak, expecting his next two-to-three seasons to land in low-end fantasy QB1 range as he continues developing. Diagnoses the market's skepticism as an archetype-recognition problem rather than a talent problem: evaluators discount "limited" throwers who lack elite arm strength or mobility, the same dynamic Waldman says has kept [[Kirk Cousins]] and [[Jared Goff]] perennially undervalued.

- 2024-04-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-15]]: no concerns about him as Miami's franchise quarterback going forward despite no new contract yet — credits how he took care of his body to stay upright and healthy through 2023, and reads the working relationship with HC Mike McDaniel as a positive signal even without a formal extension announced.
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Tua is grouped with Brock Purdy as an undervalued late 'pocket passer' pairing against CJ Stroud, citing Tua's strong supporting cast and offensive environment. Waldman's caveat: watch Miami's receiver depth chart -- if Jonnu Smith ends up leading the team in receiving, 'some shit has really gone wrong,' signaling real trouble for Tua's passing efficiency, since Smith is more of a nice dart-throw than a lead option.
- 2024-11-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-26]]): Tua played great against New England on re-watch, with 'a lot of guts,' real control of the offense and balls fired downfield, which is what drove the [[Jaylen Waddle]] explosion. The disagreement is about durability of that: co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) counters that New England is not good, that Miami is a strong front-runner but has a poor record against winning teams and especially Buffalo, and that the offense 'lacks juice' when it needs first downs. Both flag the Thanksgiving night game in Green Bay as a problem — Koh notes Tua has more interceptions than touchdowns in cold weather and has never won a sub-40-degree game, with kickoff temperatures possibly in the 20s. Tua said he is ready to beat those allegations.
- 2024-12-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-17]]): this was probably the worst game he has seen Tua play under Mike McDaniel, especially downfield — 3-of-10 on throws over 10 air yards per NextGen, with all three interceptions coming on those attempts. Harmon rejects the framing that Tua doesn't need a good offensive line because he gets the ball out fast: that worked for Peyton Manning because Manning was an elite processor and thrower, and Tua, while a good processor within this specific system, is not that physical specimen and cannot consistently work off platform. Harmon notes Tua throws to his first read at the fourth-highest rate among quarterbacks with 200+ dropbacks (fantasy points data), a list otherwise populated by [[Trevor Lawrence]], [[Anthony Richardson]] and [[Will Levis]]. He splits blame: the line was set up to fail, but Tua is what he is, and the system now needs to evolve because evolving the player this late is harder.
- 2025-01-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-06]]): Waldman's criticism of Mike McDaniel is that he only trusts Tua — he argues McDaniel has made an emotional as well as professional investment in him, and that whenever [[Skyler Thompson]] or Tyler Huntley played, the offense was dumbed down and over-scripted to a conservative, predictable degree rather than letting a backup make mistakes and play aggressively. Waldman has thought Thompson could become a competent NFL quarterback and calls the reaction an overreaction, though he still rates McDaniel a good coach with an innovative offensive mind that fantasy managers benefit from. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) framed it as McDaniel being too emotionally invested in one guy, and argued that with Tua's concussion history creating real fragility, Miami should be putting much more intense effort into plan B — which he considers partly on Grier and partly on a league-wide shortage of viable backups.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'fuck it' on Tagovailoa at Underdog QB20 — an aging [[Tyreek Hill]] who could fall off the cliff, an offense that has been found out at times in the passing game, and a situation that still feels in flux. Untracked co-host Bob Harris, who describes himself as injury-agnostic, said the bigger issue is that Tagovailoa is one big hit away from an extended absence and that at some point the league or his family may push him to stop. Harris also relayed reporting from Chris Perkins of the Sun Sentinel that Mike McDaniel is on the hot seat and needs to go deep into the playoffs and get more creative, with Bobby Slowik added as passing game coordinator; Harris was unsure that helps much. Waldman also criticized Brian Flores for how he treated Tagovailoa.
- 2025-05-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Tagovailoa at QB22 is a price Waldman is happy to pay — he thinks Tua should be valued around QB15 to QB17 at worst, in the same range as [[Dak Prescott]] and [[Brock Purdy]]. He names the drags on the price: the injury tag, [[Tyreek Hill]]'s offseason instability, [[Jaylen Waddle]]'s odd drop issues, and the perception that [[Jonnu Smith]]'s TE4 season was flukish. Waldman calls the market's position cognitive dissonance — the same drafters taking [[De'Von Achane]] at RB7 are assuming the rest of the Miami offense collapses. 'I guess they feel like the witch is dead there. I think that is 100% true, and I think there is value to be had. I like things that are cheap, so feeling it all day long.'
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): asked whether Mike McDaniel can engineer the turnaround Miami needs, Waldman said yes in theory given the skill personnel, but flagged two limiters — the offensive line, and Tagovailoa's ability to throw with velocity in certain ranges of the field. His core concern is that when the offense gets forced into a corner, Tagovailoa cannot throw his way out of it the way top quarterbacks can, and if that proves decisive the offense falls apart again or is hindered enough that fantasy managers will have over-invested in Miami's pass catchers. Waldman half-joked that Miami may need to go back from 'South Beach McDaniel' to 'Nerdy McDaniel'. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) noted local media talk about a culture problem and an impatient ownership situation.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): the worst performance by any quarterback in Week 1 — worse than [[Drake Maye]] or [[Bo Nix]], both of whom he also called bad. First drive, an interception sailed infinitely high; second drive, an unaccounted-for blitzer he did not have to fumble against; third drive, another near-pick; third quarter, an interception thrown into an invisible dropping linebacker right in front of his face. Harris says you cannot even tell whether the offensive line or Mike McDaniel's play-calling was at fault because Tua was so bad, and that the only good news is he was not concussed. He also eats his own take from the previous week, when he contrarily predicted Miami would be okay: 'not great commentary there, my friend.' He allows the Colts defense might turn out terrific.
- 2025-09-19 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-19]]): Harris does not want Tua outside of superflex. Miami's game plan at Buffalo was massaged 'within an inch of its life' — 10-play and 16-play scoring drives and two nine-play drives in the fourth quarter, explicitly to keep the ball away from [[Josh Allen]] — and Tua reached 100 passing yards only on his 26th attempt. Harris gives credit where due: three third-and-long conversions before halftime including a good read and throw against a corner blitz to [[Tyreek Hill]], and nine attempts of 10-plus air yards versus three for Allen. But the game ended on a 'horrendous back-breaking interception' from the Bills' 21 where he predetermined the throw to [[Jaylen Waddle]]. Harris's overall verdict: the 2023 offense is off the table, but the environment still supports fantasy weapons.
- 2025-10-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-21]]): Tua Tagovailoa went 12-of-23 for 100 yards and 3 interceptions in Miami's 31-6 blowout loss to Cleveland and was benched for Quinn Ewers. Harmon pushed back on the take that Mike McDaniel has 'done nothing wrong' because the scheme is still good, arguing McDaniel bet his whole offense and job on 'saving' Tua (FaceTiming him at hiring to say 'my job is to make you look good'), and that ownership (Chris Grier, Stephen Ross) never wanted to be proven wrong about drafting Tua over Justin Herbert. Harmon said the team has fallen apart culturally, the quarterback is unhappy and playing poorly, and speculated Tua's concussion history may be limiting how aggressively McDaniel can scheme him. He called it a 'castle built on sand' that was always going to collapse without offensive evolution.
- 2025-10-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-21 (Week 8 Waivers & W7 Reviews)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Tua threw three interceptions for the second straight week amid an ugly overall Dolphins performance (penalties, a fumbled shotgun snap, a false start), and was eventually benched for [[Quinn Ewers]] (who himself nearly threw a pick on his first attempt, dropped by the defender). Harris said he was 'not in the tank for Tua to start this year' and, without [[Tyreek Hill]] and with the offense's confidence visibly shaken, said either a coaching change (Mike McDaniel) or a QB benching 'could be in the offing soon,' though as of recording neither had happened.
- 2025-10-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-28]]): Tua entered Week 8 off a 100-yard, 3-interception game that saw him essentially benched for Quinn Ewers, with real postgame questions about whether he'd play again this season. He played through a swollen/possibly infected left eye against Atlanta and, per Harmon, 'played better with one eye than he did the whole course of the season with two eyes.' Harmon does not expect Tua, Tyreek Hill or Miami's current coaching staff back next year and views the franchise as headed for a full rebuild -- this game doesn't change that outlook even though the offense looked much better with a simplified, condensed-formation, motion-heavy scheme.
- 2025-12-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Tua did not play well in the Dolphins' 21-17 win over the Saints, with multiple awful throws that killed drives, an interception on a deep shot to Jalen Waddle, and a late-game overthrow that cost [[Darren Waller]] a wide-open touchdown. Harris said Tua has been 'so bad the last month and a half' that he's now behind Marcus Mariota, Tyrod Taylor and Trey Lance as a streaming quarterback option.
- 2025-12-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-16]]): [[Tua Tagovailoa]] threw a crusher red-zone interception to open Monday's loss to Pittsburgh, and Harris cited an ESPN stat that all 15 of Tua's interceptions this season have come against zone defense. Harris said Tua's limitations aren't new -- he's never had a big arm and has always needed a Tyreek-Hill-type speed threat a defense must respect with extra bodies to make the offense function, and Miami's post-Hill reinvention 'has not been flattering to Tua.'
- 2025-12-18 — According to Matt Harmon and James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-12-18]]): Tua Tagovailoa has been benched in favor of [[Quinn Ewers]], and both hosts agree it looks essentially over between Tua and Mike McDaniel in Miami. Koh called Tua 'an unplayable asset at this point,' pointing to the Monday night loss at Pittsburgh in the cold where the box score (fine completion numbers, decent passer rating) masked a genuinely bad performance -- bad sacks and an outrageous interception. Harmon noted the benching mirrors Russell Wilson in Denver and Derek Carr with the Raiders: teams sitting a struggling veteran before games trigger contract guarantees they'd otherwise be stuck with. Both hosts framed this within a broader league trend (citing Hayden Winks of Underdog) that paying a mid quarterback like a top-five quarterback to justify a prior investment -- 'the ego extension' -- is as costly a mistake as drafting the wrong quarterback, and that most owners lack the stomach to walk away from 'quarterback purgatory.' Harmon speculated Tua could still be a decent player two years from now in the right system, but flagged the concussion history as a real long-term concern.
- 2026-02-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-17]]): despite Miami's fire sale of other veterans (Bradley Chubb, Tyreek Hill, Nick Westbrook-Ikhine, James Daniels), Harmon thinks Miami holds onto Tua Tagovailoa rather than eat a $99M dead-cap hit this year -- that number drops to $31.8M next offseason, so he expects Tua to stick as a bridge/backup rather than be cut, even though Tua reportedly said a fresh start elsewhere 'would be dope.' He floated that a cap-rich but notoriously frugal franchise like Tennessee (~$104M in space) could absorb Tua's $56M cap hit for as little as a 2nd-round pick, but doesn't expect a real trade market to develop given the contract size.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-05]]): Tagovailoa is widely expected to be cut by Miami despite a $100M cap hit and $54M in guaranteed 2026 money -- more real dollars than Kyler Murray was owed. Harris says concussions have shortened his game plan and it's genuinely uncertain whether he can still play the position at an NFL starter level, raising the possibility he has to settle for a backup role. He floats the dark-comedy scenario of Arizona and Miami essentially swapping Murray for Tua since it's the only path where both teams' guaranteed money gets offset.
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-12]]): Tua signed with Atlanta for close to the veteran's minimum after Miami released him (paying him over $50 million in actual cash to go away). He'll be paired with new head coach Kevin Stefanski while Michael Penix Jr. races to return from a torn ACL. Harris reiterates a take from the prior week's show that Tua is 'a degraded version of the pre-concussions guy' and doesn't expect that to change -- he'll either be a check-down passer who physically shouldn't be taking hits to make downfield plays, or he'll be aggressive again and risk another concussion. Atlanta has weapons and Tua may well be the Week 1 starter, but Harris is unsure fans should actually want to see that play out.
- 2026-07-07 — According to untracked guest Jim McCormick ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: McCormick says, to Harris's surprise, that he actually prefers a Tua Tagovailoa-led Falcons offense over a [[Michael Penix Jr.]]-led one, because Tua's accuracy and predictability -- despite acknowledging Tua's brutal final stretch in Miami -- would make [[Kyle Pitts]] and [[Drake London]] more consistent producers than a bigger-armed but less reliable Penix. Harris pushes back, citing his own history 'in the tank for Tua' years ago and expressing surprise at McCormick's framing, but both agree the choice mainly reshuffles which Falcons skill players you'd emphasize rather than changing the team's overall outlook.
