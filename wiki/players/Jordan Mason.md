# Jordan Mason

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Dynasty]: prefers Mason over [[Elijah Mitchell]] as the answer if [[Christian McCaffrey]] falters, even though Mitchell got more of the workload down the stretch of 2023 (a decision driven by an early-week report that Mitchell wasn't practicing, which turned out to be inaccurate). Waldman's reasoning is versatility and durability with a physical running style, plus price — Mitchell will cost more to roster, so he'd rather spend on Mason.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: asked whether drafting a 49ers reserve back is a waste of time, Waldman totally felt it. One of them will have value; the problem is duration, because Kyle Shanahan is a perfectionist who will bench a back over one missed hole and go find another. Mason is 'the guy for now' that everyone is recommending, but if he fumbles or hits the wrong hole a couple of times, [[Isaac Guerendo]] comes in — and after that Shanahan will look at the practice squad or the waiver wire. Waldman's advice: if you do not have [[Christian McCaffrey]], just play the waiver wire, because you will get a week or two out of whichever 49ers back is up. Untracked co-host Bob Harris said Mason is fine as a handcuff provided you know he may only be your handcuff for one game, and warned against overpaying — citing [[Alexander Mattison]] as a handcuff premium that fell short.
- 2024-09-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-11]]): Waldman flagged that his pre-draft scouting report on Mason is free at the RSP site, noting Mason shared the Georgia Tech backfield with [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and was the more elusive of the two, and crediting Jeff Collins for developing two beasts at running back at a school that is not easy to recruit to. He also previewed his roundtable topic of how to manage the 49ers backfield after [[Christian McCaffrey]]'s absence, including what to do if the injury lingers.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): an emphatic yes to the premise that Mason could start for 60 percent of the league. Waldman calls him a good NFL running back who runs hard, breaks tackles and is excellent at making people miss. He reached back to Georgia Tech, where a local stat service graded Mason as the most elusive back in college football in a season when [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] was his teammate — which Waldman says tells you something on its own. What impressed him in this game was the small stuff: giving a leg and taking it away, dipping a shoulder to avoid disaster and turn a lost play into a positive. His central principle is that the best backs in football mitigate losses rather than pile up huge gains, and Mason does both.
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-25 (Campus2Canton with Felix Sharpe)]]): Mason is a legitimate starting-caliber back — Waldman said he could start for 60% of NFL teams if needed, and while he stopped short of calling him better than 60% of current starters, he said it is close. He praised how Mason mitigates losses, how he moves, his hands, and how hard he runs for his size, but argued the underrated trait is elusiveness and the efficiency with which he uses it — noting that in the season Mason was paired with [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], PFF graded him the most elusive back in football. Cited alongside [[Jaleel McLaughlin]], [[Blake Watson]] and [[Carson Steele]] as evidence that undrafted and late-round backs win jobs when teams hold open competitions, with [[Elijah Mitchell]] and [[Isaac Guerendo]] behind him in San Francisco.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): Mason is still a viable option but the return of [[Christian McCaffrey]] — 13 of 15 carries in his first game back — has effectively ended the split. Waldman notes Mason is a little banged up himself and argues against a true committee on rhythm grounds: splitting time risks neither back ever being warmed up or in a groove. With the 49ers 5-4 and in the thick of a weak division, he expects 'a hell of a lot' of McCaffrey the rest of the way.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]) [Best Ball]: on Mason's trade to Minnesota just after [[Aaron Jones]] was re-signed for two years at starter money, Waldman says it looks on the surface like a loser for both, but could be a winner for people who wait and draft Mason late again — exactly the late-round profile that paid off in 2024. He argues Mason deserved a real contract and another opportunity, calls him a good back, and notes the fit: Mason and Jones are very similar versatile players, with Mason a little more physical and Jones a little shiftier, both good receivers and blockers. He flags the contract structure as the key — if Jones has an out after 2025, Jones leads this year and Mason takes over the next, which would make both winners. Bob Harris (untracked) agrees it is a good NFL move but poor for fantasy.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]) [Dynasty]: a buy now behind [[Aaron Jones]] in Minnesota, because Waldman thinks Mason is the future starter even if it is not this year. He explicitly rejects the Ty Chandler comparison — Chandler drew hype off a couple of big volume games two years ago on the theory that Jones would decline with age, and turned out to be nothing in this offense. Waldman sees Mason differently: a back Minnesota can use to give Jones more of a break, potentially producing a two-headed backfield by season's end, and a genuinely excellent plug-in play if Jones gets hurt. He noted Jones played well last year, and very well when healthy.
- 2025-08-14 — According to Sigmund Bloom of Footballguys (not a tracked expert) with [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Mason is Bloom's favorite piece of his number-two situation, Minnesota shifting toward balance. The Vikings signed run-blockers Will Fries and Ryan Kelly away from the Colts and added Mason as a hard-nosed between-the-tackles runner, which Bloom expects to mean more running in the red zone and Mason leading the team in carries and rushing touchdowns — matching or surpassing [[David Montgomery]]'s numbers, though he is not the receiver Montgomery was and may not add the occasional three-catch, 36-yard line. He calls Mason a potential point of the spear for the offense while conceding he has no idea if it is true. [[Chris Harris]] doubts it: he does not think that team is built to be run-focused, though he allows he could be wrong.
