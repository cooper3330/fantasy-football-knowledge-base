# Kenneth Gainwell

## Expert Takes

- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): the Steelers backfield was the truly unexpected result of the week and shattered Harris's mind palace, with Mike Tomlin apparently having told the truth on his depth chart. Gainwell was clearly the starter in the first quarter, out-snapping [[Jaylen Warren]] seven to four, and was in for the two-minute drill at the end of the half, where he got crushed and nearly fumbled. He also made arguably the biggest play of the game, forcing a fumble on a kickoff after a Pittsburgh touchdown. Harris is nonetheless not that excited about adding him and does not think he is a particularly impressive player, so the news value here is mostly what it does to [[Kaleb Johnson]] and to Warren's workload rather than Gainwell as a fantasy asset.
- 2025-09-09 — According to guest Jake Ciely of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): Gainwell is his number four add and the only running back on his list, at 1% rostered. Pittsburgh drafted [[Kaleb Johnson]] to be the new [[Najee Harris]], but Johnson is not a factor right now and might not be all year; meanwhile [[Jaylen Warren]] is the same Warren he has always been despite the new contract, and Gainwell stepped in for roughly a 50-50 split. Ciely likes the touch volume as an RB3 in an offense he expects to be more run-heavy than in Week 1 without [[Aaron Rodgers]] lighting it up; he would not go crazy with FAAB. Harris pushes back harder — he knows what Gainwell is 'down to a fair thee well', calls him diminishing returns, notes the Eagles kept trying him and then thought better of it, and expects Pittsburgh to do the same. Both agree there are no great running back answers on waivers right now.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): with [[Jaylen Warren]] a surprise inactive, this was a Gainwell game — and Harris pointedly notes that inside the Vikings five it was the journeyman sub-200-pound back in the game rather than the 225-pounder. Gainwell got three carries inside the five: he converted the first at the end of the first quarter after a third-and-short deep ball to [[DK Metcalf]], converted a second in the third quarter, and was stuffed on a fourth-down call with the Steelers up 10 and four minutes left, which helped launch a Minnesota comeback. Harris will not call him amazing, says he played okay, and expects Warren back after the Week 5 bye — so he does not see Gainwell as rosterable in most leagues going forward. He is similarly dismissive of [[Kaleb Johnson]], who barely out-snapped Warren.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-02]]): Gainwell's big yardage game against Minnesota (25 fantasy points, more than [[Bijan Robinson]] or [[Tyler Allgeier]] managed on that defense) came with huge creases, but the cutbacks and setups were 'a perfect game from a run standpoint' and teaching tape for what good running back play looks like behind good blocking. His profile: essentially Tiki Barber with Devin Singletary's athleticism minus the short-area quickness — smart, technically and conceptually sound, does everything well enough, but without the athletic ability to be more. Waldman recalled hoping Gainwell's pre-draft training year would add weight and twitch from 190 to 205-210, and the combine showed he had not gained much. [[Brandon Angelo]] agreed, calling him a better receiver than he was credited for, a back who does not give you negative plays: [[Jaylen Warren]] hits home runs, Gainwell hits singles and keeps an offense on schedule. Angelo would not be surprised by a closer-to-50/50 split after the bye and expects a strong second quarter of the season; Waldman capped him at roughly 600-800 yards this year and warned against long-term over-excitement — 'he's not the next Priest Holmes' — while both agreed he is a valuable Chester Taylor/Jerome Harrison type who would have real fantasy value if Warren missed time.
- 2025-11-17 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-17 (Week 11 Game Film Review)]]): Gainwell scored two touchdowns on red-zone screens against Cincinnati and continued to see more overall work than rookie Kaleb Johnson (who logged just 8 snaps) even before Jalen Warren's injury. Harris admitted his own recent 'trial balloon' theory that Johnson was taking over the backfield 'was wrong,' and with Warren now hurt, Gainwell looks like the clear beneficiary entering Week 12.
- 2025-11-18 — According to Jake Seeley (The Athletic, not a tracked expert; [[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-11-18]]): Gainwell, at 33% rostered, ranks just ahead of [[Emmanuel Wilson]] on his personal waiver board because Jalen Warren's health is uncertain (Warren said he could have played through his injury but was also seen limping), and Gainwell could realistically hold a flex role for the rest of the season if Warren's absence lingers, whereas Wilson's ceiling is capped once Jacobs returns. [[Chris Harris]] disagrees on that ordering, ranking Gainwell only sixth on his own list and saying plainly 'I just don't think he's good' in a Steelers offense he expects to be bad regardless of who's healthy at quarterback. Both agree that for winning this specific week, Wilson is clearly the better play than Gainwell.
- 2025-11-21 — According to Chris Harris on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-21]] [Redraft (Standard)]: With [[Jalen Warren]] trending back from a sprained ankle, Harris said Gainwell 'no longer looks like anything other than a Hail Mary' for fantasy purposes.
- 2025-11-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: added six dump-off targets to his workload as the two backs combined for 34 touches against Chicago. After the Steelers were stuffed on a tush push, they handed it sideways to Gainwell on a similar look and he rambled 50 yards, initially ruled a touchdown before being overturned to the one-yard line (Warren scored on the next play) -- Harris called it 'a little bit lucky.'
- 2025-12-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris ranks Gainwell fifth on his handcuff list, calling him not a good player on a bad Steelers offense but noting he's used heavily in the passing game via dump-offs, which is enough to matter -- he's 59% rostered, higher than Harris expected.
- 2025-12-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-16]]): [[Kenneth Gainwell]] saw more work once [[Jalen Warren]] (questionable, illness) was stuffed twice at the goal line and Miami stopped resisting in the second half. His box score is inflated by one 38-yard run that didn't get called back; the two backs' underlying success rates were similar. Harris still ranks Warren ahead of him for Week 16 vs. Detroit.
- 2026-01-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-05]]) [Dynasty]: noted as playing on a one-year contract that Pittsburgh will need to re-sign; Waldman says if Gainwell returns, he expects [[Caleb Johnson]] to stay buried behind him given how assignment-sound and experienced Gainwell is in the role.
- 2026-01-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-26]]) [Dynasty]: Gainwell did excellent work as part of a tandem with [[Jalen Warren]] in Pittsburgh. Waldman comps him to a young Tiki Barber, a smart and versatile runner he had hoped would develop Barber's quick-twitch explosiveness in Memphis but never did; even so, when the line is blocked well he maximizes what's available, in the mold of a Chester Taylor or Justin Fargas -- a smart, reliable back who gets what's schemed for him and occasionally squeezes out a bit more, but Waldman is skeptical that translates into a long-term lead role. Gainwell is on a one-year deal and now hits free agency, per Waldman having 'made himself a lot of money,' whether that's back in Pittsburgh or elsewhere.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Andy Behrens (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-05]]): Gainwell became a fantasy playoff darling in Pittsburgh's 'freak' scheme. Neither host believes he's an inherently great or needle-moving back; Harris frames him as 'the backfield version of Allen Lazard' -- a player maximized specifically by Aaron Rodgers' preference for short, quick throws to backs -- and expects Gainwell to angle for wherever Rodgers lands next rather than bet on his standalone talent translating elsewhere.
- 2026-03-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman feels a Jacksonville landing (replacing [[Travis Etienne Jr.]]) is a legitimate possibility even though Gainwell would probably prefer to stay in Pittsburgh. He frames Gainwell as an 'upper echelon, more evolved version' of LeQuint Allen who knows exactly what he is -- a possible one-year bridge starter valued as a coach's-favorite, high-floor teammate who can play multiple roles at a high level, and who could mentor Bhayshul Tuten before eventually ceding the lead job to him. On [[Rashad White]] as an alternative given his history with OC Liam Cohen, Waldman said White has comparable or better physical ability -- more explosive, both are strong between-the-tackles and receiving backs, Gainwell 'a little savvier' -- but White would be a clear second choice because he lacks Gainwell's internal leadership value.
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Dave Kluge (Footballguys.com, untracked guest) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-12]]): Gainwell leaves Pittsburgh for Tampa Bay as an effective [[Rachaad White]] replacement -- Kluge calls their games very similar. [[Sean Tucker]] is also still in the mix. A co-host (Jeff Bell, cited but not on air) raised the idea that signing a specialized pass-catching back could signal the Buccaneers want more running-back involvement in the passing game generally, which would help [[Bucky Irving]] too, but Harris dismisses that as overthinking a spreadsheet -- the team likely just wanted a good receiving/blocking fill-in and hasn't decided the split yet. Both frame Gainwell as offering real 'contingency upside' if Irving gets hurt, without expecting the addition to meaningfully change Irving's role.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Gainwell is his top dynasty running back buy-low target given his current price. Waldman was highest on Gainwell as 'the best back in that backfield' in Pittsburgh 'by a good margin' and expects him to get the lion's share of Buccaneers touches, which matters more given the offense's lost receiver production without Mike Evans -- Waldman argued that gap is being underrated ('people are like, okay, Emeka can get more touches here, and Godwin, he's never going to be the same player'). He also flagged that [[Bucky Irving]]'s reported recovery timeline may be more optimistic than warranted. Gainwell projects for a significant target share and to be an important underneath option for [[Baker Mayfield]].
- 2026-04-09 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: called Kenneth Gainwell Pittsburgh's best back 'by a mile' last season and projects him to be the best back in Tampa Bay's room this year too, crediting his all-around game -- not explosive, but makes good decisions, wins coaches' trust, and adds value as a blocker, receiver and rusher. Angelo used him as the archetype comp for sleeper [[Miles Montgomery]] and said this well-rounded, versatile-role type of back is becoming more valuable and more common as defenses adjust to bigger personnel groupings.
- 2026-07-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris lists Kenneth Gainwell among his '10th least favorite players in the league' as Tampa's replacement for departed third-down back [[Rashad White]], who took about 75% of those passing-down snaps. Harris expects Gainwell to simply inherit that same specialized role and doesn't see anything learnable from camp beyond noise about him being a 'leader' despite having just joined the team. Untracked guest Jim McCormick agrees Gainwell isn't 'a world-beater' or a player worth rostering just because he was on a good Pittsburgh offense last year, calling him 'annoying' -- present mainly to chip away at [[Bucky Irving]]'s touches if Irving is ever limited.
- 2026-07-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman fully feels the reported 50/50-plus split with Bucky Irving, citing both the coaching staff and GM publicly framing Gainwell as the '1A' over Irving and Sean Tucker as '1B.' His concern with Irving is durability -- Irving played most of his career around 178-185 lbs, bulked up for the NFL, only played 10 games last year, and Waldman projects him for 10 games again in 2026; his midline projection has Irving and Tucker each around 100 carries with Gainwell around 200 (an admittedly aggressive number). He notes Gainwell's ADP (RB34, round 8) reflects last year's overall points-per-game finish (RB18) but not his week 11-on finish, where his 13.3 touches/85 yards/17.8 PPR points per game ranked behind only six backs over that final 6-8 week span. Scouting-wise, Waldman compares Gainwell (a smart, versatile Memphis product who ran a 4.44 at his combine after taking a COVID year off for family reasons) to slow-developing backs Tiki Barber and Priest Holmes, both of whom took ~5 years to have an emergent season; he projects a 2-3 year peak window with Baker Mayfield behind an improved line worth 1,000-1,200 rushing yards plus 300-500 receiving yards and double-digit total TDs, a top-10-to-15 RB finish, and calls him a value at his current RB33 price given he's only 27.
- 2026-08-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-06]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman builds an extended measurables case comparing Gainwell to Priest Holmes and Tiki Barber -- similar size, none of the three lit up combine explosiveness (Gainwell ran a 4.44 forty, 4.46 twenty-yard shuttle, 7.26 three-cone, and the second-highest vertical of the group at just under 35.5 inches, versus Holmes' 4.73/7.4 and Barber's 4.56/7.18), yet both comps took 4-5 years to break out and then had some of the best seasons in the league. He notes both Jason Licht and Todd Bowles have called Tampa's backfield a '1A/1B' and says if [[Bucky Irving]] misses time (which he projects, given Irving's injury pattern), Gainwell could see roughly 200 carries for 850-900 yards and 6-8 TDs plus 50-55 targets against Irving's 25-35 -- and if Irving goes down for the season, Gainwell could approach an 85% backfield touch share, making him a 'set-and-forget RB2 at the least,' possibly a 13-1400 yard scrimmage back. He places Gainwell in a value tier alongside players like [[David Montgomery]], [[Michael Pittman Jr.]], [[Alec Pierce]], [[Jordan Tyson]], [[Mike Evans]], [[Christian Watson]] and [[Rico Dowdle]] -- all being drafted ahead of him at typical ADP. [[Brandon Angelo]] backs the case with tape: Gainwell was the best running back on a good Steelers offense last year, an excellent receiver and pass protector, calling him one of his personal must-haves this year.
- 2026-08-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-24 (Feel It or Fk It)]]), agreeing with co-host Bob Harris: [[Bucky Irving]] playing through the preseason (rather than being rested) is a good sign, though less about Irving's health and more about proving his role -- and, more tellingly, the team is deliberately sitting Kenneth Gainwell in the preseason to protect him, which Waldman said 'tells you right there who the 1A actually might be' in Tampa Bay's backfield pecking order.
