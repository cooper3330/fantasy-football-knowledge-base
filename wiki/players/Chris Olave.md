# Chris Olave

## Expert Takes

- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon thinks Olave is 'so good he's liable to pop off an elite season at any point' and is too talented to keep producing merely 1,100 yards/5 TDs like last year. The friction he flags is with QB Derek Carr: citing Derek Klassen's 2022 charting, Carr graded below league average throwing the nine route and corner route deep and only average on posts, yet Olave was run deep constantly and repeatedly won those routes without Carr getting him the ball -- Harmon also noted Carr reportedly turns into 'a completely different, horrible' quarterback once he crosses into the red zone. His top priority for new OC Clint Kubiak (son of Gary Kubiak), who he expects to scheme a more modern offense than outgoing OC Pete Carmichael, is figuring out how to get Olave and Carr aligned, potentially with a reworked route tree.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-18]]): [[Chris Olave]]'s RP profile has been remarkably consistent across two seasons -- 75.8% success rate vs. man as a rookie, 76.3% in year two (87th percentile), 81.2%/81.5% vs. zone, and press numbers locked at 77.5%/77.1%. Harmon calls it '95% good, 5% bad' -- the good being elite, replicable separation skill; the bad being there's no year-two leap the way [[Garrett Wilson]] showed. Route tree is heavily downfield: only 7.4% of his rookie routes were slants (lowest Harmon has charted since 2014), rising to just 13.4% in year two, with roughly 40% of routes being nines/posts/corners combined and 19% curls. Harmon and James Koh (co-host, not tracked) argue this vertical-only usage was a poor fit for checkdown-heavy [[Derek Carr]] (36.4% success on nines, 37.5% on corners, 50% on posts) in Pete Carmichael's static, motion-averse Saints offense, comping the mismatch to [[Calvin Ridley]] in Jacksonville, though Harmon rates Olave the better, more explosive prospect. Both are hopeful new OC Clint Kubiak's motion-heavy scheme unlocks more middle-of-field work. Harmon's median 2024 projection: 92 catches, ~1,200 yards (up from 87/1,123 in 2023); high end 107/1,600. He grades Olave a 'very good number one receiver,' not yet a superstar, in a 'third tier' below elite status.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Olave's route tree needs to diversify -- his slant route percentage was just 13.4% in 2023 (up from a 'absolutely bonkers' 7.4% in 2022), well below the 20-25%+ Harmon associates with true WR1s like [[Keenan Allen]], [[Davante Adams]], [[Cooper Kupp]], [[Deebo Samuel]] and [[Stefon Diggs]]; only Jameson Williams, Calvin Ridley and Zay Flowers ran fewer slants among charted receivers, and Olave ran slants on 18.6% of routes at Ohio State, so the ability is there. Harmon compares Olave's speed/route-running/separation package to a lesser Tyreek Hill and thinks he's a strong fit for the full-speed 'cheat motion' concepts new OC Clint Kubiak may install. Both hosts flag that Derek Carr's pressure sensitivity behind a bottom-tier offensive line (ranked dead last by one outlet) led him to check down early and often last season, frustrating open receivers -- getting Olave and Shaheed onto quicker-developing slants and drag routes would help mitigate that.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon said he's 'that high' on Olave as a separator that he thinks Olave 'could be a guy that takes that leap into the top 12, top 10 receivers in the league,' contingent on new OC Klint Kubiak's Shanahan-tree motion-and-play-action scheme better maximizing him -- New Orleans ranked 31st in play-action usage last year, which is expected to change under Kubiak. But Harmon and guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn (not tracked) both flagged real downside risk from the Saints' offensive line: Thorn called it 'below average' even with starting RT Ryan Ramczyk healthy, and said if Ramczyk (lingering knee injury, questionable to play) sits, forcing since-benched Trevor Penning into the lineup alongside rookie LT Taliese Fuaga, the line's floor could get very low regardless of scheme, undercutting the ceiling both hosts otherwise like for Olave.
- 2024-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-25]]): Olave's Reception Perception profile is remarkably static year one to year two — 75.8% then 76.3% success rate vs man, 81.5% then 81.2% vs zone, 77.5% then 77.1% vs press (roughly 82nd percentile). Harmon ranked him 11th on his Yahoo top-15 receivers 25-and-under and already regretted it a week later, saying receivers seven through eleven were a coin flip. The open question is whether Olave stays a very high tier-two type like [[Terry McLaurin]] or [[Tyler Lockett]], or takes a [[Nico Collins]]-style year-three jump; Harmon's high-end comp is [[Stefon Diggs]], who spiked to a league-leading 78.7% vs man in his own third year. Both hosts want a more diverse route tree — more slants and layup routes — under new coordinator Klint Kubiak, since [[Derek Carr]] and Pete Carmichael's system gave the quarterback few pre-snap answers. James Koh (not a tracked expert) would not be surprised by a step forward.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): Waldman is lower on Olave than the market despite loving him coming out of school, ranking him only a low-end WR2 against a WR11-ish ADP around 17th overall. The concern is not Olave — it is [[Derek Carr]] and the offensive line. Waldman called Carr the Kerry Collins of this generation: give him time and he supports fantasy production, but break the tackle spots down and Olave becomes the six-for-67 or four-for-50 receiver who looks hot early and then disappears while you scratch your head in the second half. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) grouped Olave with [[Drake London]] as receivers being drafted at a perceived ceiling neither has actually reached, adding that Klint Kubiak's arrival and positive talk around [[Alvin Kamara]] are the hope-springs-eternal side of the case.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]) [Dynasty]: he has always loved Olave's game but was worried about [[Derek Carr]] and the offensive line; he is now bumping Olave up. The reasoning is Klint Kubiak's system adding extra gaps for the run game to get [[Alvin Kamara]] going, plus New Orleans shoring up two tackle spots and getting help from tight ends. He also thinks last year's context — Carr's legal issues as a distraction, Carr banged up, the line — suppressed the connection, and he has faith they build rapport the way Carr eventually did with Michael Crabtree and [[Amari Cooper]]. Ceiling framing: maybe never the top-10 receiver people hoped for, but top 15 is very possible.
- 2024-09-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-17]]): buy the dip. Olave sits at WR48 in fantasy with 92 yards on the season — nothing versus Carolina, 81 the following week — and Harmon says both he and Koh feel a big game coming: two touchdowns and 120-plus yards. His reasoning is scheme fit; the long-standing complaint on this show has been that Olave got no layup routes and no yards-after-catch opportunities, and Kubiak's offense started manufacturing them in Week 2, when Harmon thought Olave looked awesome. He also flags the softer stuff: Olave has played with more edge and more physicality, and in Week 1, even with two catches for 11 yards, he was visibly engaged, dapping up [[Derek Carr]] and celebrating teammates — a contrast with a strange 2023 that included an arrest and apparent friction with Carr. The structural caveat Harmon keeps: Olave is excellent downfield and Carr is not a good downfield thrower, so the skill sets are somewhat oil and water. Harmon says he will probably chart Olave.
- 2024-10-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-03]]) [Dynasty]: Olave is a very good player who has not jumped, and the Reception Perception numbers are 'eerily similar' year to year — 75.8% vs man in year one to 76.3% in year two, 81.2% vs zone to 81.5%, and within 0.4 percentage points vs press. Harmon's point is that a flat second-year profile is the norm, not a red flag: most receivers plateau somewhere and never become [[Justin Jefferson]]. He is on pace for yet another ~1,100 yards, the best of the 2022 group but still not a breakout, and Harmon notes [[Rashid Shaheed]]'s development has taken attention off him. He also shouts out Olave's effort as a blocker. Caveat repeated throughout: four games in, a new offense, and he points to [[CeeDee Lamb]]'s slow 2023 start as a warning against writing the story now.
- 2024-11-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-05]]): Olave suffered his fourth known NFL concussion against Carolina, needing a stretcher and a hospital trip after a high in-breaking throw from [[Derek Carr]]. Harmon lays out the timeline — one in October 2022, one in November 2023, and two in 2024 (Week 6 vs Tampa Bay, then this one), plus a Week 8 check against the Chargers after a similar hit. He expects an IR stint and says the retirement conversation is fair but is the player's personal decision, not an analyst's. On the player himself: one of the best young receivers in the league, elite by any efficiency stat, a fantastic route runner by his Reception Perception charting who also makes big plays — a receiver you can build a passing game around, which New Orleans has never managed. Harmon frames 2024 as one of those seasons (slow start, hamstring, then concussions) and contrasts Olave being 'stuck on the tarmac' with 2022 classmates [[Drake London]] and [[Garrett Wilson]] breaking out.
- 2024-11-22 — According to Adam Harstad, untracked *Film and Theory* co-host ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]) [Dynasty]: Olave is 'the' dented can for 2025 and, in Harstad's words, the number one buy in dynasty right now. His case is built on [[Serial Correlation in Player Evaluation]] — four processes he considers largely uncorrelated all love Olave: [[Matt Waldman]]'s film/fundamentals work, [[Matt Harmon]]'s [[Reception Perception Methodology]] (binary did-he-get-open charting), Harstad's own yards-per-route-run sheet and rookie production model, and market share of team air yards. Olave grades as the 7th-best rookie season since 2008 in that model, behind [[Odell Beckham Jr.]], [[Ja'Marr Chase]], [[Justin Jefferson]], [[A.J. Brown]], [[Puka Nacua]] and [[Mike Evans]]; the worst outcome on that top-of-list group is [[Terry McLaurin]]. Harstad's point: he isn't a top-20 dynasty WR because of injuries, a [[Derek Carr]] mismatch and New Orleans turmoil, none of which touch the talent. Waldman agreed completely, calling Olave every bit as talented as — and a better player coming out than — [[Garrett Wilson]], able to play all three receiver spots with good after-catch skills.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]): asked for a one-word, one-number answer on what round he would draft Olave in 2025, Waldman said the fifth or sixth round — a low-end WR2 to high-end WR3 — and framed that as where he would start in June, with room for more if the situation actually improves. The context is New Orleans keeping [[Derek Carr]] under a new head coach, and Waldman's note that Carr has irritated his receiving corps and their families, Olave's included. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) landed at the same range independently.
- 2025-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-30]]): with Kellen Moore the front-runner in New Orleans, the open question for the receiver room is health first — Olave coming off the concussions and [[Rashid Shaheed]] off the lower-body injury. Harmon's scheme note is that Moore has a real track record of positioning his top receiver into favorable matchups: [[CeeDee Lamb]] used inside-out in Dallas, [[Keenan Allen]] fed underneath in Los Angeles to keep him away from physical outside coverage, [[DeVonta Smith]] moved into the slot more in Philadelphia. He is explicitly unsure whether Olave is that type of player. Harmon separately notes Olave ran a lot of the Shanahan-style in-breaking routes under Klint Kubiak in 2024 without it materializing into much production, though he looked good when active.
- 2025-02-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-21]]): Harmon wants to talk himself into Olave in the new Kellen Moore offense, because Olave has been asked to do the hardest job in football — his 7.1% slant-route rate as a rookie is the lowest Harmon has charted for any receiver since 2014, meaning a quick-twitch, polished route runner has essentially never been given layup routes. He cites Moore's history of manufacturing easy targets for [[CeeDee Lamb]] and [[Keenan Allen]], and points to [[DeVonta Smith]] jumping from a 24.3% slot rate to a career-high 47.5% with career-best YAC and eight touchdowns as the template. The blocker is availability: two concussions inside one month in 2024 plus one in 2023 and one in 2022 — four known in three years, with a suspected college concussion as well. Harmon calls it 'really, really scary' and says history says he cannot trust him to stay on the field.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman still believes in Olave and thinks a WR31 ADP (roughly round five) is low, noting he has been around that range or lower for a while. The constraint is quarterback — Waldman says if Olave had a quarterback he believed in, Olave would be no later than a third-round pick and he would be thrilled to have him there. Forced to choose between Olave at WR31 and [[Rashid Shaheed]] at WR51, he takes Shaheed, precisely because he is not counting on Shaheed as a starter but could still get starter value from that cost.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): Olave gets 'a little sliver of light' from [[Derek Carr]]'s retirement, but Waldman would not bump him up boards for it. His image was the bombing of Dresden — the bombing is over, you still have to deal with the cleanup — because none of [[Tyler Shough]], Jake Haener or [[Spencer Rattler]] stands out as someone Olave benefits unbelievably from. Everything still depends on the room processing well and being on the same page, and there are a lot of ifs and unknowns. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) supplied the context: Michael Thomas, Olave's brother and Robert Griffin III were all heavily critical of Carr's 'hospital balls' into Olave, which contributed to multiple concussions.
- 2025-05-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-21]]): with Pittsburgh reportedly calling on Olave's availability and New Orleans saying no, Harmon would not trade him for a second-round pick even though he thinks the Saints should be selling — because if [[Tyler Shough]] is to succeed, or even as a placeholder before a 2026-27 quarterback, you want him throwing to a receiver this good. Harmon calls Olave still underrated: 12th in yards per route run since entering the league in 2022, and 10th in first downs per route run among players with 500-plus routes, sandwiched between [[Drake London]] (9th) and [[Ja'Marr Chase]] (11th). He blames bad luck — injuries plus a chemistry with [[Derek Carr]] that never worked, saying they simply did not see the game the same way — and hopes 2025 under Kellen Moore, who has said Olave will be the focal point, is the breakout. On the concussion history he points to [[Brandin Cooks]], who had repeated concussion problems until he did not. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) notes the room behind Olave — Cooks, [[Rashid Shaheed]] off major injury, [[Bub Means]], Mason Tipton — is fragile enough that it goes from respectable to disaster if Olave misses time.
- 2025-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-18]]): Harmon calls Olave a genuine number one receiver being 'shat on' by the market — he ranked him ninth in his Yahoo top-15 receivers 25-and-under and got mocked for it. Charting: despite a big 2024 role change under Klint Kubiak's 12-personnel condensed sets (career-high 79.1% outside snaps and 68.3% on the line of scrimmage, versus under 36% in each of his first two seasons), he held a 76.3% success rate vs man, an identical 77.1% vs press in 2023 and 2024, and nudged his zone number up. Career-high 20% contested target rate at an 80% contested catch rate and a broken tackle on 41% of in-space attempts. His 84% dig success rate at 20% participation puts him on a list with only [[Davante Adams]], [[Brandon Aiyuk]], [[Julio Jones]], [[Michael Pittman Jr.]], [[Ladd McConkey]], [[Nico Collins]], [[Justin Jefferson]] and [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]. Since he entered the league he ranks 8th in successful targets per route run, 11th in first downs per route run and 13th in yards per route run among 400-plus-route receivers. Concussions limited him to eight games in 2024 (44 targets, 32 catches, ~400 yards, one TD, 73% catch rate); Harmon calls the injury risk real but unknowable and points to [[Brandon Cooks]] as a counterexample, and blames bad luck plus poor [[Derek Carr]] chemistry (best game was 14 targets with [[Spencer Rattler]] vs the Chargers). He expects Kellen Moore to slide Olave into more slot work, citing [[DeVonta Smith]]'s 47.5% slot rate and most efficient season, and calls him a buy outside the top 50 picks even though he is not excited about the 2025 Saints.
- 2025-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-30]]): Olave is badly underrated after a concussion-shortened 32-catch, 400-yard, one-touchdown 2024, and is one of the most efficient receivers in the NFL on a per-route basis. His new [[Reception Perception Methodology]] profile shows a 76.3% success rate vs man (identical to the prior year), 82.2% vs zone (a slight rise) and 77.1% vs press (also identical) — Harmon notes very few players clear 75% vs man and 82% vs zone simultaneously. He calls 2024 a breakthrough because Olave held those rates while playing more as the outside X in Clint Kubiak's scheme, and adds a career-best 80% contested catch rate on a higher contested target rate, plus a tackle broken on 41% of in-space attempts. What separates him from [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] is not route running or separation but yards after catch and tackle breaking; Harmon puts him at the bottom of tier two or first name in tier three, and wouldn't rule out a year-four graduation. Caveats: the concussion history is genuinely scary, and he was massively overdrafted last year though he is far cheaper now.
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]): both hosts are 'feeling it' that New Orleans produces a top-15 fantasy option other than [[Alvin Kamara]], and Olave is a leading candidate. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked) argues [[Tyler Shough]] and [[Spencer Rattler]] are not good and will turn it over with head-scratching decisions, but both have a gunslinger willingness to push the ball downfield — and he would rather lean on receiver talent than fret over quarterback uncertainty. He sketches a 2-15 Saints team playing from behind funneling heavy volume to Olave and [[Rashid Shaheed]]. Kluge cites 2024, when [[Jameis Winston]] came in and Olave immediately looked like the WR1 people wanted. Waldman agrees, with the caveat that it works only as long as none of these quarterbacks has 'the [[Derek Carr]] gene' for throwing hospital balls.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-07]]): Olave is 'definitely a PPR scam right now' — second in the NFL in targets, fourth in receptions, but 33rd among receivers in receiving yards, and Koh stresses he has not cracked 60 receiving yards in any of five games while averaging over 10 targets. Harmon is deliberately of two minds. He likes the deployment: air yards per target down to 7.9 (from 14.2 as a rookie, 13.0 in 2023, 10.3 in 2024), slot rate up to 37% after a career-low 21%, lots of motion, and a genuine Olave–[[Spencer Rattler]] connection on quick out routes — a real answer to the vertical-only pigeonhole. The problem is that Olave is not a tackle breaker, so out-breaking layups generate no yards after catch, and the vertical shots where he is getting open are not being hit. Koh wants a happy medium around 11-12 air yards per target. Harmon adds a caveat that whole shots over the middle may never be his game given his concussion history, and calls him a perennial buy-low candidate.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): Olave's first catch of the game did not come until a third-and-two at the end of the first half where [[Tyler Shough]] scrambled to extend. His okay-looking numbers, like [[Rashid Shaheed]]'s, all came down 24 in garbage time. Harris's caution: 'it's hard to rely on that happening every week', though he concedes 'especially for Olave, it kind of has' — while warning 'maybe don't bet into a winning streak'.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-06]]): with [[Rashid Shaheed]] traded, New Orleans will 'just get the ball to Chris Olave,' who already sits at 87 targets on the season — Harmon notes Olave has historically been a player hurting for targets and is now maybe getting too many, adding that he is not having a perfect season but is having a really nice one. He describes the structural support Olave receives: [[Brandin Cooks]] frequently starts in the slot, motions out wide and runs a clear-out route so Olave can work a deep hook, deep cross or deep dig underneath it. Harmon wonders whether the offense becomes more static without Shaheed, since New Orleans currently blurs alignment lines with motion — five non-backs run routes on over 23% of dropbacks with slot rates all between 38% and 43%.
- 2025-11-07 — According to guest Jim McCormick (not a tracked expert) on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-07]] [Redraft (Standard)]: Olave was his most pivotal player — a genuine league-changer whose experience is 'like the Olive Garden,' very good but wildly inconsistent. With [[Rashid Shaheed]] traded away, McCormick argues Olave is left with essentially nobody else in New Orleans and could see enormous target volume ('25 targets a game') but from fullbacks and [[Taysom Hill]], so volume alone will not reliably deliver; some weeks will be duds and some will make seasons. [[Chris Harris]] had Olave as an honorable mention on his own list and broadly agrees: this seems like an okay time to trade him, except everyone knows the same thing, so realistically you keep him — he 'keeps rescuing himself late in these games' and you wonder how long that can continue. Later both agreed they would take Olave over [[TreVeyon Henderson]] in a straight swap.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-11]]): Olave had a big game with [[Tyler Shough]] pushing the ball downfield, targeted on 31% of his routes for 4.0 yards per route run, including a contested touchdown where Jaycee Horn fell and a one-handed catch — precisely the tough contested plays critics say he doesn't make. He led Saints receivers in slot rate at 52.8%, and his production came outside the numbers on out-breakers, only 18 passing yards on slants, ins and posts. His catchable target rate was 63%. Harmon notes Olave is second in the NFL in targets and is having a quietly strong efficiency season despite the Saints offense. Emotional context: it was a year since the concussion at Bank of America Stadium on a bad ball from [[Derek Carr]] that ended his 2024 season, and Olave said he felt anxiety in the building beforehand.
