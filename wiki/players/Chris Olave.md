- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon thinks Olave is 'so good he's liable to pop off an elite season at any point' and is too talented to keep producing merely 1,100 yards/5 TDs like last year. The friction he flags is with QB Derek Carr: citing Derek Klassen's 2022 charting, Carr graded below league average throwing the nine route and corner route deep and only average on posts, yet Olave was run deep constantly and repeatedly won those routes without Carr getting him the ball -- Harmon also noted Carr reportedly turns into 'a completely different, horrible' quarterback once he crosses into the red zone. His top priority for new OC Clint Kubiak (son of Gary Kubiak), who he expects to scheme a more modern offense than outgoing OC Pete Carmichael, is figuring out how to get Olave and Carr aligned, potentially with a reworked route tree.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-18]]): [[Chris Olave]]'s RP profile has been remarkably consistent across two seasons -- 75.8% success rate vs. man as a rookie, 76.3% in year two (87th percentile), 81.2%/81.5% vs. zone, and press numbers locked at 77.5%/77.1%. Harmon calls it '95% good, 5% bad' -- the good being elite, replicable separation skill; the bad being there's no year-two leap the way [[Garrett Wilson]] showed. Route tree is heavily downfield: only 7.4% of his rookie routes were slants (lowest Harmon has charted since 2014), rising to just 13.4% in year two, with roughly 40% of routes being nines/posts/corners combined and 19% curls. Harmon and James Koh (co-host, not tracked) argue this vertical-only usage was a poor fit for checkdown-heavy [[Derek Carr]] (36.4% success on nines, 37.5% on corners, 50% on posts) in Pete Carmichael's static, motion-averse Saints offense, comping the mismatch to [[Calvin Ridley]] in Jacksonville, though Harmon rates Olave the better, more explosive prospect. Both are hopeful new OC Clint Kubiak's motion-heavy scheme unlocks more middle-of-field work. Harmon's median 2024 projection: 92 catches, ~1,200 yards (up from 87/1,123 in 2023); high end 107/1,600. He grades Olave a 'very good number one receiver,' not yet a superstar, in a 'third tier' below elite status.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Olave's route tree needs to diversify -- his slant route percentage was just 13.4% in 2023 (up from a 'absolutely bonkers' 7.4% in 2022), well below the 20-25%+ Harmon associates with true WR1s like [[Keenan Allen]], [[Davante Adams]], [[Cooper Kupp]], [[Deebo Samuel]] and [[Stefon Diggs]]; only Jameson Williams, Calvin Ridley and Zay Flowers ran fewer slants among charted receivers, and Olave ran slants on 18.6% of routes at Ohio State, so the ability is there. Harmon compares Olave's speed/route-running/separation package to a lesser Tyreek Hill and thinks he's a strong fit for the full-speed 'cheat motion' concepts new OC Clint Kubiak may install. Both hosts flag that Derek Carr's pressure sensitivity behind a bottom-tier offensive line (ranked dead last by one outlet) led him to check down early and often last season, frustrating open receivers -- getting Olave and Shaheed onto quicker-developing slants and drag routes would help mitigate that.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon said he's 'that high' on Olave as a separator that he thinks Olave 'could be a guy that takes that leap into the top 12, top 10 receivers in the league,' contingent on new OC Klint Kubiak's Shanahan-tree motion-and-play-action scheme better maximizing him -- New Orleans ranked 31st in play-action usage last year, which is expected to change under Kubiak. But Harmon and guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn (not tracked) both flagged real downside risk from the Saints' offensive line: Thorn called it 'below average' even with starting RT Ryan Ramczyk healthy, and said if Ramczyk (lingering knee injury, questionable to play) sits, forcing since-benched Trevor Penning into the lineup alongside rookie LT Taliese Fuaga, the line's floor could get very low regardless of scheme, undercutting the ceiling both hosts otherwise like for Olave.
- 2024-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-25]]): Olave's Reception Perception profile is remarkably static year one to year two — 75.8% then 76.3% success rate vs man, 81.5% then 81.2% vs zone, 77.5% then 77.1% vs press (roughly 82nd percentile). Harmon ranked him 11th on his Yahoo top-15 receivers 25-and-under and already regretted it a week later, saying receivers seven through eleven were a coin flip. James Koh (not a tracked expert) would not be surprised by a step forward.

On the year he has 156 targets (top-4 among WRs), 100 receptions (only the second Saints WR ever to reach 100 in a season, after Michael Thomas), 1,163 yards (6th in the NFL) and 9 TDs -- more than his prior three seasons combined (10). Harmon says nothing about Olave's talent changed; he'd always rated him this way, taking heavy pushback for ranking him too high on his Yahoo Top-15 WRs 25-and-under list, with fans dismissing him as 'not good' partly due to concussion history (Olave has discussed considering retirement at 24 and the emotional weight of returning to the site of his worst concussion, in Carolina). What changed: (1) Kellen Moore's scheme is by far the best-designed offense of his career, moving him into the slot (35% of snaps) and using heavy motion versus being locked outside/X under the prior 'prehistoric' Pete Carmichael offense, and (2) his chemistry with rookie QB [[Tyler Shough]] is the best of his career, contrasted with a more combative, less trusting dynamic under Derek Carr. Olave has said he's playing through late-season injuries specifically to help get Shough Offensive Rookie of the Year, though he managed just 1 fantasy point in the Week 17 finale.
- 2026-02-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-27]]): after posting career highs across the board in 2025 -- 156 targets, 100 receptions, 1,163 yards and 9 touchdowns despite a middling Saints offense (28th in Harmon's composite offensive score) -- Olave has graduated from a 'tier three' evaluation to a 'tier two' receiver, ranked around 15th overall, inside the top 20 but not yet top 10. Harmon credits [[Kellen Moore]]'s pre-snap motion usage (Saints were number one in pre-snap motion rate and seconds per play) for weaponizing Olave's slot alignment, the same effect Moore had on Devonta Smith in Philadelphia. Co-host James Koh (untracked) went further, calling it a 'great' season and arguing New Orleans should extend Olave at '30-plus million a year' rather than entertain trading Tyler Shough. Both hosts note real chemistry with new starter [[Tyler Shough]] after Olave never clicked with Derek Carr and only partially with Spencer Rattler; Harmon says Olave still needs to win more contested, high-difficulty catches (comping Puka Nacua/Jaxon Smith-Njigba) to reach tier one. Asked to choose between Olave and [[AJ Brown]] heading into 2026, Harmon leaned Olave given trajectory -- Brown trending down, Olave trending up.
- 2026-04-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-28]]): remains New Orleans' clear number one and a 'pro-level route runner' who wins man coverage, complementing new arrival [[Jordan Tyson]] as the zone-beating counterpart -- 'Chris Olave will be in there crushing man coverage.' Last year he got open in the intermediate/in-breaking area more than Spencer Rattler could hit him; Harmon expects new starter Tyler Shough to convert more of that separation into production in 2026.
- 2026-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-06]]) [Dynasty]: Olave's new extension ($31M/year) is a fair price given his charting profile, which Harmon says finally confirmed talent he'd flagged for years once health, scheme, and quarterback play all lined up. Route diet was extremely balanced -- six different routes each run in double digits, none above 17.3% -- which Harmon says is rare even among top receivers. His slant rate jumped from a career-low 7.4% as a rookie (third-lowest Harmon has ever charted) to 17.3% at an 85.5% success rate, and success rate versus press coverage was eerily consistent across all four seasons (77.5%, 77.1%, 77.1%, 77.5%). Chemistry markedly improved with new QB Tyler Shough over the middle: 78.3% success on digs and 78.4% on posts, whereas prior QB Spencer Rattler was excellent on quick out-breaking routes (87.5% success on flats) but had little chemistry with Olave on in-breakers. Harmon floated rookie Jordan Tyson as a complementary zone-beater to pair with Olave's man-coverage strength (77.1% success vs. man, 91st percentile) in the 2026 Saints passing game.
