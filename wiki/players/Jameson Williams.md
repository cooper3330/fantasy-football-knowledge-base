# Jameson Williams

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): a real but explicitly hedged optimism after a big NFC Championship showing — "I'm optimistic for him" — paired immediately with a sharp warning against overreacting to a single game: "how many people are going to overdraft Jamison Williams based on this game? The Gabe Davis factor will be in effect, people. Be careful of it." Not given a specific 2024 ranking or role projection beyond that caution.
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]), independent light corroboration: acknowledges the pre-draft trade-up to select him "hasn't worked out" so far as a value proposition, but immediately tempers that criticism given the two-touchdown NFC Championship performance — "I don't want to slam him too hard right now."
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): the most technically critical read on Williams yet, delivered alongside the news that OC Ben Johnson is *staying* in Detroit (resolving the scheme-change risk this bullet would otherwise flag). Harmon's specific complaint isn't just "not yet refined" route running — it's "the shaky hands" and weak ball-tracking, calling him explicitly "not a go-up-and-get-it guy." His projection: a career "splash-play dude" rather than a full-route-tree receiver, with an added dependency flag — many of his highlight plays are specifically Ben-Johnson-designed structure plays, so his ceiling is somewhat tied to Johnson staying as play-caller (which, as of this same episode, he has).

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): revisited while assessing Detroit's wide receiver need, graded only "substantial," not "critical," partly because Williams still profiles as only the Lions' fifth-most-important skill-position player behind [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[Sam LaPorta]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and [[David Montgomery]]. One host is skeptical he "amounts to very much" and doesn't view him as a true X receiver even at his best; the other credits his volatile off-field history (injuries, a gambling suspension) rather than talent as the reason he's still unproven entering year three, noting the team was "insistent at the combine that he is ready to be a starter."
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: Detroit staying out of the receiver market signals internal faith in Williams, but Harmon is openly skeptical -- his dynasty rankings currently carry a note that 'film's not good,' based on charting only a partial sample so far, though he saw positive flashes late in the playoffs. He rated Williams a clear first-round prospect but not in the same tier as Drake London, Chris Olave, or Garrett Wilson, and attributes his stalled momentum largely to injuries and off-field issues (the gambling suspension) rather than pure ability. Structurally, Harmon argues Williams can never be more than the 5th-most-involved player in Detroit's offense behind David Montgomery (235 touches), Jahmyr Gibbs (234), Amon-Ra St. Brown (123 touches) and Sam LaPorta (87) -- last year he touched the ball only 27 times (42 targets, 354 yards, 2 TDs) -- and separately questions whether his timed speed translates to functional football speed, saying it hasn't consistently shown up at the NFL level. Co-host James Koh (untracked) largely agreed, framing this as a real-football evaluation rather than a fantasy one.
- 2024-06-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-04]]): Williams' full 2023 Reception Perception sample (12 games, 24-42-354-2TD, 57.1% catch rate) shows a poor season-long profile lined up almost exclusively as an outside X (85.6% on the line, 72.6% outside) -- 57% success rate vs. man, 72% vs. zone, 53.3% vs. press, in roughly the 13th/12th/15th percentiles. But Harmon flags this as misleading: weeks 11-13 (Bears/Saints/Packers) were catastrophic, worse than the Quentin Johnston numbers charted a few episodes prior (~30% vs. man, ~23% vs. press), while the week-15-on sample (Broncos game through the NFC Championship) checks in at a much stronger 67.3% vs. man, 84.9% vs. zone, 64.7% vs. press -- Harmon compares that split directly to [[Zay Flowers]]'s profile: not a superstar but a legitimate NFL starter. His route strengths are big-over concepts (post route 74.3% success, dig 77.8%, both run above the NFL-average rate), but he remains weak on slants (61%, worse than his zone number implies) and nine routes (45.2%), meaning the go-route speed threat doesn't show up as elite separation; ball skills graded better than expected (83.5% contested catch rate, no drops on 21.4% target share). Harmon projects a target ceiling of roughly 5-6 per game (~100-119 for a full season) behind [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and [[Sam LaPorta]], not the volume spike some fans expect, and lays out historical comps ranging from bust outcomes (Corey Coleman, Kelvin Benjamin, MVS, Paris Campbell) to a median outcome of [[Gabe Davis]] (a 'win for Detroit' given the draft capital spent) up to a ceiling of early-career Brandon Cooks or Marvin Jones. Co-host James Koh (untracked), while agreeing the late-season data is encouraging, pushed back on excusing Williams' rookie-year struggles as 'still learning the position,' arguing that's not acceptable for a first-round pick, and noted he could see Williams going as a fifth-round ADP pick this summer given the name value.
