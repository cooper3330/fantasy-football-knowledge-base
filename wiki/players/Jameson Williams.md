# Jameson Williams

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): a real but explicitly hedged optimism after a big NFC Championship showing — "I'm optimistic for him" — paired immediately with a sharp warning against overreacting to a single game: "how many people are going to overdraft Jamison Williams based on this game? The Gabe Davis factor will be in effect, people. Be careful of it." Not given a specific 2024 ranking or role projection beyond that caution.
- 2024-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-30]]), independent light corroboration: acknowledges the pre-draft trade-up to select him "hasn't worked out" so far as a value proposition, but immediately tempers that criticism given the two-touchdown NFC Championship performance — "I don't want to slam him too hard right now."
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): the most technically critical read on Williams yet, delivered alongside the news that OC Ben Johnson is *staying* in Detroit (resolving the scheme-change risk this bullet would otherwise flag). Harmon's specific complaint isn't just "not yet refined" route running — it's "the shaky hands" and weak ball-tracking, calling him explicitly "not a go-up-and-get-it guy." His projection: a career "splash-play dude" rather than a full-route-tree receiver, with an added dependency flag — many of his highlight plays are specifically Ben-Johnson-designed structure plays, so his ceiling is somewhat tied to Johnson staying as play-caller (which, as of this same episode, he has).

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): revisited while assessing Detroit's wide receiver need, graded only "substantial," not "critical," partly because Williams still profiles as only the Lions' fifth-most-important skill-position player behind [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[Sam LaPorta]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and [[David Montgomery]]. One host is skeptical he "amounts to very much" and doesn't view him as a true X receiver even at his best; the other credits his volatile off-field history (injuries, a gambling suspension) rather than talent as the reason he's still unproven entering year three, noting the team was "insistent at the combine that he is ready to be a starter."
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: Detroit staying out of the receiver market signals internal faith in Williams, but Harmon is openly skeptical -- his dynasty rankings currently carry a note that 'film's not good,' based on charting only a partial sample so far, though he saw positive flashes late in the playoffs. He rated Williams a clear first-round prospect but not in the same tier as Drake London, Chris Olave, or Garrett Wilson, and attributes his stalled momentum largely to injuries and off-field issues (the gambling suspension) rather than pure ability. Structurally, Harmon argues Williams can never be more than the 5th-most-involved player in Detroit's offense behind David Montgomery (235 touches), Jahmyr Gibbs (234), Amon-Ra St. Brown (123 touches) and Sam LaPorta (87) -- last year he touched the ball only 27 times (42 targets, 354 yards, 2 TDs) -- and separately questions whether his timed speed translates to functional football speed, saying it hasn't consistently shown up at the NFL level. Co-host James Koh (untracked) largely agreed, framing this as a real-football evaluation rather than a fantasy one.
- 2024-06-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-04]]): Williams' full 2023 Reception Perception sample (12 games, 24-42-354-2TD, 57.1% catch rate) shows a poor season-long profile lined up almost exclusively as an outside X (85.6% on the line, 72.6% outside) -- 57% success rate vs. man, 72% vs. zone, 53.3% vs. press, in roughly the 13th/12th/15th percentiles. But Harmon flags this as misleading: weeks 11-13 (Bears/Saints/Packers) were catastrophic, worse than the Quentin Johnston numbers charted a few episodes prior (~30% vs. man, ~23% vs. press), while the week-15-on sample (Broncos game through the NFC Championship) checks in at a much stronger 67.3% vs. man, 84.9% vs. zone, 64.7% vs. press -- Harmon compares that split directly to [[Zay Flowers]]'s profile: not a superstar but a legitimate NFL starter. His route strengths are big-over concepts (post route 74.3% success, dig 77.8%, both run above the NFL-average rate), but he remains weak on slants (61%, worse than his zone number implies) and nine routes (45.2%), meaning the go-route speed threat doesn't show up as elite separation; ball skills graded better than expected (83.5% contested catch rate, no drops on 21.4% target share). Harmon projects a target ceiling of roughly 5-6 per game (~100-119 for a full season) behind [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and [[Sam LaPorta]], not the volume spike some fans expect, and lays out historical comps ranging from bust outcomes (Corey Coleman, Kelvin Benjamin, MVS, Paris Campbell) to a median outcome of [[Gabe Davis]] (a 'win for Detroit' given the draft capital spent) up to a ceiling of early-career Brandon Cooks or Marvin Jones. Co-host James Koh (untracked), while agreeing the late-season data is encouraging, pushed back on excusing Williams' rookie-year struggles as 'still learning the position,' arguing that's not acceptable for a first-round pick, and noted he could see Williams going as a fifth-round ADP pick this summer given the name value. - 2024-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-30]]): he is 'buying-ish' on the glowing camp reports — 'not a full dollar, but 75 cents on the dollar'. Williams has the longest Reception Perception profile Harmon has written, and the story is a split sample: across the full charting the numbers are objectively bad (13th percentile vs man, 12th vs zone, 15th vs press), with weeks 11-13 at 'sub-[[Quentin Johnston]] level'. But from week 15 through the NFC Championship he posted 67% success vs man, 84.8% vs zone and 64.7% vs press, and a 94.1% success rate on curls — a man-beater route requiring deception, technique and timing that Harmon did not think Williams could run as a college prospect. Harmon's live concern is usage, not performance: even in that hot stretch Williams was on the line of scrimmage on 81.9% of sampled snaps and outside on 70.4%, and Harmon has never wanted him as a pure boundary X because press is consistently his weakest score and his speed is best deployed off the line. He suggests Detroit find another body (Donovan Peoples-Jones?) to run the sacrificial clear-out X so Williams can be moved around. If Williams is the final-five-games player, Harmon thinks he is a solid number two who takes the offense to another level; if not, Detroit has nothing behind [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and [[Sam LaPorta]]. He explicitly refuses a strong take either way and flags that Williams has simply not played much football after injury and suspension. Koh asks whether [[Jared Goff]] can access those areas of the field; Harmon says probably yes when protected, less so under pressure.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): Williams is the buzzy preseason player he is least sold on. Angelo grants the camp reports have been good and that this is the classic year-three-breakout setup after an injury year and the gambling suspension, but questions whether it is good enough to supplant targets from [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and [[Sam LaPorta]], with [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] reportedly working more in the slot and being used as a traditional receiver while [[David Montgomery]] handles the backfield — meaning Gibbs eats more target share than last year. Angelo's core objection is that Detroit is an efficient offense whose greatest efficiency is running the football, so the WR2 role may not support a startable fantasy asset. His comp: a 'Will Fuller minus' type — big weeks, no consistency. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed, calling him a dart throw with some big weeks and nothing more.
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): Detroit is hoping for the third-year breakout, but Harmon says even the best version of Williams may not be a true number two receiver — the open question is whether he is only a lid lifter, a [[Brandin Cooks]] type, or something in between. That matters because Detroit's hole is specifically the outside possession/flanker role [[Josh Reynolds]] vacated: [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] is mostly a slot player, and third receiver Kalif Raymond is a tiny speed threat, so Williams does not by himself fill the gap. Harmon reads the late additions of [[Tim Patrick]], Allen Robinson and Donovan Peoples-Jones as Detroit declining to lean on Williams for that role. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) frames it the same way — Detroit is deliberately assembling hyper-specific role players rather than betting on one WR2.

- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): recapping his 40-plus-minute summer profile, Harmon's position is that Williams' total RP picture looks bad but the final five games parse out to roughly a starting-level receiver who can be in the mix, with the curl route increasing, slant success rate holding and dig routes coming up. He expected the Detroit hype to force a big ADP push-up that never came — Williams finished the offseason as the consensus WR49 — so Harmon ended up drafting him a decent bit despite his own skepticism, purely on price. The Week 1 question is whether he is a five-target splash and clear-out player or a true full-field number two behind [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and [[Sam LaPorta]]. Koh (not tracked) argues Detroit is quietly hedging by adding outside receivers like [[Tim Patrick]] and Allen Robinson, suggesting the team itself sees him as a fourth or fifth option; Harmon partly agrees while noting some of the camp-clip posting is engagement farming.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): Williams' 9-target, 5-catch, 121-yard, 1-TD Sunday night was his first 100-yard game and only his third over 50 receiving yards, and Harmon calls it a very encouraging debut that lines up with the good half of his charting. He re-states the 2023 Reception Perception profile — the longest on the site — where the first three games sampled were 'unspeakably bad' but the final five (Broncos game onward) produced a 67.3% success rate vs man, an 84.8% vs zone that would have been a top-five 2023 mark, and a still-problematic 64.7% vs press. His prospect profile showed slant 82%, dig 73%, post 80% at high volume and 82.5% vs zone (77th percentile). Usage was the story: 71.4% of snaps out wide, a 42.9% first-read target share per Fantasy Points, 68 of his 121 yards after the catch, and heavy full-speed motion by Ben Johnson to get him off the line and away from press man. Harmon's caveat is that it is one game — 'he's got to string it together' — but says you can't put the toothpaste back in the tube.

Koh (not tracked) counters that the offense may be worse for it — 20 targets in two games with a 50 percent catch rate, stalled drives, and deep overs that are not [[Jared Goff]]'s game. - 2024-10-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-03]]): after charting week 1 and the Seahawks game, Harmon says Detroit got 'a little too carried away' feeding Williams early — a new toy, plus a real human element in keeping an emotionally invested player engaged — then dialed it back, which matches the target line Koh reads out (nine then eleven targets, down to three vs Arizona and two vs Seattle). The fit is what Harmon flagged from his college route charts: in-breaking intermediate and deep work, digs and posts, dropped into a play-action offense, as on the long Seahawks touchdown. He is a good, effortful blocker despite his frame. Verdict: much more a role player than [[Drake London]], [[Garrett Wilson]] or [[Chris Olave]], production will be volatile and up-and-down, but there is staying power in the role. Koh credits Harmon with nailing this evaluation; Harmon's stated lesson is that he opened the offseason with 'I don't have a strong take on this' and mapped ranges of outcomes rather than a hot take off the raw success rates.

- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-23]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon would put Williams at the bottom of the complementary-two group despite acknowledging he had a great year, calling him a genuinely odd player. His film read is a direct correction of the popular one: Williams is not really the guy who takes the top off, because Detroit is not running him vertically and he is 'actually not that great of a go route runner' — his value comes on big exaggerated digs and overs where he reaches the break point and simply runs away from the coverage, which suits [[Jared Goff]]'s middle-of-the-field strengths. Harmon showed in 2023 he could be a more consistent zone beater. The risk is scheme dependence: with Ben Johnson gone, the right coordinator expresses this skill set and the wrong one does not — co-host James Koh (not tracked) asked directly how confident Harmon is in Williams's development now, and Harmon said that is exactly what he gets hung up on. He had planned a Reception Perception underrated bounce-back feature on Williams and [[Rashad Bateman]] before both teams were eliminated.
