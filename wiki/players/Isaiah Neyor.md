# Isaiah Neyor

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): the Nebraska (previously Texas and Wyoming) receiver plays to his size and speed at 6'4 and 220, is a ball winner with athletic ability and good vertical route-running skills — traits that remind Waldman of [[Nico Collins]]. He can play flanker or split end with justification for either, but if the offense wants explosive ball carrying and space work at flanker he probably will not fit; the boundary-winning deep threat role at split end suits him. Waldman's ideal fit: on a team whose split end is a [[Ja'Marr Chase]] or [[Justin Jefferson]] type, Neyor could be the [[Tee Higgins]]-like complement with a Collins-style game.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: Neyor is a quick-hitter on his too-bearish list. Watching essentially every catch from Wyoming, Nebraska and some Texas film, Waldman never saw him have to show patience, decision making or creativity in traffic — only veering away from defenders in wide-open space — so he could not judge vision and elusiveness the way he grades other receivers. He said Neyor could have been much higher on his board had that evidence existed, and that the gap is about role, not proven deficiency.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]) [Dynasty]: 'feel it' as a deep-league watch only. Neyor's 87 catches in five seasons explains going undrafted, but San Francisco signing him matters because after moving on from [[Deebo Samuel]] they lack a true X, and Neyor is one of the few real X types available — 4.4 speed at 6-4, 218, with good hands and ball-tracking. He was terrific at Wyoming, transferred to Texas to pair with [[Xavier Worthy]] as Sarkisian's one-two punch, tore his ACL, then played well at Nebraska. Waldman qualifies it hard: only relevant in 40-50 roster-spot leagues, and otherwise a camp name to monitor, with no character red flags he has heard of. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) adds that the SF receiver room has real questions and room to climb, calling [[Jauan Jennings]] the clear WR1 there until further notice.
- 2025-06-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-05]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman's third pick from the camp-buzz list, chosen mostly on speed reports but backed by contested grabs he has seen — no one else in San Francisco has his size-speed combination. He notes Neyor was hurt at Texas after Steve Sarkisian talked up a [[Xavier Worthy]]-and-Neyor one-two punch, and that ACL recoveries typically cost a full year (his comp: Jordy Nelson), so Neyor may be three quarters of the way through the season before he feels himself, with Angelo suggesting year two. Waldman does not expect him to win a starting job but expects him to hang around, then look like a different player in year two. Both see the opening: [[Ricky Pearsall]] already has a hamstring issue, [[Brandon Aiyuk]] may be out the door, [[George Kittle]] is aging, and only [[Jauan Jennings]] is a reliable down-to-down option. Waldman's comp is a lower-draft-capital [[Nico Collins]] — not as good, but a plausible secondary target — and both close by likening him to a [[George Pickens]] type.
- 2025-06-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-30]]) [Dynasty]: George Kittle's public shout-out is worth acting on — Waldman says in dynasty you should be adding Neyor on expanded rosters or taking a late-draft chance, as he has been doing for weeks. Neyor ripped it up at Wyoming, was recruited to Texas by Steve Sarkisian, who talked up a Neyor-[[Xavier Worthy]] one-two punch before Neyor tore his ACL, and rebuilt himself at Nebraska. Waldman describes a 6-4, roughly 218-pound receiver running low 4.4s to high 4.3s who tracks the ball well and is good after the catch; he says Neyor would have been an easy top-12 receiver in the RSP if he had seen more than deep targets where Neyor was simply running away from defenders, which left his vision in traffic unevaluated. He projects Neyor as a potential surprise of the 49ers receiving corps within a year or two — better than [[Jordan Watkins]], a bigger play threat than [[Jacob Cowing]], a good enough route runner to be the man-to-man threat [[Jauan Jennings]] hopes to be, and a possible replacement for [[Brandon Aiyuk]] with [[Ricky Pearsall]] at flanker. He caveats that Kittle's evaluation is player-analysis of the big/fast/strong variety, not technical.
- 2025-07-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-28]]) [Dynasty]: 'feel it' — good chance Neyor makes the 49ers active roster, with [[George Kittle]] and a starting cornerback both talking him up, and Waldman treats corner praise as a meaningful signal. He tempers it with the Juwan Winfree precedent: Denver corners praised him similarly and he bounced around a zillion teams without ever starting. On the 'built like Randy Moss, runs like Randy Moss' buzz, Waldman's line is that he still has to learn to catch and run routes like Moss, and could spend three careers without getting there — the Grand Canyon looks close but is thirty miles away. Notably, Waldman says if he had seen enough after-catch reps to grade Neyor positively navigating traffic, he might have been his eighth or ninth ranked receiver on the board this year. He sees a possible starter within a year or two, but not in 2025 given how much this offense demands of its receivers. Bob Harris (untracked) says he has prioritized Neyor in 32-team, 50-man dynasty leagues.
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-22 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: cited as one of the rookies (with [[De'Zhaun Stribling]]) showing well at Bengals OTAs and being talked up as a threat to [[Andrei Iosivas]]'s WR3 job, but Waldman warns this is a textbook case of OTA hype ('audentatitis') that often fades once real defensive reps start in August.
