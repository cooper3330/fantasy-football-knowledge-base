# Isaiah Bond

## Expert Takes

- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]): Bond is a better route runner than people think — smooth, with good route running and good catch-point work — and Waldman puts him in the same upper category of route runners he was discussing. The reservation is explosion: he is not sure about it, and says that if Bond is simply fast enough he could be one of those players we hear about in two or three years. (Waldman briefly confused him with UNLV's Ricky White before correcting himself.)
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: both Waldman and guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) say 'f**k it' to Bond being a better prospect than teammate [[Matthew Golden]], though Waldman calls it close. Waldman thinks Bond has more in his game than Texas and Alabama used — mostly a deep threat and after-the-catch player there — and in isolated snaps he shows the all-around techniques, so Bond is closer to Golden than Waldman expected. McFarland's model is cooler: 36th percentile adjusted production, a 102.4 career quarterback rating when targeted (49th percentile), and a 6.33 Lance Zierlein grade (61st percentile) which helps him. The model does credit Bond for tough target competition at both stops. Net: Bond behind Golden for both, but with untapped route-running upside in Waldman's read.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]) [Dynasty]: after Bond publicly predicted a world-record forty and ran 4.39, Waldman says the miss doesn't move him much. He ranks Bond behind teammate [[Matthew Golden]] but says Bond is nipping at his heels with a path to being the better player: he is the better route runner right now and very good after the catch, and is only clearly behind because his smaller frame limits his blocking upside. Take blocking out of the equation, Waldman says, and Bond is probably slightly better than Golden in a lot of the respects that matter to fantasy GMs. His projection is a high-end WR2 rather than a true primary, capable of doing as well or better than Golden depending on landing spot. Bob Harris (untracked) is the one hung up on the forty time.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): the Texas receiver has a strong array of releases, stacks defenders well on intermediate and vertical routes, and has the long speed of a legitimate deep threat — none of which is news to anyone casually familiar with him. Waldman's less-obvious point of praise is that Bond's route running against man-to-man coverage is really good: he sets up breaks with a wide array of tools, executes precise breaks, and works back to the ball. One of the more unambiguously positive capsules in the entire A-Z run, with no listed technique caveat.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: the hosts split hard. Waldman had Bond close to [[Matthew Golden]] and thought he might end up with the better career — the most notable acceleration of any receiver in the class, sharp flat breaks, accelerates into the top of his stem, good underneath route runner and dangerous after the catch — with the correctable flaws being that he claps at some targets and gets crossed up using an overhand attack on shoulder-height throws away from his frame. Angelo has him below a 10th-percentile grade, citing down-to-down lapses in interest and effort and a visible collapse when the game was not going his way (his 'panic button', the same one he flagged on [[Adonai Mitchell]]), plus struggles securing the ball short-to-intermediate over the middle. Both noted reports of an off-field issue compared around the league to [[Jermaine Burton]]'s, alongside a poor combine, that could push a projected first-rounder (mocked 17-22 last August) to day three. Waldman said he deliberately excludes off-field information from his grades while Angelo incorporates it; Waldman added that a day-three landing spot could be the value play, since a traits-based developmental pick is not expected to start and the fall may force Bond to fix his flaws. Comps for the league's tolerance: Brandon Marshall, Terrell Owens, and last year's [[Jermaine Burton]]/[[Javon Baker]].
- 2025-04-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-08]]) [Dynasty]: pretty mixed on Bond after charting three of his games, and flags serious character concerns that in his view could push him to day three of the draft. What he likes: he can imagine Bond in a [[Tutu Atwell]]-style role stressing a defense with speed, but with more actual receiver ability attached than that archetype usually carries. Framed inside Harmon's broader point that teams this year will be hunting WR2s, WR3s and WR4s rather than true number ones, and that the meat of this class sits in the day-two and day-three tiers.
- 2025-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-16]]): the Texas receiver (5'11, 180) is a pure speed-based, incomplete route runner. He predicted he would break [[Xavier Worthy]]'s 4.21 and ran 4.39, but Harmon says the play speed is real — 69.2% success on the nine, 77% on the post, 80.8% on the dig, with nearly 45% of his routes on the nine/post/dig and 15% dig participation. The switch-release, full-speed motion work reminded Harmon of what [[Tyreek Hill]] is asked to do in Miami, explicitly not a projection that he becomes Hill. The underneath game is the problem: 59% on curls (Koh: 'straight up bad'), 75% on slants, 69% vs man, 75% vs zone (24th percentile), and 57.7% vs press on 15.1% pressed routes. Harmon says he cannot throttle down and work back to the quarterback. Character is the bigger issue — Harmon confirms [[Jermaine Burton]]-level red flags plus a recent sexual assault arrest, and expects that to push him to day three even though the on-field profile is late-day-two. Value case is tactical: like [[Marvin Mims Jr.]], he clears space and can be a design-touches weapon as a third receiver.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21 (Film & Data with Dwain McFarland)]]): Bond is a top-five RSP receiver and one of the three best route runners in the class — a better route runner than [[Matthew Golden]], who is sometimes ranked above him. He accelerates into the top of his stems and uses speed as a 'bully pulpit' to unlock his timing routes, wins over the middle, adjusts and uncovers late the way [[Luther Burden III]] does; Waldman contrasts him with fast receivers who can't decelerate or break back to the ball. His comp is a Ty Hilton-type speedy slot who can also win outside, or '[[Marvin Mims Jr.]] if Mims had technical skills', and he says Bond is like [[Terry McLaurin]] but with better hands. Waldman explains the thin college production as the Henry Ruggs sacrificial-X role at Alabama plus a Texas offense where [[Quinn Ewers]] struggled with the vertical game. The stated risk is entirely off-field — Waldman admits he is 'a magnet' for receivers with off-field problems (Michael Westbrook, Josh Gordon, [[Jermaine Burton]]) and says of Bond, 'you don't know if he's going to be out.' Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) has him WR10, the 88th WR prospect since 2018, with a 70 production rating and 36th-percentile adjusted receiving yards per team pass attempt — a projection pick propped up by Zierlein's 80 film grade.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-18]]) [Dynasty]: with the grand jury returning a no-bill and Cleveland signing him, Waldman is 'feeling it' — Bond reminds him of an aspiring T.Y. Hilton, and if he closes that gap quickly the ceiling comp becomes an aspiring Isaac Bruce. His first burst of speed is more palpable than any receiver in this class, he runs good routes with strong break mechanics on timing patterns, and he catches the ball well; Waldman thinks there is a good chance he ends up as good as or better than [[Matthew Golden]], who is getting sterling reviews. The caveat is organizational: Golden landed in a class organization while Bond landed with a franchise that seems perpetually in turmoil, and the quarterback situation is unresolved, so Waldman puts it at roughly 50-50 that Bond contributes to fantasy lineups this year. He is a clear dynasty buy whom Waldman would confidently rank over [[Jack Bech]] and [[Tre Harris]], and a redraft waiver-wire watch-list stash behind [[Jerry Jeudy]], [[Cedric Tillman]] and [[Diontae Johnson]] in the pecking order.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-29]]): Bond has more upside than [[Cedric Tillman]] — he is 'more of an instant starter type of player' already showing it to a fair degree, with a couple of really nice catches, though he failed to continue a route on one play and nearly caused an interception. With Tillman dealing with a hamstring issue, Waldman sees this as the opening for Bond to solidify a lineup spot. The stated risk is off-field reliability: Waldman says the concern with Bond is whether he is someone you can count on away from the field to fulfil what he can do on it. Bob Harris (not tracked) jokes about Bond promising the fastest 40 time ever and not delivering, and is broadly unexcited about Cleveland's receiving assets given the quarterback situation and clamoring to bench [[Joe Flacco]].
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-02]]): Bond could quickly become Cleveland's second-most compelling passing-down option, and Waldman said he liked him more than [[Matthew Golden]] pre-draft because of his route skills, comping him to a T.Y. Hilton type — better inside but able to give you compelling work outside. He praised how well Bond has played given he had almost no training camp, and rated him a better contested-catch third-level player than [[Jerry Jeudy]]. [[Brandon Angelo]] agreed Bond is the interesting piece precisely because [[Dillon Gabriel]] can deliver to the third level when the operation is clean, but warned Bond will not be bailed out by higher-level playmakers if the pocket is muddy. Waldman's conditional fantasy case: if the run game with [[Quinshon Judkins]] keeps them ahead of the sticks and Bond and Gabriel connect on third-level shots, it opens the whole offense — though he called Browns optimism about Gabriel 'Pollyanna-ish' overall.
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-10-09]]): Bond played 57% of snaps with [[Cedric Tillman]] on IR — Harmon admitted he was 'cheating' by including him in a rookie snap-share breakthrough segment since Bond has played higher shares before — but the notable number was seven targets, the most on the team. Harmon sees him as a speed player with man-coverage ability, particularly on in-breaking routes, who is smaller and probably needs to work off the line of scrimmage, so he is not Cleveland's X candidate. He noted Bond was a day-two caliber prospect who went undrafted and signed very late after turning himself in to police on a sexual assault allegation in April, the month of the draft — serious character flags, but Harmon still considers him a talented player worth tracking. Open question he raised: whether [[Dillon Gabriel]] has the arm to get him the ball downfield, though he thinks he does.
- 2025-10-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-14 (Week 7 Waiver Wire & More W6 Film)]]): Bond drew a bunch of short-to-intermediate targets from [[Dillon Gabriel]] in his first start but didn't catch half of them, and had a deep shot thrown out of bounds intentionally overshot to avoid a pick; a fourth-and-two target later in the game clanged off his helmet.
- 2025-12-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-15]]) [Dynasty]: after a couple of big plays, Waldman called Bond worth 'monitoring' but not adding yet -- he's a talented prospect but a maturity risk (potential to fall asleep in meetings or clash with teammates), and Waldman referenced Cleveland's history with an unnamed past receiver of 'massively higher upside' (explicitly not [[Jermaine Burton]], whom he clarified he wasn't referring to) who couldn't stay professional enough to realize it. If Bond shows reliability over the next 12-18 months, Waldman said he's worth adding in deeper dynasty leagues given the talent.
- 2026-01-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-05]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman gives Bond the talent edge over [[Matthew Golden]] but still takes Golden overall, because Bond carries real off-field reputation risk on top of a dysfunctional organization -- comparing the caution to what he learned the hard way with [[Jermaine Burton]]. Waldman says he'd flip fully onto Bond only if reports surface that he's genuinely turned a corner as a professional; until then he expects Bond to flash brilliantly at moments while Golden quietly climbs.
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-22 (Feel It or Fk It)]]) [Dynasty]: viewed as 'the most explosive element' of Cleveland's young receiver trio (with [[Denzel Boston]] and [[Casey Concepcion]]), and a quicker, faster version of what [[Jerry Jeudy]] offers -- part of why Waldman expects the team to favor the youth movement over Jeudy this season.
- 2026-07-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-31]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Cleveland is talking up Isaiah Bond as their third receiver, and Harmon says that isn't coming out of nowhere -- Bond fell to undrafted free agency purely because of off-field legal issues, not lack of talent, and Harmon evaluates him as more of a third-round-to-early-fourth-round-caliber prospect. He's a name worth tracking as camp buzz builds around [[Cedric Tillman]]'s health questions.
- 2026-08-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-10]]) [Dynasty]: in a straight comparison, Waldman prefers [[Matthew Golden]] over Bond mainly because of Green Bay's superior quarterback play relative to Cleveland's unsettled situation. Separately, on the Browns, Waldman relays that beat writers see [[Denzel Boston]] as the receiver likely to lead the WR corps in targets and catches, but flag Bond as a player worth monitoring for a role -- he should be among the top-20 names on fantasy managers' waiver wire watch lists. Both hosts caution that Cleveland's overall situation, with Deshaun Watson unexpectedly back atop the QB depth chart, is 'a soap opera' worth avoiding for fantasy purposes outside of [[Quinshon Judkins]].
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-20]]): Harmon called Isaiah Bond 'an interesting wide receiver' worth monitoring in a suddenly deep Jets skill-position group, without elaborating further on role or ranking.
