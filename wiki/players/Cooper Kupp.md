# Cooper Kupp

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-01 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]]: not hanging Kupp up, but notably cooler than in past years. Had Kupp as his own fantasy wide receiver one for the stretch he played in 2023 and calls that call "perfectly defensible" in hindsight.
- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode [Redraft (Standard)]: agrees on hanging on but is more explicit about the ceiling change — [[Puka Nacua]]'s emergence caps Kupp's role as the offense's true alpha even once fully healthy. Credits Matthew Stafford's play as a positive for the offense generally, but concludes "Kupp's days of being that true alpha... are over, and you should draft accordingly" — i.e., a tier down from his peak wide-receiver-one valuation.
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]): real concern after a quiet Wild Card game (9 targets, 5 catches, 27 yards) that didn't reflect his target share. Harmon: "the baton has so clearly been passed" to [[Puka Nacua]], and — citing Reception Perception's own in-season man-coverage separation charting — Nacua now separates better versus man coverage than Kupp does. Puts the odds at "a pure 50/50 shot" that multiple high-level ankle injuries (including tightrope surgery) have permanently sapped his explosiveness rather than this being a temporary dip; explicitly rules out a return to his 2021 "sun and moon of the offense" level regardless of outcome. See [[Reception Perception Methodology]].

- 2024-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-05]]): renewed health concern cited as part of the Rams' "justifiable pursuit" wide receiver grade — Kupp "showed some concerning health signs last year," and even when on the field "his charting data did not look the same" from a Reception Perception standpoint, supporting the case that Los Angeles should keep adding to the receiver room rather than stand pat.

- 2024-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-04]]), in a GPS-vs-stopwatch aside during the [[Keon Coleman]] breakdown: notes Kupp, like [[Puka Nacua]] the year before, posted a slow 40 time but a high GPS-tracked top speed at the Senior Bowl — cited as a reason to be skeptical of straight-line 40 times as a talent gauge. Also lands in the historical "inarguable hit" bucket of poor college man/press separators (with [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[Rashee Rice]], Tyler Boyd and Juju Smith-Schuster) who became big slot receivers in the NFL. See [[NFL Combine and Pro Day Skepticism]].
- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]) [Best Ball]: feels a Kupp rebound is coming now that he's healthy, at the expense of some of [[Puka Nacua]]'s target share, with both settling into similar round 5-6 best-ball value.
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: The Rams' late, light receiver draft (Jordan Whittington, after pick 200) tells Harmon they're betting Kupp is fully healthy and back to career-level impact, which makes him nervous given Kupp turns 31 next season, has a lengthy recent injury history, and Matthew Stafford is also aging (36-37). He called the Rams roster 'very fragile' beyond Kupp and [[Puka Nacua]] -- Demarcus Robinson is a solid WR3 but not someone to lean on as a true No. 2, Tutu Atwell is undersized, and the tight end room (Tyler Higbee coming off a serious injury, Davis Allen, Colby Parkinson) lacks real needle-movers. If Kupp misses time, Harmon said he'd be 'suddenly a little worried' about the whole passing offense.
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): projects Kupp second on the Rams in targets (~110) and receptions (~73) but only a distant second in yardage (887 yards, 7 TDs) behind [[Puka Nacua]], because Waldman projects games played (13, based on recent injury history at age 31) rather than assuming a full 17-game slate. He called Kupp 'playable, startable' when healthy but 'a ticking time bomb' for roster construction -- if he stays fully healthy and the Rams' bullish June rhetoric holds, he could close the gap with Nacua, but Waldman's base case has [[DeMarcus Robinson]] running close behind him in production.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon's not-yet-published Reception Perception charting on Kupp's 2023 season showed a real decline versus man coverage coming off his injury-plagued year, though his zone-coverage success rate remained strong at 83.3% -- down from the 2nd-highest zone success rate Harmon has charted since 2014, two years earlier. His slant rate jumped from 10.3% of charted routes in 2022 to 22.7% last year, reflecting a shift toward quicker, safer routes. Harmon still projects Kupp as a quality secondary receiver behind [[Puka Nacua]] when healthy, and both hosts noted his deeper out-breaking/corner routes remain a big-play source, but guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn (not tracked) flagged the Rams' line as only middling in true drop-back pass protection despite elite run blocking, and both agreed health for Kupp and Nacua is the swing risk given a lack of proven depth behind them.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): Waldman had been lower on Kupp than most this offseason but raised him back to roughly a top-15 receiver, calling him the better value of the two Rams at WR23 ADP versus [[Puka Nacua]] near the 10th pick overall, and expecting that gap to close within weeks. He has Kupp WR16 on his Footballguys board, one spot ahead of Nacua. The camp reporting behind the move: Jeremy Fowler of ESPN and Kirk Morrison of SiriusXM both described Kupp getting clear WR1 treatment with [[Matthew Stafford]] barely looking elsewhere in a session, evoking his triple-crown year. Waldman's caveat is that the supporting cast is different now — during the triple-crown season Kupp had no Nacua, no [[DeMarcus Robinson]] and no run game the staff trusted, so he expects a more balanced distribution even with Kupp fully healthy.
- 2024-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-06]]): Kupp's success rate vs man fell to 65.2% from 70.6% in 2022, the one big decrease in his profile, while his 83.3% vs zone remains excellent but still below his absurd prior marks — 2021 was the second-highest zone score ever charted. Harmon frames it as a warning sign rather than a cliff: he does not expect the 2021 or early-2022 player to walk back through the door, notes the drop is comparable to [[Keenan Allen]]'s and to Allen Robinson's late-Chicago dip that turned out to matter, and observes Kupp's numbers improved in the back half as he got further from injury. He considers the number-one target role already ceded to [[Puka Nacua]]. James Koh (not tracked) is far harsher, calling Harmon euphemistic: a 70% to 65% man drop is a chunky dip, and the route-level decline is a red flag in a McVay offense — dig 76% to 59%, slant 80% to 72% — with the ankle and foot injury history unlikely to go away.
- 2024-08-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-12]]): he is moving Kupp back up. Camp reports say Kupp looks like he is back in peak shape, and with [[Puka Nacua]] hurt Waldman expects Kupp to draw a lot more of [[Matthew Stafford]]'s attention, with some of the remaining looks going to [[DeMarcus Robinson]] and the like. His reasoning leans on the distinction between being healthy enough to be on the field and execute assignments and being healthy enough to produce at your top athletic level — even if Nacua returns Week 1, Waldman expects at minimum a two-to-four-week window, possibly longer, where Nacua is not quite himself, and that window is Kupp's opportunity.
- 2024-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): he tweeted, and repeats here, that neither Kupp nor [[Puka Nacua]] finishes as a top-12 fantasy receiver this season. No team did he sour on more between early and late August than the Rams, which he calls the most fragile offense in the league and a house of cards: right tackle time missed, Larrick Jackson suspended two games, Jonah Jackson banged up, plus [[Matthew Stafford]], [[Kyren Williams]], Nacua and Kupp all carrying injury risk. He concedes injury-prone as a concept is overstated, but says these are specifically guys who have dealt with injuries. He also believes Kupp at this stage of his career plays almost exclusively inside.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): Kupp's 21-target Sunday night had the timeline calling him the overall WR1, but Harmon is wary — it 'feels very 2022 Rams,' with a bad offensive line, 20 targets and roughly eight yards per reception, though he grants McVay is in a better place schematically and personally than in 2022. On usage, Harmon cites his 2023 Reception Perception charting: an 83.3% success rate versus zone (a top-eight mark among players he charted), only 8.1% of routes against press (one of the lowest rates in years, which helped him), snaps behind the line of scrimmage up to a career-high 81.9%, and slot rate up to 60.8% from 46.6% in 2022. In Week 1 he took just 25% of snaps outside. Both Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) doubt Kupp can hold up as a flanker at age 31, and Harmon adds that piling blocking and rushing usage on top of 20 targets a game is too much to ask now.
- 2024-10-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-17]]): Kupp is very close to returning — possibly Week 7, Week 8 at the latest — with [[Puka Nacua]] also expected back in a couple of weeks. Harmon's caution is usage, not health: the Rams were the number one offender on his targets-versus-points rule, with Kupp taking 21 targets in a Week 1 game the Rams scored 20 points in. Throwing to Kupp is a good thing, he says, but you have to have other receivers to throw to. He notes Kupp will reclaim the slot, which squeezes [[Jordan Whittington]], and he still wants the [[Tutu Atwell]] designed shot plays kept in the offense once Kupp is back. Harmon also observes that Rams condensed splits and heavy pre-snap motion blur alignment data, so outside receivers there run slot-like route trees.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Kupp has fallen off the map and is not even a factor in these playoff games — Harmon called it crazy how little of a difference maker he is now. He took the under on 40.5 receiving yards against Philadelphia, noting the Rams were trying to get Tyler Higbee designed targets down the field ahead of Kupp and that Higbee was expected back. He also pointed to the earlier meeting between the teams, in which Philadelphia blotted Kupp out entirely while doing less to [[Puka Nacua]], because Nacua is the pre-snap motion guy. Harmon's framing is that the Rams are now an alpha-receiver offense and the alpha is Nacua, not Kupp.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]): after a rough run of seasons, Waldman says he would probably invest in Kupp as a WR3 next year — and probably regret it — but calls that a reasonable expectation for him. His comp is [[Adam Thielen]]: a type of player who in the right circumstances can still give you strong starter production, and whose career is likely to drag on longer than the aging formulas predict. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) partially disagreed, saying he tends to agree with the reasoning but may let somebody else be the hero and go younger, while admitting he will be tempted.
- 2025-01-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-21]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon does not think Kupp, on a huge cap hit at 32, is on the Rams next year — and says it does not really matter that much, because he had not been an impact player down the stretch. Once Kupp was dangled at the trade deadline (nearly moved before the Vikings loss) Harmon stopped considering him part of the team's future plans regardless of how he finished. He argues what this offense needs is not what Kupp provides: more speed and man-coverage-beating ability outside, even though the Rams' most successful receiver archetype has been the gritty dirty-work type — Robert Woods, Kupp, [[Puka Nacua]]. He notes [[Tyler Higbee]] was a bigger part of the playoff game plan and it never felt wrong.
- 2025-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-05]]): the Rams telling Kupp they will seek a trade immediately most likely ends in a release — Harmon says the odds favor him being cut, since a team with no leverage and $22.2M of dead cap on the books rarely finds a partner. Harmon still rates peak Kupp as one of the most singular players in Reception Perception history: 80%+ success rate vs zone every year from 2017-2022, a 91.4% mark in 2021 that is the second-best zone season he has ever charted, and 87.3% (96th percentile) in 2022. What made those two years nuclear was a leap vs man and press. That is what left: man-coverage success fell to 65.2% in 2023 while his off-line snap rate hit a career-high 81.9%. Entering his age-32 season Harmon says Kupp can no longer play all three receiver spots, cannot play X, and must be a 70-80% interior player who blocks — closer to a [[Jauan Jennings]] or Marcus Colston facsimile than a 1B. Harmon also notes his skills are redundant with [[Puka Nacua]] and that McVay falls out of love hard. Fits Harmon and James Koh liked: Jacksonville (Liam Coen, dump-off for Trevor Lawrence, run blocking next to [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]), Houston (Nick Caley, better blocker than [[Stefon Diggs]]), Tampa Bay if [[Chris Godwin]] walks, Minnesota (Kevin O'Connell's stated want for his own Jauan Jennings), New England ($120M in cap room, a binky for [[Drake Maye]]) and Washington. Both were cooler on Denver and Cincinnati.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): with the Rams moving on from Kupp and [[Tutu Atwell]] not expected back, Waldman calls losing Kupp a big blow specifically for [[Matthew Stafford]], who at the tail end of his career needs the savviest and most experienced weapons available, and says the vacated Rams WR2 and tight end roles are worth paying close attention to. He doubts a Stafford trade actually happens, but offers a hypothetical he likes: the Giants sending draft capital and [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] for Kupp and Stafford to pair with [[Malik Nabers]] would make them a legitimate wild card. He also names Kupp, along with [[Chris Godwin]], as the kind of complement [[Russell Wilson]] would want in New York.
- 2025-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-19]]): with the Rams expected to move on, Harmon and James Koh (not tracked) debate Minnesota as a landing spot, since the Vikings' clearest need is a slot who does dirty work in the run game rather than a high-target earner — the 'their version of [[Jauan Jennings]]' idea Harmon says he has heard from inside the league. Koh likes it: Kupp is a lunch-pail veteran presence who knows Kevin O'Connell's system inside out, would slide straight in, blocks hard enough to sell two-receiver run/pass looks with [[Justin Jefferson]], and could be valuable on as few as four targets a game while resetting expectations after the triple-crown context in LA. Harmon's counter is target competition on a young quarterback: with [[J.J. McCarthy]] taking over from [[Sam Darnold]], adding Kupp to Jefferson, [[Jordan Addison]] and [[T.J. Hockenson]] risks a 2024 Bears situation where the roster makes sense in Madden and fantasy but not in team building.
- 2025-02-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-26]]): Kupp will not be on the 2025 Rams — Harmon called it clear, with the only open question being whether he is traded or outright cut if no pick can be had. On value, Harmon says Kupp can still be a useful player somewhere but was not a high-impact one in 2024, especially down the stretch: he ranked 42nd in first downs per route run among receivers with 250-plus routes, sandwiched between [[Christian Watson]] and [[Quentin Johnston]], a bucket Harmon characterizes as sporadic, inconsistent big-play types alongside [[Jaylen Waddle]] and [[Jerry Jeudy]]. He judges that instructive of where Kupp is now — no longer a receiver defenses game-plan around. Harmon separately flagged Kupp as one of the best blocking receivers in the league, a real loss for the Rams beyond the pass-catching. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) defended him as still a credible zone-beating threat with elite spacing awareness.
- 2025-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-12]]): discussing the Rams' addition of [[Davante Adams]], Harmon states flatly that Adams is an automatic upgrade on Kupp because Kupp at this point cannot play outside and can no longer beat man coverage — Adams gives Los Angeles far more against man and from an outside alignment. It is a brief but pointed aside within the alignment segment rather than a full charting breakdown.
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]): after the Rams released him, Kupp is 'definitely a declining player' who is now a slot-only, underneath-game contributor going into his age-32 season. Harmon's in-progress charting shows trouble uncovering against man even in Week 1 before the injuries — he was pummeled with targets but on low-area stuff — while quick separation against zone and quick man routes near the goal line still show up. Kupp is 'absolutely not' a number one anymore; he was surpassed by [[Puka Nacua]], and being slot-locked is a big part of why LA moved on beyond salary. Harmon is skeptical of the [[Dallas]] and Seattle smoke since both already have a primary slot — Dallas needs perimeter ball-winners or a vertical threat, which Kupp is 'quite literally neither'. Koh notes he ranked 106th among qualified WRs in EPA per target, company that includes [[Van Jefferson]], [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]] and [[Amari Cooper]]; Koh still rates him a strong red-zone touchdown maker with sure hands.
- 2025-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-19]]): pressed by Koh to say it plainly, Harmon calls Kupp's 2024 charting a disaster-level profile against man and press — 50.5% success vs man (third percentile) and 41.4% vs press (third percentile), back to his poor early-career levels, with zone success down to 76.2% from 83% in 2023 and 91.4% in 2021 (still second-best in the RP database). The in-breaking routes that are the base of the [[Sean McVay]] offense have evaporated: 64.7% on slants (a route he ran on 14.4% of routes, where Harmon wants ~80%), versus 81% slants, 85.2% digs and 87.1% posts in 2021. He is red on nearly every route except the out, flat and screen, and remains effective on free-release out-breaking routes toward the sideline. Alignment: 86.9% of snaps off the line of scrimmage, up from 81.9%, one of only eight such seasons in the database alongside Jordan Matthews, [[Wan'Dale Robinson]], Jarvis Landry, Golden Tate and Jamison Crowder — i.e. a slot-only specialist needing extreme role catering. Harmon finds Seattle's three-year, $45M deal hard to explain, suspects funny money making it effectively a one-to-two-year deal, and says the Rams' ruthless, callous push-out — plus zero interest from Rams-offshoot Jacksonville — should alarm Seattle. Best case: a Golden Tate season as a blocking, reliable check-down option for [[Sam Darnold]]. Koh: 'I don't understand how this guy can be successful in the NFL in 2025.'
- 2025-06-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-11]]): on the basis of Kupp's current Reception Perception profile, he now has to be an almost slot-exclusive player in Seattle — that is where Harmon says you maximize what is left of him, and both hosts expect him to play primarily in the slot with [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] pushed outside. Harmon also uses Kupp as a measuring stick against [[Puka Nacua]] and pushes back on the idea that Nacua simply replaced him in Los Angeles: Kupp's best charted season does not come close to Nacua's 76% success rate versus man coverage, so Nacua is 'his own entity' in that offense rather than a like-for-like successor. In discussing condensed formations, Harmon uses Kupp as the archetype of the on-the-line slot who can chip in the run game and release to the flat.
- 2025-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]): Kupp is a severely declined player and the Reception Perception trend line is stark — success rate versus man of 70.6% (2022), 75.2% (2023) and 50.5% (2024), with versus-zone falling 87.3% to 83.3% to 76% over the same span. Harmon's conclusion is that he has to be a slot-only or at least slot-mostly, off-the-line player in Seattle: he cannot play X at all and would be far too easy to jam up on the line of scrimmage. That constraint is what forces [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] outside more and creates the need for a field-stretching X like [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]]. Harmon separately notes that Kupp's disappearance from the Rams' target distribution while Tyler Higbee got spammed was his first signal that Los Angeles was done with him.
- 2025-08-18 — According to Bob Harris (co-host, not a tracked expert) on ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-18]]): Seattle reporting on Kupp has been that he looks fantastic now that he is healthy. Harris cited Greg Bell of the Tacoma News Tribune, who told him there is a reason that guy was really good and it is visible every day in practice — the cuts, the moves and the routes are back, and people are having a very hard time defending him. Harris's caveat is the obvious one: he has to stay healthy. He mentioned Kupp alongside [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] as the established quality in a receiver room where [[Tory Horton]] is pushing [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]].
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: The July question in Seattle was whether beat reporting on how Kupp and [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] were deployed would change how you drafted them. It sounded all August like both were moving all over the formation, but Harris concludes the market simply is not buying that Kupp has much left — despite a quiet and healthy August he barely stayed inside the top 100, while Smith-Njigba held round-three range.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): [[Sam Darnold]] did enough to get Kupp rolling in the second half — a stretch of about four catches on five plays, mostly on his little dig and slant routes — finishing seven for 90, which Harris says 'certainly re-injects some life into his stock.' The caveat is Darnold himself: he threw an interception looking for Kupp with Jalen Ramsey standing right there, and Harris says it would be fair to question whether Darnold looked good enough to trust secondary weapons in Seattle week to week.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): Kupp caught a screen down to the three in Seattle's rout of New Orleans, but the film note was unflattering and unprompted — 'Cup doesn't look fast out there' — offered as part of the argument that [[Tory Horton]], who plays in Seattle's base three-wide look, might be the more interesting long-term piece.
- 2025-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-25]]): Kupp is a diminished version of the player from a few years ago with one good game in three, and almost all of his production has come underneath versus zone — flats, quick game, check-and-release, essentially the role he had early in his Rams career. Harmon says that may be the only role he has left on this team and that it is a lot of money for it in theory, but it is defensible: [[Sam Darnold]] can get into panic mode under pressure and having Kupp reliably open underneath helps, and as long as Seattle has two players who win at all three levels in [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] and a developing [[Tory Horton]], they can carry Kupp in that role. Koh (not tracked) frames it more positively — he argues Horton's emergence is rejuvenating Kupp because defenses are stretching to the edges, leaving Kupp the middle of the field, and that the trio is now hard to guard down to down.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-30]]): Harmon calls him 'the shadow of Cooper Kupp' — 162 yards through four games — and takes [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] over him comfortably rest of season. His view is that Kupp is a nice role player at this point in his career who will never threaten [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]]'s orbit-guy status in Seattle; he thinks it is likelier that rookie [[Tory Horton]], who is having a nice season, eventually usurps Kupp than that Kupp usurps JSN as the lead target, though he doubts Horton does it either given blocking limitations.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-04]]): Kupp missed Sunday Night with a hamstring injury and Harmon notes he is a 30-plus-year-old receiver with an injury history who could miss more time. Production has been thin — one good receiving game all year (about 90 yards versus Pittsburgh) and, Harmon believes, no other 50-yard game — though Kupp still matters as a blocker in the run game, a safety outlet for [[Sam Darnold]], and a body who blurs 11 and 12 personnel. Harmon's problem is that Kupp is not a credible vertical/comeback threat on the perimeter, so he does not unlock moving JSN around the way [[Tory Horton]] does. Both hosts take the mentorship seriously rather than dismissing it as broadcast talk: [[Puka Nacua]] told Harmon after his rookie year that Kupp taught him professionalism, and JSN credited him on air. James Koh (not a tracked expert) says Kupp already looks like a coach on the sideline.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-06]]): Kupp is a 30-plus-year-old receiver with a lengthy injury history now dealing with a hamstring/heel issue that could be a multi-week absence, and he 'has not been impactful as a receiver' — one game over 60 receiving yards and four under 35 on the season. Harmon still expects him to play when available as the big slot because he is important to the offense, and thinks he could out-snap [[Tory Horton]] for the third/fourth receiver job in 11 personnel, though not [[Rashid Shaheed]]. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) reads the Shaheed trade as a possible signal about Kupp's standing: the deal 'makes so much sense if Cooper Kupp isn't on the team,' so he wonders whether Seattle believes the injury is more serious than reported or is simply done with him. Harmon does not endorse that inference but does not dispute the production picture.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-10]]): Kupp's Sunday performance was an 'I'm not dead yet' moment. The mechanism Waldman describes is [[Rashid Shaheed]] aligning to the same side and clearing space for Kupp to work underneath, which he expects to keep producing opportunities rather than being a one-week spike.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): Kupp took a sideline check-down from [[Sam Darnold]] up 28-0 that nobody tackled and turned it into a big gain inside the five, but Harris calls him 'forgotten man Cooper Kupp, who for the moment you should continue to forget'. He explicitly says he would still roster [[Rashid Shaheed]] - who is getting his feet wet and will 'be in the sadness for the time being' - over Kupp.
- 2026-01-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-27]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Kupp has been a genuinely 'contributing piece' for Seattle this season even though he's clearly 'not what he was' at his peak, part of GM John Schneider's broader offseason additions around [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] and [[Sam Darnold]] that helped get the Seahawks to the Super Bowl.
- 2026-01-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-29]]: Harmon reiterates a preseason call that Kupp was in severe decline as a receiver by 2025, but says he still brought real value as a blocker, which helped Seattle blur the line between receiver and tight-end usage in their personnel packages even as his receiving role shrank.
- 2026-02-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-09]]): Kupp had a 'nice veteran performance' in the Super Bowl, leading Seattle in targets, catches and yards (12 targets, 6 catches, 61 yards), helping keep the chains moving as part of Seattle's early game-plan attack on a slot-size mismatch.
- 2026-07-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman projected [[Cooper Kupp]] could be a 'distant third' in Seattle's receiving offense this year behind [[Rashid Shaheed]], who he believes Seattle acquired specifically to be a featured receiver rather than a complementary deep threat. Bob Harris (untracked co-host) responded with a 'sad trombone' acknowledgment of Kupp's declining outlook.
