# Dalton Kincaid

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): "I'm still a Kincaid over [[Sam LaPorta]] guy" despite LaPorta's rookie-year production being clearly ahead of Kincaid's — his stated principle is that a tight end's year-one output is usually not his final product. Had Kincaid ranked 5th overall pre-draft and post-draft (LaPorta 11th), calling Kincaid the exception to the rule that rookie tight ends don't produce — a call he says "didn't turn out to be" fully vindicated in year one, though Kincaid still finished a startable TE1 (roughly 70 catches on pace). His long-term case: once Buffalo unlocks Kincaid's vertical, contested-catch game, "it's all over" — a "primary wide receiver in a tight end's body" comparable to prime Rob Gronkowski or Travis Kelce.

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: yes, a top-5 tight end for 2024 — argues Buffalo's offense has room for three top-15/20-caliber producers between Kincaid, [[Khalil Shakir]] and [[James Cook]]. Notes Kincaid getting more intermediate targets (20-40 yard gains) over the final weeks of 2023 after Buffalo "steadfastly refused" to throw him anything past 10 yards for the first half of the season, and says he wouldn't be surprised if Kincaid challenges [[Sam LaPorta]] for the second-year tight end production lead. Explicitly rejects the "Dawson Knox blocks Kincaid's role" framing as reducing a multi-variable offense to a single variable.

- 2024-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-30]]: Kincaid's rookie season (675 yards, 73 catches, two TDs) ranked 11th all-time among rookie tight ends, just behind Rob Gronkowski's 2010 campaign — "even as disappointing as it might have been for what he could have been, he was still 11th-best all time." Waldman's diagnosis, restated: Buffalo was too conservative with Kincaid, limiting him to a checkdown/underneath role when he was "elite... as an aerial ball winner up the seam" — "there was no way I was thinking Buffalo would be that conservative." Had Kincaid been used that way, Waldman thinks he'd have joined [[Sam LaPorta]] near the top of the rookie-production list instead of settling for 11th.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]): if there's nothing behind the scenes changing the plan, Kincaid is Buffalo's wide receiver one or two in the passing game as a tight end. Reiterates Buffalo underused his two best traits as a rookie — run-after-catch ability in the secondary and winning contested catches in the air — instead leaning on flat routes and dump-offs, similar to how the team eased James Cook in as a rookie. Repeats that Kincaid had (per this episode's framing) the 12th-best rookie season ever for a tight end, and worries casual fans who don't follow the RSP will miss that under the [[Sam LaPorta]] buzz. Rejects the "traditional tight end is a limiting factor" framing outright — teams have fielded two productive tight ends before (Patriots and others) — and says it's about role and opportunity, not a hard ceiling.
- 2024-07-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]): he ranks the top four tight ends (Travis Kelce, Sam LaPorta, Mark Andrews, Trey McBride) above Kincaid and Kyle Pitts, and says he's 'probably at market' with Kincaid rather than above it -- he'd rather target McBride, whom he sees with a stronger target-share outlook. He backs this with a market trend JJ Zachariason (not tracked) cited: since 2014, no top-12 ADP tight end who failed to hit 10 PPR points/game the prior season went on to hit 10+ in the current season, though most such cases were low-end TE1s, a softer flag for Kincaid specifically as a year-two breakout candidate. Waldman projects Kincaid around a 22% target share and 120-135 targets, enough to lead Buffalo's target board, but flags two risks: his 2023 surge came largely without a healthy Dawson Knox on the field, and it depends on OC Joe Brady using more condensed/heavy personnel like the sets that fueled LaPorta, Kittle and Kelce. He also noted Buffalo philosophically pivoted away from Stefon Diggs before trading him, which could open more overall passing-game targets.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon noted Kincaid ran more slot routes than any other highly-targeted tight end last season, calling him another Buffalo weapon best maximized from the slot -- part of a receiver-room puzzle Harmon expects to feature several players in the 80-90 target range rather than one clear top option, aided by an ascending Bills offensive line (ranked 13th by guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn, not tracked) and new OC Joe Brady's stated emphasis on the run game and play-action to reduce the burden on [[Josh Allen]].
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]): flatly rejects the Joe Buscaglia report that fantasy managers should proceed with caution on Kincaid because of Dawson Knox. Waldman grants Knox is a good all-around player, but says the best option for getting open, catching the ball and generating yards after catch in the middle of the field is Kincaid, who can also beat Knox at winning downfield at the catch point — something Buffalo did not exploit last year. He expects the Bills to do enough to work Kincaid in as the offense's primary option. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) agrees, citing Ryan O'Halloran's view that Kincaid will be the most targeted receiving asset in the offense and that the things Buffalo wanted to do with him last year will be fully implemented in 2024.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Kincaid being persona non grata in week one is a disturbing sign, especially since people inside the organization had told the broadcast crew he would be the Bills' primary receiver this year and it simply didn't happen. Waldman's advice is nonetheless to hold rather than sell low — give it a couple of weeks and see how it develops, noting Buffalo faces a short turnaround to a Thursday night game against Miami and may have been managing players. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) said he is not unexcited about his Kincaid shares and expects it to be fine.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): at 7.3 fantasy points per game Kincaid is not what drafters signed up for, and Waldman is unsure it gets better — indeed he says 'this is what I expected from Dalton Kincaid.' The diagnosis: most of his production is flat routes where he turns outside off the line and wins with yards after the catch, which he does very well, but he rarely draws downfield targets even when run vertically. Like [[Mark Andrews]] against the Chiefs, defenses now treat Kincaid as Buffalo's most dangerous passing-down weapon and prioritize him against zone, which is why underneath players like [[Khalil Shakir]] get the wide-open looks. Waldman wants [[Josh Allen]] to force him a few contested opportunities but does not believe Allen trusts him that way yet. Advice: hold rather than sell into a lowball, and if you do trade him, wait for a couple of good games and interrogate what those games actually consisted of.
- 2024-10-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon is skeptical of the whole premise of building a passing game around a tight end as the number-one target — it has essentially only worked with [[Travis Kelce]] and Gronkowski, and Kincaid, while a nice player, is probably not Kelce, so those expectations are too much. Kincaid does lead Buffalo with 13 targets over the last two weeks but Harmon calls the result a neutered offense with a move-around number one. Both hosts hammer Joe Brady's snap distribution: no Bills receiver and no tight end played more than 65% of snaps in Week 5. Koh (not tracked) contrasts that with [[Dalton Schultz]] at 98.5% in the same game and argues that if the staff truly believes Kincaid is a difference-maker he needs to be at 85-90%. Harmon adds that Allen just barely overthrew Kincaid on what would have been a walk-in touchdown.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10]]): unbelievably misused — [[Brock Bowers]] is being deployed the way Kincaid should be, even though Kincaid is not the athlete Bowers is. [[Brandon Angelo]] agreed completely, saying that based on the Utah tape Kincaid has the ability to be one of the true three-level tight ends: enough speed to beat you up the seam, excellent in contested-catch situations when allowed to win one-on-one, and better after the catch than people think. Both noted the odd sequence against Houston where [[Josh Allen]] went to him three straight times without connecting, plus a disputed catch-no-catch. Waldman's prescription for Joe Brady is either to make Allen take the short stuff to Kincaid as a chain-mover, or to lean the other way and design multiple downfield jump-ball options per play — Kincaid and [[Keon Coleman]] can both win those — so Allen has a menu instead of one read.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): prompted by his friend Alex, Waldman went back to the tape and agrees [[Josh Allen]] is a poor match for Kincaid — Allen is either hesitant to throw where he should on certain routes, or the placement is bad when he does. His theory is that Allen thrives with short, quick receivers who change direction fast and can keep up with his own improvisation. Kincaid is not awful with Allen, but 'there's something missing here, it's just not clicking fully,' and it is frustrating. The counterfactual: imagine Kincaid with [[Joe Burrow]] in the Mike Gesicki role and Waldman thinks he would be a top-five and probably top-three tight end easily. Bob Harris (not tracked) counters that Kincaid is the most targeted receiver on the roster heading into this week, so he is not valueless. Kincaid hurt his knee in this game, adding risk for the coming week.
- 2025-01-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-27]]) [Dynasty]: Kincaid's disappointing season is 'usage', not ability. Waldman calls him a go-up-and-get-it player along the lines of what we sometimes see from [[Brock Bowers]], a player you want attacking the seams and winning the ball over safeties — and [[Josh Allen]] simply does not make those throws to him, while he does trust [[Keon Coleman]] to do that work up the boundary. Bob Harris (not tracked) was more open to the idea that Kincaid has never emerged, though he allowed it may be as much about expectations as ability. On the AFC Championship's final play bouncing off Kincaid's forearm, Waldman defended him — 'fuck that' to Tony Romo putting it on Kincaid, it was a genuinely difficult play. Both agree Buffalo could get more out of him by targeting him the way he is best used.
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): Kincaid failed to come up with the wide-open downfield throw on the decisive fourth down, though Harmon declines to slam him — it was a ball he had to come back to with traffic around him. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is harsher on the acquisition than the player: moving up in the first round implies you believed you were getting a coverage-warping move tight end, even a prime Hunter Henry, and what Buffalo actually has is 'just a guy' — a really solid player, but 'a clear miss' relative to the trade-up cost. Harmon adds the broader frame that the fantasy market's error was expecting Kincaid to become the No. 1 read in the concept, which almost no tight end is. See [[Tight End as Number One Read]].
- 2025-02-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-28]]): Kincaid 'hasn't really developed into a needle mover' — he is fine and solid, and Harmon thinks he has taken too much hate for the late Bills-Chiefs drop and for busting as a high fantasy pick, but the archetype no longer fits the team. Buffalo transitioned from the Brian Daboll/Ken Dorsey spread-and-shred era to Joe Brady's run-heavy, big-bodies offense, and neither Kincaid nor Dawson Knox (a solid but unexceptional blocker on a bonkers 14.5M cap hit in his age-29 season) fits that well. Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) both called it strange to pay Knox and then spend that draft capital on Kincaid; Koh added that Kincaid looks physically small and needs the weight room. Harmon: the Bills are caught between two identities.
- 2025-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-07]]): Kincaid is the second cautionary tale for the tight-end-as-receiver idea. Harmon says he has not been a bad player but simply has not been a needle mover heading into year three, and that the structural problem is that he is a big slot receiver and a small tight end — he joked he would bet his 401k that he outweighs Kincaid at 6-3, 252, and said flatly that a player that skinny cannot play tight end. The result is being squeezed from both directions: completely blocked in the slot by [[Khalil Shakir]], a fifth-round pick Harmon thought should have gone on day two, and blocked inline by Dawson Knox. Koh (not tracked) agreed the slot-receiver experiment has not been as productive as expected, though stopped short of calling it a failure and noted there is still time in year three.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): asked whether Kincaid's Buffalo career is essentially dead, Waldman said 'oh fuck yes it's dead.' His reasoning is a fit problem, not a talent problem — what Kincaid does best is win 'throw me open' targets, and Joe Brady's spread-it-around offense will not feed that. Waldman went as far as saying that if [[Kyle Pitts]] does not work out in Atlanta, the Falcons should come 'break this guy loose from the jail that's Buffalo's offense', because [[Michael Penix Jr.]] throwing receivers open would be a match made in heaven with Kincaid. Absent a rash of injuries forcing Brady to abandon the whack-a-mole approach, he does not see Kincaid reaching the zenith of his potential. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) allowed that Kincaid has looked reasonably good in camp but agreed the bigger-picture usage is the limiting factor, which is why he is a near-free late-round square.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): 'fuck that' on the idea that Kincaid finally breaks out in 2025. The Week 1 touchdown was a nice throw from [[Josh Allen]] and a good catch, but unless Buffalo uses Kincaid the way it used [[Keon Coleman]] on Sunday night, he stays what he has been — a low-end TE1 / high-end TE2, and possibly interchangeable with [[Jackson Hawes]], whom Waldman graded as the best blocker in that RSP class. Co-host Bob Harris (Footballguys, not a tracked expert) agreed, adding that Joe Brady's stated intent is to throw to the open man rather than a designated go-to guy — the approach that produced Allen's lowest career interception rate last season, and structurally a cap on any one Bills pass catcher.
- 2025-09-19 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-19]]): Kincaid belongs in the top 10 at tight end 'just because he's attached to Allen,' and Harris says the dozen 'are you sure I should start Dalton Kincaid' questions he fielded turned out pretty well. Kincaid scored on the opening drive when [[Josh Allen]] faked a screen to spring him in the red zone, nearly had a second on an out-and-up from the 18 (the crowd booed the incompletion before the replay showed a lunging defensive breakup), and caught a screen to the one to open the third quarter. Harris's criticism: outweighing Minkah Fitzpatrick by more than 30 pounds, Kincaid should not have been stoned short of the goal line — 'can't you wriggle, can't you spin?' — and [[James Cook]] vultured the one-yard score instead. He notes tight end remains 'the world's worst position,' most of them a kicker-level dart throw.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-06]]): asked whether Kincaid stays a top-five tight end this year, Waldman said he is 'starting to feel it.' The reason is usage, not the usage he expected — it is not the go-up-and-get-it downfield ball he thought Kincaid would get from [[Josh Allen]] coming out of Utah, but work over the middle and in the intermediate range where most teams feed primary options. He is no longer the shallow dump-off-to-the-flat guy: more corner routes, more deep overs, and plays where he and Allen are on the same page so that when the first break is covered he immediately slides to the next window and Allen finds him — Waldman saw that three times in one game. Waldman credited [[Joe Brady]] for stopping using a good route runner and pass catcher as a de facto running back in the flat. Bob Harris (not tracked) cited 20 catches, 287 yards and three touchdowns through five games, the 287 yards second among tight ends behind [[Tyler Warren]].
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Kincaid had 'a very big night down the seam' against New England, but Harris's stated problem with him is volatility — 'just last week he did one for 28, so it's tough to know the weeks that's going to happen.' He would still take [[Hunter Henry]] over Kincaid right now, while allowing that both belong in starting consideration at what he repeatedly calls fantasy's dumbest position.
- 2025-11-03 — According to guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert) on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): Kincaid 'has been a TE1, still has not gotten more than six targets in any one game', and could easily string together a run of TE19, TE28, TE17 finishes. His touchdown here came on a corner route where the defender fell down, which Doherty says is 'just as sustainable as the [[Colston Loveland]] 58-yard touchdown where the Bengals defense simultaneously retired' — 'I'm now betting my entire fantasy season on Dalton Kincaid scoring on corner routes when the defender falls down. That doesn't feel good.' Context: Buffalo used tons of two-tight-end sets and ran 29 running back carries.
- 2025-11-07 — According to guest Jim McCormick (not a tracked expert) on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-07]] [Redraft (Standard)]: Kincaid is 'the pure variance tight end' and made his pivotal-five list. McCormick rosters him in a few spots and says it never feels good to start him — he gets only three to six targets, never more, and has to catch balls that travel 68 yards to pay off. He frames it as hard to remember a quarterback as good as [[Josh Allen]] with so few usable fantasy options around him: 'can't I just get a slice of Josh Allen's pizza?' [[Chris Harris]] agrees and adds the specific warning that the two-target, one-catch game is coming — 'it's going to happen to him three more times this year, I don't know which games.' Later, both suggest Kincaid is the kind of depth piece you would package to a contender chasing [[Bucky Irving]] or [[Ricky Pearsall]] upside.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): Kincaid caught back-to-back crossers on nice throws in the second quarter, then left in the third quarter with a hamstring injury and did not return. Harris says that 'probably removes the touchdown seeking temptation out of him for a little while.'
