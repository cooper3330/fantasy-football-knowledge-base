# Troy Franklin

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — Guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Dynasty]: a player he genuinely likes with a floor that scares him — real speed, a growing feel for route leverage, and good (not great) downfield ball tracking. The specific worry: he disappears against physicality — cites a Washington game where a single physical cornerback (Jabbar Muhammad) erased him — and, being a slightly built receiver who isn't going to add functional bulk without losing speed, that vulnerability isn't fixable by simply getting stronger. Illustrates the NFL-speed bar he'll be measured against with a Kansas City practice anecdote: Trent McDuffie, "the fastest lateral player maybe ever," putting [[Tyreek Hill]] "in the dirt" on a route. Names two outcome comps as the real range: [[Jordan Addison]] (a similarly built receiver who thrived because his scheme created easy releases and didn't force him into contested/physical reps) versus a marginal role player like Mecole Hardman — the swing factor being scheme fit and release timing more than talent.

- 2024-02-29 — According to guest **Eric Froton** (NBC Sports college football analyst — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, hosted solo by James Koh with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]) [Dynasty]: in a direct comparison to similarly-built [[Brian Thomas Jr.]], says Thomas is "a little more sturdy" and can win short-to-intermediate reps that Franklin sometimes can't because he "gets bodied" by the weight difference — otherwise groups him among this class's "freak athlete" tier.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): a light speedster with excellent acceleration and change of direction, proven release skills at the line, and someone who routinely stacks defenders to win the vertical game. Has room to improve how he manipulates defenders with his route stems to become an even better route runner, but Waldman thinks he can already do a good job in the NFL against defenders with a lot of these same skills.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]), naming a high-end career-outcome comp: DJ Chark (not tracked) — a perennial 1,000-yard receiver in the right (vertical) offense if he stays healthy, not a multi-time Pro Bowler. Grades him as needing to be a No. 2 in a vertically oriented passing offense, calling Buffalo the best fit and Franklin "a much, much better version of" Marquez Valdes-Scantling. [[Matt Waldman]], same episode: agrees Chark is the closest current comp and the realistic outcome, but says he's historically thought of bigger, more physical comps — Chris Chambers and Lee Evans (neither tracked) — and that Paul Richardson (not tracked) is the closer physical match, though a more complete player than Franklin who couldn't stay healthy long enough to fully cash in.
- 2024-04-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-18]]) [Dynasty]: Franklin's 2023 RP profile shows a genuinely good route runner (70.3% success vs. man, 82.4% vs. zone, 85% on curl routes on heavy curl usage) and a plus athlete (6'2, 176 lbs, 4.41 40, 39" vertical) who Harmon says plays faster than his testing suggests, but weak press-coverage success (26th percentile) tied to his thin frame and, separately, persistent and hard-to-explain drops despite good hand/body positioning. Harmon rejects the MVS comp (Franklin is a much better route runner) and instead comps his likely role to Jordan Addison — a favorable-coverage flanker/lid-lifter behind a true No. 1 rather than a high-volume X or slot option over the middle. He grades Franklin a Round 2 pick with a lower ceiling but higher floor than Legette, since a lid-lifting deep threat always has a role in the NFL. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) agreed and suggested Franklin add 10-15 lbs of NFL strength programming.
