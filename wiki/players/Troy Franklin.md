# Troy Franklin

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — Guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Dynasty]: a player he genuinely likes with a floor that scares him — real speed, a growing feel for route leverage, and good (not great) downfield ball tracking. The specific worry: he disappears against physicality — cites a Washington game where a single physical cornerback (Jabbar Muhammad) erased him — and, being a slightly built receiver who isn't going to add functional bulk without losing speed, that vulnerability isn't fixable by simply getting stronger. Illustrates the NFL-speed bar he'll be measured against with a Kansas City practice anecdote: Trent McDuffie, "the fastest lateral player maybe ever," putting [[Tyreek Hill]] "in the dirt" on a route. Names two outcome comps as the real range: [[Jordan Addison]] (a similarly built receiver who thrived because his scheme created easy releases and didn't force him into contested/physical reps) versus a marginal role player like Mecole Hardman — the swing factor being scheme fit and release timing more than talent.

- 2024-02-29 — According to guest **Eric Froton** (NBC Sports college football analyst — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, hosted solo by James Koh with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]) [Dynasty]: in a direct comparison to similarly-built [[Brian Thomas Jr.]], says Thomas is "a little more sturdy" and can win short-to-intermediate reps that Franklin sometimes can't because he "gets bodied" by the weight difference — otherwise groups him among this class's "freak athlete" tier.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): a light speedster with excellent acceleration and change of direction, proven release skills at the line, and someone who routinely stacks defenders to win the vertical game. Has room to improve how he manipulates defenders with his route stems to become an even better route runner, but Waldman thinks he can already do a good job in the NFL against defenders with a lot of these same skills.

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals, Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]), naming a high-end career-outcome comp: DJ Chark (not tracked) — a perennial 1,000-yard receiver in the right (vertical) offense if he stays healthy, not a multi-time Pro Bowler. Grades him as needing to be a No. 2 in a vertically oriented passing offense, calling Buffalo the best fit and Franklin "a much, much better version of" Marquez Valdes-Scantling. [[Matt Waldman]], same episode: agrees Chark is the closest current comp and the realistic outcome, but says he's historically thought of bigger, more physical comps — Chris Chambers and Lee Evans (neither tracked) — and that Paul Richardson (not tracked) is the closer physical match, though a more complete player than Franklin who couldn't stay healthy long enough to fully cash in.
- 2024-04-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-18]]) [Dynasty]: Franklin's 2023 RP profile shows a genuinely good route runner (70.3% success vs. man, 82.4% vs. zone, 85% on curl routes on heavy curl usage) and a plus athlete (6'2, 176 lbs, 4.41 40, 39" vertical) who Harmon says plays faster than his testing suggests, but weak press-coverage success (26th percentile) tied to his thin frame and, separately, persistent and hard-to-explain drops despite good hand/body positioning. Harmon rejects the MVS comp (Franklin is a much better route runner) and instead comps his likely role to Jordan Addison — a favorable-coverage flanker/lid-lifter behind a true No. 1 rather than a high-volume X or slot option over the middle. He grades Franklin a Round 2 pick with a lower ceiling but higher floor than Legette, since a lid-lifting deep threat always has a role in the NFL. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) agreed and suggested Franklin add 10-15 lbs of NFL strength programming.
- 2024-04-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (untracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-23]]) [Dynasty]: in a comparison prompted by discussing [[Javon Baker]], both hosts said they prefer Baker over Franklin. Koh noted Franklin's elite speed profile (4.4 40, 39-inch vertical) is undercut by a lack of physicality at only 176 pounds, plus 'seriously bad drops' in his charted profile. Harmon said he doesn't trust Franklin's hands and called his tape 'a very mid-profile,' adding he lacks the same conviction in Franklin developing into a good downfield threat that he has for Baker. As a result Harmon said he's bumping Franklin down from an initial priority-Round-2 grade to a regular Day 2 grade, placing him in the same tier as Baker and other 'good Day 2' X-receiver projects rather than above them.
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]) [Dynasty]: labeled Troy Franklin the class's Ted Ginn-type deep-ball specialist -- a legit vertical threat (75% success on post routes, 66.7% on corners, 74.7% on nine routes) who Harmon fears is correctly pigeonholed into that role because his press-coverage and tight-window ability still lag the sizzle of his route running. He's bumped Franklin down his personal rankings alongside [[Xavier Legette]] as one of the two toughest evaluations in the class. Measurables at 6'2"/176 (light) with a 4.41 forty and 39" vertical -- Harmon says Franklin "plays faster" than even that fast a time suggests. The recurring concern is drops ("soul-crushing" ones, per Harmon). Grades notably better vs. zone than vs. man. Long-term projection: a valuable but limited vertical-stretch specialist.
- 2024-05-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-02]]) [Dynasty]: Franklin fell to the second pick of round four, which Harmon says surprised people but tracks with negative character/coachability reports he'd heard and mentioned to Koh off-air before the draft; Harmon also felt Franklin was overrated as a round-one prospect to begin with, having him graded as a Day 2 talent. His success rates were strong versus man (70.3%) and zone (82.4%), but only 26th percentile versus press, plus poor contested-catch and drop rates — Harmon calls the hands 'not good.' Size (6'1"/176 lbs) is a real, tape-visible issue, not just a numbers hang-up, and Harmon projects him as a speed Z rather than a vertical X in Sean Payton's offense, reunited with college QB [[Bo Nix]]. Denver's room ([[Courtland Sutton]] X, [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] slot) leaves a path to climb, but Harmon flags a genuine bust risk if Franklin never beats press coverage or wins the trust to work the middle of the field. His route-running upside is real: an 85.4% success rate on curl routes plus strong nine-route success, a combination Harmon calls 'beautiful' — but he's unsure the traits will translate given the size and press issues.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo reinforces his willingness to take a chance on Franklin given his familiarity with [[Bo Nix]] from Oregon, projecting a Denver role with quick-hitter volume and occasional deep-shot opportunities that could carve out an early role.
- 2024-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-01]]): Franklin's deep charting is excellent — 65% on nines, 75% on posts, 67% on corners, with a 70% vs man / 82% vs zone split that both hosts call the profile of a functional NFL player. The reservations are all frame-related at 176 pounds: poor press-coverage and contested-catch data, below-average digs and slants over the middle, and what co-host James Koh (not tracked) calls maybe the worst hands in the class. Harmon says he is not normally a size guy — he did not care about [[DeVonta Smith]], [[Tank Dell]] or [[Josh Downs]] because they do not play small — but Franklin plays thin, and he thinks that is why he fell to the fourth round, alongside unspecified character notes Harmon declined to detail. Harmon rates Franklin's route nuance highly but says he has more considerable press and catch-point issues than [[Ladd McConkey]] and is not a YAC player, so scheming him space does not help; he projects a low-volume stretch receiver who is not a year-one featured piece. Koh doubts play strength and doggedness solve themselves at the NFL level.
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): Franklin has had a terrible offseason and camp, and Harmon reiterates that he was an overrated prospect who deservedly fell to day three of the draft — he says he was 'lower than most' on Franklin. Denver is reportedly signalling he is not ready for a real role, which Harmon finds absurd alongside cutting [[Tim Patrick]]: if the young picks are not ready and the veteran is gone, no one is left. His position is that Denver should be playing Franklin and [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] anyway, since the team is not competing and the only sensible thing is to find out what the young draft picks are. Notably, Harmon still says he would rather have had Franklin than [[Luke McCaffrey]] in the same draft, so the criticism is relative, not absolute.
- 2024-11-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-21]]): Franklin is on the field for Denver and has made some plays, but Harmon is 'really not all that interested' and has not charted him for the rookie report. From the RP prospect profile: dropped passes, questionable ball tracking, and his worst routes were the in-breakers and horizontal stuff — he was a vertical receiver living on curls and go routes, and Harmon attributes the in-breaker weakness partly to size, since he gets muscled up and is not a reliable big target over the middle against zone. Strangely, Harmon says he and [[Bo Nix]] do not appear to have chemistry despite their Oregon history, which cuts against the usual built-in-chemistry assumption. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) cites the profile's 64.7% success rate on the nine, 75% on the post and 85% on the curl as workable NFL traits, and says the missing Nix-Franklin connection is what surprises him.
- 2024-12-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-31]]): a blunt negative. Harmon says he has 'seen enough' of Franklin and wants to see less of him — 'the next downfield pass they throw to Troy Franklin, it will be the first one that looks good.' He frames Denver as having tried to make Franklin the traditional outside, work-back-to-the-ball receiver, the same role that failed [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] as a rookie, and says the results this year have not been good. The contrast is deliberate: Denver found a role that fits Mims's profile while continuing to ask Franklin to do the thing he has not shown he can do.

That said, the take does not really move: Koh calls Franklin up and down and 'mostly down' on the season, and both hosts group him with [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] and [[Devaughn Vele]] as interesting pieces, none of whom is a number one, in a Denver room that needs another high-volume addition behind [[Courtland Sutton]].

[[Matt Harmon]] added that this is a big part of why he fell to the fourth round despite dynasty enthusiasts ranking him a top-five receiver in his class: he can win as a man-coverage route runner but only in specific ways, and he struggles to catch the football consistently. He can play and hold a role, but Harmon ranks him behind [[Pat Bryant]] and [[Devaughn Vele]] in the queue to become Denver's real number two behind [[Courtland Sutton]], and classes him as situational alongside [[Marvin Mims Jr.]].
