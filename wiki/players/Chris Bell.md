# Chris Bell

## Expert Takes

- 2025-12-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-01 (Feel It or Fk It)]]): Waldman compared Louisville receiver Chris Bell (listed 6'2'-6'3', around 220 lbs) to former Chiefs receiver Dwayne Bowe, citing strong after-the-catch running -- Waldman likened Bowe's LSU screen usage to a modern Rashee Rice-style toss-sweep role -- plus a good contested-catch streak, solid ball-tracking, occasional one-handed grabs, and the ability to break tackles. Waldman's central caveat is speed: on some reps Bell looks notably faster than others, and Waldman said he's unsure how to grade his real long speed. If Bell has the speed Waldman suspects, he profiles like Bowe; if not, he could be more limited, or alternately develop like Martavis Bryant did after adding weight for his breakout Pittsburgh season -- Waldman said Bell likely needs added mass to fully hit either comp.
- 2026-01-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-26]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman pushes back on Bell's borderline-top-prospect media buzz, saying that of the roughly 62 wide receivers he's charted this cycle, a majority grade out above Bell -- he saw some hands and route potential but 'nothing unbelievably high.' He worries the hype is driven more by Bell's size, strength, and speed (build-up speed, in his view, rather than true top-end burst) than by the details of his game, and draws a cautionary comp to Treylon Burks, another big/strong/fast prospect people were high on based on traits and projection rather than tape. Waldman says he plans to re-watch Bell given how wide the gap is between his own evaluation and Bell's media reputation.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Bell is 'the player in this draft I'm afraid to consider and equally afraid to not consider' -- Waldman says he flashes as an ascending, star-level talent on some tape and looks like an inconsistent, frustrating tease on others, comparing him to Devonta Parker, a talented Louisville receiver whose game didn't fully come together with Miami until year three or four, by which point expectations had already dropped. Angelo agreed with the dual read. Both hosts treat him as a genuinely volatile evaluation rather than a settled grade -- worth a speculative dynasty stash, not a safe pick.
- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Bell is a massively good athlete, but inconsistent execution -- especially settling routes against zone at the wrong tempo -- is the recurring theme in Waldman's tape, including at least one route per game charted where Bell settled behind a fallen defender and miscommunicated with his quarterback, leading to a near-turnover. Waldman's broader point: 'massive physical talents don't usually have the same success as really skilled technicians with the baseline of physical talent that you want from a starter.'
- 2026-04-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman put Chris Bell in his top 10 but called him 'the scariest player on the board' -- a rare case where he's as afraid to draft the player as to pass on him. At 6'2', 222 with real speed on crossing routes and good hands/route skill when locked in, Bell would be a clear starter-tier prospect, but a knee injury cost him most of last season and Waldman won't project a first-round grade for a player he couldn't fully evaluate healthy. His larger concern is consistency: sloppy, inconsistent execution that leans too heavily on raw athletic talent rather than repeatable technique, which Waldman said Bell must prove he can tighten up week to week rather than just producing highlight-reel flashes.
- 2026-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: named as the prospect Waldman is most scared to draft and almost equally scared to pass on -- 'a coin flip whether the pick is genius or insanity.' On his best reps he looks like AJ Brown or Josh Court splitting defenders on a crossing route; on far too many others he shows 'death by a thousand paper cuts' of things that don't quite work. Waldman's downside comp is Michael Westbrook -- explosive tools, injury and makeup questions capping him to one or two big seasons -- and he's coming off a torn ACL that will cost him rookie offseason reps, making him, in Waldman's words, 'probably the most boom-or-bust player in the class.' Waldman would rather let another team take the swing and prioritize [[Chris Brazzell]] or De'Zhaun Stribling as safer bets in that range.
- 2026-04-09 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-09]]) [Dynasty]: his biggest 'lower than consensus' call is Chris Bell, whom he projects as a roster-bubble player for most of his career -- durability is the driver, citing a December ACL surgery on top of an already-heavy 220-lb frame that strains deceleration and change-of-direction, plus a likely October-ish return that could push him behind a Day 3 pick's typical development cycle. Archetypally, Angelo grouped him with the rare 6'2'/220-plus receiver body type that has produced very few true No. 1s -- naming only [[DK Metcalf]] and [[A.J. Brown]] as hits versus busts like [[Xavier Legette]] and [[Jonathan Mingo]] -- because most players in that mold lack the route-tree diversity to be more than crossers/slants guys. Angelo called Bell the player he's simultaneously 'most afraid to draft and most afraid not to draft,' with a Pro Bowl-level ceiling and an out-of-the-league-in-three-to-four-years floor, comparing the range to what evaluators once hoped [[Treylon Burks]] would become.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-30 (Round 3 WRs)]]) [Dynasty]: Miami's second third-round WR (pick 3.30), Bell fell out of round two solely due to a torn ACL late in 2025 -- Harmon had him as his top receiver in the tier after Denzel Boston and says he'd have been an early-second-round pick healthy. He resists an AJ Brown comp ('history is littered with bad AJ Brown comparisons'), instead placing Bell's range somewhere between Brown and Xavier Legette/Jonathan Mingo/Treylon Burks. RP marks: 70% success vs. man, 68% vs. press, 77% vs. zone -- all just below the 70-80% threshold the hosts like to see, but 'knocking on the door.' His signature trait is winning back-shoulder throws (Harmon cites a UVA game with roughly 10 back-shoulder catches); at 6'1.7', 222 with 87th-percentile hands, Harmon notes he doesn't look as physically imposing as his measurables suggest but has room to add strength. Bell needs development on in-breaking routes but shows promising press-man reps. Harmon likes the Miami fit specifically because QB Malik Willis reportedly developed a back-shoulder throw in Green Bay, and Miami's offensive line averages 325 pounds with a 296-pound center, projecting a heavy run/play-action scheme with sail routes and quick crossers that suits Bell. Expects him to miss offseason reps recovering from the ACL but to be a real factor by November/December and 'locked in' heading into 2027.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-07 (Going Deep with Brandon Angelo)]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo grades Bell low despite frequent reader questions defending him, arguing his archetype -- a 220-plus-pound receiver under 6-foot-3 -- has a brutal recent hit rate. His comp list of size-comparable receivers drafted since 2019: [[A.J. Brown]], JJ Arcega-Whiteside, Laviska Shenault Jr., Joe Reed, [[Treylon Burks]], Denzel Mims, [[Jonathan Mingo]] and [[Xavier Legette]]. Every one besides Brown is out of the league, hurt, or a reserve who's never posted more than a 500-yard season. Angelo's reasoning: it's exceptionally hard for a player that big and heavy to drop weight and out-maneuver faster, increasingly athletic cornerbacks, and Bell's tape doesn't show the pace variation, release nuance or ability to bait defenders that separates the archetype's outliers. He flagged Bell as one to avoid once the tier where Denzel Boston and Chris Brazell go off the board is gone.
- 2026-07-17 — According to Patrick Doherty (Rotoworld, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-17]]): Bell was viewed as a potential mid-first-round pick before a knee injury and fell all the way to the third round to Miami; Doherty flags real uncertainty about whether he plays at all as a rookie in 2026.
- 2026-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-23]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon views rookie third-rounder Chris Bell as an early second-round talent on his prospect board, but says it's hard to bet on him for 2026 given he's on the NFI list recovering from a torn college ACL and will start behind the depth chart even once healthy, in a wide-open Miami receiver room alongside fellow rookie [[Caleb Douglas]] and [[Malik Washington]].
- 2026-08-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-11]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon still considers Bell the highest-ceiling receiver of Miami's rookie class and says he was his highest-graded prospect of that group, even though Bell fell in the draft (taken after [[Caleb Douglas]]) due to injury concerns.
- 2026-08-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Rookie Chris Bell, for whom Harris wrote an Almanac profile, has done nothing in Dolphins camp because he's still in knee rehab from a reconstruction and the team is playing it safe with him. Harris said 'we're not drafting Chris Bell at all' right now and pulled him out of his redraft ranks entirely, ceding the Dolphins rookie-receiver spotlight to [[Caleb Douglas]].
- 2026-08-27 — According to Jake Seeley of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-27]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Seeley's pick is Dolphins rookie WR Chris Bell, who tore his ACL in December after being talked about as a potential first-round pick, with 'everybody in the industry' having him ranked ahead of teammate [[Caleb Douglas]], whom Miami drafted first anyway. Bell recently returned to practice drills but is unlikely to play much even in September. Seeley's case is pure talent-over-draft-slot: if Bell gets back to 100% and the scouting consensus was right, he could end up the Dolphins' clear top target over Douglas once healthy, in an offense where the receiver pecking order (Tolbert, [[Malik Washington]]) keeps shifting day to day. He's stashing Bell only as a late-bench/IR-spot name, not drafting him outright.
