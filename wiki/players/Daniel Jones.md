# Daniel Jones

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-12]]): flat "not the answer" for the Giants. Predicts a two-step succession rather than an immediate reset — New York drafts a developmental (second- or third-round) quarterback first, gives him a runway behind Jones, then takes a true first-round quarterback once they're ready to actually bench him and move on, rather than benching him for a rookie right away.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: revisits the Giants' quarterback situation, arguing they "should have paid [[Saquon Barkley]]" instead of extending Jones and could have found a cheaper quarterback in the second or third round instead — cites the Jones contract, not the pick itself, as the harder-to-recover-from decision. Notes the Giants are publicly committed to Jones as the 2024 starter and have brought in Drew Lock as competition, but flags him as a name to watch if he isn't clearly the starter by midseason, coming off injury as well.
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]): Waldman expects Daniel Jones to open 2024 still working back from ACL surgery, with [[Drew Lock]] likely to open the season as the Giants' starter in his place.
- 2024-07-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]): with Jones reportedly avoiding the PUP list, the Giants will roll with him given what they are paying, and given time he can give you QB12–15 fantasy production, maybe top-10 in a great year, helped by having a strong rookie receiver in [[Malik Nabers]]. But Waldman will not start him outside superflex. His concerns: the run game is weak enough that defenses may not respect it and will come after Jones with numbers, and Jones makes erratic decisions and does not get rid of the ball quickly — so Waldman expects to see [[Drew Lock]] at some point this season. Bob Harris (not tracked) adds that Jones helps fantasy managers 'accidentally' in the games where he leads the team in rushing, which happened six times even alongside [[Saquon Barkley]].
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) calls Jones the governor on the Giants offense — his accuracy has actually been decent, but he has never converted from a turnover-avoider into someone who makes the big play when the pocket breaks down, holding the ball too long and taking hits. McFarland ties everything to the line: if two early-round tackles, an early-round center and two free-agent guards give above-average play, Jones can deliver 3,500-3,600 yards and maybe 20 touchdowns; his career average of 215 passing yards per game is what McFarland has projected again. Waldman says he doesn't think Jones can ruin [[Malik Nabers]] and expects [[Drew Lock]] to take over late in the season, calling Lock a bridge with an outside chance of being more.
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): both argue that not all bad quarterbacks are created equal. Koh says Jones is not an accurate passer and makes bad throws routinely, and that he is not worth his contract, but he has the arm talent to get the ball to any part of the field — a low bar that still matters, because when a quarterback cannot threaten the whole field the defense only has to defend a truncated area. Harmon calls him not good but functional, and explicitly better than [[Zach Wilson]] or Desmond Ridder, the tier of quarterback [[Drake London]] and [[Garrett Wilson]] have had to play with, where you cannot run a functional NFL offense at all. His conclusion: you cannot run a good offense with Jones, but with smoke, mirrors and good coaching you can get to functional, and that is enough to keep feeding [[Malik Nabers]].
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): Bob Harris (not tracked) calls Jones 'a fraud'; Waldman disagrees on the wording — 'he's not a fraud, he's exactly who he should be' — while agreeing the outcome is bad. His point is that the Giants defrauded themselves: Duke marketed Jones as a David Cutcliffe disciple in the Manning mold, but an insider told scouts who asked deep enough questions that Cutcliffe was not remotely as involved as he had been with the Mannings, and it would show. Waldman says the same things Jones did wrong at Duke are the same things he does wrong now. He hates that Jones was picked as early as he was, not the player personally. He closes with 'I like Daniel Jones as much as [[Malik Nabers]] likes Daniel Jones,' and notes Nabers needs a quarterback who isn't 'so miserly with his dimes.'
- 2024-11-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]]): the Giants offense is cooked and Jones's time is over — he reads the tea leaves as decisive, with Brian Daboll declining to confirm Jones as the starter and saying only that they are 'evaluating things', plus Adam Schefter tweeting out the injury-guarantee language on Jones's contract (roughly 23 million fully guaranteed next year if he is hurt). Harmon cites [[Derek Carr]] in Las Vegas and [[Russell Wilson]] in Denver as precedent for that exact sequence. His evaluation: Jones is a functional quarterback when everything around him is perfect, but nothing is ever perfect in the NFL, and the whole operation collapsed once All-Pro left tackle Andrew Thomas went out because the quarterback cannot adjust. He also faults the organization for a patience failure in the other direction — a smoke-and-mirrors season should not have bought a multi-year commitment, and the moment to move on passed two seasons ago.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2024-11-14]]): Jones is the reason to discount [[Malik Nabers]]'s worst numbers rather than the receiver — Harmon says if you want to harp on a receiver's drops you have to show the rest of the routes where he is wide open and the quarterback does not pull the trigger, and nobody is failing to grip it and rip it more than Jones. He and co-host James Koh (not tracked) argue over whether Brian Daboll's stripped-down, basic offense is what Daboll wants or all Jones can handle; Harmon leans toward the latter, noting it is year three of the same staff and the same quarterback. Both expect the Giants to finally be allowed a quarterback change, though Harmon does not think [[Drew Lock]] is an upgrade in himself.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): Angelo says Jones has had time and timing available and still is not a winning player in this iteration, even if he could turn into a good quarterback later. Waldman describes his core flaw as 'pocket death' — his internal pocket clock rings and he hits the snooze button, producing sacks and turnovers, and he does not see the field well. Waldman calls Jones a fraud on draft day, clarifying he means the draft capital was fraudulent in how it was perceived, not the person: roughly 40-55 percent of the league and Dave Gettleman bought the idea he was a David Cutcliffe quarterback like the Manning brothers, but Cutcliffe was Duke's head coach, not his position coach, and a Duke staffer told scouts he was not a Cutcliffe quarterback while the school had recruiting reasons to leave the story alone. Waldman comps him to Mitchell Trubisky — same deficiencies, same athleticism, a three-year developmental project given six years.
- 2025-01-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-06]]): Waldman is emphatic that Jones sank the Giants organization — he calls the drafting of Jones a first-round fraud left behind by Dave Gettleman, describes the cleanup as needing hazmat suits because the contract made him effectively untradeable, and argues Brian Daboll and Joe Schoen deserve another year precisely because they were saddled with him and never got to collaborate on their own offensive vision. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed continuity is the right call and passed along John Mara's rationale about doing the rational rather than the emotional thing, plus a suggestion that Daboll may need to hand off play-calling. Both acknowledged the choice to keep the quarterback over [[Saquon Barkley]] looks bad given Philadelphia's success, but note running backs are treated as fungible and that Barkley's Eagles production owes a lot to a superior offensive line.
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]) on Jones signing a one-year deal with Indianapolis worth $14M-$17.7M with incentives: the hosts disagree. Koh believes it is not a real competition — the QB-rebuilding 'widgets' will go to Jones, who fits what Shane Steichen wants better than [[Anthony Richardson]] does, and Koh argues Jones is an underrated downfield thrower who has never had good pass catchers. Harmon predicts instead that both quarterbacks start games and everyone gets fired at year end. His reasons for not buying another reclamation: Indianapolis's culture and team build do not compare to Tampa Bay ([[Baker Mayfield]]) or Minnesota ([[Sam Darnold]]); Jones never had Darnold's physical ability even at his peak; and the injury history — shoulder surgery, multiple neck injuries and a torn ACL — showed up last year as a lack of zip on the ball. To save Chris Ballard and Steichen, Harmon says Jones would have to run away with the job in camp and make Richardson tradeable, which nothing in his career suggests.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]): Waldman rejects Sigmund Bloom's framing of Jones as real competition for [[Anthony Richardson]], expecting Richardson to beat him easily. His specific objections: Jones lacks pinpoint deep accuracy and needs receivers to erase that, having only 'catchable' accuracy; and he is 'pocket deaf', among the worst starters at feeling and managing pressure — so behind a line that already sank Richardson he has no chance to look good. He dismisses the rushing-upside counter with a car analogy: Jones is a 2024 Chevy Malibu, Richardson a 1967 Chevelle Malibu SS. He grants Jones value as a decent backup with a rehabilitation path if the line improves. Bob Harris (untracked) offers the peanut-butter-versus-Vegemite version — Indianapolis promised competition and delivered something labeled as such.
- 2025-06-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-06]]): Jones was probably already the Week 1 favorite in Indianapolis, and is a better Steichen fit than [[Anthony Richardson]] because Steichen elevates traditional trigger men through play design — but Harmon calls him only 'the safer path to like an okay season' with no path to true upside. Koh (not tracked) cited PFF that Jones's 2022 completion percentage was 11 points better on play action than off it, evidence that the 2022 Giants offense was smoke-and-mirrors RPOs and QB run. Harmon thinks Jones has fallen off physically since 2022 (ACL, neck), can still get the ball out quickly to playmakers as he did with [[Malik Nabers]], but doubts he can push the ball down the field vertically the way this Colts receiver room wants. He warns the Colts must build in layups because Jones 'can go into chaos mode pretty quick' and bad things happen under pressure. Harmon's stated expectation since the signing: both Jones and Richardson start games and everybody gets fired at year's end.
- 2025-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-13]]): Jones will start week one and is the favourite to start every game for Indianapolis given where [[Anthony Richardson]] is health-wise. Harmon expects the offense to lean into RPOs and quick hitters, which suits [[Josh Downs]] and [[Michael Pittman Jr.]], and believes Jones was signed partly because he can run the quarterback-run elements Richardson would have. Both hosts framed him as an upgrade on Gardner Minshew on physical tools while joking that Minshew's competitiveness is the missing ingredient, and pointed to Jones getting [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] and [[Malik Nabers]] the football in New York as the reason to hope he feeds Downs.
- 2025-06-25 — According to guest Cory Kinnan (Reception Perception QB charting, NOT a tracked expert) with [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-25]]): Kinnan does not trust Jones but does trust Shane Steichen's preparation to extract what he calls the 'Minshew minimum' — the 2023 Colts baseline that came a dropped third-down conversion from a wild-card berth. Jones frustrates every head coach he plays for, though Kinnan discounts that by noting the coaches were not high-caliber and credits the Buffalo [[Josh Allen]] turnaround more to Ken Dorsey, Jordan Palmer and biomechanics work than to Brian Daboll. He sees a 2023 Cleveland/Flacco path — inefficient offense, enough points, a defense carrying it under Lou Anarumo — worth eight to ten wins, which in a middling AFC South can mean a division title. Harmon adds that Jones could not push the ball downfield last year even relative to his pre-injury self, citing the serious neck injury and a torn ACL since the smoke-and-mirrors 2022 season, and sees him living on quick game to [[Josh Downs]], RPO in-breakers to [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] and occasional shots to [[Alec Pierce]] and [[Tyler Warren]].
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-20]]): named the Colts' week one starter, Jones does not unlock anything for Indianapolis pass catchers. Harmon expects Jones, [[Anthony Richardson]] and eventually [[Riley Leonard]] all to start games this year and 'it's not going to work out well for anybody', noting Shane Steichen's stated no-short-leash promise mirrors the Joe Flacco promise he broke in two weeks. From Jones's Reception Perception QB profile: a near-77% success rate on the post but on only 5.7% route participation, about league average on slants, and below league average on the dig, comeback and curl — the ~50% dig success rate being what Harmon is 'stuck like glue' on, since the Minshew minimum required accurate on-time intermediate balls over the middle. He argues Jones has lost the arm strength to drive throws through zone, and offers nothing vertically. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) offered the counter-hopium: the Kevin O'Connell half-season and Steichen's offensive competence, comparing it to [[Baker Mayfield]]'s McVay bump; Harmon concedes he was wrong on [[Sam Darnold]] and Mayfield before.

- 2025-10-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman published a Football Guys 'gut check' arguing Jones and the Colts offense will stay hot, despite his own prior scouting report calling Jones a poor first-round prospect with erratic footwork at Duke.
