# J.K. Dobbins

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]) [Best Ball]: buying Dobbins at his outside-RB20 ADP with the Chargers, reasoning that pairing him with Gus Edwards under Greg Roman signals the staff trusts both to translate the Roman scheme; says he'll keep believing in Dobbins 'until he's no longer in the league.'
- 2024-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-01]]): Harmon is not buying a full healthy 2024 season from Dobbins no matter how hard the Chargers sell it, and points to Dobbins publicly dunking on Ian Rapoport's earlier injury reporting only for Rapoport to be proven right. He adds that Jim Harbaugh will not be a truthful source on player health — a Pete Carroll-style camp information blackout — so positive Dobbins reports should be discounted. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) prefers to plead the fifth as a pro-player guy but agrees the sales pitch has been running.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): Waldman is buying Dobbins everywhere he can get him at his RB47 / pick-146 ADP, saying that if the Achilles recovery holds and Dobbins really does feel the way he did in college, you could strike the three from RB36 and he is talented enough to be a top-six running back. He notes modern Achilles rehabs are not what they used to be. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) framed the same range more cautiously, saying the odds are better that [[Antonio Gibson]]-tier alternatives disappoint but specifically that [[DeAndre Swift]] is more likely to have the better season, and that he personally holds more shares of [[Gus Edwards]] at RB37 because people who cover the team daily expect Edwards to lead the backfield. Both framed it as a roster-construction question: swing on Dobbins late if you built stability early, take the perceived-safer Edwards if you already stacked upside.
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): he wants to believe, and is picking Dobbins in every draft at his late cost. The case is Dobbins' own account — that in past returns he tried to play through injuries before he was ready, got frustrated with people poking holes in what they saw, and later admitted to the media that he had not been fully there and had not realized it until he started playing. Now he says he has not felt this good since Ohio State, where he never got hurt, and Waldman is inclined to take that at face value. He also likes the setup: [[Kimani Vidal]] is waiting in the wings but is not getting many reps and has drawn little attention, and Dobbins and [[Gus Edwards]] can run Greg Roman's show one more time. Framed explicitly as a shot worth taking only at that late a pick.
- 2024-09-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Dobbins is a must-start until the wheels fall off. A 40-yard and a 60-yard run on 10 carries is indicative, but the more important tell is that he was a dog in pass protection and was used on third downs and obvious passing situations more than [[Gus Edwards]] — that is the role that sustains fantasy value. Waldman's caveat is blunt: it could end next week given his recent injury history, though he notes that if you go back to Dobbins's Ohio State durability record it wouldn't. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) agreed, said Dobbins is the player everyone should be rooting for this year, and admitted he owns both Chargers backs and regretted not starting Dobbins in week one.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): Dobbins was the surprise of the Chargers' win, with Harmon calling the run game explosive under him and saying it was 'a shock' given he is only about 12 months removed from an Achilles tear. Koh (not tracked) adds the honest first-half context — both Dobbins and [[Gus Edwards]] looked horrific early and he was checking his watch wondering when [[Kimani Vidal]] would get a look — before Dobbins went 'absolutely ham' in the second half. Harmon has been higher than consensus on this offense all offseason because of Jim Harbaugh, while granting Greg Roman's passing designs are never going to be perfect; Joe Alt and the tackles played well in protection though the interior is a pass-pro concern. The team can set the tempo running the ball.
- 2024-10-10 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert), co-host with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): Dobbins belongs on the negative-regression list alongside [[Saquon Barkley]] and [[Derrick Henry]] — a great back on an unsustainable efficiency run. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees, calling him a fantastic story given everything he has come back from. Both hosts find it striking that Dobbins and [[Gus Edwards]] produced nearly identically in Baltimore, to the point the standing question was why anyone cared about Dobbins when Edwards returned the same numbers, and have now diverged as far as possible in the same Chargers backfield. Harstad's conclusion is that this is randomness rather than talent: Edwards is the player he always was, and Dobbins is the player he was, or the one he could have been had he stayed healthy. See [[Yards Per Carry as Noise]].
- 2024-11-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-18]]): asked by Bob Harris (not tracked) whether Dobbins would be a top-12 fantasy back in 2025, Waldman said yes — he expects the Chargers' offensive line to improve inside Jim Harbaugh's system, and Dobbins is playing as a clutch, all-around back right now. Film detail: on a power run the Bengals had three defenders against two blockers at the point of attack, and Dobbins cut back in the face of an unblocked defender — something you do not normally do on power — then set up a second-level defender with a dead leg and stiff-arm; on another he decelerated left, shed a defensive tackle wrapped high on him, reversed field and gained 15-16. Waldman said that with the line gelling Dobbins is capable of top-five production, but he deliberately caps his own projection at top-12 because of the injury history and 'the Chargers being the Chargers'.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: both hosts 'felt' Dobbins at Underdog RB31 over [[Rhamondre Stevenson]] at RB30. Untracked co-host Bob Harris, who flagged his own bias, went with Dobbins based on what he showed and what he overcame to play well despite the persistent injury risk, and thinks the Chargers may re-sign him — he is an unrestricted free agent — if they do not land the back they want. Waldman agreed, and when asked he said he would take Dobbins over every rookie back discussed on the show except [[Ashton Jeanty]] — a direct statement of how far he is fading pre-draft rookie running back ADP.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): 'absolutely feeling' that Dobbins still has league-winning upside. The injury history is a legitimate concern given how bad the injuries were, but Waldman weighted more heavily that Dobbins came back and played as well as he did, including returning from a mid-season injury to produce decent work. The condition is that he stays with the Chargers — and even there Waldman expects Jim Harbaugh, who likes to collect running backs and build a stable, to add another back from what he considers a good class, meaning more competition for [[Kimani Vidal]] and [[Gus Edwards]] as well.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]): still-unsigned Dobbins would be an excellent fit in Ben Johnson's Bears offense, because when healthy he can be [[DeAndre Swift]] and [[David Montgomery]] combined — you could hand him the whole job or slot him into either single role and still get strong play. Asked about workload concerns, Waldman says he would not be too worried and thinks Dobbins is still good to go. He raises this in the context of Chicago waiting until the seventh round for a back ([[Kyle Monangai]]) and of Ben Johnson visibly wanting one more offensive toy than GM Ryan Poles gave him.
- 2025-05-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-26]]): Waldman is 'feeling' [[J.K. Dobbins]] signing with the Chicago Bears late in the preseason, after Chicago has tried out the backs already on the roster — his stated reason for the timing is that a later signing avoids owing the Chargers draft-pick compensation tied to the tender Los Angeles assigned him. He contrasts Dobbins with [[Nick Chubb]]: teams at least saw a track record last year of what Dobbins could do coming back from injury, which is also why he was tendered the way he was, whereas Chubb reads to Waldman as an in-season injury replacement.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): he fully endorses Cecil Lammey's report (Footballguys, not tracked) that Denver's plan is Dobbins as the 1A to [[RJ Harvey]]'s 1B, projecting close to 200 carries for a little over 800 yards and four touchdowns plus roughly 40 receptions for 300 more — about 1,100 scrimmage yards, conditional on Dobbins playing at least 14 games. He calls Dobbins a very good receiver out of the backfield who simply was not used enough there. The market call: analysts will refuse to see him as the legitimate RB1 because he signed late, because of the injury history, and because the Chargers let him go — Waldman expects him to stay projected as an RB3 even as the role establishes itself, which is the buying window. Bob Harris (not tracked) adds the overlooked detail that the Chargers used the rarely applied franchise tag, so compensation attached to an early signing and may have chilled other teams' interest. Both admit they may be over-invested as long-time Dobbins fans.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): Sean Payton's comments about [[RJ Harvey]] still developing in the receiving role reinforce that Dobbins is the number one back in Denver. Waldman called the signing 'great' — both because it gives Denver a 1A they otherwise lacked and because the tandem is a good learning environment for Harvey, who has pass-protection and ball-security work to do.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): Dobbins won the Week 1 Denver backfield clearly — 37 snaps to [[RJ Harvey]]'s 27 and 18 touches to seven, with Tyler Badie also touching the ball before Harvey did. He finished off Harvey's 50-yard run with a good red-zone carry to put Denver up 20-12. Harris frames it as Harvey being hard to trust rather than Dobbins being locked in permanently, noting things can change and Harvey had only just played his first NFL game.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): Dobbins 'has kind of looked like the man' in Denver — 25 snaps and 15 touches against [[RJ Harvey]]'s 17 snaps and six touches. He was stuffed inside the five on one drive but punched in a touchdown after a [[Troy Franklin]] screen in the third quarter. Harris's stance is that the second-round rookie has not looked all that good yet, so Dobbins is the back to use while Harvey profiles as supplemental; he cannot see a way to get Harvey into a Week 3 lineup.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Denver appeared to settle the backfield in-game — [[RJ Harvey]] started and played more early, but after two three-and-outs Harris's note was 'let's stop messing around with this Harvey guy,' and Dobbins opened the third quarter with a 41-yard run around the left side. Dobbins was also credited with a touchdown run that was actually a scoop of an unintentional backwards pass from [[Bo Nix]], which he tightroped into the end zone. Caveat: Harris thinks both the Chargers and Broncos defenses will be really good, and the Chargers in particular are a bad matchup for opposing backs.
- 2025-09-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-26]]): Harris would rank [[Zay Flowers]] ahead of Dobbins rest-of-season and still doesn't really want to give Dobbins away, because the scarce position rules the market — there aren't many teams who found Dobbins in the couch cushions and can shrug him off. Josh likes Dobbins and thinks that unless he gets hurt he holds the role for a while and produces consistent points; his caution is that dealing a usable running back for a receiver is how you get annihilated if Flowers goes 4-for-45 and Dobbins goes 17-80 with a touchdown the same week.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): Dobbins hit 100 rushing yards against Cincinnati and Harris believes Sean Payton knew going in exactly what he could do — Payton even told the broadcast crew beforehand that he expected Dobbins to get 100, and Denver leaned on run-run-run-and-punt through the second half knowing the Bengals could not threaten them. Harris notes the historical footnote the booth pulled out early: Dobbins is Payton's first running back to have a 100-yard game with the Broncos. He was taken out once the century mark was reached.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Dobbins 'was really good too' in the Denver comeback and, along with [[Courtland Sutton]], was one of only two Broncos guest **Sam Gold** (NOT tracked) would point to. Harris adds that [[RJ Harvey]] 'doesn't feel like he's playing' — okay, one good moment showing a little burst, but 'a purely supplemental role' — so 'people who have Dobbins should feel good about it.'
- 2025-10-09 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys; NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-09]]) [Dynasty]: Dobbins is a dented can he likes buying — a back who showed a lot in his first stint but ultimately disappointed relative to expectations, which prices him well below what a second act would be worth. He named him alongside [[Javonte Williams]] and [[Leonard Fournette]] as the discounted-asset class that lets a rebuilding roster gain value instead of bleeding the usual ~20% a year.
- 2025-10-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-10]]): Dobbins is his number three do-not-trade-away. Harris says the only reason to sell is fear that the injury history means this is the best it will ever be — Dobbins has never made it through a season — but he is not a nervous fantasy player and injury-prone players do sometimes get through a full year. He would take Dobbins over [[George Pickens]] straight up, 100 times out of 100, on positional scarcity. Guest Cousin Josh (Detective Fish, NOT a tracked expert) disagrees on temperament rather than talent: he drafted Dobbins expecting a good offense and thinking the [[RJ Harvey]] takeover wouldn't arrive until the second half, and now says he no longer expects Harvey to take the job — more likely a 60/40 split like most backfields — but the injury risk means he'd be sending feelers out on his RB3 right now, trying to package Dobbins with an underperforming receiver for a better back, or trade him for a high-performing receiver. Josh calls the production so far 'house money.'
- 2025-11-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Dobbins was 'the only thing that was working all night' in Denver's 10-7 win over the Raiders, and Harris criticized [[Sean Payton]] for calling a backward pass to [[Courtland Sutton]] on third-and-one in Raider territory rather than simply handing it to Dobbins. Harris flagged an injury scare — Dobbins rolled his ankle in the third quarter and the replay looked like the mechanism for a high ankle sprain — but he returned and played the entire fourth quarter, so 'my worries were for naught.' Harris was also frustrated that in Denver's only trip inside the five, Dobbins and [[RJ Harvey]] were on the field together and the play call was a play-action screen to Harvey rather than a Dobbins carry.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-10]]): Dobbins is a what-could-have-been story and still a very good back. Pre-injury, Waldman had him on par with [[Jonathan Taylor]] as a prospect — just as explosive, shiftier, and a better receiver, where Taylor was the great power player. He compares the arc to Frank Gore: a skilled technician, conceptually strong, still quick, still able to run in traffic, create and set up defenders, and a joy to watch — but with a tinge of sadness, because absent the injuries it could have been a one-two conversation between Dobbins and Taylor rather than a one-man one.
