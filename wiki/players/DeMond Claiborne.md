# DeMond Claiborne

## Expert Takes

- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls Claiborne the obvious name in his early 2026 look and says he is fun. Good feet, mobile hips, presses creases well, a good windback runner, catches the ball reasonably well, and decent contact balance for his size. He is not unbelievably fast and not a breakaway guy, but he can extend against safeties and force them to chase another 15 to 20 yards once he reaches the secondary, so he can flip the field. Waldman shows more fluidity and better lower-body answers than [[Bucky Irving]] did, and Hatman (Scouting Academy, NOT tracked) expects people to place him on the [[RJ Harvey]] and [[De'Von Achane]] continuum minus the peak speed — the smaller body type the NFL has found a way to use, so size knocks should carry less weight now. Waldman's caveat is weight: a spring verification in the low 190s, and if he can get to a true 210 to 212 he sees a neighbourhood a few miles down the road from LeSean McCoy's.
- 2026-02-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-09]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is 'kind of feeling' Claiborne as underrated but flags a serious ball-security red flag -- three fumbles across his career entering this year, then seven in one season. He notes teams monitor fumble rates privately even without discussing it publicly, and warns against a player getting 'too tight' overthinking it while still needing to actually fix it, citing [[Marshawn Lloyd]] and [[Bhayshul Tuten]] as cautionary examples of backs whose fumbling/injuries stalled their emergence. On tape, Waldman likens Claiborne's game to a smaller LeSean McCoy (about 195 pounds, similar to a young Clinton Portis in Denver) -- shifty, capable between the tackles, a plus receiver -- with upside as 'an upper-middle-class version of [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]' in the right system.
- 2026-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-01]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman addressed whether Claiborne's 188-pound frame caps his workload ceiling, citing Jahvid Best's concussion-shortened career and current questions about [[Bucky Irving]]'s workload at 5'10'/195. He contrasted Claiborne with three lighter backs who thrived on volume -- Clinton Portis (190-195 as a rookie, later bulked to 215-225), Charlie Garner (190, averaged 1,718 yards from scrimmage and 7.5 TDs over a four-year starter stretch), and Jamaal Charles (195-200, five of six seasons topping Garner's average) -- noting all three carried 330-613 college touches, comparable to Claiborne's 613 at Wake Forest. Waldman's conclusion: '188 pounds may not be the question mark we believe it to be.' He doesn't expect Claiborne to open as a lead back but projects him to flash as a playmaker with enough touches that teammates and fans want more, given his skilled pass-catching, decent blocking flashes, and promising route running.
- 2026-04-09 — According to Paul Pertichese, guest analyst on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-09]] (not a tracked expert) [Dynasty]: Wake Forest's DeMond Claiborne (5'10', 188 lbs) is one of the few backs in this class with true stop-start, stack-multiple-cuts explosiveness, but his receiving skills are subpar for his size, and he lacks the frame to work near the goal line or pass-protect on third down -- raising real doubt about how much he'll actually play. Pertichese compared his evaluation to James Cook's, saying he had far fewer concerns about Cook as a receiver coming out, even though Cook also struggled as a rookie runner before developing into a good one. Pertichese would still gamble on Claiborne in the fourth round of the NFL draft for his rare big-play ability, but flagged him as the type of size/speed profile that historically 'doesn't make it' without a clear receiving role.
- 2026-04-09 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-09]]) [Dynasty]: both have DeMond Claiborne (sub-190 lbs) in their top tier of the 2026 RB class alongside [[KC Concepcion]]- and [[Omar Cooper Jr.]]-caliber prospects, projecting a first- or early-second-round NFL pick. Angelo comps him to [[James Cook]] for movement fluidity and calls him, with [[Jonah Coleman]], one of the two best low-red-zone backs in the class, praising his contact balance, initial acceleration, pass-pro communication (a 'co-pilot' role for the QB) and football IQ. Waldman independently landed on the same James Cook comp plus Charlie Garner, and said if not for the fumbles Claiborne would be his outright RB1 in the class over Emmett Johnson (the two are separated by a tenth of a grading point), and the most likely alternative to [[Jeremiah Love]] as eventual RB1 of the class. Both flag size and ball security as the real risk: 9 fumbles over two seasons, 7 of them in a single season, though the other three seasons were clean on high workloads -- which Waldman reads as possibly one bad year rather than a chronic flaw. Angelo expects him gettable in rounds 2-3 of rookie drafts.
- 2026-04-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-13]]) [Dynasty]: he is 'feeling it' on the 188-lb Wake Forest back despite size concerns, comping him to James Cook (about 194-195 lbs) and noting he could add five to seven pounds of muscle. Says Claiborne has a proven track record of volume and working between the tackles, calling him a smart runner with 'a lot of Shady McCoy' in his game and a Jamaal Charles-like ability to dictate contact without absorbing big hits -- possibly the best pound-for-pound playmaker in the class due to speed and pace manipulation. Flags a fumble-rate spike to seven last year versus just two across his prior three years as a starter. Says he wouldn't be shocked if Claiborne ends up the best back in the class if [[Jeremiah Love]] disappoints. Best scheme fits named: a Bills-style gap offense with two tight ends and a rushing QB (like [[James Cook]]); pairing with [[Cam Skattebo]] in New York as the 'dash' to Skattebo's 'smash'; complementing [[Rico Dowdle]] in Pittsburgh once [[Jalen Warren]] departs; or pushing [[R.J. Harvey]] in Denver, whom he says Claiborne is a smarter runner than. Projects him as a fourth- or fifth-round pick who'll have to work his way up.
- 2026-06-25 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Claiborne is his 'big one' among late-round rookie backs -- Minnesota's backfield lacks explosiveness behind [[Justin Jefferson]] (Angelo says the team had one run over 20 yards all last season) with an aging [[Aaron Jones]] moving toward a third-down role and [[Jordan Mason]] merely 'fine' outside the Shanahan system, leaving real runway for Claiborne to pass [[Xavier Guillory|Xavier Scott]] on the depth chart. [[Matt Waldman]] backed the grade, noting Claiborne's explosive-run-rate metric in college looked worse than an equivalent check on [[James Cook]]'s college numbers despite Claiborne having comparable or better physical tools -- Waldman explained he intentionally ignores explosive-run-rate metrics when scouting because they're too noisy with offensive-line and competition-caliber context baked in, preferring to isolate a runner's raw physical/technical skills from his blocking (citing past cases like [[Ahmad Bradshaw]] and Joseph Addai). Both hosts said they've each personally drafted Claiborne in rookie/IDP drafts this cycle.
- 2026-07-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-28]]): Harris is firmly not buying camp hype on Claiborne (also flagged by co-host Andy Behrens, not tracked, as a 'nonsense fantasy riser'), comparing him to a long line of small, fast, converted-slot-receiver backs who never became real fantasy factors -- Dri Archer, Chris Rainey, Leon Washington, Garrett Wolfe, Chad Morton -- and to last year's version of the same archetype, Brashard Smith. Harris profiled Claiborne at #75 in his Almanac and notes he's listed at 5'10/188, arguing De'Von Achane is the exception that broke the mold only because Miami built him into a true feature back rather than a gadget slot piece, which nothing suggests Minnesota intends to do here. Both hosts expect a hype week (5 catches, 60 yards) followed by a quiet one, and neither believes Claiborne is a real threat to 15-carry volume even if Aaron Jones gets hurt.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls Claiborne, a small-framed back, 'pound for pound the most exciting back in this class,' comping his size to rookie-year [[James Cook]] and [[Aaron Jones]]. He's drafting Claiborne in 20-round leagues purely as a dart throw given a crowded Minnesota backfield ([[Aaron Jones]] injury-prone, [[Jordan Mason]] has 'spit the bit' before). Waldman's path to Claiborne mattering runs through Minnesota's lead back faltering or getting hurt; if that happens Claiborne could see promising run, but Waldman says that value may not arrive until next year -- he'd still take him now in dynasty.
