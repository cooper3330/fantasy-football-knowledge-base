# Colson Loveland

## Expert Takes

- 2026-01-20 — According to James Koh (untracked co-host) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-20]] [Dynasty]: rookie Colson Loveland had 'some really bad drops and missed opportunities' in the Divisional Round loss but also produced one of the most impressive individual plays of the game for any Bears player -- singled up as a blocker on Jared Verse on Caleb Williams' backside and shutting him down. Koh named Loveland the Bears rookie closest to superstar status relative to his position, ahead of Luther Burden and Rome Odunze, while stressing nobody on that young offense is fully there yet.
- 2026-08-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-18]]): guest Jeff Bell (not tracked) says Loveland is the player who 'eats first' in Ben Johnson's Bears offense, projecting him for a 23% target share -- ahead of both [[Rome Odunze]] and [[Luther Burden III]] -- and points to Johnson's first two play calls as Bears OC both being targets to Loveland as an early signal of how the offense wants to attack. Both hosts rank Loveland ahead of both Bears WRs individually in their overall rankings.
- 2026-09-22 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-09-22]]) [Dynasty]: Loveland has one catch for three yards through two games, which Harmon called one of the most shocking developments of the young season. His route share with 3+ WRs on the field fell from 85% in Week 1 to under 70% in Week 2, as Chicago left him in to pass-protect against Minnesota's blitz-heavy gameplan (the Vikings blitzed Caleb Williams on 82% of dropbacks, a top-3 rate since 2018) rather than releasing him. He also got beaten badly in coverage by Dallas Turner on the game's final defensive snap. Harmon isn't worried long-term, arguing Week 1 was actually unlucky -- Caleb Williams missed him on a couple of his only bad throws that game, including a would-be touchdown -- and that Week 2's near-shutout was more about scheme (keeping him in to block against the blitz) than any decline in his ability. Harmon still wants to see him get more involved given the added value he holds beyond blocking.
