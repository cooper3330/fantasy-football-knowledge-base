# Skylar Bell

- 2026-04-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman ranked Skylar Bell notably higher than consensus (widely mocked round 3) largely because of an explicit exception he rarely grants: Bell is a serial 'clap attacker' like Quentin Johnston and Marvin Harrison Jr, a technique that normally predicts NFL drop trouble, but Waldman said Bell is one of the rare exceptions -- comping the archetype to Golden Tate, Terry McLaurin, Early Doucet and Hall of Famer Marvin Harrison Sr., all serial clap-attackers who still had strong NFL careers -- because Bell has big hands and can also catch with correct technique when needed, without a major drop problem. Without the exception Bell would grade roughly 10 spots lower, around the back of Waldman's top 15. He gave Bell a star-caliber elusiveness/vision grade with an aspirational (not projected) Ja'Marr Chase after-the-catch comp, citing one specific curvilinear cutback run against Boston College as among the sharpest angle-bends he's charted, and projected Bell as a potential big-play, high-volume weapon if his catching translates.
- 2026-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon's flag-plant sleeper beyond the top names, generally slotted WR20-24 in industry consensus per Waldman. Waldman is a 'big fan' -- Bell is a serial 'clap-attacker' at the catch point but is one of only a handful of players Waldman has studied in 21 years (alongside Golden Tate and, unstudied, Marvin Harrison Sr.) whose drop rate stays manageable despite that technique. He comps Bell's open-field vision/agility favorably to KC Concepcion's, and stylistically to Ja'Marr Chase's size/build (not Chase's route running or power) and more directly to old-school return man Laveranues Coles. Harmon hadn't finished charting but flagged an early clean sample: only 4 drops on 101 catches over 141 targets last season, with solid contested-catch marks. Both expect him to rise into early day-two range.
- 2026-04-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-09]]) [Dynasty]: both have Skylar Bell in their WR top-five, same tier as [[KC Concepcion]] and [[Omar Cooper Jr.]], grading him a late-first/early-second-round talent on speed, explosion and contested-catch ability. Waldman highlighted him as a 'serial clap attacker' (trapping the ball against the palm rather than webbing it with the fingertips) who nonetheless has an unusually low drop rate -- around four career drops -- unlike most serial clap-attackers; he compared Bell favorably to the rare group of successful clap-attackers he's tracked since the late '90s (Golden Tate, Early Doucet, [[Terry McLaurin]], and Marvin Harrison Sr., the former Colts great, not the current rookie) versus busts in the same mold like Quentin Johnston. Waldman is willing to grade Bell that high as a projected exception to the rule given the technique shows up clean at times, though he flagged risk of the flaw magnifying early in the NFL and a possible temporary confidence dip. Both cited a standout curvilinear cut against Boston College as a signature, class-defining play.
- 2026-06-16 — According to Matt Bove ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-06-16]]): Bove is confident rookie Skylar Bell makes the Bills' final four locked-in receivers (with DJ Moore, Khalil Shakir and Keon Coleman) because of the draft capital used on him. He described a red-zone drill where Josh Allen's fastball hit Bell's hands and popped straight up, leaving him visibly stunned by NFL-level velocity. Bove says the staff views Bell as a versatile chess piece who can line up at X, Z, or even in the slot, and values him partly as insurance if DJ Moore or another receiver goes down.
- 2026-06-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-06-22]]) [Dynasty]: [[Skylar Bell]] is an interesting name to watch in Buffalo's receiver room -- Harmon thinks he fits best as an off-ball speed Z receiver who could be moved around and get some slot reps, alongside a similar plan floated for [[Keon Coleman]].
- 2026-07-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-20]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman called [[Skylar Bell]] worth monitoring late in redraft and best ball at his WR106 ADP -- he's a big-play option who can line up in multiple spots and could squeeze out Josh Palmer or [[Keon Coleman]] for the wide receiver three role, or step up if Khalil Shakir gets hurt, but it's a boom-or-bust dart throw even that late.
- 2026-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-23]]): Harmon said Buffalo's Day 3 rookie Skylar Bell, projected as a 'speed Z' option, went in the right range of the draft for his skill set and could open the season as the Bills' No. 3 receiver over [[Keon Coleman]] or [[Josh Palmer]] -- a bar he called 'low to clear' -- which he framed as a big win for Bell if it happens, though Harmon views [[DJ Moore]]'s addition as Buffalo's only real difference-maker acquisition this offseason.
- 2026-08-10 — According to Bob Harris and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-10]]) [Dynasty]: reports out of Buffalo camp have Bell dropping too many passes despite running good routes and generating separation. Waldman -- who says he 'rarely' invokes an exception to his rule about receivers with hands issues -- was tempted to make Bell that exception, but concludes the technique flaws are being magnified early in camp against higher-level competition and expects him to shore it up. He points to Khalil Shakir's rookie year, when Shakir also dropped a fair number of passes and barely played despite flashes, as the comp. Neither host was drafting Bell in redraft leagues anyway, and both frame the drops as another chance to buy low on him in dynasty; the reporting suggests he'll make the roster but may not see the field on game days early behind other depth-chart pieces at receiver.
- 2026-09-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-09-01]]): projects Bell as a low-volume, speed-based Z receiver, noting he was personally mixed on him pre-draft even though some evaluators were higher. He'd be surprised if Bell is a big-time, highly involved player early in his rookie year, expecting Buffalo's passing offense to flow primarily through [[DJ Moore]] instead.
