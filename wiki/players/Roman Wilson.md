# Roman Wilson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-01]]): flashed at Michigan despite a run-heavy Blake Corum-centered offense that limited his opportunities, and graded as one of the best three receivers at the Senior Bowl through the early week of practice — a highlight one-handed catch falling out of bounds, strong reps in one-on-ones, and a "really good deep beat" Angelo expects to show up as a high-4.3s 40 at the combine. Reads him as "creeping up" and solidifying himself in the top 100 of the class.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-01]]), relaying secondhand Senior Bowl reports rather than his own tape (he hadn't watched the event live): names Wilson as one of the prospects he specifically needs to revisit based on what he's hearing.

- 2024-02-29 — According to **James Koh** (co-host — not a tracked expert) and **Kevin Knight** (Falcons reporter, SB Nation's Falcoholic — guest, not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]): Koh calls his athletic testing profile potentially "one of the best overall ... we've ever seen from a wideout," with standout fast-twitch quickness that popped at the Senior Bowl despite a limited target share in Michigan's run-heavy, Blake-Corum-centered offense; Knight, separately, names him one of the Senior Bowl players "everyone" was impressed by and a name Atlanta could look at on day two.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): has learned how to deliver footwork and hand counters against press and off coverage at the top of his stems — "but he hasn't learned how to perform them." Draws the distinction to route trees as sentence diagrams: they show the components but not how to be creative within the structure. Right now Wilson "runs routes like someone who has memorized the process, but hasn't figured out how to say anything compelling with the process."
- 2024-04-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]], finished full-sample Reception Perception profile ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-09]]): "probably my least favorite" of the receivers charted so far this class — below the 20th percentile versus both man and zone coverage on every coverage form, only a 50% success rate versus press despite limited reps. Calls the combination of "needs to play the slot" and "11th percentile versus zone" contradictory ("those two things are just, they're butting heads"). A one-note, linear route runner with little tempo variation or route deception, which he thinks makes him an easy cover for good corners in man; went down on first contact on 88% of his charted in-space attempts, so not a tackle-breaker either. Route-tree splits are inconsistent — an excellent 93.3% success rate on the dig (9% of his routes) but subpar on the slant, which Harmon attributes to the slant specifically requiring free releases Wilson can't reliably win against press. Weighs his prospect profile below [[Puka Nacua]]'s and [[Tank Dell]]'s (both late Day 2 picks in their draft classes) and below Jalen Tolbert's and Deiadra "Diami" Brown's early-fourth-round profiles; lands him in the [[Rondale Moore]]/[[John Metchie III]] range — an early Day 3 grade, more real-life value than fantasy value, a situational speed-slot piece rather than a high-volume slot receiver. Explicitly lower than draft-industry consensus on Wilson; a notable reversal from the more optimistic secondhand Senior Bowl buzz relayed on the show on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]].
- 2024-05-02 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-02]]): Waldman is skeptical of Roman Wilson in Pittsburgh, saying he sees the athlete but 'not remotely' the technician, and rates incumbent Calvin Austin III as a much better route technician despite being undersized, adding Austin simply hasn't stayed healthy. Waldman thinks Pittsburgh — a franchise he says has a reputation for finding underrated receivers ([[Diontae Johnson]], Emmanuel Sanders, Antonio Brown) more than developing them — will likely look elsewhere for a true WR2 in 2025 rather than hand the role to Wilson, especially given reported interest in [[Brandon Aiyuk]]. He noted Wilson's blocking effort fits the Steelers' identity but that alone won't be enough given light production at Michigan.
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): pushed back on the common assumption that Wilson is the answer at Steelers WR2, saying 'a lot of people are thinking Roman Wilson... maybe not.' He sees Wilson as best suited to a third-option, big-play-inside role for now, comparable to a Skyy Moore-type prospect, with a path to becoming a good slot or eventual outside receiver but not the immediate answer the market expects.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon says he was relatively higher on Wilson pre-draft than most, projecting him as a speed-slot/flanker role player best used on vertical and intermediate play-action routes -- he posted a 93.3% success rate on dig routes on 9.1% of his college routes. Harmon takes the under on Wilson matching the roughly 140 targets Diontae Johnson typically drew, calling him a narrow-application rather than complete player, but thinks his specific skill set (vertical/intermediate shots off play action) projects well into Pittsburgh's scheme need after Johnson's departure.
- 2024-07-04 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-04]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo described Wilson as a 'dirty work' receiver -- a great, physical blocker who is reliable but didn't produce much at the collegiate level and isn't a 'world beater' talent -- and said Arthur Smith likely prefers him over [[Calvin Austin III]] because Wilson extends the run game (motioning in to pick up a linebacker) in a way that fits Smith's conservative scheme. Waldman said he would prefer Austin's dynamic upside as a play-caller, but both agreed Wilson's role looks more locked-in as things currently stand.
- 2024-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-23]]): Wilson was not one of Harmon's favourite receivers in the class and not a guy he was high on, though he was not disgusted by the third-round landing spot. What Wilson does well — working the middle of the field, crossing routes, play-action work, and blocking — makes him a poor fit for many offenses but a sensible one for Arthur Smith in Pittsburgh. Harmon's conclusion is a utility projection rather than a fantasy one: 'I don't think he's ever going to be a highly targeted player, but from a utility perspective, I think this is a good spot for him.' James Koh (not tracked) noted Wilson impressed at the Senior Bowl but that the Reception Perception profile was less stellar than the all-star-week reputation suggested.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): Waldman is bearish for 2024 and calls Wilson a long-term bet. He does not trust Wilson to get off the line of scrimmage or to win with stems and setups against man coverage, thought he was a bad route runner needing work coming out, and expects Pittsburgh to figure that out by Week 5 — even suggesting Scotty Miller could see more ball than Wilson this season. The slot could mask some of it if Wilson reads coverage well. Guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT tracked) adds that camp and OTA news has not been positive and that Wilson is being coached hard; Waldman's response is that he needs it. Both read the team's talk of trading for another receiver as a sign coaches aren't happy with the group.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]): Wilson is his example of the RSP's other job — flagging players who will NOT be ready despite consensus optimism. Coming out, Waldman says, Wilson could not run routes at a high level, could not release from the line of scrimmage at a high level, and was a raw player who could catch and run in space. Unless he makes massive gains, [[Calvin Austin III]] is the dude in Pittsburgh. Waldman also notes Wilson got off on the wrong foot with Mike Tomlin, which is not a great idea in Pittsburgh — though he allows Tomlin is the kind of coach who can accept a player's evolution if anyone can.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]) [Dynasty]: discussing whether Pittsburgh has the usual next-man-up receiver waiting behind [[George Pickens]], Waldman said he does not know that Wilson is that guy — he had a lot to learn, and the team appeared to expect immediate production. He used it as a caution against logo-scouting pro teams for player development: the Steelers' history of hits came with prospects who had a better talent profile coming out, so crediting the organisation rather than the input talent is the error.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): on Jeremy Fowler's ESPN report that the Steelers believe Wilson is ready for a year-two leap and is a different player, Waldman split the answer — he feels the idea, but says 'fuck it' to the fact that it is being talked about in May, which he called the standard window for leaking flattering information to reporters to build a young player's confidence. He will not buy in until he sees Wilson beating defenders one-on-one at the boundary on timing routes and deeper routes against genuine press man; if Wilson is playing slot, that evidence never appears and the job is easier anyway. He is skeptical the higher draft capital automatically means Wilson overtakes [[Calvin Austin III]]. The tell will come in scrimmages, preseason and early-season route running and releases against physical coverage.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): on Mike DeFabo's report that Wilson has a chance to prove he deserves a role, Waldman says there is nothing earth-shattering in that phrasing — plenty of players have an opportunity. He sees the Steelers passing game as running through [[DK Metcalf]], [[Calvin Austin III]] and [[Pat Freiermuth]], with a dose of [[Darnell Washington]], leaving Wilson to fit in around them. His projection is a 300-500 yard receiver if he performs well, with no massive upside barring injuries ahead of him and everything suddenly clicking. Waldman had him as a prospect with plenty still to learn, so he expects the ramp to take time.
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-13]]): Wilson is the intriguing name in an otherwise barren Pittsburgh room, entering his second year after a rookie season wrecked by injury, but Harmon was never high on him — he graded him a day-three player when the market had him higher. His prospect charting had him inside on 65.6% of sampled snaps and off the line on 64.1%, with a 93.3% success rate on digs that Harmon calls crazy high but essentially nothing else on the route tree. Harmon's conclusion is that Wilson only works as a vertical or dig/crosser slot receiver, which is a nice theoretical fit for Arthur Smith, except that Pittsburgh's 12-personnel (and reportedly even four-tight-end) tendencies would push him outside instead. He is a willing blocker but small. Harmon also rated [[Calvin Austin III]] a useful player, though also a speed slot, and called this the worst receiver supporting cast in the NFL — worse than the Giants, who at least have [[Darius Slayton]].
- 2025-08-21 — According to Jake Ciely of The Athletic (guest, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]] [Redraft (Standard)]: Wilson was his No. 1, deepest sleeper — still on the board at the end of 18-round drafts and well outside the top 200 because nobody wants Pittsburgh pass catchers. Ciely's case: a third-round pick, not a sixth or seventh, taken after Mike Tomlin coached him at the Senior Bowl and matched him against Quinyon Mitchell to see what he could do; [[Aaron Rodgers]] should still throw 500-plus attempts and [[DK Metcalf]] cannot catch all of them, so somebody gets the targets, and he prefers Wilson to [[Calvin Austin III]], whom he finds gimmicky, or a [[Jonnu Smith]] two-tight-end bet. [[Chris Harris]] loved it as the living embodiment of the super deeps — a player who has done nothing and did not play last year, who may just be a third-round bust, but whose Week 1 six-for-80 without a touchdown would be exactly the flyer this list exists to pre-load. Both flagged it could end up a [[Nick Westbrook-Ikhine]] type situation.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Wilson is guest BRANDON FUNSTON's number three waiver name. Funston liked his college skill set, notes good press out of Pittsburgh, and asks who exists in that passing game beyond [[DK Metcalf]] — he likes how Wilson's speed would pair with Metcalf and treats last year as a reset since Wilson barely got on the field. Harris grants that [[Calvin Austin III]] beat Wilson out fairly easily last year but agrees the excuse holds: Wilson got hurt early in camp as a rookie and the show makes that allowance for rookies it likes all the time, so a highly drafted receiver deserves another chance and both could be on the field a lot. His caveat is that you would need to see something from [[Aaron Rodgers]] you have not seen to get a secondary receiver going who is not one of the two tight ends. Both agree these are radar names, not adds.
