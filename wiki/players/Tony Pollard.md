# Tony Pollard

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: sees real rebound odds for 2024 (roughly 60/70% he's not the diminished version seen in 2023), but flags a genuine chance the decline sticks. Relays a framework from [[Brandon Angelo]] — a performance specialist who works with pro athletes and was a running back at Purdue — distinguishing "healthy enough to play" from "healthy enough to play at the level you were drafted to play at"; Pollard's two significant 2023 injuries left him only in the former category. Comps the rebound case to DeMarco Murray's arc (a down year leaving Dallas, then a huge year in Tennessee), and expects Pollard's price to drop enough to make the bet worthwhile. Separately, criticizes how Mike McCarthy actually used him in 2023 — deployed in "limited doses" as a change-of-pace piece rather than sprung into space to use his speed (a J.K. Dobbins-in-Baltimore-style role would fit him better) — meaning situation and touches, not just health, will determine the rebound.
- 2024-01-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: a real reversal one week later — both now "f\*\*k it," down from the January 8 rebound-odds framing. Harms's mechanism: Pollard lacks the elite vision needed to find the crease once the field condenses (specifically red-zone touches, where Dallas kept trying to force him the ball), and "he lost a bit of his juice this year" as well — an explosiveness decline layered on top of the vision gap. Waldman folds this back into [[Healthy Enough to Play vs. Healthy Enough to Perform]]: "sometimes we forget that players who are healthy... are healthy enough to play, but not healthy enough to be the player we remember." The verdict isn't a total write-off — both still put a complementary, passing-down/space-creation role (a DeAndre Swift comp) in the "top 15 if everything breaks right" range, but the workhorse-role case from a week earlier is gone, and the margin for error is now "so much finer."
- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): cited only as a reference point, not a new grade — floated speed/power/receiving archetype that "everyone wanted" Pollard to be (though Waldman himself says he never bought it) is used as the explanatory comp for what a free-agent [[Joe Mixon]] would give Dallas if signed, explicitly because Mixon actually delivers the two-way (rushing plus receiving) profile Pollard's pre-2023 hype implied.

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]): grouped with [[Austin Ekeler]] and [[Travis Kelce]] under the same read — their "poor showings" last season likely owed more to health than to skill decline. For Pollard specifically, repeats the lingering workload-management concern, tying it to Dallas's own previously-stated concerns about the same issue with Ezekiel Elliott (not tracked) — a continuation of the [[Healthy Enough to Play vs. Healthy Enough to Perform]] framework already on this page.

- 2024-03-01 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host on *RSP Film and Theory* — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-01]] [Dynasty]: says he was "kind of out" on Pollard heading into 2023 given he was put into an unproven workhorse role, but is "buying back into" him now that the market has swung the other way and assumes he's washed — Pollard was demonstrably productive in a specific complementary role before, and would likely reclaim real value in that role again. Pressure-tested Pollard's own explanation for his down year (an interview, possibly with [[Matt Harmon]], where Pollard said he didn't feel right until the Carolina game) by comparing his production before vs. after that game — found it "exactly the same," undercutting the in-season-recovery narrative. Ranks him third of the "Big Four" free agents, behind [[Josh Jacobs]] and [[Austin Ekeler]].
- 2024-03-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode [Dynasty]: Pollard's age profile still suits his general preference for buying free-agent RBs off a down year, but he ties the case explicitly to role and full recovery from injury rather than health alone — "whether he really bounces back from injury fully and plays to pre-injury level, Tony Pollard is there." Groups him with [[Austin Ekeler]] in the "Column A" receiving-back tier of the free-agent class, as opposed to the bell-cow "Column B" of [[Josh Jacobs]]/[[Derrick Henry]].

- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: predicts he lands with the Philadelphia Eagles in free agency — "totally feeling that" — reasoning the Eagles would get in one player what they'd hoped to get separately from Kenneth Gainwell and DeAndre Swift (both not tracked pages).


- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: reacts to the finalized Tennessee signing as "short-term fuck it" for [[Tyjae Spears]]'s redraft value, but reads the contract structure (with a likely out in year two or three) as Tennessee hedging rather than fully committing to Pollard as the starter — if Pollard struggles or gets hurt, Spears is still positioned to take over. Calls Pollard "the shiny object" who may end up priced too high for what he actually provides. Separately flags Bill Callahan (new Titans assistant, HC Brian Callahan's father) as the real story to watch, crediting him as one of the league's best offensive-line minds.

- 2024-04-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-15]] [Best Ball]: reacting to new Titans OC Nick Holz calling Pollard and [[Tyjae Spears]] "interchangeable, 1A/1B," Harris hopes the news depresses Pollard's best-ball ADP (currently RB22) without inflating Spears too much, preferring Spears as the better pass protector, an equal-or-better receiver, and a slightly better between-the-tackles runner. Waldman says he holds few shares of either back at current price and likes other players better at their respective cost points, but still credits Pollard's per-touch efficiency when used correctly — his breakaway run rate and top-end speed have been "impressive."
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]): the market flipped hard on Pollard and Tyjae Spears this offseason -- Pollard was broadly disliked in fantasy circles last year (mostly a touchdown-driven down year) while Spears' dynasty value spiked after Derrick Henry left, yet Tennessee then signed Pollard and he's now going 1-2 rounds ahead of Spears in best ball drafts. JJ still prefers Spears, citing Pollard's age (entering year six, past the point where middle-round RBs typically decline) and speculating his 2023 dip could be the start of a talent downturn rather than pure touchdown regression. Waldman raised Brandon Angelo's 'healthy enough to play vs. healthy enough to perform' framework as a possible explanation for Pollard's down year. New OC Brian Callahan has publicly called Pollard and Spears 'interchangeable' in a straight timeshare rather than divided roles.
- 2024-11-14 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14 (Age Cliffs and Dented Cans)]] [Dynasty]: Pollard is 'a classic dented can' and the cautionary tale for efficiency metrics in the same breath. He was very useful behind [[Ezekiel Elliott]], got labelled a star on 'efficiency' — Harstad says he will always do air quotes around running back efficiency — then underwhelmed as the lead back in Dallas, though he still finished a top-20 back in 2023. Because he hurt the people who paid up, he was completely written off; now Tennessee wants him to be useful and he is producing again. Harstad's conclusion is that there are maybe five to ten legitimate star backs in the league and everyone else is a skill set looking for a role: fill a role, get the workload, and the production follows.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Pollard is the player who fits the weapon-back mold on talent but whose organization Waldman does not believe will use him that way. He has the vertical speed that [[Tyjae Spears]] lacks — Spears is quick but not fast — so the clean design is Pollard in the slot with Spears in the backfield, but Waldman said the Titans are not conceptually equipped and there is a ridiculous lack of creativity in that offense. He noted Pollard played receiver in college. Both hosts drew the parallel to [[Antonio Gibson]], whom both forecast as a slot receiver rather than a running back coming out and who they felt was never properly deployed [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Pollard is both hosts' round-six safe pick. Guest Scott Pianowski (Yahoo, not tracked) likes him because his touches look secure, [[Tyjae Spears]] is dinged up, and Tennessee's offense should at least matriculate the ball if rookie [[Cam Ward]] merely keeps the ship afloat. Harris agrees, adding that Spears's injury removes the camp-hype risk of Pollard sliding further — he is what he is draft-wise. Harris's caveat is the same one that frustrated Dallas: the Titans had only eight running back carries from inside the opponent three all season, Pollard got three of them and converted one, and Spears got more; Pollard has a hard time sticking his nose in the end zone and wants to bounce too many runs, so long term a coach may not fall in love with him — but for 2025 he looks really safe as a settling flex.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): with [[Tyjae Spears]] on IR for at least four games with a high ankle sprain, Pollard figures to get a bunch of work in September and probably will not be challenged much — rookie [[Kalel Mullings]] projects as the backup rather than a threat to the role.
- 2025-09-22 — According to guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]] [Redraft (Standard)]: Pollard 'missed his window' — the whole case for drafting him this summer was Weeks 1-4 while [[Tyjae Spears]] was on injured reserve, and after three really nice workloads the fantasy return is one total touchdown. Spears is expected back when first eligible in Week 5 (Week 6 if the bye intervenes). [[Chris Harris]] was slightly more forgiving: Pollard is fine, he is what he is, there is a lot of work there, and he should wind up okay as a flex — but both agreed the difference-making window has closed.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): 'Tony Pollard has been fine, and I think Tony Pollard is a flex — you're just going to keep plugging him in,' but with a persistent complaint: 'he always leaves meat on the bone, I always want him to hit a guy a little harder,' so if something changes it may be toward someone more physical on the outside. Guest SCOTT FISH (Fantasy Cares, NOT tracked) added that Pollard 'needs the volume to be a fantasy guy' — in Dallas he had far more explosive plays, and now he needs a lot of work just to make his points. Harris partly attributed that to the environment, since nobody can run through Tennessee's blocking, and maintained Pollard is still a pretty fast, pretty explosive player who just needs a runway.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: on a team he thinks is the worst in the league, Pollard is the only startable Titan — 'I think it's Pollard and pretty much nobody else.' In [[Tyjae Spears]]' return Pollard had the pure lead, 49 snaps to 17, with physical runs to open the game (Spears got a first-drive wildcat carry) and the fourth-quarter touchdown after [[Gunnar Helm]]'s fade toe-tap to the one. Harris notes Arizona looked to be trying to let Pollard score late to get the ball back, except one defender didn't get the message and knocked him down.
- 2025-10-10 — According to guest Cousin Josh (Detective Fish, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-10]]): Pollard is a cold start he will not buy low on. He is around RB24 with roughly 6.5 points a game absent touchdown rescue, which Josh calls worse than an average start for a full-time back who had the job outright while [[Tyjae Spears]] was dinged up early. The reasons to stay away: the Titans have been far worse than expected, Pollard likely gives third-down duties back to Spears, and the schedule (at Chiefs, at San Francisco, at Colts, at Seattle) reads like games that get out of hand. Harris agrees, with a methodological caveat he insists on: he won't rank Pollard RB37 in a given week because he is positive about that week's game flow, but over the sweep of a season, once a team is confirmed bad, it is fair to say Pollard will be playing snaps without a lead. Josh's one exception: a 1-4 team could buy him cheap for the Raiders game as a touchdown dart throw, and if he plays decently that is the moment to sell.
- 2025-10-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Jake Seeley (The Athletic, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-14 (Week 7 Waiver Wire & More W6 Film)]]): Seeley notes Pollard had all the touches for Tennessee last year and still finished as only RB24 in points per game; this year, with [[Tyjae Spears]] now taking passing-game work, Pollard can't even crack RB24 and sits at RB28. Seeley says it's clear Pollard is now the pure volume/early-down piece while Spears offers the higher fantasy ceiling as the pass-catching, explosive option -- Harris agrees they're not chasing Pollard on waivers.
- 2025-10-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Dave Kluge of FootballGuys.com (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-16 (Week 7 Flex Decisions)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: both hosts agree Tony Pollard's poor efficiency is circumstantial rather than a talent decline -- last week he had only 10 carries with 9 total yards before contact (under 1 yard per carry before being hit), and faced 8-plus defenders in the box on 6 of those 10 carries, a 60% loaded-box rate that led all backs. Kluge wondered whether the 28-year-old, coming off a heavy 2024 workload, is starting to feel the toll and prefers [[Tajay Spears]] as this week's flex given the new coaching staff and Spears' higher ceiling; Harris was not ready to move off Pollard without seeing an actual change in usage first, calling his situation 'perhaps the worst hand in the NFL.'
- 2025-10-17 — According to co-host Josh (not tracked), with [[Chris Harris]] agreeing ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-17]]) [Redraft (Standard), Dynasty]: Tennessee is the lone team in Josh's 'please discontinue this wagon model' tier -- he says he wants 'no part' of the Titans offense, including [[Cam Ward]] and [[Tyjae Spears]], and would sell everything at 50-60 cents on the dollar, explicitly including selling Pollard at a loss. Harris agreed and added Cleveland to the same 'discontinue' list, though both hosts think a Browns player like [[Quinshon Judkins]] would still fetch more in trade than a Titans player like Pollard, since Cleveland's offense is merely matchup-dependent rather than uniformly bad.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Pollard was the main Titans back while the game was competitive, though most of his production came in the fourth quarter with the game decided. Harris pushed back on guest Jake Ciely (not tracked), who'd said on last week's show that he doesn't think Pollard is very good -- Harris said 'he's okay,' but the bigger issue is a non-functional Tennessee offense with no blocking (the QB gave up an unhit fumble under pressure) rather than Pollard's own talent.
- 2025-10-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-28]]): Pollard is drawing trade rumors ahead of the deadline, which is part of why Harris and guest Scott Fish (not tracked) are both stashing [[Tajay Spears]] as a speculative handcuff behind him.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): 'rumors of [[Tyjae Spears]] quote-unquote taking over the Tennessee backfield were greatly exaggerated'. Pollard started, started well — the first drive was mostly because of him — and got the red-zone wildcat snap that Spears had gotten a week earlier, nearly scoring. Guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert) partly dissents, saying 'the Spears takeover might have been more real' but that Tennessee probably did not expect this game to be close, and a close game is more of a Pollard game environment. Both agree the deeper problem is that Tennessee may be 'the rare zero-start offense' — the team follows through on promises to involve young players, but the passing volume is so low and efficiency so bad that it does not matter.
- 2025-12-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Pollard was 'kind of wrecking' on Tennessee's first drive against Jacksonville (three carries for 36 yards), but the Titans fell behind big after a Chimere Dike muffed kickoff return and never got the offense going again the rest of the game, including a missed fourth-down connection with Pollard right before halftime.
- 2025-12-09 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Pollard broke a career-long 65-yard touchdown run and added a second score after a Shedeur Sanders interception, doing clearly good physical work on tape. Asked directly whether this merits ranking Pollard significantly higher for Week 15 vs. San Francisco, Harris says he understands the argument but won't rank him that high given how bad Tennessee's overall offense is -- though he'd rather start Pollard than Ashton Jeanty if forced to pick between the two struggling backfields, and admits he could be wrong.
- 2025-12-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-16]]): Tennessee's offensive line played well for a second straight week, and [[Tony Pollard]] benefited, running effectively behind it. Harris grouped Pollard with [[Breece Hall]], [[Quinshon Judkins]] and [[Ashton Jeanty]] as good players who can produce if their circumstances improve, even from otherwise bad situations. With the line trending up two games running, Harris said he'd 'be a little more aggressive' with Pollard for a Week 16 matchup against a Chiefs defense that's still solid but playing out the string (with Chris Jones' hamstring a question mark), calling him a sneaky path to a top-20 RB week.
- 2025-12-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-26]]): Pollard was his #4 undervalued player. He said December showed Pollard 'doesn't stink' even playing behind a bad Titans offense, and while he doesn't expect a world-beater season, he pegged Pollard as a likely round-6 pick who returns RB2 value if the offense is merely competent -- noting he's also exactly the profile Zero-RB-style drafters would target, which could push his ADP up if that trend catches on. Cousin Josh (untracked co-host) agreed it was a good pick and guessed Pollard goes closer to round 7.
- 2026-01-02 — Cousin Josh (guest co-host, not a tracked expert), speaking on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-02]] [Redraft (Standard)]: named Pollard his 'Chipotle Award' recipient -- a player he doesn't hate but who keeps making him sick as an owner and yet he can't quit. Pollard finished with 1,034 rushing yards and 189 receiving yards at age 28. Despite the frustration, Josh says he'd likely draft Pollard again around the fifth or sixth round in 2026, betting on continued opportunity behind a young quarterback and an offensive line expected to improve, and notes this category has now gone to a player he personally owned and was frustrated by in each of the last two seasons.
- 2026-01-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Ben Gretsch (untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-15]]): Pollard turns 29 in April with one year and a $6.7M base left, none guaranteed -- cutting him saves $7.2M against a $2M cap hit. Both note he closed the season strong: three straight 100-yard rushing games in December (starting with long touchdowns vs. Cleveland) plus an 85-yard week, finishing at 4.5 YPC against stacked boxes, and he's now strung together three straight years of 275+ touches and 1,200+ scrimmage yards after starting his career as a change-of-pace back. Gretsch calls the decision a 'luxury' question for Tennessee's new coach given where the roster is in its rebuild, while Harris says it 'wouldn't shock' him if the new staff moves on anyway. Both later note that if the Titans cut ties with [[Calvin Ridley]] to free cap room, that actually raises the odds they keep and pay Pollard instead.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris said he's 'intrigued' by [[Tony Pollard]], jokingly calling himself a 'subscriber' rather than someone who merely owns stock in him, noting Pollard was solid last year at nearly 1,300 yards even though touchdowns were rough on a bad Titans offense. He's optimistic about offensive upgrades around him -- [[Wan'Dale Robinson]] signed, [[Carnell Tate]] drafted, and a likely year-two leap for [[Cam Ward]] -- and floated Pollard as a potential 'warp zone' breakout, in the same mold as last year's [[Travis Etienne Jr.]], available in the middle-to-late rounds. Harris was clear he's never called Pollard a true top-10 back and doesn't expect that to change, just that he's a good, underpriced starting-level option given how much the market has soured on him.
- 2026-07-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Dave Kluge (footballguys.com, untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-10]]): Harris calls Tony Pollard 'cromulent' -- a fine, affordable starting-quality veteran -- and says he's always been squarely in the middle on him, drafting him in the 7th round when the market hated him and fading him in the 3rd round when the market loved him, calling last year's talk of him as a first-round Dallas-caliber back overblown. Both flag [[Nicholas Singleton]]'s health (broken foot suffered in spring) as the real swing factor for 2026: a healthy, involved Singleton in camp would push Pollard and [[Tajay Spears]] both down given it's a new coaching staff and Pollard/Spears are both on expiring contracts, but if Singleton is a camp non-factor, Pollard keeps the early-down job much like the last few years.
- 2026-07-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: both hosts think Tennessee will keep running Pollard, 29, hard in the final stretch of his career much like Indianapolis with [[Jonathan Taylor]] -- he handled 300-plus touches in 2023 and 2024 and 275 last year, and if the Titans wanted to lighten his load 'they already would have.' [[Nicholas Singleton]]'s arrival as a fifth-round rookie could muddy the picture slightly, but neither host expects a real workload cut for Pollard.
- 2026-08-04 — According to Ross Tucker (guest, not tracked), citing Greg Cosell's Titans minicamp report ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-04]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the Titans are planning to play up-tempo this season under a new offensive coordinator, hiring a center specifically suited to getting the offense lined up and snapped quickly. Harris flags the volume implications for Pollard directly -- more plays run means more possible touches -- while cautioning that up-tempo plans made in August frequently get abandoned once a team falls behind, and the new interior linemen enabling the plan (center and right guard) are unproven starters.
