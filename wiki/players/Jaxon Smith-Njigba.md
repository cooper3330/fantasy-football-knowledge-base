# Jaxon Smith-Njigba

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): tops his personal 2023 rookie wide receiver tier — ahead of [[Jordan Addison]], [[Puka Nacua]], [[Jayden Reed]] and [[Zay Flowers]] — despite not getting the volume, target priority, or quarterback play Waldman would have wanted. Notes he visibly built as the season went on and made bigger plays late, and says there's "a lot more to his game" based on his pre-draft evaluation than the box score shows. His stated risk to the ranking is entirely situational — whether Seattle resolves its own quarterback question, since "if you don't trust the Seahawks, you probably put Puka first."
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: Harstad's [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] treats his rookie season as a "post-it note," not a strong signal either direction — usage above average, efficiency held back by playing mostly in 3-WR sets behind DK Metcalf and Tyler Lockett, a known deflator (see the model's own methodology). Waldman adds a structural point beyond the model: even if Smith-Njigba outplayed Metcalf and Lockett on tape, their salaries make it politically difficult for Seattle to actually shift the offense toward him — comped to how it took a front-office fight (Doug Marrone/Leonard Fournette) or a Pete Carroll buy-in (Russell Wilson over Matt Flynn) to move a veteran-heavy pecking order. Reinforces that the outlook is more a role/politics question than a talent one.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): the "politics" blocker from January just weakened — new HC Mike McDonald inherits a receiver room where [[Tyler Lockett]]'s roster spot is now in real doubt given his cap hit, and Harmon explicitly floats Seattle wanting "to move JSN into more of a premier role" as a live offseason storyline, on top of DK Metcalf, Lockett and JSN still being called "one of the best three-person receiver trios in the NFL" as a unit.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast on the 2024 WR class ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): used as a grading-philosophy example — Smith-Njigba was Waldman's #1-graded receiver in the 2023 class, and would only be the #8 receiver in the much deeper 2024 class. Explicitly not a knock on JSN or the prior class: he played behind [[Tyler Lockett]] and DK Metcalf and "still managed to do pretty darn well when you look at him on film, not necessarily in a fantasy lens."
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]) [Dynasty]: JSN's rookie season was actually solid despite the 'disappointment' narrative -- 630 yards, 4 TDs, and an RP overall success rate of 69.7%, playing behind two superstar-tier receivers in [[Tyler Lockett]] and DK Metcalf on a Seahawks offense that still targeted WRs at the 10th-highest volume in the league (361 targets) even with Geno Smith at QB. Harmon flagged real weaknesses -- inconsistent hands with drops, below-average after-the-catch play, and a merely average press score -- but praised his separation in man coverage and one-on-one iso reps with a free release. He's still in 'discovery mode' on JSN's true ceiling and isn't projecting a monster season (explicitly pushing back on fans wanting 1,000 yards), but expects target share to open up as Lockett declines and Seattle's Ryan Grubb offense pushes downfield if pass protection improves. Co-host James Koh (untracked) agreed it was a fine rookie year given the circumstances.
