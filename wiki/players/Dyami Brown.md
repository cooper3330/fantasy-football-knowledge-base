# Dyami Brown

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: untracked co-host Bob Harris said he has been adding Brown late in drafts and asked whether he has value in a three-receiver lineup. Waldman called it 'six of one, half dozen of another' against [[D.J. Chark]] at the same cost, and said he would rather have Chark himself — Brown is a younger version of that profile, the guy who has stayed healthy but simply has not proven it, where Chark has proven it but cannot stay on the field. Waldman is bullish on [[Jayden Daniels]]' downfield passing, which is the mechanism by which Brown would hit, but flagged Daniels' decision-making speed as the worry.
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-13]]): [[Dyami Brown]]'s late run was nice but is a small body of work — 89 yards against Tampa Bay and 98 against Detroit in the playoffs, both of which Harmon flags as two of the weaker pass defenses in the league (Detroit especially with injuries), then 42 yards against Philadelphia in the elimination game. In mid-October nobody would have been saying Washington had to lock him up. Harmon liked the over-the-middle dimension he added and thinks Washington could bring him back on a short, low-money deal since he does not expect other teams to beat down his door. Both hosts agree he is a relatively one-dimensional deep threat, valuable mainly because playing opposite [[Terry McLaurin]] generates one-on-one looks; Washington finished eighth in deep yards in the NFL with a rookie quarterback.
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]) on Brown's one-year, $10M deal with Jacksonville: Harmon thinks he does two things. First, he is a better sacrificial X receiver than [[Gabe Davis]], who was terrible in Jacksonville last year, is on $13M a year and whom Harmon expects to be 'the most expensive game-day scratch all year'. Second, he lets Liam Cohen kick [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] inside — Cohen has been insistent about getting Thomas slot reps — with Brown taking the vertical stretch role outside. Harmon's RP prospect profile on Brown from his UNC days had him as a true vertical stretch receiver, but he saw a little run-after-catch ability flash very late in his Washington tenure. Koh calls it a smart, low-risk way to find out what they have.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-04-28]]): Brown is a useful role player in Jacksonville rather than a starter you must rely on, and Harmon's specific projection is that he can be the sacrificial X — letting neither [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] nor [[Travis Hunter]] be pinned to the boundary. He profiles him as a vertical receiver dating back to North Carolina who only showed the ability to run crossing routes at the very end of his time in Washington, and thinks that is why Jacksonville targeted him. Harmon adds that the new staff has not mentioned [[Gabe Davis]] once, which he says 'says a lot without saying something.'
- 2025-06-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Brown is a real part of the Jacksonville equation alongside [[Travis Hunter]] and [[Brian Thomas Jr.]], and people put off by his college yards-per-catch line are missing that with Sam Howell at North Carolina he was the designated field stretcher. Waldman noted Mike Dempsey of SiriusXM (not a tracked expert), who covers the Jaguars daily, reports Brown getting hammered with targets at every practice — while ESPN's Mike DiRocco (also not tracked) cautions that this is the annual pattern for Brown, who looks very good at OTAs and minicamps and less good in the regular season. Waldman gave weight to Brown's late-season run in Washington, which he thinks impressed the Jaguars, and floated Brown and [[Parker Washington]] combining for as many as 150 targets.
- 2025-06-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-30]]): asked whether Brown is a slam-dunk WR3 in Jacksonville, Waldman said no — he is not even the slam-dunk fourth or fifth option. He counts [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] as a high-priority receiving option, [[LeQuint Allen]] as a slot contributor, [[Parker Washington]] for situational touches given his rapport with [[Trevor Lawrence]], and [[Brenton Strange]] as likely the third-most-targeted player in the offense. That leaves Brown splitting targets with Washington as roughly the fifth option, and Waldman notes that if the fifth option in that offense returns starter-level value, then Trevor Lawrence is a top-three fantasy quarterback and a massive bargain. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed, bifurcating Brown into the May-June monster in shorts and shoulder pads versus the far less monstrous in-season version, while still taking late flier and stacking shares.
- 2025-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-01]]): Brown is the sacrificial X piece Jacksonville needed, and Harmon agrees with James Koh that he is a near-perfect roster fit. Coming out of North Carolina he ran a mostly vertical route tree on one side of the field, which Harmon puts in the [[Alec Pierce]] mold. His function in 11 personnel is to hold the X spot so [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and [[Travis Hunter]] can be moved inside — Harmon notes Thomas averaged over three yards per route run from the slot and that Hunter needs slot work too. Harmon also flags an underrated secondary use: Brown caught 18 screens last year for Washington under Kliff Kingsbury, so he can contribute in a Liam Coben screen game as well. Koh's version is simpler — they needed someone to take the top off, and if safeties creep down you throw it up to Brown.
- 2026-05-11 — According to Matt Waldman ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-11]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman called Dyami Brown 'a good fourth receiver' and nothing more in Washington's pecking order -- one of the players fantasy managers have been waiting on for a breakout that he doesn't think is coming, overshadowed in the receiver room by rookie [[Antonio Williams]] and others.
