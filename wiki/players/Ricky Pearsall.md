# Ricky Pearsall

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name inconsistently, including "Ricky Purcell"; normalized here to the real
> name of the Florida receiver, Ricky Pearsall.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-15 — Guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Dynasty]: names him his favorite sleeper in the 2024 class, explicitly comping him to [[Puka Nacua]] — not a highlight-reel athlete, but a clean-breaks, sure-handed, finishes-through-contact timing-route receiver whose fantasy outcome will be determined almost entirely by scheme fit and quarterback pairing rather than his own talent ceiling, the same way Nacua's rookie explosion depended on landing with Matthew Stafford and Sean McVay. Names Bobby Slowik and Ben Johnson as the kind of play-callers who could unlock him. [[Matt Waldman]] adds his own [[Puka Nacua]] aspirational comp for context (Golden Tate, another Stafford favorite) and floats a speculative fit reuniting Pearsall with [[Anthony Richardson]] in Indianapolis, potentially as the intermediate complement to [[Josh Downs]] if incumbent depth doesn't return.
- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]), on Senior Bowl prospects: says he's "the guy that's getting overlooked" relative to the more-hyped Georgia receiver Ladd McConkey (dismissed as no longer actually underrated, "because all y'all are talking about him now"). Watched him at Florida with Anthony Richardson and calls him what Alec Pierce "was supposed to be, with a better route game" — good adjustment skills, very good acrobatic catch-point skills, and route running Waldman explicitly praises. Predicts he'll generate real Senior Bowl buzz for his route running specifically, while cautioning that one-on-one drill settings inherently favor receivers over defensive backs and can make the tape look better than it is.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-01]]): the prediction plays out live during Senior Bowl week. Recalls watching Florida tape after Pearsall himself claimed to be "the best separator in the whole nation" and being skeptical — "then I watched him play. Ricky Pearsall can ball." Grades him a late Day 2/early Day 3 pick, projects an immediate-impact slot role in the NFL — "I think he's going to be like your Cole Beasley" — and explicitly flags him as someone who "could be a relevant player someday" for fantasy purposes.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): may not look like the quintessential primary weapon, but his overall skills make him a capable "primary 1B" in a lot of offenses. Few receivers in this class deliver breaks with his snap, which should help him against tight man coverage in the league, especially on in-and-out breaking routes where every step matters. Works well with his quarterbacks — cites how he meshed at Florida with [[Anthony Richardson]], who excelled at finding his second and third options in and out of the pocket under pressure.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): Angelo's "door two" safe pick — a fantastic athlete with great change-of-direction quickness and acceleration, a good route runner and one of the best "aerial artists" as a pass catcher in the class, though (like [[Javon Baker]]) sometimes tries the acrobatic catch when a simpler one would do. Waldman independently has him very high, a top-20 overall prospect and his WR7, comping him to Chris Olave or a Chad Johnson-type "aspiring 1B," and reiterates how much of Anthony Richardson's 2023 tape depended on Pearsall adjusting his routes to get open for him.

- 2024-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Feel It or F@#k It* ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-01]]), directly weighing him against [[Ladd McConkey]]: the two are close and back-to-back on his board, but Pearsall grades as "the better all-around player right now" — more proven against press coverage with his hands and feet, more experience winning contested balls outside, and enough evidence he can play inside or outside. McConkey has promise to do the same but hasn't shown the press-release skill in games yet ("if I don't see it, I can't say he will"); McConkey could still start immediately and, if used heavily in the slot, be the class's most productive receiver as a rookie. Waldman's summary: "if all things were equal... Pearsall is just slightly better," but both project as solid pros.
- 2024-04-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]], finished full-sample Reception Perception profile ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-09]]): the receiver he had the most fun charting outside the class's top three, and one he came away "really excited about." Alignment shows genuine versatility — 51.4% of sampled snaps in the slot, 21.6% outside right, 22.3% outside left — with an 87th-percentile success rate versus man coverage that argues he isn't an exclusive slot receiver even though that may end up his best NFL home. Directly comparing him to [[Ladd McConkey]]: Pearsall is better versus press coverage (67.7% success, a little above average, vs. McConkey's 44%) and Harmon views him as clearly more than a "PPR scam" slot type. Elite hands — 70.3% catch rate, only one drop across the 8-game sample, 64.7% contested-catch rate. Route-tree strength is in-breaking: over 80% success on the dig, curl and slant (88% on the dig specifically), which Harmon frames as "quarterback's best friend" traits — clean, on-time, reliable in progression. Weaker downfield, with a 47.4% success rate on the nine route (below the prospect average), though solid post/corner numbers (74%/65%) suggest a path to more of a downfield threat. Rates his prospect profile better than [[Jordan Addison]]'s from the prior class and comps him as part-[[Puka Nacua]], part-Addison — less physically dominant than Nacua but more finished/versatile than a pure slot. Notes he could plausibly play X, flanker or slot for an NFL team and specifically floats him as a fit for Philadelphia's need at the WR3 spot. Higher than draft consensus, expecting him off the board Round 2-3.
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon's choice for best overall route runner once the "big three" ([[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], [[Rome Odunze]], [[Malik Nabers]]) are excluded, citing 75.8% success vs. man (second in the class) and 82.2% vs. zone, plus strong route pacing and the ability to snap off comebacks, curls, slants, digs and outs. His nine-route success is weak and he can't consistently stack/shed press coverage deep, but a 70.4% post-route success rate shows he sells the go route well before breaking underneath. Harmon ranked Pearsall one spot above [[Ladd McConkey]] in his personal stack: better play strength through contact, a wider and more effective release-move arsenal versus press (67.7% success vs. McConkey's class-low 44%), and better contested-catch ability were the tiebreakers.

Harmon frames Pearsall as filling that specific hole and helping Brock Purdy on real drop-back, high-leverage third downs. Koh ultimately agrees it's a great pick. - 2024-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-03]]): Pearsall was shot in the chest, walked himself to the ambulance and left the hospital the next day without surgery — Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) call him tough as nails but expect essentially a redshirt rookie year. On [[Reception Perception Methodology]] he charted 51% of snaps in the slot, 56% off the line, with a 75% success rate vs man (87th percentile) and 82% vs zone (69th percentile) — a slot/flanker who beats both coverages, closer to Deebo's alignment than Aiyuk's, with real 2025 upside.
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Harmon charted Pearsall's first real game and calls the results mixed but contextualized. He ran a route on 83.8% of dropbacks per PFF — clearly not the plan, only happening because [[Brandon Aiyuk]] went down, [[Jauan Jennings]] was out and [[Deebo Samuel]] was ill (5.4% route participation). Harmon has him just under 40% slot snaps, 54.2% on the line and 45.8% off it, so he played X, flanker and slot in one afternoon. Reps against zone were really good; man coverage was 'definitely not the prettiest' at just over a 50% success rate, but Harmon refuses to bang on a rookie who was shot in the chest, missed camp with a shoulder issue and drew a suffocating Chiefs secondary. Qualitatively he moved smoothly, not slow or stiff; the fixes are decisiveness and flattening routes out to give [[Brock Purdy]] more separation. Harmon thinks San Francisco drafted him as their [[Cooper Kupp]]-style chain-moving man-coverage answer on third down, and now he has to become that far earlier than intended.

- 2024-11-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]]): [[Jauan Jennings]] taking the X job is good news for Pearsall, because Pearsall is a man-coverage beater but not a press-coverage beater and projects as a flanker/slot rather than an X. Harmon believes San Francisco drafted him to move the offense toward more 11 personnel in future seasons and to add an option-route runner who can beat man coverage from the slot — the dimension they lacked anywhere but [[Brandon Aiyuk]]'s spot in the Super Bowl. Pearsall led the team in slot rate in this game and can move around and win on in-breakers, as he did against Tampa Bay. Harmon's caveat is that he wants to see it again against a defense that is not the Buccaneers, but calls it a good first start for both Pearsall and Jennings in their new roles.
- 2024-11-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-20]]): Pearsall is 'probably a 2025 bet' — the film has been up and down with some good signal, but he is not ready to be a full-time player, which is unsurprising given his injuries and being shot in the offseason. His Reception Perception rookie-report route distribution is heavily horizontal: 21.1% dig routes, 15.6% outs, 13.8% slants, plus whips and returns — slot-style option-route work in the mold of what [[Cooper Kupp]] does in the McVay branch of the Shanahan tree, not vertical stuff. Harmon argued that usage says something about where the 49ers offense is heading, and sees a future trio of a healthy [[Brandon Aiyuk]] as the X with Pearsall in the slot and [[Jauan Jennings]] flexing inside-outside as a strong room. Koh (not tracked) thinks Pearsall could become the YAC creator the Shanahan scheme is maximized by; Harmon agreed he could get there but said it is hard to judge off this rookie film.
- 2024-12-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-03]]): Pearsall was the player Harmon was highest on of the second-tier rookies pre-draft, and he gives him an incomplete grade for 2024 given the circumstances of his season — 'he's another guy that just shouldn't be in the position that he's in right now.' On upside, though, Harmon ranks Pearsall first of [[Xavier Worthy]], [[Xavier Legette]] and [[Keon Coleman]]: 'he's a separator, he looks like a guy who has good instincts for the position', which Harmon is not sure he can say for Coleman and really is not sure he can say for Legette. He grounds it in his upside-versus-theoretical-upside framework — with Pearsall he has actually seen the fully maximised version of the skill set, whereas the case for the bigger receivers is 'the idea of an idea'. Harmon has hope Pearsall develops into a startable player.

- 2025-05-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-16]]): an incomplete but genuinely encouraging rookie profile given he was shot in the chest in August, missed the whole preseason and played 11 games (46 targets, 31 catches, 400 yards, three touchdowns, 67.4% catch rate, 12.9 yards per catch). Full-sample Reception Perception: 69.7% vs man, 75% vs press, 82.7% vs zone. Harmon insists the trajectory is the point — in the four games charted for the November rookie report the marks were 64.2% vs man, 78.6% vs zone and just 57.1% vs press, so the final four games carried him to 75% vs press, and the dig route improved from 60% to about 70%. He points to the week 15 Rams tape as the turn and the week 17 Lions game (8-141-1, over six yards per route run vs man against a man-heavy defense) as the coaching staff deliberately featuring him. He lined up outside about 60% of the time but took 75% of his receptions and yards from outside, with 79% on comebacks and 86.7% on curls. The most striking finding: his above-average routes are out, comeback, flat and dig plus a 9.5% rate of option/return/whip routes — outbreaking and option work Harmon calls [[Cooper Kupp]]-type usage the 49ers have never had, and he suspects Pearsall may be more critical to the offense than people assume, potentially paired with [[Jauan Jennings]] in condensed formations and switch releases. Criticisms: a 71.4% slant success rate where he wants 80%, since he gets muscled on quick breaks and needs time to build speed, and he is not a run-after-catch player and may never be. Both hosts call him a redraft bargain — Koh expects him in round 11 or later, Harmon took him in round 11 of a staff mock, noting consensus jumbles [[Brandon Aiyuk]], Jennings and Pearsall between WR39 and WR45 and someone must outproduce WR39.
