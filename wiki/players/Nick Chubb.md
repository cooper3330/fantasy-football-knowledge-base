# Nick Chubb

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023, during the season Chubb lost to a severe knee
> injury. Team/role are as of that date and are almost certainly stale — treat
> as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]): Chubb is the named exception to Waldman's own rule that big backs cannot change direction efficiently in tight space. He is "as big as [[Saquon Barkley]]," still relies on jump-cut-class movement, "and has gotten away with it and been efficient." Waldman walks through the rep that convinced him — a goal-line toss where Chubb hurdles a fallen body and then cuts *outside* around a defensive back waiting at the five, "his outside foot literally turned to the boundary when he lands," pivoting as he lands in extremely tight quarters. He says Chubb has done it more than once and has a similar play against the Eagles.
- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]], same episode, supplying the mechanism: "how he prepares for the ground is super important — he doesn't prepare to catch himself, he prepares to re-accelerate. He's one of the best ever at re-accelerating post-contact." Both hosts call him badly underappreciated; Waldman: "I'd argue he's a Hall of Fame caliber talent — we'll see if he gets the opportunity from the media." Both flag the 2023 knee injury as the live question over what remains of the career.

**Note:** this is the one case in the episode that cuts *against* the
size-and-movement framework rather than for it — recorded as such on
[[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]].
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]) [Dynasty]: Both hosts land at roughly 50/50 on whether Nick Chubb returns to full starter form in 2024, with Harris admitting his number is inflated by fandom. Waldman details the injury severity: the Week 2 2023 knee injury included a torn MCL (surgery immediately) and a torn ACL (surgery delayed to November, per Cleveland's staggered-rehab approach), and this is the second time in Chubb's career he's torn three ligaments in the same knee (also happened in college). He relays that a doctor he's consulted called that combination one of the hardest injuries to fully recover from, especially for a running back, because all three ligaments must heal in balance or the knee ends up too loose or too tight. The Browns renegotiated Chubb to a one-year prove-it deal and added a depth hedge, both signs Waldman reads as genuine uncertainty about his timeline even as the team publicly says he's expected back 'at some point in 2024.' His personal stance: he's keeping every share of Chubb he owns and won't sell, but isn't recommending others buy in given the medical uncertainty.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]): Waldman has Nick Chubb 40th on his running back board and, despite the multi-ligament knee injury requiring a second surgery in November, says he isn't worried because 'Chubb's just too damn good.' He relays secondhand detail from a trainer contact (Gene Bramall) that successfully tying off three torn ligaments simultaneously is difficult and that a clean recovery implies Chubb's surgeries were executed well. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) disagrees, saying he is not drafting Chubb this year given he's entering his age-28 season on a severe injury history and telling listeners to 'keep your powder a little drier' on him - a direct split between the two hosts on this evaluation.
- 2024-07-01 — According to Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: Bob is skeptical Chubb's RB43 ADP (roughly an 11th/12th-round pick) is a good bet, noting the injury required a second surgery to repair collateral damage before the ACL itself was even repaired in November -- he wants proof before believing the recovery hype, calling it different from Adrian Peterson's famously fast ACL return. Waldman agrees he wants to see it in person, and reminds listeners he once ranked Chubb ahead of [[Saquon Barkley]] in a past Rookie Scouting Portfolio and still calls him one of his favorite players ever -- but says he already owns Chubb on every dynasty roster he has, so doesn't need the redraft risk, and would rather spend that pick on receivers like Adam Thielen or Josh Downs, or rookies like Adonai Mitchell, Michael Wilson or Dontayvion Wicks.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]) [Best Ball]: at a round-10 price he would burn the pick in best ball if the first ten rounds went well, but in redraft he wants to see the price fall further. Parsing the November ACL surgery that followed an earlier MCL repair, Waldman calls straight-line running and agility cone drills a nice sign, and the absence of limping reports (unlike [[J.K. Dobbins]]) another — but says the proof is scrimmage work: jump cuts, pressure cuts, accelerating out of cuts, and absorbing the two or three hits a game that bend a knee the wrong way, which for Chubb may take far less contact than for a healthy player. He expects no contact work all camp. The bet's justification is that Chubb is conceptually the best and most physically imposing back in the game and 'has the roadmap' back. Bob Harris (not tracked) expects Chubb to slide to rounds 13-15 and would rather spend on contributors and take his chances in free agency if Chubb opens on PUP; he notes Cleveland extended Chubb's expiring contract, so the return may not be this year.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): asked whether Chubb is worth a 10th-round investment with four games on the shelf to start the year, Waldman said flatly no. He is less optimistic than the market and would not be surprised if Chubb misses more than four games. His reasoning comes from a pre-draft conversation with Gene Bramwell at the Senior Bowl, when Waldman had Chubb ranked above [[Saquon Barkley]]: reconstructing three ligaments in one knee with exactly the right tightness is very difficult, and doing it a second time on the same knee is harder still — most athletes end up too loose or too tight and never fully trust the knee at that athletic level. Lifting well shows strength, not dynamic movement. Waldman noted the redone contract pays a roster bonus at 12 active games as a breadcrumb, but said he would rather be wrong than spend. [Dynasty]: hold if you already roster him; do not pay to acquire.
