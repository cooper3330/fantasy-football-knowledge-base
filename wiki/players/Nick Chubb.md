# Nick Chubb

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023, during the season Chubb lost to a severe knee
> injury. Team/role are as of that date and are almost certainly stale — treat
> as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]): Chubb is the named exception to Waldman's own rule that big backs cannot change direction efficiently in tight space. He is "as big as [[Saquon Barkley]]," still relies on jump-cut-class movement, "and has gotten away with it and been efficient." Waldman walks through the rep that convinced him — a goal-line toss where Chubb hurdles a fallen body and then cuts *outside* around a defensive back waiting at the five, "his outside foot literally turned to the boundary when he lands," pivoting as he lands in extremely tight quarters. He says Chubb has done it more than once and has a similar play against the Eagles.
- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]], same episode, supplying the mechanism: "how he prepares for the ground is super important — he doesn't prepare to catch himself, he prepares to re-accelerate. He's one of the best ever at re-accelerating post-contact." Both hosts call him badly underappreciated; Waldman: "I'd argue he's a Hall of Fame caliber talent — we'll see if he gets the opportunity from the media." Both flag the 2023 knee injury as the live question over what remains of the career.

**Note:** this is the one case in the episode that cuts *against* the
size-and-movement framework rather than for it — recorded as such on
[[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]].
- 2024-05-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-06]]) [Dynasty]: Both hosts land at roughly 50/50 on whether Nick Chubb returns to full starter form in 2024, with Harris admitting his number is inflated by fandom. Waldman details the injury severity: the Week 2 2023 knee injury included a torn MCL (surgery immediately) and a torn ACL (surgery delayed to November, per Cleveland's staggered-rehab approach), and this is the second time in Chubb's career he's torn three ligaments in the same knee (also happened in college). He relays that a doctor he's consulted called that combination one of the hardest injuries to fully recover from, especially for a running back, because all three ligaments must heal in balance or the knee ends up too loose or too tight. The Browns renegotiated Chubb to a one-year prove-it deal and added a depth hedge, both signs Waldman reads as genuine uncertainty about his timeline even as the team publicly says he's expected back 'at some point in 2024.' His personal stance: he's keeping every share of Chubb he owns and won't sell, but isn't recommending others buy in given the medical uncertainty.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]): Waldman has Nick Chubb 40th on his running back board and, despite the multi-ligament knee injury requiring a second surgery in November, says he isn't worried because 'Chubb's just too damn good.' He relays secondhand detail from a trainer contact (Gene Bramall) that successfully tying off three torn ligaments simultaneously is difficult and that a clean recovery implies Chubb's surgeries were executed well. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) disagrees, saying he is not drafting Chubb this year given he's entering his age-28 season on a severe injury history and telling listeners to 'keep your powder a little drier' on him - a direct split between the two hosts on this evaluation.
- 2024-07-01 — According to Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: Bob is skeptical Chubb's RB43 ADP (roughly an 11th/12th-round pick) is a good bet, noting the injury required a second surgery to repair collateral damage before the ACL itself was even repaired in November -- he wants proof before believing the recovery hype, calling it different from Adrian Peterson's famously fast ACL return. Waldman agrees he wants to see it in person, and reminds listeners he once ranked Chubb ahead of [[Saquon Barkley]] in a past Rookie Scouting Portfolio and still calls him one of his favorite players ever -- but says he already owns Chubb on every dynasty roster he has, so doesn't need the redraft risk, and would rather spend that pick on receivers like Adam Thielen or Josh Downs, or rookies like Adonai Mitchell, Michael Wilson or Dontayvion Wicks.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]) [Best Ball]: at a round-10 price he would burn the pick in best ball if the first ten rounds went well, but in redraft he wants to see the price fall further. Parsing the November ACL surgery that followed an earlier MCL repair, Waldman calls straight-line running and agility cone drills a nice sign, and the absence of limping reports (unlike [[J.K. Dobbins]]) another — but says the proof is scrimmage work: jump cuts, pressure cuts, accelerating out of cuts, and absorbing the two or three hits a game that bend a knee the wrong way, which for Chubb may take far less contact than for a healthy player. He expects no contact work all camp. The bet's justification is that Chubb is conceptually the best and most physically imposing back in the game and 'has the roadmap' back. Bob Harris (not tracked) expects Chubb to slide to rounds 13-15 and would rather spend on contributors and take his chances in free agency if Chubb opens on PUP; he notes Cleveland extended Chubb's expiring contract, so the return may not be this year.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): asked whether Chubb is worth a 10th-round investment with four games on the shelf to start the year, Waldman said flatly no. He is less optimistic than the market and would not be surprised if Chubb misses more than four games. His reasoning comes from a pre-draft conversation with Gene Bramwell at the Senior Bowl, when Waldman had Chubb ranked above [[Saquon Barkley]]: reconstructing three ligaments in one knee with exactly the right tightness is very difficult, and doing it a second time on the same knee is harder still — most athletes end up too loose or too tight and never fully trust the knee at that athletic level. Lifting well shows strength, not dynamic movement. Waldman noted the redone contract pays a roster bonus at 12 active games as a breadcrumb, but said he would rather be wrong than spend. [Dynasty]: hold if you already roster him; do not pay to acquire.
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: asked whether Sunday proved 'Nick Chubb is back,' Waldman would not go there — 'I can't say that for sure. I'd like to say that.' He liked what he saw in isolation, praising the spin move and noting 'you can see that he still sees the field pretty well,' and approved of Kevin Stefanski giving Chubb three goal-line shots to score. But he framed the game as a ramp-up: 'we need to see more touches. This was more of a getting his feet wet, getting him acclimated, see how his body responds to game conditions and how well he recovers.' Elsewhere in the same segment he used Chubb's contact balance — running through a defensive back with a linebacker on top of him and a safety hit and still gaining five — as the standard for real power, contrasted against flashier backs.
- 2024-11-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-25]]): Chubb is still a genuinely good NFL back but not a good fantasy play, and the gap is the offense around him. Watching the snow game at Pittsburgh (20 carries, 59 yards, 2 TDs) Waldman said the pre-injury top-end speed is gone but the power, quick set-up moves, peripheral vision, footwork and balance are all intact — 'like a snowplow' — and several good runs came independent of the blocking. He blamed a Cleveland line that used to be a strength: replacement-level tackle play, guards making assignment errors and getting pushed back, and a new center whose communication may explain the lack of gel. With [[Jameis Winston]] possibly throwing 40 times a game there is no bandwidth for the 15-18 carries Chubb needs before defenses make business decisions. Waldman called him a future buy-low.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]): Chubb suffered a broken foot ending his season. Waldman expects him to be re-signed by Cleveland — he showed enough, and because a broken foot is not a ligament issue he should be ready enough to go at some point early next season. He therefore projects a genuine 2025 committee with [[Jerome Ford]], with a drafted back also possible, and declines to call the backfield: 'the picture's a little too cloudy right now.'
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): Waldman feels it on Chubb's future — but somewhere else, behind a good offensive line. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) notes Waldman was as high on Chubb as anyone coming out in 2018 and that the 2018 RSP takes on him are now consensus. Waldman argues you cannot judge Chubb by his yardage; on tape he looked good, the second ACL injury was serious but the broken foot is a healable one with a full offseason to rehab (he comps it to [[Chris Godwin]]'s shoulder — no lingering issues). Behind a good line he could again be a top-12 back, even a bell cow at 1,300-1,400 yards and double-digit touchdowns if healthy. Fits named: Kansas City (letting [[Kareem Hunt]] walk, [[Isiah Pacheco]] as insurance) and the Giants, whose trench work he rates.
- 2025-02-27 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-27]]): in the injury-recovery discussion Angelo calls it 'unbelievable' in hindsight that [[Nick Chubb]] tore all three ligaments in college and came back to be the player he was, given how much of recovery is neurologic rather than structural. Both hosts say they would like to see Chubb signed cheaply by the Bears in 2025 — [[Matt Waldman]] pairing that with a day-two rookie back like [[TreVeyon Henderson]] and moving [[DeAndre Swift]] on — while Waldman notes someone else is promoting a Kansas City fit alongside [[Isiah Pacheco]].
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]): more uncertain than [[J.K. Dobbins]] as a free-agent Bears fit. There are real questions about whether Chubb is still the athlete he was, and Waldman is explicit that we have not seen enough to be sure either way — while noting most people will simply paint it as over for him. His constructive framing: if Chicago works him out and decides to use him in a [[David Montgomery]]-style role, that could be a really good thing for the team on a one- or two-year deal. He would consider either back.
- 2025-05-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-26]]): Waldman expects [[Nick Chubb]] to sign as an early-midseason addition after an injury rather than in the spring — a team losing a starter and wanting a veteran to take over. He is 'feeling it' on Chubb, and singles out Baltimore as the scenario he would love (and fears for fantasy reasons): if [[Derrick Henry]] went down and Chubb were good to go, Waldman says that would be nasty. His read on the market chill is risk aversion rather than tape — Mike Tomlin, asked what he saw from Chubb, said he did not see anything different from that dude other than a worse offensive line and fewer chances to run; Waldman thinks teams are simply scared of an older back with that injury history and will not give it a shot. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) says he has already stacked Chubb behind his traded-for Derrick Henry shares in best ball as a handcuff [Best Ball].
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): Chubb is fantasy relevant in Houston, and Waldman flags that he has him ranked at Footballguys higher than he would currently draft him — deliberately, to get people's attention on the situation. The reasoning is [[Joe Mixon]]'s ankle: if Mixon is still in a walking boot from last season's injury and the team went out and signed Chubb, that is a signal worth taking heat for. He can see an even split, or Chubb opening the year as the main back while Mixon recovers. He cites Mike Tomlin's assessment that Chubb looked like the same back last year behind a bad offensive line. Risk noted: Mixon rushing back out of the boot and re-injuring himself is a live scenario for either direction.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): with [[Joe Mixon]] possibly not ready for the start of camp, Waldman reads the Chubb signing as exactly the insurance it looks like and calls him a sneaky play — if you can get him outside the 12th round, take the shot and see whether he still has it.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman is openly rooting for Chubb but says his 2024 film showed no major decline once you account for a bad Cleveland offensive line — the Browns ran at a minus-10 point-margin differential and defenses stacked the box only 17% of the time, among the league's lowest, effectively inviting [[Jerome Ford]] and Pierre Strong to gash them on clock-killing carries while saving loaded boxes for Chubb, who was still the only one of the four backs to score touchdowns. He also relays a second-hand report that Chubb ran 23 mph in a spring workout — caveated as t-shirt-and-shorts, straight-line, and subject to timing variance — but argues even a discounted 20+ mph is excellent post-injury. [[Brandon Angelo]] frames the key question as whether 90% Chubb beats 65% [[Joe Mixon]], and both see a possible near-even split, with Waldman preferring Chubb at a 15th-round cost to Mixon at his ADP.
- 2025-07-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-28]]) [Best Ball]: 'feel it' on Chubb as AFC Comeback Player of the Year, while openly admitting he is completely unreliable on anything Chubb-related. The setup is [[Joe Mixon]]'s foot landing him on the non-football injury list after reassurances it was fine — Waldman and Bob Harris (untracked) both invoke the [[Christian McCaffrey]] 2024 lesson about trusting those reassurances. Waldman cites Mike Tomlin saying Chubb looked like the same back behind a bad offensive line, and thinks Houston is a better spot than Cleveland because of superior all-around skill talent and an established quarterback, projecting an 1,100–1,200 yard season if Mixon is out the first month to month and a half. He also stresses that Houston's problems compound: young or injured receivers missing blocks and blowing quarterback adjustments cost the line snaps to assert itself, on top of a run-blocking unit that was the league's worst in stuffs. Harris says he has started grabbing Chubb cheaply in best ball as insurance on his Mixon shares.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]): on reports out of Houston that Chubb has lost his burst, Waldman says 'fuck it' — practice is one of the worst places to judge a running back, Chubb is learning new blockers rather than a new scheme, and his best trait (setting up defenders into his blocks) requires knowledge of teammates he has not built yet; he is also not going to run over his own blockers in a low-contact camp. Waldman cites Kyle Shanahan saying you never know what you have in a back until live action, plus Terrell Davis and Isaiah Crowell as camp-report misses, and says a UGA-connected source clocked Chubb at 23 mph in spring workouts — even discounted, still NFL speed. He is still cautious on the knee holding up over a season, and notes Chubb went unsigned until [[Joe Mixon]]'s foot issue persisted. Net: he does not think highly of Chubb right now but takes him in the 18th–19th round for contingent upside, the same way he holds [[Woody Marks]] shares.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]): Waldman names his 'Nick Chubb theory' after Chubb's rookie debut against the Giants, when former safety Will Blackmon posted on Twitter that Chubb did not look like an NFL starter or like anything to speak of. Watching the same tape contextually, Waldman thought Chubb actually did quite well; the one bounce out of roughly 10 to 12 carries came in the second half and lost a yard, plausibly out of frustration at taking what was there all game, and it was made into a big deal. The episode was Waldman's first realization that having played defense in the NFL does not mean you know how to evaluate another position. He applies the same reading to [[Tahj Brooks]].
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-18]]): the reports of Chubb's demise are 'greatly exaggerated' — beat writers watching live practice on deadline cannot see what Chubb does, because his game is built on subtlety of decision-making, minute footwork changes and power that arrives at the end of the run, and camp tackling is 'car wash curtains posing as tacklers.' Waldman said Chubb is mostly learning how his linemen move and set things up, the way [[Tom Brady]] or [[Patrick Mahomes]] would test non-viable throws in camp. In the preseason game the power, quick decision-making and burst were all there, and with [[Joe Mixon]] questionable Waldman would count on Chubb for the next three to four weeks. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) countered that John McClain, a 49-year Texans beat veteran, has not been impressed with Chubb in practice — but McClain's real concern is the offensive line, which has tried five combinations and where the Texans 'have no clue' if it will be better, worse or the same. Harris landed on Chubb as a matchup starter, tempered expectations, and 'kind of fuck it because it's the Texans.'
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): with [[Joe Mixon]] opening on NFI, Chubb is Harris's 'highest guess' to lead the Houston backfield — but only a 10th- or 11th-round pick, and he is 'a little skeptical' based on what Chubb looked like in 2024. He does not expect Chubb to be the answer much beyond September, while adding he would love to be surprised. Harris flags that Houston could still trade for a bigger name (he floats [[Breece Hall]]), which would say something both about Mixon's October timeline and about how the Texans rate the three backs already on the roster. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): a truly nauseating standoff of a backfield, with snaps going 28 Chubb, 15 Dare Ogunbowale, seven [[Dameon Pierce]] and seven [[Woody Marks]]. Through three quarters Chubb was stuck on 10 carries for 40 yards, though he added two good fourth-quarter runs and a power run in the second. Harris's film read is blunt: he used to love Chubb and does not love him so much anymore, because when Chubb is not running through a good-sized hole that you or I could run through, and is not running in a straight line, it is not very good; he had to take evasive action on a carry and there is not a lot of juice left. The mitigating note is that nobody in this backfield looked better, including Marks, so it reads as a bad backfield that only gets what is blocked. Ogunbowale also fumbled the game away.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Chubb had a big gain on an early screen but Harris's film read is bleak — every time you see Chubb try to cut, you can see an overlay of where the pre-injury version would have been, and it is a little sad seeing the invisible guy be further ahead than the guy we have now. He patently does not have the juice anymore. Chubb played 24 snaps and did score a very late touchdown, but Harris suspects Tampa may have simply let him score to preserve clock — it was an awfully big hole and Antoine Winfield Jr. did not appear to hustle. With [[Joe Mixon]] out, Harris wonders whether Houston turns to [[Woody Marks]] at some point.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-22]]): 'feel it' — Chubb needs more touches. Waldman said Chubb has looked pretty good and has shown enough, but the offense is not letting any back get into a rhythm behind a bad offensive line, and that this Houston offense has been fairly direct anyway. His pointed line was that D'Amico Ryans needs to get out of the denial and the preconceived notion he had of Chubb. Both hosts joked that this argument generalizes — every running back needs more touches — before agreeing that with Chubb it is a simpler case than most: give him touches and something good will happen.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): Chubb led Houston's backfield only narrowly at Jacksonville — 30 snaps to [[Woody Marks]]'s 29, 12 touches to seven — and in an environment with no running room Harris said it is hard to tell what any back can do. His summary is that 'Nick Chubb is not a sure thing,' the reason he is willing to speculate on Marks. Guest SCOTT FISH (Fantasy Cares, NOT tracked) was more explicit: yards per carry, two prior injuries and age all point to limited time in the role, with Marks either sharing the load or taking it. Both agreed the real hope is [[Joe Mixon]] returning.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): Chubb started against Tennessee and took the first three carries of the game, and the first-half snap count was pretty much split, but he finished 26 snaps to 38 for [[Woody Marks]] as the game wore on. Harris's verdict is affectionate but clear: 'we love Nick Chubb for his past contributions, he's not going away' — he made a really good hands catch on a poorly thrown ball in the third quarter — but anyone who actually ran their eyes across the film would agree Marks has more juice.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-02]]): Chubb is now a short-yardage and goal-line specialist in Houston, a Jamaal Williams-in-Detroit type role — not the explosive back who ripped off 60-70 yards at will, but a back who understands leverage, timing and second-level spacing and will regularly get you past the sticks on third and fourth and short and in the low red zone. [[Matt Waldman]] said he has not seen Chubb break a run beyond 20 yards on this team, but noted his charting of the Week 4 game: only three carries of two yards or less to [[Woody Marks]]'s five, and a 21-yard run erased by a soft holding call, so Chubb's efficiency was roughly Marks's on fewer touches. Both still expect the volume edge to tilt to the younger Marks, leaving Chubb as high-leverage/touchdown-dependent fantasy value.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Chubb still started for Houston, but Harris said [[Woody Marks]] looked better and that he would be starting Marks well before Chubb. Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) added that unless the Buccaneers let him score from 25 yards out, the Texans know Chubb is probably not the answer at running back, which is why a team hunting offensive solutions keeps auditioning alternatives.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-06]]): Waldman keeps watching Chubb make nice runs and will not concede the job to [[Woody Marks]]. Against Baltimore he looked like the Nick Chubb of old minus the burst — cutbacks, a stiff-arm on the biggest man on the field, and a 27-yard touchdown run where he beat two safeties who misjudged the angle and he flew past them. He pre-empted the objection that the Ravens defense was injured and gettable by pointing out Marks did not run on it nearly as well, and that Marks's good runs came untouched behind convoys. Waldman's conclusion is that Houston stays a split backfield rather than a Marks takeover.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Chubb started against Baltimore despite the widespread claim that [[Woody Marks]] had taken the job, had a drop on the second drive, and then broke a third-quarter house call by bouncing outside against 'truly horrendous run fills by the Baltimore defense.' He was later stuffed at the goal line in the fourth. Harris had ranked Marks ahead of him this week, but only by a few spots, and treats the backfield as genuinely split rather than resolved.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Chubb out-touched [[Woody Marks]] 11-to-7 in Week 5 after Marks had briefly been declared the backfield winner following Week 4, and Harris expects a fairly traditional big-back role split going forward, which caps both as only 'usable' options.
- 2025-10-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-23]]): Harmon has charted Chubb as 'fantastic' in pass protection this year and is confused why Houston isn't using him as the pass-pro security blanket/enforcer back given how poor the offensive line has been, instead having him run routes while [[Woody Marks]] -- who graded as the worst pass-blocking RB Harmon charted this season -- handles more early-down and protection-adjacent work.
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-10-27]]) [Redraft]: got the start but the run game didn't have much going until San Francisco's defense got further banged up; on a third-and-one 'attitude' run everyone in the building knew was coming, Harris notes 'old Nick Chubb would have gotten it' and this version did not. Touches/snaps were roughly even with [[Woody Marks]], and Harris says he doesn't trust either back right now.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): Houston has seen what he has seen - 'he definitely is chubbersome to the Texans' chances of winning' - and the snap split against Jacksonville was 54 for [[Woody Marks]] to 10 for Chubb, with Chubb touching it on six of his 10 snaps. Harris's verdict: you want Marks over Chubb, and 'I don't think Nick Chubb really needs to be rostered'.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-13]]): Chubb played only 10 snaps against the Jaguars, taking six touches on them including a couple of targets — a great touch rate per snap, but 10 snaps cannot feed a fantasy lineup. Harris says he had been ranking Chubb and [[Woody Marks]] close together most weeks but this is the first week he put major separation between them, with Marks ahead. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees Houston is still keeping Chubb in the mix but not as much, and is trying to give Marks the chance to show what he can do week in and week out.
- 2025-12-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-23]]): with late word that [[Woody Marks]] would be inactive, Harris assumed Chubb would lead the Texans backfield and admits he was flat wrong -- usage clearly favored [[Jawhar Jordan]] (28 snaps to Chubb's 20), with Chubb reduced to an 'emeritus starter' role getting a carry to open each half before going to the bench, despite busting a 31-yard run in the fourth quarter. Harris says he's 'completely out of the picture' for fantasy purposes and hopes the whole backfield picture clarifies if Marks returns for week 17 against the Chargers.
- 2026-05-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-28 (Going Deep)]]) [Dynasty]: as a fallback veteran option for a team like Green Bay behind [[James Connor]], Angelo said he'd take Chubb over [[Joe Mixon]] -- Chubb was still effective and competent last season behind a genuinely terrible offensive line, so there's recent tape to trust, whereas Mixon is more of an unknown quantity right now.
- 2026-08-24 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-24]]): Chubb retired over the weekend at age 30. Harris called him one of the best running backs in the league over the 2018-2023 stretch, but said the catastrophic knee injury from that era meant he was never the same player again, spending his final three seasons 'in the wilderness trying to recapture the old glory.' Harris praised him as a player who broke fantasy's reductive rules about needing 50 catches or a rushing quarterback around him to be draft-relevant.
- 2026-08-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-24 (Feel It or Fk It)]]): on Nick Chubb's retirement, Waldman said he's 'definitely' a Hall of Fame-worthy talent, comparing his case to Terrell Davis and Floyd Little -- backs whose resume points (Chubb's 2,000-yard season, Super Bowl appearances) help with voters even though Waldman argued some Hall voters underrate Chubb's underrated explosive strength because they haven't studied enough of his film. He expects Chubb will most likely need a veterans' committee path rather than first-ballot induction, given the receiving-yardage and longevity criticisms likely to be raised against him despite Waldman's view that the tape supports a Hall of Fame case.
