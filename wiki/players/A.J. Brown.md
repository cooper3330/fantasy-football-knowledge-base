# A.J. Brown

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]): reacting to Brown's call into Philadelphia sports radio (WIP) to deny locker-room trouble, says he's not worried about Brown for having made the call — he's "worried about the Eagles" that Brown felt compelled to say it publicly. Reads the Philadelphia media/organizational environment as inconsistent (media darlings one year, villains the next), and thinks Brown — savvy about his own organization — called in preemptively to head off a "flaky" front-office reaction rather than because there's an actual problem; frames it as Brown wanting to be heard on his own terms rather than have the story projected onto him. Calls Brown an "unbelievable talent" and a kinder, more mature, more versatile (inside/outside) version of Terrell Owens (not tracked) — real vocal-leadership presence without T.O.'s baggage. On price: still worth a first-round pick in early best ball drafts — "I'd pay that premium all day long" — and explicitly rejects the idea that [[Tee Higgins]] would be a like-for-like replacement if Brown were unavailable. [Best Ball]

- 2024-04-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-15]]: reacting to [[DeVonta Smith]]'s new 3-year, $75M extension, restates the "A.J. Brown show with DeVonta Smith playing a highly coveted supporting role" framing of Philadelphia's passing game, and reads Smith signing long-term rather than pushing for a trade (as [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and [[Tee Higgins]] have) as a good sign Brown himself isn't going anywhere either.
- 2024-06-10 — According to Bob Harris (untracked co-host) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): said he feels more confident in A.J. Brown than in [[Puka Nacua]] at similar draft cost, and would lean toward Brown before leaning toward Nacua, meaning he's taking fewer shares of Nacua as a result.
- 2024-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-25]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon's contrarian prediction is that new OC Kellen Moore moves Brown into the slot as the Eagles' primary read, arguing Brown's elite press-beating ability and after-catch skill would get the ball out faster against the blitzes that plagued Jalen Hurts last year, and citing that Kellen Moore's prior top receivers (CeeDee Lamb, Keenan Allen) were deployed heavily inside. Snap-alignment data: Brown was 68.9% outside in his final Titans year and 68% outside in year one with Philadelphia, sliding from 80%+ on the line of scrimmage in Tennessee to 45.8% on the line with the Eagles. Co-host James Koh (untracked) disagrees, calling this contrarian and arguing Brown is too special a downfield weapon to move inside, since NFL offenses generally reserve deep shots for outside alignment. Both agree DeVonta Smith, Aeneas Smith or Johnny Wilson could take the outside role Brown vacates if he moves in.
- 2024-07-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-02]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon hasn't charted Brown's 2023 season yet (profile due July 22) but calls him a 'capital-E elite' receiver, a view he's held since Brown's Titans days. Harmon and co-host James Koh (untracked) expect new OC Kellen Moore to build the passing game around Brown getting a 'full head of steam' -- deep overs, crossing routes, nine routes from the slot, and pre-snap motion designed to get him moving away from defenders -- contrasting with [[DeVonta Smith]]'s more technical, boundary/press-beating role. They note Brown was arguably the one Eagles player who didn't take a step back in 2023's underwhelming offense, and that in his final Titans season he had already shifted off pure X duties into more flanker/slot work once Julio Jones arrived and took the X role.
- 2024-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-16]]): after re-charting him ahead of this episode, Harmon called Brown 'incredible' and 'a top-five receiver in the league,' pointing to his 2023 start -- on a 1,995-yard full-season pace through eight weeks, including six straight games over 125 yards from weeks 3-8 -- before Philadelphia's offense collapsed down the stretch. With new OC Kellen Moore installing what Jalen Hurts called a '95% new offense,' Harmon expects Brown to play more in motion and off the ball/inside than he did last year, closer to his rookie-year usage, and puts a genuine 1,800-2,000-yard season in his range of outcomes -- the only drag on 2,000 being a strong second receiver in [[DeVonta Smith]] siphoning targets and Harmon's own mixed read on [[Jalen Hurts]] as a passer. [Redraft (Standard)] [Dynasty]
- 2024-10-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-03]]): Brown is the benchmark for what a real leap looks like in Reception Perception. Koh initially frames Brown's first three years as relatively static with improvement arriving alongside a better quarterback and offense — pointing to a rise in zone success rate from his rookie season into 2022, his first year with [[Jalen Hurts]] — and argues the human element (effort on every rep) improves when the offense and team are better. Harmon corrects the framing: Koh glossed over the rookie-to-sophomore jump from 73.9% to 79% success rate vs man, 79% being a league-leading number in 2020. That is the size of move Harmon says none of the 2022 class has produced yet, [[Garrett Wilson]] excepted in part.
- 2024-10-03 — According to co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-03]]): Brown is the reason [[Jalen Hurts]] stopped looking like a running quarterback in 2022 despite an unchanged run/pass attempt profile — Brown was 'bending defenses into pretzels', catching contested balls and legging them out for long yards after catch, lifting Hurts from 7.3 to 8.0 yards per attempt. Harstad used this to argue the correlation between rushing quarterbacks and the absence of a WR1 runs backwards: a quarterback with that profile looks like a passer if he happens to get a top-end receiver and a runner if he doesn't. He also noted that every Hurts carry still eats a snap where Brown or [[DeVonta Smith]] might have broken a coverage for 60.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14 (Age Cliffs and Dented Cans)]]: Adam Harstad (NOT tracked) says he 'always wants it to be A.J. Brown' as best receiver in the NFL and thinks the argument belongs inside the Overton window, but ranks him No. 3 behind [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Justin Jefferson]] and does not actually see the case over them. His reason for liking Brown is force multiplication rather than raw production: Tennessee's passing game fell off when he left, Philadelphia's went through the roof when he arrived, and the Eagles look materially different in games with Brown in versus out — an effect he compares to Terrell Owens, who he would take over Randy Moss on the same logic. [[Matt Waldman]] calls Brown one of his three favorite receivers ever evaluated, notes he was a slot receiver at Ole Miss whom people wrongly thought could not move to X or flanker, and describes him as a slightly faster Anquan Boldin with better hands — 'everything people wanted [[DK Metcalf]] to be and more.'
- 2024-12-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-12]]): Brown is having the most efficient season of his career on collapsed volume — a 67% success rate as the targeted player, 43.8% of receptions going for explosive plays (career high, past 40.3% as a rookie), 0.72 EPA per target and 3.2 yards per route run overall with over four against man. The problem is team context, not the player: Philadelphia is 31st in neutral pass rate behind an elite defense and a strong run game, and Brown has not seen double-digit targets since week one. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) noted that over the nine games since, Brown averaged 6.2 targets, 4.8 receptions and under 80 yards with just three touchdowns. Harmon defended Brown's locker-room comment as obviously correct — the clarification being that he meant the whole passing game, not [[Jalen Hurts]] — and called it the dumbest controversy in recent memory, while warning Philadelphia will need more from the pass game in the postseason when the talent gap narrows.
- 2024-12-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-17]]): Brown's 11-target, eight-catch, 110-yard, one-touchdown game against Pittsburgh was his first double-digit target game since Week 1 — a statistic Harmon calls insane. He and James Koh (not a tracked expert) both dismiss the Brown-[[Jalen Hurts]] 'beef' as a very dumb controversy while insisting the underlying complaint is legitimate: the passing game can perform and simply doesn't every week. Harmon's matchup case is concrete — Brown went 6-of-9 for 89 yards and a touchdown against man coverage versus a single-high, man-heavy Pittsburgh defense, his routes are man-coverage beaters, and Philadelphia faces three top-15 man-rate teams (Washington, New York, Dallas) down the stretch. His caveat is structural: with [[Saquon Barkley]] and a run-heavy identity, Brown will never approach a running back's touch floor, so frustration is baked in.
- 2025-01-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-09]]): Brown wins 2024 Most Productive Receiver vs Man Coverage, first in both yards per route run and first downs per route run against man, at 4.27 yards per route run versus 3.85 for second-place [[Puka Nacua]] — a gap Harmon calls insane. He was second only to [[CeeDee Lamb]] in receiving yards against man and tied for fifth-most with six touchdowns against man per Next Gen Stats. Harmon says Brown has always been a top-five man-coverage beater in Reception Perception and is a big reason Philadelphia's offense is dangerous beyond [[Saquon Barkley]]. The caveats are stated plainly: injuries kept him from a full season and Philadelphia simply did not throw much, so a lot of the splits are small-sample. Koh adds a negative — Brown's 38% contested-catch rate was a little below what you would expect from him, a down year in that specific area.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): Brown is the direct beneficiary if Los Angeles repeats the cover-1-heavy plan it used on [[Sam Darnold]] — Harmon said man coverage on the outside just means [[Jalen Hurts]] goes to Brown over and over and over again. James Koh (not a tracked expert) took the other side of the same coin: if Chris Shula's Rams instead sit in cover-2 and focus on taking away deep passes, Koh expects to see a lot of Brown reading a book on the sideline, trying to find his way through the game. Harmon rates the Rams defense highly after the wild-card round — young, fast, physical, and not a one-game fluke, allowing double-digit points only once since week 15.
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): Brown is a top-five wide receiver whose stat sheet understates him — he led the league at 4.27 yards per route run this season, with a healthy gap back to second-place [[Puka Nacua]], who was under four. Against Washington he posted 96 yards, 62 of them against man coverage, and could not be covered even by a corner Harmon rates as having played well this year in Mike Sainristil. Harmon's schematic point is that Brown is what makes Philadelphia uniquely hard to defend: commit resources to the two-pronged [[Saquon Barkley]]/[[Jalen Hurts]] run game and you must iso your corners on Brown, and if your corners are not great you get fried.
- 2025-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-05]]): Brown has been absurd against man coverage — 4.27 yards per route run vs man in 2024 — and Harmon expects Kansas City to travel Trent McDuffie with him for much of Super Bowl LIX, the same way they used McDuffie on [[Nico Collins]] in the divisional round. Co-host James Koh argues the Chiefs cannot survive playing their usual man rate against him, saying you cannot put even a corner as good as McDuffie on an island against Brown; Harmon agrees on the coverage logic but declines to go as far as Koh's blowout prediction. Against zone Brown's numbers come down to 2.54 yards per route run, which is why Harmon thinks the McDuffie attention opens things for [[DeVonta Smith]].
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) reports Brown falling to the middle of the second round in early mocks and asks whether Philadelphia's diminished passing volume is a real reservation. Waldman calls him a nice bargain at that price and the sort of player where, when he falls to you early, you say yes and take the chance. His reasoning is that the Chiefs showed a template for slowing [[Saquon Barkley]] and the Eagles' ground game, and as more defenses copy it, Brown should get more involved in the passing game — so the volume dip that pushed his ADP down is the very thing likely to correct.
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-13]]): [[A.J. Brown]] is the classic X on the Eagles roster — regular season plus postseason, he ran the fewest slot snaps on the team at 17%, though Philadelphia does move him around the formation. He is locked up through 2029 at $32 million per year, which Harmon uses as the top benchmark when pricing [[Terry McLaurin]]. Harmon's broader point is that Philadelphia locked up both Brown and [[DeVonta Smith]] last offseason, paid a quarterback, and still won a Super Bowl with cap room left — a lesson he aims squarely at Cincinnati.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'feel' Brown over [[Drake London]] at Underdog WR10. Waldman expects a rebound year because defenses will force [[Jalen Hurts]] to throw more, and he pointed to roughly a hundred fewer completions for Hurts than the prior season as the big difference-making factor in Brown's down output. Untracked co-host Bob Harris agreed he would rather have Brown at this price.
- 2025-05-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-26]]): asked whether any player is play-caller proof, Waldman answers that nobody truly is, but [[A.J. Brown]] is the closest he would come to naming one. His caveat is environment rather than talent — Brown is absolutely still a good fantasy starter, but Waldman argues he would be mentioned in the same breath as [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Justin Jefferson]] if he played in a higher-octane passing offense, citing Atlanta with [[Michael Penix Jr.]] over the next year or two as the kind of situation that would do it. The implication for Philadelphia is that the run-heavy 2024 shape (roughly 100 fewer pass attempts) caps Brown's ceiling relative to his talent rather than his talent capping it.
- 2025-05-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-30]]) [Dynasty]: Brown is in tier three, which Koh (not tracked) calls inconsistent with how bullish Harmon has been on Brown his whole career. Harmon concedes he may be slightly overreacting to how run-heavy the Eagles were, notes Brown was still WR13 in points per game last season, and insists that on NFL talent 'he's a tier-one guy to me'. The downgrade is age and situation: Brown turns 28 soon and is the oldest player in this range, while every other name around him was drafted 2020 or later. More decisive is volume — with [[Jalen Hurts]], [[Saquon Barkley]] and a great defense, Philadelphia will never push for top-ten passing volume, so Brown could handle a big target load but will not be fed one. Harmon still owns Brown in dynasty and is not looking to move him except for a tier-two player, which he doubts is obtainable; he thinks Brown's window to be a tier-one dynasty asset has come and gone through no fault of his own.
- 2025-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-08]]): Harmon had charted four 2024 games of Brown that morning for an in-progress Reception Perception profile and gave a preview — Brown is still pushing 80% success rate versus man coverage and posted one of his better zone-coverage seasons, keeping him an elite tier-one, top-five receiver. He also notes Brown is a player who does not want targets going away from him, which limits how much any Philadelphia third receiver can be schemed up.
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-20]]): having just finished Brown's Reception Perception profile, Harmon calls the numbers incredible — 97th-percentile success rate versus man and 96th versus press — and notes he has posted dominant success rates every year for four or five running. He flags that Brown is currently dealing with a hamstring injury, which is part of why he likes the [[DeVonta Smith]] receiving-yard over: if anything happened to Brown, Smith would 'soar past' an 850.5 line. Harmon also expects more passing volume in Philadelphia generally, since [[Jalen Hurts]] can play better, the run game and defense are likely to regress from best-in-league, and more scripts will force throwing.
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Brown is his round-two safe pick for the same reason as [[DeVonta Smith]] — 2024 was probably the least pass-heavy the Eagles offense can ever be and it was still a really good season for him. Harris calls him a monster in the red zone and a monster of a player generally, one of the first five or six receivers you would pick in a schoolyard draft, and says he passes 'the grandmother test': show a non-fan the field and they will point at Brown as the star. He finds the bear case — that the offense is too good and too run-heavy, and he will be unhappy and reading books on the sideline — a dumb argument, because year to year the NFL changes, someone gets hurt, the defense regresses and they have to throw to keep up; if your objection to a great player is that his team is too good, that inverts what you actually want. Guest Scott Pianowski (Yahoo, not tracked) says he would feel very confident about anyone holding shares of both Brown and Smith.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): Harris rates Brown a 4 out of 10 — he should be safer than he is, but Philadelphia's offense was so efficient and they clobbered people so thoroughly that they threw the fewest pass attempts in the league. Guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) goes to 6, adding the current injury (Brown is back at practice, probably fine by week one, similar to the [[Justin Jefferson]] situation) and the same structural point: at 2-2 four games in, Philadelphia decided to stop letting [[Jalen Hurts]] throw and hand it to [[Saquon Barkley]], and they did not lose another game. Harris relays Andy Behrens' opposing view that Brown would be a 1 or 2 because every receiver drafted near him has a ceiling equal to what Brown does every year, and that Brown was on pace for it before missing four games with a hamstring.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Brown's only catch of the opener came inside two minutes, 'certainly not why we were drafting him in the second round.' Harris rejects the excuse that Brown was slow out of the gate because he barely practiced in camp while hurt — he says he watched plays where the call was a throw to Brown and [[Jalen Hurts]] looked at it, didn't like it and pulled the ball down: 'that's just going to be the story with him.' Guest JEFF ERICKSON (RotoWire, NOT tracked) said he doesn't own Brown, would be 'very frustrated' if he did, but isn't that worried — 'they're going to be fallow weeks for every receiver' — while conceding it wasn't a great look and that you'd want a better thrower attached to a second-round receiver. Erickson also listed Brown among the receivers he has zero exposure to (with [[Puka Nacua]], [[Ladd McConkey]], [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]]) as his No. 5 fear.
- 2025-09-08 — According to Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): Joe Dolan of Fantasy Points reported their charters had Brown winning zero routes against Dallas in Week 1, when he is normally at the top of that weekly charting metric. Harris's explanation is not decline but missed reps — Brown did not practice much in August — and he groups Brown with [[Terry McLaurin]], [[Jauan Jennings]], [[Justin Jefferson]], [[De'Von Achane]] and [[Tyreek Hill]] as players who may need time to get up to speed. [[Matt Waldman]] endorsed the broader caution that Week 1 conclusions have no context. Harris later conceded that Cincinnati's slow start despite a full August may undercut the theory, and that nothing is real until October.
