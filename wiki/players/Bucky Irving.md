# Bucky Irving

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]) [Dynasty]: named as a player Angelo has mixed feelings about relative to outside hype. Direct comp to [[Devin Singletary]]'s pre-draft profile — extreme range from "best player on the field" flashes (breaking multiple tackles on the same play, real explosive-gain ability Singletary himself never had) to plays where he goes off schedule for no reason on a run that was blocked to work as designed. Angelo's explicit ceiling case: if Irving finds the same "on schedule" discipline Singletary eventually found in the NFL (tempering dramatic movement into more controlled, contact-balance-based play), he projects as a "Singletary-plus" player because Irving already has more explosiveness in space than Singletary ever did. [[Matt Waldman]]'s same-conversation caveat: doesn't think Irving currently has Singletary's decision-making/vision consistency — Singletary's game was built almost entirely on compensating for a lack of NFL-average speed and power with elite vision and footwork, whereas Irving (who is faster than Singletary) still needs to *add* skills rather than simply calibrate ones he already has. Both agree he has a real NFL role; they disagree on how close he already is to unlocking it. See [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]] for the "on schedule vs. off schedule" framework this take draws on.
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: a shifty space player with functional power, but flags two specific fixable issues before he can be trusted as a receiving weapon — "clapping" at targets with his body rather than his hands (leads to fights/drops) and slow, indecisive transitions to run after the catch that hand pursuit extra time. Says his "reliability of process is lacking" on these mundane reps even though he can make difficult contested catches — "it has upside, but you need the baseline."

- 2024-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Feel It or F@#k It* ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-01]]), with co-host **Bob Harris** (not a tracked expert): at 192 lbs, thinks he's "just a situational player" and "a bit... overrated" in draft media relative to his ceiling. Reacting to Daniel Jeremiah's (not tracked) [[Devin Singletary]] comp, Waldman says Irving would need to become a better pass catcher and route runner to justify it — specifically his hand positioning, snap, and body positioning to win underneath as a receiver need fixing, and he lacks Singletary's power-for-size as a runner even though he's quicker. Sees his realistic ceiling as a contributor in a running back committee, currently more like "the third wheel" in a three-headed backfield than the lead back. Bob Harris (not tracked) separately flags that automated models tend to penalize backs under 200 lbs regardless of tape, using [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] (199 lbs) as the counter-example of a sub-200-pound back who succeeded anyway.
- 2024-05-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-13]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is skeptical of the growing narrative that Irving will meaningfully cut into [[Rachaad White]]'s workload, since Irving lacks prototypical size and speed — the two traits Waldman says can't be coached. His real asset is creative movement — the ability to get 'lost' between the tackles on duo blocks and squirt through tight creases, comparable to Devin Singletary — which could carve out a role, but Waldman thinks Tampa Bay drafted him early enough that the team has incentive to hype him up regardless of actual role certainty.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Going Deep)]]): Irving is a second 'hot start, cold finish' candidate, mostly because the hype has run ahead of the situation. Angelo thinks Irving is the more instinctual runner than [[Rachaad White]] but may need time to adjust to NFL speed, and notes Todd Bowles had just said Tampa Bay would use a hot-hand approach — making the split a live weekly question rather than a settled ascent. [[Matt Waldman]] adds a third name to the same backfield in [[Sean Tucker]], arguing Tucker is the better cutback runner as Tampa incorporates more zone, and rates Irving only 'okay' at that specific skill. Both frame the Tampa backfield as something to monitor rather than trust early.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): Waldman likes what Irving has done and treats the takeover talk as credible, citing the share of Tampa Bay running back rushes rising 37.5 percent to 41.2 percent to 56.3 percent across the first three weeks (numbers supplied by co-host Bob Harris, untracked). He tempered it two ways: [[Rachaad White]] has been banged up, which inflates the trend, and Tampa Bay will still need a complement who can do the passing-game work, so he does not see White losing the job completely at this stage.
- 2024-09-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-30]]): pushed by co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert), who called Irving taking the lead job from [[Rachaad White]] inevitable, Waldman only half-felt it. His view: Irving can be the lead rusher but is not an every-down back, and Tampa Bay will keep a split. That caps the fantasy outcome — Waldman put Irving in low-end RB2 territory rather than treating him as a workhorse takeover, and noted White equaled Irving's ground production the day before.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]): it is not Irving or [[Rachaad White]], it is both. Waldman rejects the talent referendum framing and instead grades the play calls — Irving made good plays and executed well, but several came through big holes White would also have converted, and Tampa Bay's red-zone sequence had the backs reversed, handing Irving a downhill power look where the 'shrimp with quickness' got four yards and got decleated by a linebacker, then throwing the quick perimeter pass to White when the slippery back was the right man to catch and turn upfield. Bottom line for managers: Irving and White are both flex-level plays capable of RB2 production on any given Sunday and RB1 weeks with touchdown luck as bye weeks and attrition hit.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): Irving is a mild negative-regression candidate. Waldman expects his efficiency to drop as Tampa Bay settles the split between him and [[Rachaad White]], and characterizes the pair bluntly — neither is a great back, but they complement each other well, which is precisely what makes a clean workload for either unlikely. The take is about role clarity rather than a change in Waldman's evaluation of Irving's ability; he does not project a collapse, only a step down from what the early-season usage and efficiency have implied.
- 2025-01-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-14]]): Irving had some nice plays on 17 carries against Washington, but Harmon's specific credit is that the production came in spite of the design rather than because of it — Tampa Bay was trying to run up the gut and Irving had to bounce runs outside to make them work. Harmon calls him a baller and folds him into praise for GM Jason Licht's drafting, alongside [[Jalen McMillan]] and center Graham Barton, as evidence Tampa keeps restocking the cupboard with young talent even as Koh worries the offense is a team in transition with [[Mike Evans]] aging and [[Chris Godwin]] likely to leave.
- 2025-01-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-27]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: RB1 territory is 'possible' but he is skeptical — Irving is 'the guy that people are going to get way too excited about because he's a flashy mover'. Waldman saw many 2024 plays that were dictated by defensive scheme where [[Rachaad White]] in the same spots would have produced a similar result, if less prettily, so he calls Irving 'a little smoke and mirrors in a fun way' and says he is not looking at the [[De'Von Achane]] of the NFC. He also flags that if [[Chris Godwin]] leaves, Tampa Bay lacks viable receiving threats unless they find a competent replacement, which cuts both ways for the run game. Bob Harris (not tracked) agreed on the price — Irving will go near RB1 territory in the second or third round, no value there — but argued the extrapolation of his stronger second half over a full season as lead back is a fair argument, and people telling themselves that story may not be wrong; Waldman would take him at top-20 back.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): 'fuck it' on Irving being worth a top-10 fantasy running back price in 2025 — not on talent but on value. Waldman said Irving is bumping right up against his ceiling in cost and you can get similar or greater production cheaper elsewhere. He allowed the workload could genuinely arrive: someone he knows who was at the Combine reported Buccaneers staffers turning their nose down at [[Rachaad White]], so a heavy Irving role is possible if the team believes he can handle the volume. He also flagged [[Sean Tucker]] as a threat to be the second-best back on the roster. Waldman pointed to his notebook for the fuller Tampa backfield breakdown.
- 2025-06-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-30]]): Irving is being drafted a little too high, and his eight rushing touchdowns all coming in the red zone is only one layer of the problem. On film, Waldman saw many red zone plays schemed to look like passing alignments where the defense was playing pass heavily and Irving got the ball — he credits Irving for finishing those, and calls him a genuinely good starting-caliber NFL back, but says the scheme deserves enough credit to soften any 'red zone monster' framing. He also flags [[Sean Tucker]], currently the third back, as fast and big enough to potentially become the second back and even push Irving. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed he is overpaying at current ADP and would rather wait and take [[Kyren Williams]].
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Irving 'doesn't look like a high-touch player on a regular basis,' so even if Tampa moves on from [[Rachaad White]] he will need a complement — and Waldman believes that complement is absolutely [[Sean Tucker]], who he rates as bigger and faster than Irving. He also noted that [[Jordan James]] was the better between-the-tackles runner of the two at Oregon.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Irving is his overpriced top-30 ADP player, despite a fantastic rookie year of roughly 1,500 total yards on 262 touches with eight touchdowns. Waldman's three reasons: much of the production came in situations where defenses expected pass and Tampa surprised them with him — similar to how [[DeAndre Swift]] accumulates yardage — after the league had guessed [[Rachaad White]] was the bell cow; 5.4 yards per carry is very hard to sustain year over year, with [[Nick Chubb]] about the only back to do it in this era; and Tristan Wirfs may open the season on PUP and miss multiple games, which hurts both pass protection and the run game. Waldman still calls Irving a clearly better between-the-tackles runner than White, but flags a three-headed backfield with real drumbeats to get [[Sean Tucker]] involved.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Irving is not a weapon back in the spacing sense — his damage comes as an option out of the backfield, and no defense worries about him beating them vertically from the slot, which is the specific skill the role requires. [[Matt Waldman]] had raised the fantasy concern that slot deployment might route work toward [[Sean Tucker]], [[Emeka Egbuka]] or [[Chris Godwin]] and make Irving overrated as a producer; Angelo's answer was that Irving simply will not be used that way, so the concern does not apply to him [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-13 — According to Dave Kluge of Footballguys (guest, not a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-13]]: he ranked Irving his RB17 and said he could not wait to draft him, but has drafted none because Irving is going as a top-10 back near ADP 21 — 'I like the player a lot, but I just cannot get behind the value.' Kluge loves the cutting ability, tackle breaking and big plays, but notes Irving's breakout only came after [[Rachaad White]] was injured, and while White has clear deficiencies as a rusher, his passing-game role is unlikely to be phased out. Kluge's projection is early-down volume with 80-100 rushing yards but fewer targets, plus regression from Tampa Bay's offense overall. [[Chris Harris]] is aligned, listing Irving with [[De'Von Achane]] as tiny backs the market irrationally throws caution to the wind on.
- 2025-08-28 — Asked whether any running back could break his receiver-heavy habits in the first two rounds, DENNY CARTER of Rotoworld (guest, NOT a tracked expert) said on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]] [Redraft (Standard)] that it is Irving — he takes a receiver first, Irving in round two, then a run of receivers — because Irving out-touched [[Rachaad White]] by so much late in 2024 that he thinks it will be impossible for Tampa Bay to walk that back. According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]]): he watched a bunch of Irving film in April and fell in love, but was frustrated to learn from Andy Behrens that Irving was already going in the second round, which he says is beyond where he can take him. His stated concern is not the talent but the workload justification Tampa can reach for — keep the small guy healthy, so roughly 55 percent Irving, 35 percent White and 10 percent [[Sean Tucker]], with Irving getting everything only when the rubber meets the road. He calls the split pure Category 2 — unknowable in advance — and grants that if Carter is right about usage, second round is a great value; Carter adds that hyper-efficiency is Irving's fallback out. Harris's nickname for him is the sprinter soldier.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): Harris rates Irving a 7 out of 10 and guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) a 6, both on the split-backfield question. Harris pushes back hard on the consensus that late-2024 usage settled it: [[Rachaad White]] was dragging himself onto the field through injury all year and finally fumbled against Dallas — 'the Old Yeller moment' — while Irving ran with fresh legs against defenses that were half checked out for the offseason. Harris says he does not draft Irving where he goes and would not spend a second-round pick, understands the ceiling case if Tampa really does make him an 80% player, but thinks the market is not acknowledging the floor. He also notes Irving is tiny, which is its own durability question.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): an explicit reversal. Harris says he questioned the August consensus that Irving would take over what passes for every-down work in the modern NFL, and for one week that questioning was wrong. The snap count was 43 Irving, 14 [[Rachaad White]], one [[Sean Tucker]], with Tucker's lone snap being a carry that came before White ever touched the ball, for an 18-3-1 touch advantage. Irving, whom Harris calls the sprinter soldier, did not do a ton overall and looked a little miscast on short yardage because he is small, but with that kind of touch advantage all you need is one play: he beat three defenders to the pylon on a red zone screen to take the lead, and later scored a receiving touchdown on a brilliant quick move. Harris says you have to leave this game feeling better about him than he did to start the year.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Irving was awfully good against the Texans and is certainly the Buccaneer you start. His move in the open field — stopping dead and letting a defender fly past him — was how Tampa got down close on the winning drive; Harris calls him an elite quickness player with jets. The snap count was 53-21 over [[Rachaad White]], and on third down with two yards or fewer to go the backfield snaps this season are 8-1 Irving. The caveat: White vultured the goal-line score, and Harris is a little scared for how tiny-boned Irving is given the punishment he absorbed, though he kept getting back up. He wonders whether Irving was simply gassed at the end or whether Tampa genuinely prefers White on the goal line — noting that was literally the only snap Tampa has taken inside an opponent's five all year.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): pre-empting the efficiency complaints about 66 rushing yards on 25 carries, Harris said 'I thought Bucky Irving was awesome — he was the best running back on the field.' The evidence was individual creation: a first-half screen where he gained 25 yards entirely on his own, running sideways at full speed to escape six surrounding defenders; a third-quarter carry where he hit it hard, broke a tackle and stiff-armed another defender. 'A lesser back, especially one without a lot of power, that guy might bounce' — Irving just smacks it, which Harris calls good and disciplined. Snaps were 50 for Irving to 15 for [[Rachaad White]], confirming him as the full-season lead back. The one worry is durability: he is small and took a lot of punishment again.
- 2025-09-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-26]]): Irving is his number four target and a stated change of position — he admits he had Irving too low and was simply wrong. Fantasy Pros shows Irving's market value down versus ADP, but not versus where Harris now has him. He loves the tape, says Irving is a better player than [[De'Von Achane]] even though Achane has been better fantasy-wise so far, and waves off the efficiency crowd's yards-per-carry and yards-over-expected framing: Irving is RB20 in fantasy points but 10th in scrimmage yards, so the gap is just touchdowns. Risk: he is a little guy and can get hurt, and the price is premium. Harris frames it as a ponies-for-a-horse consolidation — [[Jaylen Warren]] plus [[Rome Odunze]] — which Josh calls really, really close and possibly needing a small bump on the receiver, though Josh would not give up [[Nico Collins]] in a two-for-one.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Irving is seeking second opinions on a foot injury and was wearing a walking boot on Wednesday, which Harris read as a strong signal against Sunday availability versus the Seahawks — possible for a booted player to suit up, but not what he would guess. He removed Irving from his running back ranks entirely and told managers to plan on not having him. Harris was pointedly unreassured by Adam Schefter rushing out to say the injury is not season-ending, joking that the eagerness to calm everyone made him more worried, not less, and comparing the vague, expert-confounding foot problem to [[Joe Mixon]]'s. He raised [[Rachaad White]] to RB22 as the direct beneficiary.
- 2025-10-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-03]]): Irving has not practiced with 'some kind of exotic foot injury' — 'not exactly what you want' — and for the moment Harris assumes he misses at least Week 5. Harris separately cites Irving's owners as exactly the running-back-needy managers you should be shopping your own backs to.
- 2025-11-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Irving was the one injured player Harris allowed onto his pivotal five, and the frustration is informational — Irving is not practicing, Todd Bowles has made reporters feel weird for asking, and the story has drifted from a foot injury to a shoulder as well ('they're not near each other'). Harris ranked him 65th in his rest-of-season top 100 and admits he does not know why that number: 'I guess I would take the chance if somebody wanted to give him to me at that level — you've got [[DJ Moore]] and you want him, sure — because he could be a league winner, but my god he might not play.' Jim McCormick (guest, not tracked), who traded a first and a third for [[Bijan Robinson]] to go all in, is exposed to Irving and calls him a Darren McFadden type disappearing into small pieces. Harris says a contender should be willing to give up non-crippling depth for the shot.
