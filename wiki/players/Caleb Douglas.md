# Caleb Douglas

## Expert Takes

- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-30 (Round 3 WRs)]]) [Dynasty]: Miami took the Texas Tech product 11th in round 3, a pick Harmon calls aggressive -- Douglas was one of the last players he had left off his rookie roundup and only charted him after the pick, alongside the Bears' Zavion Thomas. At 6'3', 205 he ran a blazing 4.39 (85th percentile) but posted a bizarre 9th-percentile vertical jump (31.5') despite a fine broad jump. Both hosts note his movement style and even his head-duck while running routes look 'eerily' like Quentin Johnston, though Douglas is a better contested-catch player and ball-tracker downfield while being less explosive and much less of a tackle-breaker. He's a skewed-alignment prospect -- 85% of sampled snaps at right outside receiver, 79% on the line -- projecting as a likely (somewhat sacrificial) X. His two most common routes, the nine and the dig, were also his worst by success rate, but he flashed well on quicker routes: 64% success vs. man, 67% vs. press in a small sample. Harmon calls him a 'classic fourth-round' talent taken a round early, developmental but with real late-hands/ball-tracking traits after the catch; he says he's higher on Douglas than consensus.
- 2026-08-06 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-06]]): Angelo is fading Douglas -- he thinks the Cowboys' scheme (heavy crossing and over routes, movable pockets) is going to make camp look better than it will translate, because it's easy for reporters to be impressed watching receivers catch in stride and run downfield before real defenses take that away and force him to win a different way. He's uncertain Douglas has more to offer once opponents remove that structure.
- 2026-08-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-11]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon flags a 'pretty consistent drumbeat' out of Dolphins camp that Douglas and [[Malik Washington]] have emerged as Miami's top two receiver options, with almost no fantasy-analyst attention attached to it. He calls Douglas (6'4', 206 lbs, 4.39 40) 'much more substance than sizzle' — a smooth, in-breaking route winner who fits an offense built around play-action and in-breakers under new play-caller Bobby Slowik — and comps his tape early to a 'poor man's' Quentin Johnson: similar skewed-alignment college tape and movement pattern (a red flag), though Douglas was a better contested-catch receiver than QJ was in college and isn't the tackle-breaker QJ was. He views Douglas as a real developmental sleeper with a legitimate runway to a starting job that goes far too late in rookie and dynasty drafts, and says he needs to bump his dynasty rankings up on him.
- 2026-08-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Despite mixed pre-draft reviews -- a 6'4' frame that didn't play as fast as his combine testing suggested, plus drops and inconsistent hands in college -- Dolphins beat reporters were unanimous that Caleb Douglas is the receiver sticking out in camp, and Harris said he's been getting raves in Dolphins camp too. Harris moved him from WR91 in the first Almanac edition to inside the top 80, and now has him right around the top 150 overall players -- borderline for standard redraft rosters, more comfortably in play in leagues that also start kickers and defenses. He's currently Harris's highest-ranked Dolphins receiver, and Harris said the buzz 'gives us license to wonder' whether it continues and pushes Douglas further up the very late rounds.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-17]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman still says 'fuck it' on Douglas despite a highlight, Odell Beckham-style contested catch in the exhibition opener, where he played every starters' snap per Pro Football Focus alongside Malik Washington. Waldman argued Douglas has likely locked down the WR2 job, but the offense is built to feed him low-difficulty, high-YAC concepts -- crossers, over routes, and deep shots down the middle designed for mismatches rather than routes that require beating tight man coverage or reading complex post-snap adjustments -- while Malik Willis's rollout-heavy play-action scheme limits total dropbacks and reads. He compared the profile to the [[Claypool-Galladay Production Privilege]] pattern: long/tall/fast receivers who produce early in easy alignments before disappearing once defenses treat them as the primary target. Waldman expects good individual games but not weekly startable value -- at best a bye-week fill-in, with Malik Washington positioned as the offense's outside volume lead.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]) [Dynasty]: The only certainty in Miami's rookie receiver room is that Douglas and [[Malik Washington]] are the two who'll start, per camp reports, with everyone else needing not apply -- Waldman says that alone has value even in a shallow role. Miami has used Douglas in soft zone coverage on schemed routes (breaking inside then out, in the Golladay/Claypool mold) that don't require high route-running proficiency, so if he's fast enough to win those manufactured looks, Waldman thinks it can work, though he ranks these Dolphins names outside his top 150 overall and expects real roster churn.
