# Derek Carr

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: notes he's written optimistic preseason pieces on Carr two years running and is wary of doing it again, but lays out a specific, checkable mechanism for why 2023 looked bad on the surface but wasn't purely a Carr problem. Left tackle Trevor Penning played poorly enough early in the season that New Orleans had to scheme around him (extra blocking help, a scaled-back offense) just to keep Carr upright; once the staff adjusted protection at midseason, his results improved markedly — three-plus-touchdown games in 5 of 6 late-season outings (vs. Detroit, Carolina, the Giants, the Rams, Tampa Bay and Atlanta), with only the Lions game limited to one score. Notes receiver Michael Thomas's on-field conduct and public criticism of Carr complicated the read further ("not the best look"). Contingent on New Orleans adding another receiver, Waldman says he'll "still take my chances" with Carr as a low-cost QB2/streamer around the QB20 range — including as a late-round second quarterback in Superflex formats, a range he says he already found value in during a live mock (took [[C.J. Stroud]] at the 1-2 turn, didn't take a second quarterback until round 7-8, and still landed Geno Smith, not a tracked page, well ahead of where Carr was going).
- 2024-09-12 — According to co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-12]]): he has been in and out on Carr repeatedly. Early-career Carr took the safest throw every time and was 'pathologically incapable of stressing a defense' — Harstad's line is that early Carr is what people wrongly imagine [[Brock Purdy]] to be. Gruden made him unlearn that and take real deep shots; 2023 in New Orleans reverted to form, not helped by Trevor Penning busting at left tackle. The 59-yard Week 1 bomb to [[Rashid Shaheed]] three minutes in was the version he wants. Ceiling framed as QB12–QB16, a solid guy — not a great quarterback — but an interesting one who makes the whole Saints passing game interesting. Caveat: [[Chris Olave]] had a poor day and the 47-10 blowout of Carolina never opened the playbook. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees, noting Carr was reckless under pressure at Fresno State and once failed Waldman's since-retired RSP 'knockout factor' (as did Jimmy Garoppolo), and that he nearly listed the Saints offense in his insanity bold predictions but could not go there.
- 2024-09-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-17]]): Carr has played really good football in Clint Kubiak's offense and the transformation is scheme, not personnel — New Orleans barely touched the offense this offseason. Per NextGenStats the Saints were under center on 75% of plays in Week 2 and used motion on 74% of plays for 9.5 yards per play and a 61.5% success rate; Carr's play-action rate is 51.2% of dropbacks after being dead last in play-action rate, with the Saints dead last in motion last year — Harmon calls it a cataclysmic change. Per PFF, Carr's 13.2 yards per attempt on play action is second among quarterbacks, behind [[Sam Darnold]]. Harmon draws the general lesson he says he keeps having to relearn: when a proven above-average starter has a bad year, assume design before decline — the [[Baker Mayfield]] / [[Geno Smith]] / [[Jared Goff]] zone. The caveat he repeats: Carr is not an aggressive or good downfield thrower, which is why he and [[Chris Olave]] were oil and water.
- 2024-11-22 — According to Adam Harstad, untracked co-host ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]): Harstad said he has had to update a long-held prior on Carr. He previously rated him a phenomenal teammate — teammates seemed to like playing with him, and his handling of the Henry Ruggs situation raised Harstad's estimation. But after Carr threw the hospital ball that got [[Chris Olave]] hurt, former Saints came out of the woodwork, [[Michael Thomas]] among them on Twitter, saying nobody who played with him was the least bit surprised. Separately, Harstad thinks Carr has played well enough in 2024 that New Orleans may be trapped: they would arguably be better off if he were worse, because then moving on would be easy. [[Matt Waldman]] said he goes back and forth on Carr — every time he is out, Carr pulls him back in.
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]): asked whether Carr is the limiting factor for New Orleans, Waldman said he personally feels it but would answer 'fuck that' for how people will take it — the priority is building an offense good enough for any quarterback to play in, and he argues that if you dropped [[Sam Darnold]] into this team you would likely get more Carr-like results than what Darnold produced in Minnesota. His specific criticism is relational rather than mechanical: Carr 'pisses off his receiving corps' — or their families, referencing [[Chris Olave]]'s family — and is less popular with players than he should be. Waldman allows that reputations can be repaired, citing [[Baker Mayfield]] leaving Cleveland, but says Carr has been in the league too long for a sudden revelation: 'he's not the Scrooged of quarterbacks.'
- 2025-02-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-21]]): Carr's headline deep-ball production early in 2024 was scheme- and personnel-specific — cover-three beaters to [[Rashid Shaheed]] — rather than a general willingness to throw contested or deep-curl targets of the kind [[Chris Olave]] lives on. Harmon expects the Saints would accept any trade offer to get him off the books but doubts an outright cut, since the alternative is a full year of [[Spencer Rattler]] and Kellen Moore is unlikely to have taken the job to punt the position. He floats Tennessee as the cleaner landing spot on a day-three pick given Brian Callahan's Raiders familiarity, while James Koh (not tracked) speculated about the Giants using him as a bridge if they do not love [[Cam Ward]] or [[Shedeur Sanders]]; Harmon countered that the Giants are too desperate to kick the can.
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]): Waldman is emphatically out on the Saints being settled at quarterback with Carr, who they named as starter while also visiting with [[Cam Ward]] at the Combine. His football read: Carr reads a defense reasonably well pre-snap and can do reasonably well when given lots of opportunities for those reads, but pressure and the cumulative effect of pressure during a game lead to him falling apart late. In Kellen Moore's offense he places Carr at the lower end of the quarterbacks Moore has worked with, because whatever Moore puts in front of him, Carr is Carr. Waldman and Bob Harris (not tracked) also stressed how openly former teammates have criticized him — [[Michael Thomas]] volunteering criticism unprompted, and Waldman noting that Thomas's history as a worker obsessed with quarterback rapport makes that rant a signal rather than diva behavior.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): with Carr reportedly contemplating shoulder surgery that would cost him the 2025 season, Waldman 'feels it' that he has played his last game as a Saint — there is 'too much rancor with this team offensively about Carr and what he is as a quarterback,' citing prior friction with [[Michael Thomas]] and with [[Chris Olave]]'s brother as second-hand signals of how teammates view him. Waldman frames New Orleans as the third or fourth best team in the NFC South already, and argues that while the division is weak enough that the worst team could still win it in 2025, Carolina ([[Bryce Young]]) and Atlanta ([[Michael Penix Jr.]]) are on the rise, so the Saints risk being bottom dwellers for a while.
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): Carr's retirement was not a surprise — Waldman and co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) both said the signs were there once the Saints spent the 40th pick on [[Tyler Shough]], their highest quarterback investment in years, and once the shoulder-injury news leaked before the draft. Harris framed it as the club sorting out the money so Carr's salary could not be clawed back, and suggested Carr could resurface competing with Kirk Cousins for a job in a year. Waldman was blunt about the fantasy consequence for New Orleans pass catchers, calling Carr 'the albatross around his neck' when discussing [[Chris Olave]] and noting the harsh criticism of Carr's throws into contact from Michael Thomas, Robert Griffin III and Olave's brother.
