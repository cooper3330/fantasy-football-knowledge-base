# Jordan James

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: a back he wants to be good and enjoys watching, but doubts. Angelo sees a little [[Isiah Pacheco]] in him with slightly better feel, calls him a tough kid and very productive at Oregon, and notes he liked [[Bucky Irving]] initially on the same tape. The concern is depth of skill: Angelo's stated rule is that a modern back without depth of skill needs a genuinely high-end top trait, and he is not sure James has one — which makes sticking on a roster the open question.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: the Oregon back is a smart runner who combines the tools of the class's scat backs like [[Trevor Etienne]] — less explosive, but more consistent and more useful between the tackles. Waldman's hesitation is size and speed: at 208-210 pounds without top-end speed, he is not who teams are looking for in this class, and in dynasty he is the type who clogs a roster until the moment you cut him, then has a Tim Hightower year because someone got hurt. [[Brandon Angelo]] made him a firm 'it depends' on landing spot and echoed that he is capable but not what teams are seeking.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]): an emphatic yes on the 49ers' fifth-round back. James is not a breakaway runner but a quick accelerator who reaches top speed in a hurry, is very shifty, extremely smart, a tackle breaker at merely average starter size, and can pass block and catch — essentially what [[Jordan Mason]] did for this offense before the Minnesota trade. Waldman rates [[Isaac Guerendo]] as the more explosive power-speed athlete but a more up-and-down player, so if the Guerendo train falls apart quickly James is the beneficiary behind [[Christian McCaffrey]]. He groups James with [[Brashard Smith]], [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] and [[Woody Marks]] as similar-score backs and calls James the least-mentioned but maybe savviest runner of the group. Verdict: late redraft flier, and an absolute must in rookie drafts of seven-plus rounds.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]) [Best Ball]: he is 'totally feeling' James as a starter if [[Christian McCaffrey]] gets hurt again — with the caveat that [[Isaac Guerendo]] will probably open as the RB2. James worked with [[Bucky Irving]] at Oregon and was the better between-the-tackles runner of the two, has good contact balance, can catch, and is one of the two best pass protectors in the 2025 rookie running back class. Waldman would not be surprised if this becomes a committee and James usurps enough touches to be 'the running back to have' if Guerendo, whom he calls talented but up-and-down, falters. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) said he would rather try to get James later than pay the Guerendo handcuff premium, though he expects others to be trying the same.
- 2025-08-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: James was his fourth super-deep sleeper, and he called the pick craven — it is really just about him being a 49er, a Jordan James-sized hole in a depth chart in the Shanahan system, with questions around [[Christian McCaffrey]] always present. He noted the momentum stalled: James broke a finger and missed a couple of weeks, [[Isaac Guerendo]] is back from a shoulder issue, and San Francisco signed old friend Jeff Wilson, possibly just a camp body. Harris said the pick only works because Guerendo is functional again — had Guerendo been hurt long-term, James would have been too obvious for this list. Ciely liked the Shanahan-elevates-backs angle, offered a [[Tony Pollard]] similarity, and argued that if James produces even briefly, Guerendo may never get the backup job back. Harris added he does not think Guerendo is a particularly good player — a Tasmanian devil who never slows down when you need to run with rhythm.
