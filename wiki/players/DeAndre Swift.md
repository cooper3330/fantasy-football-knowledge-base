# DeAndre Swift

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not
  a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: reacting to
  Swift's 3-year, $24M ($8M/year) free-agent deal with Chicago. Bob Harris:
  "Khalil Herbert's a better running back, period. DeAndre Swift is the most
  hyped running back, period" — with that offensive line, it won't be the
  Eagles on a regular basis for him in Chicago. Waldman: [[Caleb Williams]]'s
  scrambling ability should manufacture favorable light-box run opportunities
  for Swift, but he remains a part-time back as at every prior stop — expects
  production in the "mid-range" of his career rather than a career year, not
  a full-time role. Both credit a fully healthy platform season behind
  Philadelphia's strong offensive line for earning him the payday, but Waldman
  calls him "a quality NFL contributor who can give you starter numbers... but
  not a top quality starting running back," wanting to see him process reads
  a little harder.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: used as the reference point for grading [[Kimani Vidal]] — again calls Swift "scheme-based," a player perceived by his production as an every-down, borderline-elite back but not actually the same thing between the tackles that he is in space; places Swift as "the mid-rent place" on a spectrum topped by Maurice Jones-Drew and Ahmad Bradshaw.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): repeats his standing critique using rookie [[Kimani Vidal]] as the foil — most fantasy GMs equate Swift with his box-score production rather than seeing a scheme-and-circumstance-dependent player with real flaws who needs a constricted set of favorable conditions to produce; when those conditions are met he can post strong numbers with strong talent around him, but "he's not a complete running back."
- 2024-05-14 — According to Adam Rank ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-14]]), not a tracked expert: Rank called the Bears' signing of [[DeAndre Swift]] 'such an underrated pickup' and 'such a great player,' expecting Chicago to run the ball more than fans assume given its now-crowded receiver room — part of why Rank's [[Caleb Williams]] passing-yardage projection (~3,500 yards) is conservative relative to the team's talent.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman agreed Swift landed in a good situation in Chicago — a strong receiving corps and dangerous QB in Caleb Williams — but doesn't think the touches translate to a great season. He expects Swift to be the trio's second-leading rusher 'but not by much' over Roschon Johnson, projects [[Khalil Herbert]] may still outplay him between the tackles, and pegs Swift at roughly 800 total yards behind an offensive line weaker than the ones he had in Philadelphia/Detroit. Going around RB22 (roughly seventh round in [[Best Ball]]), Waldman said he'd rather hand Swift's ADP slot to the field — Najee Harris, Jonathan Brooks, Jaylen Warren, Zack Moss or James Conner — and let a competitor draft Swift, calling Johnson 'the better deal' if the touches truly split three ways.
- 2024-06-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-10]]): admitted bias against Swift ('I never feel DeAndre Swift') and projects only about 150-160 touches, 800 yards and 6-7 touchdowns despite a 1,049-yard 2023 season, noting Bears colleague Jason Wood couldn't draw a straight line between last year's box score and this year's crowded backfield. He called the Chicago group a three-headed (potentially two-headed if [[Khalil Herbert]] gets hurt or traded to Dallas) committee: Swift doesn't run well between the tackles but is explosive on draws/screens and should get plus yards-per-catch; Herbert is the best inside runner with the best game-breaking skill but isn't powerful and isn't a good receiver; [[Roschon Johnson]] catches, blocks and runs with power. Combined, Waldman sees the trio for about 1,600 yards/10 TDs rushing, with Herbert closest to 700 yards. Crucially he expects Swift's receiving role to go the way it did behind A.J. Brown/DeVonta Smith in Philly -- capped, this time by DJ Moore, Keenan Allen and Rome Odunze -- so Swift will be efficient but low-volume.
- 2024-10-10 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert), co-host with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): the industry noise around Swift this season is misplaced because he is the same player he has always been. Harstad points out Swift averaged 4.6 yards per carry over the first four years of his career, then opened this season with one of the ten lowest three-week yards-per-carry marks in NFL history, then promptly went for roughly 5.8 in the following game. His framing is that you are reaching into a hat and pulling out a number: yards per carry has a repeatable component, but it is straight-line speed rather than general running back talent, and most backs who finish above league average only need one or two long runs removed to fall below it. He does not offer a target-share or role argument, only that the underlying player has not changed. See [[Yards Per Carry as Noise]].
- 2024-11-14 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14 (Age Cliffs and Dented Cans)]] [Dynasty]: Swift is the proof-of-concept for the dented-can trade. Managers were done with him heading into 2023 in Philadelphia and he finished a surprise top-20 back; they were done with him again in 2024 in Chicago and he is a surprise top-20 back again. Harstad's question is 'at what point do we stop being surprised that DeAndre Swift, whose skill set is very tailored to PPR fantasy production, winds up producing in PPR fantasy leagues.' He groups Swift with [[Najee Harris]] and [[Javonte Williams]] as the trio ranked as top-five dynasty backs prematurely, then written off emotionally when they missed unrealistic expectations, and uses him as the template for what he expects from Williams in free agency.
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18 (Feel It or F-It Free Agency Winners and Losers)]]): Waldman splits from Sigmund Bloom (untracked), who listed both [[Caleb Williams]] and Swift as free-agency winners. Waldman is on board with the play-caller upgrade to Ben Johnson and the interior line rebuild — Joe Thuney, Jonah Jackson and center Drew Dalman signed away from Atlanta — but says 'fuck it' on Swift specifically. He expects Chicago to draft a running back, and draft one early, leaving Swift as a sidekick more often than not. His read of the roster logic: Swift is insurance, a hedge that lets Chicago improve the run game behind the new line either way, and only if the back they want is gone before their pick does Swift matter more than hoped. Bob Harris (untracked) agrees.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]) [Best Ball]: RB32 is a nice value on Swift, but with a timing caveat — take shares now if you are doing volume Best Ball drafts, and wait until July before making him a centerpiece investment for August drafts, because Waldman expects Chicago to add another back ([[J.K. Dobbins]] and [[Nick Chubb]] have been mentioned; Dobbins is complicated by the rare unrestricted tender from the Chargers) who eats into his work. Waldman still likes what Swift does in the open field. His worst case is Chicago adding both a Jamal Williams type as the B-back and Dobbins as the A-back, which would squeeze Swift; if it is only Swift and Dobbins, Swift returning RB30 is still fine at an RB32 cost. If instead the competition is only [[Roschon Johnson]] and rookie [[Kyle Monangai]], Waldman calls Swift 'an absolute steal.'
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): asked whether Swift at RB29 is ridiculously underpriced, Waldman says he has him at RB24 — better than ADP, and probably the floor of his price. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) supplies the argument: the market baked in a Chicago running back addition, assuming either [[J.K. Dobbins]] or [[Nick Chubb]] was bound for Ben Johnson's offense because Johnson's first year with Swift in Detroit was immediately followed by Swift's departure — and those moves never came, so the discount was priced against an event that did not happen. Waldman agrees the departure may have been the impersonal search for a more complete back rather than a verdict on Swift, and notes Ben Johnson's Lions had multiple backs inside the top 24 — sometimes top 12, occasionally top five — in each of the last three years. The caveat: he has rookie [[Kyle Monangai]] at RB32, and thinks Swift and Monangai could move in opposite directions, with Roschon Johnson also in the mix.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): no on the weapon-back question. He described Swift as a poor-man's [[Alvin Kamara]] in the angle-and-option receiving game, and was blunt that he is not a very good runner and not a great route runner either, which makes pinpointing a role difficult — and noted Ben Johnson historically has not loved him. He also flagged that Chicago has 'weapons on weapons' and too many mouths to feed. [[Matt Waldman]] added that Swift's speed is not matched by burst. Angelo's follow-on: if Swift is a 3.6-yards-per-carry back, carries flow elsewhere, including to [[D.J. Moore]] [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-15 — According to Cousin Josh (untracked co-host of the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast]]) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-15]]): Swift is his number 5 frag player, and he says he hates everything about him — his Almanac comp was Folgers coffee, emergency use only, never an every-week starter. Swift's ADP of 60, the last pick of round five, is where league winners come from, and Josh sees capped upside instead: an inefficient runner who has never been a strong touchdown scorer, in a Bears offense where he does not believe every skill player between [[D.J. Moore]], [[Rome Odunze]] and Swift can hit. He ranks him RB28, not even an RB2, and would rather take [[David Montgomery]], [[Tony Pollard]], [[Calvin Ridley]] or a [[Travis Hunter]] upside shot; round seven or eight is his price. He notes the same pattern in Detroit, where Swift went around pick 15 in 2022 and finished about RB26. [[Chris Harris]] agreed, making Swift his number one film room zero, and noted the irony that Swift was his own top undervalued player in December 2022 before Harris changed his mind about discipline and running the play as designed.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): the workload is not in question — Swift played 51 snaps to [[Kyle Monangai]]'s nine, so 'there's certainly no threat there'. Harris liked the tape too: at the end of the third quarter, on a play that was not soft at all, Swift simply ran over corner Isaiah Rodgers, which Harris contrasts favourably with his 'normal bounce everything routine'. Verdict: definitely keep Swift in the RB2 conversation. The caveat attached to the whole Bears offense is quarterback play — Harris says the Vikings defense looked good and Chicago essentially managed three points between the opening drive and a meaningless late possession.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Film & Theory)]]): Swift is the other Ryan Poles-picked holdover Ben Johnson is stuck with — drafted ahead of where Chicago could have paid [[Saquon Barkley]] because he was cheaper and the front office believed they could get as much out of him. Waldman points to the Bears leading the league in rushing yards before contact while sitting dead last or near it after contact, framing that as an indictment of Swift rather than the blocking. He believes Johnson would already have moved on from Swift if he could, and reads Johnson's public 'he's fine and we don't have much proven behind him' comments as a holding pattern. If the split does not improve within a couple of weeks, Waldman expects Swift's workload to shrink.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Going Deep Listener Q&A)]]): a strong negative, framed statistically. Angelo notes Chicago is best in the NFL in yards before contact per attempt at about 1.89 while ranking second-to-last in per-down rushing efficiency, which he calls a direct indictment of the back — Swift is roughly 31st or 32nd in efficiency per rush behind a line producing top-two yards before contact, averaging about 58 yards a game on 17-18 carries. Waldman says Swift 'plays hard, just does not play well' and repeats a line he has held since Swift's Georgia days: without fixing the internal clock and down-to-down management he is not an every-down back. Angelo cites him as the archetype of a back who keeps earning contracts on missed tackles forced. Waldman expects Swift's role to lighten and change.
- 2025-09-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-26]]): Harris calls Swift the player he likes to poop on, but concedes he has been average rather than bad — still getting 15-16 touches and catching balls, with everyone enjoying the Dallas game. He would rank [[DeVonta Smith]] higher than Swift rest-of-season, yet argues Swift is way likelier than Smith to pry [[George Kittle]] loose, because the team dealing a star is usually the one desperate for 15 running back touches a week. Swift is also the running back in Josh's rejected [[Chase Brown]] offer alongside [[Chris Olave]].
- 2025-10-10 — According to guest Cousin Josh (Detective Fish, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-10]]): Swift is his cold-start honorable mention, 'agent zero,' and a player he will simply never buy — he says he pooped on Swift plenty in the preseason and in magazines, has been a notorious hater, and once dealt him right before an eruption and still won't change. Harris lays out the line: scrimmage yards of 65, 69, 78 and 60 with two scrimmage touchdowns in four games, which he concedes is better than some options you have to roll out, and he groups Swift with [[Tony Pollard]] as players clearly drafted as RB2s who aren't killing a fifth- or sixth-round investment. The bear case is that watching the games, neither looks like he is playing well enough to keep the job. Harris notes [[Kyle Monangai]] is injured and uncertain coming out of the bye, which could benefit Swift, and mentions [[Matt Waldman]] said on the prior day's show he is willing to start [[Luther Burden III]] out of the bye while Harris is not. Harris's bottom line: even needing a running back, he isn't buying Swift, and would buy Pollard before Swift.
- 2025-10-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-14 (Week 7 Waiver Wire & More W6 Film)]]): Swift carried the Bears offense in the MNF win over Washington -- 108 yards rushing plus a broken-tackle 55-yard receiving TD off a simple screen, aided by a Chicago offensive line Aikman repeatedly praised as much-improved. Harris credits Swift for taking full advantage of the improved run lanes rather than just the blocking itself.
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman credits Chicago's improved run blocking, not a change in Swift's skill set, for his better recent play -- the offensive line is now getting to the second level consistently, letting Swift simply get downhill and find the cutback lane rather than needing to create something out of nothing, which he called a weakness of his game ('he doesn't have to make lemonade out of lemons... but if you give him lemonade and tell him to put a little ice in it, he can do that'). Waldman ties this to how long a new scheme's run-blocking timing takes to click, since reduced-speed practice reps can't replicate live-tackle timing. Bob Harris pushed back that run scheme is the easiest offensive component to install since backs 'just do what backs do,' which Waldman disagreed with, attributing the surge specifically to Ben Johnson's complex scheme finally clicking with the line.
- 2025-10-20 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon credited scheme and offensive line more than pure talent for Chicago's run-game surge -- over the last two weeks the Bears rank first in RB yards per game, first in percentage of carries going 5-plus and 10-plus yards, and first in yards before contact per carry, but only 13th in yards after contact. He said Ben Johnson has 'really leaned into the strengths' of Swift specifically -- perimeter runs and clear lanes rather than asking him to read things out on inside zone -- aided by [[Darnell Wright]] playing well at right tackle since returning from the bye. Harmon's bottom line: 'if you have him, you can just use him,' but he wouldn't trade for Swift given a track record where 'there's something dumb that he's going to do at some point.'
- 2025-10-23 — According to Jake Trowbridge (untracked, DFS lineup-reveal co-host, [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-23]]): Swift has been 'on a little bit of a heater' and represented good DFS value at $5,900, with his matchup against a Ravens run defense that 'has given up to everything' this year (hopefully healthier post-bye) framed as a real referendum on that unit.
- 2025-10-24 — According to Cousin Josh (not a tracked expert, co-host) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: names DeAndre Swift his pick for likeliest to fall off a cliff, likening him to a straight-to-DVD-tier movie -- 'below serviceable' but still killing time. Swift is currently RB14 on what Josh calls 'ridiculous efficiency,' averaging roughly seven yards per carry over his last two games behind breakaway speed, but Josh isn't a fan of the underlying player and flags a committee forming with a teammate now getting 13-14 carries as a bruiser/goal-line complement, plus Swift already getting dinged up and a difficult closing schedule (at Philadelphia, at Green Bay, and two games against Green Bay's division). Chris Harris agreed this was a legitimate pick and contrasted it favorably against 'wimpy' alternatives like [[George Pickens]] or [[Rico Dowdle]], whose current roles are purely a product of a teammate's injury rather than genuine opportunity.
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-10-27]]) [Redraft]: scored from in close on a well-designed screen [[Caleb Williams]] checked into after reading the defense; split backfield work with Kyle Monangai (31-28 snap edge, 13-7 touches favoring Swift). Harris calls [[DJ Moore]] more of a flex given Chicago's muddled red-zone offense, implicitly putting Swift ahead in the pecking order.
- 2025-10-31 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-31]]): Swift, whom Harris used in DFS the day before, hasn't practiced this week with the same groin injury he's been managing for about a month. Harris doesn't believe it's a re-injury and expects him to be fine given he's played through it in recent weeks, though he allows the team could simply give him a rest.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06]]): Swift is playing much better, but Angelo credits the offensive line and Eric Bieniemy drilling on-schedule running more than a change in Swift. His frustration is that Swift has an off-schedule habit in an offense whose inside zone, outside zone and duo calls are entirely timing-and-block based, and Ben Johnson has helped by giving him fewer options post-bye and calling more inside runs. Angelo still rates him the better player in space, as a pass catcher and in the screen game. He expects [[Kyle Monangai]] to hold a large role because he keeps the offense on schedule better, and does not think Swift is back in Chicago next year — projecting a Monangai 1B with a drafted or acquired 1A. Waldman adds Swift as his standing example of a back whose production swings with offensive line quality across every stop.
- 2025-11-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: had a 'brutal' game against Pittsburgh -- nine touches for 29 scrimmage yards and a probable lost fumble Harris thinks he was likely down on before it was wrenched away at the bottom of the pile. Kyle Monangai took over the meaningful work, especially on early downs (18-8 in Monangai's favor on first downs specifically), even though the overall snap count was close to even (34 Monangai, 26 Swift) until the clock-killing stretch. Harris says it 'feels super wise to temper expectations' for Swift heading into his revenge game against Philadelphia on Black Friday.
- 2025-12-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-01 (Feel It or Fk It)]]): Waldman said DeAndre Swift is more likely than [[Kyle Monangai]] to be phased out of Chicago's backfield mix as the team looks for a faster back to pair with Monangai's power role.
- 2025-12-01 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-01]]): Chicago's beatdown of Philadelphia was, per Harris, 'the kind of win that makes me take Chicago a lot more seriously,' with the run game the story -- Philadelphia's defense, which had been keeping them in first place, 'didn't even pretend' to stop it. Harris said he and Andy Behrens (untracked, Yacht Club co-host) debated on their Wednesday show whether Andy had DeAndre Swift ranked too high; Harris had the Bears' backfield 'side by side' with Philadelphia's in his own ranks, and 'it didn't matter, we were both right' -- Chicago's run game crushed the Eagles regardless of exactly which back got the work.
- 2025-12-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-16]]): [[DeAndre Swift]] was the best player on the field in Chicago's win over Cleveland, running more direct and tougher and breaking more tackles than usual, including a good physical touchdown run. He out-snapped [[Kyle Monangai]] 35-28 in a non-competitive fourth quarter. Harris said Swift deserves rank deference over Monangai but thinks they should still be valued close together since touchdowns could go to either, with both in viable starting territory as they've been for a while.
- 2026-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: explained Swift's Philadelphia success versus his regression under Eric Bieniemy in Chicago through pre-snap box counts -- in Philly, [[Jalen Hurts]]'s running threat forced defenses to account for him as an extra box defender, giving Swift a structural numbers advantage even on non-designed quarterback runs; Chicago's scheme adjustment removed that structural edge, which Angelo frames as a system change rather than a decline in Swift's own skill.
- 2026-07-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman said he prefers [[DeAndre Swift]] over both [[Bhayshul Tuten]] and [[Jadarian Price]] at a similar ADP range, even knowing Swift will share touches with Kyle Monangai, citing Ben Johnson's public comment wanting 'more' from Swift and Johnson's history of feeding Swift 78 targets in a single season back in Detroit as evidence of how he could be used in Chicago.
- 2026-08-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-10]]) [Film Room Zeros] [[Ambiguous Backfields and Market Risk Aversion]]: Harris's #1 zero, for the second straight year, is DeAndre Swift -- a legitimately fast home-run hitter who 'doesn't hit as many home runs as you think' because he constantly bounces runs outside instead of taking what's blocked, which Harris thinks makes him susceptible to losing touches. He notes Chicago's per-game splits: from Week 7 on, Swift was fantasy's RB13 while [[Kyle Monangai]] was RB19, but Monangai earned progressively more red-zone work because the coaching staff clearly trusted his downhill, take-what's-there style more. Harris still ranks Swift RB23 -- he's not without value and remains in his prime -- but calls him 'the prince who was promised' every year without ever fully cashing in, and says he'd still rather have Swift than Monangai on tape value alone even though he doubts Swift stays the clear lead back all season.
- 2026-08-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: despite [[Kyle Monangai]]'s knee injury opening the door for more early-season work, Harris did not bump Swift, whom he already has going around round 5 and says he's 'allergic' to taking any higher -- relying on Swift for a full workload, or even a 50-50 split, 'has not exactly been profitable' historically, and Harris expects Monangai back by October even if Swift outperforms his ADP in September. He expects the broader market to push Swift's price up and says he'll be fine not participating in that.
