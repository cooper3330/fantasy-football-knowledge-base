# Malik Willis

## Expert Takes

- 2024-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-20]]), with guest Jagger May noting Willis is rumored to be cut or on the roster bubble in Tennessee: Waldman's ceiling comp for Malik Willis is Tyler Huntley at best. He described Willis as always having been a slower processor of the middle of the field, with the arm talent and movement teams covet but neither the accuracy/placement nor processing speed needed to start. Waldman was skeptical Willis has fixed those flaws behind the scenes and expects him to either wash out, catch on elsewhere as a fringe backup, or be asked to switch positions the way Terrelle Pryor moved from QB to WR and had a genuinely productive year doing it.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-19]]): Willis came up twice. Waldman listed him alongside Andy Dalton and [[Skylar Thompson]] as one of three quarterbacks stepping in for injured or benched starters in Week 3, and separately noted Willis was part of the 2022 draft class in which Waldman graded Thompson highest. Co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) praised Willis' character — asked whether he held a grudge against the Titans, Willis said they paid him, gave him his shot and did right by him — but then said, half-joking, that this is also why Willis will never be great: 'I'm moving him down my fantasy football rankings after that answer,' contrasting him with the Brady, Rodgers and Jordan archetype where 'you've got to be a little bit on the hinge to achieve that level of greatness.' Waldman agreed Willis comes across as a genuinely wholesome, well-adjusted person.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): not a legit NFL starter yet — the crucial qualifier being that no defense has scouted him as a Packer and built a game plan against him. Waldman noted you could have plugged [[Will Levis]], Marcus Mariota, Paxton Lynch, [[Drew Lock]] or Josh Dobbs into the same early-season narrative, and that Tennessee's knowledge of Willis does not transfer because they do not know what Green Bay is doing with him. He gave Willis real credit: low-mistake football, runs the ball very well, a cannon arm, and genuinely good when set up to make quick, accurate decisions — which is exactly what Matt LaFleur, whom Waldman does call a legit NFL coach, has been doing. The risk is when defenses find a way to make him overthink. Waldman allows a path to becoming a real starter; his point is that it was never going to happen immediately, and never on a team that handed him a bad offensive line and asked him to be the savior.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): Willis is a live example of a cast-off quarterback resetting his career. A third-stringer in Tennessee, he started for Green Bay after Jordan Love went down and, in Angelo's telling, admitted in post-trade interviews that he had no idea what he was doing in Tennessee and was not equipped to start at that time. Angelo says if you polled GMs now on Willis versus [[Will Levis]], most would take Willis — a reversal he treats as evidence that inventory of information and experience, not traits, is what separates young quarterbacks. He puts Willis alongside [[Anthony Richardson]], Geno Smith and [[Drew Lock]] in the group who have seen the career divide and responded.
- 2024-11-22 — According to Adam Harstad, untracked co-host ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]) [Dynasty]: Willis is a highly drafted backup quarterback Harstad sees sitting on waivers in a lot of leagues and likes as a free lottery ticket. He is explicit that he is not predicting anything — probably Willis is nothing — but there is a really good chance he gets another starting shot and a non-trivial chance of a Brad Johnson or Rich Gannon style late-blooming career. [[Matt Waldman]] added the developmental angle: Willis has admitted he did not know what he was doing in Tennessee, and that admission is itself a positive signal that he has figured out how to work at it. Harstad noted culture and fit matter, but tempered it with the Mitch Trubisky-in-Buffalo warning that team praise often amounts to nothing.
- 2025-02-27 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-27]]): [[Malik Willis]] is the cautionary tale for the 2025 class — Angelo says that if you had not traded him to Matt LaFleur's Green Bay he would probably be out of the league, and that playing a not-ready rookie in year one can sink him permanently. He uses Willis explicitly as the risk case for [[Jalen Milroe]]. [[Matt Waldman]] adds that Willis genuinely needed more development than [[Patrick Mahomes]] did, but what he showed in relief in 2024 'looked more like an NFL quarterback and a guy who wasn't so lost'. Both also recall that the pre-draft market badly missed on Willis's draft slot — expectations of Detroit at two, then a wait until the third round — as evidence that a prospect like [[Shedeur Sanders]] could still slide.
- 2025-12-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-22]]): Waldman credits Matt LaFleur for developing Willis -- a 'great physical specimen of a thrower' -- into a much more structured, improved player, and expects him to get another shot as a starter somewhere, floating Arizona as a plausible landing spot given their lack of a clear long-term answer at quarterback. Even so, he doesn't trust Willis over a full season; he could see a team game-planning to win a single game with Willis under center, but not relying on him across multiple starts given his thin experience as a starter.
- 2025-12-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-26]]): Willis is questionable with a shoulder injury but has told reporters he'll be able to play; Harris guessed Willis, not [[Jordan Love]], starts Saturday, though that could change. He flagged that Willis under center makes Green Bay's pass-catchers less attractive than if Love plays.
- 2026-01-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-15]]): Willis played well when called upon for Green Bay in 2025, a real step up from his rough early-career stints filling in for Tennessee and the 'smoke and mirrors,' run-heavy relief snaps he took for Jordan Love in 2024. Harmon counted roughly ten teams (Dolphins, Jets, Steelers, Browns, Raiders, Cardinals as clear openings; Colts, Falcons and Chiefs pending starter health; Vikings and Panthers as open competitions) needing a Week 1 starting quarterback in 2026, with only Fernando Mendoza broadly liked among incoming options. That thin market, plus limited game tape to pick apart, positions Willis to land a big free-agent contract 'for no reason' relative to his sample size.
- 2026-01-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-26]]) [Dynasty]: Willis is 'probably about to pay off' for dynasty holders who have had the roster room to stash him while he waits for an opportunity. Elsewhere in the episode Willis is noted as a pending free agent with reported ties to Green Bay through the new Pittsburgh coaching staff's connections, which Waldman flags as an easy dot to connect for his next landing spot.
- 2026-02-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-02]]) [Dynasty]: with Willis hitting unrestricted free agency, Waldman floated Miami as a plausible landing spot given the Green Bay-to-Miami staff pipeline, calling it 'a nice tryout for a year.' He said Green Bay schemed more play-action and one-side-of-the-field reads for Willis than they did for [[Jordan Love]], so Willis is still learning the position as a starter, and a full season would show whether he can take the next step. Waldman added that if a new team builds around what Willis does well as a runner -- as he speculated Miami might alongside [[De'Von Achane]] -- his outlook on that offense would improve.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-05]]): Harris predicts Willis will land the largest free-agent QB contract this cycle -- something like $30M/year, roughly $60M guaranteed, as a mid-range starter -- with the Dolphins, Cardinals, Jets, Browns and Falcons all possible suitors. He calls it 'crazy' that a passer who couldn't beat out Ryan Tannehill and Will Levis in Green Bay has rehabbed his image on just three total starts over two years plus mop-up snaps, but notes Willis played well in that sample. Harris is skeptical it works out but frames it as inevitable given how much cap-flexible money NFL teams have to spend on a quarterback gamble, comparing the skepticism to what Sam Darnold faced before Seattle.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-05]]): Willis is reportedly the free agent Arizona is 'hot and heavy' for after cutting Kyler Murray, with ties running through new OC Mike LaFleur's brother and GM Monti Ossenfort's Titans front-office history drafting Willis. Harmon calls Willis the most intriguing free-agent QB available but warns that if he starts, Cardinals pass volume -- inflated to a 'total fantasy scam' level under Murray/Drew Petzing in 2025 -- should regress hard, dragging down [[Michael Wilson]], [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] and even Trey McBride from a pure production standpoint, even though Harmon still likes the offense's outlook overall. Harmon and James Koh both treat rampant Aaron Rodgers-to-Arizona rumors as a running joke, not a real possibility.
- 2026-03-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-09]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman called Willis an 'A+ human being' (citing the well-known story of him giving his own clothes to a homeless man at the combine) who, given more time, could develop into a good starting quarterback. He named both Arizona and Miami as plausible $20-30M/year bridge-QB landing spots -- Miami's new coaching/front-office regime came from Green Bay, where Willis played -- but ultimately favored Arizona winning that sweepstakes given the organization's desperation for a QB answer. Waldman's overall read is that Willis has more developmental runway than finished-product certainty right now, and that he'd rather roster [[Kyler Murray]] on a cheap deal than pay Willis top bridge-QB money.
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-12]]): Willis signed a two-year deal with Miami, joining a number of former Green Bay executives and coaches there. Harris says Willis represents real hope relative to the retread quarterbacks available this cycle -- his limited Green Bay tape (89 total attempts over two years) looked enticing in several high-pressure spots where he moved the offense -- but cautions that not all 89 attempts are created equal and that Willis was genuinely bad in Tennessee, so the two-year term (rather than a longer deal) is appropriate given how unproven he still is.
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-12]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Willis signed with Miami at roughly $22.5M average per year, a modest markup on last year's Justin Fields deal, and Koh cautioned this is 'really a trial run' -- Willis has taken only two career games with 20+ pass attempts (23 in 2022, 21 in 2025), and his 2024 Green Bay starts leaned on a run-heavy, almost high-school-style offense rather than proving him as a passer. Both hosts noted Miami's decision-makers came over from Green Bay and know him personally, which they see as giving this reclamation bet a real ceiling despite the thin sample. Harmon added that mobile-QB, RPO-heavy offenses tend to boost the first read's overall target share, drawing a comp to Michael Pittman's big 2023 season under a similarly run-heavy Gardner Minshew -- which could be good news for [[Jaylen Waddle]] if Willis wins the job.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host, untracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-19]]): Willis re-signs with Miami on what Harmon describes as essentially a markup on the Justin Fields contract -- not a commitment to him as a long-term starter -- taking a discount to reunite with a coaching staff that already knows him after what the hosts call a 'traumatizing' Tennessee experience. The hosts flag that Willis inherits a nearly empty receiver room after Jalen Waddle's trade to Denver (Tutu Atwell, Malik Washington, and little else), and note mobile quarterbacks like Willis tend to take off running before working through progressions to the running back, which threatens [[De'Von Achane]]'s pass-game usage. Miami holds two first-round picks (11th own, 30th via Denver) plus multiple third-rounders and is expected to add receivers early in the draft to build around him.
- 2026-05-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-25]]) [Dynasty]: on whether Miami's public commitment to Malik Willis -- with coaching staff comments about developing his performance before leadership, and calling it a long-term plan -- will pay off, Waldman said 'I think it could.' He framed Willis's arc from an 'overpicked Tennessee Titan' to a competent executor of Green Bay's scheme, and likened Miami's plan (play-action, run game, letting Willis's legs set up his arm, pairing him with after-the-catch receivers who can work the middle and vertical seams) to what a team would build around an early-career Steve Young: conceptually simpler reads that are still hard to execute without Willis's arm talent and athleticism. Waldman said within a few years Willis could develop into a quarterback worth paying long-term, contingent on Miami's offensive line and a currently weak-looking receiving corps supporting the plan; he floated a healthy [[Malik Washington]], [[Chris Bell]] and [[Kevin Coleman]] as a trio that could eventually rival Atlanta's group.
- 2026-06-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-01]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman compared Miami's plan for Malik Willis to a 'Jordan Love-type' player but with more rushing upside and less passing upside, cautioning that his gaudy 10-12 yards-per-attempt game logs come from a small sample and won't hold over a full season. Instead he projected roughly 3,000-3,200 passing yards, about 20 total touchdowns, and 700-800 rushing yards, with a completion profile in the 50s-to-60s percent range built on shorter passes over the middle rather than the 70%-plus efficiency some might project. Waldman said Willis will be 'fantasy viable' in that range.
- 2026-07-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman is skeptical of what Willis's Green Bay tape actually proved, arguing the Packers ran a simplified, 'training-wheels' scheme built on misdirection with one-to-two reads and just one side of the field read -- effective early in games and in college/high school ball, but something defenses solve after enough tape, at which point they dare a QB like Willis to try to win with his legs alone (comparing him to Justin Fields or early-career Josh Allen). If Willis can't show more as Miami's starter, Waldman expects more three-and-outs and fewer checkdowns for [[De'Von Achane]].
- 2026-07-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-27]]) [Dynasty]: given uncertainty around the [[Kyler Murray]]/[[JJ McCarthy]] QB competition, Waldman says Willis 'looks tastier' as a later-round dynasty gamble, adding he's fairly confident Willis becomes a starter somewhere eventually and is comfortable speculating on that upside since neither Willis nor the competition's other names cost much draft capital.
- 2026-08-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-06]]): Waldman says Willis is not a good full-field, drop-back thrower -- what he did well in Green Bay the past two years was almost entirely within a highly manufactured scheme (throwbacks, screens, designed rollouts to a half-field read), and defenses that saw him for more than 4-5 games learned to take away roughly 70% of that playbook and force reads he doesn't handle well. He expects Miami's offense to be run-heavy off zone read (an area Willis is genuinely good at, citing his Liberty tape and tuck mechanics) and expects lots of three-and-outs, sacks, and scrambles, cutting into [[De'Von Achane]]'s passing-game role, though his own goal-line rushing ability could siphon some short touchdowns from the backfield as well.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]): Waldman is blunt that 'Malik Willis, that's probably all I need to say' as a red flag for Miami's passing-game rookies -- Willis's good raw numbers in Green Bay came in a scheme mashed down to one read and a scramble option, and Waldman doesn't think Willis develops into a QB who reliably finds a second or third read in Miami.
