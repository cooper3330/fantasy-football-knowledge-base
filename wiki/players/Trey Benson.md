# Trey Benson

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-22]]) [Dynasty]: named, alongside [[Dillon Johnson]], as a running back with real physical "nasty" to his game — an aggressive, physical finisher NFL rooms value for exactly that trait. Distinguished from a pure power runner by pairing that aggression with genuine abrupt-accelerator speed, comped favorably to [[Isiah Pacheco]]'s acceleration but without Pacheco's unconventional ("wonky") movement signature — Angelo frames him as combining physicality with a cleaner, more translatable movement profile.
- 2024-03-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-23]]), solo RB-class cast [Dynasty]: reaffirms Benson's power but flags a specific issue — he doesn't attack unblocked linebackers aggressively enough at the crease, the same early-career flaw Rashad White and Travis Etienne (neither tracked) both had and both overcame. Unlike those two, credits Benson for already working ahead as a route runner beyond screens/wide routes/swing passes, though a "bounce" attack technique on contested catches needs fixing to cut down drops.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): a brief contrast case in the episode's "problem solver" segment against [[Blake Corum]] — Angelo relays Benson's own combine answer for how he plays ("I see managers run") as evidence of a simple, hard-running style without much strategic layering, calling him a good player but not a problem solver in the way Corum is.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: both hosts like Benson as a prospect but expect him to sizzle in camp on speed and aggression, then fizzle as a rookie because, per Angelo, the game moves too fast for him to process — he's 'ultra-aggressive, hits it hard' with shades of Tevin Coleman, and won't realize the benefit of playing behind [[James Connor]] until he's already lost touches around September. Waldman agrees, grouping Benson with [[Marshawn Lloyd]] in an [[Isaiah Pacheco]]-style 'see it, hit it' archetype that needs time to learn to slow the game down; he notes Benson and Lloyd actually played behind cleaner college blocking than Pacheco did, so their processing lag is more purely cognitive and, in his view, more correctable long-term. Waldman separately says he undervalued Benson pre-draft but likes him more now given the chance to sit behind Connor, a free agent after 2024, before presumably inheriting the lead role.
- 2024-05-20 — According to guest Jagger May (Football Guys, untracked) and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-20]]) [Dynasty]: prompted by David Johnson's retirement, both compared Trey Benson's profile to peak David Johnson — fast, big, "quicker than fast," shifty, with strong hand-eye coordination — and argued early-second-round rookie capital like Benson's carries first-round value given a weak 2024 class; May called it a potential dynasty Hall-of-Fame-caliber outcome. Waldman extended the comp: Johnson entered the league as a gap-scheme runner who couldn't run zone and bounced too many runs outside but hit the hole with power, and Benson shows similar traits while still needing to refine his route running. Both see [[James Connor]] as a bridge veteran who can mentor Benson through year one before Benson takes over as lead back, potentially breaking out as soon as his second season.
- 2024-07-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman likes [[Trey Benson]] as the clear heir apparent behind [[James Conner]] but called him 'more of a down-the-road play' than an immediate difference-maker, flagging pass protection, route running and decision-making as his weakest areas -- weaknesses that matter because they're exactly where teammate [[Michael Carter]] is strong. Waldman framed the real value question in Arizona's backfield as Carter vs. Benson rather than Benson vs. Conner, saying he expects Carter to out-snap Benson early if Benson struggles in protection.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): a passing but positive note — Benson had a nice rushing touchdown as one of three Cardinals to score a first-half rushing touchdown alongside [[Trey McBride]] and Emari Demercado, the first time in more than a century Arizona had done that. Waldman frames it as a sign of life behind [[James Conner]], while still treating Conner as the unquestioned heartbeat of the backfield.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]) [Best Ball]: asked whether the market is too low on Benson at RB48 in the 13th round, Waldman says 'feel it.' He points to Jonathan Gannon floating a 1A/1B backfield at the combine and to [[James Conner]] himself endorsing a split, and describes Benson as a talented player with good speed, decent enough power and pass-catching potential who is more of a big-play threat than Conner ever was. He still expects Conner to lead the backfield at roughly two-thirds to one-third, but says that is substantial enough that Benson cannot be written off. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) warns the market will overreact to this single item and bid Benson up past where it should, but says he will take shares now while the price stays in double-digit rounds — young talent plus contingent upside if the full role ever arrives.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): the Arizona backfield was 'more Trey Benson than you might suspect' — 39 snaps [[James Conner]] to 21 Benson — and curiously, after the deep ball to [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] put Arizona inside the red zone, the first two carries went to Benson. He could not get in, and the next time Arizona got down there Conner took the shovel-pass touchdown. Benson also had a 52-yard run off tackle that Harris discounts: the Saints botched the edge and he had nobody to beat, so Conner or any other back would likely have broken it too, though Harris does credit Benson's speed.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with [[James Conner]] carted off, Benson took 20 snaps to Emari Demercado's seven and looked pretty good — a 29-yard run on a duo-style power play where the line opened a huge hole but Benson still had to accelerate diagonally to make the meeting linebacker miss. Harris: 'it seems very clear that Benson will get first crack at a much larger role.' Demercado is what he is, a pass-down specialist whose main contribution was a drop inside the ten; Bam Knight is a special-teamer and [[Michael Carter]] is on the practice squad. 'This is why we drafted Benson.'
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): at 52 percent rostered Benson was too widely owned for the one-third-of-leagues waiver list, but Harris said 'the big name, if you're in a shallow league and he's available, is Trey Benson — he would be our one.' Asked to choose between Benson and [[Chris Godwin]] (61 percent) for a neutral manager who is not a [[James Conner]] owner, both Harris and guest SCOTT FISH (Fantasy Cares, NOT tracked) took Benson, on running-back scarcity — 'you've got to add the running back, even if you're just going to trade him.'
- 2025-09-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: previewing Seahawks at Cardinals with [[James Conner]] out, Harris said he is aggressive on Benson at roughly RB22-23, right around [[Cam Skattebo]], and guest Ben Gretch (Stealing Signals, not tracked) said they were in lockstep, thinking of both as mid-RB2s. Gretch's case: a 23-year-old former second-round pick Arizona clearly likes, a very strong missed-tackles-forced-per-touch rate, roughly 87% of snaps after Conner went down in Week 2 including heavy route usage, no running backs added this year, and only Emari Demercado on obvious passing downs plus Bam Knight as competition — a long runway where one bad game will not cost him the job. The shared caveat is that Benson was never a college workhorse; Gretch treats consistent 20-carry college seasons as a real skill Benson has not demonstrated. Harris was cooler than Gretch, saying he is only okay on Benson and sees 'a little Raheem Mostert' in his game — fast and big but not someone who makes people miss, having made just one man miss in the hole last week — and would happily sit him a week if he could. Both expect a lot of information from the Thursday game.
- 2025-09-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-26]]): Benson's first game without [[James Conner]] was rough — 13 total touches for 54 yards, eight carries for 35, never sniffing the end zone, and he sat out the entire final drive while [[Emari Demercado]] scored the tying touchdown. Harris is not giving up after one game and notes Benson was constantly met in the backfield where Conner likely wouldn't have done better either. Best carry was a well-blocked 12-yarder around the left side; his last carry showed real power, plowing through a pile for six yards where he should have had zero. Weaknesses: no moves when taking on a linebacker, and one-on-one in space defenders simply tackled him. Hands were reliable. Harris concludes Benson may not match Conner's power but hasn't been oversold as a strong back, and his top-end speed could be great though he never got close to it. The game established a pecking order: Demercado on third downs (12 snaps to Benson's two), little Benson in the two-minute drill.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Benson is on IR after needing a scope on his knee meniscus, with a four-to-six-week timeline Harris called optimistic — he had understood Benson to be merely questionable after taking a knee hit late in the Thursday night game, and learned about the IR designation from Jake Seeley minutes before recording. Harris framed it as the collapse of the Arizona backfield in a fortnight: from [[James Conner]], one of his favorite backs to watch, to one week of Benson, to 'not much left.' Practically, Benson does not help fantasy rosters for most of the remaining fantasy regular season, and Harris noted the waiver advice he had given on Tuesday would have looked very different with this information.
- 2025-11-04 — According to Jeff Erickson of RotoWire, a guest and not a tracked expert ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-04]]): at 59% rostered in Yahoo, Benson is not a big-FAAB target even in a shallow ten-team league — we do not even know he will be the full-time guy when he returns, and Erickson is not certain when that return comes. He is a spec add: great if you get him cheap. The opportunity-cost argument is the real one — in a well-managed ten-team roster the drop would be someone more usable than Benson-if-things-break-right. [[Chris Harris]] agrees, adding that a regular listener already has several players at Benson's level on the bench, so why borrow problems.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): with Benson still on IR and nobody outside the organization knowing his timeline, guest **Brandon Funston** (The Athletic, not tracked) would take Benson over [[Woody Marks]] in a 10-team league, purely for the highest ceiling - he cannot get past the Houston offensive line ('the Mr. Yuck sticker face'), Arizona has been juggling with [[Emari Demercado]] flashing, and Benson looked pretty good before the injury. Harris tends to agree, and adds that other than Benson you should not be aggressively pursuing any of the healthy Cardinals rushers.
