# Amari Cooper

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]): reads him as Cleveland's secure top target regardless of the [[Jerry Jeudy]] trade — "Amari Cooper ain't going anywhere" — and grades him "certainly an upgrade" over [[Courtland Sutton]] as [[Deshaun Watson]]'s top receiver, with Jeudy more likely to slot in as the offense's third option behind Cooper and [[David Njoku]].

- 2024-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-21]]): names Cooper (with [[David Njoku]]) as the one part of Cleveland's offense whose identity is settled — "I think the identity can be Amari Cooper and David Njoku" — with [[Jerry Jeudy]], [[Elijah Moore]] and [[Cedric Tillman]] as supporting pieces around them rather than a settled receiver-room hierarchy of their own. Points to the play-action passing success Cooper had with [[Joe Flacco]] late last season as a template the offense can lean back on.
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: Waldman, a self-described Browns and Cooper fan, says Cooper's WR23 ADP might be slightly high because he doubts Deshaun Watson returns to his pre-injury form unless Cleveland tailors the offense specifically to Watson's strengths -- which would waste some of Cooper's talent. He notes Cooper hasn't attended voluntary offseason work amid a contract dispute (wants to be paid market rate, with a strong case based on recent production) but hasn't formally held out. Digging into last year's splits, Waldman found Cooper was roughly a WR18 during Watson's healthy stretch and played to a WR1 level in the two games Watson looked good on return, which pushes him toward calling Cooper fairly priced with outperformance upside that is 'highly dependent' on Watson's health and play.
- 2024-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-23]]): Cooper has played the best football of his career in Cleveland and is now 'a true number one receiver' — something Harmon says he never believed about the Raiders/Cowboys version, whom he saw as a genuinely boom-bust, half-speed-at-times player rather than just volatile in the box score. 2023: 72 catches on 128 targets for 1,250 yards, a career-high 17.4 yards per reception against a career-third-lowest 56.3% catch rate. Reception Perception: 71% success rate vs man, 78% vs zone — right at the 70/80 benchmark — with an exceptional intermediate profile of 87.1% on digs, 91% on outs (never cracked 80% before Cleveland) and 78.7% on slants, routes making up about half his tree. Harmon credits the pure perimeter X role plus play-action off the [[Nick Chubb]] run game, and speculates the way Dallas shipped him out for nothing lit a fire under him. Risks: age-30 season, holding out over his $20M final year (fined over $100k for skipping minicamp), and possible scheme change under new OC Ken Dorsey. Harmon still called him the best player in the division's four rooms when ranking them; James Koh (not tracked) leaned younger, preferring Baltimore's room to Cleveland's partly on Cooper's age.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Harmon says he does not have a strong read on Cooper's start (5 catches on 17 targets, 27 yards, per Koh). He notes three drops and a 17.6 percent drop rate, that Cooper has left plays on the field, and that he is still the lead man in first-read targets — but with [[Jerry Jeudy]] and [[Elijah Moore]] neck and neck behind him, Harmon wonders whether that means Cooper is not a true alpha-alpha receiver. His larger point is that none of the Cleveland receivers matter much until [[Deshaun Watson]] can be a functional NFL thrower; the Browns are 31st in dropback success rate. He ends genuinely mixed rather than buying or selling.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]): Cooper is a positive-regression candidate tied to a quarterback change. He is averaging only 10 yards per catch, but Waldman expects [[Jameis Winston]] to take over in Cleveland, and says if there is one quarterback you can count on to force-feed a receiver who runs good routes and still has a vertical game left, it is Winston. Winston will make enough mistakes to drive Browns fans crazy, but Cooper will get the opportunities and Waldman expects him to convert them. He frames this as a player who has been grinding through the season whose outlook people should start feeling better about. Waldman also notes he personally finds the Browns hard to watch this year and has not seen a game.
- 2024-10-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-17]]), discussing Cooper's trade from Cleveland to Buffalo: charting Cooper's Browns season, Harmon was struck by 'just how much man coverage' he was seeing — partly because he is the X receiver and X receivers draw more man-press, but mostly because nobody respects the Browns passing game with [[Deshaun Watson]] at quarterback. Per fantasy points data, Cleveland had the fifth-most snaps against man coverage in the NFL. Harmon frames Cooper as the player nobody in the remaining room replaces — 'you're not going to play [[Cedric Tillman]] over Amari Cooper at the X spot.' Co-host James Koh (not tracked) noted Cooper was still the fantasy WR36 in full-PPR despite that context, which he used as the bar [[Jerry Jeudy]] would have to clear.
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Cooper ran a route on just 34.3% of Buffalo dropbacks in his early Bills usage but was targeted on 42% of those routes — when he was out there, he got the ball. Harmon rejects the narrative that Cooper's presence created [[Keon Coleman]]'s big game, citing JJ Zachariason's (not tracked) charting that Cooper wasn't on the field for any of Coleman's seven targets. What he does think matters long term is Cooper as a high-leverage 'bucket getter' who keeps the offense moving, and the alignment knock-on: Cooper at outside X lets Coleman play off the line.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]) [Dynasty]: the film on Cooper has been okay this year and he cautioned against holding [[Deshaun Watson]]-era Cleveland production against him, but the Buffalo usage is the problem — even with [[Keon Coleman]] out he played behind [[Mack Hollins]] (blocking value, snow game caveat), and Harmon asked whether he will ever out-target [[Khalil Shakir]] in a game. Cooper turns 31 entering 2025, which he flagged as the central question for what any team pays him. He thinks a couple of big playoff games could still secure a deal, noting Cooper averaged over 20 million per year on his last contract. James Koh (co-host, not tracked) was more optimistic: after Buffalo's dominant win over San Francisco it is Super Bowl or bust, Cooper is still a pretty good player attached to one of the two best quarterbacks on earth in [[Josh Allen]], and a strong finish would make Buffalo hard pressed not to re-sign him at a reasonable number — though Koh conceded aging receiver plus brand new team usually does not work out.
- 2024-12-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-10]]): Cooper has been out-produced by [[Khalil Shakir]] since the trade — 5.4 targets, 3.2 receptions and 46 yards per game on a 59% catch rate across five games — and Harmon says Shakir is the more important receiver to Buffalo's passing game. He defends Cooper's evaluation, arguing you cannot judge any receiver by what they did with [[Deshaun Watson]] in a dead-in-the-water Cleveland passing game, and points back to the RP results he cited at the time of the trade to say Cooper can still be a fine player. He is more intrigued after the Rams game, where Cooper drew 14 targets with a lot of air yards, easily his most productive and most utilized game as a Bill. Harmon's overall verdict: not a true number one at this point in his career, but a dimension the room did not have before.
- 2024-12-24 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-24]]): Cooper is not washed but is a step slower and no longer the separator he was; the Cleveland production should be thrown out because nothing was activated in that offense before [[Jameis Winston]] played, and the tape still showed him beating man coverage on perimeter routes. But in Buffalo he has basically done nothing outside one Rams game. Harmon's route counts: since Week 14 the Bills go [[Khalil Shakir]] 106, [[Mack Hollins]] 90, Dawson Knox 88, then Cooper at 71; over the last two weeks Shakir 55, Hollins 48, Knox 46, [[Keon Coleman]] 43, [[Dalton Kincaid]] 36, Cooper 33 — sixth on the team. Week 14 he ran a route on 63% of dropbacks for 14 targets and 95 yards; the very next week zero targets on a 40.5% route share. Harmon concedes he is at least doing [[Sacrificial X Receiver]] work. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is harsher: watching him now he sees a player who is not engaged, 'all the worst bits' of Cooper, not trying to beat his man off the line, and says Reception Perception has long shown his highs are high and lows are low.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): if the Cooper trade is ever going to pay off, the divisional round against Baltimore is the week — Harmon's memory of charting the earlier Bills-Ravens game was that nobody was open on the outside. Cooper has not looked like a perfect player this year but still looks like a guy who can stack corners, get a little separation in man coverage and win at the top of the route and at the catch point. The caveat: Baltimore's secondary is much better now, with rookie Nate Wiggins coming alive and Marlon Humphrey playing well in the slot since Kyle Hamilton moved back to safety. Cooper ran routes on only 41% of dropbacks with three targets versus Denver, part of Buffalo's committee. Harmon also listed him among a weak 2025 free-agent class of past-prime and injury-recovery names.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-23]]): the Buffalo trade has been a total bust — Cooper has seen four targets for two catches and eight yards this postseason and zero receiving yards in the divisional round, and Harmon says he 'never really found his footing as a separator' with the Bills, being used mostly on downfield contested throws rather than as a priority target. Harmon does not hate the low usage because Buffalo has so many other answers ([[Khalil Shakir]] underneath, [[Curtis Samuel]], [[James Cook]]) and plays deliberately risk-averse, mistake-free football. He adds the wrist injury set Cooper back. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) went further: he does not see a fully engaged player, and says Cooper's long-standing MO is that he must be involved early and often, which Buffalo rarely does — one target arriving in the late third or fourth quarter.
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): Cooper 'has not really been' a true man-beater for Buffalo this year, and on the decisive drive the Bills were reduced to throwing him screens against man coverage and cover-zero looks. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) added the damning detail that all four of Cooper's targets came inside nine air yards — nothing on the sideline, nothing intermediate, nothing over the middle — with at least three appearing designed simply to activate him early; he finished four-for-four for 33 yards. Harmon later files Cooper alongside [[DeAndre Hopkins]] and [[Stefon Diggs]] as the 'number one receiver circa 2019' flavor of free agent, contrasted unfavourably with the younger [[Chris Godwin]].
- 2025-02-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-28]]): the mid-season trade the football world loved 'ended up being a big old nothing burger.' Harmon says he actually thought Cooper played decent football in Cleveland in isolation away from [[Deshaun Watson]], but he fell off a cliff in Buffalo and was never fully used. Buffalo gave up a third-round pick; Harmon expects them to move on rather than bring him back cheap. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) grouped Cooper with [[DeAndre Hopkins]] as prime-three-years-ago X receivers who flamed out in new homes, to the chagrin of fantasy managers — evidence for their recurring point that the league has a genuine dearth of real X receivers.
- 2025-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-21]]): Harmon rates Cooper the weakest of the veteran free agents on film — he says [[Stefon Diggs]] was clearly better than [[Keenan Allen]] or [[DeAndre Hopkins]] and 'definitely way better' than Cooper — and among the older receivers who win out-breaking routes from condensed alignments he ranks Diggs first, Hopkins second and [[Cooper Kupp]] third on explosiveness. Even so, he has thought all offseason that Arizona is an Amari Cooper team precisely because they would not need volume from him: he would be the third or even fourth option behind [[Trey McBride]], [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] and possibly [[Michael Wilson]], while providing the X-flanker flexibility that lets Harrison move around. Cooper had his best years in Cleveland under Drew Petzing's former offense.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): asked whether the Jets are making a mistake by not pursuing Cooper, [[Tyler Lockett]] or [[Keenan Allen]], Waldman says yes — Cooper or Lockett would be a worthwhile complementary option in that passing game — but flips the framing, noting the three veterans may equally be declining New York. His explanation for the standoff: the Jets may see Cooper and Lockett as redundant with [[Garrett Wilson]]; Allen's poor start to last season may read as decline; and a run-heavy Jets plan pointing toward more tight end usage makes paying slot-receiver money unattractive. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) suspects all three are waiting on post-draft or camp-injury developments, and that the money simply is not right yet.
- 2025-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-07]]) amid rumors of a Dallas reunion: Harmon is skeptical of what Cooper has left, saying the film was not good last year and his final Reception Perception profile came in at a 23rd-percentile success rate versus man, 34th versus zone and 29th versus press. The pattern was contradictory — with Cleveland's poor quarterback play he separated at normal levels but could not catch anything; in Buffalo he caught nearly everything but could not get open, which Harmon called concerning because a separation X was exactly what the Bills needed. Koh (not tracked) added the box score: one 14-target, six-catch, 95-yard game and never more than three targets from Weeks 8 through 17, plus three targets against Denver and four for 33 yards versus Kansas City. Harmon still sees a fit in a play-action in-breaker role — 86% slant, 87.5% post, 70.6% dig success rates — but terrible curl and a 30% nine route make traditional X work untenable. He thought Arizona under Drew Petzing fit, and noted the Dallas relationship ended badly over vaccination-status rumors. If he lands in Dallas he would be the flanker with [[Jalen Tolbert]] as a sacrificial X.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Cooper is hard to place because his alignment history is genuinely scrambled — an X early with the Raiders, moved to flanker by Jon Gruden (Harmon notes Dallas gets credit that belongs to Gruden), flanker/slot with the Cowboys, then what Harmon calls the best years of his career as an X in Cleveland. His 2024 Reception Perception profile splits oddly: good separation in Cleveland but constant drops and contested-catch failures with one of the league's worst quarterbacks, then almost everything caught in Buffalo while separation 'fell off a cliff'. Route success rates still held up on posts, digs and slants, so that should be his diet. Harmon's preferred fit is Arizona under Drew Petzing (Kevin Stefanski's former QB coach), mostly to take perimeter snaps off [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]; co-host James Koh (not tracked) floated San Francisco, Washington and a Dallas reunion, which Harmon thinks the [[Gabe Davis]] trade killed.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]] (ASR: 'Omari Cooper'): asked whether the receiver-starved 49ers might sign the still-unsigned Cooper, Nguyen said he does not know if Cooper is an NFL wide receiver anymore — nobody seems interested, and he disappeared during Buffalo's playoff run last year, leaving Nguyen unsure the speed is still there. He doubts Cooper will be on a team at all this year.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): the Raiders signed Cooper at the same time news broke that [[Jakobi Meyers]] had requested a trade in August over his contract. Harris notes Cooper 'famously looked utterly done' after Buffalo acquired him in 2024, but that we are told he was playing through a really bad wrist injury, which may explain it. He has added Cooper back to his receiver list: if you have a soft spot in the 12th round or so, 'take a shot'. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-27]]): Cooper's charting shows clear decline, and Harmon is 'pretty skeptical about what Cooper has left' after the Raiders signed him following [[Jakobi Meyers]]'s trade request. In his Browns/Bills sample he posted a 61% success rate vs man (23rd percentile) and 61.1% vs press (29th percentile) — roughly a full 10-point drop from his 2022 Browns peak of 71% vs man and 71.4% vs press, and far off the 70.7% vs man of his rookie year. He remained a pure perimeter player: 84.9% of sampled snaps outside, 68% on the line of scrimmage. He ran a go route on 9.4% of sampled routes with only a 30% success rate — go and curl were his two most common routes relative to NFL average and he was bad on both; Harmon thinks he can still help on in-breakers (slants, posts, digs at 70.6%) if used inside. Harmon calls it an odd, late signing with no coaching familiarity with Pete Carroll or Chip Kelly, likely to slot Cooper at X or flanker to avoid relying on a fourth-round rookie. James Koh (not tracked) adds Cooper has never strung together three straight 100-yard games and wonders whether last year was skill decline or a disengaged, mercurial player; Harmon agrees the question lingers.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-05]]): Cooper retired days before the season, roughly 10 days after signing with the Raiders, and Harmon called it 'surprising but not surprising' — a mercurial player whose heart and body were no longer in it, consistent with a career-long complicated relationship with football. Harmon said Cooper was not a good player in 2024 for either Cleveland or Buffalo and that he had thought Cooper was finished before the Raiders signed him out of nowhere; when people asked why Minnesota did not sign him instead of [[Adam Thielen]], that was his answer. He tied it to a recurring theme on receiver decline: sometimes there is no warning sign, it simply goes. Koh (not tracked) catalogued the career — drafted fourth overall in 2015 at age 21, five Pro Bowls, seven 1,000-yard seasons, a high-water 2023 with Cleveland (128 targets, 72 catches, 1,250 yards), never a 100-catch season (92 in 2020 with Dallas) and never a double-digit touchdown year — plus Cooper's post-Chiefs-win quote in November that it was the first time in a long time he felt joy. Reporting from Ian Rapoport said he had no desire to play.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Cooper announced his retirement on Thursday after roughly ten days back with the Raiders where his career began — he 'said lots of flowery things, then got 10 days worth of PD sunshine and decided that was enough.' Harris' sendoff is unsentimental: 'still fumbling the ball and running to the wrong spot to the very end of his career.' The fantasy consequence is that Las Vegas is left with 'the same rotating cast of receiver miscreants that Cooper was hired to replace,' none of whom Harris finds appealing until further notice.
