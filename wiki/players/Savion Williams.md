# Savion Williams

## Expert Takes

- 2024-12-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-12]]): Williams is his favorite under-the-radar technician — he runs the routes TCU asks of him well, with the weight drop, patience-then-suddenness and the ability to stem and manipulate a defender. Waldman rejects the 'he doesn't run a lot of routes' knock: he'd rather have a receiver who shows real craft on five routes than one with high draft capital who 'ran a lot of them but sucked at pretty much all of them', because concept understanding is what transfers. What separates Williams from [[Quentin Johnston]] and Josh Doctson is that he can actually go up and win the football — understands jump-up-and-through, which hand to attack with, and catches low-and-away, low-and-behind and between defenders in clutch spots. Rare contact balance for a receiver, with a little [[Cordarrelle Patterson]] in the open field. Angelo agrees he can climb boards after the Senior Bowl and Combine since, apart from [[Tetairoa McMillan]], the class lacks big ball-winners.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-23]]): Waldman calls Williams very intriguing and says he is a fan — explicitly rating him above [[Tetairoa McMillan]], while acknowledging a lot of people love McMillan and that his own view could change once combine work lets him cross-check the specific things he flagged on film. He offers it as an example of how differently he reads this class from consensus.
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: an explicit disagreement the hosts chose to leave standing. Waldman says 'f**k yes he's underrated' — a good pass catcher who makes tough plays and tracks well, emphatically 'not [[Quentin Johnston]]', closer to a [[Cordarrelle Patterson]] with the ability to learn and implement the receiver position, bigger and a very good athlete. Waldman ranks him about 12th with a score of 81.4, which in his scale means a player who can contribute early and develop into a starter, and he expects the number to rise if Williams tests well. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) says 'f it': 19th percentile adjusted career receiving yards per team attempt after age adjustment, 55th percentile quarterback rating when targeted, a 51st percentile pre-draft model score that rises with round-three draft capital — comparable model territory to [[Terry McLaurin]], [[Josh Palmer]] and roughly [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]. He has Williams around 15. Waldman reframes the gap as opportunity: a round-three ADP is exactly where you can wait on him after taking running backs and quarterbacks.
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): 'feel it' that Williams may be a better running back than wide receiver, and he began scouting him as a running back the night before, ahead of his normal RSP schedule — preliminary work suggests Williams could grade as a player who eventually starts at running back. At 6'2 and 222 pounds he shows high-end running-back contact balance, excellent agility to turn the corner, speed, footwork, power and vision. As a receiver Waldman rates him above fellow TCU alumni Quentin Johnston and Jalen Reagor: a good route runner on par with Johnston, but a better receiver and better after the catch, with some ball-attack issues that he does not consider the tracking problem Johnston had. His season-long comp has been [[Cordarrelle Patterson]] — specifically the version of Patterson that could have stayed a receiver had he retained information better, since Patterson executed routes fine but never translated defensive reads and game-plan detail.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): Williams makes difficult clutch catches and the easy ones too, and Waldman preemptively dismisses the coming criticism — when a TCU receiver drops a pass, armchair logo scouting invokes [[Quentin Johnston]], Jalen Reagor and [[Jahan Dotson]] to conclude TCU does not produce NFL receivers, which Waldman calls like arguing with a drunk flat-earther. He judges Williams's lapses far easier to correct than Johnston's were. The upside is after the catch: Waldman names him one of the two best ball carriers at the position in this class alongside [[Luther Burden III]], and says what Williams does with the ball in his hands is unique to any receiver or tight end this year. He notes teams are working him out at running back, and thinks it can still work if he stays at receiver.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): he wants New England to stop drafting slot and flanker types and 'rip the Band-Aid off' by taking Williams and playing him at X, with the fallback that if it fails he can be converted to running back or used as a wildcat quarterback next to [[Rhamondre Stevenson]] in the red zone. Waldman calls him a legitimate ball-winning receiver who is great through contact and at reading and redirecting, though his footwork is not good and he has weird mechanisms attacking the ball, and says he is a better ball-in-the-air winner and better through contact than [[Omarion Hampton]]. [[Brandon Angelo]] adds that Williams sees the game like a running back does and has starting-caliber running back talent, at least as a gap-scheme runner, calling him a better version of [[Antonio Gibson]] while Waldman leans more [[Cordarrelle Patterson]]. Both would love him as Denver's Taysom Hill dark horse.
- 2025-04-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-29]]): Green Bay double-dipping at receiver for Williams is a very Packers pick — 6-4, 222 with a 4.48, and a telling 2024 line of 60 receptions and 51 carries. Harmon is blunt that as a pure wide receiver it will not work: 46.4% success rate vs man, 66.7% vs zone, 39% on the nine route, with a press success rate in the same ballpark as [[Malachi Corley]], [[Kadarius Toney]] and [[Wan'Dale Robinson]]. He is a gadget, create-a-touch weapon, and the critical difference from Toney is that he catches the ball reliably — one drop over three games and a 100% contested catch rate — which Harmon says is the non-negotiable for a manufactured-touch player. James Koh (not tracked) comps him to [[Cordarrelle Patterson]] as a mover and to [[Mike Williams]] at the catch point for how he sells out his body, with the caveat that going for broke is what kept Williams off the field. Both like the fit given [[Christian Watson]]'s uncertain recovery timeline, and Harmon's main hope is that Williams absorbs the receiver-carry work so [[Jayden Reed]] can be a real receiver.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: a genuine value — Waldman has him at pick 25 on his board, at the lower end of his second tier, while drafters take him in the middle of the third round, and he found him still available in round five of a live draft. He rates Williams a starter-caliber prospect at two positions: graded purely as a runner he was Waldman's RB5 on gap-scheme work and would have been a day-two pick and a 1,500-total-yard college back had TCU used him there. [[Brandon Angelo]] calls him a positionless chess piece — wide, tight, wildcat — who takes pressure off [[Josh Jacobs]] and [[Jordan Love]] in an unexplosive Packers offense, with [[Christian Watson]] and [[Romeo Doubs]] likely gone after 2025 and [[Jayden Reed]] trade rumors floating. Waldman offers the comp of a younger, better [[Cordarrelle Patterson]] — Patterson was an RB8-to-RB10 in Atlanta at 30, so imagine the conversion happening at 23.
- 2025-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman flagged Williams as essentially free at a fifth or sixth-round cost in Green Bay and a fun look for that offense. He said Williams was a pretty good running back on college tape — if he graded the Packers backfield purely on what he saw and projected, Williams would be the number two back on the depth chart behind [[Josh Jacobs]], who is 27, ahead of [[Christopher Brooks]] and [[Marshawn Lloyd]], while also being a legitimate receiver prospect. The caveat is a crowded, cloudy receiving room where Waldman expects [[Jayden Reed]] to lead at around 800 yards with [[Romeo Doubs]] and [[Matthew Golden]] next.
- 2025-06-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-27]]): Williams is Waldman's favorite lottery ticket in the Green Bay room and possibly the most fun late fantasy-draft pick anywhere, because nobody knows what he is. As a receiver he has the running skills of a devastating flanker — hurdles defenders, runs through them, strong, fast, great vision for angles — and at the catch point Waldman rates him far above [[Quentin Johnston]], citing clutch catches against Texas with real technique, going up and winning the ball and adjusting in tight coverage. The route tree is unsophisticated so far. Separately, Waldman graded him as his RB4 or RB5 in a strong class purely on gap plays: the best contact balance of any back he watched, good timing of holes, great footwork, shifty, no hero-ball bouncing — a Cordarrelle Patterson fit who could become Green Bay's answer at running back once [[Josh Jacobs]] moves on if [[Marshawn Lloyd]] and the backups do not hold. [[Matt Harmon]] agrees he may have the best hands in the room, which he calls a strange thing to say about a player who may not end up at receiver, and wondered whether Williams takes rushing work from [[Jayden Reed]].
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]) [Dynasty]: 'totally feeling it' — Williams is his stated dynasty sleeper and a target in any draft with seven or more rounds, especially IDP formats. The TCU pedigree makes people think Jalen Reagor and [[Quentin Johnston]] and pass, but Waldman argues Williams has better hands than Johnston, positions himself better and can win the contested plays Johnston was drafted to win, and is a better runner after the catch than Johnston — in fact than just about any receiver in this class, and a better runner between the tackles or in the open field than many of the class's running backs. If [[Jayden Reed]] has been playing Green Bay's [[Deebo Samuel]] role, Waldman thinks Williams cuts into it and shows what that role really looks like, and could 'make Deebo Samuel look like a chump' if he grows into his pacing as a route runner — a tall order, but that is how good the promise is. He could become what people hoped [[Christian Watson]] would be, with better hands, or even a successor to [[Josh Jacobs]] if Green Bay leans into the ground work: already a good gap runner with great contact balance, spatial awareness and blockset-up feel to become a nuanced zone runner. His comp is what [[Cordarrelle Patterson]] could have been if a team had figured it out earlier. Risk: consistency has been the whole receiver room's problem.
- 2026-01-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-26]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman's favorite of the three names discussed with [[Kyle Williams]] and [[Isaac TeSlaa]], though he says leave Savion Williams on the table rather than make a firm decision yet -- he needs to see whether Green Bay's offensive coordinator departs. Waldman calls him the most versatile of the group, a good route runner and pass catcher at TCU who he thinks could become either a very good NFL running back or a good wide receiver; he says Williams has better contact balance than every running back on Green Bay's depth chart, including [[Josh Jacobs]], is faster than [[Chris Brooks]] and everyone but [[Marshawn Lloyd]], and holds onto the ball better than either. Comped to [[Jayden Reed]] but bigger, stronger, faster, and less savvy, Waldman says if Williams can learn to run a zone scheme (he currently profiles better in gap/pulling-guard toss concepts) he could become the eventual successor to Josh Jacobs as Green Bay's high-volume back. He was excited about Williams early in the season before an injury limited what the offense could do with him.
- 2026-04-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-14]]): Williams, a third-round pick last year, finished with an odd usage split of 11 rush attempts to just 10 targets. Harmon calls it 'a weird pick at the time' and grades him as more of a fifth-round talent, though he is 'a crazy good athlete' worth developing. Harmon's one positive: some of the gadget/jet-rush touches currently given to [[Jayden Reed]] could reasonably shift to Williams, and player development in Green Bay will determine whether that upside shows up.
- 2026-05-28 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-28 (Going Deep)]]) [Dynasty]: Williams is the 'wild card of the wild card' in Green Bay's backfield picture -- both hosts see him used as a receiver out of the backfield in a [[Cordarrelle Patterson]]-style role (toss plays, jet sweeps, gap plays), and Angelo said Williams may be the best gap runner on the roster if given reps, a skill he flashed at TCU. They floated pairing him with [[Jayden Reed]] and [[Matthew Golden]] on jet-sweep action to make the Packers' backfield picture less predictable if [[Josh Jacobs]] misses time.
- 2026-07-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (untracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-07]]) [Dynasty]: expect Williams (6'4, 220) to keep siphoning a gadget/'sacrificial-X'-style role in Green Bay's run and play-action packages -- Koh repeatedly predicted this as near-inevitable and a fantasy annoyance. Harmon credited his hands and catch technique as a real prospect strength but said his route running 'has such a long way to go,' and criticized the Packers' heavy gadget usage of him as something he'd like to see scaled back, particularly if it migrates onto an already injury-prone Jayden Reed's plate.
