# Romeo Doubs

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Dynasty]: counted among Green Bay's "big three" wide receivers with [[Christian Watson]] and [[Jayden Reed]], and Waldman says he's earned the love he's gotten — but calls him "a little less multi-dimensional" than what [[Dontayvion Wicks]] can offer, and flags him as the receiver most at risk of losing fantasy-relevant role share if Wicks keeps developing.
- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]): even ranking him "probably" Green Bay's fourth-best receiver behind [[Jayden Reed]], [[Dontayvion Wicks]] and [[Christian Watson]] "when all these guys are healthy and at their peak," he still went for 140 yards in the Wild Card blowout of Dallas.
- 2024-01-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-22]]) [Best Ball]: still finds a role in the crowded receiver room — reads coverage well and is "pretty adept at finding open spots" in zone. No change to his position as the receiver most at risk of losing role share if Wicks keeps developing.
- 2024-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-23]]): the clearest fourth-place ranking yet among Green Bay's four — "scrappy," doesn't have great hands and will misplay things at times, but runs good routes and is a "pretty good vertical player." Notes he had the best postseason of the four when [[Jayden Reed]], [[Dontayvion Wicks]] and [[Christian Watson]] are weighed against health/potential, going for 140 yards against Dallas — good enough to be a solid WR4 in the room, not a path to more.


- 2024-04-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-08]]) [Best Ball]: currently going as only a WR4, one of several cheap values in Green Bay's receiver room that Waldman ties to [[Jordan Love]]'s QB9 best-ball bargain price.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon used Doubs as his running example of a replaceable 'sacrificial X-receiver' -- saying that if Doubs 'was 10% worse the player you could just take him out of this rotation,' he is only just good enough to hold down the snap-eating outside role while Green Bay's other receivers ([[Jayden Reed]], [[Dontayvion Wicks]], Christian Watson) get more schemed/manufactured usage. Harmon said this is a point he makes repeatedly on the show -- Doubs graded as replaceable-level rather than a true difference-maker in a crowded, offensive-line-dependent room.
- 2024-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-30]]): Doubs is the player who muddies the whole Green Bay room — 'if he was 10% worse as a player he would be the guy' you could remove from the mix, but he isn't, because he is a genuinely solid outside ball winner. Harmon points to a Bleacher Report video in which [[Jordan Love]] diagrammed his ideal play and cast Doubs as the backside X running a clear-out for [[Davante Adams]], plus Love's comment that Doubs is someone he trusts on a fade — Harmon finds it telling because it matches his own read that Doubs fits the sacrificial-X, contested-ball role. Koh adds that Doubs is very tough at the catch point and has built real chemistry with Love, and that a quarterback simply liking a guy is not something to discount. Asked who leads the room in touchdowns, Harmon picks Doubs, 'just to piss everybody off', while Koh leans [[Christian Watson]] as a proven touchdown maker. Harmon rates Doubs first or second in the room as an outside ball winner alongside [[Dontayvion Wicks]], but thinks Wicks is the better all-round boundary receiver.
- 2024-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-05]]): Harmon describes Doubs as something a little more than a sacrificial X receiver but still handling backside work so Green Bay can do the fun things with [[Jayden Reed]], [[Christian Watson]] and [[Dontayvion Wicks]]. Koh (not tracked) frames him as the contested-catch, move-the-chains option the quarterback trusts. Harmon's open question is whether Doubs holds enough of the rotation once Reed and Wicks take the snaps he believes they have earned.
- 2024-10-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-07]]) [Dynasty]: Doubs skipping practice without telling anyone, and being benched for it, may have hurt his career at least temporarily. Waldman's framing is that diva behavior comes with the receiver position but you have to reach a certain level first — Doubs' actual role is primary chain-mover and second-highest target getter through four games, only two of them with his regular starting quarterback, which he considers thin grounds for a complaint, especially with one of the other receivers hurt and Doubs still slated for an important part of the game plan. He also reminds listeners that in the preseason he flagged Doubs as the player with the most to lose in this offense, and says the episode raises Green Bay's odds of moving on when the contract is up.
- 2024-10-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-17]]), after Doubs returned from his one-game suspension and scored twice vs Arizona: we learned nothing new, just 'confirmation of who he is' — the best [[Sacrificial X Receiver]] in the league, better than [[Gabe Davis]], which Harmon concedes may be damning with faint praise but insists is a real and valuable role. Doubs is a boundary player who runs the most routes per drop-back on the team and is always on the field, yet is targeted on only 18% of his routes — well below [[Jayden Reed]], below [[Dontayvion Wicks]] when he plays, and below [[Tucker Kraft]]. Harmon suspects the clear-out role is exactly what Doubs is annoyed about. The mitigating factor: [[Jordan Love]] 'will do the inadvisable,' looking pre-snap and chucking it up on clear-out routes — the touchdown was a classic fadeaway Love throw. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) stressed the Doubs-Love chemistry, apparent in 2022, less so in 2023.
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): asked to buy or sell Doubs as Green Bay's best overall receiver after 11 catches on 14 targets for 143 yards and two touchdowns in two weeks, Harmon sells — he still takes [[Jayden Reed]] as the best player — but says Doubs is the most reliable of the room, with the rookie hands issues long since resolved and no other Packer a consistent catcher of the football. Usage in Week 7 was, to Harmon, ideal: Doubs led WRs at 86.1% route participation as the primary X, 87% of snaps out wide. He defends him against the yards-per-route-run crowd who 'hate' Doubs, calling him the best sacrificial X in the league — then wonders aloud whether it's time to stop calling him sacrificial and just call him good. James Koh (not tracked) objects that 'sacrificial anything' sounds disrespectful, and argues Doubs' real edge is unquantifiable chemistry: [[Jordan Love]] simply likes throwing him the football.
- 2024-11-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-26]]): it is 'past time' for the fantasy and NFL communities to stop dismissing Doubs — he is a really good player who improved from a shaky, up-and-down rookie season into a better second year and better again in year three. Harmon was low on him after year one but higher than consensus after year two, because Doubs holds a genuinely valuable role on iso route reps that Green Bay has fed him consistently, including an excellent game against San Francisco before leaving injured. He says Doubs is not a player to be actively removed from the equation, should be on the field almost every snap, and already leads the room in snaps and routes. Koh agrees strongly, citing his chemistry with [[Jordan Love]]. Harmon notes the earlier conduct-detrimental suspension is largely forgotten.
- 2024-12-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-10]]): Harmon thought Doubs was pretty terrible as a rookie but improved in year two, and notes Doubs is the player who made him start talking about the [[Sacrificial X Receiver]] archetype. He still calls him a limited player, yet probably Green Bay's best option on iso routes on the perimeter right now because he has been the most consistent of the group this season. Doubs pitched a fit early in the year about involvement and the Packers responded by peppering him with targets. Harmon's frustration is structural: Doubs and [[Christian Watson]] should have roles, not prominent ones ahead of [[Jayden Reed]].
- 2024-12-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-31]]): Doubs has gone from an 'actively bad player as a rookie' to a good, solid contributor and is now probably Green Bay's most consistent perimeter receiver. Harmon explains his near-constant usage — he ran 100% of the dropbacks in the first half against Minnesota — as a function of role rather than stardom: Doubs is the X receiver who runs the ISO routes, and he is the most consistent player doing that job. The caveat is explicit and repeated: none of these Packers receivers, Doubs included, is a true No. 1 that a defense dedicates extra resources to take away, which is why Green Bay got played in man coverage across the board.
- 2025-01-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-14]]): Doubs left the wild card loss with another concussion, and Koh flags it as a major area of concern going forward — the hit itself did not look big, but Doubs 'was out of it for a while,' and concussions have been a recurring issue for him across the season. Both hosts otherwise like his role in the offense: Koh says Green Bay's passing game has looked its best when [[Jordan Love]] simply works to Doubs and [[Jayden Reed]] with the tight ends mixed in, rather than manufacturing touches for the fifth and sixth receivers. The caveat is that Harmon sees Reed, not Doubs, as the guy who should be empowered as the true number one.
- 2025-06-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-04]]): with [[Christian Watson]] ahead of his ACL schedule and [[Matthew Golden]] drafted in the first round, Harmon thinks Green Bay may explore trading a receiver, and Doubs — on the last year of his rookie deal, as is Watson — is the most likely candidate along with [[Dontayvion Wicks]], who profiles as the fifth receiver in a healthy room. Harmon speculates about Pittsburgh, who were hosting [[Gabe Davis]] that day, calling Doubs the same family of receiver as Davis and noting he has played with [[Aaron Rodgers]] before. He points back to their earlier X-receiver-need episode, listing Buffalo, New England, Tennessee, Kansas City, Las Vegas and San Francisco (if [[Brandon Aiyuk]] is unavailable) as teams that could pay a late-round pick for that archetype.
- 2025-06-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-27]]): Doubs looks like a star X from the release all the way to the point the ball arrives — 'an All-Pro until he has to catch it' — and the catch point is where Waldman's questions start. [[Matt Harmon]] notes Doubs's rookie year holds the highest drop rate in Reception Perception history, ahead of teammate [[Dontayvion Wicks]]. Waldman uses Doubs as the template for a wider point about Green Bay's scouting type: a room of athletic after-the-catch players with catching problems, chosen over better pass catchers and route runners. He also uses him for the camp-hype lesson — Doubs starting hot in camp on deep balls, then fading once [[Aaron Rodgers]] required receivers to read defenses the same way he did. Harmon's phrasing: if Doubs were 10% worse you could just take him off the field, but he isn't, so he stays in the rotation.
- 2025-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-11]]): Doubs' rookie season remains the highest drop rate in Reception Perception history, just ahead of [[Dontayvion Wicks]]' 15.4% mark in 2024. Depth-chart-wise Harmon sees Doubs as the one Green Bay receiver who can really only play outside, which is what keeps him ahead of Wicks. He flags Doubs as a trade candidate on the last year of his contract — floating Pittsburgh, where he has history and rapport with Aaron Rodgers — either in the preseason if [[Christian Watson]] returns strong or at the deadline, which is one of the few paths that would open real snaps for Wicks.
- 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]): asked on a Yahoo Football 301 episode to name the most average starter at every position, Harmon chose Doubs at wide receiver and defends it as a compliment — a great functional NFL player, 'total pro', who belongs on the field. His caveat is placement, not talent: if Doubs is your number one or a high-level number two, that is a bad sign for your roster. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) made the same point in raising him, that fandom has lost the plot in treating average as an insult when the league is full of median cases at receiver.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon frames Doubs as the boundary X in Green Bay's three-receiver set and, riffing on co-host James Koh (not tracked), calls him 'the [[Darius Slayton]] of the Packers' — the guy the team keeps trying to draft over, first [[Christian Watson]] and now [[Matthew Golden]], who nevertheless keeps starting every week. With Watson likely opening on PUP, [[Jayden Reed]] in a boot and [[Dontayvion Wicks]] banged up, Harmon expects a pretty healthy snap load for Doubs and Golden right off the jump. He does not credit Doubs with frontline talent — his broader point is that Green Bay has a deep collection with no true number one — but treats his role as durable regardless of the additions around him.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]): Doubs is Waldman's retrospective template for camp buzz that outran the film. In his first Packers camp everyone was starstruck at how good he looked from May through early August, and he did produce a couple of good preseason games with good stats, but the tape showed technique flaws against tight man-to-man coverage. The buzz came in the window where camp tests isolated skills; it cooled once layers were added, defenses played more sophisticated coverage, and [[Aaron Rodgers]] began changing plays at the line and demanding that young receivers see the field the way he did. Waldman uses him alongside [[Kyle Williams]] to show the pattern is recurring rather than player-specific. Cross-reference [[Speed of Instinct and Overthinking]].
- 2025-09-09 — According to guest Jake Ciely of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): Doubs is his number five waiver add at 15% rostered and was his only wide receiver honourable mention. He had been high on [[Matthew Golden]] and feels everyone got fooled — possibly by an injury picked up in the game, possibly just the Packers being the Packers, where nobody runs 75% of the routes. The one clear situation is that Doubs is either the one or the two: if Golden takes over, Doubs is still the two; if Golden never does, Doubs is the one. He concedes Doubs is a downfield boom-bust option, not a lock-steady lineup piece, and rates him a WR4 with big-play upside. [[Jayden Reed]] is the three-wide slot and is on the field less than the top two.
- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-11]]): Doubs is the Packers receiver on the field most — 73.9% route participation in Week 1, most on the team by a wide margin, with 93.9% of snaps out wide, which Harmon frames as a somewhat sacrificial X role. He says people still hate Doubs and he has had his own reservations about the limitations, but Doubs has gotten better every single year, the team trusts him, and Jordan Love plainly likes throwing to him. On the FanDuel prop he takes the over 3.5 receptions at minus-102, arguing the market is overreacting to a low-catch, high-yardage Week 1 and that Doubs' best work — quick slants and in-breakers against man — is exactly what Dan Quinn's Washington defense invites on critical downs.
- 2025-09-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-12]]): Doubs scored on an easy slant near the goal line, a play Harris credits entirely to [[Josh Jacobs]]'s gravity — Green Bay went heavy and imbalanced up front, faked the handoff, and the defense jumped, leaving the throw wide open. He notes the same look was replicated in the fourth quarter for a [[Tucker Kraft]] red zone touchdown, framing the scores as a function of the play-action design rather than of Doubs winning routes.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris's number-two Week 3 waiver add (an honorable mention for guest John Daigle of Establish The Run, not tracked). He concedes that when anybody tries to predict Green Bay receiver work share, god laughs, and that he does not know what happens even with [[Jayden Reed]] out — but Doubs is a good player he has liked for as long as he has been in the league, in an aggressive enough offense with [[Jordan Love]]. His supporting logic: opponents have come in determined to stop [[Josh Jacobs]] — Jacobs has broken more tackles than any other team through two weeks and has still been bottled up, getting by on touchdowns — which leaves room for a receiver. Harris will rank Doubs as his highest Packers receiver, a low-end WR3 around 34-36 with everyone playing, and would take Doubs over [[Tyler Allgeier]] in a flex for guaranteed on-field participation, though Allgeier is safer and Doubs higher upside. He would understand [[Dontayvion Wicks]] as an honorable mention and expects [[Matthew Golden]] backers to be excited.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-29]]): three touchdowns in the 40-40 tie with Dallas, including a fade from the six and a goal-line score off a fake to [[Josh Jacobs]]. Harris says Doubs certainly looked like the alpha in this Green Bay receiver room, with [[Jayden Reed]] out probably until November, though he frames the whole game as a track meet in which roughly 80 combined scoreboard points meant almost nobody who started a player in it got let down. [[Matthew Golden]] also made a huge fourth-down catch in overtime. Harris does not commit to Doubs sustaining a three-touchdown role, treating the alpha read as an observation from one game rather than a settled ranking.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-30]]): Doubs has 179 receiving yards through four games, more than [[Ladd McConkey]], and Harmon states flatly that he is Green Bay's number one receiver — not necessarily their best player, and he allows for a 1A/1B/1C reading, but Doubs is on the field the most by a huge margin at 83.6% of routes and runs the most routes. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) pushes back that 19 targets on the season is not a number-one workload. Harmon still picks McConkey for more rest-of-season yards, and separately uses Doubs as the archetype comp for [[Elic Ayomanor]]: a good, solid starting X receiver who will pop for three touchdowns on a Sunday night.
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-13]]): Waldman is beginning to say 'feel it' on starting Doubs every week. Everyone expected Doubs to be traded, which Waldman read as the Packers deciding he is what he is — and instead he has suddenly become their best receiver. The mechanism is a specific fix: Doubs was always a great route runner but did not know what would happen at the catch point, and now he is attacking the football more consistently and earning winning position early enough, which makes him a complete receiver. Waldman went as far as saying that if this continues, we may slowly see him emerge as one of the better wide receivers in football, while flagging he did not want to go too far. On five catches for 55 yards in this game, Waldman said Doubs — not [[Matthew Golden]], who led the way — is the Packer he trusts for consistent production.
