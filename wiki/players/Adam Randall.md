# Adam Randall

## Expert Takes

- 2026-03-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-09]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is 'fuck it' on Randall developing into a future NFL starter, projecting him as a decent backup at best. He likes Randall's size and top-end speed but flags poor acceleration, calling him more of a contact-balance/power, cloud-of-dust runner who can push piles and lean through wraps for chain-moving gains -- comping him to 'a bigger version of Trey Sermon.' The bigger red flag for Waldman is ball security: Randall grades below the RSP's acceptable fumble-rate threshold (under one fumble per 60 touches, even excluding his best season), and Waldman notes RSP's decade-plus study found backs below that threshold have only about a 10% chance of even sticking as a league RB2.
- 2026-03-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-30]]): Waldman rates Adam Randall's ceiling as close to, and slightly above, [[Chip Trainum]]'s. He pushes back on the assumption that Randall's background as a former receiver makes him a plus receiving weapon out of the backfield -- 'I would say that's a poor assumption' -- citing real issues with his catching and route-running that make him more 'a big back with a true receiver's background' than an actual pass-catching threat. Waldman also flags that Randall needs a long runway to get to top speed (a comp he draws to Omarion Hampton), which limits the offensive schemes that fit him, and says he's still learning to leverage his pad level at 235 pounds. Waldman gives Randall the overall edge over Trainum for now, but thinks Trainum could pass him with continued development.
- 2026-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-01]]) [Dynasty]: A former wide receiver over 230 pounds, Randall's size should be an asset against contact, but Waldman said his pad level is inconsistent -- on some runs his pads ride too high and he takes hits he could otherwise run through, while on others he drops his pads over his waist and loses balance before reaching the point of attack. Waldman wants to see him develop a more consistent feel for pad level.
- 2026-04-09 — According to Paul Pertichese, guest analyst on the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-09]] (not a tracked expert) [Dynasty]: Adam Randall is a wide-receiver-to-running-back convert drawing hopeful David Johnson comps from some evaluators, including guest Eric Edholm on the Yacht Club show, but Pertichese pushes back hard -- he thinks Randall converted positions specifically because he was a genuinely poor receiver (weak routes and hands), not a hidden weapon, and projects him as a big, strong interior runner more in the Antonio Gibson mold who won't see much third-down work. He expects decent Day 3 draft capital (round 4-5) and buzz purely from the 'former WR' pedigree, without the tape to back it up. For comparison, Pertichese was previously high on fellow WR-convert Tyrone Tracy, who has had a solid NFL career.
- 2026-05-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-21]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon's Reception Perception profile on the Ravens' fifth-round pick opens by comparing Randall to Le'Veon Bell -- a comp he stressed he almost never makes -- citing shared size (Randall 6'3', 232 lbs vs. Bell's 6'2', 230 lbs), patience, instinct, and a power-plus-shifty (not explosively lateral) play style. Randall, a converted wide receiver, posted what Harmon called the best zone-running score in this class plus real strength running outside, and an 88.9% pass-block success rate. Harmon flagged that new Ravens OC Declan Doyle is expected to import Ben Johnson's Chicago zone-and-outside-heavy run scheme (Chicago was top-10 in zone rate and top-5 in outside rate in 2025), which projects as a strong scheme fit for Randall. With [[Derrick Henry]] entering his age-32 season off four straight 300-plus-touch years, Harmon said it's worth knowing Baltimore's backup plan now, and argued incumbent [[Justice Hill]] (himself aging, entering his age-29 season, and limited historically to a passing-down role) and other depth backs are unlikely to inherit the early-down banger role over Randall if Henry were to miss time.
- 2026-07-31 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-31]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris says he's admittedly skeptical of rookie Adam Randall and hasn't scouted him personally, but notes he's been publicly reported as 'the owner's pick' (owner Steve Bisciotti) -- information Harris says never should have been made public because of the unfair pressure it puts on the kid. He points out Baltimore let Keaton Mitchell go and has only [[Justice Hill]] behind a 32-year-old Derrick Henry, which is the actual opening Randall would need to exploit. Harris's business partner James Koh, who Harris notes has been doing running back-focused work and is not a tracked source on this show, reportedly 'loves' Randall. Harris says he has no strong take of his own but is watching for beat reports if Henry ever slows down or misses time.
- 2026-08-13 — According to James Koh (untracked co-host) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-13]]): Adam Randall is Koh's favorite handcuff this cycle -- he says he's 'overflowing and gushing' in praise of a player going outside the top-300 on ADP. Koh's case: Ravens defensive coordinator Jesse Minter praised Randall's physical running style in camp, comparing his contact tempo directly to [[Derrick Henry]]'s, quoted as saying Randall 'runs behind his pads' with a 'thud' tempo similar to Henry and has improved each time he's in pads. Koh notes Randall (listed 6'3', 230) is built like a smaller version of Henry, that owner Steve Bisciotti personally selected him in the draft, and that this is a brand-new coaching staff with no loyalty to incumbent backups [[Justice Hill]] or [[Rasheen Ali]] -- Koh expects Randall, not Hill, to be the clear backup behind Henry. Matt Harmon agreed with the framing.
