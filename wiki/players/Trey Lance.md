# Trey Lance

> ⚠️ Take below is from April 2024. Team/role are as of that date and are
> almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-04-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-15]]: unchanged from his original scouting grade — one of the top two-to-three quarterbacks in his draft class and a player who could become a good NFL starter; says people calling him a bust are "overstating and over-dramatizing" a situation where he barely got an opportunity in San Francisco. Reads Dallas as a good landing spot, since he'll play in an offense that lets him create rather than forcing him to be a strict "unstructured grammarian" the way Kyle Shanahan's system demanded. Ties his outlook to [[CeeDee Lamb]]'s: if Lamb stays in Dallas long-term, Lance has a lead receiver to grow with. Separately reasons the Cowboys wouldn't have let Dak Prescott's contract situation reach a $55M dead-cap crossroads without believing they already have a plan at quarterback in hand.
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]) [Dynasty]: 'feel it' on Lance eventually developing into a viable starter, mostly as an argument about patience — Waldman says almost everyone in the world is far too impatient with young quarterbacks. He grants the negatives: Dallas has emphatically signaled he is a backup, he has made bad throws again, and his confidence has been shaken. But he was 19 when he came out, offenses are more sophisticated now, and the skills are there. Waldman's thought experiment: if [[Aaron Rodgers]] were six years younger and [[Jordan Love]] had gotten a couple of bad starts, looked poor in preseason and then been shipped elsewhere to sit behind another starter, the same people would call him the biggest bust ever. Practical advice: you do not need to roster him, because nobody else values him — you can pick him up and drop him repeatedly until it is time. He calls Lance the most talented of the quarterbacks in this segment.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): Waldman thinks Lance definitely gets a shot in Dallas — he does not see how they avoid it — and expects it to be 'the Trey Lance show' for at least the final four to six weeks after they went through Cooper Rush early. He expects some moments of success, but not enough to close the door on Lance being a colossal bust; enough, though, to generate interest from teams outside Dallas, or for Jerry Jones to anoint him the number two next year. Waldman is otherwise not feeling the Cowboys the rest of the way and does not expect a strong finish.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): Dallas should play Lance in a season that is already sunk, because both outcomes are useful — either you learn he is not your backup of the future and move on, or he develops and you have a bridge quarterback someone else will pay for. Angelo sees nothing to gain from Cooper Rush, whom he calls a worse version of Mason Rudolph and never a real NFL starter. Lance has starting-caliber traits, which is why a good organization drafted him high, but Angelo says the traits did not matter because he lacked the cognitive skill set and timing to make plays consistently — the same time-and-timing framework the hosts apply to [[Caleb Williams]] and [[C.J. Stroud]]. He calls [[Brock Purdy]] vastly better at this stage and notes Lance's situation in Dallas is not a strong developmental one, though learning behind [[Dak Prescott]] has value.
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman still has hope for Lance, noting he ran a West Coast scheme at North Dakota State and read it well, and that the gap between that and NFL coverage disguises was always the leap. He thinks Lance was buried in San Francisco — rumors that half the staff wanted Mac Jones, Kyle Shanahan publicly pushing [[Brock Purdy]] — and that a young, inexperienced player who hears his own building doesn't want him loses confidence, then got shipped to a Dallas 'clown show'. The physical skills, base accuracy and field-reading are there when he's comfortable, so Waldman frames him as a possible [[Geno Smith]] or [[Sam Darnold]] style reclamation, but in fantasy terms only 'end-of-the-bench dynasty hope in large leagues' worth monitoring for a starting opportunity. Guest Dave Kluge (Footballguys, not tracked) 'really wants to feel it', notes Lance is only 25 and younger than [[Tyler Shough]], liked that he played within structure in the Hall of Fame game including a touch touchdown to [[Will Dissly]], but comps him to Joshua Dobbs — physical tools, spot starts, roster churn — rather than a top-five fantasy dual threat.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-18]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman is 'starting to feel' that Lance gets another chance to start in the NFL at some point, and said Lance may be waiting at the bottom of some of his dynasty rosters. His preseason performances have shown a reasonably sharp player, and crucially there is no pressure on him to start — the market has forgotten him to the point of asking only whether he can make a roster, and Waldman thinks he makes the Chargers. The setup is the argument: a former quarterback as head coach, a very good quarterback ahead of him, and for the first time an organization that is not an absolute clown show, giving him a genuine watch-and-learn runway.
