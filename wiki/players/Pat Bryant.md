# Pat Bryant

## Expert Takes

- 2025-04-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-29]]): Bryant to Denver only has a three-game Reception Perception mini sample, so the numbers are directional rather than settled — 50% success rate vs man, 77% vs zone. Harmon argues the route chart explains the depressed overall marks: he ran nine routes on 18.6% of snaps with an atrocious 27.8% success rate, unsurprising at 6-2, 204 with a 4.6 forty, and James Koh (not tracked) flags a 55% slant success rate on another 19% route participation as one of the worst numbers he has seen — the two routes he ran most are the two he was worst at. Where he wins is the dig (16.5% participation, 75% success), curls and comebacks, plus tracking and hands. After the catch he was in space on 78.6% of catches, down on first contact on only 36.4% and broke multiple tackles on 27.3%. Harmon's comp is [[Tim Patrick]] — a bigger receiver who is not an elite separator but wins over the middle, breaks tackles and moves the chains — and he calls Bryant a natural Sean Payton receiver, probably not an X but fine off the line, similar to what Denver got from [[Devaughn Vele]]. Koh suspects a full sample would look better than the mini sample suggests.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: a clear rookie-draft value — he grabbed Bryant in the fourth round of one draft and would be comfortable taking him at the back end of the second (around 208 in his ADP terms). The more he watched after Sean Payton drafted him higher than expected, the more he liked him: super physical, plays 'with a whistle', a good blocker and a better athlete at the catch point than his reputation. The opportunity case is that Denver's receiving room empties out after 2025 — [[Courtland Sutton]] is 30 and in a contract year, and Angelo is not scared by [[Marvin Mims Jr.]], [[Devaughn Vele]] or [[Troy Franklin]], with [[Evan Engram]] a complementary piece at best. Payton's first comparison for Bryant was [[Michael Thomas]] — not at that level, but Angelo will pay a third-round price for a receiver with that coach's confidence, and sees him possibly starting in three-receiver sets from day one with 125-plus target upside in 2026.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Harmon is curious how Denver actually uses Bryant, noting his post-draft breakdown likened him to [[Tim Patrick]] as a big slot, while Sean Payton has floated a [[Michael Thomas]] play-style comparison and handed Bryant number 13. Harmon pushes back on the comp's implication: Thomas is misremembered as a Marques Colston-style big slot when he was almost exclusively an X for New Orleans who ran a huge volume of slants because that was what a late-career Drew Brees could throw. The open question is whether Bryant plays big slot immediately or whether that is [[Devaughn Vele]]'s job in 2025 with Bryant becoming [[Courtland Sutton]]'s heir apparent in 2026 — and Harmon argues signing [[Keenan Allen]] would progress-block one or both.
- 2025-06-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-27]]): Bryant's RSP elevator pitch is that he sets up and executes breaks well — the hardest part of route running — with good acceleration but no top-end speed, making him a Cooper Kupp-ish player who is quick into routes but cannot pull away, so most of his work lives within 30 yards of the line and profiles better against off-man and zone. He uncovers late, has late hands and is undistracted in tight coverage, though he does 'clap attack' some targets in the mold of [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] or [[Quentin Johnston]], if less exaggerated and easier to fix. Absent the Denver landing spot Waldman graded him a third or fourth receiver/playmaker. He explicitly discounts the Sean Payton Michael Thomas comp, noting Payton speaks in Saints archetypes — he talked up [[RJ Harvey]] then signed [[J.K. Dobbins]], and comped [[Blake Watson]] to [[Alvin Kamara]] — and for every Michael Thomas there was a Robert Meachem or Devery Henderson. Projects roughly 50 targets, 35 catches, ~400 yards and a couple of scores, with the upside case being 95-100 catches for 900-1,000 yards if he simply seizes the job.
- 2025-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-25]]): Harmon picks the third-round rookie, not [[Marvin Mims Jr.]], to emerge as Denver's number two behind [[Courtland Sutton]] — while conceding some of his charting numbers are literally the worst he has ever seen from a prospect. He runs a 4.6, is a 50 percent success rate player versus man, and his nine-route success rate is 27.8 percent, both on routes he ran often (slant 55 percent on 19 percent participation, which Harmon calls the worst he has seen). The bull case is deployment: Harmon expects him used as a power slot on digs and curls, where he charts well (75 percent on digs, good curls, 77 percent versus zone), with good hands and surprising after-catch work — in space on 78.6 percent of sampled catches, down on first contact just 36.4 percent, multiple broken tackles on 27.3 percent. Comp: [[Tim Patrick]]. He scored 17 touchdowns over two years at Illinois. Koh (not tracked) says he looked better in real life than the numbers suggest and trusts Sean Payton's rotation to put him in the right spots.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-29]]): trading [[Devaughn Vele]] to New Orleans gives Bryant an opportunity to work into Denver's power slot rotation. Harmon's charting shows big slot indicators — an outrageously low nine-route (go) success rate, unsurprising off a 4.6 forty, so no deep separation, but he was really good in the intermediate area and specifically working over the middle of the field, which fits the Sean Payton big-slot archetype. Harmon posted the route graphics for Bryant, [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] and [[Troy Franklin]] side by side and called them three completely different players — Mims a pure vertical and screen player, Bryant intermediate, Franklin unable to work the middle because he is skinny and linear but able to win on quick slants and deep outbreakers. Caveat: unclear how many targets are available.
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-10-09]]): Bryant hit a season-high 54% snap rate with zero targets, so there is no production breakthrough, but Harmon's vision of him as Denver's big power slot is still live — 47.4% of his snaps came in the slot and he had nice run-game blocks. He described Denver's structure as [[Courtland Sutton]] the true X with everyone else rotating between slot and flanker: [[Troy Franklin]] the small speed slot at 49%, Bryant the bigger power slot at 47%, [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] a gadget player. He allowed it may not happen this year but believes that is how the team views him, and flagged pairing him with [[Evan Engram]] — who Harmon says is not a real tight end — as something to watch.
- 2025-10-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-30]]): while working on his rookie report, Harmon has 'really liked' what he's seen from Bryant in isolation, viewing him as a good answer for Denver both against zone coverage and as a big-slot body -- Bryant leads the Broncos WR room with 53.8%+ slot-adjacent usage context alongside Troy Franklin. Harmon wants a 'big slot/speed slot tandem' of Bryant and Franklin to keep stepping up for Sean Payton's offense.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-13]]): Koh notes Bryant has settled almost exclusively into the slot — 79% inside alignment in Week 10 — which is what pushed [[Troy Franklin]] outside for 76% of his snaps that week after being a 50/50 inside-outside player at 49% outside on the season. Bryant's overall snap share has not moved much and he remains in roughly a 20% role, but Koh reads intention in Denver's effort to get him on the field despite him having played mostly outside in college. Harmon likes the fit better than Franklin's, saying he would prefer to see Bryant in the slot because he brings physicality as a big slot, an element this offense could really benefit from right now.
- 2025-12-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris flagged Bryant as suddenly the Broncos' number-two receiver, but is wary given how often the rug has been pulled out from under that receiving room -- he expects the same eventually happens to Bryant too.
- 2025-12-09 — According to Chris Harris and Jeff Bell (Football Guys, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: The rookie has taken over Denver's early-down receiver role, out-snapping Troy Franklin 28 routes to 15 in two-WR sets, and Harris flatly recommends dropping Franklin to add him. Bell floats a scheme explanation -- with J.K. Dobbins injured and RJ Harvey lacking Dobbins's vision/feel, Denver may be leaning into more run-heavy, WR-heavy personnel rather than Bryant's play simply forcing the change -- while Harris counters it could just as easily be a rookie needing time before the staff now views him as part of the future. Both agree the reason is genuinely unclear. Bell ranks Bryant his #4 Week 15 waiver add at 3% rostered.
- 2025-12-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-12-11]]) [Dynasty]: Bryant went 4-for-4 for 32 yards and, per Harmon's weekly Yahoo snap-share report, is the story of Denver's receiver room -- ascending week over week as [[Troy Franklin]] fades, and now running 65-70% of his snaps as an inside receiver. Since Denver's Week 12 bye Bryant has played 71% of dropbacks to Franklin's 52.7%, posting 7 catches, 5 first downs, and roughly 4.3-4.8 yards after catch per catch. Harmon frames Bryant as the 'downfield power slot' the Sean Payton offense specifically needs alongside X-only [[Courtland Sutton]] -- a zone-beating second option who moves the chains, unlike speed-slot Franklin. Harmon isn't calling Bryant a star, but sees him as a 'nice reliable player' important to Denver's stretch run.
- 2025-12-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Dave Kluge of FootballGuys.com (untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-16]]): the hosts debated a contingent Broncos WR2 battle -- [[Pat Bryant]] (3% rostered) reportedly stole the starting role from [[Troy Franklin]] before an injury, after which Franklin reclaimed the job and looked good. Both said if Bryant returns healthy he probably regains the role, but called Sean Payton's receiver usage impossible to predict reliably (targets could also flow to Evan Engram or Marvin Mims). Kluge ranked Bryant his No. 4 add specifically because Franklin (51% rostered) exceeds the eligibility threshold; Harris leaned toward preferring Bryant of the two if both are healthy but stressed low confidence in either.
- 2025-12-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-23]]): Bryant, returning from injury, was crushed on a meaningless throw with 30 seconds left and had to be carted off, likely ruling him out for week 16... err, week 17. Harris had been about to discuss whether Bryant's return was bad news for [[Troy Franklin]]'s target share, but the injury flips that question -- Franklin's role could expand again if Bryant is out, contingent on how much Denver airs it out with a big lead expected.
- 2025-12-23 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-12-23]]): Bryant suffered a concussion on a big hit at the end of Denver's loss to the Chargers -- the same play as Bo Nix's criticized late 'hospital ball' -- and may miss the rest of the regular season; Harmon called him a nice player whose absence pushes more of the passing game workload back onto Troy Franklin.
- 2026-01-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-27]]): Harmon called Bryant 'a nice player,' but noted that every time he seems to build momentum he gets hurt, an injury pattern that has repeatedly cut off his opportunities in Denver's receiver room this season.
- 2026-03-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-10]]): Harmon said he wishes [[Pat Bryant]] could have stayed healthy in 2025 -- concussions and hamstring issues repeatedly interrupted stretches (including a playoff game where he had 3 catches for 32 yards before getting hurt) right when the Broncos were clearly trying to feature him. Harmon likes Bryant as a power-slot type and called him a good zone-beater both in his pre-draft prospect profile and in early-career Reception Perception rookie charting -- a trait complementary Broncos receivers Troy Franklin and Marvin Mims lack -- and said if the team believes in him and he can stay on the field, he could take on a much larger role.
- 2026-04-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: Harris noted Bryant, a 2025 third-round pick who flashed speed and tantalizing moments as a rookie, is one of two young Broncos pieces (with [[Troy Franklin]]) that analysts liked heading into 2026 -- but with [[Jaylen Waddle]] now traded to Denver, Harris said both are 'certainly rendered useless for fantasy, at least from the perspective of right now' even though he doesn't expect either to disappear from the offense entirely.
- 2026-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-28]]) charting Bryant's rookie season: overall numbers were average to slightly below -- 65% success rate versus man, 77% versus zone, 50% versus press, just under the 70/80 thresholds Harmon uses as strong-grade benchmarks. His alignment split 39% outside, 58.4% slot, 2.3% backfield, reinforcing a 'power slot' profile -- a strong blocker who wins underneath and versus zone but ran a nine route on only 5.8% of routes and posted a rough 45.5% success rate on the ones he did run, meaning little straight-line vertical separation. He thrived on contested targets (caught 70% of 26 sampled contested looks). Harmon's season-comp tool flags former Bronco Tim Patrick as his top comp. Verdict: a solid complementary WR3-type role player behind [[Courtland Sutton]] and [[Jaylen Waddle]] in Denver, not a true No. 2.
- 2026-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-23]]): Harmon projects Pat Bryant as Denver's likely third receiver and de facto big slot, noting his 2025 rookie charting (58% snaps in the slot) actually skewed more on-ball than off-ball, consistent with his prospect profile and rugged blocking, which could let the Broncos play more four-receiver, spread sets with Bryant functioning almost like a tight end alongside Marvin Mims and Troy Franklin while [[Jalen Waddle]] moves around as the Z.
- 2026-07-24 — According to Chris Harris and Andy Behrens (untracked co-host) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris references last year's since-deflated hype comparing Bryant to Michael Thomas (attributed to Sean Payton) and says the reported shift to more four-wide personnel in Denver doesn't actually rescue Bryant's outlook -- both hosts conclude the Broncos aren't an offense worth chasing training-camp clicks on absent an injury elsewhere on the depth chart.
- 2026-08-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-07]]): Bryant is getting more positive camp buzz than [[Troy Franklin]] in their Broncos receiver-room battle as of August 7th -- a matchup Harris had flagged in advance as worth watching. He says he's leaning toward writing Bryant a full Almanac profile and possibly swapping their ranks, but hasn't pulled the trigger yet.
- 2026-08-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-08-18]]): both Harmon and guest Jeff Bell (not tracked) see Bryant as a potential power-slot/X answer for Denver whose physicality and run-blocking could help solve last year's run-game issues, sliding into a clean three-WR rotation alongside [[Courtland Sutton]] and [[Jalen Waddle]] rather than displacing either -- with Troy Franklin fading and Marvin Mims Jr. possibly traded out of the picture as a result.
