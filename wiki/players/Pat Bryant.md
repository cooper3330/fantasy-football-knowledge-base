# Pat Bryant

## Expert Takes

- 2025-04-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-29]]): Bryant to Denver only has a three-game Reception Perception mini sample, so the numbers are directional rather than settled — 50% success rate vs man, 77% vs zone. Harmon argues the route chart explains the depressed overall marks: he ran nine routes on 18.6% of snaps with an atrocious 27.8% success rate, unsurprising at 6-2, 204 with a 4.6 forty, and James Koh (not tracked) flags a 55% slant success rate on another 19% route participation as one of the worst numbers he has seen — the two routes he ran most are the two he was worst at. Where he wins is the dig (16.5% participation, 75% success), curls and comebacks, plus tracking and hands. After the catch he was in space on 78.6% of catches, down on first contact on only 36.4% and broke multiple tackles on 27.3%. Harmon's comp is [[Tim Patrick]] — a bigger receiver who is not an elite separator but wins over the middle, breaks tackles and moves the chains — and he calls Bryant a natural Sean Payton receiver, probably not an X but fine off the line, similar to what Denver got from [[Devaughn Vele]]. Koh suspects a full sample would look better than the mini sample suggests.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: a clear rookie-draft value — he grabbed Bryant in the fourth round of one draft and would be comfortable taking him at the back end of the second (around 208 in his ADP terms). The more he watched after Sean Payton drafted him higher than expected, the more he liked him: super physical, plays 'with a whistle', a good blocker and a better athlete at the catch point than his reputation. The opportunity case is that Denver's receiving room empties out after 2025 — [[Courtland Sutton]] is 30 and in a contract year, and Angelo is not scared by [[Marvin Mims Jr.]], [[Devaughn Vele]] or [[Troy Franklin]], with [[Evan Engram]] a complementary piece at best. Payton's first comparison for Bryant was [[Michael Thomas]] — not at that level, but Angelo will pay a third-round price for a receiver with that coach's confidence, and sees him possibly starting in three-receiver sets from day one with 125-plus target upside in 2026.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Harmon is curious how Denver actually uses Bryant, noting his post-draft breakdown likened him to [[Tim Patrick]] as a big slot, while Sean Payton has floated a [[Michael Thomas]] play-style comparison and handed Bryant number 13. Harmon pushes back on the comp's implication: Thomas is misremembered as a Marques Colston-style big slot when he was almost exclusively an X for New Orleans who ran a huge volume of slants because that was what a late-career Drew Brees could throw. The open question is whether Bryant plays big slot immediately or whether that is [[Devaughn Vele]]'s job in 2025 with Bryant becoming [[Courtland Sutton]]'s heir apparent in 2026 — and Harmon argues signing [[Keenan Allen]] would progress-block one or both.
- 2025-06-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-27]]): Bryant's RSP elevator pitch is that he sets up and executes breaks well — the hardest part of route running — with good acceleration but no top-end speed, making him a Cooper Kupp-ish player who is quick into routes but cannot pull away, so most of his work lives within 30 yards of the line and profiles better against off-man and zone. He uncovers late, has late hands and is undistracted in tight coverage, though he does 'clap attack' some targets in the mold of [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] or [[Quentin Johnston]], if less exaggerated and easier to fix. Absent the Denver landing spot Waldman graded him a third or fourth receiver/playmaker. He explicitly discounts the Sean Payton Michael Thomas comp, noting Payton speaks in Saints archetypes — he talked up [[RJ Harvey]] then signed [[J.K. Dobbins]], and comped [[Blake Watson]] to [[Alvin Kamara]] — and for every Michael Thomas there was a Robert Meachem or Devery Henderson. Projects roughly 50 targets, 35 catches, ~400 yards and a couple of scores, with the upside case being 95-100 catches for 900-1,000 yards if he simply seizes the job.
- 2025-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-25]]): Harmon picks the third-round rookie, not [[Marvin Mims Jr.]], to emerge as Denver's number two behind [[Courtland Sutton]] — while conceding some of his charting numbers are literally the worst he has ever seen from a prospect. He runs a 4.6, is a 50 percent success rate player versus man, and his nine-route success rate is 27.8 percent, both on routes he ran often (slant 55 percent on 19 percent participation, which Harmon calls the worst he has seen). The bull case is deployment: Harmon expects him used as a power slot on digs and curls, where he charts well (75 percent on digs, good curls, 77 percent versus zone), with good hands and surprising after-catch work — in space on 78.6 percent of sampled catches, down on first contact just 36.4 percent, multiple broken tackles on 27.3 percent. Comp: [[Tim Patrick]]. He scored 17 touchdowns over two years at Illinois. Koh (not tracked) says he looked better in real life than the numbers suggest and trusts Sean Payton's rotation to put him in the right spots.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-29]]): trading [[Devaughn Vele]] to New Orleans gives Bryant an opportunity to work into Denver's power slot rotation. Harmon's charting shows big slot indicators — an outrageously low nine-route (go) success rate, unsurprising off a 4.6 forty, so no deep separation, but he was really good in the intermediate area and specifically working over the middle of the field, which fits the Sean Payton big-slot archetype. Harmon posted the route graphics for Bryant, [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] and [[Troy Franklin]] side by side and called them three completely different players — Mims a pure vertical and screen player, Bryant intermediate, Franklin unable to work the middle because he is skinny and linear but able to win on quick slants and deep outbreakers. Caveat: unclear how many targets are available.
