# Chris Rodriguez Jr.

## Expert Takes

- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): there is legitimate short-term value — early in the season or on an injury to anyone in Washington's trio he can be a low-end RB2 or flex — but the long-term picture with everyone healthy is 'the [[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]] show' as the lead back, [[Austin Ekeler]] used situationally, and Rodriguez limited to blocking, some short yardage split with Croskey-Merritt, and special teams. Waldman's comp: if Spencer Ware were a band, Rodriguez would be the weekend cover band your rib joint hires once or twice a week — competent, gets it done, but not worth arena money. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) accepted the framing, adding that it is the Croskey-Merritt show until it is not, and when it is not, it becomes the Rodriguez show.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Rodriguez, 7% rostered, is Harris's number two waiver name and was on guest BRANDON FUNSTON's short list without quite making it. Harris says he wrote this in an Almanac update: there were stretches last year, mostly in blowouts either way, where Rodriguez would get a couple of carries and look okay, even interesting. He wants to see how much Rodriguez works in against the Giants, and frames the open question as whether the Washington backfield picture is about [[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]], about [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] being a pill over his contract, or about the team genuinely seeing a three-headed monster. He is not necessarily adding him, but wants him on the list.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Going Deep Rookie Receivers and RB Depth Charts)]]): Angelo expects Rodriguez to get the first crack at short yardage and low-red-zone work in Washington behind [[Austin Ekeler]], having earned it with an efficient preseason and an efficient finish to his rookie year — 'they're going to want to give him his pie.' Waldman comped him to a 'middle-class Spencer Ware' without the special skills that could elevate Ware, and framed the edge as executional: he does the offense the way Cliff Kingsbury wants it done, and most offensive coaches will take reliable execution over higher-end athletic ability as a tiebreaker. Waldman still expects [[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]] to be able to leapfrog him if [[Austin Ekeler]] goes down.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): with [[Austin Ekeler]] out for the year on a torn Achilles, Rodriguez started and took the first two full series — four straight carries to open the game before [[Marcus Mariota]]'s sneak touchdown — finishing with 21 snaps, level with [[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]] and ahead of Jeremy McNichols's 14. Harris made him his number one Week 4 waiver add at five percent rostered: 'if there's a dude who I can imagine developing into a lead on his team, which might not be such a bad team, it would be Rodriguez,' while conceding he would not want to start any back on this week's list. Guest SCOTT FISH (Fantasy Cares, NOT tracked) had him fourth for the same reasons — the between-the-tackles guy who knows the offense, got the goal line, more snaps, touches and carries — but 'I don't love it,' since Croskey-Merritt is going to be annoying.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): Washington turned back to Rodriguez late in the first half after [[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]] got nowhere against Seattle, and he was also used to run out the clock late and scored what Harris calls 'an absolute basura touchdown'. Harris says he has argued multiple times on the show that Rodriguez should be part of the mix on the small-back/big-back split, while conceding 'they don't ask me' — and that with [[Jayden Daniels]] out the whole backfield is hard to trust week to week.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): Rodriguez was the actual starter against Detroit, rewarded after being the only injection of energy in the Seattle blowout - he took the first two carries and the first-half snap count while the game was competitive went 17 Rodriguez, 6 [[Jeremy McNichols]], 4 [[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]], and he scored easily from the one after [[Deebo Samuel]] was stuffed there. He appears to have hurt his shoulder covering a punt late in the third quarter. Guest **Brandon Funston** (The Athletic, not tracked) made him his number four Week 11 waiver add at 6 percent rostered pending the shoulder news, with the Commanders playing Miami in Spain. Harris will not tell you who to use in that backfield - 'someone in Washington's backfield could produce, I don't currently have the means to tell you who' - and would be wise not to chase.
- 2025-11-17 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-17 (Week 11 Game Film Review)]]): Rodriguez started again and played the most snaps of Washington's running backs in the Madrid overtime loss to Miami, and Harris said 'that's the right call' -- Rodriguez looked like the best, most direct and physical back Washington has, ahead of Jacory Croskey-Merritt, whose first carry didn't come until his own 5-yard line with 5 minutes left in the first half (with more work in the second half). Harris called Rodriguez the only Commanders back worth using in an emergency right now, though he was also stopped short on multiple goal-line looks late in regulation.
- 2025-11-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: ranked as Harris's #2 (and Scott Fish's #1) Week 13 waiver add at 17% rostered, citing favorable end-of-season matchups and an offense Harris expects to be more efficient once Jayden Daniels returns. Both hosts describe Rodriguez as not a super dynamic player -- Harris calls him a 'David Montgomery phylum' runner who doesn't make many people miss but is tough and reliable near the goal line, having scored from the one in Europe against Miami. Both see him flip-flopping the lead role with Jacory Croskey-Merritt, whom neither is impressed by -- Harris says 'I don't know that I think he's all that good...he tries to bounce when he doesn't need to,' speculating Washington may be intentionally scaling back Croskey-Merritt's workload in a busted season.
- 2025-12-01 — According to Andy Behrens (untracked, Yacht Club/The Deep Shot co-host, [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-01]]): Chris Rodriguez Jr. 'looked awesome' against Denver -- 'can't arm-tackle the guy' -- a no-nonsense, no-frills 225-pound runner who Behrens said gets every bit out of his touches and is great near the goal line. He said Rodriguez is a back you can 'happily flex sometimes,' and he wishes, not via injury, that Washington's crowded backfield (also featuring Jeremy McNichols and a since-diminished Jacory Croskey-Merritt) would settle to two backs instead of three, since he thinks McNichols also looks good. Harris separately said watching Rodriguez made him think Sean Payton must covet a back like him for New Orleans' offense.
- 2025-12-02 — According to guest John Daigle of Establish the Run (not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Rodriguez was Daigle's #1 waiver pickup for week 14 (Harris's too), calling him immediately usable -- a rarity this time of year -- against a Vikings front seven that shut down Sam Darnold through the air (credit to Brian Flores's blitz-heavy scheme) but still leaked rushing yardage to [[Kenneth Walker III]] and [[Zach Charbonnet]]. Over the two games around Washington's bye, Rodriguez has out-carried [[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]] 26-13, and Daigle said he's startable as a top-24 back even in 10-team leagues. Harris agreed, comping Rodriguez to a lesser version of David Montgomery -- a big, assignment-sound back who runs through contact and has earned Washington's trust, adding that the JCM 'dancing' phase got old with the staff quickly.
- 2025-12-16 — According to guest Dave Kluge of FootballGuys.com (untracked), Chris Harris concurring ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-16]]): Kluge's No. 1 waiver add was [[Chris Rodriguez Jr.]] (only 33% rostered on Yahoo, despite already well-rostered on Sleeper), calling him Washington's clear RB1 -- a thumper who doesn't take negative-yardage losses and mitigates contact well, exactly the 'old-school Dan Quinn football' the coaching staff wants. He was a game-time decision the prior week, which Kluge read as a good sign he'll play Week 16 (tough matchup vs. Philadelphia), but flagged Week 17 vs. Dallas as a clear plug-and-play start, projecting him inside the top 24 backs. Harris, who ranked Rodriguez No. 6 on his own list, agreed that if active he's clearly Washington's top back but noted uncertainty about his health/availability as the reason he ranked him lower than Kluge did.
- 2025-12-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-23]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris lists Rodriguez (29% rostered) among his championship-week waiver adds mainly because he's Washington's clear starter and will play a lot of snaps, even with backup quarterback Brady Cook (Giants) back rather than a stronger passing attack; he narrowly edged out Adonai Mitchell for a roster spot on Harris's list, though Harris frames it as a fairly thin add.
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Dave Kluge (Footballguys.com, untracked guest) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-12]]) [Dynasty]: Rodriguez becomes part of the Jacksonville backfield replacing [[Travis Etienne Jr.]], though as a very different, bruising David Montgomery-mold runner rather than a like-for-like swap. Kluge flags a real history: new OC Liam Cohen's first year calling plays was at Kentucky, where Rodriguez was a nearly-1,500-scrimmage-yard workhorse, and Cohen has repeatedly praised him on record as a hard-nosed back who 'gets you four yards even if the blocking isn't there.' Both see this as a genuine threat to [[Bhayshul Tuten]]'s workout, comparing the muddled situation to Arizona's backfield -- with [[LeQuint Allen]] also in the mix for passing-down work -- and conclude it's too unclear to plant a flag on any one back; the plan is to wait for ADP and take the cheapest option.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): Rodriguez is likely to see the low red-zone/goal-line touches in Jacksonville's backfield, based on the role he played for Liam Coen at Kentucky. Waldman isn't certain that exact dynamic repeats in Jacksonville, but leans toward Rodriguez as 'the one to own' for early-down/goal-line work over [[Bhayshul Tuten]], with [[LeQuint Allen]] taking the clear third-down role.
- 2026-04-09 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: names Chris Rodriguez Jr. a 'don't forget' back after Jacksonville signed him, citing his fit with new OC Liam Cohen, who Angelo says has publicly stated his top evaluation criteria for backs are yards-after-contact and cognitive processing per attempt -- Cohen worked with Rodriguez at Kentucky and had success with him, where Rodriguez racked up almost 1,400 yards under him, including 100-plus yards per game before a senior-year injury. Angelo expects [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] to keep the between-the-tackles, goal-line-agnostic role similar to how [[Bucky Irving]] is used in Tampa, while Rodriguez adds yards-after-contact, low-red-zone and short-yardage work -- projecting 10-plus TD upside and realistic RB3 fantasy value despite being 'near zero' as a receiver.
- 2026-06-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-06-08]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman projects Chris Rodriguez Jr. in a near-even rushing split with [[Bhayshul Tuten]] heading into camp -- similar touchdown and yardage shares -- but flagged that Rodriguez has been mysteriously absent from OTAs, calling it something to watch closely. He said he'll carry more Rodriguez shares in best ball given the price uncertainty, while noting Tuten is doing the offseason work (pass protection, ball security, between-the-tackles decisiveness) that could let him pull away from a straight timeshare.
- 2026-07-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Dave Kluge (footballguys.com, untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-10]]): Kluge is 'buying into' Rodriguez being far more involved than expected in Jacksonville's post-[[Travis Etienne Jr.]] backfield, citing a strong analytical profile (top-5 in broken tackle rate and success rate last year) plus tape showing contact balance, acceleration through contact, and keeping the offense on schedule, along with a prior relationship with OC Liam Cohen. Harris is cooler on him, saying he likes Rodriguez 'fine' but sees him as limited -- comparing him to '[[Tyler Allgeier]] if Tyler Allgeier had no long speed' -- and doubts the price gap to [[Bhayshul Tuten]] closes even if Rodriguez is the better all-around back. Both think LeQuint Allen taking clear passing-down work and goal-line speculation for Rodriguez are unreliable narratives without a unified beat 'drumbeat.'
- 2026-07-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman currently gives Rodriguez the slight edge over [[Bhayshul Tuten]] for Jacksonville's early-season lead role, describing him as a solid, known-commodity option ('things aren't going to be bad') even while acknowledging Tuten has the higher name recognition and possible ceiling if he takes the job outright. Waldman says he may re-evaluate Rodriguez's tape in the coming weeks and could revise this take.
- 2026-08-21 — According to Chris Harris ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Rodriguez is Harris's #5 flag player, a deep 'later-round guy' whose exact draft slot depends entirely on platform -- not even inside the top 168 default ranks on Yahoo/ESPN, more like round 11-12 elsewhere. Harris notes Liam Coen has a long history with Rodriguez from Kentucky and brought him in for a reason; Rodriguez was slow out of camp recovering from foot surgery but has been full-go for most of August. He's stashable insurance now that LeQuint Allen is out for a stretch with a fresh injury. Harris pushes back hard on people comparing Rodriguez to a forgotten afterthought like [[Brian Robinson Jr.]], arguing 'every team basically has other guys' and very few backs get 70%+ of the carries -- Rodriguez is a handpicked power/touchdown back likely to play a meaningful platoon role, worth stashing with no obligation to use him if it doesn't pan out.
