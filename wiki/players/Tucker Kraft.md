# Tucker Kraft

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): had Kraft in the same pre-draft tier as teammate [[Luke Musgrave]], 15 spots lower on his overall board — meaning Kraft was available at a discount relative to his grade. Calls him "a more rugged all-around player" with more remaining growth room as an intermediate/vertical route option than the more athletically-hyped Musgrave, and says if forced to bet on only one Packers rookie tight end at a discount, Kraft is the one he'd take.

- 2024-01-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-08]]) [Dynasty]: reframes the Kraft-vs-Musgrave question as "more of a price thing than anything else" — Kraft is a little more rugged, makes more plays in the middle of the field and the shallow areas, and may be a better blocker in some of Green Bay's scheme concepts, which Waldman summarizes as making Kraft "just a little more cost-efficient" than [[Luke Musgrave]].
- 2024-07-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]): he 'liked [Kraft] as a prospect' and Kraft showed up in the 2023 stretch run, but Waldman cautions that Kraft's late-season snap/route edge over Luke Musgrave was largely a product of Musgrave playing hurt rather than a real changing of the pecking order, and he expects Musgrave to be the lead receiving tight end again in 2024.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Going Deep Rookie Receivers and RB Depth Charts)]]): with [[Jayden Reed]] dealing with a Jones fracture and [[Christian Watson]] still rehabbing an ACL, Kraft becomes the second-leading receiver in the Green Bay offense and represents big fantasy value this year. [[Matt Waldman]] partly pushed back, noting talk that [[Luke Musgrave]] had a strong offseason and was ahead of Kraft two years ago and is more of a downfield threat, so Waldman wondered whether Matt LaFleur and [[Jordan Love]] might target Musgrave more in a moment of desperation — while stopping short of predicting Musgrave jumps him. Both agreed the post-Reed Packers passing game is chaotic and week-to-week unpredictable behind [[Matthew Golden]].
- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Both Harris and guest Dave Kluge (Football Guys, not a tracked expert) treat Kraft as the way to play a Green Bay passing game that spreads the ball too thin — Kluge says it would not shock him if [[Jayden Reed]], [[Matthew Golden]], [[Romeo Doubs]] and Kraft all finished with similar output, so the one with positional scarcity is the one to own. Kluge liked him in the preseason but saw his 2024 value as catch-and-rumble work in the flat; he saw more genuine downfield targets in Week 1, and with disappointing weeks from [[Mark Andrews]] and [[T.J. Hockenson]] he moved Kraft above both. Harris has him TE7, behind [[Dallas Goedert]] at TE6, but says Goedert may not stay there. Both say if Kraft is your Thursday-nighter, just start him.
- 2025-09-12 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-12]]): Kraft is very clearly the Packers' TE1 and there is absolutely no reason not to ride him for now. Backed up near their own goal line he blocked and then ran a delayed screen for about 15 yards to get Green Bay out of trouble, then in a three-tight-end set went uncovered up the right seam for 57 yards, and later caught a wide-open red zone touchdown off a [[Josh Jacobs]] play-action fake. Harris's caveat: Kraft is not the athlete [[George Kittle]] is, is not a great route runner and is not explosive before the catch, though he has good speed after it and, like Kittle, is a monster blocker who plays the actual position. A guy scoring in two straight weeks at a bad fantasy position is enough — Harris says he can be your Tonyan for right now, while warning frustrating weeks are inevitable at tight end and that [[Luke Musgrave]] also caught a couple of passes. He deliberately removed [[Dallas Goedert]] from his tight end ranks before kickoff to push people onto Kraft.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-15]]): after watching him in depth, Waldman pushed back on the Pro Bowl hype — he sees Kraft as a good mid-tier TE1 whose production is more a function of the offense than of elite ability, explicitly not an elite tight end in the sense of being a man-beater. What is ridiculous, he said, is how much open space Green Bay's scheme creates for him. He used Kraft as the benchmark in arguing that [[Pat Freiermuth]] could be schemed open the same way in Pittsburgh but is not, and said [[Luke Musgrave]] could do about 80% of what Kraft does with more deep speed but less ruggedness after the catch. Even with [[Jayden Reed]] out, Waldman expects it to stay mostly the Kraft show among Green Bay tight ends.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Going Deep Listener Q&A)]]): Angelo calls Kraft the probable number one option in the Green Bay passing game right now, with [[Romeo Doubs]] not really that good and [[Dontayvion Wicks]] hot and cold as a pure receiver of the football. Waldman likes Kraft as a player and notes Green Bay is winning on out routes and schemed seam routes to him, but groups him with [[Tyler Warren]] as a schemed first-read tight end rather than a [[George Kittle]]-style matchup player who can be sent at tight man coverage and trusted to win any route. Waldman's stated caution is that fantasy drafters put both in a tier they have not earned; Chad Reuter's case for both rests on complete-tight-end work at the line.
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-13]]): 'feeling' that Kraft gets special treatment and that this is exactly what a fantasy manager wants. The Packers know what to do with him — get him into open space with the ball — and they do it well. Waldman detailed the touchdown design: an insert block where a receiver motions toward a gap at the line, forcing the corner to slide inside and leaving the space outside wide open, run frequently on run plays and here off play action with [[Savion Williams]], with Kraft delay-releasing off the block into wide-open grass. He credited real skill too — a diving low catch on a seam route, and finishing a touchdown by running through a defender with momentum. His caveat, wearing his NFL hat rather than his fantasy hat, is that he would call bullshit on any analyst claiming Kraft is on [[George Kittle]]'s level as a route runner. He now considers Kraft a consistent weekly start alongside [[Romeo Doubs]], contra his old whack-a-mole read of the Packers' pass catchers, and noted [[Tyler Warren]] gets similar scheme treatment.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): news broke during the episode that Kraft's knee injury is a torn ACL, ending his season. Waldman noted that Kraft and [[Tyler Warren]] have led the league in the kind of free, uncovered flat catches where no defender is nearby, and that Kraft's distinguishing trait is running over whoever is there — physicality after the catch that [[Luke Musgrave]] does not replicate.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-04]]): Kraft tore his ACL run blocking in the loss to Carolina — landed on sideways by his own lineman who had been shoved — and is done for the year, which Harris calls brutal and the big one from that game. Harris's evaluation of what made Kraft work: he has never bought that Kraft is an ungodly menace of an athlete relative to his peers, putting [[Trey McBride]] on a different athletic tier, but Kraft is a very good athlete whose secret sauce is blocking well enough to never leave the field — a throwback, Kittle-style player in that regard. Jeff Erickson of RotoWire (not a tracked expert) agreed, adding Kraft is not otherworldly but is good, and is a better open-field runner than his replacement.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06 (Players We Think We Know)]]) [Dynasty]: Kraft is Waldman's reference point for the schemed-open tight end — a good player whose production will confuse people because coordinators get him the ball with nobody within ten yards, he builds speed and does his damage, and he wins on seam shots. Waldman relayed that JJ Watt's first pre-broadcast question to Kraft was what it feels like to catch a ball with no defender within twenty yards on a regular basis. He believes both Kraft and [[Tyler Warren]] are better than that framing implies and understandably bristle at it, but that neither runs the timing and man-beating route tree that separates the elite tight ends. Waldman rates [[Trey McBride]] slightly above Kraft.
