# Tyreek Hill

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-01]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: ranks Hill third overall for 2024 among a three-way debate with [[Christian McCaffrey]] and [[CeeDee Lamb]], though he stresses "there's no wrong answer" among the three.
- 2024-01-01 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode: would rank Hill above Lamb (second overall behind McCaffrey) despite being the oldest of the three — calls him "an old-school wide receiver one" who plays all three receiver alignments at an elite level, does "the dirty work," and wins after the catch. Notes Hill was on a record-breaking pace at one point in 2023 before a minor injury cost him a game, and argues his crossing routes out of the slot are as hard to stop as Davante Adams's goal-line bubble screens were in Green Bay.

- 2024-03-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-25]]: notes Hill has talked publicly about a retirement timeline — "the end of next year or something" — and casts him as the ideal veteran mentor for incoming rookie [[Xavier Worthy]], specifically because Hill's own speed-plus-technique game gives him credibility to tell a younger burner not to lean on pure speed.
- 2024-09-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-16]]): with [[Tua Tagovailoa]] out, Waldman still starts the Miami pass catchers — they will be used in the short passing game with plenty of designed touches — but everyone drops a tier. Hill goes from 'an elite wide receiver one' to 'probably a low-end wide receiver one, high-end wide receiver two,' with [[Jaylen Waddle]] a WR3 and [[De'Von Achane]] gaining from an increased dump-off role. Waldman leaves the door open: if Skylar Thompson plays the way he has flashed on college tape and Miami acclimates quickly to a right-handed quarterback, 'Tyreek Hill will be just fine' and he would revise the projection upward.
- 2024-12-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-17]]): he openly wonders whether Hill is simply not the same player anymore, while stressing that would be no revelation for a 30-plus-year-old receiver. His mechanism is workload rather than decline alone — Miami's offense is 'a bit of a house of cards' because it runs receivers at full speed on pre-snap motion, then asks them to run deep in-breaking routes on top of that, which he argues gasses a small receiver over a season. He says he will need offseason [[Reception Perception Methodology]] charting to be sure. James Koh (not tracked) framed the same problem from the other direction: the offense is too committed to getting the ball out quick and isn't manufacturing shotgun or drop-back looks that let Hill work downfield.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-19]]): drawing Charvarius Ward and a 49ers defense that has been stingy against outside receivers but gives up the most points per dropback in man coverage. Harmon is non-committal on a big game and notes Miami's offense has skewed more toward [[Jonnu Smith]] than Hill lately. On man-coverage looks specifically, [[Jaylen Waddle]] has been the more productive Dolphin at 2.18 yards per route run to Hill's 1.93 — not low, but the big man-beating plays have gone to Waddle. Both hosts also discussed Hill's cryptic 'time for me to go coach' tweet with no comma and the follow-up 'got y'all exactly how I want', concluding he is most likely trolling, while noting he has previously said he only wanted to play through 2025 and runs a popular Twitch account he might prefer.
- 2025-01-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-06]]): Hill's post-game comments that the door is open and he is on his way out were, in Waldman's read, a player popping off in the fourth quarter of a disappointing season, and he agrees with Mike McDaniel's own response that you should not weight the immediate post-game words of a disappointed player. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) was blunter: Hill talked his way out of Kansas City, but the contract he talked his way into in Miami will make it hard to move him — and the prescription is to heal up the wrist and play better. Both used the episode's continuity argument to defend McDaniel and GM Chris Grier being retained rather than reading the Hill noise as a real exit signal.
- 2025-01-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-07]]): after Hill told reporters 'I'm out bro' in Week 18, co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) put the odds of him returning to Miami at 0.1%. Harmon believes Hill, who turns 31 next season, took a step back in 2024 — less explosive separation, less space down the field once Tua returned, partly a wrist injury — and said he would be surprised if Hill's Reception Perception numbers came back at the peak of the last few seasons, noting Hill has been an elite press-man beater since an 80% success rate against press as a rookie. Harmon was also wary of the character risk, citing Hill pulling himself out of the Week 18 game and forcing Mike McDaniel to explain it. Koh countered that the contract is cheap and tradable, essentially a one-year prove-it. Landing spots: Harmon sliced off Minnesota, the Chargers, Detroit and Cincinnati; both liked Washington, the Rams and Denver, with Koh most intrigued by Sean Payton and Koh floating a Deebo-for-Tyreek swap with San Francisco.
- 2025-01-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-13]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon says fuck it to Hill remaining in Miami in 2025 — he expects him to stay, but is not enthusiastic about it. He reads Hill as hard to predict off the field, noting Hill had many chances to walk back the 'I'm out, I'm done here' comments and instead escalated, including photoshopping his face onto Antonio Brown's body. More importantly Harmon calls him a declining player who will not impact games the way he used to, which is natural for his stage of career, and says signs of that already crept into his game this season. If he were Miami he would cash out on the Tyreek experience now, because the offense is too reliant on his skill set, and instead find out what a post-Hill world looks like — make [[Jaylen Waddle]] the one, elevate [[Malik Washington]], keep feeding [[Jonnu Smith]]. [[Matt Waldman]] calls that a wise course.
- 2025-01-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-27]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: a qualified 'feel it' on a Hill rebound after a 959-yard season in which he pulled himself from the finale and asked out. Waldman says he is going against his gut: with defenses playing two-high and removing the deep game, Hill's biggest fear element is blunted, so the margin is tight — 1,000 to 1,100 yards is reachable but the old 1,300–1,500 ceiling probably is not. His verdict is that a WR1 rebound is unlikely while 'having some value is very likely'. Bob Harris (not tracked) added a data point: scanning the Next Gen Stats fastest ball carriers of the season, he found [[Derrick Henry]], [[Jonathan Taylor]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], [[KaVontae Turpin]], [[Nico Collins]], [[Saquon Barkley]] and [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] but no Hill for the first time in years — either slowing down at 30-plus or not getting the chances. Both would buy at a price; Harris called a third-round mock ADP 'a little rich' unless Hill is a third receiver in a receiver-heavy zero-RB build.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: with Hill going anywhere from round two to round three in early drafts, Waldman says round three is a relative bargain and he would expect a rebound year. Context from Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked): Hill failed to record a catch of 30+ yards after week one and, for the first time in years, was not inside the top 20 in recorded speeds, plus the in-season outburst about wanting out — which he has since apologised for and owned, with Mike McDaniel saying he will be back. Waldman's line on the production: 81 catches, 959 yards and six touchdowns as WR18 is not horrible, but it is a long way from the 1,300-1,700 yards you would normally expect, so treat him as a WR3 with upside or a solid WR2 rather than a WR1 price.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'fuck it' on Hill at Underdog WR12 in favor of [[Ladd McConkey]] — Waldman's frame is one player on the way up and one on the way down, and separately in the quarterback segment he raised whether an aging Hill is about to fall off a cliff as a reason to fade [[Tua Tagovailoa]]. He allowed the bull case that in PPR Hill could still post another 1,300-1,400 yard season if everything works out. Untracked co-host Bob Harris noted Hill has said he is fine returning to Miami and apologized for his end-of-season comments, but that reports now suggest Miami will listen if someone calls — so his situation is worth watching.
- 2025-02-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-28]]): co-host James Koh (not tracked) has moved from 99 percent sure Hill was gone to under 50 percent, and now thinks there is a better than even chance he opens 2025 in Miami — reading Drew Rosenhaus' Super Bowl-week chatter as market-gauging that found no takers for a 31-year-old on 30M a year coming off a mutiny moment. The 2024 decline is broad: 959 yards and six touchdowns after nearly 1,800 and 13; 1.79 yards per route run (career low, from 3.85 and 3.21); YAC share 36 to 30.4 percent; first downs per target 48.5 to 44; success rate 57 to 52 percent; explosive receptions 38.7 to 27.2 percent; EPA per target 0.23; first down on 10.47 percent of routes, from 18.2. Harmon has only spot-charted him but sees a small real step back, trending like [[Davante Adams]] — maybe no longer tier one, still able to lead a passing game — and would not bank on a bounce back to top-three status. He also questions how sustainable Mike McDaniel's full-speed motion is on speed receivers' knees and hamstrings. Koh counters that the speed is still there and it may be a motivation question, and that a trade market will reignite three or four weeks into the season.
- 2025-05-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-30]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon has Hill down in tier five with a blurb ending 'no thanks' — he believes the decline has started, points to Hill having tried to talk his way out of Miami at the end of last season regardless of what he says now, and to a messy offseason including police at his house and domestic incidents. His advice is to 'get out', and he would happily trade Hill for a lot of the younger players sitting in the same tier. Koh (not tracked) counters that Hill went for roughly 1,800 yards two years ago and 1,700 three years ago, but concedes that at age 31 he is close to untradeable — a Hill manager is really hoping to catch lightning in a bottle one more time. Harmon separately says he wants more of the priority layup looks going to [[Jaylen Waddle]] and fewer to Hill.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Hill is his pick for the most overpriced player inside the top 30 of ADP. He is nearly 32, coming off a wrist injury that Angelo does not expect to allow catching until August, and is going roughly half a round before [[Davante Adams]] — Angelo would rather move back and take Adams. He expects Miami to lean on a run game and take pressure off [[Tua Tagovailoa]] rather than run a big vertical offense, doubts Tua stays on the field for 17 games, and sees [[Jaylen Waddle]] and pass-catching back [[De'Von Achane]] absorbing share, with [[Darren Waller]]'s addition doing little for Hill. The peak version was force-fed 10-12 targets a game; Angelo does not see that recurring, and comps Hill's realistic outcome closer to [[Calvin Ridley]] than to his current ADP. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees.
- 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]): Harmon lays out a broad 2024 decline in Hill's charting. Across 2021-23 he sat between 75.7 and 75.8 percent success rate versus man; in 2024 that fell to 67.3 percent. He had cleared 80 percent versus press every season from 2016 to 2023 except one (79 percent) and dropped to 72 percent. Versus zone he was still 81.8 percent, down from around 84 percent but the best relative result. Deep digs against man, once deadly, graded average at 72.7 percent and the slant fell below NFL average — Harmon reads that as lost change-of-direction ability in the middle of the field, not lost straight-line speed. He still sees a useful, maybe good starting receiver — 65 percent success on the nine route, 87 percent on the curl, tackles broken after the catch — just not an elite one, and at 31 he would bet against a return to elite form, noting some decliners fall off a cliff (Dez Bryant, [[Keenan Allen]] style steady slide as the alternative). He is unmoved by the wrist-injury and 'wasn't bought in' excuses, saying he is not high on acquiring a player who was dogging it. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) believes Hill is plainly not bought in and is trying, unsuccessfully, not to be crazy; Harmon says he would have tried to get out of the Tyreek Hill business this offseason.
- 2025-08-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Hill has fallen from a top-five pick last year to the third round, and Harris says he has taken him in the third a fair amount because he is still a really good player — if he gets traded that is a bonus, and if not you hope the wrist holds up and he avoids being a knucklehead. Guest Sigmund Bloom (Footballguys, not tracked) makes Miami his number one 'could get worse' offense, comparing the franchise to a car teetering on a cliff edge: he cites the Lions joint practice where Nick Baumgartner called it the worst disparity he had seen and another writer said either Miami is very bad or Detroit is a juggernaut, plus Jeremy Fowler reporting teams are still monitoring Hill's availability despite Miami saying he would not be traded — Bloom half-jokes Hill could be back in a Chiefs uniform by Halloween.
- 2025-08-18 — According to guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert), on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Hill is his No. 4 bust — a speed receiver on the wrong side of 30 whose efficiency cratered. Hill played all 17 games and still finished with 840 fewer receiving yards than the prior season, which Doherty thinks may be a record drop for a player who did not miss time; he failed to reach two yards per route run for the first time in his career after leading the league the year before, and his 20-plus-yard receptions fell from 29 to 13. The alibis (wrist injury, [[Tua Tagovailoa]] missing time) are real, but with Tua already getting rid of the ball faster than anyone and saying he wants to be faster still, Doherty does not see how the downfield game recovers. [[Chris Harris]] disagrees on price: his rank is right around ADP or a touch higher, he has drafted Hill in the fourth round, he has research showing all-time receivers age better than 'slappies,' and he frames last year's alibis as possibly explanations rather than excuses — while granting Hill carries more risk than a typical third/fourth-rounder.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): the episode's only 10 out of 10. Guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) gives Hill a 10 and says he does not understand any argument for taking him in the third round; his framework is that either Hill fell off the age cliff — citing Adam Harstad's work at Football Guys that receivers do not decline gradually but nosedive, and that a speed-dependent profile nosedives hardest — or the collapse was environmental, in which case nothing has changed, since Hill is still with the quarterback he hates and the coach he does not want to play for. Harris gives him a 9 and defends drafting him: Hill was the number one fantasy receiver two years ago in the same environment, the film shows he was still fast last year, and what actually happened was [[Tua Tagovailoa]]'s concussion, the offensive line breaking and Miami shutting it down. Harris is not predicting the ceiling, only arguing Hill is a legitimately high-variance league-winner from the middle of round three.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Hill is on two teams, both drafted in the first half of August (the Fishbowl and an August 4 Reddit experts draft), and Harris notes he did not end up taking Hill in any second-half-of-August draft. He explicitly disagrees with JEFF BELL (a guest on the show the previous Friday, NOT a tracked expert), who argued Hill's ADP is wrong because he has only downside and nothing can go right for him. Harris says he cannot see the future but can see a future where the Miami offense gets more functional, where Hill is not literally handcuffed on his way to the stadium and hurting his wrist in Week 1, and where he is still fast and still makes big plays. At a third-round price for a player who was the no-doubt 1.02 in fantasy a year ago, he does not find that a terrible cost if you are willing to shoot the moon for upside.
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-05]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Hill is Harris' No. 3 Week 1 terror and he has kept drafting him in the third round, sometimes the fourth — 'everybody's just pretending that he wasn't the number two pick in all drafts this time last year.' He gave Hill an 8 out of 10 risk factor a week earlier opposite Jeff Bell (NOT a tracked expert), who said he no longer sees any upside at all; Harris disagrees, arguing you have to acknowledge what could go right and 'he still's fast.' His broader case is contrarian: the whole market has decided Miami collapses and Mike McDaniel gets fired, the win total has dropped from 8.5 to 7.5, and he can't remember a team so universally chosen as the disappointment pick — which is usually when they go to the AFC Championship game. Erickson said he disliked Miami's direction even before the Ramsey trade. Harris' summary: 'I'm terrified to draft him and I'm terrified not to draft him.'
