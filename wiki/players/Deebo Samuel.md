# Deebo Samuel

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): the episode's cleanest example of a player whose fantasy value is inseparable from his scheme. Explicitly endorsing a segment he had seen from [[Matt Harmon]] — "a dead-on explanation" — Waldman's version: as a zone-beater and a gadget runner Samuel is outstanding, "there are few that do it as well as him in those situations," and because the NFL plays enough zone the production shows up. Against man coverage he is "not remotely as good." The conclusion is a warning about team change rather than about talent: "he might not even be remotely the player he is right now in fantasy football if he were on another team that forced him to be in situations where he was going to get covered man-to-man more often." Waldman is careful that this is not pejorative — leveraging that skill set is the organization's job. See [[Zone vs Man Route Running]] and [[Scheme vs Talent]].
- 2023-12-21 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode, two framings that cut against pricing Samuel as a type: (1) if the league's next scheme-centric swing is teams copying the Kyle Shanahan offense, "everybody's looking for a Deebo Samuel type and a George Kittle type" — Harstad is skeptical it is copyable; (2) Percy Harvin was "a better Deebo Samuel — he could do all the stuff Deebo did and he could also do stuff Deebo didn't do," and never got that level of commitment, because the league was in a scheme-centric mode at the time. The role, on this reading, is scarcer than the skill set.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): a good Divisional Round spot against Green Bay for a "pop pass" or a big designed run, though without a specific target-share case made beyond that.
- 2024-01-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-25]]): ranked #3 of the playoffs' remaining receivers, behind [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] but explicitly defensible as #1 by James Koh's own admission — "a true game wrecker" who "just does things that really nobody else in the receiver position does," which Harmon says makes him a genuinely hard player to rank against more traditional route-runners. Flagged as a real injury doubt for the NFC Championship at Detroit, but the 49ers' offense is deep enough (George Kittle, [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Brandon Aiyuk]], Juwan Jennings) to get by without him if he sits.
- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]), on Super Bowl LVIII: a fresh, game-level confirmation of the man-vs-zone read — 11 targets but only 3 catches for 33 yards, the product of a deliberate Chiefs plan to press him at the line and take away the zone-beating/YAC role that's his real strength, live with letting Brandon Aiyuk win a couple of in-breakers against a safety instead. Harmon calls it "quietly the most brilliant part" of Kansas City's defensive game plan and, separately, notes Samuel "doesn't have the best hands in the world" — he should have caught a late fourth-quarter pass that nearly became an interception. Also flagged in the offseason-outlook segment as one leg of an approaching roster crunch: not all three of Samuel, [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and George Kittle survive on San Francisco's roster past 2024 given the cap picture.
- 2024-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-30]]): discussing the Ricky Pearsall pick, Harmon argues Deebo Samuel is not a true No. 1 receiver league-wide or even necessarily on his own team -- he says San Francisco's offense was actually at its best when Brandon Aiyuk was the WR1. Harmon calls Samuel a very good receiver and a strong zone-beater (multiple 90th-percentile-plus RSP scores vs. zone) but says he does not consistently win true third-down, high-leverage man-coverage matchups, an issue Harmon says was exposed in the Super Bowl loss and part of why the 49ers drafted Pearsall. Co-host James Koh (untracked) initially pushes back, noting many would consider Samuel a true No. 1, before Harmon's case wins him over.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: discussing San Francisco's crowded receiver room, Angelo argues Deebo has become somewhat 'obsolete' to the offense's flow now that [[Christian McCaffrey]] handles a similar role, since Deebo's touches have always needed to be schemed for him — he's not a polished route runner and lacks refined route-running inside or outside. Angelo suggests trading Deebo would be the 49ers' lower-risk move relative to moving [[Brandon Aiyuk]], since [[Ricky Pearsall]] could approximate Deebo's scheme role, if not his rushing production.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman flatly rejected the idea (raised by unnamed San Francisco beat writers) that [[Christian McCaffrey]] could eventually replace Samuel as the team's gadget receiver, saying McCaffrey is focused on running back training and won't develop into that role at 30-plus. He characterized Samuel as not a good man-coverage route runner — he wins almost exclusively in zone coverage — which makes him 'a premium gadget player' rather than a true X receiver, a phrase Waldman said 'may be insulting to some' but is accurate; teams can dedicate a lesser corner to him in man coverage and still win that matchup, unlike against [[Brandon Aiyuk]]. He ranked Samuel just behind Aiyuk (around WR21) but noted Samuel is the more likely of the two to produce standalone spike weeks if either 49ers receiver breaks out this year.
- 2024-07-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]): if San Francisco has to move one receiver, 'absolutely' trade Samuel and keep [[Brandon Aiyuk]]. Waldman calls him 'the highest-end gadget player in the NFL' — great in the run game, dangerous motioned across formations, excellent in zone after the catch — but not good against man press or timing routes of that type. The distinction he draws: in the pivotal moment when a defense says 'we've stopped you, we're going to make you beat our top guy,' you need a route runner who can win in tight coverage, and that is Aiyuk (or maybe [[Ricky Pearsall]] one day), not Samuel, 'as good as he is at what he does.' He thinks Samuel could produce instantly in Washington with [[Jayden Daniels]] or in New England, but the ceiling with a good quarterback is lower than Aiyuk's.
- 2024-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-06]]): Samuel's 2022 profile — 10th percentile vs man, 89th percentile vs zone — is the MO of his whole career, and Harmon says RP has flagged it long before it became a talking point after the Super Bowl. He is one of the most dangerous and unique weapons at the position and a smart zone beater who knows where to sit down and read coverage, but he is not a true one-on-one iso route runner and lacks the route-running chops to win against man. That makes removing [[Brandon Aiyuk]] super problematic, since Aiyuk is the room's man-coverage beater and best vertical threat. Harmon's preferred plan was to lock Aiyuk up early and treat 2024 as the last run with Samuel, and he questions whether Samuel is worth $15M on the fifth-year option.
