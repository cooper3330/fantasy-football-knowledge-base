# Deebo Samuel

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): the episode's cleanest example of a player whose fantasy value is inseparable from his scheme. Explicitly endorsing a segment he had seen from [[Matt Harmon]] — "a dead-on explanation" — Waldman's version: as a zone-beater and a gadget runner Samuel is outstanding, "there are few that do it as well as him in those situations," and because the NFL plays enough zone the production shows up. Against man coverage he is "not remotely as good." The conclusion is a warning about team change rather than about talent: "he might not even be remotely the player he is right now in fantasy football if he were on another team that forced him to be in situations where he was going to get covered man-to-man more often." Waldman is careful that this is not pejorative — leveraging that skill set is the organization's job. See [[Zone vs Man Route Running]] and [[Scheme vs Talent]].
- 2023-12-21 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert), same episode, two framings that cut against pricing Samuel as a type: (1) if the league's next scheme-centric swing is teams copying the Kyle Shanahan offense, "everybody's looking for a Deebo Samuel type and a George Kittle type" — Harstad is skeptical it is copyable; (2) Percy Harvin was "a better Deebo Samuel — he could do all the stuff Deebo did and he could also do stuff Deebo didn't do," and never got that level of commitment, because the league was in a scheme-centric mode at the time. The role, on this reading, is scarcer than the skill set.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): a good Divisional Round spot against Green Bay for a "pop pass" or a big designed run, though without a specific target-share case made beyond that.
- 2024-01-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-25]]): ranked #3 of the playoffs' remaining receivers, behind [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] but explicitly defensible as #1 by James Koh's own admission — "a true game wrecker" who "just does things that really nobody else in the receiver position does," which Harmon says makes him a genuinely hard player to rank against more traditional route-runners. Flagged as a real injury doubt for the NFC Championship at Detroit, but the 49ers' offense is deep enough (George Kittle, [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Brandon Aiyuk]], Juwan Jennings) to get by without him if he sits.
- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]), on Super Bowl LVIII: a fresh, game-level confirmation of the man-vs-zone read — 11 targets but only 3 catches for 33 yards, the product of a deliberate Chiefs plan to press him at the line and take away the zone-beating/YAC role that's his real strength, live with letting Brandon Aiyuk win a couple of in-breakers against a safety instead. Harmon calls it "quietly the most brilliant part" of Kansas City's defensive game plan and, separately, notes Samuel "doesn't have the best hands in the world" — he should have caught a late fourth-quarter pass that nearly became an interception. Also flagged in the offseason-outlook segment as one leg of an approaching roster crunch: not all three of Samuel, [[Brandon Aiyuk]] and George Kittle survive on San Francisco's roster past 2024 given the cap picture.
- 2024-04-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-30]]): discussing the Ricky Pearsall pick, Harmon argues Deebo Samuel is not a true No. 1 receiver league-wide or even necessarily on his own team -- he says San Francisco's offense was actually at its best when Brandon Aiyuk was the WR1. Harmon calls Samuel a very good receiver and a strong zone-beater (multiple 90th-percentile-plus RSP scores vs. zone) but says he does not consistently win true third-down, high-leverage man-coverage matchups, an issue Harmon says was exposed in the Super Bowl loss and part of why the 49ers drafted Pearsall. Co-host James Koh (untracked) initially pushes back, noting many would consider Samuel a true No. 1, before Harmon's case wins him over.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: discussing San Francisco's crowded receiver room, Angelo argues Deebo has become somewhat 'obsolete' to the offense's flow now that [[Christian McCaffrey]] handles a similar role, since Deebo's touches have always needed to be schemed for him — he's not a polished route runner and lacks refined route-running inside or outside. Angelo suggests trading Deebo would be the 49ers' lower-risk move relative to moving [[Brandon Aiyuk]], since [[Ricky Pearsall]] could approximate Deebo's scheme role, if not his rushing production.
- 2024-06-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman flatly rejected the idea (raised by unnamed San Francisco beat writers) that [[Christian McCaffrey]] could eventually replace Samuel as the team's gadget receiver, saying McCaffrey is focused on running back training and won't develop into that role at 30-plus. He characterized Samuel as not a good man-coverage route runner — he wins almost exclusively in zone coverage — which makes him 'a premium gadget player' rather than a true X receiver, a phrase Waldman said 'may be insulting to some' but is accurate; teams can dedicate a lesser corner to him in man coverage and still win that matchup, unlike against [[Brandon Aiyuk]]. He ranked Samuel just behind Aiyuk (around WR21) but noted Samuel is the more likely of the two to produce standalone spike weeks if either 49ers receiver breaks out this year.
- 2024-07-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-22]]): if San Francisco has to move one receiver, 'absolutely' trade Samuel and keep [[Brandon Aiyuk]]. Waldman calls him 'the highest-end gadget player in the NFL' — great in the run game, dangerous motioned across formations, excellent in zone after the catch — but not good against man press or timing routes of that type. The distinction he draws: in the pivotal moment when a defense says 'we've stopped you, we're going to make you beat our top guy,' you need a route runner who can win in tight coverage, and that is Aiyuk (or maybe [[Ricky Pearsall]] one day), not Samuel, 'as good as he is at what he does.' He thinks Samuel could produce instantly in Washington with [[Jayden Daniels]] or in New England, but the ceiling with a good quarterback is lower than Aiyuk's.
- 2024-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-06]]): Samuel's 2022 profile — 10th percentile vs man, 89th percentile vs zone — is the MO of his whole career, and Harmon says RP has flagged it long before it became a talking point after the Super Bowl. He is one of the most dangerous and unique weapons at the position and a smart zone beater who knows where to sit down and read coverage, but he is not a true one-on-one iso route runner and lacks the route-running chops to win against man. That makes removing [[Brandon Aiyuk]] super problematic, since Aiyuk is the room's man-coverage beater and best vertical threat. Harmon's preferred plan was to lock Aiyuk up early and treat 2024 as the last run with Samuel, and he questions whether Samuel is worth $15M on the fifth-year option.
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Samuel ran a route on only 5.4% of dropbacks versus Kansas City because of an illness. Harmon is blunt that he is not the answer to San Francisco's post-[[Brandon Aiyuk]] problem: Deebo has never been a man-coverage beater, which is why he has to be schemed into position, and team success rate when throwing to him is 40.6% — closer to [[Ronnie Bell]]'s 40% than to [[George Kittle]] (61.9%), [[Jauan Jennings]] (58.3%) or even Aiyuk (51.1%). James Koh (not tracked) takes the fantasy side of it and expects Deebo's volume, targets and air yards to be 'absolutely insane' with Aiyuk gone, so the two split on whether the coming target share makes him a buy.
- 2024-11-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-20]]): Koh laid out a steady, dramatic YAC decline — yards after catch per reception of 12.3 in 2020, 10.4, then 9.0, then 8.86 in 2024 — and said the after-the-catch explosiveness that made Deebo special is not just diminished but absent on tape; he called it roughly a 50% reduction and said 8 YAC per reception is fine but not special. Harmon agreed he does not see that player either and said it is unsurprising given how heavily Deebo has been used, at times literally as a running back. Harmon's added framing: the 49ers offense is evolving toward drop-back passing and 'big boy route concepts' rather than designed gimmick touches — the [[Ricky Pearsall]] pick was more telling about that shift than the industry credited — so Deebo is a hyper-specific, role-catered player who no longer fits. He expects Deebo to be squeezed out in a 2025 salary crunch and become an offseason trade candidate, and does not think he is on the roster next year. Both noted he is still a positive YAC force at 7.8 yards after catch per reception.
- 2024-11-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-26]]): a total non-factor in Week 12 at 0.75 yards per route run, though he did have a big play to open the third quarter called back. Harmon frames it as an environment problem more than a player problem — San Francisco's offense 'looked broken' with a backup quarterback who had thrown three passes in three years, after a season spent walking a razor's edge (Trent Williams and [[Brandon Aiyuk]] holdouts deep into camp, [[Christian McCaffrey]] injured, first-round rookie receiver shot). Harmon says [[Christian McCaffrey]] and Samuel simply do not look like the versions of the players people are familiar with, and adds a half-joking aside that he dislikes veterans changing jersey numbers on the same team (Samuel 19 to 1, [[Dalvin Cook]] comp).
- 2024-12-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-10]]): in the true-or-false round Harmon says it is true that [[Jauan Jennings]] has been San Francisco's number one receiver, and stresses the finding is more of a statement about Samuel's decline than an endorsement of Jennings. In the 49ers structure he lays out for the future — [[Brandon Aiyuk]] first, Jennings second, [[Ricky Pearsall]] developing in the slot — Samuel is not named at all.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-19]]): Harmon thinks Deebo is over the 49ers and the 49ers are over him — reading the Thursday night script, where San Francisco forced him early targets that he dropped, as the coaching staff making a point rather than getting a struggling star going. He drew a community note for tweeting he was not struggling but not getting the ball, then led the team in drops; three of seven targets for 16 yards against the Rams. On the Week 16 Miami matchup, Harmon expects Jalen Ramsey to shadow Deebo because he is 'still the name', but says there is no question [[Jauan Jennings]] has been the better player. Against cover two — Miami is fifth in the league in cover-two rate, and the historical Deebo answer to cover two was middle-of-field YAC touches — Deebo is the lowest of San Francisco's four main pass catchers this year, all of whom clear two yards per route run.
- 2025-01-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-07]]): Harmon does not account for Samuel being part of the 49ers receiver room next season and does not believe San Francisco is interested in signing him long term. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) floated a rare player-for-player swap sending Samuel to Miami for [[Tyreek Hill]], with Mike McDaniel free to tinker with a role for him. Harmon agreed the connective tissue is real — Samuel has said McDaniel was a big part of crafting his San Francisco role and one of his favorite coaches to work with — and named Miami as a spot he would expect Samuel to land, particularly if Hill is gone, while still calling the reverse leg of the trade a long shot for the Niners.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): with San Francisco granting Samuel permission to seek a trade (Patriots, Eagles, Broncos, Chargers and Rams floated), Waldman says most of those teams should pass. His evaluation: Samuel is not great at separating and is not a great route runner — he is skilled at catching the ball and running after the catch, plus gadget usage and even filling in at running back — so he is effectively an aging running back who catches it well downfield, and acquiring him means adapting your passing offense around him. The one exception he names is the Rams, whose scheme is cousins with San Francisco's: there he would be a Robert Woods-light with more physicality as a runner, adding value in the run game. Waldman reads the 49ers as happy to move on, partly because it was their idea too.
- 2025-02-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-19]]): Samuel was given permission to seek a trade during Super Bowl week after a reportedly tough conversation with Kyle Shanahan, and Harmon sees no remaining fit in San Francisco — the 49ers moved to a true dropback pass game in 2024, and Samuel is not a traditional route runner or a man-coverage beater, while [[Ricky Pearsall]] covers the slot, [[Jauan Jennings]] the flanker/slot work and a healthy [[Brandon Aiyuk]] the X. Harmon's preferred landing spot is Washington: ties to GM Adam Peters, screen and layup concepts under Kliff Kingsbury, and a room that is just [[Terry McLaurin]], [[Luke McCaffrey]] and [[Zach Ertz]] — he would happily flip a sixth-rounder and take the whole contract. James Koh (not tracked) pushed back that Kingsbury's offense has few in-breaking concepts, which is where Reception Perception has Samuel best (80% success on digs, 75% on slants in 2022), and noted the decline: rushing YPC of 6.2 (2021), 5.5, 6.1, then 3.2 in 2024, with long runs falling 49-51-23-13 yards, plus four straight years of falling yards after catch. Both allowed pneumonia and a 49ers Super Bowl hangover as mitigation; Harmon's expectation entering his age-29 season is a role player, not a 100-catch receiver.
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]): Waldman likes the fit after the trade to Washington — Cliff Kingsbury's offense is screen, screen, RPO, and Samuel gives them the body type of [[Noah Brown]], the speed of [[Dyami Brown]] and the route ability and quickness after the catch of [[Olamide Zaccheaus]] wrapped into one player, plus backfield usage, so he should be very busy in a horizontal offense with a vertical component. Two cautions: Waldman says Kingsbury will be Kingsbury and defenses may figure the scheme out (survivable only if the weapons are good enough to win known matchups), and the bigger worry is health — he suspects the 49ers rode Samuel until they had worn out every last carrot and that Washington's front office, high on its own fumes, may have fallen for a bait-and-switch. Bob Harris (not tracked) added that Washington averaged 5.1 yards after catch last year (21st) against Samuel's 9.0 career average, but that Samuel is coming off a career-worst season; Waldman said he was down on Samuel last year and never bought in.
- 2025-03-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-05]]): Samuel was traded to Washington for a fifth-round pick with the Commanders absorbing his full contract, a landing spot Harmon says he called in advance because of the Adam Peters 49ers front-office pipeline. He likes the trade for Washington — cap space they cannot otherwise spend, minimal draft cost — while insisting Samuel is 'not a needle mover' at this point; Koh noted Vegas did not move Washington's title odds at all. Harmon's early Reception Perception charting covers four of eight 2024 games, all pre-pneumonia, and it 'is not looking so good': Samuel posted 50.8%, 64.3% and 56% success rate vs man from 2020-2022, all slightly below average to poor, and the 2024 sample is tracking well below that, with his usually elite success rate vs zone also on pace for one of his worst marks in the database. Declining explosiveness off the ball and four straight years of declining yards after catch. He still breaks tackles, and Washington ranked fourth in receptions on WR/TE screens (21st in yards per reception on them), so Harmon expects heavy manufactured usage under Cliff Kingsbury — his joke was a '90 reception, 500 yard season'. He doubts Samuel is a true number two anymore and worries about a room where he and [[Terry McLaurin]] are both entering age-29 seasons with poor depth behind them.
- 2025-05-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-16]]): Samuel's 2024 success rate vs man coverage is the third-lowest Harmon has ever charted, and he has never been a good man beater. Harmon uses him as the model rather than a warning, though: Kyle Shanahan spent years crafting specific receiver usage — not just gadget touches — around a route chart of posts, digs, slants and screens, which is why Harmon proposes the same scheme-around-the-deficiency approach for [[Xavier Legette]] instead of expecting coaching to turn a poor press beater into a good one.
- 2025-05-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-26]]): Waldman calls [[Deebo Samuel]] an ideal addition for Kliff Kingsbury first and [[Jayden Daniels]] second. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) cited 55 plays of at least 20 yards in the passing game over the last four years; Waldman's point is what those are — mostly catch-and-run, not winning one-on-one on timing routes. So Samuel will not expand what Daniels has to do to win through the air the way a true WR1 would; what he does is maximize Kingsbury's preference for work within 10 yards of the line of scrimmage and let Samuel create yards after the catch, plus getting open against zone downfield. Short and long plays, not the contested-catch, beat-the-top-corner timing work — which Waldman says is [[Terry McLaurin]]'s job. Net: an ideal addition for the offense that helps Daniels, but Waldman suspects the market is overpaying for Samuel as a fantasy investment.
- 2025-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-16]]): Harmon refuses to argue about training-camp body-shape chatter and goes to the charting instead — in 2024 Samuel posted the fourth-worst success rate versus man coverage Harmon has ever charted in Reception Perception history. He grants Samuel was never a great man-coverage beater, but says this is a move from below average to genuinely bad, and that the number predates the pneumonia episode. His summary is blunt: Samuel simply was not a good player for San Francisco last season, which is why Washington's receiver room collapses to nothing behind [[Terry McLaurin]] if McLaurin holds out. Harmon also flags that Samuel's average annual value is slightly higher than McLaurin's on the same roster, and that Washington paid his full contract on acquiring him.
- 2025-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-06]]): Samuel fits what Cliff Kingsbury wants on screens and horizontal underneath work, 'but that's all he can do' at this stage — Harmon says you cannot ask him to be an every-down outside perimeter receiver anymore, citing the worst success rate versus man coverage of any player he sampled in 2024, below 40 percent, and one of the worst in the history of his charting. Even with Samuel added, Harmon thinks Washington still needs a real secondary perimeter presence alongside [[Terry McLaurin]]. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) is blunter, calling Samuel 'more name than game' and the rest of the room — [[Noah Brown]], [[Luke McCaffrey]], Jaylin Lane, [[Michael Gallup]] — a collection of wide receiver threes with no genuine number two.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): Deebo is an odd fit in Washington — unless the offense manufactures targets for him he does not get open, and that damages the timing [[Jayden Daniels]] depends on. Angelo said he would rather have [[Jaylin Lane]] play the slot than Deebo, with [[Matt Waldman]] surprised by the strength of the take but conceding Lane has the body-catching issue rather than a broader inability. In the same conversation Waldman argued [[D.J. Moore]] is 'a better Deebo than Deebo' — more productive, versatile and resilient — while Deebo at around 215 pounds plays closer to [[Isiah Pacheco]], running through walls with little nuance as a runner, which works at receiver only because of his size and aggression [Redraft (Standard)].
- 2025-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-08]]): Samuel posted a 39.7% success rate versus man coverage in 2024 — not 40th percentile, 40 percent — the third lowest Harmon has recorded across hundreds of Reception Perception profiles, behind only Justin Hunter (31%) and Terrace Marshall. Harmon charted him from a Boston hotel room and says the pneumonia excuse does not hold: the man-coverage tape was already bad at the start of the season. Samuel was never a strong man beater even at his peak (66.9% as a rookie, 64.3% in his 2021 breakout, some seasons in the 50s), but this is a precipitous drop. The zone number that carried him also fell, from 85.6%/82.2%/86%/84% across 2019-22 to 76.3%, and his in-breakers — slants and digs — dropped from good to merely average. Harmon says a bounce-back has to come from the zone number, which is hard to bet on for a player who has absorbed more punishment than most receivers. He fits as a screen/gadget replacement for [[Dyami Brown]], but warns Washington may be forced to ask him for far more than that.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): Samuel led [[Terry McLaurin]] 10 targets to 4 and scored on a jet-sweep carry where he ran over half the defense in typical 49ers fashion. Harris says Samuel was pretty good, made some mistakes, but was clearly more part of the offense than McLaurin was, and that this could last a while. Guest **Sam Gold** (not tracked) adds that Samuel is absorbing the over-the-middle routes that previously went to McLaurin in Cliff Kingsbury's scheme.
