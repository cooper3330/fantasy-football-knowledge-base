# Devaughn Vele

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-10-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-14]]) [Dynasty]: Vele is worth keeping tabs on and, in deep-roster dynasty leagues, worth an add or a practice-squad spot. He catches the ball well, runs well after the catch, and runs decent routes, and has genuinely impressed the Broncos staff — something they have told beat writers all summer, and which shows up on the field. Waldman's caveat is age: as an older rookie, 'age heads' will dismiss him out of hand. The organizational comp he reports is a young [[Tim Patrick]] — or, allowing for the age, an already middle-aged Tim Patrick. Deep-league value only.
- 2024-11-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-18]]): asked whether Vele is a future WR1, Waldman said no — but he thinks Vele will be a good player. He endorsed the framing Cecil Lammey (not a tracked expert) offered in the summer that Denver views Vele as their future [[Tim Patrick]], and pushed it slightly higher: 'Tim Patrick plus', which Waldman defined as a good WR2 rather than a true alpha.
- 2024-11-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-21]]): Harmon is intrigued by Vele, noting he has 90 routes since Week 8 and saying he wants to get him into the rookie report, hopefully within a week. He describes him as a 26-year-old massive rookie and is careful to caveat that he does not really know how good Vele is yet — only that Denver has him in a clear, singular role and he is doing good stuff in it. Harmon groups him with [[Courtland Sutton]] and Lil'Jordan Humphrey as the bigger targets [[Bo Nix]] operates well with over the intermediate middle, in contrast to [[Troy Franklin]], with whom Nix seems to lack chemistry.
- 2024-11-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-26]]): Harmon charted Vele for the rookie report and likes him more than he expected. He is 6-5, 210, ran a 4.4, and looks like a prototypical X, but charted as roughly a 64% slot player — a 52.3% success rate versus man that Harmon calls not good, and around league average versus zone, which is what you want from a slot. His best routes are flats, digs and outs, so intermediate middle-of-field work, and Harmon gives him a [[Tim Patrick]] comparison for hands through contact, ball tracking and clutch third-down play. He fits [[Sean Payton]]'s stated love of big receivers, like [[Courtland Sutton]]. Caveats: he turns 27 next month as a rookie after a two-year mission, so Harmon does not see a future star. Koh notes 32 catches on 41 targets (78%) and a role that has climbed from healthy scratch to 68% route participation.
- 2024-11-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-29]]): Vele is the second half of what Harmon sees as [[Bo Nix]]'s real need — a big body who will work over the middle of the field. He frames Denver's receiver room as [[Courtland Sutton]] playing traditional X with Vele as 'the power slot,' and contrasts both with [[Troy Franklin]], whose prospect Reception Perception profile flagged the over-the-middle routes as the ones he could not run because of size, despite Franklin having been Nix's college number one. Harmon references having covered Vele's in-season RP data on an earlier episode.
- 2024-12-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-03]]): Vele is 'a really interesting player' who 'fits so cleanly into that power slot profile' at 6-foot-5, 210 pounds. The caveat Harmon puts alongside it is age — Vele is about to turn 27 in a matter of weeks, which is a very different proposition from [[Xavier Worthy]] being fresh out of college. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) said he might be more intrigued by Vele than by Worthy at this point, and separately argued [[Jordan Whittington]] would be a solid contributor if you swapped him into Vele's role in Denver, though not a game-breaker.
- 2025-01-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-13]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon lightly feels the modern-day Marques Colston framing for Vele in Sean Payton's offense, leaving the door cracked. He notes the oddity of a 27-year-old rookie, and says Vele played well in a big-slot, almost tight-end-ish role and was really good at sensing his way through zone coverage — but is never going to be a downfield press-man beater. Denver has a lot of useful role players in [[Courtland Sutton]]'s orbit, and Harmon liked both what they started doing with [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] and Vele's usage; he does think they still need another genuine needle mover, questioning how long Sutton stays one. He does not expect Vele to command heavy volume, but buys him as a not-embarrassing player in the role. Vele sits in the average band of Harstad's rookie model.
- 2025-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-05]]): Vele is already playing what Harmon calls a poor-man's [[Cooper Kupp]] role in Denver, which is why signing Kupp would likely end the Vele era — Harmon frames him as more of a No. 3 who can give you some power-slot reps rather than a player Denver should displace lightly, and says the Broncos got a nice little pop out of him in that job. James Koh had floated Denver as a Kupp landing spot with Sean Payton designing a hybrid Michael Thomas role; Harmon pushed back, arguing Denver's actual need is a true perimeter receiver, not another interior body.
- 2025-06-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-27]]): Vele was reliable enough in exactly the role [[Pat Bryant]] is competing for, and Waldman still gives the incumbent the edge at roughly 70-75 targets, 50-55 catches, 600-700 yards and five scores. He expects the Vele/Bryant competition to run through the season, so the fantasy play is the last man standing. He notes the age oddity — Vele is the oldest man in the Denver room behind [[Courtland Sutton]] despite being a second-year player, around 27. [[Matt Harmon]] made the [[Romeo Doubs]] argument for him: if Vele were 10% worse you could simply leapfrog him with a third-round rookie, but he was solid as a rookie in a power slot role, which is the same role Harmon sees Bryant fitting. Waldman also cautioned that camp buzz will overweight the vertical game because that is what beat writers can see.
- 2025-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-25]]): Harmon calls Vele a solid player who showed all right in 2024 and does not want to unseat him entirely, but gives the edge to rookie [[Pat Bryant]] as the more fluid player with more pop and far better after-catch numbers for the every-down slot job. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) disagrees and takes Vele as the one Bronco who can be a genuine every-down player — dependent on where he lines up, but if he can play flanker then he holds up in both 11 and 12 personnel, which Koh says would shore up the room considerably, with Bryant, [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] and [[Troy Franklin]] all situational.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-29]]): Denver traded Vele to New Orleans for a fourth and a seventh, which Harmon called a bizarre overpay by a cap-strapped Saints team that should be hoarding picks — Denver turned a seventh-round pick into original value plus a fourth for a player who had merely an okay rookie year, an offer you have to take. On the player: he charted Vele for the in-season rookie report at 64% slot alignment, nothing much against man coverage at a 52.3% success rate, 77.2% against zone — 'fine', a passable big slot. The fit is logical because New Orleans needs size in a room where [[Chris Olave]] and [[Rashid Shaheed]] are tiny, and both hosts noted he turns 28 this year despite being a second-year player after a mission.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06]]): with [[Rashid Shaheed]] gone, New Orleans plans to get Vele more time, and Waldman thinks the target volume alone makes him worth considering even though he doubts it will be quality time with a quality quarterback. He expects mostly underneath usage, and floats that Vele may develop rapport with [[Tyler Shough]] — or, if Shough is bad enough, with [[Spencer Rattler]] again sooner than expected.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-06]]): Vele 'better do something' for New Orleans, since the Saints effectively swapped [[Rashid Shaheed]] for him — they surrendered a fourth and a seventh to acquire Vele in late August and got back only a fifth and a sixth for Shaheed, who Harmon calls a significantly better player. He does find Vele interesting as a big slot and thinks there is a need for that archetype in Kellen Moore's offense, but his evaluation is limited: 'he's like a possession guy at best' who cannot get vertical, less so than even [[Brandin Cooks]] at this stage. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) frames Vele as the more interesting name than Cooks purely on age. Both note the opportunity is real — New Orleans runs 11 personnel 71.5% of the time, third-highest in the league — while agreeing the team is playing out the string chasing the number one pick, so it 'almost doesn't matter.'
- 2025-11-11 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-11]]): after the Rashid Shaheed trade, Vele has become New Orleans' most-used wide receiver, leading the room in both routes run and snaps at 86.6% of snaps, well ahead of [[Chris Olave]] at 53.7% and [[Brandin Cooks]] at 49.3%. Koh flags the snap share as the notable trickle-down from the trade even though the downfield production went to Olave and [[Juwan Johnson]] in this game. Vele ran 24% of his routes from the slot, sitting between Olave (52.8%) and Cooks in alignment. Neither host projects a target-share breakout off it, but the volume of playing time is now clearly established under [[Tyler Shough]].
