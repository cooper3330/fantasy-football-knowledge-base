# Ladd McConkey

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name as "Lad McConkie"/"Lad McConkey"; normalized here to the real name of
> the Georgia receiver, Ladd McConkey. A prior source summary — see
> [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]] — dismissed him only in passing as
> "not actually underrated anymore"; this episode provides the first
> substantive evaluative take, hence the new page.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): the strongest of three early-charted 2024 receiver prospects covered this episode (with [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and [[Keon Coleman]]), despite explicit self-described skepticism going in — Harmon names his own bias against undersized white slot receivers getting overrated off flashy practice-rep highlights (his term: the "Hunter Renfrow corollary") and says McConkey overcame it. Strengths, from a 3-game early sample (explicitly caveated, coming off injury): excellent at selling vertical stems and snapping back on comeback/out routes, working from X, flanker and slot alike — "that is NFL veteran stuff." Comps his game to early-career (pre-triple-crown) Cooper Kupp in the McVay offense, with a real size caveat flagged (Kupp measured 6'2"/205 at Eastern Washington vs. McConkey's 6'0"/185 — "a very long way" apart). Daniel Jeremiah's board has him 44th overall among his top 50 (with 11 total receivers in that top 50), which Harmon reads as a real signal given how many receivers Jeremiah is comparing him against. Alignment/role — outside vs. slot — still an open question.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): good footwork with his release packages, but his hands aren't as capable — he paws at defenders with his hand counters and lacks the violence and timing to win consistently. Restates the Cooper Kupp comp others draw for him, but says Kupp's selection of techniques — which tool to use in a given situation against off coverage or press, before and after the catch — is better than what McConkey currently shows; McConkey "gets hung up" on wonky release choices. Not a fatal flaw, but it currently makes him less versatile and dangerous on the outside than he could become with more dedicated pro-level work; without that development it could cap his outside ceiling, though Waldman expects him to be productive from the slot regardless.

- 2024-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Feel It or F@#k It* ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-01]]), directly weighing him against [[Ricky Pearsall]] (close, back-to-back on his board): grades Pearsall slightly ahead as "the better all-around player right now" because McConkey hasn't yet shown he can release against press coverage using his hands in game action, even though Waldman thinks he likely can. Still expects McConkey to start immediately and, if used heavily in the slot, to be the class's most productive rookie receiver depending on landing spot.
- 2024-04-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-02]]): full-sample Reception Perception profile, superseding the 2024-02-01 early read. Elite testing (RAS 9.33/10, ranking 232nd of 3,107 WRs charted since 1987; 4.39 40, 36" vertical, 10'4" broad jump). Despite the size, not actually a slot receiver — only 26.3% of snaps in the slot (Georgia's primary slot receiver was tight end [[Brock Bowers]]), 65.8% off the line of scrimmage, properly a flanker/outside receiver. Elite success rates versus both zone (83.3%, right with [[Rome Odunze]]'s 83.5% and [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]'s 83.2%) and man coverage (74.6%, right with Odunze, [[Malik Nabers]] and Harrison) — caveated by a lower-than-typical snap/route sample due to 2023 injuries and Georgia's normal receiver rotation. Standout ability to sell vertical routes before snapping off comebacks/outs — grades him a strong candidate for best out-route runner in the class (83.3% success rate on a 13.5% route share). Real weakness versus press coverage (44.4% success rate, 8th percentile), so best deployed off the line with some inside/slot reps mixed in rather than as a true X. Zero drops across the sample. Strong after-catch/tackle-breaking marks — broke multiple tackles on 31.3% of in-space plays (higher rate than Nabers' 20.5%) and evaded first contact half the time — credited to an ability to "downshift" speed (creating separation both in routes and after the catch). Comps him to [[Tyler Lockett]] (same size, ~6'0"/185, plus real return-game/RAC pedigree); co-host James Koh separately floats an Emmanuel Sanders comp. Injury history at his size flagged as a real risk to monitor. Views McConkey as close to Thomas in overall class rank ("WR5 or 6"-range) despite the very different archetypes, and says he wouldn't be bothered by a team taking him in the late first round.
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]) [Dynasty]: flagged real concern that Ladd McConkey posted the lowest success rate vs. press coverage among the class's main profiled players at 44%, which Harmon says effectively caps his ability to play X regardless of scheme -- "he cannot play X" with that number. Still firmly in the best-route-runner conversation behind [[Ricky Pearsall]], but Harmon ultimately ranked Pearsall a spot higher in his personal stack due to superior physicality, release-move variety, and contested-catch ability.
- 2024-04-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-29]]) [Dynasty]: said he 'absolutely flirted' with ranking McConkey his overall #1 post-draft rookie receiver, largely because a guaranteed slot role provides volume in a Chargers offense replacing Keenan Allen. He projects McConkey as instantly the team's best receiver, filling the 'next Keenan Allen' role under Jim Harbaugh/Greg Roman, noting Roman's Baltimore offenses funneled receiving volume through the middle of the field (citing Mark Andrews and Marquise Brown as past beneficiaries). He flagged Quentin Johnston as needing to improve and dealing with a tracking/drops issue, with Cornelius Johnson and Brendan Rice added as competition, while debating whether the run-heavy Harbaugh scheme reaches ~500 pass attempts — though he noted such run-first offenses tend to be highly efficient through the air because defenses must respect the run.
- 2024-05-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-02]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon loved the Chargers trading up to take McConkey at the top of round two after using the No. 5 overall pick on a tackle, calling him from day one 'easily the best separator on the roster' and probably the best receiver on the roster outright, ahead of [[Josh Palmer]] (an 'acceptable WR3') and well clear of [[Quentin Johnston]], whom Harmon says had a disaster rookie year and is 'never gonna work,' comping him to Jalen Reagor. McConkey ranked 4th in the class in man-coverage success rate and 3rd in zone-coverage success rate, with genuinely elite route running and hands, and Harmon calls him the class's best out-route runner — a plus fit with [[Justin Herbert]]'s arm strength on outbreaking throws. His weaknesses are press coverage and contested catches (he's 'a back-shoulder guy, not a contested-catch guy,' owing to route timing). Harmon calls him pro-ready and a Day 1 starter, praising the scheme fit under Greg Roman for year one (crossing routes, motion, middle-of-field work) while predicting the Herbert/Roman pairing eventually hits a ceiling the way the Ravens did with Lamar Jackson. Koh initially argued McConkey wasn't a Bills fit; Harmon disagreed with that specific point but agreed McConkey-to-Chargers was the correct marriage.
- 2024-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (untracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-07]]) [Best Ball]: with no Vegas line yet posted, Koh projects 76 receptions, 912 yards and 7.7 TDs -- one of his more aggressive rookie WR projections behind only the top tier and Malik Nabers -- on a median 110 targets (35% of the team's WR target share). Harmon calls the 7.7-TD figure aggressive but takes the over on a DraftKings line of 775.5 yards, noting McConkey was drafted as the clear best separator on the roster and the Chargers didn't close that gap by adding DJ Chark or Marquise Valdes-Scantling. Koh points to a thin supporting cast (Josh Palmer, Quentin Johnston, an aging Hayden Hurst, seventh-rounder Brendan Rice) after Austin Ekeler's departure. Harmon separately pushes back on the fantasy-community consensus that Jim Harbaugh's Chargers will be extremely run-heavy, questioning whether an improved-but-still-shaky defense (Bosa/Mack retained only via pay cuts, Derwin James coming off a down year, a weak CB room, an unproven backfield led by sixth-rounder Kimani Vidal and an aging Gus Edwards) really supports that plan, suggesting Justin Herbert could throw more than expected.
- 2024-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (untracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-13]]), discussing the wide-open Chargers WR room: Ladd McConkey projects as the clear leader of the group, expected to 'never come off the field' and to lead the team in targets, receiving yards and catches by a wide margin, likely working primarily as a flanker/slot piece in 11 and 12 personnel. [Redraft (Standard)] [Best Ball]
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]) [Best Ball]: Waldman has Ladd McConkey as his WR7 overall and is 'feeling him' as the clear lead receiver in the Chargers' room, citing glowing player/teammate reports out of OTAs and minicamp - Justin Herbert reportedly said McConkey 'plays like a four or five year veteran,' runs great routes and 'understands leverage,' which Waldman calls one of the most telling traits a receiver can show. With Keenan Allen and Mike Williams gone, Josh Palmer viewed as 'a competent contributor but not a competent starter,' and Quentin Johnston still unproven, Waldman expects McConkey to replace much of what Allen did last year in Greg Roman's middle-of-field-heavy scheme, plus RPO and screen usage outside given his sub-4.4 quickness. Waldman says McConkey isn't necessarily the most talented rookie receiver in a stacked class but landed in the best situation for immediate starter production, and that opportunity is his top evaluation filter here.
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: He is pushing McConkey aggressively in his Late Round Draft Guide, writing McConkey has 'top 15 upside' this year even while ranking him outside his own top-36 WRs -- a deliberate high-conviction outlier bet, not a median projection. He argues middle-round rookie WRs are historically strong values and he may be overweight McConkey relative to the market. But he flags real bust risk: McConkey's college profile (elite per-route-run efficiency at Georgia, but only 5th on the team in routes run) mirrors an 'Andy Isabella' downside case as much as a Brandon Cooks breakout, and new OC Greg Roman's run-heavy history (Kaepernick, Lamar Jackson) plus Jim Harbaugh's control-the-clock philosophy could mean fewer two-WR sets and less overall passing volume than the prior Justin Herbert offense. Waldman pushed back that McConkey is a far more refined route technician than Isabella ever was, tempering the bust comp.
- 2024-07-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-15]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Waldman revealed he has Ladd McConkey ranked as a top-15 fantasy WR, calling it evidence of how much he 'feels' the Justin Herbert fit -- new OC Greg Roman likes to throw into the middle of the field, which is where Herbert has always excelled, and McConkey projects as the Chargers' best true slot option given his leverage understanding, pre-snap chemistry, contested/traffic skill on schemed plays (RPOs, screens) and after-the-catch ability on double moves. He compared McConkey's projected role to Cecil Lammey's 'Cooper Kupp type' comp and to Keenan Allen's peak target share under Herbert (a WR2 through 12-13 weeks at times). Waldman dismissed concerns that the Chargers will run more under Jim Harbaugh, noting LA ran the ball only 36% of the time last year (about 38% the two years prior) and would need a dramatic overhaul to reach even the low-40s, so passing volume for McConkey should hold regardless of alignment.
- 2024-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-18]]): Harmon said rookie McConkey 'has a chance to be a really good rookie' and is already 'a really solid separator,' projecting that as the Chargers' offensive line (7th-ranked per guest OL analyst Brandon Thorn, not tracked) and run game mature under Jim Harbaugh, McConkey could eventually anchor true drop-back passing situations for [[Justin Herbert]] alongside [[Quentin Johnston]] and [[Josh Palmer]]. Both hosts framed 2024 as a run-funnel, efficiency-first offense with 2025-26 upside if Joe Alt and McConkey both develop as hoped.
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29 (Feel It or F-It)]]): totally feeling McConkey after he opened camp number one on the Chargers depth chart — Waldman calls it a 'big f-you' to anyone who thought he was not ready. His argument is a scouting-process one: people over-weight McConkey's Georgia production and misunderstand that scouting is about the process that leads to production, which can be suppressed by system, injury or other things outside a player's control. Waldman ranks him a top-ten player at the position. Bob Harris (not tracked) agrees McConkey will be the Chargers' WR1 and that his WR3-range price is reasonable, but says he would rather buy [[Josh Palmer]] at a WR50-something cost and could see Palmer leading the team in receptions — prompting Waldman's Porsche-vs-Toyota-at-the-same-price analogy.
- 2024-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-01]]): McConkey is a full-field separator, not the 'white slot' stereotype — 85th percentile success vs man, 81st vs zone, 69.7% of sampled snaps outside and 65.8% off the line, which Harmon reads as a classic flanker. Route data: 88.9% on curls, 83.3% on outs (13.5% of his charted routes, maybe the class's best out-breaking runner), 81% on slants, 62.5% on nines, and multiple broken tackles on 31.3% of routes in space. Harmon comps him to [[Tyler Lockett]] with a [[Golden Tate]]-ish tackle-breaking streak and notes his start-stop is like [[Zay Flowers]]. Weaknesses: press coverage and contested/high-point situations — develop there and Harmon sees a top-10 ceiling. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) says there is no world where McConkey doesn't lead the Chargers in targets, receptions and yards; Harmon agrees on targets but worries the staff typecasts him as a low-aDOT pop-gun slot, the [[Curtis Samuel]] / [[Elijah Moore]] outcome.
- 2024-09-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: pressed by untracked co-host Bob Harris that his [[Josh Palmer]] target-leader take was 'McConkey slander,' Waldman clarified the opposite — he expects McConkey to lead all Chargers receivers in targets, a call he says he made in May and describes as a hill he is ready to die on. Palmer, in his framing, only leads among outside receivers. Waldman also argued the Chargers' run-heavy reputation is being overplayed as a reason to fade the passing game, given the defense and the division.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): a really good debut in a dismal Chargers passing game — McConkey led the team with seven targets, five catches and 39 yards, and his touchdown, juking both safeties, was 'one of the sickest plays I saw,' the same golden-tape open-field badassery he showed in college. He was third in routes run with 21, behind [[Quentin Johnston]] (22) and [[Josh Palmer]] (26), which Harmon can't imagine holding up; he wants McConkey eating into Palmer's snaps. Alignment split was about 45% out wide, 55% in the slot, versus 92% outside for Johnston. Harmon likes the 13-personnel and single-receiver looks where McConkey can align off the line for a free release or head of steam against man, and expects him to be the play-action target on those. Koh (not tracked) agrees he established himself as the Chargers' No. 1 pass-catching option once the game was on the line.
