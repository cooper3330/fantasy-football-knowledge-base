# Ladd McConkey

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes. (ASR note: the raw transcript renders his
> name as "Lad McConkie"/"Lad McConkey"; normalized here to the real name of
> the Georgia receiver, Ladd McConkey. A prior source summary — see
> [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]] — dismissed him only in passing as
> "not actually underrated anymore"; this episode provides the first
> substantive evaluative take, hence the new page.)

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): the strongest of three early-charted 2024 receiver prospects covered this episode (with [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and [[Keon Coleman]]), despite explicit self-described skepticism going in — Harmon names his own bias against undersized white slot receivers getting overrated off flashy practice-rep highlights (his term: the "Hunter Renfrow corollary") and says McConkey overcame it. Strengths, from a 3-game early sample (explicitly caveated, coming off injury): excellent at selling vertical stems and snapping back on comeback/out routes, working from X, flanker and slot alike — "that is NFL veteran stuff." Comps his game to early-career (pre-triple-crown) Cooper Kupp in the McVay offense, with a real size caveat flagged (Kupp measured 6'2"/205 at Eastern Washington vs. McConkey's 6'0"/185 — "a very long way" apart). Daniel Jeremiah's board has him 44th overall among his top 50 (with 11 total receivers in that top 50), which Harmon reads as a real signal given how many receivers Jeremiah is comparing him against. Alignment/role — outside vs. slot — still an open question.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): good footwork with his release packages, but his hands aren't as capable — he paws at defenders with his hand counters and lacks the violence and timing to win consistently. Restates the Cooper Kupp comp others draw for him, but says Kupp's selection of techniques — which tool to use in a given situation against off coverage or press, before and after the catch — is better than what McConkey currently shows; McConkey "gets hung up" on wonky release choices. Not a fatal flaw, but it currently makes him less versatile and dangerous on the outside than he could become with more dedicated pro-level work; without that development it could cap his outside ceiling, though Waldman expects him to be productive from the slot regardless.

- 2024-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Feel It or F@#k It* ([[Feel It Or F@#k It - 2024-04-01]]), directly weighing him against [[Ricky Pearsall]] (close, back-to-back on his board): grades Pearsall slightly ahead as "the better all-around player right now" because McConkey hasn't yet shown he can release against press coverage using his hands in game action, even though Waldman thinks he likely can. Still expects McConkey to start immediately and, if used heavily in the slot, to be the class's most productive rookie receiver depending on landing spot.
- 2024-04-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-02]]): full-sample Reception Perception profile, superseding the 2024-02-01 early read. Elite testing (RAS 9.33/10, ranking 232nd of 3,107 WRs charted since 1987; 4.39 40, 36" vertical, 10'4" broad jump). Despite the size, not actually a slot receiver — only 26.3% of snaps in the slot (Georgia's primary slot receiver was tight end [[Brock Bowers]]), 65.8% off the line of scrimmage, properly a flanker/outside receiver. Elite success rates versus both zone (83.3%, right with [[Rome Odunze]]'s 83.5% and [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]'s 83.2%) and man coverage (74.6%, right with Odunze, [[Malik Nabers]] and Harrison) — caveated by a lower-than-typical snap/route sample due to 2023 injuries and Georgia's normal receiver rotation. Standout ability to sell vertical routes before snapping off comebacks/outs — grades him a strong candidate for best out-route runner in the class (83.3% success rate on a 13.5% route share). Real weakness versus press coverage (44.4% success rate, 8th percentile), so best deployed off the line with some inside/slot reps mixed in rather than as a true X. Zero drops across the sample. Strong after-catch/tackle-breaking marks — broke multiple tackles on 31.3% of in-space plays (higher rate than Nabers' 20.5%) and evaded first contact half the time — credited to an ability to "downshift" speed (creating separation both in routes and after the catch). Comps him to [[Tyler Lockett]] (same size, ~6'0"/185, plus real return-game/RAC pedigree); co-host James Koh separately floats an Emmanuel Sanders comp. Injury history at his size flagged as a real risk to monitor. Views McConkey as close to Thomas in overall class rank ("WR5 or 6"-range) despite the very different archetypes, and says he wouldn't be bothered by a team taking him in the late first round.
