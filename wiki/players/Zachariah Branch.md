# Zachariah Branch

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.

- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]) [Dynasty]: Branch is explosive enough to eventually play inside or outside, but his route running lacks the refinement to keep him on the field every down at either spot -- he has enough release moves to beat man or set up zone, but doesn't execute them with the patience-and-suddenness contrast Waldman wants. If he doesn't nail down a full-time role, Waldman's downside comp is a Mecole Hardman-type special-teams/gadget-only career.
- 2026-04-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-20]]) [Dynasty]: Asked whether Zachariah Branch's arrest in Athens, GA days before the draft (a $439 bail, a sidewalk/loitering dispute with an officer outside a club) hurts his draft capital, Waldman said 'fuck it' -- no, unless more layers of information surface. He noted police reports involving students and young, overzealous officers on late-night shifts are often vague and open to interpretation, and that the incident reads as a likely misunderstanding rather than real misconduct. He does not expect it to move Branch's stock.
- 2026-04-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman sees decent production potential for Branch in Atlanta given a thin depth chart behind [[Drake London]] and [[Olamide Zaccheaus]], with room for Branch or Zaccheaus in the flanker/slot role. But he calls Branch one of the bigger boom-bust picks of the early rounds, saying his route running has to improve dramatically before defenses will respect him.
- 2026-05-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-05]]) [Dynasty]: Branch landed at pick 79 to Atlanta behind [[Drake London]] and [[Jahan Dotson]], a fine range for a player Harmon calls a legit playmaker despite an unusual profile. Branch ran 28.9% of his routes as screens at Georgia -- the highest screen-route rate Harmon has ever charted for a full prospect, well above the next-closest (Rondale Moore, 20.9%) -- which he says inflates Branch's 81.3% success rate versus zone coverage; Branch's non-screen work against zone is where Harmon felt him 'left wanting the most,' citing a lack of spatial awareness and feel. Versus man coverage he graded 4th-best in the class at 74% success (83rd percentile), just behind KC Concepcion, Carnell Tate and Makai Lemon, with strong dig-route work (83.3% success on 11.6% route share). He was in space on 68% of his catches, broke a tackle on 23% of them, and is stronger than his 177 lbs suggests, per a strong combine bench press. Co-host James Koh (untracked) floated Branch's projected role as creating leverage for Drake London the way Jimmy Horn Jr. does for [[Tetairoa McMillan]] in Carolina. Harmon expects Tua Tagovailoa (stronger underneath, weaker from under center) to start over Michael Penix Jr. for most of the season, which favors Branch's short-area game over a vertical role.
- 2026-05-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-25]]) [Dynasty]: responding to effusive minicamp reports on Zachariah Branch (juke moves and a catch-and-spin drawing Percy Harvin comparisons), Waldman was dismissive of the hype itself -- 'water is wet... Branch is slippery and has great moves' isn't news if you'd already scouted him -- and said the real test is unresolved: whether he can win against live coverage and contact, not in shorts-and-tees or two-man go drills. He flagged the situational opportunity as the more interesting story: a thin depth chart behind the Giants' top receiver, with the rookie quarterback situation (Jaxson Dart) still to be settled as part of what will shape Branch's role.
