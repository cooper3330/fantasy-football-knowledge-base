# Dallas Goedert

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-08-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-26]]): Goedert should be preferred to [[Jahan Dotson]] for targets in a run-heavy offense where defenses pack the box for [[Saquon Barkley]] and the tight end becomes a play-action threat. Bob Harris (not tracked) added the historical case for coordinator Kellen Moore: [[Dalton Schultz]] finished TE3 and TE10 under him in Dallas, and Eagles website reporter Dave Spadaro expects a Goedert-heavier year, with practice reports pointing the same way. Bob Harris said this makes him feel better about his late-round Goedert picks, since Goedert has slipped to roughly the TE10 range in ADP. Neither host raised a health caveat in this segment.
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): Goedert's 10-catch Week 3 explosion was largely circumstantial — it came with [[A.J. Brown]] out and [[DeVonta Smith]] leaving at halftime, and Philadelphia installed slow-developing concepts that would normally have gone to Brown, including a play-action deep post that isolated Goedert on a linebacker and crossing routes that produced yards after the catch (one flipping the field on third down when [[Marshon Lattimore]] and two other Saints defenders collided). Waldman still thinks that even discounting this game entirely, Goedert has a shot at top-12 tight end production the rest of the way, but says he is not in the pecking order in general — the offense funnels to Barkley and the top two receivers when they are healthy, so Goedert is best deployed as a rest week for an underperforming star tight end. Bob Harris (untracked) noted Dave Spadaro and others had predicted a bigger Goedert role under Kellen Moore, citing Moore's use of [[Dalton Schultz]]; Waldman said he is not sure he believes it.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): a Goedert trade during draft weekend is genuinely in the cards, given his lack of durability, his age, and production that is not what it used to be; Philadelphia has everything else it needs offensively and would rather have a week-to-week consistent tight end, with Grant Calcaterra only an okay secondary piece. [[Matt Waldman]] frames it as a contract question — Goedert is effectively a free agent at 31 and the deal can be voided — which is why Philadelphia should be drafting his successor now while it has the luxury of doing so rather than reaching for a blocker later.
- 2025-06-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-30]]): asked whether Goedert is a super value going deep into the TE2 range, Waldman says fuck it as a TE1 but is fine taking him as a TE2 provided you pair him with a promising young tight end likely to get opportunity. The blocker is availability — Waldman projects only 12 games, at close to six targets and just over four catches a game at nearly 12 yards a catch, so roughly 600 yards and a couple of scores, which he calls great for 12 games but not a full season. He adds that Philadelphia's offense is now run-first ([[Jalen Hurts]] threw about 100 fewer attempts than the prior year because of [[Saquon Barkley]]), which may make Goedert's touches more efficient and boost his touchdown rate. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert), who raised the buzz about new coordinator Kevin Patullo, said he will have TE2 shares but might rather have [[Zach Ertz]] in the same range.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-04]]): Goedert is the one Eagles pass-catcher Harris does not think you have to use in Week 1 against Dallas. His case: Goedert has never really evolved past being a 70-catch, 800-yard player, his health was 'kind of a mess' last year, and the Eagles spent the winter signaling loudly that they would have preferred to be entirely without his contract — up to and including threatening to cut or trade him — before he agreed to take less money and return for the last year of the deal. Harris still expects Philadelphia to score points; he just doesn't route that expectation through the tight end.
