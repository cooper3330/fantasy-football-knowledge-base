# Rhamondre Stevenson

## Expert Takes

- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Redraft]: Bob notes Stevenson just signed an extension making him the seventh-highest-paid running back in the NFL and calls him 'the only draftable New England Patriot for redraft purposes' on a team that tied Carolina for fewest points scored last year. Waldman feels great about him at RB20 ADP, expecting New England to lean on a ball-control offense with Jacoby Brissett under center. He praises Stevenson's decision-making, ability to set up blocks, patient outside-zone running (comping him to Mike Anderson and Terrell Davis as backs who didn't need elite speed to excel in that scheme, which fits incoming OC Alex Van Pelt's wide-zone install built around Nick Chubb-style film) and his receiving chops -- capable of 50-60 catches if New England chooses to target him that heavily, though usage could be capped by a committee approach.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14 (Age Cliffs and Dented Cans)]]) [Dynasty]: Stevenson is a dented can — RB21 while attached to a rookie quarterback on what Waldman calls a 'craptastic' offense, with the talent still intact. He can do it all, breaks tackles and makes defenders miss, and Waldman stresses the work ethic behind it: a 2,000-yard juco runner at 250 pounds who had already dropped 15-20 pounds and rebuilt his body before his second Patriots season, and who had to prove to the staff he would do the work. Adam Harstad (NOT tracked) partially disagreed on the framing rather than the player: he always thinks of the 2023 Stevenson-over-[[Josh Jacobs]] debate and did not understand it, since they are the same age and Jacobs has always been more productive — the mileage argument against Jacobs being 'pseudoscience.' Harstad grants Stevenson is the better receiver of the two and a good back who was steamed up prematurely like [[Javonte Williams]] and [[DeAndre Swift]], but says he will not be a star; the bar at the position is being useful, because useful backs get used.
- 2024-12-05 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-05]]) [Dynasty]: Stevenson passing [[Josh Jacobs]] in dynasty value roughly two years earlier was the trade that prompted Harstad's whole workload argument, and he considered it a market error rather than a read on the players. Jacobs had outperformed Stevenson every season to that point and is only twelve days older, so the entire gap came from crediting Stevenson with less accumulated 'mileage'. Harstad's objection is that mileage untethered from age is not information, and that the market was effectively penalizing Jacobs for having been trusted with more work. He did not offer a fresh evaluation of Stevenson's own play here; the point was the pricing mechanism.
- 2024-12-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-23]]): asked whether Stevenson could be a 1,500-yard rusher in a different setting — specifically with an offensive line — Waldman said yes, 'times a hundred or a thousand,' and thinks he can do it in New England if they fix the line quickly, which he concedes is easier said than done. He calls Stevenson versatile and notes he can still run through defenders even after losing 25-30 pounds. Bob Harris (untracked co-host) recalled the Rookie Scouting Portfolio write-up flagging him as a very good receiver with a lot to like, and both agreed much of that has come to fruition; the limiter is supporting cast, not talent.
- 2025-09-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Stevenson is the loser of the New England camp story the show flagged in July. On August 1 [[TreVeyon Henderson]] was not being drafted far ahead of him; by the Labor Day weekend drafts Henderson was going in the fourth round on average and Stevenson had fallen way outside the top 100. Harris presents this as a case where paying attention to the camp storyline genuinely mattered to how you drafted.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): Stevenson keeps a real role and should not be written off for lacking speed. Waldman points to a Week 1 play where Stevenson 'literally jackhammered' the blitzing middle linebacker — his B-minus reenactment of the Jim McMahon-to-Willie Gault play with Walter Payton wiping out the Vikings blitzer — noting it only drew pass interference rather than the catch. What he does inside, plus his pass protection and receiving, keeps him on the field under Mike Vrabel, and [[TreVeyon Henderson]], while decent in protection, does not displace that.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-15]]): asked if Stevenson is underrated, Waldman said absolutely. He pointed to an over-the-shoulder reception downfield on a linebacker and recalled writing in the Rookie Scouting Portfolio what an exceptional receiver Stevenson was coming out of college — but the public only sees a guy shaped like a refrigerator. Waldman noted he is a slimmed-down version now, having been 250 to 270 pounds at one point, and has legitimately worked at his craft and improved every year: a smart runner between the tackles with the best contact balance on the team, arguably the best footwork, and the best decision-making and vision. The limits are real speed and a bad offensive line, and Waldman argued fans demonized him for line failures over the past two years — which is now visible as [[TreVeyon Henderson]] cannot simply take the backfield over on speed alone.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): Stevenson 'was very good' — after roughly 15 rushing yards all of Week 1 he was over 50 by halftime here, and he added a late-third-quarter scramble-drill catch from [[Drake Maye]] down the right sideline, an over-the-shoulder grab Harris reacted to with 'where was this guy the last year and a half,' for about 55 yards to the red zone. He also ran through two defenders on a third-and-13 middle screen for a first down. The caveat is that when he was gassed it was [[Antonio Gibson]], not [[TreVeyon Henderson]], who came in and got the carry close to the goal line.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest Scott Pianowski of Yahoo Fantasy (not a tracked expert) said he did not see this resurgence coming and now ranks Stevenson ahead of rookie [[TreVeyon Henderson]] even in PPR. His skepticism is rooted in the receiving profile: last season Stevenson posted 5.1 yards per reception, 4.1 yards per target and just 168 receiving yards across 15 games with 14 starts — and he is already near 100 receiving yards two weeks into 2025, so he has almost matched a full prior season. Pianowski kept his feet on the ground about eight targets and seven catches being a small sample, but said New England has 'found something with him as a receiver' with Josh McDaniels back in the building and adults in Foxborough again, and he does not see why it would change. In DFS talk Harris noted Stevenson was priced at $5,300, below Henderson, which he called wrong.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Going Deep Listener Q&A)]]) [Dynasty]: an explicit low-key dynasty buy if available cheap. Waldman says people want to bury Stevenson the way they wanted to bury [[James Conner]] years ago, and calls him a worker — asked to lose weight, he lost it, having already reshaped himself from a 250-pound power back at Oklahoma. He can catch and block, which Waldman says [[TreVeyon Henderson]] has not shown yet, and he is a lemonade-from-lemons runner who keeps an offense on schedule in down-and-distance situations, exactly what a defensive head coach like Mike Vrabel appreciates. He notes a route designed for Henderson ended up going to Stevenson and that he did not look bad catching it. The thesis: Stevenson will not be in New England forever, and wherever he lands people will realize he is better than they thought. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees and adds that Vrabel is a make-you-earn-it coach, as he was with [[A.J. Brown]] in Tennessee.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Stevenson fumbled twice against Pittsburgh — a soft one on the Patriots' second play, then, after he had apparently been forgiven with a fourth-and-one read-option conversion from their own 20 and a big screen catch, another one about to go in for a touchdown at 13:17 of the third quarter. He never touched the ball again, playing five more plays, three of them pass protection in scramble mode. Harris read that as a dog-housing and said he would be shocked if this were not the moment New England decides to stop favoring veterans who turn the ball over, in favor of [[TreVeyon Henderson]].
- 2025-09-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest Ben Gretch (Stealing Signals, not tracked) said Stevenson fumbled twice in Week 3, including going into the end zone inside the five, which got him sat down in favor of [[Antonio Gibson]] (who fumbled immediately) and then [[TreVeyon Henderson]]. On top of roughly seven or eight fumbles last year, Gretch called it such a high-profile problem now that he expects New England to put Stevenson in the doghouse for a week or so, which is the main reason Henderson's Week 4 usage could rise. Harris agreed the coaching staff seems to pay attention to and harp on ball security, and used that expectation to justify stacking Henderson in DFS.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Stevenson fumbled on a check-down screen on New England's first play and fumbled again, to the point Harris was 'calculating in my head, do they cut him after this' and assumed he was dog-housed forever — [[Antonio Gibson]] took over as lead back and ran well. But Stevenson was back in on the very next two-back formation, finished with 17 snaps to [[TreVeyon Henderson]]'s 14, and got both goal-line looks in the second half: a short third-quarter touchdown and a fourth-quarter reverse pitch-out from inside the five that he walked in. Harris's summary is sardonic — 'a two-touchdown night for the fumbler' — but the usage point stands: the coaching staff did not remove him from the high-value work despite the fumbles.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): Stevenson's toe injury is the swing factor in the New England backfield — if it turns out to be a real problem, Waldman expects [[TreVeyon Henderson]] to be the back to roll with over the following weeks, with the caveat that the Patriots could instead trade for someone if they do not like Henderson.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-06]]): Harris is assuming Stevenson is out again for Week 10 in his ranks. Guest Ben Gretch (Stealing Signals, not tracked) notes Stevenson did not practice Wednesday and that rumors of the Patriots pursuing a running back at the deadline — a trade they never made — hinted the injury may be less minor than the lack of a stated multi-week timeline suggests. Gretch adds that New England had been preferring Stevenson over [[TreVeyon Henderson]] specifically because Henderson had struggled to find holes and be a success-rate runner.
- 2025-11-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-07]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Stevenson has not practiced at all this week and it seems unlikely he plays against Tampa Bay with a toe injury that Harris thinks 'could be kind of bad' and possibly long-term — though he is not certain Stevenson is 'done, done,' and notes the bigger question is whether the staff trusts him even when healthy. That uncertainty is what makes [[TreVeyon Henderson]] a potential league winner. Harris jokes about Mike Vrabel's motivational streak but the fantasy point is straightforward: the New England backfield is trending away from Stevenson while he is out.
