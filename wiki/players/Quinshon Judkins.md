# Quinshon Judkins

## Expert Takes

- 2024-09-25 — According to guest Felix Sharpe of Campus2Canton (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-25 (Campus2Canton with Felix Sharpe)]]) [Dynasty]: Judkins is cited as Campus2Canton's flagship early hit — a three-star recruit ranked roughly the 82nd back in his class, in [[Nicholas Singleton]]'s recruiting cycle, whom they preferred over the higher-rated Raleek Brown and Emmanuel Henderson at a time nobody else did. Both of those backs have since moved to wide receiver, while Judkins is now, in Sharpe's estimation, one of the top two or three running backs in the upcoming draft class.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10]]): based on last year's tape (he had not yet watched 2024), Judkins has a chance to be a high-end starter but most likely profiles as a [[David Montgomery]] type — closer to a Jamaal Williams or old Kareem Hunt momentum ball carrier, which Waldman describes as a better, more conceptually sound version of [[Tank Bigsby]]. Angelo places him in the second tier of the class alongside [[Ollie Gordon II]] and TreVeyon Henderson, behind [[Ashton Jeanty]] and [[Nicholas Singleton]]. Waldman's broader framing for the class: it is deep, but the question is whether any of these backs can supplant the aging incumbents — [[Derrick Henry]], [[James Conner]], [[Alvin Kamara]], [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Aaron Jones]].
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: his pick for the back least dependent on a specific landing spot. Waldman praises the Ole Miss and Ohio State tape for pass protection that is already close to pro-ready, mobile hips and good footwork, explosion out of cuts, patience and efficiency creating in broken situations, spatial awareness and maturing use of pace for contact balance. As a receiver he is not [[Aaron Jones]] as a route runner but catches leaks, works the screen game and can win one-on-one against linebackers. Waldman thinks the burst concerns are 'a little overblown', pointing to Judkins turning the short corner against Alabama-caliber athletes, and calls him a conceptually smart problem solver against the run blitz. He can be a [[Cam Skattebo]]-style B back or the A back outright. The stated risk is a [[Trey Sermon]] outcome — slower than believed and overthinking — but Waldman does not expect it.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'fuck it' at Underdog RB25 in favor of [[Tony Pollard]] one pick later — but Waldman was explicit the fade is 'not as hard as [[Omarion Hampton]] and [[TreVeyon Henderson]]' and is purely about price, because Judkins is 'one of the safer backs in this class'. His profile: around 6-foot-2 and 215-220 pounds, better burst than people credit plus good speed, productive in both the SEC and the Big Ten, creative, can pass block, and pass-catching that is okay if used on screens and leaks. Comp: a bit more going for him than [[David Montgomery]] in the speed department, maybe not as rugged, but still capable. Waldman noted he has taken no rookies in any best ball or mock draft so far because the ones he was comfortable with were gone before his comfortable price.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: asked to rank Judkins, [[TreVeyon Henderson]] and [[Omarion Hampton]] at 1.02-1.04, Angelo called Judkins the steadiest — you know the role, a bell-cow back in the NFL — and said that if he learns to play slower (he plays like a Tasmanian Devil) he can add angle and option routes and become a multidimensional receiving threat; those movement tools showed more at Ole Miss than at Ohio State, and if they return his ceiling is as high as Henderson's, with a nice floor. He still took Henderson at 1.02 for role clarity. [[Matt Waldman]] likes Judkins more as a pure talent and as a player you can build usage around, noting angle routes and good work at the top of the stem in playoff games while Ohio State used him as a grinder with easy toss and option-toss looks. Waldman's framing: Judkins' role is more variable, possibly a third back who ascends into the No. 2 bell-cow role like [[David Montgomery]] — patient owners may get as good a player with a longer career, but Henderson is the better instant-impact option.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: 'feel it' — Judkins still holds starter value for 2025 despite his domestic battery arrest, unreported status and unsigned rookie deal. Waldman leans on non-tracked legal analyst Drew Davenport's read that it is a misdemeanor with a low bail amount and a genuinely wild-card outcome — anywhere from no season at all to a four-game suspension; anywhere in a six-to-eight-game band still leaves him draftable at the back end. He likes the landing spot: a healthier Browns offensive line, a scheme that fits him, and manageable quarterback play from [[Joe Flacco]] (or better, if [[Shedeur Sanders]] beats him out). Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) adds he is a fine gamble in casual leagues with deep waiver wires. Waldman's caveat is portfolio-dependent: across hundreds of leagues he will buy the ADP dip, but in a single league 'uncertainty is the enemy' and he would rather take the many similarly-priced backs with fewer questions. Judkins had fallen 19 ADP spots in seven days since the arrest became public.
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-15]]): Judkins had his domestic battery charge dismissed, which clears his path to signing a rookie contract, though the NFL immediately announced its own investigation and a suspension remains possible. Harris was pointedly unwilling to treat dismissal as exoneration, noting we do not know anything for sure. He assumes the Browns sign him soon, at which point he will have to pick a place to rank him — and that it would be a big kink in the [[Jerome Ford]] or [[Dylan Sampson]] plan.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-18]]): with charges not filed, Waldman is feeling Judkins as a worthwhile fantasy starter. He rejected the 'Browns were one of the worst running teams last year' argument as backward-looking: they had multiple offensive line injuries, ran from a spread system with a toothless [[Deshaun Watson]] for half the season, then [[Jameis Winston]] and Dorian Thompson-Robinson, and a five-lineman spread with one tight end simply does not create the extra gaps a two-tight-end offense does — and two-tight-end run scheme is Kevin Stefanski's specialty. Waldman believes Judkins is the best back in the group, while conceding [[Dylan Sampson]] is a good young back who could become a nice committee piece, closer to equal than Judkins managers would like in some years. Bob Harris (not tracked) pointed to ADP: RB39 around pick 116, going ahead of [[Tank Bigsby]], [[Najee Harris]], [[Jaydon Blue]], [[Tyjae Spears]], [[Rachaad White]], [[Trey Benson]], [[Austin Ekeler]] and [[Nick Chubb]] — a round-nine-or-ten price for a possible partial-season starter, the same logic Harris uses on Bigsby, though he said he might rather gamble on [[Bhayshul Tuten]] depending on roster construction.
- 2025-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: a brief and deliberately hedged mention, since as of recording Cleveland had not even signed Judkins amid his off-field situation, which [[Chris Harris]] flags as separate from the evaluation. Harris recalls Waldman grading him around the number two back in the class on skills alone. Waldman says the obvious plan was to take him in the second round as the big back who carries the mail with satellite backs around him, and it may still work out that way, but neither he nor Harris has any idea whether it will given the signing limbo. Waldman's broader Browns backfield projection puts Judkins, [[Dylan Sampson]] and [[Jerome Ford]] all in a range of roughly 500-700 yards with four to six touchdowns each — bye-week matchup plays rather than reliable starters.
- 2025-08-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he had still not adjusted his Cleveland backfield ranks, calling himself a hypocrite for acting on Washington but not here. A full week after Judkins got free of the charges, he remained unsigned; Harris said the Browns do not strike him as a team that would restrain themselves on moral grounds given other players they employ, so the delay may mean the NFL is stepping in, noting league leaks that in their eyes the matter is not fully adjudicated. Practically: Judkins should probably get stashed but it is hard to know where without knowing whether he plays at all this year, with a week left in August and no clarity on practice reps or game shape. [[Jerome Ford]] and [[Dylan Sampson]] are worth more if he never signs and less if he does.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): the unsigned standoff is partly a negotiation tactic. Waldman reads Cleveland calling other teams about running backs as pressure on Judkins's agent, with the ongoing NFL investigation giving the club a reason to seek a discount; Judkins has not budged on the offer, so the team is trying to get him to capitulate. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked) tied it to the wider second-round guarantee fight — the slotted money moved after someone above him got a better guarantee, so this may simply be Cleveland resisting fully guaranteed money — and mentioned speculation, which he did not endorse, about a return to college. Harris's fantasy conclusion: by the 12th round Judkins is a 'why not', since if he plays at all you are getting a likely NFL starting running back, and he predicted analysts who overthought him will be breathless about adding him by Week 8 or 9. Harris is not yet there on [[Joe Mixon]], calling that too mysterious.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-09]]): with Adam Schefter reporting Judkins will avoid a suspension and play in Week 2, he is the looming specter over [[Dylan Sampson]]'s good debut — 'Judkins is the moose', so short touchdowns feel likelier to go to him. In the waiver segment Harris says that if you are choosing between [[Bhayshul Tuten]] (57% rostered) and Judkins (55%) in a shallow league, Judkins is easily the bid — while noting he would not want his sister to date him, separating the person from the fantasy call. Guest Jake Ciely of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) agreed on Judkins over Tuten but said he would take Sampson over Judkins.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-15]]): Waldman said he is 'wish casting' that Judkins' offseason legal trouble is a blip on the long-term radar, noting no charges were filed and that it is hard for the NFL to suspend without charges, and describing the situation as more nuanced than it first appeared. On the football, he expects Judkins to prove he is far and away the most talented back in the Cleveland backfield — visible already on very little preparation. He noted the Browns made a concerted effort to draft good citizens this year (an effort he called ironic given the [[Isaiah Bond]] signing). Waldman's biggest takeaway was methodological: Judkins took the bulk of the workload after one full padded practice and a walkthrough, his first practices since early June, which Waldman said throws out his own theory that a player needs August to be ready to play.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the pre-game line that Judkins would only play backup snaps turned out to be a lie if you watched rather than read the box score — he got the first carry, an early carry on the second series, and three straight to start the second quarter. Harris says do not be fooled by the snap count of 35 [[Jerome Ford]] / 19 Judkins / 17 [[Dylan Sampson]]: Ford had a red-zone drop on a screen right before halftime and six of his snaps came in garbage time, while Sampson's touchdown catch came down 41-10. On film Judkins looked like a properly running-back-sized player with power who outran Marlon Humphrey to the edge on a bounce-out, so speed as well. Not attached to a very good offense and the other two are not vanishing, but Harris says he will rank Judkins highest of the three Browns backs for Week 3.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: in an otherwise dire Browns offense, Judkins 'kind of was' thrilling — 33 offensive snaps to four for [[Dylan Sampson]], with [[Jerome Ford]] as the alternative. He was stuck on eight carries for 24 yards at halftime, then converted a fourth-and-one and ripped off back-to-back carries Harris urged listeners to go watch. The 38-yard one was mostly a lane most backs would hit; the better rep was the one before it, where he made Javon Bullard flat-out whiff in the hole — Harris's point is that a clearly powerful, strong back showing that hard cutting ability and acceleration is what should impress. Judkins also powered in Cleveland's only touchdown in driving rain out of an inverted wishbone.
- 2025-09-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-26]]): asked about a listener's chance to swap Judkins for [[Ashton Jeanty]] straight up, Harris says he would do it in a heartbeat — he has no guarantee Jeanty is better than Judkins this year, but thinks the odds are that by season's end Jeanty looks better than Judkins. Andy Behrens (not tracked) said no to the same question. Josh thinks most Jeanty owners will refuse the straight swap.
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-29]]): asked whether Judkins is a fantasy RB1, Waldman is 'not quite ready to go there' — he is comfortable calling him a top-15 and probably top-10 to top-12 back, but Cleveland's margin for error is too tight, since the defense can be taken out of the game and the run game goes with it. He pushes back gently on the workload argument (attributed to Dwain McFarland on X) that Judkins earned usage while trailing: Cleveland trailed by around 13 for most of it and the fourth quarter was shortened by a Khalif Raymond kick return touchdown and turnovers. Waldman credits Judkins for running well behind two reserve-level tackles with both starters out and working reasonably as a leak option in the pass game. Bob Harris (not tracked) is more bullish on the usage — 39 snaps to [[Jerome Ford]]'s 18, more routes and targets — but lands on RB2 with James Conner-style weekly RB1 upside.
- 2025-09-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-30]]): Judkins had a very good day against Detroit and looks like a player — 'more a power slasher than a speed back' — scoring on fourth down from the one after being stuffed and after [[Joe Flacco]] failed on two sneaks. But Harris pushes back directly on Andy Behrens musing on a live stream about whether Judkins is a top-10 rest-of-season back: 'you guys overreact to the moment', and given the situation he is not anything close to top 10. He is a found-money RB2 or flex, and the only Brown you would seriously consider using. Evidence against special traits: a late-third-quarter inside zone where he cut left into a wide-open left side and got only 10 yards, which Harris calls the absolute minimum — [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]-style acceleration makes that a house call.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-02]]): Judkins is Cleveland's second-best offensive playmaker behind [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] and the engine of the only viable game plan for [[Dillon Gabriel]] — stay ahead of the sticks and run it. [[Matt Waldman]] expects them to put the ball in Judkins's belly 25-plus times against a Minnesota run defense he called soft (16th on paper but propped up by facing the Bears and Bengals; the Vikings gave up 25 fantasy points to [[Kenneth Gainwell]], more than [[Bijan Robinson]] or [[Tyler Allgeier]] managed). Waldman's broader point is that clean play action off that run game is Gabriel's route to a Bridgewater-like stretch, and the schedule after Minnesota is favorable.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Judkins was 'impressive' in a hard matchup against Minnesota's defense — 110 rushing yards despite multiple good runs called back for penalties, plus a first-quarter house call that Harris honestly stipulates came behind two holding calls. He credits Cleveland's line for mashing and opening big lanes on the early 32-yard run 'where I think almost anybody could have gotten it,' but insists Judkins made other runs himself: 'absolutely my kind of on-the-field running back,' able to power into a pile, punish and break tackles, with obvious acceleration and stop-foot/go-foot ability. Harris comes away 'believing in Quinshon Judkins probably as an RB1 or that neighborhood.' He is openly uncomfortable praising him this soon after the off-field allegation, saying the discomfort will recede in time the way it did with [[Joe Mixon]].
- 2025-10-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-13]]): 'feeling it' that Judkins is not an RB1 this year, and he has felt that all along despite loving the player. His formulation: Judkins is a very good runner and an RB1-caliber talent, but he is not on an RB1-caliber team right now, and Cleveland trading away its best quarterback was not the way to fix that. Against Pittsburgh the Steelers did what good defenses do to a rookie quarterback — varied blitz looks to confuse him, forcing predictable run situations — then played the run with strong gap discipline, generating push and redirecting Judkins into places he did not want to go and had no choice but to go. Waldman expects him to be an RB1 someday if the Browns get their act together. Co-host Bob Harris (not tracked) said he still started Judkins on volume, roughly 20 carries a game with a 53-plus-yard touchdown called back.
