# Mike Williams

> ⚠️ Takes below are from March 2024. He signed with the New York Jets in
> March 2024 free agency, coming off a torn ACL — team reflects that
> signing, but takes are otherwise historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]): floated as the standard [[Tee Higgins]] is "aspiring to" — a bigger, more physical after-the-catch runner than Higgins, with catch-point ability Higgins is "almost as good" at (if not quite as strong a route runner). Says Williams "could argue... might be a wide receiver one somewhere else," and reads the Chargers' cap situation (needing to clear room, alongside Khalil Mack and Joey Bosa as other possible cap casualties, not tracked pages) as making him a real cut/trade candidate this offseason, with Keenan Allen read as the least likely of that group to be moved. The one flagged downside: "Williams can't stay healthy."

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-19]]: names Williams as the top remaining name on the open wide receiver market once [[Gabe Davis]] and [[Calvin Ridley]] came off the board.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]): reported meeting with Buffalo, plus the Panthers, Steelers and Jets, while still unsigned in free agency. Of the remaining outside-receiver free agents (Williams, Odell Beckham Jr. and Michael Gallup, neither a tracked page), Harmon calls Williams the most interesting despite coming off injury. Notes the Steelers fit is a near-identical archetype overlap with [[George Pickens]] — both contested-catch X receivers without elite separation — while the Jets fit (alongside Garrett Wilson, not a tracked page) and Panthers fit (needing an X after DJ Chark) are read as more logical.

- 2024-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-26]]): reacting to his signing with the Jets to pair with [[Garrett Wilson]]. Coming off a major ACL injury with real availability questions — "17 games would be a damn near miracle" at this point given his age and injury history — but if close to his old form, calls him "a really good quality starting X receiver" who wins vertically and along the boundary in contested situations, fitting Aaron Rodgers's downfield-throwing preference. Frames his real value as freeing [[Garrett Wilson]] to move around the formation (slot/flanker) rather than being pinned to the perimeter as the offense's only true X. Still expects the Jets to address receiver early in the draft regardless — "wouldn't blink" at a receiver 10th overall even after the signing.
- 2024-07-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Coe (co-host, not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Both hosts agreed Mike Williams, turning 30 and coming off an ACL tear (played only 3 games in 2023) on top of an extensive injury history, is the swing factor for the entire Jets receiver room -- a classic X who, at even '90% of peak,' pairs well with [[Garrett Wilson]] the way he once complemented Keenan Allen in LA. Both were skeptical he plays a full, healthy season (the team is reportedly hoping for 20+ games given a playoff push), and if he goes down, the room falls back on a shaky Plan B: [[Garrett Wilson]] forced into X with diminishing returns, [[Allen Lazard]] bumped to flanker, and [[Malachi Corley]] as the lone gadget piece -- 'that's a problem,' per Coe, who also noted the Jets 'aren't good planning' by not adding more depth behind him.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): Williams and [[Russell Wilson]] are 'an absolutely perfect match' — that is precisely why Pittsburgh acquired him. Williams is an excellent ball tracker and very physical, a receiver who wins in tight coverage and can occasionally get downfield to win moon balls, and he benefits enormously from Wilson's loft on deep throws; big, strong receivers who can come down with the ball make it much harder on defensive backs when the quarterback throws with that touch. Waldman contrasts him explicitly with [[Van Jefferson]], who he says does not track the deep ball well — he nearly posted a clip of a bad tracking rep from this game. With [[George Pickens]] outside and [[Calvin Austin III]] (hurt late in this game) working across the middle and catching on the run, Waldman sees a really nice three-receiver combination once Austin is healthy enough to return. He scored in this game after Austin's injury opened snaps.
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]): Harmon thinks it is pretty much over for Williams. After the touchdown in his first game back made it look interesting, he essentially has not played since — though Harmon noted, unlike [[Diontae Johnson]], Williams is not quitting on the team to get there. He added that Pittsburgh, unlike Baltimore, actually does need receiver help.
- 2025-03-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-21]]): on Williams returning to the Chargers for up to $6 million on a mostly incentive-laden deal, Harmon frames him as a part-time sacrificial X — he knows the offense, he can absorb the hard perimeter snaps in 11 personnel so [[Quentin Johnston]] can slide to flanker and [[Ladd McConkey]] to the slot, but he is not a full-time player and Harmon jokes the problem is Williams is going to play about four games. His ideal outcome is the Chargers drafting an X who learns under Williams for four weeks and then takes the job.
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): reports that Williams is a favorite of [[Justin Herbert]] are ones he is 'feeling' — the two already had rapport, and the Chargers had no real contested-catch receiver last season, since [[Quentin Johnston]] was a contested-catch winner in college but is not one for the pro game because of his hands and ball-tracking problems. Waldman expects Williams to start at 'X' as long as he is healthy enough to go, and cites Herbert's line that 50-50 balls are not quite 50-50 with Mike. He frames him as a late-round veteran worth considering rather than a weekly starter: not an every-week fantasy guy, but someone who will deliver some good weeks. He notes Williams made a big play with [[Russell Wilson]] in Pittsburgh but that the Steelers offense was not really a fit.
- 2025-06-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-27]]): Williams looks likely to open the season as the Chargers' X at age 31, but the optimistic case is only about 13 games, ~70 targets, 40-45 catches, close to 600 yards and four touchdowns — and the reminder that [[Quentin Johnston]] became the starting X as a rookie precisely because Williams lasted three and a half games. The bigger drag is scheme: under Jim Harbaugh and Greg Roman the Chargers threw the ball 7% less than the prior staff and are still not at a 50/50 run-pass split, so Waldman warns against wish-casting a [[Justin Herbert]] fantasy leap. He projects roughly 1,100-1,200 yards and about eight touchdowns split across Williams, Johnston and [[Tre Harris]] combined — one good player's worth, spread three ways.
- 2025-07-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-18]]): Williams informed his agent he is retiring after eight NFL seasons and 330 receptions, 5,100 yards and 32 touchdowns. Harmon argues Williams was underrated as a separator relative to his contested-catch typecast — three charted seasons between 67% and 69% success rate vs man coverage and four seasons between 74% and 79% vs zone — because pure contested-catch-only receivers do not last as long as he did as a starter. Still a 'live by the sword, die by the sword' X receiver whose injuries caught up with him, and Harmon says counting on him for anything in 2025 beyond an early-season placeholder for [[Tre Harris]] was always a bad idea after he fell out of favor with both the Jets and Steelers in 2024. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) framed the career as 'an exciting player'.
