# Marvin Mims Jr.

> ⚠️ Takes below are from early January 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by
> newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-04]]): had Mims ranked 13th overall pre-draft (just outside his top tier, alongside [[Jayden Reed]] at 14th) and thinks the Broncos underused him as a rookie — "why aren't they playing him more?" Projects he can deliver at least what [[Jordan Addison]] has already shown, and notes he's exactly the deep-threat archetype that normally thrives in a Sean Payton offense (comps: Devery Henderson, Lance Moore), though he flags unspecified "friction" between the coaching staff and the passing game that has kept it from clicking so far.
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: Harstad's [[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] came in almost exactly average on efficiency — lower than expected given some big early-season plays, dragged down by a low-volume Denver passing offense (513 attempts all year) that couldn't get him consistent snaps. Harstad calls the sample "the smallest and least compelling" in the class and treats it as barely worth noting; Waldman pushes back with a specific mechanism rather than a vibe — Sean Payton has historically played rookies plenty (Marques Colston, Kenny Stills, Lance Moore all got early run), so the "friction" flagged in January is more likely a personnel decision (Courtland Sutton/Jerry Jeudy ahead of him) than a scheme mismatch.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): a sharply
  more negative update than January's — "the RP data is not looking good,"
  with his rookie film described as "a disaster in year one... nonfunctional
  as an NFL receiver," largely limited to occasional clear-out routes and
  designed touches. Grouped in "the [[Quentin Johnston]] territory" as a name
  the Broncos need real outside insurance behind, not a receiver they can be
  "cool with" as their number two.
- 2024-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-01]]): Mims's in-season rookie charting was rough and explains why he never played — second-worst against man, third-worst against zone and second-worst against press in the rookie report, behind only [[Quentin Johnston]] and by a comfortable gap over [[Jonathan Mingo]]. He cleared a 50% snap share in only two games despite splash plays. Harmon says Mims was almost exclusively a speed slot in wide-open Oklahoma offenses and that his intriguing success rates were athleticism-based rather than nuance-based; he was pretty decent on corners and posts (75% on both at roughly league-average rates). Harmon's ceiling framing is a 'very, very poor man's [[Brandin Cooks]]' under Sean Payton, and he stresses Mims was simply not a functional receiver as a rookie. He notes a Ben Albright report that Mims is in line to be a starting receiver, and says he would not want Mims as a featured piece; co-host James Koh (not tracked) is one of the many who wants to get excited about him.
- 2024-08-27 — According to host James Koh (not a tracked expert) and guest Derek Klassen (not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): both are baffled that Mims cannot get on the field, with [[Tim Patrick]] — who has not played football in two years — operating as Denver's WR2. Koh stresses this is not a holdover pick but a Sean Payton pick, high draft capital spent on a player the staff scouted and wanted, and says he genuinely expected the system to work: Mims as the Robert Meachem / Devery Henderson role, working the slot, running curls and comebacks and then getting hit over the top on the deep seam. Klassen's read is the same — Payton saw the Meachem/Henderson archetype and it simply has not turned out to be another one. Left unresolved and openly frustrating to both hosts.
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): Mims 'didn't do anything as a rookie' and Denver is briefing that he will not have a big role, which Harmon calls incoherent given they simultaneously cut [[Tim Patrick]]. His stance is that a non-competing Denver team has no excuse not to play him — 'what are you doing if you're the Broncos and you're not trying to see what your young draft picks have at this point?' In the Sean Payton positional template Harmon sketches, Mims would be the Devery Henderson field-stretcher behind [[Courtland Sutton]]'s big X role, though he concedes the comparison is a stretch. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) is blunter, saying the reporting amounts to Denver telling you 'Marvin Mims is a nothing' while leaving no one else to play.
- 2024-11-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-21]]): Harmon reiterates that Mims's rookie-year route running 'sucked' — he was just not a good route runner, as covered in the rookie report — and says Denver has essentially given up on him as a real receiver and converted him into a gadget player. Evidence: six rush attempts across the last two games without his snap count trending up. Harmon notes Sean Payton has been saying since Mims's rookie year that they need to find ways to get him the ball, and that they have finally found one and it is working pretty well. The framing matters for projection: this is manufactured touch usage rather than an expanding route role.
- 2024-11-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-26]]): Mims is a nice gadget player for Denver rather than a conventional receiver. Harmon says the fantasy community memed [[Sean Payton]]'s 'we have to find ways to get Marvin Mims involved' talk to death, but Payton genuinely did have to manufacture touches because Mims' rookie-year route running was disastrous and is not something he can rely on yet — maybe he develops into it. What works is the contrived version: a target out of the backfield into wide-open space behind blocking from Denver's big receivers. Harmon frames the ceiling bluntly, noting Mims made the Pro Bowl as a kick returner and joking the plan is to make him a kick returner on offense too.
- 2024-12-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-30]]): yes, Mims is Sean Payton's next Lance Moore. Waldman's coming-out comp was Derrick Mason — a do-it-all receiver with enough speed to play split end if needed and a good route runner, but more naturally a flanker/slot option. He argues Mims can be moved around the formation profitably because he wins deep, wins after the catch and is a good ball winner; Denver simply could not find a way to maximize him earlier, and this season it clicked. Payton is excited about him, he is stringing together games making plays, and he is quickly becoming a [[Bo Nix]] favorite. Waldman is enthused but adds a price caveat: this is an under-the-radar player a lot of fantasy analysts will pump as a value, so he may end up slightly overdrafted.
- 2024-12-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-31]]): a genuine revision upward. Harmon called Mims's rookie route running 'a disaster' — 42.3% success rate vs man and 36.4% vs press, with 42% of his charted routes being straight go routes, 68% lined up outside and only a 23% slot rate. The change is deployment, not a new skill set: since Week 12 he is 43% out wide, 38% slot and 20% in the backfield, with 334 receiving yards, barely behind [[Courtland Sutton]]'s 336 despite ranking only fourth on the team in routes. Koh adds that over the last six games he averages 4.7 targets, 3.8 receptions, 63 yards and has four touchdowns with an 82% catch rate, after zero touchdowns in the first ten weeks. Harmon credits growth at the catch point and more decisiveness, and says his college profile (72% success vs man, wins on posts, nines, corners, slants and flats; poor on curls, comebacks and digs) matches the speed-slot role [[Bo Nix]] and Sean Payton now ask of him. Both flag a year-three breakout as the thing to watch, with Harmon's caveat that he does not know Mims's ceiling and is not calling him a future superstar.
- 2025-01-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-07]]): following up the previous episode's segment, Harmon said Mims was cooking in exactly the ways they had described, even allowing that it came against a Kansas City team resting starters. He grouped Mims with [[Quentin Johnston]] as a player who had a disaster-level rookie year and has become a certifiably useful contributor — not a future superstar or necessarily a high-quality starter, but no longer someone you are embarrassed to have on the field. Harmon then used Mims as an argument against Denver trading for [[Tyreek Hill]]: Mims is a cheaper, younger, non-malcontent version of the same speed/vertical role Sean Payton would use Hill in, so the Broncos arguably already have that player.
- 2025-01-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-13]]) [Dynasty]: on whether Mims is a modern-day Lance Moore, Harmon says he is feeling the result but not the details of the comparison — he was too young to have charted Moore's prime. He does not mince words that Mims' rookie film was a disaster, and [[Matt Waldman]] agrees it was awful, both attributing it to Denver trying to make him a real all-purpose receiver. Harmon credits Sean Payton for saying fuck it and simplifying: stop asking him to work back to the football, put him on pure runway routes — posts, gos, corners, slot fades — and add roughly 20 percent of his snaps out of the backfield on flares and swings, where his athleticism makes plays in space. On that usage Harmon buys a lot of what Mims did in the back half of his rookie season and projects him developing into a nice WR3 over the course of his career.
- 2025-06-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-27]]): Mims is the gadget piece in Denver — a Lance Moore comp but less technically sound, a player Sean Payton has to scheme into catchable positions because he is not a man-to-man route runner at a high level. Waldman doubts he clears 60 targets in that role and would be surprised by 700+ yards, though five or six touchdowns and big plays are live. [[Matt Harmon]] backed this with charting: Mims has one of the two most bizarre route charts in a ~500-player Reception Perception database going back to 2014 — the highest or second-highest screen rate ever charted at 24.4%, the sixth-lowest success rate versus man coverage in the database, and 30.7% of sampled snaps aligned in the backfield, blowing past the previous high of 18.6% set by [[Deebo Samuel]] when Deebo was taking running back snaps. Nothing in the middle of the route tree.
- 2025-07-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-09]]): in passing, Harmon says Mims is not really being used as a wide receiver — he is one of only two players out of roughly 50 charted so far in 2025 with a lower dig-route rate than [[Garrett Wilson]]'s 7.5%, and is running screens on about 24% of his sampled routes. Harmon flags the forthcoming Mims profile as possibly the weirdest Reception Perception has ever charted and promises a full breakdown on a later show.
- 2025-07-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-16]]): Mims produced 'one of the stranger profiles in Reception Perception history' in 2024 — 52 targets, 39 catches, 503 yards, six touchdowns on a role Harmon calls an innovative attempt by Sean Payton to put a kick returner on offense. He faced zone on 79.7% of routes (second most ever) and ran screens on 24.4% (second most ever), both behind only 2021 [[Rondale Moore]]; he took 30.7% of snaps from the backfield, easily the RP record, nearly double [[Deebo Samuel]]'s 18.6% in 2022. It worked because he is elite in space: 80.3% of sampled catches were in-space attempts and he went down on first contact just 44% of the time, generating layup checkdowns for [[Bo Nix]]. But the route chart is green deep, green on screens and red in the middle — 5.7% slant participation is the lowest in RP history, 1.6% dig participation third lowest behind [[Chase Claypool]] and DeSean Jackson, zero comebacks, and no in-breaking route at NFL average. His 44% success rate versus man is first percentile. Koh (not tracked) is holding out hope, pointing to a 72% man success rate and 80% on slants in Mims's Oklahoma prospect profile; Harmon counters that those slot reps in a spread Lincoln Riley offense did not translate, which is why he graded Mims a day-two prospect, and doubts there is more than this role-catered version.
- 2025-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-25]]): Harmon explicitly does not expect Mims to be the Bronco who emerges alongside [[Courtland Sutton]], calling his one of the weirdest Reception Perception profiles of all time. He is a gadget player, not a route runner — a 44 percent success rate versus man coverage is a first-percentile mark. Denver ran a screen on 24.4 percent of his sampled routes, the second-most in Reception Perception history, and he took 30.7 percent of sampled snaps from the backfield, the most in RP history (number two is [[Deebo Samuel]] at 18.6 percent), though not on between-the-tackles work — mostly flares and checkdowns. Harmon credits the creativity and says he was genuinely good with the ball in space, joking he was used like a kick returner on offense: screens and flats, or long down the field on nines, corners and posts, where his nine success rate was solid and his corner rate very good. What is missing is the dig-and-curl meat of a full-time route tree. Harmon classes him as situational.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-25]]): he is 'feeling' that Mims is nominally the Denver WR2 behind [[Courtland Sutton]], but says the label is close to meaningless. Waldman notes Mims's route profile is one of the strangest [[Matt Harmon]]'s [[Reception Perception Methodology]] has charted, and that Denver had to work hard last year to manufacture offense for him, using him out of the backfield rather than as a regular receiver. He expects [[Evan Engram]] to be the actual No. 2 pass catcher, [[Pat Bryant]] possibly the No. 3 pass catcher while nominally WR3, and [[Troy Franklin]] potentially overtaking Mims outright — so calling anyone the WR2, as Sean Payton has, is a crapshoot not worth chasing in fantasy. Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT tracked), heavily invested in [[Bo Nix]], holds late-round Mims shares but says he will not add more and will focus on Sutton and Engram; he rates Mims's playmaking as a [[Best Ball]] asset more than a redraft one.
- 2025-08-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Mims apparently left practice with a groin injury, though the Broncos are so far saying it is okay. Harris relays it as a wait-and-see item rather than a downgrade.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-15]]): Mims caught a terrific one-on-one end-zone touchdown from [[Bo Nix]], but ran only 13 routes while [[Troy Franklin]] went eight for 89 and was the receiver on the field with [[Courtland Sutton]] in two-receiver sets. Harris says it looked like [[Sean Payton]] was finally trying to make a receiver 'a thing' the way listeners have always hoped he would with Mims — and it was Franklin, not Mims. He will not predict the future, but on what this looked like, if he were drafting today he would probably take Franklin over Mims.
- 2025-09-16 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: asked whether he would drop Mims for [[Troy Franklin]] going into Week 3, guest John Daigle (Establish The Run, not tracked) said yes and revealed he had already put Mims on his unofficial cut list the week before — Mims made a big play in Week 2 and it counts, and he is a good player, but how much more evidence do we need that Sean Payton does not view him as the guy? Harris agreed with the swap. Both note Payton constantly shifts his receiver and running back rotations, and Daigle is not certain Payton views Franklin as the guy either.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Mims got open deep at least twice against the Chargers — on a third-and-19 flea flicker and on a deep post out of the slot that should have been an easy touchdown — and [[Bo Nix]] overthrew him both times. The separation is there; the connection is the bottleneck, which Harris attributed to Nix's arm and touch on deep throws rather than to anything Mims did.
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-10-09]]): Mims has not been removed from the gadget role he held last season — all go routes, long balls and underneath gadget touches — and because Denver is trying to get the run game going they are not manufacturing those created touches for him, which Harmon called a problem for his fantasy consistency. He is third on the team in route participation at 52%, behind [[Courtland Sutton]] and [[Troy Franklin]], and Harmon explicitly says he is not a true slot, which is why he thinks there is still room for [[Pat Bryant]] as the big slot.
- 2025-10-23 — According to Scott Pianowski (untracked Yahoo analyst) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-23]]): both ranked Mims just behind [[Troy Franklin]] and outside their Week 8 top 50, preferring Franklin's goal-line role and the Dallas matchup, though Harris noted Mims was close enough that he nearly included him in his own top 50.
- 2025-12-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-30]]), revisiting last year's film-love list: Harris said he 'hasn't changed his mind' about Mims -- Denver still uses him mainly as a gadget piece and returner rather than a true target earner, which he called super fun to watch but reiterated was never meant as a projection of fantasy stardom.
- 2026-01-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and untracked co-host James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-27]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Mims had one 52-yard gadget/deep-shot play against New England and only 10 yards the rest of the game, which the hosts said is exactly his profile -- either schemed touches underneath or a straight 'moon ball' go route down the field, with little in between. Koh said he 'wouldn't be penciling in any more' for Mims than a role as a gadget player and returner, adding that Denver's own usage of him this season suggests they see him the same way, and noting his best game of the year actually came earlier in the season against Buffalo rather than anything recent.
- 2026-04-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: Harris was initially skeptical the [[Jaylen Waddle]] trade would boost value elsewhere in Denver, but co-host Andy Behrens (not tracked) pointed out Mims had 25 catches of three air yards or less last year, arguing the Sean Payton offense could funnel a lot of short, high-percentage targets to its top options the way it seemingly wants to with Waddle. Harris conceded the point but wondered aloud whether Mims instead becomes a 'full-time special teams guy' or is phased out now that Waddle has arrived, calling his outlook murkier than before the trade.
- 2026-07-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: like [[Troy Franklin]], Waldman says Mims never became the WR1 Denver hoped for -- that role now belongs to newly-acquired [[Jaylen Waddle]] -- but he could still pop for occasional big plays given the defensive attention Waddle, [[Courtland Sutton]] and [[Evan Engram]] will draw, even if he's mostly a bye-week fantasy option.
