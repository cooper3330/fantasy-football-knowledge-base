# Courtland Sutton

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February–March 2024. Team/role are as of that date
> and are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until
> superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-19]]): a standing negative grade, delivered as supporting context for a [[Russell Wilson]] discussion — "always thought was overrated as a player." Specific technical knock: not a great route runner, the kind of receiver "you've got to throw five balls for him to catch three." Grouped with [[Jerry Jeudy]] as a receiving duo Waldman ranks below roughly 25 other teams' top-two options, and part of why he's reluctant to pin Denver's 2023 passing-game struggles entirely on Wilson.
- 2024-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-11]]): revisits the grade the day [[Russell Wilson]] officially signs with Pittsburgh — still "a good contested catch player," but "you kind of have to throw him for every five balls that you throw him that are accurate, he's probably going to drop two of them, maybe three," and grades [[George Pickens]] as the more talented of the two receivers Wilson has thrown to.

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a
  tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-28]]): "a good
  starter," a "solid starting outside receiver in the league," but "if he's
  your number one, I don't think it's fine — I think you want something a
  little higher ceiling." Ranked below [[George Pickens]] and roughly
  even-with-or-below [[Christian Kirk]] in the "disaster-adjacent" tier.
- 2024-04-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-22]]) [Best Ball]: if Sutton stays in Denver with a rookie QB, projects him as a target-hog WR4/5 in best ball — 'the black hole power forward you've got to keep feeding the ball to' — capable of hot stretches and touchdown spikes despite inconsistency.
- 2024-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-01]]): Sutton is a good starting X but not a true No. 1 — Harmon can get comfortably to about 35 receivers in his real-life tiers before considering him, and would not run a passing offense through him. The profile is lopsided: a 93.3% slant success rate in 2021 with the rest of the route tree unimpressive, a downfield ball-winner rather than a separator, and by mid-October 2023 he had 123 of 351 receiving yards on slants, ins and shallow crossers per Fantasy Points data. Harmon concedes some of the downfield picture may be a Russell Wilson artifact. Both hosts flag age 29 and a restructured contract, and Harmon says receiver aging is unpredictable; co-host James Koh (not tracked) adds Sutton has never been quite the same since his ACL and that his game is not aging well, despite 10 touchdowns on 59 catches and 90 targets for 772 yards in 2023. Harmon expects everyone to end 2024 higher on [[Ladd McConkey]] than Sutton.
- 2024-08-27 — According to guest Derek Klassen (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): calls the Denver pass-game fit weird, because Sutton (like [[Josh Reynolds]] and [[Tim Patrick]]) is at his best on contested 50-50 balls and tough throws, and that is exactly what [[Bo Nix]] is least inclined to attempt — Nix does not tend to throw it up and trust his guy. Klassen allows a world where having a room full of that archetype opens Nix's eyes to just throwing the ball at highly paid receivers and letting them make a play, but wants to see it before getting excited. He also frames a Sutton return to 'true number one' play as one of the conditions for the offense reaching its realistic Teddy-Bridgewater-with-legs ceiling.
- 2024-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-29]]): Sutton is the lone real receiver on a Denver depth chart Harmon considers one of the five worst wide receiver rooms in the NFL after the [[Tim Patrick]] cut, and he flags that this is 'an aging Cortland Sutton' — if anything happens to him the room collapses, and he says 'good luck, [[Bo Nix]].' Harmon sketches a Sean Payton template where Sutton is the big X receiver, the role Payton used [[Michael Thomas]] in, with [[Josh Reynolds]] alongside him and Denver otherwise needing to find out what its young draft picks are. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) agrees Sutton and Reynolds are the only starters he can identify and calls the whole roster construction incoherent. Harmon thinks Denver 'could end up being the worst offense in the league,' with the personnel — not the coaching or a roughly league-average offensive line — being the problem.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Harmon is selling on anything Broncos-related for the rest of season. He says he does not feel good about Denver, and not just because of [[Bo Nix]] — watching Sean Payton's offense he does not see it working in today's league, with no real answers for pushing the ball downfield, a heavy, slow, unexplosive unit. On Sutton specifically he says he has always thought he is fine, he thinks he is fine, but he is not a true number one receiver, so he sells the whole thing.
- 2024-10-31 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-31]]): at this stage of his career Sutton is a number two receiver, not a true number one — Angelo says [[Devaughn Vele]] might actually be Denver's best receiver, which he calls 'not great', and dismisses the rest of the room ([[Troy Franklin]] and others) as 'just dudes'. Both expect Denver to retool the receiver room this offseason or possibly trade at the deadline, and Angelo says once [[Bo Nix]] gets a true number one option he thinks Nix takes off. [[Adam Trautman]] is name-checked by Waldman as an intriguing long-time favourite at tight end.
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): Waldman is sharply critical of Sutton's hands, saying he 'clap-attacks everything' rather than making the tough catches a number one receiver should make. His view is that [[Bo Nix]] would be a 300-yard passer far more often if Denver had a receiver who caught what a WR1 catches, and that Denver could then use Sutton as a good WR2. He uses Sutton as the benchmark for bad hands elsewhere in the episode — [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]] has 'worse hands than Courtland Sutton or maybe Gabriel Davis,' and [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] clapped a drop 'better than Courtland Sutton even could.' See [[Catch Technique and Ball Tracking]].
- 2024-11-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-21]]): Denver has only one proven receiver and has deliberately made Sutton the number one target — he has 30% of the targets since Week 8 and is clearly the first read, with the play design essentially forcing chemistry with [[Bo Nix]]. Harmon likes him specifically as Sean Payton's X because he is good on in-breaking routes: people picture Sutton as a big contested-catch, low-separation receiver, but even his down years in Reception Perception show strong in-breaker work, and Harmon has joked about his slant rate making him 'the new [[Michael Thomas]]' — those slants and digs are base routes in this offense. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) pushes back that Sutton is a declining, aging player who can no longer run a go route and win over the top the way he could five years ago, and that Denver has had to cater the play calls to his current athletic profile.
- 2024-11-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-29]]): Sutton is tenth in receiving yards and on pace for a career-high 81 catches, about 1,054 yards, seven touchdowns and a 60% catch rate at age 29, which Harmon calls one of the two most surprising quarterback-receiver connections of the year alongside [[Kirk Cousins]] to [[Darnell Mooney]]. He argues the mocked story of Sean Payton making Sutton study [[Michael Thomas]] film was right: even in a bad 2021 profile where Sutton graded 21st-percentile vs man, his slants hit 93.3%, and his 2019 profile showed 80.4% on slants and 85.2% on digs. Payton leaned into that micro detail where previous staffs did not, and the in-breaking, big-body-over-the-middle role is exactly what [[Bo Nix]] lacked in college, where [[Troy Franklin]] was worst on those very routes. Harmon flags that Sutton is not the same player he was pre-ACL — 2021 and 2022 were rough, and both hosts recall trade-, cut- and day-three-pick-dump talk — but says he has gotten healthier and is now a perfect scheme fit alongside [[Devaughn Vele]]. Ranked fifth of six on the surprise list, partly because the quarterback is a rookie.
- 2025-01-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-14]]): Sutton went 5 of 9 for 75 yards in the 31-7 loss to Buffalo and remains 'that guy' for Denver, but Koh calls the situation fragile — given his age and injury history, if Sutton is off the roster or goes down, Koh says it is 'a very ugly looking room.' Harmon agrees Denver needs another high-volume pass catcher, suggesting a tight end, and a significant investment at running back, since [[Troy Franklin]], [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] and [[Devaughn Vele]] are interesting but none is a number one. Both hosts thought Sean Payton called an oddly conservative second half with [[Bo Nix]] despite having been a needle-moving play caller all season.
- 2025-02-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-05]]): Sutton is clearly Denver's best wideout and is probably underpaid relative to the production he gave them in 2024 — Harmon says an extension is something the Broncos should be thinking about, and that contract question is one reason he does not love the idea of Denver spending on [[Cooper Kupp]]. He calls Sutton a classic X receiver. Harmon adds that if he were Denver he would still be hunting a true perimeter presence to complement him, since [[Devaughn Vele]] is a power-slot type, [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] is a move-around gadget player and [[Troy Franklin]] is unproven there.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]) [Best Ball]: Sutton at a WR30 ADP is a 'feel it' after finishing around WR12 in 2024. Waldman is blunt that he does not like the Broncos and has long criticized Sutton's hands technique, but says Sutton played well, is in a good offense, and can build on last season — he was a talented player coming out of the draft who needed to clean up his technique, and it is now good enough. Bob Harris (not tracked) adds the Sean Payton and [[Bo Nix]] continuity into a second year as part of the case.
- 2025-06-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-27]]): Sutton remains Denver's clear leading receiver and Waldman projects 70-80 catches, 1,100-1,200 yards and six to eight touchdowns, with everyone else orbiting him. [[Matt Harmon]] quantified the orbit: Sutton ran a route on 90.5% of Denver's dropbacks last season and no other Bronco was above 49%, and he expects the same shape in 2025. Waldman separately used Sutton as the reference for [[Tre Harris]]'s hands profile — a receiver who fights the ball and will give you a share of drops but also a share of big plays.
- 2025-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-25]]): Denver in 2024 was Sutton and everybody else orbiting around him — he ran a route on 90.5 percent of dropbacks and nobody else on the roster, receiver, tight end or back, crossed 49 percent, with Jordan Humphrey third. Harmon calls him a very good starting X and a legitimate number one receiver who would probably be better cast as a two; he is really good against man coverage but not a great zone beater, and he caught 81 balls as the offense's clear focal point. Harmon credits him for finally coming back well after tough post-ACL years and notes his full profile lands on the site July 28. The open question Harmon poses for 2025 is whether anyone else emerges as a feature player or whether the orbit structure simply repeats — and after walking the room, both he and Koh conclude it sounds like the latter.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Sutton was Denver's engine when the offense finally moved — a comeback route and a deep slant on the drive that got them going, an easy touchdown into stride on a fourth-and-two when the Chargers blew the coverage, a fourth-and-one conversion where he was face-masked and tackled short, and a wide-open deep shot [[Bo Nix]] lofted too far for him to run under. Harris's read: the volume and the openings are there, but the quarterback play is capping the payoff.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Sutton 'saved Bo Nix's tushy many times' in Denver's comeback from 17-3 and was 'an absolute baller in this game.' Harris says he loved Sutton pre-knee-injury and spent years insisting post-injury that he was still really good; it has clearly taken years, and he is not a burner who will outrun people, but he is a really good player. Guest **Sam Gold** (NOT a tracked expert) called Sutton the main reason for the comeback and singled out route variety as the standout trait: unlike a [[DK Metcalf]] slant-and-fade type with three routes, 'he ran literally every route on the field' — sit routes over the middle, fades from the slot, outs, ins — which Gold reads as Sean Payton wanting him involved on premium looks. Eight catches on 10 targets for 99 yards.
- 2025-10-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-07]]): Sutton beat Quinyon Mitchell — whom Harmon rates among the league's better corners this season — for six catches on eight targets and 81 yards when matched on him, including 64 fourth-quarter yards, finishing 8-of-10 for 99. Koh admits he did not think Sutton was playable in that matchup and was wrong. Harmon's read: Sutton is 'the classic X', one of the few receivers who has not been moved around, and Sean Payton's playbook makes the X the number one read — the same reason [[Michael Thomas]], remembered as a power slot, played so much X in New Orleans. He usually charts as a dig/post winner; this game was more back-shoulder and quick out-breakers off a vertical sell, and he is a genuine ball-winner in tight coverage that [[Bo Nix]] will target regardless of matchup. One down game all season; otherwise you can set your watch to the production.
- 2025-10-10 — According to guest Cousin Josh (Detective Fish, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Chris Harris]]'s show ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-10]]): Sutton is his number five hot start not to sell. Josh argues Sutton has become a set-it-and-forget-it player over two years, finished as WR9 last season, is around WR10 now, is 'not far off from that [[Drake London]] group,' is getting 20% of the targets with the air yards there, and has a great schedule — Jets, Giants, Cowboys among the worst defenses — so if you are considering a sale, wait until after that run. He was a fringe round-five pick and is now closer to an end-of-round-three redraft player. Josh's core reason for holding is trade math, not just talent: he doesn't think he could flip Sutton for the running back he'd actually want. Harris agrees Sutton is good and notes he had been calling for this breakout well before it happened, ranking Sutton WR13; but Harris's counter-position is that every receiver outside his top three is tradable in the right deal for the right running back.
- 2025-10-23 — According to Jake Trowbridge (untracked, DFS lineup-reveal co-host, [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-23]]): Trowbridge rostered Sutton at $6,200 to get a piece of the Broncos-Cowboys game script, saying he's repeatedly targeted players facing Dallas specifically, even without fully buying into a [[Bo Nix]] stack.
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2025-10-27]]) [Redraft]: had a touchdown wiped out by offensive pass interference on a pick play, then dropped a perfect deep-post laser the very next play -- Harris, a Sutton fantasy manager himself, says 'we got robbed' but also 'he gave it back.' Denver kept looking for him late in garbage time, including one broken-communication run-action target he didn't turn to catch.
- 2025-10-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-30]]): Sutton is 'really, really good' but his reception-perception profile from last year showed he's strong vs. man coverage and notably weaker vs. zone, and that split has borne out in 2025 production -- he's been targeted on only 17% of his routes against zone this season and is more effective vs. man than zone, same as last year. Harmon says [[Troy Franklin]] has recently outproduced Sutton fantasy-wise because of touchdowns, and that Denver needed exactly the kind of zone-coverage answer Franklin (and Pat Bryant) provide alongside him.
- 2025-10-30 — According to Jake Trowbridge (not tracked) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-30]]): Trowbridge picked Sutton at $6,500 in DFS, betting on a bounce-back after his target share stepped back as Denver spread the ball to Troy Franklin and Pat Bryant. Both note it may be tougher to feature Sutton against a significantly better Houston defense than it was against Dallas, where he scored a touchdown that was controversially called back on offensive pass interference and was otherwise a clear red-zone priority.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-13]]): Sutton has been passed in targets by [[Troy Franklin]] but Harmon still considers him the player the offense should be designed around — the X receiver you want as the read on the play, and the guy who should be getting big in-breaking routes with [[Bo Nix]]. His evaluation is measured: a good number one receiver, not a great one, a strong man-coverage beater at 71% success but not nearly as good against zone, and close to an 80% outside-on-the-line-of-scrimmage player, which adds volatility when an offense goes into chaos. He also notes Sutton is getting up there in age and has definitely slowed down, and says he is not low on him so much as lower than others.
- 2025-11-17 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-17 (Week 11 Game Film Review)]]): Sutton made two big clutch third-down conversions (third-and-15 at the two-minute warning, then third-and-six) to help set up Denver's game-winning field goal drive against Kansas City, and was robbed of what looked like a completed tightrope catch on the sideline. Even so, Harris said Sutton 'just didn't feel that involved for much of this game' and is now flip-flopping him behind [[Troy Franklin]] in the Denver receiver pecking order, though he stressed that could reverse again next week.
- 2025-12-01 — According to Andy Behrens (untracked, Yacht Club/The Deep Shot co-host, [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-01]]): Courtland Sutton caught a phenomenal deep touchdown from [[Bo Nix]] and a huge catch on a long third down against zone coverage in the overtime win over Washington. Behrens said it was a nice reminder Sutton is still a pretty good player, and noted there's a real chance he finishes with almost the same statistical season as last year -- he's got 10 and 8 catches the last two games and now six touchdowns on the year -- even though the route to get there has felt unsatisfying.
- 2025-12-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-15]]): Sutton set up Denver's first score, gave up two touchdowns in coverage in the first half, then caught a great third-quarter touchdown himself in the comeback win over Green Bay. Harris says Sutton is 'back to the land of' being someone he's willing to start heading into a fantasy semifinal, with the caveat that 'nothing's guaranteed' at this point in the season.
- 2025-12-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-23]]): Sutton scored a second-quarter touchdown and is 'clearly in your lineup' for week 17's Christmas night game against the Chiefs -- Harris highlighted him as red hot heading in, projecting him over 59.5 receiving yards.
- 2025-12-23 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-12-23]]): entering his age-31 season next year, Sutton has quietly put together his best stretch of the season -- over his last five games he's averaging 9.2 targets, 5.6 receptions, 76 yards and 3 touchdowns (a 17-game pace of roughly 95 catches, 1,300 yards, 10 touchdowns), and sits 11th among all NFL wide receivers in receiving yards, all while attention went to Troy Franklin's slot role instead. Harmon frames Sutton, per his own 2024 Reception Perception charting, as a below-average zone-beater but a strong X-receiver with a 70%+ success rate versus man coverage, which makes him vulnerable whenever Bo Nix is playing 'in chaos' (pressure, protection issues) but reliably productive on a full-season sample regardless. Denver just extended him; Harmon expects similar X-receiver production next year, with the real swing factor being the health and rotation of the receivers around him -- Pat Bryant suffered a concussion this week -- rather than any decline in Sutton's own play, though separation has become a more visible issue with age.
- 2026-01-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-27]]) [Dynasty]: after Denver signed Sutton to a contract extension, Harmon called him more of a 'passable' number-one receiver in practice, but really more of a true number-two given he's 'getting longer in the tooth' at this stage of his career -- adding urgency for Denver to add a bigger-time receiver this offseason even with [[Troy Franklin]] and [[Pat Bryant]] already on the roster.
- 2026-03-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-10]]): [[Courtland Sutton]] posted his first-ever back-to-back 1,000-yard seasons (124 targets, 74 catches, 1,017 yards, 7 TDs in 2025), but turning 31 in 2026, Harmon sees him as 'clearly on the back nine of his career' with 'declining returns,' despite calling 2025 a genuinely good season. He cited charting showing a 65th-percentile success rate vs. man coverage but just a 9th-percentile success rate vs. zone -- a profile he attributes both to alignment (Sutton plays almost exclusively X) and to Sutton's own separation limits; he had four games with only one catch and five games under 30 yards. Harmon calls him 'a really good starting X receiver' and 'an acceptable number one' but not elite, and pushed back on in-season narratives that Troy Franklin or Pat Bryant had passed him as the offense's true first read -- Harmon argues Sutton remained the QB's primary progression target, but Sean Payton's offense requires pocket time and patience to actually get him the ball on his deeper outside digs, which the Broncos' QB play didn't consistently provide. Harmon said he wouldn't build a passing game entirely around Sutton at this stage of his career.
- 2026-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-12]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Cited as another volatile-X comp alongside [[DK Metcalf]] while discussing [[Alec Pierce]]'s contract -- Sutton makes $23M/year and still had games with as little as one catch last year, which Harmon attributes to erratic quarterback play in Denver rather than a Troy Franklin usurpation narrative.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host, untracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-19]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Sutton remains Denver's outside/X-only receiver, but the hosts expect [[Jalen Waddle]] to out-target him now that Waddle moves around the formation and gets 'layup' looks. Koh notes the risk of being an X-only receiver in chaos -- Sutton had three or four games last year with a single catch when Bo Nix played in scramble situations and Sutton was stuck running routes that don't come open within 2.5 seconds. Harmon still expects a competitive, not one-sided, target split between the two.

He said Sutton does 'kind of a different thing' than Denver's other receivers and pushed back on a simple 'too many mouths to feed' target-diet argument, noting talented receivers eat regardless of target competition and that injuries inevitably reshuffle target order over a season anyway.

He called Sutton's current WR35 ADP -- down from WR14 and WR11 finishes the last two seasons -- too cheap and said he'll be buying heavily at that price.

- 2026-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-23]]): Harmon described Courtland Sutton as a 'limited' but solid contested-catch X receiver for Denver -- 86% snaps outside in 2024 and similar in 2025, never below 73% on-ball for his career -- who runs a good dig route (56% success, above the QB's average) but struggles to separate against zone coverage and lacks the deep speed to threaten defenses consistently, which Harmon contrasted with incoming [[Jalen Waddle]] as a better answer on breaking routes and versus zone.

Harmon is much lower, arguing he's been far ahead of consensus on Waddle (both in [[Dynasty]] and redraft) since the trade, expecting Waddle to become the offense's 'easy button' Z-receiver, siphoning the short-game targets Sutton has historically relied on as Payton's traditional 'other guy' rotates. Both see Marvin Mims Jr. as increasingly expendable/a trade candidate.
