# Oronde Gadsden II

## Expert Takes

- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18]]) [Dynasty]: Gadsden, the son of former Dolphins receiver Oronde Gadsden Sr., is a converted wide receiver whose route repertoire is small — the moves, techniques and concepts he owns are not expansive, which restricts him to being effective mostly against zone coverage. Waldman is blunt that from what he has seen Gadsden has 'zero craft' against man-to-man coverage and must develop it. At the catch point he wins the ball against tight coverage and attacks it early, and he can take hits to earn the football, though he has lapses where he clap-attacks the ball when high-pointing. Waldman's summary is that he is mostly going to have use in space.
- 2025-07-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-28]]): 'fuck it' on Gadsden having immediate fantasy value in the Chargers offense, but with an unusually explicit caveat — if the camp buzz translates, Gadsden is the one rookie Waldman says he completely whiffed on, and the first player he would go back and re-evaluate against his tape. His concerns from Syracuse are route running above all: he did not think Gadsden won consistently man-to-man, which Waldman says top tight ends must do, and he saw issues in how Gadsden attacked the ball even though he could still win it. Waldman loved the father's game and Syracuse's willingness to let him do what he did best. Bob Harris (untracked) says he is not yet chasing Gadsden in the 19th or 20th round of best ball but sees more people doing it, and calls it a defensible play while cautioning that early buzz is just early buzz.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the rookie tight end was 'really important in the fourth quarter' on the Chargers' game-winning drive against Denver, and Harris folded him into the discussion of a Chargers passing game that regularly feeds three receivers and sometimes a fourth mouth — evidence the offense has moved well past the run-heavy Greg Roman story told all summer.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-23]]): guest SCOTT FISH (Fantasy Cares, NOT tracked) made Gadsden his number two Week 4 add on tight-end scarcity — five catches for 46 yards on seven targets in his first real game, immediately more snaps than Tyler Conklin and Will Dissly, and more targets, red-zone targets and end-zone targets than Conklin and Dissly had in all three weeks combined. Fish's context: [[Justin Herbert]] has been throwing it 30-35 times a game over the last eight or so games dating to last season, so 'we may have stumbled into a guy that could look at five, six, seven targets, maybe a few catches, 30-40-50 yards a game and be a helper for you' — not 'the truth at tight end.' Harris agreed it looked on film like Gadsden took over the position but pushed back on cost: 'I wouldn't spend any FAAB on any tight end ever,' and 'the position is obviously hot garbage.'
- 2025-09-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-29]]): Waldman is feeling the Gadsden breakout and concedes he 'probably needs to go back and look at what I missed with him if this continues.' What he sees so far fits his tape read — the Chargers are using him as Syracuse did, on straight-line routes into open field and zones, and he is making the catches. The open question is whether there is more to his game than Waldman saw, and whether it grows beyond his current role. Bob Harris (not tracked) notes opportunity matters in this league and Gadsden clearly has the coaching staff's support; he also teases Waldman that [[Elijah Arroyo]] is 'no Oronde Gadsden.'
- 2025-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Gadsden had a huge day against Indianapolis, including a body-control catch down the sideline and a contested catch-through-contact that he turned into a long gain into the red zone before adding a garbage-time touchdown. Harris cautioned that the game script (a blowout loss) won't repeat every week, but said Gadsden 'is showing some bona fides' and can be trusted going forward, at least for now.
- 2025-10-21 — According to guest John Daigle (Establish the Run, not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-21 (Week 8 Waivers & W7 Reviews)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Daigle's #2 Week 8 waiver add, calling Gadsden 'streamable weekly' and startable even in tight-end-premium leagues for the rest of the season. His route participation has climbed steadily over [[Will Dissly]] over the past month. Daigle had been concerned a couple weeks ago that Gadsden's target share was only inflated because [[Quentin Johnston]] (developing well in year three) was out, but Gadsden remained an every-down tight end even with Johnston back, including one highlight sideline catch. Chris Harris agreed, calling him his own first honorable mention and comparing his ceiling role to a young [[Robert Tonyan]]-style tight end, noting the role should hold as long as the Chargers keep throwing a lot with Justin Herbert healthy.
- 2025-10-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Scott Pianowski (untracked Yahoo analyst, [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-23]]) discussing a DraftKings Pick Six prop of 44.5 receiving yards against Minnesota: Harris took the under, arguing the betting line has 'corrected' upward after recent weeks and expecting some regression, while still crediting Gadsden with 'showing something on tape' that means Minnesota won't take him lightly. Pianowski separately credited much of Gadsden's recent volume to the Chargers leaning on [[Justin Herbert]] and playing from a pass-heavy script (including garbage time against Miami and against the Colts), and noted the Chargers' offensive receiver room, if Gadsden's included, is now effectively a four-man rotation.
- 2025-10-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-23]]): Gadsden, a rookie tight end, is breaking out for the Chargers right now -- Harmon says flatly 'this guy can play... he's really, really good' and flagged him as a factor that could pull target share away from [[Keenan Allen]] going forward.
- 2025-10-24 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: recaps a strong Thursday-night showing from rookie tight end Oronde Gadsden II against the Vikings -- he was five catches for 77 yards by midway through the second quarter (helped by a blown coverage on a 41-yard gain) but didn't add to that total the rest of the way until reappearing in the two-minute drill before halftime. Harris notes Gadsden's size makes him easy to mistake for a big receiver on tape, and frames him as a continuing breakout-in-progress storyline given his big-play ability, calling this game another step in that direction even though the overall counting stats stalled after the first half.
- 2025-10-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-27]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman said [[Oronde Gadsden II]] is playing in the best circumstance of any rookie tight end not named [[Tyler Warren]], crediting [[Justin Herbert]] for elevating him -- 'you could insert a lot of tight ends' into that role and get production, but Gadsden is making good plays within it regardless. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) is an outspoken Gadsden fan and pushed Waldman to fully endorse him.
- 2025-10-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and Jake Trowbridge (not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-30]]): priced at $5,300 -- higher than [[Brock Bowers]] this week, which both hosts flagged as notable given the broader shift in Chargers passing-game investment toward Gadsden and [[Ladd McConkey]] and away from other target competitors.
- 2025-10-31 — According to Adam Harstad (untracked Film and Theory co-host, [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-31]]) [Dynasty]: Gadsden is the real-world counterexample to the platonic-ideal model -- he was valued quite low in rookie drafts, then strung together a couple of big games, and his lowercase-v value has since risen sharply, with many now ranking him ahead of or near [[Colston Loveland]] in tight end rankings despite Loveland being drafted much higher by both the NFL and fantasy managers and being ranked well above Gadsden as recently as a month ago. Harstad framed this as the expected real-world behavior when a player's big game genuinely teaches the market something new about his ability -- the opposite of the flat-value pattern seen with already-proven stars like Mahomes.
- 2025-11-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-14]]): Gadsden had an injury scare coming out of last week's Steelers game but is back practicing in full and should be in lineups for Week 11.
- 2026-01-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-05]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls Gadsden a good contested-catch player who gets open in zones and fits what the Chargers' offense asks of him, though he expects week-to-week volatility since the team spreads targets across multiple talents. He doesn't view Gadsden as a strong man-to-man route runner and doesn't expect that to change, projecting him as more of an intermediate-passing-tree option in the mold of [[Tucker Kraft]] and [[Tyler Warren]] -- or, in role terms, essentially [[Noah Brown]] playing as a big-slot tight end. Waldman's ceiling for Gadsden is a viable top-10-to-12 TE, not top-five, barring multiple injuries ahead of him.
- 2026-01-13 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-13]]): flagged as underused in the Chargers' wild-card loss -- Gadsden ran only 22 routes (44% of snaps), sixth on the team in routes run, despite tight-end leak-out concepts he did run being 'surprisingly effective.' Harmon said New England's pressure was coming up the A-gap and Los Angeles needed to get Gadsden and Ladd McConkey more involved rather than leaning on a blocking-oriented tight end (285 lbs, targeted on 37% of his routes, 32% of snaps).
- 2026-01-22 — According to James Koh and Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-22]]) [Dynasty]: Koh called it 'wheels up' for Gadsden with Mike McDaniel now the Chargers OC, pointing to the heavy tight end usage McDaniel got out of Miami's offense and projecting a 'huge upcoming season' for him; Harmon didn't push back while previewing how the McDaniel hire reshapes the Chargers' target distribution.
- 2026-01-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-26]]) [Dynasty]: fuck it -- Gadsden got 'exposed' in the second half of the season once the team stopped scheming him free in open zones, and his production plummeted. Waldman's read is that Gadsden is essentially a zone-only player who struggles against man coverage; he says it's fine to hold him if acquired cheaply while watching for real, substantial reported improvement in year two, but if you can sell for a profit, this offseason is the window to do it.
- 2026-03-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-10]]): Harmon called [[Oronde Gadsden II]] 'such an interesting player' and 'good,' flagging him as likely the biggest beneficiary of the Mike McDaniel hire in Los Angeles. The open question Harmon raised is role -- whether Gadsden plays a lot in-line or needs to be more of a move tight end -- especially with the Chargers also signing fullback Alec Ingold, pointing toward more multi-tight-end, 21-personnel looks (with Ingold and Omarion Hampton in the backfield) and likely fewer 3-receiver snaps overall in 2026.
- 2026-04-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-23]]) [Dynasty]: Harris slotted Gadsden at #19, calling him a nod to boom-then-fade rookie tight ends generally -- he had good moments before 'completely falling off a cliff' and running out of steam late in the year, which Harris suspects reflects an undisclosed injury. Harris expects him to be the Chargers' primary tight end into 2026 and views the offense as likely to be good overall.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris pointed to [[Oronde Gadsden II]] as the poster child for how volatile mid-tier tight end rankings get -- he was a top-five/top-six weekly tight end for about six weeks last season before falling off, and despite liking his traits (he 'looked like an interesting player'), the team simply stopped throwing it to him down the field, which Harris expects to define the streaky unpredictability of the position generally this year.
- 2026-05-25 — On whether Oronde Gadsden II retains dynasty value with a new offensive coordinator arriving and [[David Njoku]] back healthy (redraft/best ball ADPs on Gadsden have already fallen), [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-25]]) [Dynasty] said 'fuck it' -- he's not there. Waldman said Gadsden flashed enough against zone coverage last year to be a top TE for a brief stretch, but once defenses started game-planning for him he 'faded pretty hard,' and he still needs to become a better route runner and improve his blocking. Waldman ranked both Njoku and Charlie Kolar as better all-around, better zone receivers than Gadsden right now, comparing the gap between Gadsden's flashes and a true difference-maker to 'a banjo player... playing the Eddie Van Halen role.' He said an offense-friendly coaching change could be Gadsden's saving grace, but not enough that he'd actively try to acquire him -- a dynasty hold if already rostered, not a buy.
- 2026-07-24 — According to Chris Harris and Andy Behrens (untracked co-host) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-24]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris has Gadsden ranked TE13 and doesn't expect training camp to teach him anything that would move him off that number, saying a steady drumbeat of 'Herbert-Gadsden connection' camp reports would just be 'usual camp sunshine.' The real threat he flags is [[David Njoku]] (ranked TE31), whom Harris thinks isn't good or important enough to be a startable tight end himself but is 'still good enough to muck things up for somebody else' -- a real, if secondary, problem for Gadsden's target share.
- 2026-08-20 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon says he can't really consider Gadsden in almost any fantasy format right now given how murky the Chargers tight end room has become.
