# Hunter Renfrow

> ⚠️ Takes below are from April 2024, while he was an unsigned free agent —
> treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show* ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-11]]), buy/sell on a reported link to Kansas City: both buy, cautiously. Harmon says he likes Renfrow and thinks he can play for a lot of teams, and believes he got iced out over his last couple of seasons under the Raiders' prior coaching staff. Notes his best season included some flanker-type snaps, not purely slot, and thinks Andy Reid could get the best out of him — but both hosts worry Kansas City is already getting "too slotty" as a receiver room with [[Rashee Rice]] and [[Marquise Brown]] and would rather see the team add a true outside X receiver who can beat man coverage.
- 2025-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-07]]): Renfrow never actually retired — he was forced to take the year off with ulcerative colitis, dropping from 185 to 150 pounds — and Harmon compared the comeback to [[Michael Gallup]]'s. He thinks Renfrow has always been slightly overrated as a route runner because he is a small scrappy white slot, but was genuinely a very good slot receiver in 2021 who could play in two-receiver sets, with an 81.6% success rate versus zone and 67.9% versus press. Koh (not tracked) added he was roughly a 60/40 slot-outside player in 2020-21, was exceptional on the dig at 88.2% but only 76.6% on the slant, which was his most-run route at 26% participation and a number you want above 80%. Both disliked the landing spot: Carolina is sneaky deep inside with [[Tetairoa McMillan]], [[Adam Thielen]], [[Jalen Coker]] — who Harmon thinks can be a starting big slot — [[Xavier Legette]] needing slot reps, Jimmy Horn Jr. and [[David Moore]]. Harmon doubts he is a special-teamer and finds it tough to see him making the 53; simply surviving camp and catching on elsewhere would be the win.
- 2025-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-30]]): Renfrow is a pure flyer coming out of semi-retirement after a condition that cost him weight and forced him away from the game, and Harmon notes his quick-twitch route running will always shine in camp reports — so the good camp buzz should be discounted. His Reception Perception profiles from his rookie year and his big 2021 Raiders season show the classic slot archetype: 81.6% success rate versus zone but not a great man beater. Harmon still finds it bizarre that Josh McDaniels soured on him right after an extension, and uses that as a caution against assuming any McDaniels slot receiver — the same enthusiasm now attached to [[Demario Douglas]] — is automatically valuable. Both hosts question the fit, since Carolina is already deep in flanker/slot bodies and Renfrow cannot play outside X; Harmon suggests his South Carolina/Clemson-area ties are part of why he is there.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-27]]): Renfrow was cut by Carolina during the recording. Harmon roots for him personally but calls him the classic archetype that beat writers go crazy for in minicamp because he runs crisp underneath routes — 'destined to smoke training camp every year', a category he also puts [[Adonai Mitchell]] in. He notes the Renfrow momentum had already dialed back before the cut, and reads the release as Carolina signalling they prefer their young guys, specifically [[Jalen Coker]].
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]) — 'fuck it' on the idea that the re-signed Renfrow will be a fantasy factor. Waldman's read is that Carolina saw real inconsistency in practice: flashes of the prime Raiders version, then long drop-offs in between, enough that they did not think he belonged on the roster. He was brought back only because after cutting Renfrow and trading [[Adam Thielen]], [[Jalen Coker]] turned up with a quad injury. Waldman calls him a desperation addition — a player who knows the offense and can give you some spot play, but not starter-level production. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed.
