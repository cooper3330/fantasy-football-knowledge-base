# Xavier Restrepo

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2025-02-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-24]]) [Dynasty]: guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) offers Restrepo as a deeper name whose model score lands just below his 60th percentile line. He is explicit that he has watched no film — the underlying numbers look pretty good, but Restrepo spent most of his time in the slot, and while the model already accounts for alignment, McFarland is unsure the game translates outside. Waldman offers no evaluation of his own beyond confirming the pronunciation.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): Waldman's short-term concern for the Miami slot is that his technique breaks down when targets are not pinpoint — balls arriving high-and-away or low-and-behind short-circuit his attack technique and he resorts to clap attacks that become drops. Once he does secure the ball and transitions downhill, Waldman likes him a lot: shifty, patient, mature as a decision maker, and thanks to his size a little more powerful than the average small slot option. See [[Catch Technique and Ball Tracking]].
- 2025-04-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-07]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman calls Restrepo a bit overrated and ranks him behind [[Daniel Jackson]] as a slot prospect. He is a good zone route runner who can add some yardage after the catch with his power, but Waldman describes him as a slower [[Parker Washington]] without the vertical element needed to win targets over his shoulder. The realistic outcome is a Braxton Berrios type of contributor for a team rather than a high-end NFL slot receiver.
- 2025-04-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-18]]) [Dynasty]: the archetypal 'pop gun slot receiver' — 5'10", 210, and 88.4% of his Reception Perception snaps in the slot with 72.9% off the line of scrimmage. Harmon charts a split profile: elite on quick stuff (82% slants, 86.7% flats, 86.4% corners — 'outrageously high') and bad everywhere else (45.5% nines, 51.9% posts, 66.7% curls, 50% comebacks, 30th percentile success rate vs man). Credits toughness, leverage and hands (68.8% contested catch, 1.4% drop rate), calls it 'old man game' at 23. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) notes he led Miami in receiving two straight years across two quarterbacks, was 13th in FBS in yards with 11 TDs and 77% of catches moving the chains. Both expect a day-three pick; Harmon's ceiling case is a random 80-catch, 750-yard season and then a 'PPR scam' the following year.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21]]): asked to choose between Restrepo and [[Parker Washington]], Waldman took Washington. Restrepo's college résumé is enormous — he passed Reggie Wayne as Miami's all-time leading receiver with 2,844 yards and set the school catch record at 200 — but Waldman thinks the market is overrating that production, because Miami's offense ran a lot of four-verts packages and RPOs that inflate targets. His comp is a Braxton Berrios type, 'but maybe not as good'. Waldman expects Restrepo to have a decent NFL career by the standard of making the league and staying in it, but frames him as a better NFL player than fantasy option — the bare minimum of what you are looking for rather than a roster piece.
- 2025-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-09]]): went undrafted after a bad forty (dinged with a 4.8) but signed with Tennessee, reuniting him with his college quarterback [[Cam Ward]], which Harmon calls the one place he was most likely to make it. Harmon likes the signing as a potential value slot receiver: real quality on the routes he is good at — slants, corners, flats — good hands and a nice feel for zone coverage, but he does not separate on the vast majority of routes because he is simply not quick, even if he is not literally 4.8 slow. He floats Restrepo as a possible [[Tyler Lockett]] replacement in that room. James Koh (not tracked) is more confident, saying Restrepo is making the team regardless of production because of the Ward relationship and the value of easing the rookie quarterback in during OTAs.
- 2025-07-10 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-10]]) [Dynasty]: Angelo reframes the Tennessee either/or as Restrepo versus [[Tyler Lockett]] rather than Lockett versus [[Calvin Ridley]], and says he could very well see Restrepo winning the slot role — the competition is Restrepo, [[Tyler Boyd]] (a tertiary or fourth option in his view) and Lockett depending on where Lockett lines up. If the rapport between [[Cam Ward]] and Restrepo shows up in training camp and the buzz keeps building, Angelo sees a legitimate chance Restrepo is a starter by midseason, and calls him the best bet among current Titans receivers to still be a starter in 2026 given Ridley's and Lockett's ages. [[Matt Waldman]] calls it a fun, spicy but reasonable take.
- 2025-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-01]]): Harmon had Restrepo as more of a day-three player, not an elite separator anywhere, but a genuinely good one on slants and flats who will occasionally get deep on a corner route from the slot — a solid zone-beater and bailout target with good hands, nothing special but useful. He bets Restrepo makes the roster and says he would not be surprised if the UDFA plays more snaps than one of the two receivers Tennessee actually drafted, depending on how the veterans ahead of him shake out. Both hosts dismiss the 4.83 forty as hamstring-related — Koh says there is no way a healthy Restrepo runs that — and Koh adds that his existing chemistry with [[Cam Ward]] from Miami makes him the most likely UDFA in the class to beat the odds.
