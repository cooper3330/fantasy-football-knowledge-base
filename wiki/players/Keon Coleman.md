# Keon Coleman

> ⚠️ Takes below are from January 2024, pre-draft. He was not yet on an NFL
> roster at the time of this take — treat as historical scouting context
> until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Going Deep - Draft Prospects)]]) [Dynasty]: both push back on a public narrative that had swung from "athletic freak" (after a huge LSU breakout while at Florida State, transferred from Michigan) to "actually stiff and overrated" — they land in between, calling him genuinely one of the more athletically talented receivers in the class (top-5 overall per Angelo) but not the special-tier athlete some pre-hype suggested. Catch-point and rebounding ability are "borderline otherworldly" per Waldman, and the route-running stem work and ability to drop weight into hard breaks already grade well on tape. The explicit risk, stated by both: he's not fast enough to threaten defenses vertically, so his fantasy outcome depends heavily on landing in a scheme built on anticipatory, timing-based throws (a "West Coast"-style offense) where his underneath separation translates into a high-volume possession-plus role; in an offense without that structure, he becomes a boom/bust outside-only receiver. The named downside comp is Courtland Sutton — "one of the best in the NFL at the catch point" but pigeonholed into a purely outside role that caps him as a good WR2 rather than the Pro-Bowl-level player his tools could support. See [[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]].
- 2024-01-15 — Guest **Daniel Harms** (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-15]] [Dynasty]: independently agrees — still top-5 in the class — while adding a specific explanation for why his draft stock had softened: Florida State's offense (and Coleman's tape) suffered once starting quarterback Jordan Travis was injured, since Travis's downfield ball placement had been enabling Coleman's best plays; the drop in stock reflects the offense, not the player. Adds a Drake London footwork/route-running comp and credits Coleman's background as a punt returner for above-average field-reading and spacing awareness that should translate positively at the next level.
- 2024-02-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-01]]): first Reception Perception charting numbers (explicitly caveated 3-game early sample), and a materially more skeptical read than the January guest takes. Lined up all over Florida's formation (X, flanker, and 35.8% in the slot), but "doesn't win vertically enough for my tastes" given his size, has real separation issues, and grades around the 33rd percentile in Harmon's early sample — "not terrible," but well short of the earlier "top-5 in the class" framing. Explicitly floats him as a better fit at big slot than X, and raises a direct bust comp: "I could see him being a little Burks-ish" — a receiver whose size/catch-point tools look special but who ends up miscast on the perimeter, referencing [[Treylon Burks]]'s NFL outcome as the precedent.

- 2024-02-29 — According to guest **Eric Froton** (NBC Sports college football analyst — not a tracked expert) and **James Koh** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on *Reception Perception: The Show*, hosted solo by Koh with [[Matt Harmon]] absent ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-29]]) [Dynasty]: a combine-week gut check that lands close to Harmon's own skeptical charting read above. Praises the elite size/athleticism (started as a basketball player at Michigan State) and highlight-reel contested/deep catches, including a breakout Week 1 vs. LSU, but flags matching separation concerns — his 27.6% downfield-target rate converted only about a third of the time, and his 1.74 yards/route ranked 4th-worst among the top 86 receivers nationally in yardage. His modest raw production (50/658 yards at FSU in 2023, sub-800 yards at Michigan State the year before) was worsened, in fairness, by starting QB Jordan Travis's midseason injury. Still, Froton calls him the group's highest-upside player behind only [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] and the most "pro-ready" of the four big receivers discussed, with a "Drake London-lite" bully-ball ceiling if his route consistency develops; Koh pushes back on any [[Quentin Johnston]] bust comp specifically because Coleman's catch-point ability is real where Johnston's college tape never was.
- 2024-03-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], solo cast ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-16]]): breaks are sharp and flat, and he earns enough separation to be a high-volume intermediate and short-game target if his quarterback consistently looks for him as the first option. If instead used as the second or third read behind a quarterback without Joe Burrow-level anticipation, field awareness and accuracy, that quarterback will be a beat or two late, letting tight man coverage close the window — reducing Coleman from a potential high-volume boundary/red-zone/deep threat to a contested-catch-only receiver, since most NFL quarterbacks won't have the confidence to fit it into him regardless. Calls this the source of Coleman's boom/bust profile — while maintaining his ceiling is "quite high."

- 2024-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] on *Going Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-03-28 (Going Deep - If We Were NFL GMs)]]): his own "problem solver" pick — floats him as the ideal replacement if [[Tee Higgins]] leaves Cincinnati, arguing Coleman would thrive with [[Joe Burrow]]'s timing and accuracy specifically because Coleman "solves problems with the ball in the air" and reads pacing/nuance well; his risk case is landing with a quarterback whose timing can't reach him when he breaks open, capping his volume the way it may have capped Devonta Parker (not tracked). Waldman calls his athletic ability underrated — cites his punt-return tape — and comps him to LeGarrette Blount for the hidden run-after-catch juice: hips flip like a running back's, and he can turn 90 degrees off one step. Says Coleman "plays too smart," integrating technique into unpracticed situations, and would take the risk of planting his flag on him as a problem-solving receiver who outplays lingering athletic-profile doubts.

- 2024-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (co-host — not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-04]]): Harmon's fuller charting sample lands materially more skeptical than his early 3-game read (see 2024-02-01 above), and he walks through it in detail against the historical Reception Perception dataset of poor-college-separator prospects who later hit in the NFL. Coleman's numbers: 20th percentile success rate vs. man coverage, 59th percentile (80.2%) vs. zone — average-or-better dig/curl routes but mostly red elsewhere, and a poor 37.8% success rate on the nine route (run on 15%+ of his sample) despite that being a route [[Treylon Burks]] actually won at 71%. Aligned outside 77.7% of snaps, in the slot only 21.6%, but off the line (flanker) 58.6% — the alignment profile Harmon says fits the "must become a big slot/flanker, can't play X" archetype pattern established by past hits like [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[Cooper Kupp]], Juju Smith-Schuster and [[Rashee Rice]] (none of whom separated well outside in college either). Athletic testing: ran a slow 4.61 40 (stood on it at his pro day) but was reportedly the fastest receiver in the combine's GPS-timed gauntlet drill, adding to Harmon's broader GPS-vs-stopwatch skepticism (see [[NFL Combine and Pro Day Skepticism]]); 38" vertical is fine for a 6'3"/215 frame. On film: an excellent contested-catch player (70.6% contested catch rate) but "a little overrated" as a pure jump-ball threat relative to Mike Evans/Mike Williams-tier players, sometimes forcing unnecessary one-handed grabs rather than simply winning the route; good after the catch (21.1% broken-tackle rate on in-space touches). Direct archetype comps: a better prospect than [[Jonathan Mingo]] was a year earlier, but a worse one than [[Treylon Burks]] was, in the same "poor college separator, needs to be a big slot" bucket — and Harmon explicitly does not think this profile supports a Round 1 selection for the "vast majority" of teams; calls him more of a Day 2 prospect who needs a specific scheme fit (analogous to how Andy Reid's Kansas City staff unlocked [[Rashee Rice]]) rather than being miscast outside the way Tennessee misused Burks. James Koh separately notes Coleman's willingness to block in the run game fits a Houston-style "power slot"/hybrid-TE usage, floating Houston and Cincinnati (post-Tee Higgins-departure scenario) as plausible landing spots, though disagrees with Harmon on how early Cincinnati would realistically draft a receiver. See [[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]] and [[Reception Perception Methodology]].
