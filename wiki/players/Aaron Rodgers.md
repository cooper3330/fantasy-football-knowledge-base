# Aaron Rodgers

> ⚠️ Takes below are from February 2024. Team/role are as of that date and
> are almost certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded
> by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-12]]: both "feel it" on a return to form after his Week 1 2023 Achilles tear. Waldman's case is intangible/effort-based rather than tape-based at this point — "too obsessed with the game," still "one of the best quarterbacks that's ever strapped it on" — and explicitly separates the player evaluation from off-field opinions of Rodgers as a person. Flagged as the Jets' best realistic path out of a season of off-field/organizational drama (friction between the team and certain media outlets) rather than a specific scheme or supporting-cast argument.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-06-18]]): Waldman projects top-10, possibly top-5, fantasy quarterback numbers for a healthy Aaron Rodgers returning from his Achilles injury, tied closely to his [[Garrett Wilson]] outlook. Hosts also discussed Rodgers' unexcused absence from mandatory June minicamp, framing it as the Jets mishandling the PR on what was really just a two-day absence rather than a genuine concern about Rodgers' commitment.
- 2024-07-09 — According to James Coe (co-host, not tracked) and [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Both hosts cautioned against assuming Aaron Rodgers returns to his prior peak form given his age and the Achilles injury that ended his 2023 debut after four snaps, with Harmon calling the assumption of a fully-recovered Rodgers 'a bad assumption.' Still, Harmon said even a diminished Rodgers is 'light years better' than what the Jets got from Zach Wilson, and Coe noted Rodgers looked genuinely 'loose' and invested on Hard Knocks before the injury. The team is otherwise running back the same core with [[Mike Williams]], Tyron Smith and first-round tackle Olu Fashanu added to protect him.
- 2024-08-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-05]]): Rodgers is going as roughly QB20 and Waldman expects him to be better than that price, dismissing the skipped-camp-time story as smaller than it was made out to be. The real watch item is timing with [[Garrett Wilson]], since Rodgers historically demands conceptual adjustments receivers take years to learn. Waldman cited Rodgers' 2022 handling of [[Romeo Doubs]] as the pattern: early camp is full of athletic highlight catches, and by week three or four Rodgers is telling receivers none of them will play if they cannot adjust with him. Waldman also relayed that Rodgers went six-of-six in a red-zone session, three to Wilson and two to [[Breece Hall]], and said afterward that his scouting report had not told him Hall had those skills and that he had never been in an offense where the primary red-zone read was the running back. If Wilson's timing lags, Waldman expects more checkdowns to Hall.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-10]]): Rodgers in London looked like late-career Ben Roethlisberger — getting the ball out fast and, per co-host James Koh (not tracked), playing 'scared' in a way Koh says he has never seen from him. Harmon's framing: he generally thinks veteran QBs are underrated right now because of their pre-snap catalog (citing [[Jared Goff]], [[Baker Mayfield]] and especially [[Kirk Cousins]] diagnosing Todd Bowles' blitzes and hitting [[Drake London]] on in-breakers and [[Darnell Mooney]] on out-breakers), but Rodgers has two problems: he cannot move at all, so he must be perfect pre-snap, and against Minnesota and Denver — the two best blitz/coverage-mixing units — he was fooled every step, including the Andrew Van Ginkle pick-six where his correct pre-snap blitz-beater read was sat on. Harmon also calls it a joke that Hackett has the job. Koh wondered aloud whether the team knows more about Rodgers' health than they are saying.
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Harmon explicitly rejects Stephen A. Smith's 'Rodgers should retire' line but delivers a narrow verdict — Rodgers still does two things really well and almost nothing else. He is excellent in quick game (plant the back foot, get it out on time, accurate) and on designed or scripted deep shots, where Harmon says he retains the arm talent to 'drop it in every single bucket' at every level. The problem is 'there's a shit ton more to playing quarterback than that': because he is no longer mobile, the margin for error is thin, and the Jets show quite literally nothing in the intermediate middle of the field. Harmon calls it the same check-down-or-touchdown offense the site has criticized for years and can't tell whether it is design or Rodgers. James Koh (not tracked) thought Rodgers looked dialed in for a full first half.
- 2024-11-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]]): the Jets are cooked for 2024, and the pertinent question is no longer what Rodgers has left but when he gets shut down and whether this is the last time we see him play. Harmon defends the actual level of play — after knocking off early rust he grades Rodgers at roughly league average and sometimes above it, comping it to Michael Jordan on the Wizards, who was still an average NBA player even though everyone could see he was done. His argument is that the roster rotted around the quarterback: the defense is bad, has severe tackling issues, and has gotten worse since the defensive head coach was fired. He also stresses that NFL windows are real and do not come back — the Jets had one in 2021-2022 and probably last year, when league-average quarterback play would have made a good defense into a great team. Koh (not tracked) thinks the Jets will not shut Rodgers down but Rodgers might shut himself down, and if he does, that is retirement.
- 2024-11-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-18]]): on 'he's toast', Waldman declined to bite — he said he was tempted but would not fall for what he called weak media bait. His actual concern is that the way Rodgers is comporting himself away from the field has him distracted from what he could be on it. He thinks the Jets need another year to get everything together after a chaotic season, some of which was Rodgers's own doing and some of which he thinks was blown out of proportion. Against the fan and media contingent expecting him gone, Waldman said he is not convinced Rodgers returns but would bet on it: Rodgers has said he wants another year, and he now gets to help hire a new coach and possibly a new GM, which Waldman expects him to love.
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]) [Dynasty]: 50-50 on whether he is finished, but worth a dynasty stash. Waldman's advice is that if you are a dynasty manager without a quarterback situation and thinking you must draft one this year, add Rodgers just in case, because he is not sure this rookie quarterback class makes a huge immediate impact. He notes Rodgers has become a toxic talking point fairly or not — the off-field media presence and the narratives about him running players out of buildings only bite because he is not playing well; if he were, nobody would care. He draws the parallel that if [[Austin Ekeler]] had aired political views on [[Matt Harmon]]'s show while playing badly, the reaction would have been the same. Bob Harris (not tracked) is more skeptical: he is 41, coming back from an extended absence like the recovery cases discussed earlier, and rather than a Favre-style Vikings redemption arc he expects Rodgers to end up in Cleveland and be awful.
- 2024-12-17 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-17]]): over the last two weeks Rodgers is top five in EPA per play, top five in passing first downs, top five in air yards per attempt (fourth in air yards per attempt), with four touchdowns and no interceptions, and fourth among quarterbacks in EPA gained on scrambles — third if you exclude [[Drew Lock]], behind only [[Josh Allen]] and [[Jayden Daniels]]. Harmon reads Rodgers as wanting to finish all 17 games because missing time bothers him, and could see him retiring on his own terms. James Koh (not a tracked expert) floated Cleveland with Kevin Stefanski as the perfect landing spot; Harmon agreed the schematic fit is strong — under center, few motions, checks at the line, a run game and play action, echoing why Rodgers disliked Matt LaFleur's motion volume — but raised the Deshaun Watson salary albatross and said Rodgers would not enjoy playing with [[Jerry Jeudy]] given his route-depth frustrations with [[Garrett Wilson]].
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-19]]): raised as the other half of a post-Achilles veteran comparison with [[Kirk Cousins]]. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) had Rodgers completing just 29% of intermediate throws (10-19 air yards) since Week 11 — worst in the NFL, ahead of even Cousins — with minus 6.5 EPA, second worst, despite throwing to [[Garrett Wilson]] and [[Davante Adams]]. Harmon's read separates the two: Rodgers has looked like a top-five quarterback in his last two games and clearly still has zip and can spin it; what he has lost is the ability to move off platform and drive the ball into tight windows. Cousins' problem, by contrast, Harmon frames as late arrival plus mental mistakes on man/zone diagnosis. Both hosts flagged the 'not a touchdown to check down' pattern as out of character for Rodgers' prime.
- 2025-01-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-06]]): Waldman does not think Rodgers is done — he names the Saints and the Raiders as teams that might take a shot, with the Raiders the better bet, and floats New Orleans moving on from [[Derek Carr]] as the mechanism; he also notes that if [[Matthew Stafford]] were to go down, a loaded win-now Rams roster could come calling. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) took the other side: if forced to bet, he thinks we have seen the last of Rodgers, who can go out on a high note after a four-touchdown game and the 500-touchdown milestone with Hall of Fame status locked in, though Rodgers has said he would be willing to be a bridge or mentor for a young quarterback. Harris joked about the Packers-to-Minnesota career arc but does not expect Rodgers there given [[Sam Darnold]]'s franchise-tag possibility. Waldman added that Rodgers might get on the same page faster with [[Chris Olave]], whom he considers the superior player to [[Garrett Wilson]].
- 2025-01-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-20]]): playing GM of a Jets team with a new head coach and GM, Waldman keeps Rodgers — if Rodgers wants to play, he would be well over a year removed from the Achilles, and Waldman argues there were far more problems with the roster than Rodgers alone. He separates the football judgement from the noise, saying people let their feelings about Rodgers off the field, including his politics, drive the evaluation, and that after watching the Netflix 'Enigma' documentary he came away thinking it would not change anyone's mind either way but did give an accurate depiction of how media handles celebrities — you can follow every reporting rule and still mischaracterise a person. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed fully, noting the Jets are unlikely to find a better bridge than [[Sam Darnold]] or [[Joe Flacco]]; both joked they may be the only two people who feel this way.
- 2025-01-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-27]]): 'feel it' that new Jets head coach Aaron Glenn would be wise to retain Rodgers, echoing the prior week's discussion. His argument is that this is like the [[Russell Wilson]] situation — a bad team that could get a lot better — and that having added [[Davante Adams]] at midseason and given [[Garrett Wilson]] only one full season with Rodgers, you owe the pairing a proper year now the learning curve is behind them, especially since 2023 was lost. The alternatives argument does the rest of the work: the free-agent bridge options ([[Sam Darnold]], [[Kirk Cousins]]) are unappealing, and Waldman says he is told the incoming rookie quarterback class is not on last year's level. Bob Harris (not tracked) reported Rodgers told Jay Glazer he intends to sort it out soon and may meet the new staff in California.
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]): Waldman is in favor of Rodgers to the Giants — he doesn't have to move far, stays in a massive media market, and joins a team with better trench work than the Jets had. He believes Rodgers still has enough left as long as he has an offensive line in front of him, and that with Rodgers the Giants become the dark-horse NFC East wild card, potentially challenging Washington. He expects Rodgers to work well with [[Malik Nabers]] and to attract another useful receiver. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) noted the NFLPA report card had the Giants 20th and the Jets 29th, with Woody Johnson the only owner graded F. Waldman also floated the Giants as the ideal landing spot for [[Shedeur Sanders]] precisely because he could sit behind Rodgers.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]): asked to pick between the two unsigned veterans for Pittsburgh, Waldman feels Rodgers over [[Russell Wilson]] on the numbers and on ceiling, but flags a real relationship risk — he would not be shocked if Rodgers does not understand what [[DK Metcalf]] is and is not as a receiver, decides Metcalf is not the WR1 he expects, and throws one of the diva fits he is known for, at which point the whole thing falls apart. On floor, he still puts Wilson in the mix. Bob Harris (not tracked) senses Steelers fans leaning Rodgers as simply better than what they have had. Waldman adds the organization has to let the quarterback make adjustments for either option to work.
- 2025-03-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-26]]): with Rodgers still unsigned and reportedly close to Pittsburgh, Harmon says that from a pure football standpoint the fit works — an ascending young offensive line, a strong defense, and [[DK Metcalf]] running in-breakers off play action in Arthur Smith's system. He frames it as 'desperate meets desperate': the Steelers need a solution behind center and Rodgers wants to keep playing but clearly does not want Pittsburgh. Harmon argues public analysis of Rodgers is colored by dislike of his off-field beliefs, and criticizes the March 25th waffling. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) agrees Rodgers is a significant upgrade at quarterback for that roster. Harmon adds the offense still needs an outside-zone back added in the draft.
- 2025-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-04]]), on the still-unresolved free agency saga: Mike Tomlin's 'productive day' quote and Steelers president Art Rooney II saying 'all signs are positive' strike Harmon as performative and reeking of desperation, and he cites a Dave Dameshek/Gregg Rosenthal theory that the holdup may be timed to a Pat McAfee live show in Pittsburgh for a pro-wrestling-style reveal. On the football, Harmon holds strongly that Pittsburgh is a great fit: Arthur Smith going under center with outside-zone stretch and play action plays to Rodgers' strengths, and two big-bodied receivers roaming outside suits him. Rodgers reportedly threw with [[DK Metcalf]]. Harmon is skeptical of the persistent Vikings smoke — Yahoo's Charles Robinson reported they may be waiting until after the draft for compensatory-pick reasons — noting Minnesota has been publicly all-in on [[J.J. McCarthy]] and that Kevin O'Connell probably thinks he could fix any quarterback after the [[Sam Darnold]] reclamation.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): he 'feels it' that Rodgers ultimately signs with Pittsburgh, though he relays via writer Steve Volk that there is rancor inside the organization — executive-level people irritated that Rodgers is stalling on a 'class organization' — while the coaching staff and personnel people are unbothered, believing Rodgers would immediately be the best quarterback on the roster and possibly in the division if fully healthy. Waldman reads the delay as Rodgers maximizing options (a [[Derek Carr]] shoulder injury or a Kellen Moore-type situation could still open up) rather than anything football-related, and notes most Rodgers discourse is about Rodgers the person, a band teams weight far less than the public does. Waldman is only interested in the downstream effect on receivers like [[DK Metcalf]] and [[George Pickens]].
- 2025-05-12 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-12]]): Waldman said 'fuck it' to the widely-shared Mike Florio report that Rodgers was waiting on the schedule release before signing, calling it a hugely clickbaity speculative piece and noting the media dynamic where everyone claims to dislike Rodgers while needing the clicks he generates. Waldman's own read is that Pittsburgh is simply happy to wait until late July rather than sign him now and then field leadership and consistency questions about why he is not attending. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agreed the Steelers appear confident, citing Art Rooney II's draft-night comments and Brooke Pryor's ESPN reporting that Rodgers was in the loop on the [[George Pickens]] trade before it happened.
- 2025-05-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-26]]): Waldman treats [[Aaron Rodgers]] to Pittsburgh as effectively settled — the alternative is opening the season with Mason Rudolph, [[Skylar Thompson]] or [[Will Howard]], none of whom he thinks are ready or perceived as what a team wants to start. He reads the delay as the Steelers giving Rodgers room after Rodgers disclosed family cancer diagnoses, letting him decide when to broach it publicly. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) lays out the breadcrumbs: Art Rooney saying at the draft that the Steelers would not change their draft strategy around Rodgers, Rodgers hinting he might play in Chicago at some point (the Steelers visit Chicago this year) while ruling out the Bears and Saints, and reporting that Rodgers was in on the [[George Pickens]] trade. Harris says he would be surprised if Rodgers is not a Steeler.
- 2025-06-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-02]]): asked to pick between Rodgers and [[Kirk Cousins]] as Pittsburgh's QB1, Waldman is 'absolutely feeling Aaron Rodgers over bologna sandwich any day' — he will take the bark chewing, the ayahuasca and the Costa Rica chanting. His football case is that Rodgers' arm is better and he is healthier, and his leadership case is that whatever Rodgers says publicly, players love working with him and view him as a good leader. He also argues football is a partial meritocracy where a four-time MVP with a Super Bowl gets leeway, and that Pittsburgh has been giving him space all along — with reports Rodgers was involved in or informed of the [[George Pickens]] trade as a sign he is coming. He suggests Rodgers may want work in before the July/August media circus, while Mason Rudolph and [[Skylar Thompson]] get reps. Bob Harris (co-host, not tracked) relays Terry Bradshaw calling Rodgers a joke, and says he still thinks the Steelers believe Rodgers will play.
- 2025-06-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-09]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Rodgers officially signing with Pittsburgh at a team-friendly number does not move him into the top 24 fantasy quarterbacks — the physical ability has passed — but he changes the passing-game picture around him because he reads and manipulates the middle of the field, throws into tight coverage windows and works within structure. Waldman expects him to spread the ball around the way he did with the 2022 Packers, where only [[Allen Lazard]] cleared 100 catches and an older Randall Cobb was made to look reasonably substantial for one last year. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) argued the drawn-out waiting game was always the plan rather than genuine doubt, pointing to Rodgers reportedly being consulted before [[George Pickens]] was traded to Dallas, and said the fan angst was overwrought throughout.
- 2025-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and guest Cory Kinnan (Reception Perception QB charting, NOT a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-25]]): Kinnan's charting has Rodgers' 2024 statistically on par with 2022 and expects more of the same in Pittsburgh, calling this a marriage of desperation — Rodgers said on Pat McAfee the day of taping that 2025 is probably his last season. Kinnan sees enough 'arm arrogance' left to float the Steelers to nine or ten wins and keep Tomlin's no-losing-seasons streak alive, but flags that Rodgers' game is timing- and rapport-driven and there is not a single familiar face on the roster. Harmon adds from the profile that Rodgers' success rates on deeper breaks — corners, posts, digs — were all below average to poor in 2024, and quotes Derek Klassen's old line that Rodgers is now 'touchdown or check down.' Both call the floor on the move pretty low; Kinnan notes Rodgers operates like a man who does not want to get hit anymore.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Film & Theory Sequences, Signal, Steelers)]]): the market has given up on [[Aaron Rodgers]] too soon, and Pittsburgh could be a sneaky team — the defense is good enough and if the offensive line holds up, Rodgers' ability to play chess pre-snap is a real edge. Waldman's stated open question is whether the weapons are conceptually advanced enough to receive it; he names [[Pat Freiermuth]] as the only sure bet, with [[DK Metcalf]] and [[Calvin Austin III]] unknowns. Co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) tied it to his sequencing argument: old quarterbacks almost universally look terrible in their final season, but ignoring that last year, a bad season followed by a rebound is common (Philip Rivers, Kurt Warner). Harstad said he is open to both possibilities — that Rodgers has hit the cliff like Johnny Unitas with the Chargers, or that last year was simply a down year of the kind Rodgers already had in Green Bay before bouncing back — and is not ruling out significant improvement.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): Rodgers is back — not to MVP caliber, because he cannot move the way he used to, but he can still operate off boots, throw off balance and throw on the move, and he cashed in on designed bootlegs all day in Week 1. More important is the fit: Waldman argues Arthur Smith is a smart coach who never had a real quarterback (Atlanta gave him passers who 'should not have been drafted'; Russell Wilson has isms that do not jive), while Rodgers had coordinators who should have let him drive. Rodgers can fill in the blanks and commands enough respect to have input. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) adds Mike Tomlin may be the one coach who can tell Rodgers this is my team, and speculates Rodgers may simply want to be another guy on an established franchise.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): four touchdowns and no interceptions in the Steelers' shootout win at the Meadowlands is a good day no matter how you slice it, but Harris will not say Rodgers played great. The same frustrating tendencies were there for long stretches, holding the ball far too long and taking too long to get the snap off. What he did not do is make big mistakes. Harris characterizes at least two of the scores as jerk-face touchdowns, a pop pass in close and one where Rodgers appears to audible to a throw near the goal line, and says that kind of production is simply part of the deal with Rodgers. Verdict: good, not great, and good enough for Pittsburgh.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-09]]): the Arthur Smith and Rodgers marriage in Pittsburgh worked far better in Week 1 than either host anticipated — co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) had expected a mess given the personnel. Harmon had not yet watched the Steelers film back but noted the offense was best when it leaned into Smith's stuff: three of Rodgers' four touchdowns came on play action, and he was more willing to stand in the pocket, with the caveat that there is little mobility left — get close enough to sneeze on him and he goes down. Harmon said he did not expect to leave Week 1 with Jets offense stock up and Jets defense stock down, and flagged Calvin Austin III as a Steelers player he wanted to chart after seeing fun designs getting him free.
- 2025-09-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-15]]): Rodgers looks good and the only thing missing is explosion — the moves and the timing are still there, but when he leaves the pocket he no longer has the initial burst to escape, so he can make one move and then must get rid of the ball rather than stringing moves together that each require fresh burst. Waldman attributed it to both age and injury and expects Rodgers to adapt. In the broader Steelers frame he is 'feeling' Pittsburgh as a legitimate number two in the AFC North now that Cincinnati is 'basically lame' without [[Joe Burrow]], citing [[Jalen Ramsey]]'s play and timely pass rush from T.J. Watt despite four missing defensive starters. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) quipped that old players find ways to compensate for their weaknesses, and pushed back that Baltimore would like a word.
- 2025-10-09 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys; NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-09]]): Rodgers is a top-ten all-time quarterback and his production is unimpeachable, but his statistical profile is uniquely weird — historically high touchdown rate and low interception rate alongside a low yards per attempt, a high sack rate and a high completion rate, a combination no other quarterback has posted. Harstad's read is that Rodgers was best at exactly the stats the public cares about and worst at the ones it ignores, and that had that been reversed he would have been more valuable. His supporting evidence is a fourth-quarter comeback record well below what you would expect from a top-ten passer, weak next to Brees, Brady and Manning — the cost of optimizing interception avoidance is not making the risky throws a comeback requires. He offered Peyton Manning as the mirror image (higher interception rate, best-ever long-shot comeback rate, elite sack avoidance) and Brett Favre as a quarterback who never let a metric become a target. Not a fantasy ranking take.
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): 'the kind of bad in this game that gets people retired.' Harris lists it — [[DK Metcalf]] wide open after a scramble and missed, and Rodgers yelling at someone when he was the one who missed; running around in his own end zone and dropping the ball for a safety; a blown throw to [[Jonnu Smith]] at the 10 that was too high; a five-feet-over-the-head pick trying Metcalf on a post at the end of the half; a double-covered end zone throw Cris Collinsworth dwelt on for ten minutes; and a missed [[Kenneth Gainwell]] touchdown thrown over his head. Guest Jamie Eisenberg of CBS Fantasy (not tracked): 'this is just what he is at this point... he's a 41-year-old guy,' likely to look great next week against Cincinnati but exposed by any defense that pressures him.
