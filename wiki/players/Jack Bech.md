# Jack Bech

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): the TCU receiver's biggest technical issue is attacking with his hands too far apart on overhand attempts, which leads to juggles and drops from clap attacking. Waldman also wants his jump-up-and-through positioning to happen earlier on back-shoulder plays so he gets his back to the defender before attacking the ball. See [[Catch Technique and Ball Tracking]].
- 2025-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-11]]): one of his favorite prospects in the 2025 class. The TCU receiver (6'1', 214, 34.5-inch vertical, 10'5' broad, no forty — Harmon believes he skipped it because it would not have been good) posted a nearly all-green route tree, 79.4% success vs press (91st percentile), zero drops in the sample and what Harmon calls the best contested-catch rate in the class, going down on first contact on only 15% of in-space attempts — one of the lowest rates he has ever charted. The one red flag: 72.4% of snaps at outside-right, and Harmon's historical list of 70%-one-side receivers (Kevin White, Corey Coleman, Laquon Treadwell, [[Javon Baker]], [[Brendan Rice]]) yields only four real dudes — [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]], [[DJ Moore]], [[Drake London]], [[Rashee Rice]] — all of whom moved around the formation. The other weakness is deep speed: 41% success on nine routes, 54.5% on the corner. Harmon sees a power-slot/flanker role, comps him to [[Keenan Allen]] and [[Jakobi Meyers]] (and jokes about the [[Cooper Kupp]] comp), grades him a second-rounder and names Jacksonville at 36 as the ideal fit under Liam Coen alongside [[Chris Godwin]]. James Koh (not tracked) argues he has shown enough to play 50/50 outside as a quarterback-friendly dig/curl target.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21 (Film & Data with Dwain McFarland)]]): Bech is his WR7 with a star, an 86 score just over starter tier — and the player Waldman most fears he has overrated. The swing is narrow: a few borderline pass-catching points he awarded on the judgment that Bech's catch issues were lapses (lost concentration on difficult, wide targets) rather than bad habits like [[Quentin Johnston]]'s clap-catching; take about four and a half points off and Bech is an 82 and a WR10-to-WR15, end of the top 24. He also earned an extra agility point for transitioning downhill at a 90-degree angle in few steps. Waldman sees him as a flanker/slot hybrid who really needs the slot to be unlocked; as a pure flanker he is more a [[Josh Palmer]] type, with [[Michael Wilson]] with a better quarterback as the realistic shape and [[Chris Godwin]] as too rich a ceiling. Guest Dwain McFarland (Fantasy Life, NOT tracked) is puzzled by the film love: a 23rd-percentile production rating, 47% of targets zero-to-nine yards, wide on only 37% of snaps, and 36 spots lower without draft capital — Zierlein's 81 film rating and a projected pick 66 are all that hold him up.
- 2025-04-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-22]]): Bech was the runner-up for best route runner in the 2025 class behind [[Jaylin Noel]], and the call was close enough that Harmon said he could have gone either way. Bech is not fast and not explosive in a straight line, but he is explosive in and out of breaks, which Harmon says is what actually matters for separation. His edge over Noel is at the line of scrimmage: a 79.4% success rate versus press, nearly ten percentage points better, with a better set of release moves. His deficit is versus zone coverage, where Noel's understanding is clearly better — that is what decided the award. James Koh (not tracked) said he loves Bech too and highlighted how much more he wins with strength.
- 2025-04-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-29]]): Bech to the Raiders is a good landing spot — 6-1, 214 with a 34.5-inch vertical and no 40 time on record, but 74.5% success rate vs man and 79.4% vs press. Harmon projects him as a power slot even though he lined up outside in college, arguing he is not slot-only but is best weaponized inside, and points to Chip Kelly's history with big slot receivers: [[Emeka Egbuka]] at Ohio State and Jordan Matthews, a 6-3 pure slot, in Philadelphia in 2014. James Koh (not tracked) worries the pick is same-y with [[Jakobi Meyers]]; Harmon counters that Meyers is on the last year of his deal, is a previous-regime holdover and can play outside, so the two can coexist. Harmon likes Bech's after-catch pop specifically — he went down on first contact on 15% of in-space attempts, where Meyers gives almost nothing after the catch — and thinks [[Geno Smith]]'s accuracy on in-breaking routes suits the group including [[Brock Bowers]].
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: a 2026 payoff to buy at the 2025 discount. When [[Jakobi Meyers]] leaves, Angelo expects Bech to be the second target in the Raiders offense unless they sign or draft another receiver — and since he was a second-round pick, Angelo doubts they spend there again, arguing Las Vegas needs to build through the trenches. He groups Bech with [[Kyle Williams]], [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] and [[Jalen Milroe]] as players non-contending teams should stockpile because the veterans ahead of them will be less productive or elsewhere next season.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether the big-bodied rookie has a path to workload, Waldman says 'fuck him' as a 2025 producer — same verdict as [[Tre Tucker]] but slightly worse on the production side. He does like Bech as a cheap dynasty dart, and separately credits him as the structural reason to buy [[Brock Bowers]]: Bech can function as a move tight end and blocker in a way Michael Mayer's limited man-coverage threat never allowed, forcing defenses to respect both bodies. So the value here is what Bech does for the offense's alignment deception more than what he catches this season.
- 2025-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-25]]): Bech is one of Harmon's guys and he pushes back hard on the idea the second-rounder is slot-only — at TCU he took 73.6 percent of sampled snaps outside and was on the line of scrimmage for 73.2 percent, with a 91st-percentile success rate versus press. He is emphatically not a vertical threat (41 percent on nines, 54.6 percent on corners, both below average for prospects) but was awesome on every other route, wins against man and zone, has reliable hands, and is explosive after the catch — down on first contact on just 15 percent of plays, consistently making the first man miss. Harmon frames him as the ideal condensed-formation, inside-outside player for a Chip Kelly offense that will run heavy 12 personnel, comparing his deployment to [[Puka Nacua]] (a comp Chris Trapasso of CBS also made) and to Kelly's use of Jordan Matthews as a big slot in Philadelphia. Koh (not tracked) expects a [[Emeka Egbuka]]-style role and a super productive rookie year. Harmon says the Raiders need him as an instant-impact player because they had nothing after the catch last year.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]] (ASR garbled him as 'Jack Bash/Besh'): Nguyen likes Bech's physicality a lot and believes run blocking was a big part of why Las Vegas drafted him, but sees him as a slot player in the NFL — and the Raiders' slot is jammed with [[Jakobi Meyers]], [[Brock Bowers]], [[Michael Mayer]] in 12 personnel and [[Ashton Jeanty]]. The team has therefore tried him outside, where Nguyen says he lacks the speed to threaten and has not performed, going without a catch across two preseason games in that role. Nguyen's conclusion: Bech will not contribute a ton this year barring injuries, though he may be the long-term Meyers replacement given Meyers' contract dispute. James Koh (not tracked) partly disagreed on deployment — he thinks keeping Bech off the line matters more than inside-versus-outside, doesn't see him as an X, but believes Bech can interchange with Meyers in an outside flanker role. Koh also called the pick 'samesy' with Meyers' skill set while still rating Bech highly.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-27]]): Harmon still loves the profile — he calls Bech's Reception Perception profile awesome and Koh (not tracked) says it looks great on film too — but urges patience. Koh relays from Raiders beat writer Ted Winn that Bech has barely been on the field in camp, which under Pete Carroll's ethos suggests he is not ready. Harmon's structural concern is that Bech took over 70% of his sampled snaps at one position, right outside receiver; that bucket of one-side-of-the-field prospects almost always needs a year, and the exceptions ([[JuJu Smith-Schuster]], [[Rashee Rice]], [[DJ Moore]], [[Drake London]]) either moved straight into the slot, were gadget/manufactured-touch guys as rookies, or were elite prospects. Rice himself only came on in the back half of his rookie year. Bech is also blocked by [[Jakobi Meyers]] and Harmon reads him as the long-term power-slot replacement for 2026 and beyond — if Meyers is traded, Bech is the beneficiary, not [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]].
- 2025-09-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-05]]): Harmon said he really liked Bech's prospect profile — published on receptionperception.com — with the caveat that Big 12 production at TCU can be wonky, and he was rooting for the player and the backstory. But he conceded there has been nothing positive to find in camp reports: no impact whatsoever. Koh (not tracked) put it bluntly that you cannot find a positive report on Bech. Harmon hoped it will not end up a bad draft pick for the Raiders and suggested rooting for him again in 2026 or later in this season, because 2025 does not look like it is on the table.
