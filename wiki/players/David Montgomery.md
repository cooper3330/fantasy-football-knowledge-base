# David Montgomery

> ⚠️ Takes below are from 2023. Team/role are as of that date and are almost
> certainly stale — treat as historical context until superseded by newer takes.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — [[Brandon Angelo]] (co-host of *Going Deep*) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21 (Pacing and Control)]]: the comparison is with [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and it is about range of outcomes, not quality. Detroit is "much more successful with Jahmyr Gibbs on the field — not because Gibbs is so much better a player than David Montgomery, but because defenses have to think a lot more when Gibbs is out there." His specific limit on Montgomery: he "very rarely will break a 25-plus-yard run," so the distribution of his carries is narrow, where Gibbs forces a defense to defend the whole range dynamically. Explicitly framed as a scheme-leverage point rather than a talent gap.
- 2023-12-21 — **Angelo**, same episode, the positive: names Montgomery among the players who define the Detroit locker room's culture — with [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], Jared Goff and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] — an "unselfish brand of football" he credits for the Lions outperforming their talent. See [[Player Development and Coachability]].

**Fantasy read as of Dec 2023:** neither host makes a start/sit or ranking call
on Montgomery; the take is that he is the lower-variance half of a committee
whose upside sits with the other back.

- 2024-01-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-29]]): named, without a ranking split, as one half of a "dynamic running back duo" that "gives you all the pieces" — part of a broader case that Detroit is built for continued success beyond its 2023 coordinators, alongside [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and [[Sam LaPorta]].
- 2024-05-20 — According to guest Jagger May (Football Guys, untracked) and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-20]]) [Dynasty] [Redraft (Standard)]: May said he's "arguably still overdrafting" David Montgomery but defends it — the "data bros" dismiss him on spreadsheet explosive-run numbers, yet he smashed expectations after leaving Chicago for Detroit, ranking alongside [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] near the top of the league in explosive plays. Waldman built the touchdown-equity case: with a statue-in-the-pocket QB like [[Jared Goff]] who adds no rushing value, offenses without a running quarterback tend to lean on a two-back split — one big-play/receiving back, one goal-line/first-down grinder — and Montgomery fills the latter role. He added that Montgomery's value isn't speed, which he called the trait that makes NFL front offices and analytics evaluators "lose their mind and stop thinking," but rather quickness, processing, vision and getting exactly the needed yardage without chasing a home run. Both project him as a fringe top-20 running back for 2024 even as Gibbs's role grows.
- 2024-07-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-01]]) [Best Ball]: Discussing the Lions backfield split, Waldman defends Montgomery against fans who 'overvalue speed' and write him off as merely slow, arguing he's still good enough to hold a high floor as a top-3-to-5 back even as [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] earns more touches, though his ceiling remains lower than Gibbs's given the receiving-work gap between the two.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): Montgomery is Detroit's designated momentum-changer. Waldman says Dan Campbell's resiliency system depends on identifying who you go to when things are not going right — the player who gets the hard yards, shows the effort, excites teammates and does not give up — and on offense that is Montgomery, with [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] as the other name. This is a role-and-usage read rather than a volume projection: it explains why Montgomery keeps meaningful work in a backfield he shares with [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], because the offense deliberately returns to him in adverse game states.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'feel' Montgomery over [[Kaleb Johnson]] at Underdog RB20 — he is the better overall running back, nicknamed 'Knuckles', and Waldman argues Detroit specifically wants him as the one hitting the tree trunk and splintering it rather than [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]. Waldman also said he would take Montgomery over [[Omarion Hampton]]. Untracked co-host Bob Harris framed the pairing as recency bias: the last thing everyone saw was Gibbs running rampant while Montgomery was out five games with a knee injury, and Harris thinks the market is forgetting that Montgomery 'is there and isn't going anywhere'.
- 2025-05-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-26]]): Waldman is 'feeling that all day long' on [[David Montgomery]] at an RB24 price and calls him definitively overlooked, saying people are discounting him on age when he is still in the prime of his career, in an offense still in its prime with the line, quarterback, receivers and tight end all intact. He was banged up last year but Waldman does not treat that as predictive. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) supplies the numbers: 404 carries, 1,290 yards and 25 touchdowns plus 52 receptions for 458 yards over the last two seasons; 16 fantasy points per game last year, 14th in the league on a per-game basis in the games he played despite missing five with a knee injury. Waldman adds that Montgomery being available as an RB3 later is precisely the reason he might pass on [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] very early.
- 2025-10-16 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Montgomery has looked more efficient of late, including a 75-80 yard run, and Angelo said defenses aren't as scared of him as they are of [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], so he isn't drawing the same defensive keys. Angelo grouped Montgomery with backs who get stronger as a game progresses (in the mold of LaDainian Tomlinson, Jamal Lewis, Marshawn Lynch), while Gibbs is more of a one-move-and-gone home-run hitter -- a 'good problem to have' for Detroit that most teams, like Buffalo with Rhamondre Stevenson and TreVeyon Henderson, don't have.
- 2025-10-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-21 (Week 8 Waivers & W7 Reviews)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Montgomery's role is clearly shrinking behind [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]. His raw line against Tampa Bay (13 carries, 21 yards) looks like a bottled-up performance but was worse than it appears -- four of those carries came on two clock-killing series to end the game, leaving only 11 touches that actually mattered and zero red-zone work, though Detroit barely got into the red zone at all. Snap share confirms the shift: 23 snaps to Gibbs's 36 on Monday night, after 16-to-37 the week before. Harris said fantasy managers should adjust expectations for Montgomery downward coming out of Detroit's Week 8 bye, framing him as the clear change-of-pace/closer role rather than a real committee partner going forward.
- 2025-12-08 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-12-08]]) [Dynasty]: despite a 35-yard touchdown run, Waldman said Montgomery's 'heyday' as Detroit's lead back is over -- [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] has clearly taken over as the every-down and red-zone back, with the touch-share gap widening from roughly RB5-to-RB9 last year to RB3-to-RB23 this year. Waldman still called Montgomery 'a quality back' and 'a very productive second piece' he's still playing in many lineups, but cautioned managers to be prepared for less consistent weekly outcomes as Gibbs' workload grows.
- 2026-01-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Ben Gretsch (untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-15]]): Montgomery is the only back on the list with two years left on his deal, but none of it is guaranteed -- a $2M cap hit would save the Lions $6M against the cap and recoup all $5.5M of his real annual salary. Both read recent leaks -- reported talks between Montgomery and the team about finding him a situation with more playing time -- as team-driven signaling that he won't be back in Detroit. His touches fell from roughly 14 carries/game in 2024 to about 9/game in 2025, and he expressed frustration with the role. Gretsch says [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] has taken over: despite being the smaller, speed-oriented back, Gibbs has shown he can still run effectively between the tackles and gets to the hole quickly enough to be trusted in short yardage, making him hard to take off the field at all now.
- 2026-01-22 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-22]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Reading Brad Holmes' press conferences and Detroit's usage trends, Angelo thinks Montgomery (29) may not be back with the Lions now that [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] has proven a top-three back under 24 who'll get the bulk of the workload; a banged-up offensive line (lost its center) already limited Montgomery's second-level usage this year. Angelo floated Montgomery as an ideal complementary/handcuff back behind [[Saquon Barkley]] in Philadelphia's run-first offense, comparing the fit to Jamal Williams backing up Gibbs in Detroit.
- 2026-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-13]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: cited as the clearest cautionary example of a college trait that doesn't survive the jump to the NFL -- Montgomery's knack at Iowa State for negating contact and bouncing runs outside to still get first downs on 3rd-and-1 got 'a rude awakening' as a rookie with the Bears, because NFL defenders close the half-step gap that college defenders couldn't.
- 2026-03-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: feels good about Montgomery's trade from Detroit to Houston, projecting an A-back/B-back committee with the Texans' incumbent backfield. He called Montgomery 'an upgrade to Nick Chubb' at this point ('time is undefeated'), citing his pass-catching out of the backfield, goal-line work and agility, and expects that edge to hold for a year or two. Waldman thinks Detroit moved off him at the right time to recoup value and Houston gains short-term value in return. Also flagged in the same breath: the deal included offensive-line pieces (one lineman going to Detroit), and Houston separately traded Titus Howard to Cleveland for a fifth-round pick -- leaving Houston still needing more O-line help for CJ Stroud.
- 2026-03-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-03-03]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon called the Texans' trade for David Montgomery (giving up a backup guard and a 4th-round pick, receiving Montgomery plus a later pick) potentially disastrous if Houston plans to run him out as its lead back -- he's entering his age-29 season, moving from one of the NFL's best offensive lines to one of its worst (Houston has ranked 31st in rushing success rate and EPA since drafting [[C.J. Stroud]]), and was never a hyper-efficient runner even in Detroit. Montgomery reportedly viewed himself as a co-starter with [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] in Detroit (320 touches for Gibbs versus roughly 180 for Montgomery last year), which Harmon says explains why he wanted a fresh start. Harmon does think Montgomery is 'a fine, good quality back' and a plus pass protector, just not a plausible three-down centerpiece paired with [[Woody Marks]], whom Harmon graded as literally the worst pass-protecting back in last year's rookie class.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Andy Behrens (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-05]]): Houston traded a fourth-round pick for Montgomery after early hype around Woody Marks as the presumptive starter. Harris predicts 2026 Montgomery will be better than 2025 Nick Chubb, though not quite peak Joe Mixon-2024 level, calling him 'a really good pro' -- 'not spectacular necessarily, but a very smart runner who is going to ring every yard out of most plays.' Behrens agrees it's a smart move for the team, while noting Houston's offensive line quality is still unproven. Both frame this as a move that pushes Marks into a more sustainable timeshare role rather than a bell-cow workload.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris ranks [[David Montgomery]] in the RB23-30 dead-zone tier and likes his Houston landing, projecting he'll be on the better side of roughly a 60/40 split with [[Woody Marks]]. Co-host Josh (untracked) argued Montgomery could move up three or four spots given he's shown this tandem role can be productive before, but Harris was noncommittal on workshare this early ('we're guessing at a lot of stuff we're not going to know until September'), and capped his ceiling relative to [[Chuba Hubbard]] -- Harris doesn't think Montgomery has the touchdown upside a truly bad, run-funnel offense could give a back, calling him more of a flex-only projection for now.
- 2026-05-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-14]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon was openly skeptical of Houston's plan to fix its run game by signing Montgomery, noting he's never held a true full-time role and was productive in Detroit behind one of the league's better offensive lines -- a very different situation than one of the worst lines in Houston. Harmon predicted flatly the run game 'will not' be fixed by the move and called it puzzling given Houston also didn't address the position in the draft.
- 2026-07-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-07-06]]) [Best Ball, Dynasty]: Waldman feels 2026 is Montgomery's year as the clear feature back after the failed [[Woody Marks]] receiving-back experiment -- 'they gave him an extensive run last year and realized he's not really the answer,' so the team is going 'smash mouth' with Montgomery as the focal point. He cites reporter Aaron Wilson's camp reporting that coaches view Montgomery as capable of 'everything, including catching the ball,' and points to the team's playoff-loss inability to run the ball against New England as the impetus for the shift. Waldman comps the outlook to Joe Mixon's first year in a featured role (RB10 in points per game) and calls Montgomery's current RB23-ish ADP 'great value' -- a solid RB2 floor with RB1-upside weeks. Bob Harris (untracked) briefly argued the contrarian side before conceding the point.
- 2026-07-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Dave Kluge (footballguys.com, untracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-10]]): Kluge is '100% in' on David Montgomery as Houston's three-down back, comparing the staff's likely commitment to how they previously leaned on [[Nick Chubb]] and [[Joe Mixon]], and says no amount of Woody Marks beat buzz -- including an Aaron Wilson report floating Marks as a pass-catching option -- will change that. Harris partially agrees Montgomery is the better back overall, especially between the tackles, but pushes back that [[Woody Marks]] is 'clearly better at some things' and could see real third-down work, cautioning that a viral Marks camp report shouldn't scare drafters off a Montgomery bargain at his mid-August ADP.
- 2026-07-21 — According to James Koh (untracked co-host) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-21]]): extremely low on new Texans RB David Montgomery (29, on his third team), calling him 'toast' and predicting he'll replicate a disappointing 2025 [[Nick Chubb]]-in-Houston outcome -- 'a lot of two-yard runs, running into the backs of his offensive line.' Koh's bar for a good outcome is simply nudging Houston's rushing offense (31st in success rate since [[C.J. Stroud]] was drafted) toward league average. [[Matt Harmon]] pushed back that Houston's offensive line, while still 'a work in progress,' has genuinely improved and that even a modest step -- something like 25th in the league -- would represent real progress, and noted he's not as bearish on Montgomery as Koh. Both agreed the bar for Montgomery to be a positive Houston acquisition is very low given the team's historically bottom-tier run-game success rate.
- 2026-08-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Dave Richard (CBS Fantasy, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-03]]): tied to the same Houston offensive-line concerns as [[Nico Collins]]. Harris has Montgomery as a top-60 PPR running back currently but says he could move him outside the top 60 if the line keeps struggling through joint practices, since Montgomery's workload only translates to fantasy value if the line 'pops lanes' for him. Richard adds Montgomery is 'staring at a workload that is appealing to fantasy managers,' but warns it 'could be a slog' behind this front five if the line doesn't gel the way Chicago's did last season.
