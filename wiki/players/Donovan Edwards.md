# Donovan Edwards

## Expert Takes
<!-- Append new dated, attributed bullets here as sources are ingested.
     Don't overwrite old ones — see CLAUDE.md rule 4. -->
- 2024-09-25 — According to guest Felix Sharpe of Campus2Canton (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-25 (Campus2Canton with Felix Sharpe)]]) [Dynasty]: the former five-star and college-football-video-game cover athlete has taken a backseat to [[Kalel Mullings]], with just five missed tackles forced on the season to Mullings' 17. Sharpe argued Edwards was always more athlete than traditional Michigan back — he ran real routes at receiver out of West Bloomfield and had a 10-reception game as a freshman on wheel routes, but Michigan has not used him that way since, instead running him into the back of the line. His big games (Ohio State, the national championship) came when the box emptied and he sprinted through, which Sharpe doubted translates. [[Matt Waldman]] noted a flat-footed 180-pound corner can send the 6'1' Edwards backwards. Both saw a path if an NFL coordinator uses him in space the way Ben Johnson uses [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]; that is not happening at Michigan.
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: still a believer despite the market souring on him. Waldman wants him in a gap scheme where he can hit it downhill and use his strength, plus wheel routes, screens and RPO work where he will catch it and let it fly. He frames two comps as sides of one coin: Darren McFadden, the overhyped athlete who could never win contact (Waldman's memory of McFadden bouncing backward off a flat-footed safety with a ten-yard head start), and David Johnson, the big fast back who was a better receiver than nuanced runner and grew into it in Arizona. Waldman does not expect a David Johnson outcome but sees real untapped skill, notes Edwards played his big Michigan season on a partially torn quad, and would not be surprised if a fifth- or sixth-round landing with a good line and narrow role produces an [[Elijah Mitchell]]-style surprise. [[Brandon Angelo]] agreed he looked like a possible second-rounder two years ago and simply did not improve, but still offers more down to down than [[Kalel Mullings]].
- 2025-09-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-01]]): Waldman 'kind of' thinks Edwards is worth monitoring in Washington, though he is probably practice-squad-bound. He recounts a spectacular Michigan season played with a partially torn quad, then being buried behind [[Blake Corum]] and later Kalel Mullings, never becoming the player many projected. The traits are still there — he can catch, has great speed, is a tough dude — but he profiles best in a gap scheme or an offense that keeps skill-player roles simple, and Waldman thinks Cliff Kingsbury can simplify enough that Edwards would return value if pressed into service, in the same way he expects [[Ollie Gordon II]] to if needed.
