## Summary
A *Film and Theory* episode with [[Matt Waldman]] and co-host Adam Harstad (Football Guys; NOT a tracked expert). Three segments:

1. **Why passing yards are down.** Waldman opened with an ESPN piece showing average passing yards per game falling from ~250 in 2016 to ~206, and the article's explanations (two-high umbrella coverages, younger and less-prepared starting quarterbacks, simplified schemes to win now). Harstad argued nearly every popular explanation is wrong: through four weeks this was the third-best season since 2016 in points per drive, so offense is not actually down — the shift is that throws to running backs have collapsed while running back carries and quarterback scrambles have risen, producing fewer clock stoppages, fewer drives per game and lower raw totals at similar per-drive efficiency. He framed running back targets as functionally equivalent to runs and scrambles, not to receiver targets, and said the current run/pass split and depressed tight end production both resemble 2004-05 — one locally optimal offensive profile replacing another rather than a structural decline.
2. **Harstad's Razor / selection bias.** Harstad's new Football Guys piece: assume anything surprising is selection bias until proven otherwise. Examples included Berkson's paradox (SAT-vs-grades correlation within one college; Chase Stuart's top-100 rushers before/after age 27 finding), and conditioning on a collider (marital satisfaction, kids and divorce).
3. **Players born in the wrong era.** Waldman picked Steve McNair, Cordarrelle Patterson and Roy Green; Harstad picked Percy Harvin, Daryl Lamonica (and Warren Wells), Henry Ellard and Tim Brown, with a Todd Marinovich / Al Davis digression.
