# Matt Waldman's RSP Cast — 2024-02-02

**Raw transcript:** `raw/ingested/rsp-cast/2024-02-02-rsp-the-nfl-hall-of-fame-and-our-picks-for-the-24-class-rsp-film-and-theor.md`

## Summary

*RSP Film and Theory* with co-host **Adam Harstad** (Footballguys — not a
tracked expert in this wiki). The entire episode is a Pro Football Hall of
Fame discussion pegged to the Class of 2024 modern-era nominees (announced
February 8): institutional philosophy (big-hall vs. small-hall, inconsistent
positional standards — Harstad's running example is wide receiver — a
separate voting path proposed for special teamers, and how the Hall's
"ignore off-field incidents" guideline is applied unevenly, worked through
via Jim Tyrer, O.J. Simpson, Ray Lewis, Jim Brown and Darren Sharper), then
personal top-five picks from the modern ballot (Eric Allen, Jared Allen,
Willie Anderson, Jahri Evans, Dwight Freeney, Antonio Gates, Rodney
Harrison, Devin Hester, Torry Holt, Andre Johnson, Julius Peppers, Fred
Taylor, Reggie Wayne, Patrick Willis, Darren Woodson) plus a senior-list
aside (Randy Gradishar, Steve Tasker). No current NFL players, prospects, or
fantasy-relevant news are discussed anywhere in the episode — this is almost
entirely outside the wiki's scope by design.

Two pieces of general evaluation reasoning are durable enough to log against
existing concept pages rather than being discarded outright: Harstad's
peak-vs-longevity framework for career value (comparing a player to the
league-average baseline over his career, rather than to a replacement-level
baseline) paired with his position-specific point that running back and
middle linebacker are the two positions where a short career is "most
forgivable" given the wear and tear — both applied to
[[Aging Curves and Career Longevity]]. And Harstad's explicit callback to
this wiki's own scheme-vs-talent debate — "it's like the Brock Purdy
discussion" — made while arguing Andre Johnson "transcended" a
talent-starved Houston passing offense, applied to [[Scheme vs Talent]] as a
reinforcing, generalized version of the same test.
