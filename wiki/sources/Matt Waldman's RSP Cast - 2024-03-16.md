# 2024 NFL Draft WR Class A-Z: Matt Waldman's RSP Solo Cast

Solo episode (no co-host) — [[Matt Waldman]] runs an alphabetical A-Z sweep of
the 54 wide receivers in his 2024 Rookie Scouting Portfolio, ahead of the
guide's April 1 release. Two framing threads before the player rundown:

1. **Is 2024 a historic WR class?** Waldman calls it one of the three best he's
   studied in the last decade, potentially rivaling the 2014 class (Mike Evans,
   Odell Beckham Jr., Brandon Cooks, Davante Adams, Jarvis Landry). Grounds the
   claim in draftable-grade counts (48 of 54 studied) and "compelling talent"
   depth beyond the top 15, but tempers it with receiver career-longevity data
   — see the new [[Aging Curves and Career Longevity]] entry — and a reminder
   that class quality talk is about *potential*, not outcome.
2. **Grading philosophy: tiers over strict rankings.** Uses a worked
   88-grade-vs-90-grade example ([[Puka Nacua]] in [[Cooper Kupp]]'s Rams role,
   [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]]'s #1-in-2023 grade sliding to #8 in this deeper
   class) to argue scheme fit and depth-chart landing spot can matter as much
   as the underlying grade — logged on [[Prospect Pro-Readiness vs Ceiling]].

## Not given pages

Prospects Waldman covered briefly without a page-worthy evaluative verdict
(role/ceiling projection). Captured here so nothing is silently lost:

- **Cornelius Johnson** (Michigan) — pass catching is "at polar extremes":
  tracks the ball well downfield but misjudges height/hand position on
  high-low targets, leading to drops; "there's potential there."
- **David White Jr.** (Western Carolina) — capable pass catcher; needs to
  refine his breaks and choice of hand-attack technique.
- **Devaughn Vele** (Washington State; ASR: "Devon Vele") — stacks defenders
  on vertical stems better than many higher-graded prospects; a "nice
  developmental prospect" if he sells the vertical concept of his routes more.
- **Hayden Hatton** (Idaho) — good pass catcher for his level, but clap-attacks
  almost everything, which could cap his career growth if uncorrected.
- **Isaiah Williams** (Illinois, former QB convert) — strong stem work, quick
  twitch and pacing; game "only going to go so far" until his breaks become a
  real strength — "his hands won't fail him."
- **Jacob Cowling** (Arizona) — momentum-generated power for his size; reliable
  after-the-catch weapon in a spread offense on RPO screens/quick-hitters.
- **Jaxon Janke** (South Dakota State; ASR: "Jayden Janky") — gets his pads
  over low shots without breaking stride; needs a better release/jump-through
  vs. tight coverage.
- **Jaquan Jackson** (Tulane) — slot-sized big-play/YAC threat; good release
  repertoire vs. off coverage but gets outmaneuvered by skilled man corners.
- **Jalen Coker** (Holy Cross) — pass-catching strength, but opens his chest to
  press defenders, inviting jams he then has to fight through.
- **Jamari Thrash** (Louisville, ex-Georgia State) — capable hands but has
  clap-attack lapses and loses the ball on full-extension targets under
  contact.
- **Javon Antonio** (Southeast Missouri State) — needs to better read whether a
  target is arriving in stride or underthrown to attack it correctly.
- **Jeremiah Hickson** (New Mexico State; name uncertain in ASR) — a quick-twitch
  comp to Desean Jackson/Paul Richardson, but shows his eyes to the QB too
  early in zone, tipping off coverage.
- **Jordan Watkins** (Ole Miss, ex-Louisville) — quick transitions and jump-cut
  ability; whether that physical tool set translates into true route running
  is still an open question.
- **Jordan Whittington** (Texas) — zone receiver with YAC skill; Waldman's
  read is his best NFL shot is special teams.
- **A small-school receiver Waldman names "Ladatric Griffin"** — ASR-garbled
  name not confidently resolved to a real prospect; not created per naming
  rule. Note only: "painfully close to" elite testable traits, projected as a
  sub-package/reserve piece who could start in an injury pinch.
- **Marcus Rosemy-Jacksaint** (Georgia) — physical, high-effort, a team leader;
  projected mainly as a special-teams ace.
- **A small-school receiver Waldman names sounding like "Sam Pinckney"** —
  spelling not confidently resolved; strength is winning the ball with
  positioning/physicality; Travis Fulgham/Tim Patrick-type fourth or fifth
  outside option comp.
- **Tahj Washington** (USC) — best in zone off coverage baiting defenders;
  separation-dependent success in man coverage.
- **Tavion Robinson** (Kentucky) — best as a short-range option; intermediate
  breaks drift and create coverage lanes; still working on eliminating drops
  on wide-open targets.
- **Tayshon Palmer** (UAB) — breaks need real work; risks a "glorified Kenny
  Golladay"-type limited role if he can't pair his deep speed with underneath
  polish.
- **Tre Mosley** (Michigan State; ASR: "Trape mostly") — absent a clearer look
  at his athletic ability on tape, projects as a UDFA candidate competing for
  a reserve/special-teams role.
- **Tyrone Howell** — leaves his feet unnecessarily on targets he could attack
  with extended arms instead.
- **Zakhari Franklin** (UTSA) — quick and explosive but speed/footwork don't
  look sufficient to threaten cornerbacks in isolation on longer stems;
  UTSA's career leading receiver in production.
- **A receiver Waldman names sounding like "Trey/Troy Harrell,"** possibly not
  combine-invited — flagged purely for elite tracked in-game speed (GPS 22+
  mph multiple times, informal 40 times as low as 4.19–4.24) rather than a
  scouted evaluation; name not confidently resolved.
