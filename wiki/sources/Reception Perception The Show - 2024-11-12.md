## Summary
[[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) react to a Week 10 slate they call one of the worst football weekends in years: 11 different teams got fewer than 200 passing yards, [[Jared Goff]] threw five interceptions, [[Joe Flacco]] and [[Sam Darnold]] three apiece, [[Daniel Jones]] two with no scores. Harmon resists the 'NFL product is broken' macro take and instead blames a widening scheme gap, an unusual wave of receiver injuries, and quarterbacks promoted above what they are good at; Koh argues the mismatch between offensive coordinators and the quarterbacks they are handed is at an all-time low, as is organizational patience.

The body of the episode is a 'cooked or not for 2024' pass through four offenses — the [[New York Jets]] ([[Aaron Rodgers]] / [[Davante Adams]] chemistry that never materialized), Chicago (a blueprint for how to ruin [[Caleb Williams]]), the Giants ([[Daniel Jones]] on the way out, [[Malik Nabers]] stalled), and Indianapolis ([[Anthony Richardson]] vs [[Joe Flacco]], with [[Bryce Young]] in Carolina as the counter-example of a benching that worked) — followed by a buy/sell on surprising Week 10 performances: [[Marquez Valdes-Scantling]], [[Jauan Jennings]] and [[John Metchie III]]. The Jennings segment includes an extended alignment breakdown from the NGS route chart.
