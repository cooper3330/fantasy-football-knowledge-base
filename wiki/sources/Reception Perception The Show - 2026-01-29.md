## Summary
Matt Harmon hosts JJ Zachariason of Late Round Fantasy Football (not a tracked expert; James Koh is out this week) for a data-driven deep dive on JJ's research into the 2025 'death of the WR2.' They cover: WR fantasy scoring hitting its lowest total since 2017 and lowest share of combined RB/WR/TE points since at least 2011; the rise of 12-personnel usage (22.4% league average, a record) and its link to two-high safety defenses lowering average aDOT and pushing offenses toward heavier, play-action-friendly packages; a collapse in target share for 10-15% target-share WRs while the elite 25%+ tier held steady; the disappearance of the pure slot archetype (300+ routes at 50/60/70% slot rate) league-wide; two of JJ's 'pet theories' (rising WR injury rates from more middle-of-field usage, and too much good WR talent diluting individual target share); a reassessment of the 'targets are earned' framework; and a closing discussion of which WR archetypes (three-way flex/X/slot players, throwback X receivers, and disappearing slot specialists) are best- and worst-positioned for 2026 in both redraft and dynasty.
