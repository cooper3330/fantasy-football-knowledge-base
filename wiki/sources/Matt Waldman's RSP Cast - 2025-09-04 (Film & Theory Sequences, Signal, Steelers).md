## Summary
Season-opening edition of *RSP Film & Theory* with [[Matt Waldman]] and co-host Adam Harstad of Footballguys (NOT a tracked expert). Harstad's framing thesis: most of what fantasy managers observe is a random walk, and the *sequence* in which good and bad weeks arrive carries far less signal than the storytelling brain wants — a late-season hot streak or a week-one explosion from a role player (he cites the Sean Payton-era Saints situational deep threats) is usually just three good games landing where they happened to land. He notes ADP remains more predictive than results through roughly the first four weeks. From there: fumbles and why the optimal fumble rate is never zero; when an injury actually justifies aggressive FAAB (clear successor vs. muddy committee); redraft trade markets as effectively dead vs. dynasty trade liquidity; the value of 'retread' head coaches and culture-building over chasing the next wunderkind; and why old quarterbacks who post a down year often rebound if they don't retire.

The back half is a long Steelers offense segment — Waldman's most-watched team — on whether [[Aaron Rodgers]] has the conceptually advanced weapons to run his pre-snap chess game, with [[DK Metcalf]], [[Calvin Austin III]], [[Pat Freiermuth]] and [[Jaylen Warren]] each assessed on that criterion, plus a Jordy Nelson comp for Metcalf and the Peyton Manning/Demaryius Thomas precedent for quarterback-driven receiver development.
