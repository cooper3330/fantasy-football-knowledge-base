## Summary
Year-end 'Feel It or F**k It' rapid-reaction episode with [[Matt Waldman]] and co-host Bob Harris (NOT a tracked expert; not to be confused with [[Chris Harris]]). Waldman opens with an RSP production update — roughly halfway through the ~150 receivers he charts for the 20th-edition Rookie Scouting Portfolio, five to seven games per player, two to three players a day, with pre-orders open at $21.95 and the written positional analysis to follow in March. He also discusses the RSP's standing alongside Dane Brugler's 'The Beast' as one of the two independent draft guides most purchased by NFL scouts and personnel staff.

The body of the show is a series of yes/no reactions: whether sophomore slumps are real, whether [[C.J. Stroud]]'s down year was a slump or infrastructure collapse, whether [[Caleb Williams]] had a bad rookie season, whether [[Ladd McConkey]] was a fluke, [[Brian Thomas Jr.]]'s emergence, whether Cleveland erred moving on from [[Baker Mayfield]], [[Xavier Worthy]]'s archetype, [[George Pickens]]'s ceiling, [[Javon Baker]]'s year-two odds, [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] as Sean Payton's next Lance Moore, [[Trey McBride]] and [[Kyler Murray]], [[Brock Bowers]]'s rookie records, Ameer Abdullah and player labeling, [[Parker Washington]]'s second-contract outlook, the [[Kenny Pickett]] 'massive hit' framing, [[Saquon Barkley]]'s 2,000-yard chase and asterisk debate, [[Malik Nabers]] as an Odell Beckham/Jarvis Landry hybrid, and the [[Michael Penix Jr.]]-led Atlanta passing game as a 2025 draft-value target. Bob Harris runs an extended argument that receiver production survives bad quarterback play.
