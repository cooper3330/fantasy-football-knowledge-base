## Summary

*Going Deep*, with [[Brandon Angelo]] (tracked expert). Opens with an extended
discussion of the Mike Vrabel firing and Bill Belichick's uncertain next move
(continuing a running Belichick-legacy thread from earlier episodes) — **not**
ingested as wiki content, as it's coaching-carousel speculation with no
fantasy-relevant player claim, aside from one real aside: [[Treylon Burks]]'s
outlook takes a real hit ("his injury ship has kind of sailed"), logged on his
page.

The bulk of the episode is 2024 NFL Draft prospect evaluation — pre-draft, so
entirely [[Dynasty]]-relevant and to be read as scouting context on players
not yet on NFL rosters at the time:

1. **[[Blake Corum]]** (Michigan RB) — Angelo's case for "the safest prospect
   in this class," built on [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]] rather
   than measurables. The discussion extends that concept with a new explicit
   mechanism, "compensatory skill" (Waldman, via a Frank Gore/Devin
   Singletary/Adrian Peterson discussion).
2. **[[Michael Penix Jr.]]** (Washington QB) — dominated by his injury
   history; both hosts want him to sit behind a veteran (Jordan Love/Geno
   Smith comps) rather than start early. Extends
   [[Player Development and Coachability]] with a QB-durability angle and a
   set of historical recovery/non-recovery comps (Steve Young, Terry
   Bradshaw, Jim Plunkett vs. Trent Edwards, Carson Strong).
3. **[[Keon Coleman]]** (Florida State WR) — the "rollercoaster" of the
   episode title: public perception swung from athletic freak to overrated
   and stiff; both hosts land in the middle, with a real scheme-dependency
   risk (a Courtland Sutton-style outside-only pigeonhole) if he lands
   somewhere without anticipatory, timing-based throws.
4. **[[Ainias Smith]]** (Texas A&M WR/RB) — the "sleeper miscast as a
   gadget," the episode's other title subject. ASR renders his name
   inconsistently ("Aniah Smith" / "Anais Smith" / "Elias Smith"); normalized
   to his real name. Both hosts are unusually high on him, contrasting his
   clean catch-point technique with Kadarius Toney's dynamic-but-inefficient
   game, and flagging the risk of a permanent gadget-role typecast (Lynn
   Bowden Jr., LaViska Shenault comps).

Closes with a Brandon Angelo bio/plug segment (his "Y series" pre-draft
methodology, Rookie Scouting Portfolio subscription info, and a Darkness to
Light charity note) — not wiki content.
