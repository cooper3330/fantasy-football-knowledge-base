## Summary
A *Film and Theory* episode with [[Matt Waldman]] and co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys; NOT a tracked expert). Three linked threads: (1) the [[Anthony Richardson]] 'tap-out' narrative and how the industry punishes players who deviate from a normative football-player archetype; (2) whether historical statistical comps are useful, and how analyst degrees of freedom turn them into p-hacking; (3) the difference between projecting confidence and projecting certainty, illustrated by Harstad's 2023 refusal to sell [[Drake London]] for [[Zay Flowers]] in [[Dynasty]].

Waldman argued that a StatHead comp list Jason Wood posted on the Footballguys board mixed 'apples, kumquats, raisins and anvils', and that Richardson should be modeled against a physical-freak QB archetype ([[Josh Allen]], Steve McNair, Terry Bradshaw, John Elway, Cam Newton) rather than against every inefficient young starter. Harstad agreed archetype sorting has value but insisted historical comps are 'voodoo that kind of works', and that a comp list with an 80-90% hit rate is proof you screwed up rather than proof of insight. Harstad also offered a trust heuristic: analysts who admit 'I was right but for the wrong reasons', not just 'wrong for the right reasons'.
