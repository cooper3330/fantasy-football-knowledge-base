## Summary
A two-part *Film and Theory* episode with [[Matt Waldman]] and co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys; NOT a tracked expert).

Part one is a long theory segment on Goodhart's Law — 'when a metric becomes a target, it ceases to be a good metric.' Harstad walked it from writing metrics (page views, time on page) through NBA shooting percentage and end-of-quarter heaves, into player evaluation: drops, receiver height/weight thresholds, SPARQ scores, Bill Parcells' planet theory and his quarterback checklist. Waldman added the Eagles' sub-6-foot receiver cutoff, the [[Dalvin Cook]] compensatory-factors argument, and his own abandoned 'shies away from pressure' quarterback rule that flagged Derek Carr and Jimmy Garoppolo. Harstad's counterexamples: [[Aaron Rodgers]] optimizing the stats the public cares about, Peyton Manning excelling at the ones it doesn't, Brett Favre and Steve McNair never letting a metric become a target, and Joey Harrington's sack-avoidance orders wrecking an offense. Harstad's slogan: 'we value what we measure, so make sure you measure what you value.'

Part two is on blowing up a [[Dynasty]] roster. Harstad has done exactly one true strip-to-studs rebuild in ~20 years of dynasty, turning over 19-20 of 24 roster spots and winning the title the next year; he argues most managers rebuild too early and then rebuild too slowly. His framework: roster value decays ~20% per year as each incoming rookie class claims ~20% of the pool, so the rebuild goal is 'don't lose value.' Preferred stores of value: rookie picks, proven veteran quarterbacks in their prime, rookie receivers bought and sophomore receivers sold, and heavily discounted dented cans. Waldman described his own 2024 blow-up and his devy-league running back stockpile; Harstad quantified why concentrating picks at one position lowers both variance and upside.
