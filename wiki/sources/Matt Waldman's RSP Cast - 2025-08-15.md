## Summary
[[Matt Waldman]] hosts Dan Hatman, director of the Scouting Academy and a former Giants and Eagles scout (a guest, NOT a tracked expert), for a long methodological conversation about what training camp and preseason reporting actually mean. Hatman argues most camp content is noise: there is a survivor bias baked into camp rosters, so every player there flashes something, and practice periods are scripted experiments in which coordinators deliberately isolate one skill (a back's blitz pickup scan across the formation, a corner's ability to play the ball in the boundary) rather than staging a fair fight. Waldman adds the draft-capital rep bias he learned from Ryan Riddle, the Carlos Hyde/Isaiah Crowell example, and the point that veterans practice to fail on purpose while roster hopefuls are only asked to do the basics fast. Both note that observers simply follow their priors, and that redraft decision season inflates the evidentiary weight of practice. The second half covers first-team-rep meaning, roster versatility versus redundancy, Hatman's on-ramp metaphor for rookie development, analyst blind spots among former players, and a first look at the 2026 college running back class.
