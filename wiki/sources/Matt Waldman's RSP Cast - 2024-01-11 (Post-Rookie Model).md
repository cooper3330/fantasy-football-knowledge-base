## Summary

*RSP Film and Theory*, with **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert).
The whole episode is built around one thing: Harstad unveils his annual
[[Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model]] — a touchdown-adjusted yards-per-route-run
and usage-rate composite score — applied to the full, unusually large 2023
rookie wide receiver class (18–19 qualifiers, versus a typical ~10 and a
previous record of 15). The two hosts work through the class from the bottom
of the score distribution to the top, cross-referencing the model against
their own pre-draft rankings and tape opinions the whole way.

The episode functions as a running track-record audit as much as a player
discussion: Waldman repeatedly checks the model's ranking of a given player
against where he had that player pre-draft, and flags several explicit misses
([[Rashee Rice]] foremost, also Kayshon Boutte, no page — see "Not given
pages" below) alongside hits he
undersells rather than oversells ([[Puka Nacua]], [[Jayden Reed]]). The
biggest standing disagreement between model and market that both hosts agree
on is [[Dontayvion Wicks]] — "the biggest buy recommendation by the model" in
the whole class relative to how cheaply he can be acquired. A long-running
open question from earlier in the season — [[Drake London]] vs. [[Zay Flowers]]
— gets closed decisively toward London. [[Rashee Rice]]'s role is the episode's
central live debate: Waldman and (independently) [[Matt Harmon]] both read his
Kansas City role as more scheme-manufactured than his SMU projection
suggested, while acknowledging the model and several historical comps
(Tyreek Hill, Chris Godwin) argue he could still be a star.

Four rookie receivers get their first evaluative page in this ingest, each on
the strength of an individual tape/model discussion rather than a passing
mention: [[Cedric Tillman]], [[Tyler Scott]], [[Jonathan Mingo]], and
[[Demario Douglas]].
