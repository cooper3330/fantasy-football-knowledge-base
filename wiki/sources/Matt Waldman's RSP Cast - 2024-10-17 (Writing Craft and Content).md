## Summary
A deliberately non-football *Film and Theory* episode: [[Matt Waldman]] and co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys; NOT a tracked expert) open the episode by saying they have 'nothing for you on the fantasy front' and instead go behind the curtain on the craft of writing and content creation in the fantasy industry.

Themes covered: the near-total absence of audience feedback for writers (Harstad's analogy of shouting into a void; his point that no feedback usually means it was fine, because 'when it's bad the universe will very quickly let you know'); the outsized impact of a two-sentence note of encouragement to someone early in their career (Harstad cites a 2014 email from Gene Bramall); how badly constructive criticism lands even when correct (Harstad recounts being 'irate' for weeks over Waldman's praise-sandwich email telling him to break up his long paragraphs, then conceding writers he admired, like Mike Tannier, wrote in two-sentence paragraphs); that fantasy football writing is two crafts, fantasy AND writing, neither subordinate to the other; deadlines and the impossibility of perfection at volume (Waldman on editing an ~1,100-page RSP annually in a month versus 72-page magazines that took 9-12 weeks); rest as part of the work (Harstad's 10%/90% split of writing on- versus off-screen, the US Army artillery-shift study, Waldman's Friday-afternoon football blackout and 3-4 burnouts a year); outlining as a tool Waldman resisted and Harstad doesn't need; the split between 'game show host' topical content creators and niche depth writers; and incentive alignment, with Harstad refusing to profit from a Pro Football Reference affiliate link (routing 100% to charity) and Waldman declining ads, gambling affiliates and 'ownership' by wealthy buyers so that everything funnels to the RSP.

No players were evaluated and no rankings were given.
