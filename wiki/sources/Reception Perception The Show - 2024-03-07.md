# Calvin Ridley, Marquise Brown & Tee Higgins Breakdown

Short free-agency-preview episode. Matt Harmon and co-host James Koh (not a
tracked expert) walk through three freshly-posted [[Reception Perception
Methodology]] profiles — [[Calvin Ridley]], [[Marquise Brown]] and [[Tee
Higgins]] — plus a closing comparative aside on [[Nico Collins]]. Harmon notes
Mike Evans's, Higgins's, Ridley's and Brown's profiles are already live on
ReceptionPerception.com, with Michael Pittman Jr., Gabe Davis, Curtis Samuel,
Odell Beckham Jr., Kendrick Bourne and Darnell Mooney landing next; full
prospect coverage starts the week of March 25.

**Calvin Ridley** posted a down, inconsistent 2023 (1,000+ yards, 8 TDs, but a
career-low 55.9% catch rate) that Harmon frames as more about deployment than
decline — still green (above-average) on every charted route but the curl,
strong success rates vs. man (71.9%) and zone (80.4%), but a real press-coverage
drop-off (66.7%, down ~10 points from his Atlanta years). Both hosts fault
Jacksonville's static X-receiver-only usage and thin route tree (48.6% of his
routes were 9/curl/comeback, under 10% slants) for compounding the press issue,
contrasted with a more 50/50 X/flanker split in his best Atlanta seasons.

**Marquise Brown** ("Hollywood") posted career lows across the board (50.5%
catch rate, career-low man/zone success rates) that Harmon partly attributes to
a heel/foot injury and Arizona's scheme — 77.4% of his snaps lined up on the
line as an X receiver despite being undersized and never a strong press-man
beater, with a concerningly low slant success rate (74%, down from 84-87% in
2020/2022) and almost no out-breaking route usage despite being an X.

**Tee Higgins** — brief update following last episode's full charting
breakdown: expected to be officially franchise-tagged by Cincinnati (not
confirmed at recording time). Harmon reiterates a "fine, injury-affected"
read on the down 2023 season and floats him as a possible trade-tag
combination, without landing on a firm 2024 outlook.

**Nico Collins aside** — asked to pick Higgins or Collins straight-up, Harmon
says the talent gap the public perceives is smaller than reality and that
Collins's 2023 season was better than any season either player has had,
making him lean Collins if forced to choose right now.
