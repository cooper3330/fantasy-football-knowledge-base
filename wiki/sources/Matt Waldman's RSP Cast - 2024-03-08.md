# 2024 NFL Draft QB Class A-T: Matt Waldman's RSP Solo Cast

## Summary

A solo episode — no co-host — in which [[Matt Waldman]] runs through top-line
notes on 20 quarterbacks in the 2024 draft class, alphabetically A through T,
ahead of the RSP's April 1 pre-draft publication. Opens with dynasty rookie-draft
strategy: [[Caleb Williams]] is the only true 2024 priority pick at QB; his
recommendation for standard formats is to spend early rookie-draft capital on
receivers (and maybe a running back or two) instead, then trade the resulting
surplus for an established starting quarterback — unless in a superflex format,
where three to four of the class's names are worth early capital. Restates two
of his standing evaluation frameworks before working through the class: that
box-score completion percentage is not "contextually sound data" (see
[[Quarterback Processing and Confidence]]), and that using prospect age as a
scouting filter is a "ham-handed" application of an otherwise-valid theory (see
[[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]]).

## Players Discussed

- [[Caleb Williams]] — reaffirmed as the class's only true priority pick and the
  only 2024 QB safe to start as a rookie without a redshirt runway.
- [[Bo Nix]] — used as the episode's central age-bias case study; reads the
  field better than credited, protects receivers, willing tight-window thrower.
- [[Drake Maye]] — most detailed critique yet: leverage/coverage-recognition
  gaps, late deliveries, took six charted games before his first throwaway.
- [[Jayden Daniels]] — multi-read timing/patience issue ("comically tragic
  timing"), needs scheme support and a development runway, real running upside.
- [[Joe Milton III]] — boom/bust arm-talent case restated; elite arm, slow
  coverage processing, hero-ball hit-taking tendency.
- [[Michael Penix Jr.]] — better middle-of-field mover than credited; still some
  specific throws of concern; floor is a low-end starter in the right situation.
- [[Spencer Rattler]] — resolves the earlier "half a game" ambiguity: strong vs.
  man coverage, weaker reading zone coverage.
- [[Tanner Mordecai]] — trusts contested-catch receivers (cites [[Rashee Rice]]
  at SMU); lacked rapport/comfort at Wisconsin, a beat slower as a result.
- [[Jack Plummer]] — efficient in the pocket once he sees pressure, but weak
  closing escape routes; ceiling now graded as an NFL reserve.
- [[Austin Reed]] *(new page)* — West Florida transfer, aggressive Stafford/Kurt
  Warner/Tony Romo-flavored style; likely journeyman.
- [[Brennan Armstrong]] *(new page)* — Virginia-to-NC State transfer, lefty,
  developmental-league grade at this stage.
- [[Carter Bradley]] *(new page)* — Toledo-to-South Alabama transfer, mechanics
  need work, overreacts to pressure.
- [[Devin Leary]] *(new page)* — arm/fundamentals for a roster spot, trail-
  coverage and pressure-diagnosis limiters.
- [[Emory Jones]] *(new page)* — athletic/accurate tools, technical foundation
  and coverage reading still behind.
- [[Gunner Watson]] *(new page)* — accurate boundary/back-shoulder thrower,
  lacks conceptual acumen to read leverage.
- [[Jordan Travis]] *(new page)* — aggressive, confident tight-window thrower;
  Jeff Blake/Case Keenum/Jeff Garcia mashup comp.
- [[Keaton Slovis]] *(new page)* — once a top USC prospect, now a developmental
  arm with real pressure/peripheral-vision concerns.
- [[Michael Pratt]] *(new page)* — good fundamentals/high floor, but slow to
  throw underneath and slow to throw away under pressure.
- [[Sam Hartman]] *(new page)* — technically sound and aggressive, but a capped
  arm limits him to a quick-game distributor projection.

## Not Given Pages (passing mentions only, nothing lost)

- **Dak Prescott, Justin Herbert** — Waldman's two self-identified biggest
  evaluation misses of the past 18 years (expected them to be worse than they
  became); no new evaluative claim this episode.
- **Russell Wilson, Patrick Mahomes, Lamar Jackson, Brock Purdy** — cited as
  prospects Waldman says he was "ahead of the curve" on; track-record context
  only, already logged on [[Matt Waldman]]'s expert page.
- **Baker Mayfield** — cited repeatedly as a comp/case study (Austin Reed's
  mobility/coverage-savvy contrast, Spencer Rattler's baseline, Michael Penix
  Jr.'s middle-field decision-making); no fresh take on Mayfield himself.
- **Drew Lock, Zach Wilson, Mitchell Trubisky, Desmond Ridder, Kenny Pickett** —
  cited as past examples illustrating the confidence/processing framework or
  Waldman's track record; not evaluated fresh here.
- **Kyler Murray** — cited alongside Mayfield as a QB who'd have had a better
  career if he found the middle of the field the way Michael Penix Jr. does.
- **Aaron Rodgers, Matthew Stafford** — cited as Caleb Williams's stylistic comp
  (Rodgers) and as an "leaned too hard on arm talent in college" precedent
  (Stafford); folded into the Williams and Austin Reed bullets.
- **Jordan Love** — cited as the redshirt/development-runway model Waldman
  thinks fits Drake Maye and, to a lesser extent, Penix and Daniels.
- **Justin Fields** — Waldman says he'd personally want Fields as his own
  team's QB and would like it if Chicago traded the No. 1 pick for more draft
  capital rather than benching him for a rookie; folded into the
  [[Caleb Williams]] bullet, no separate update to the Justin Fields page.
- **Malik Willis** — cited as Waldman's definition-check example of true
  scouting "rawness" (unskilled, not just inexperienced); not a tracked page.
- **Isaac Bruce, Steve Smith, Antonio Brown, Stefon Diggs** — cited as the
  receivers an old height/weight filter model would have wrongly excluded, used
  as the analogy for age-filter skepticism.
- **Keon Coleman, Rashee Rice** — cited only as receivers whose rapport with,
  respectively, Jordan Travis and Tanner Mordecai is credited as part of those
  quarterbacks' upside; no new evaluative claim about the receivers themselves.
- **Alex Brown** (SMU recruiting director, not tracked) and the RSP product
  pitch — promotional material, not a wiki-relevant claim.
