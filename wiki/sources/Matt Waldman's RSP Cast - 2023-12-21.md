# Matt Waldman's RSP Cast — 2023-12-21

## Summary

*RSP Film and Theory* — the conceptual format [[Matt Waldman]] co-hosts with
**Adam Harstad** (Footballguys — *not* a tracked expert in this wiki; his
analysis is attributed to him by name throughout). Same pairing as
[[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-14]]. Note this is a different co-host from
the *Feel It or F**k It* episodes, which Bob Harris hosts.

Almost entirely conceptual. Very few start/sit or ranking calls; the value is
in three durable frameworks plus a handful of film-level player evaluations
dropped in as illustrations.

1. **Running back size and movement skills.** Waldman opens on a point raised
   by Brandon Angelo on *Going Deep* the night before: the shared trait among
   [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], [[James Cook]], [[Austin Ekeler]] and
   [[Lamar Jackson]] is changing direction inside tight creases at speed, and
   Waldman argues the trait has a weight ceiling around 205–215 pounds. The
   contrast is the space-expensive jump cut — Adrian Peterson's move, still run
   by [[Saquon Barkley]]. See [[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]].

2. **Everything is cyclical.** Harstad's game-theory answer: there is no
   correct body type or front, only what everyone else is doing. Pittsburgh's
   1990s 3-4 switch as the worked example of a market inefficiency being
   discovered, copied, and arbitraged away, with an equilibrium of roughly
   eight to ten teams. [[Derrick Henry]] as the outlier people wrongly read as
   a trend. Waldman adds that trends seed in divisions first and that the
   *extremes* get rewarded, not the middle. See
   [[League Trend Cycles and Market Inefficiency]].

3. **Talent-centric vs scheme-centric eras** — the episode's title thread, and
   a direct continuation of the Houston discussion three days earlier. Merged
   into the existing [[Scheme vs Talent]] page rather than given its own.
   Harstad reads December 2023 as a talent-centric swing ("if you can do
   something good, somebody is going to find you"), names the Kyle Shanahan
   offense as the candidate for the next scheme swing, and rejects the "system
   quarterback" label outright using Peyton Manning as the reductio. Waldman's
   contrarian call within it: [[Lamar Jackson]], not [[Brock Purdy]], is the
   most scheme-dependent of Prescott / Jackson / [[C.J. Stroud]] / Purdy.

4. **NIL.** Whether paid college players staying in school forces the NFL to
   commit earlier or to develop players. Harstad is skeptical on both counts,
   on revenue-size grounds and on his stated belief that neither league does
   anything unless forced. Both hosts are emphatically pro-NIL; the segment is
   as much an indictment of NCAA amateurism as an analysis. See
   [[NIL and Player Development]].

Closing segment: Waldman on his own process — the RSP's 19th year, rebuilding
his wide receiver system after the Hakeem Butler miss by studying wide receiver
coach Drew Lieberman's work, and patience with evaluations that look wrong after
one season ([[James Cook]] as the example).
