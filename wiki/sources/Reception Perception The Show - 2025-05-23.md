## Summary
[[Matt Harmon]] with co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) close out the 2024 rookie receiver charting series with three players who struggled to varying degrees: [[Ja'Lynn Polk]], [[Adonai Mitchell]] and [[Jalen McMillan]].

Harmon opens by copping to a miss on Polk, whose rookie Reception Perception profile collapsed in exactly the areas Harmon had graded as strengths — contested catch rate fell from over 80% as a prospect to 20%, drop rate rose to 10.7%, and he graded as the worst blocking receiver Harmon watched from the class. Both hosts argue New England set him up to fail with a nine-route-heavy, on-the-line deployment that bore no resemblance to his flanker/slot usage at Washington, while also insisting Polk earned none of the role-catering a young receiver needs.

Adonai Mitchell is described as the most extreme X-receiver profile Harmon has charted: elite slant (90.5%), out (90.9%), curl (88.2%) and a functional 59% nine-route success rate, paired with a 14th-percentile zone-coverage grade and terrible post/corner numbers. Harmon argues the Colts misused him in the slot and as a flanker after the [[Josh Downs]] ankle injury, and that his path forward is as a pure X — though he doubts Mitchell fully displaces [[Alec Pierce]].

[[Jalen McMillan]] gets the most optimistic read: overall success rates are poor (24th percentile vs man and press, 20th vs zone) but Tampa Bay's move of a college slot receiver to the perimeter produced strong downfield numbers, and Harmon credits the organization for drafting [[Emeka Egbuka]] rather than over-extrapolating McMillan's touchdown-heavy finish.

The episode also carries a recurring methodological point: rookie-year charting data should override prospect-profile priors once role changes.
