## Summary
[[Matt Waldman]] with Adam Harstad (Football Guys; NOT a tracked expert) on the recurring 'Film and Theory' segment. Harstad opens on why early-season fantasy results are dominated by randomness, using his Dynasty and Theory article: a 2011 dynasty title won on three fluke weeks from Darrius Heyward-Bey, Brent Celek and a slow-starting rookie Julio Jones; the Cleveland 'Hail Mary specialist' Frisman Jackson week-one outburst that meant nothing versus Miles Austin's 250-yard game that turned out to be real signal. Harstad's warning: models that explain every outcome after the fact but predict nothing before it are storytelling, not analysis. Waldman counters that some 'weirdness' is simply film knowledge the box-score-driven industry ignores (3-4 fronts being easier to run up the middle against, 4-3 fronts easier outside, applied to the ambiguous Seattle backfield).

Waldman then presents his own film study of [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] after the Bengals game, the 'soft' discourse, and the alligator-arm plays. The pair move to [[Ashton Jeanty]] and the 'next Trent Richardson' label, [[Tyler Warren]]'s one-read usage in Indianapolis, and the Bears' organizational mess ([[D.J. Moore]], [[DeAndre Swift]], Ryan Poles, Ben Johnson), including a Brandon Angelo theory relayed by Waldman about Poles's height-weight-speed scouting profile. Closes on ex-player analysts' blind spots, coach/GM pairing philosophy, Jerry Jones, and the World Football League.
