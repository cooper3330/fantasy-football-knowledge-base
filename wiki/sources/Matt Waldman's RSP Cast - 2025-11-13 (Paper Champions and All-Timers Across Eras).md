## Summary
A *Film and Theory* episode with [[Matt Waldman]] and Adam Harstad (Footballguys; NOT a tracked expert). Two segments. First, Harstad expands his 'Dynasty in Theory' work on paper value: if we had platonic-ideal player values, the roster with the most paper value would win the most championships, so mocking managers who 'win the offseason' is only half right — real-world values (Dan Hendry trade values, FantasyCalc, KeepTradeCut) are flawed but directionally correlated, and consistently holding the most valuable roster over months and years should convert to wins. He illustrates calibration with a bowl-of-M&Ms poison-detector analogy, notes a zero-correlation machine is a coin flip (whereas being wrong 100% of the time is as useful as being right), and cites Trent Richardson as paper value that evaporated, [[Chase Brown]] as a 2025 value he has cooled on, and [[Rico Dowdle]] / [[Jonathan Brooks]] as the Carolina backfield lesson. Waldman adds the analyst-accountability angle: predictions are easy when you grade them yourself afterward.

Second segment is a riff on players they would move to other eras — Marino, Sonny Jurgensen, Bert Jones, Randall Cunningham, Steve McNair, Brady, Peyton Manning, Jerry Rice, Lawrence Taylor, Deacon Jones, Alan Page, Bill George, Ted Hendricks, Herschel Walker, Lenny Moore, Percy Harvin, Walter Payton, Earl Campbell, John Riggins, Ricky Williams, Dwight Stephenson, Joe Greene, Ndamukong Suh, Ozzie Newsome and [[Cordarrelle Patterson]] — plus Harstad on formative-era nostalgia and the early-2000s running back surplus that warped fantasy expectations for a generation.
