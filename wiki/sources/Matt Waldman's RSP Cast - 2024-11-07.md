## Summary
A *Film and Theory* episode with [[Matt Waldman]] and co-host Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert). Almost entirely theory and off-field discussion rather than player evaluation. Three segments:

1. **Generalists vs. specialists**, built on David Epstein's books 'The Sports Gene' (2013) and 'Range' (2019). Harstad's case: elite performers are usually built from broad athletic and non-athletic backgrounds, not early hyper-specialization (Joel Embiid's soccer footwork, Nikola Jokic's point-guard development before a late growth spurt, Jim Thorpe as national ballroom dancing champion, Otto Graham as a pro basketball champion). Waldman applied it to football evaluation: [[Patrick Mahomes]] as 'a shortstop playing quarterback', Joe Montana as a point guard in Bill Walsh's distribution system, Tom Brady's college boxing showing up in pocket movement. Waldman also traced his own evaluation process to a generalist path (music, journalism, operations management) before specializing.

2. **Optimal development behavior** — Harstad on the deliberate-practice sweet spot of roughly 80-90% success and 10-20% failure, and Waldman on slow, fragmented, patient practice using his own bass and saxophone routines. Applied to football: early-camp footwork clips ([[Derrick Henry]]) and mechanical blips ([[Anthony Richardson]]) get over-read because observers ignore the development path; and to the sit-vs-start rookie QB debate, where intermittent usage may be the best way to hold a passer inside that band.

3. **Politics and sport** — Harstad's amateur-historian argument that football has never been apolitical (Teddy Roosevelt forcing the forward pass, Jim Thorpe's stripped Olympic records and the aristocratic origins of amateurism, NIL created by the courts, the Rams and Washington integrating under government pressure, Vince Lombardi's hotel policy, government-funded patriotic displays post-2010, the USFL antitrust suit), plus a long closing exchange on the 2024 election, the 'ignorant, idiot, evil' model of disagreement, and good-faith value differences.
