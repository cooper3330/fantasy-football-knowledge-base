## Summary
A two-topic 'Film and Theory' episode with [[Matt Waldman]] and co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys; NOT a tracked expert). Topic one: the running back workload myth. Harstad argues that every study finding a link between accumulated carries and decline fails to control for age — comparing a back at 1,000 carries to a back at 500 carries is often comparing Emmitt Smith to himself. Studies that do control for age find no relationship, and some find a positive one, because carries are not randomly assigned: heavy workload is a coaches' preference reveal that the back can absorb it. He allows a real short-term effect (Jason Lisk's work on 30-carry games, worse in close games than blowouts) and cites Andre Ellington/Willie Parker under Bruce Arians as the cautionary case. Waldman adds a recovery-rate framing: the limit is per-game workload and how fast a player recovers, not lifetime mileage. Topic two: the hit on [[Trevor Lawrence]], the three-game suspension, Tom Brady's comments on quarterbacks protecting themselves, [[Patrick Mahomes]] faking slides, and NFL discipline driven by public relations rather than consistent standards.
