## Summary
An RSP *Film and Theory* episode with [[Matt Waldman]] and co-host Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert). Three segments.

1. Harstad revisits a piece he wrote ten years ago on 'irrational exuberance': if every manager in a 12-team league estimates their own title odds, the sum runs well north of 100%, because each manager is the highest person in the league on their own players (otherwise they would trade them). His old conclusion was to apply a blanket 10% pessimism haircut; he now disagrees with his past self, citing a manager who started 0-4 in 2017, kept competing, finished 6-7 and won the title. His revised position is that accuracy — not calculated pessimism — is the goal, and better still is a process where the estimate does not matter: build the best team you can and trust wins to follow. He concedes the estimate still matters for win-now trades (a Travis Kelce trade is different at 4-1 than 1-4). Waldman's amendment: know thyself, and deliberately stretch in the direction opposite your temperament. The actionable dynasty implication Harstad draws is that future firsts may be systematically undervalued.

2. Regression candidates in both directions, driven largely by Harstad's argument that yards per carry is close to a pseudo-random number generator whose only real repeatable component is straight-line speed, not general talent.

3. A long closing discussion of separating the player from the person, prompted by Brett Favre's birthday — Jim Tyrer's Hall of Fame case, OJ Simpson, Marvin Harrison Sr., Ray Lewis, CTE, and whether the NFL should be the body that punishes off-field conduct. No fantasy content; recorded here for completeness rather than given pages.
