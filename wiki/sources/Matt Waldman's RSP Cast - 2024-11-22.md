## Summary
A *Film and Theory* episode with [[Matt Waldman]] and co-host Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert). Two segments.

**1. Why free agents mostly don't matter, and where waiver value actually hides.** Harstad's framing: if waivers mattered, top dynasty and redraft rosters would be full of waiver adds, and they aren't — he tracks acquisition source on a spreadsheet and roughly 5% of his player starts come from waiver pickups, despite being historically strong on the wire ([[Adam Thielen]], [[Austin Ekeler]], a revolving door of tight ends). He puts 80-90% of a team's fate as decided by the end of the draft. He then screened the top 100 players by FantasyCalc dynasty consensus value and found only about 11-12 were plausibly on waivers at some point; the repeatable archetypes were **highly drafted backup quarterbacks** and **later-round backup running backs with visible organizational support**. Waldman added IDP archetypes (aging pass rushers, tackle-heavy safeties on bad defenses, rotating cornerbacks) and his own tell for skill players: praise about route-running detail or 'he gets the yards we ask him to get' rather than highlight-reel wins. Both stressed snap counts, roster-bubble reporting, and unprompted mentions from coaches or quarterbacks as the strongest signals.

**2. 'The' dented can for 2025: [[Chris Olave]].** Harstad argued four largely uncorrelated processes — Waldman's film work, [[Matt Harmon]]'s [[Reception Perception Methodology]], Harstad's own yards-per-route-run and rookie production models, and air-yards market share — all love Olave, and the odds all four are wrong for different reasons plummet. Olave grades as the 7th-best rookie season since 2008 in Harstad's model. Also covered: [[Drake London]] at 14th in the same model, [[Terry McLaurin]] as a process-defying outlier, and an updated prior on [[Derek Carr]].
