## Summary
Solo cast by [[Matt Waldman]] running alphabetically through 69 wide receivers featured in the 2025 pre-draft Rookie Scouting Portfolio, one or two notable observations each — praise, criticism, or a lingering question. No co-hosts or guests; the only outside voice referenced is Waldman's colleague Chad Reuter, cited as disagreeing with him on [[Emeka Egbuka]] (not a tracked expert).

The long opening frames the class-quality question. Waldman's baseline: a receiver class historically yields about 4.5 players who deliver at least three years of starter production within their first four seasons, and most multi-year starters rarely reach top-24 or top-12 finishes. By that yardstick he says 2025 is NOT a bad class — it lacks the obvious alpha timing-route winner archetype, but has the depth to hit the 4-5 starter baseline. He counts 38 prospects with an RSP depth-of-talent score of 80+ (rotational starter or better), versus 25 last year and 22 the year before, and attributes the rise to seven-on-seven, flag football, year-round private receiver coaching — a higher technical baseline, concentrated in the contributor and rotational tiers rather than the top. He distinguishes having skills from being skilled, and flags four to six talents with instant-impact potential given the right landing spot. [Dynasty] note: if rebuilding with multiple early picks, this is a year to trade 2025 picks for earlier 2026 and 2027 picks; he also warns that [[Travis Hunter]] playing mostly cornerback could make the class look artificially bad after year one.
