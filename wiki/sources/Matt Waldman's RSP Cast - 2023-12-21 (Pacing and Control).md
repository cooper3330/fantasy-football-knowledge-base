## Summary

*Going Deep* — the every-other-week format [[Matt Waldman]] co-hosts with
[[Brandon Angelo]] (X: @Angelo_Fantasy; **not** a tracked expert in this wiki —
his views are attributed to him by name throughout). Note this is a third
co-host, distinct from Adam Harstad (*RSP Film and Theory*) and Bob Harris
(*Feel It or F**k It*).

Angelo drives most of this one, and he is a movement/biomechanics analyst by
temperament — the vocabulary is re-acceleration, contact type, bandwidth. Two
long segments plus a third that runs away with the back half.

1. **Pacing and control of elite movers.** The framing idea, which Angelo
   brought to Waldman: what [[Lamar Jackson]], [[James Cook]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]]
   and [[Christian McCaffrey]] share is control of their own gears play to play
   — "Lamar Jackson never reaches top gear" — plus the discipline to take what
   is blocked instead of hunting the explosive. [[Isiah Pacheco]] is the named
   negative case ("zero or a hundred"); [[Breece Hall]] is the sympathetic one
   ("he averts one disaster at a time"). Waldman's addition is that the trait
   is a *marriage* — controlling your own pace and gauging everyone else's —
   grounded in body awareness and knowing your own boundaries. New page:
   [[Pace Control and Movement Intellect]].

   Two spin-offs from the same segment. **(a)** Over a Georgia clip of
   [[James Cook]] splitting two defenders in a phone-booth-width crease, Angelo
   states the size claim on film — "this wouldn't be possible if James Cook was
   210, 205" — which is the argument Waldman relays on *Film and Theory*. This
   is the origin of [[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]], and it was
   updated rather than duplicated. **(b)** A genuine counter-example:
   [[Nick Chubb]], as big as [[Saquon Barkley]], gets away with the movement
   class anyway.

2. **[[Malik Nabers]] vs [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]].** Both hosts have Nabers
   ahead "by a healthy margin," and the separator is explicitly pro-readiness
   rather than ceiling — Harrison gets the higher ceiling and both are expected
   to be top-10 receivers within five years. Waldman supplies a three-part
   receiver technique checklist (release work, catch-window selection, overhand
   vs underhand attack position) as the evidence. New page:
   [[Prospect Pro-Readiness vs Ceiling]].

3. **Development, coachability, and organizational culture.** Runs long and
   ends up the episode's biggest surprise. Angelo: "a lot of pros don't want to
   be developed," and coaches don't want to change either — Atlanta running
   "scheme over personnel" with [[Bijan Robinson]] is his exhibit. Waldman's
   [[Sam LaPorta]] floor scenario (coach and GM in a standoff over what the
   player is for) as the recurring way careers die, and a long [[Drew Lock]]
   case study built around the Peyton Manning phone call. [[Gardner Minshew]]
   as the counter-model. New page:
   [[Player Development and Coachability]].

The NIL/transfer-portal material (teased as next episode's topic but discussed
at length here) was merged into the existing
[[NIL and Player Development]] rather than duplicated.
