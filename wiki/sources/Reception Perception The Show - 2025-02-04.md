## Summary
[[Matt Harmon]] (on location in New Orleans for Super Bowl week) and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) break down two offensive coordinator hires and what they mean for pass-catcher and running back roles.

Segment 1 — Chip Kelly to the Raiders as the NFL's highest-paid coordinator (reportedly $6M/yr), his first NFL job since 2016, alongside Pete Carroll and Tom Brady's ownership stake. Harmon likes the narrower role (coordinator only, not the Philadelphia-style total control) and says the Raiders will be a team to draft a running back from. Koh walks through Kelly's rushing history (2013 Eagles led the league at 2,566 rush yards with LeSean McCoy; 2014 ninth; 2015 14th with the DeMarco Murray fit problem; 2016 49ers fourth with Colin Kaepernick and Carlos Hyde) and his habit of producing career-high receiver seasons (DeSean Jackson 1,300, Jeremy Maclin 1,300, Jordan Matthews 997). Harmon maps the Jordan Matthews / Jeremy Kerley (115 targets, 2016 SF) short-game role onto [[Brock Bowers]] and [[Jakobi Meyers]], and says the roster still lacks a boundary lid-lifter. Quarterback fits discussed: the Chip Kelly 'Rajon Rondo, not Michael Vick' distributor quote, Will Howard, and [[Matthew Stafford]].

Segment 2 — Nick Caley to Houston as OC over Bobby Slowik. Caley: 42, Patriots 2015-2022, Rams tight ends coach, first year as Rams pass game coordinator in 2024. Harmon on the McVay system being wrongly bundled with Shanahan, Koh on the 2023 shift from exclusively outside zone to gap scheme. Hopes for scheme diversity and more [[C.J. Stroud]] control in year three. Then the Texans pass-catcher inventory: [[Tank Dell]]'s outlook, [[Nico Collins]] as the hub, [[Xavier Hutchinson]], [[Dalton Schultz]], [[Joe Mixon]], free agents [[Stefon Diggs]], [[Keenan Allen]], [[Marquise Brown]], and prospect [[Emeka Egbuka]]. Houston's cap situation (about $381k of room) framed as the reason they could not afford to stand pat on offense.
