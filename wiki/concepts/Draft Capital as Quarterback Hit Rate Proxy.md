## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-09-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-26 (Win-Now Trades and Jayden Daniels)]]) [Dynasty]: co-host Adam Harstad (Football Guys, NOT a tracked expert) described a deliberately name-blind rookie-draft heuristic — at the end of the second round he simply takes whichever quarterback went in the NFL top ten and is still on the board, because the hit rate on highly drafted quarterbacks is far higher than on a fourth-round running back or fifth-round receiver. He does not scout progressions or protections; he says he sees the history of quarterbacks taken second overall, and that history is good ([[C.J. Stroud]] as the recent example). Rosters built this way for him include [[Patrick Mahomes]], [[Dak Prescott]], [[Bryce Young]], [[C.J. Stroud]] and [[Jayden Daniels]]. Waldman endorsed it as a complement rather than a rival to film work, using a shoe analogy: NFL teams reasonably care about brand and stitching, but fantasy only needs the criteria that predict production, so draft followers often over-index on minutiae that matters to a front office and not to a fantasy manager. He also framed quarterbacks as a safety net that lets you take risks elsewhere in a rebuild.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): draft capital can itself be manufactured by a story the league buys. Waldman calls [[Daniel Jones]] a fraud on draft day — meaning the draft capital was fraudulent in how it was perceived, not the player as a person — because Dave Gettleman and roughly 40 to 55 percent of the league bought the premise that Jones was a David Cutcliffe quarterback the way the Manning brothers were, when Cutcliffe was Duke's head coach without the time to do that position work. Waldman says the savviest scouts asked a variety of people inside the program rather than one, and a staffer told them plainly he was not a Cutcliffe quarterback — a story Duke had recruiting and donor incentives to leave unchallenged. The lesson: treat draft capital as a signal that can be contaminated by narrative, and check who inside a program is telling you what.
- 2025-03-28 — According to Cory Kinnan (quarterback charter, not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Harmon]]'s show ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-28]]): where a quarterback is drafted should change what kind of bet you are willing to make on him. He says he will happily take a raw upside shot like [[Jalen Milroe]] at picks 33-50 because there is little guaranteed money to eat if it fails, but the same profile at fourth overall is untenable — explicitly contrasting Milroe with Anthony Richardson's price. He applies the same logic backwards to Will Levis at 33rd overall as appropriately drafted, and files [[Jackson Dart]] with the third-round Davis Mills / Kellen Mond / Desmond Ridder group where 'if it works out, works out.' The corollary is that outside round one he prefers upside over what-you-see-is-what-you-get profiles like Dart, [[Will Howard]] and [[Tyler Shough]].
- 2025-03-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-31]]) [Dynasty]: sizing up the 2025 class, Waldman says the top is not as strong as 2024's ([[Caleb Williams]], [[Michael Penix Jr.]], [[Bo Nix]], [[Jayden Daniels]], [[Drake Maye]], [[J.J. McCarthy]]) but the depth is better — 12 quarterbacks he charted have NFL-caliber ability and eight score well enough to have realistic potential to become high-end contributors, journeyman backups who see the field, or starters by the end of their rookie deals. Because roughly a coin flip hit rate exists for first-round quarterback prospects, and two to four of those eight will be first-round picks, he calls expecting one to two of the eight to actually hit realistic. Critically, he says there is no [[Caleb Williams]]-like prospect here — nobody built to withstand the onslaught of a bad organization making horrific decisions that derail play calling, execution and coaching, as Chicago did in 2024.
