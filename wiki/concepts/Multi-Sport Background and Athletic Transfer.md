## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-11-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-07]]): building on David Epstein's 'Range' (2019) and 'The Sports Gene' (2013), Harstad argued the best way to get very good at a sport is generally not hyper-focusing on it — Joel Embiid's basketball footwork came from soccer, and Nikola Jokic became a better center precisely because a late growth spurt let him train as a point guard first; had either specialized early they would be worse today. Physical genetics still gate the ceiling (Epstein's Michael Phelps vs. the world's top marathoner: identical leg length, opposite torso proportions; Michael Jordan's sociopathic work ethic still needed his height). Waldman applied the transfer directly to evaluation: [[Patrick Mahomes]] as a shortstop playing QB, Joe Montana as a point guard in Walsh's system, Tom Brady's boxing footwork as pocket movement, Jim Thorpe's national ballroom dancing title as the grace behind his football, Otto Graham's pro basketball championship. Waldman's framing: be a generalist through the developmental period, then specialize once you have absorbed enough — his own evaluation process took 15 adult years of generalist work (music, journalism, operations management) before it cohered. Harstad noted Nobel-winning scientists are roughly five times as likely to play an instrument as non-winners, while conceding selection bias; Waldman said quarterback coach Will Hewlett tells young players not to quit other sports, because exposure to more spatial and motor problems produces more transferable solutions.
- 2025-04-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-22]]): a prospect's prior sport often explains a specific receiving trait, and several 2025 receivers came to the position late. He cited [[Tory Horton]] as a high school outfielder, which he and James Koh (not tracked) tied directly to his downfield ball tracking and the way he attacks and extends for the football; [[Tetairoa McMillan]] as a volleyball player, which shows in contorting and bending at the catch point for acrobatic grabs — a comparison Harmon says people make both complimentarily and derogatorily; [[Jayden Higgins]] as a basketball player who plays receiver like one, meaning a rebounder mindset rather than a separator, which Harmon frames as a limitation since you still have to get open; [[Drake London]] as a legitimate two-sport USC basketball player, which shows in boxing out; and [[Isaac TeSlaa]] as a former wing-T high school quarterback who is still learning the position.
