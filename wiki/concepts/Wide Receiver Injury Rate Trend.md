## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-02]]): Harmon floats — while repeatedly labelling it a theory he pulled out of the air and stressing he is not a doctor — that the Zero RB-era assumption that wide receiver injury rates are much lower than running back injury rates may be eroding. He notes this is the second straight season of what feels like more major injuries to big-name receivers, citing [[Mike Evans]], [[CeeDee Lamb]], [[Malik Nabers]] and [[Tyreek Hill]] within the previous two weeks alone, while acknowledging very different severities. His mechanism is usage: while overall passing production is spreading out because of positional depth, the alpha targets are absorbing enormous per-route volume on designed, manufactured targets over the middle rather than the 2015-style sideline X-receiver diet. He does not convert this into a recommendation; he says it is on his radar and something to monitor.
- 2026-01-29 — According to JJ Zachariason (not tracked) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-29]] - explicitly framed as an unproven 'pet theory': part of 2025's WR2 collapse is straightforward injury attrition to big names like [[Malik Nabers]] and a declining [[Tyreek Hill]], but he also floats a structural theory - as receivers get used more over the middle against zone coverage (a byproduct of the 11.5/12-personnel blurring above) rather than getting tackled out of bounds by corners on the perimeter, they take more hits from linebackers and safeties. He points to [[Garrett Wilson]], who ran a higher slot rate and worked over the middle more under the Jets' outgoing coaching staff in 2025 and got hurt, as the example that made him wonder if WR injury rates could start trending toward what running backs have historically seen. Harmon treats it as speculative rather than proven.
