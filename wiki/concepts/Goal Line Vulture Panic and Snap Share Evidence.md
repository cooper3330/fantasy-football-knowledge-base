## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-11-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-10]]): the recurring in-season panic that a star back is being 'vultured' at the goal line should be tested against actual snap and touch counts before it changes anything. Responding to live chat insistence that [[Tyler Allgeier]] had been taking all of Atlanta's goal-line work, he looked it up: over the previous month the red zone snap split was 23-13 for [[Bijan Robinson]], the Falcons had barely been inside the five at all (three Robinson snaps in four games), and the only two running back touches inside the five were both Allgeier's, both from that one Sunday — so 'there hasn't been any goal line work until yesterday.' He applies the same discipline to [[Christian McCaffrey]] ceding a one-yard score to [[Brian Robinson Jr.]] and to [[Justice Hill]] taking a goal-line touch from [[Derrick Henry]]: you are allowed to be worried that a backup converted, but a single occurrence is not a trend and there is 'nothing actionable in that.'
- 2026-01-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-08]]): Harris and guest Jim McCormick (untracked) argue most in-season goal-line 'he's lost the score-line touches' panics aren't sticky year over year and are usually explained by offensive-line quality or red-zone efficiency rather than a player losing trust -- cited for [[Saquon Barkley]], [[Bijan Robinson]], and [[Breece Hall]], all low goal-line-carry seasons Harris attributes to poor surrounding talent, not skill loss. The exception the two flag as a real, durable shift is [[Kenneth Walker III]] losing roughly double his goal-line share to [[Zach Charbonnet]] in Seattle, and [[Chase Brown]] beginning to cede some short-yardage work to [[Samaje Perine]] late in the year -- both framed as genuine role erosion rather than small-sample noise.
- 2026-01-22 — According to James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-22]]): reacting to Seattle using backup [[George Holani]] instead of the hot-handed [[Kenneth Walker III]] near the goal line, Koh vented that this pattern held all season despite Walker clearly being the superior back, calling it needlessly frustrating given how well Walker performed once given the chance in the divisional round.
- 2026-10-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-10-20]]): discussing the Saints backfield, Harris and guest Scott Fish (untracked) note [[Kendre Miller]] lost a goal-line touchdown to fullback C.J. Donaldson on a single play despite reportedly getting roughly eight of nine such chances previously -- both treat it as a one-off and not a role change, with Fish explicitly saying Miller 'probably should be' the week's top-ranked waiver running back anyway because he still gets 'basically all the red zone and goal-line looks.'
- 2026-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-11-03]]): multiple Week 8 games showed goal-line work concentrated in one specific back regardless of overall workload - [[Mike Washington]] got every carry inside the five against the Raiders (0-for-6), [[DeAndre Swift]] got all three Bears goal-line touchdowns over a healthier-snapped Jadarian Price/Zach Charbonnet-style backup role for Chicago, and [[Quinshon Judkins]] was stuffed at the goal line in Cleveland purely because of a blocking breakdown rather than a talent gap. Harris treated the specific role assignment, not raw snap or carry totals, as the fantasy-relevant signal worth tracking.
