# Value-Based Drafting and Early Tight End Cost

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-13]]): his case against early tight ends is value-based drafting — VBD, the fantasy analogue of baseball's VORP, measuring how many raw points a player exceeds replacement level at his own position, with replacement defined as the player available at the end of round 10 in a 12-team league. Of the 24 tight ends drafted inside the first three rounds of ADP over the past decade, only four outperformed their ADP in VBD terms, and never once when the tight end was a first-round pick. His worked 2024 example: [[Brock Bowers]] scored 150 standard points as TE2 against TE11 [[David Njoku]]'s 84, a delta of 66, versus [[Saquon Barkley]]'s 237 over replacement running back and [[Ja'Marr Chase]]'s 169 over replacement receiver (full PPR: 114 / 241 / 228). Harris adds the structural reason — replacement-level tight ends are always available on waivers — and cites the years analysts told drafters to spend a first on [[Travis Kelce]].
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-15]]): tight end is a bad fantasy position and investing heavily in it is a losing strategy — the counter-argument that 'I win the position' is a category error, because championships are decided by total points, not by how many positional matchups you won. He argues tight end premium scoring does not rescue the case: the rising tide lifts all boats, and giving [[Brock Bowers]] an extra half point per catch is worth only about 30 points a season over [[David Njoku]], against a value gap measured in the hundreds for [[Ja'Marr Chase]] or [[Saquon Barkley]]. He says no format short of a two-tight-end superflex creates genuine scarcity, and such a league is miserable because half the teams must start replacement tight ends. Cousin Josh (untracked co-host) framed the same idea through replacement level — Bowers beat TE11 by 66 points, while an elite receiver beats his own replacement by far more — and added a practical alternative: build with positional players, then trade for the elite tight end mid-season once you know you are a playoff team.
- 2025-08-18 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris states the rule categorically — no tight end should ever be taken in the first or second round of a fantasy draft, regardless of talent, using [[Brock Bowers]] as the test case and [[Travis Kelce]] at fifth overall as the worse historical version. The argument is that you are not in fantasy football to win a position: winning tight end every week necessarily means losing another, and tight end is no longer a scarce enough position to justify the trade. Guest Patrick Doherty of Rotoworld (not a tracked expert) sharpens the math — a legendary tight end season is roughly 1,200 yards, which is close to the floor for the wide receivers being drafted in that same range, so the ceiling case at tight end buys you the baseline case elsewhere. Doherty allows the counter that being alone in a tier at your position is real edge, but notes [[Trey McBride]] can reach Bowers' tier and [[George Kittle]] finished TE1 last year.
- 2025-08-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-26]]): the case against a first-round tight end, made against [[Brock Bowers]]. Harris's Almanac number — over the past decade, 24 tight ends were drafted in the first three rounds and only 4 of 24 outperformed their ADP in value-based-drafting terms. The mechanism: an early tight end has to gap the replacement-level TE9/TE10 off the waiver wire by as much as the running back or receiver you could have taken there gaps their own replacement, and tight ends almost never do. Even Bowers' record rookie season, held to five touchdowns, graded out as only about the 26th pick in PPR; eleven touchdowns would change it. Ben Gretch (guest, NOT tracked) counters that the historical sample is thin — roughly eight to ten elite tight end careers and maybe 30 player seasons, mostly [[Travis Kelce]], with the high-TE-drafting era only really starting after Gronkowski and Jimmy Graham in 2011-13 — so trend claims about drafting tight ends early rest on very limited data, and Kelce once gapped the position by over 100 points. [Redraft (Standard)]
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): the risk-quotient exercise exposed that a 'risk' number can encode either player risk or build risk, and Harris used it for the latter at tight end — an 8 for [[Brock Bowers]] and a 5 for [[Trey McBride]] that were admittedly 'entirely about taking a tight end in the second round' rather than about the players, both of whom he rates highly. Guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) scored the same players as pure evaluations, 2 for Bowers and 2 for McBride. Harris conceded on air that for Bowers 'two is probably closer to the right answer than eight' and that he was off, while maintaining that the plunge into an early tight end is a decision about your roster you have to be ready to make. Bell applied Harris's own logic back at quarterback, scoring [[Lamar Jackson]] and [[Josh Allen]] as 6s on cost.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris restated value-based drafting as a scarcity argument against taking a receiver 1.01 even in full PPR — the replacement-level receiver is simply a better performer than the replacement-level running back, because 'there's so many receivers that are just fine, there are not very many running backs who are just fine.' His sharpest framing is aimed at analysts who speak in market language while ignoring what a market is: 'the scarce commodity is the thing it's supposed to cost the most.' Guest Andy Behrens (not tracked) added the behavioral half — best-ball and high-stakes full-PPR drafting habits, where stacking a receiver with his quarterback is correct, get migrated wholesale into 10- and 12-team half-PPR redraft leagues where they are not.
- 2025-10-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-03]]): with FantasyPros still ranking [[Brock Bowers]] 19th and [[Trey McBride]] 21st overall — ahead of [[Ashton Jeanty]] — Harris asks whether anyone would actually spend an end-of-second-round pick on either today, and answers no: 'we wouldn't have done it in August, let alone now when it's been revealed as ridiculous strategy.' He'd draft them in round four or five instead, because the other positions help you more relative to the replacement-level tight ends most teams start. He is careful not to call them worthless — the playoff-week advantage when your tight end scores 16 and the opponent gets five is 'so fun,' and they are not free — but if the market prices them as top-20-to-25 assets he is dealing. Both he and guest Josh Van Blank (NOT tracked) say they care about roughly four tight ends and joke about petitioning to remove the position from fantasy entirely.
- 2026-01-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-08]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris reiterates his standing refusal to spend an early pick on a tight end, even off historic seasons, using [[Trey McBride]]'s 2025 (fantasy point total equivalent to WR5 standard/WR4 PPR) as the example -- because a TE11/12 is available in the 11th round, the VBD gap between a great tight-end season and replacement level is much smaller than at RB/WR, so McBride's year (25th in standard VBD, 14th in PPR) still doesn't clear the bar for an early pick. He applies the same logic to [[Kyle Pitts]], whom Jim McCormick (untracked) calls an even riskier pick than McBride, and points to recent tight-end hype busts like [[Brock Bowers]], [[Sam LaPorta]], and repeated false dawns from [[Evan Engram]] as the pattern the market keeps repeating.
- 2026-08-28 — According to cousin Josh (Chris Harris's untracked co-host) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-28]]): built around [[Juwan Johnson]] going 40 picks after Travis Kelce and Dalton Kincaid with minimal real production gap, both hosts restate their preference to wait at tight end and take one of the mid-tier options (Johnson, Brenton Strange, Dallas Goedert types) rather than pay an early premium -- Harris notes he'll still take an elite TE early if the value specifically breaks that way (as he did with Trey McBride in the middle of the 4th round last year), but that it's not his default plan.
