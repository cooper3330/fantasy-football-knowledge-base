## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-01-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-14]]): the word 'functional' hides two very different receiver profiles. Koh's distinction, which Harmon immediately endorses as a great point, is that a functional receiver is a metronome like [[Darius Slayton]] — consistently average, never embarrassing on the field — whereas [[Quentin Johnston]] is an inconsistent, splashy player whose volatility is tough to live with, capable of 13 catches for 180-plus in Week 18 and zero catches on five targets in a playoff game. Harmon's addition is that analysts wrongly model players as fixed ranks ('he's the 35th-best receiver'), when some profiles are genuinely both players. The practical consequence they draw is roster-level: a volatile splash guy is survivable as a third option and disqualifying as your second-best pass catcher.
- 2025-02-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-26]]): Harmon used first downs per route run, among receivers with 250-plus routes in 2024, as his separator between receivers defenses must plan for and receivers who merely produce occasional splash plays. [[Puka Nacua]] was first by a wide margin at 17.7%, with [[A.J. Brown]] second at 14.4%. [[Cooper Kupp]] ranked 42nd, sandwiched between [[Christian Watson]] and [[Quentin Johnston]], and [[Keenan Allen]] 48th — a bucket Harmon described as sporadic, inconsistent big-play players, grouping [[Jaylen Waddle]] and calling [[Jerry Jeudy]] 'Mr. Inconsistent'. His conclusion is that the metric is instructive about where an aging or declining receiver actually is: still capable of impact, but no longer someone a defensive game plan starts with. He added the caveat that nobody in the 2024 Bears offense looks good by efficiency measures, so context still matters.
