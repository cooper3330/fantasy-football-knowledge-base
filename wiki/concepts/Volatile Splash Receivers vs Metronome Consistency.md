## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-01-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-14]]): the word 'functional' hides two very different receiver profiles. Koh's distinction, which Harmon immediately endorses as a great point, is that a functional receiver is a metronome like [[Darius Slayton]] — consistently average, never embarrassing on the field — whereas [[Quentin Johnston]] is an inconsistent, splashy player whose volatility is tough to live with, capable of 13 catches for 180-plus in Week 18 and zero catches on five targets in a playoff game. Harmon's addition is that analysts wrongly model players as fixed ranks ('he's the 35th-best receiver'), when some profiles are genuinely both players. The practical consequence they draw is roster-level: a volatile splash guy is survivable as a third option and disqualifying as your second-best pass catcher.
- 2025-02-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-26]]): Harmon used first downs per route run, among receivers with 250-plus routes in 2024, as his separator between receivers defenses must plan for and receivers who merely produce occasional splash plays. [[Puka Nacua]] was first by a wide margin at 17.7%, with [[A.J. Brown]] second at 14.4%. [[Cooper Kupp]] ranked 42nd, sandwiched between [[Christian Watson]] and [[Quentin Johnston]], and [[Keenan Allen]] 48th — a bucket Harmon described as sporadic, inconsistent big-play players, grouping [[Jaylen Waddle]] and calling [[Jerry Jeudy]] 'Mr. Inconsistent'. His conclusion is that the metric is instructive about where an aging or declining receiver actually is: still capable of impact, but no longer someone a defensive game plan starts with. He added the caveat that nobody in the 2024 Bears offense looks good by efficiency measures, so context still matters.
- 2025-10-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-28]]): citing a chart from Hayden Winks showing top-50 wide receiver weekly scoring, Harmon says Puka Nacua and Jaxon Smith-Njigba are the only two receivers this season truly immune to down weeks -- everyone else, including a hot-starting rookie like [[Emeka Egbuka]], will hit stretches where 'shit just happens' from a fantasy-scoring standpoint. His broader point: wide receiver evaluation needs at least a half-season sample, and a two-game cold stretch shouldn't by itself trigger reassessing a player's talent.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-04]]): [[Kayshon Boutte]] is Harmon's live example of a role that manufactures volatility — charted almost exclusively at X running vertical routes, his touchdown rate per target (16.1%) still exceeds his targets per route run (13.5%), a split Harmon calls very volatile and unsustainable as an offense's steady input. He argues New England needs someone hitting singles and doubles alongside [[Stefon Diggs]], and that giving [[Kyle Williams]] snaps would let Boutte slide to flanker and into the slot, smoothing his production rather than replacing him. He applies the same lens to [[Tory Horton]], whose 5 touchdowns on 22 targets is splash-heavy, and to [[Demario Douglas]] as a low-usage, high-yield player.
- 2026-01-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-01-08]]): discussing [[Jameson Williams]], guest Jim McCormick (untracked) compares him to a young DeSean Jackson -- a receiver whose big weeks make the season-total 'back of the baseball card' look great, but whose week-to-week fantasy experience feels more like a streaky home-run hitter than a reliable starter. By contrast, both hosts hope [[Emeka Egbuka]] develops into more of a metronome, comparing him to [[Chris Godwin]] rather than a pure boundary/spike-week receiver, based on his tape and pedigree even after a quiet three-game stretch (Weeks 11-13) without a touchdown.
- 2026-01-13 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-13]]): pushed back on 'Maddenification' of NFL analysis after Philadelphia's playoff loss -- fans and analysts expect star players to execute identically on every single snap, but 'these guys are not metronomes,' citing [[AJ Brown]]'s drops and [[Puka Nacua]]'s drop in an otherwise 18-catch, 18-target eruption against Carolina as evidence that variance within even elite performances is normal and shouldn't be read as a referendum on talent.
