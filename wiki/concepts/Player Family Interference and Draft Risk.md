## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]): a rarely-stated but real component of NFL draft boards is the player's family. Teams fear a parent who plays little-league dad at the pro level — parents who turn up at the facility bugging operations staff who only handle travel, ticket sales or marketing, who create office drama, or whose crises force a player to take time off. None of it reaches the media. Waldman's escalated version is the celebrity parent: Deion Sanders can call a press conference at any moment, command every major outlet and lean on ownership relationships, which he ties to the Tim Tebow media circus — if you invite the clown car in, the driver has to be really good. His rule: teams tolerate the circus only when the player clears a high enough talent bar (Antonio Brown, temporarily), and he applies it directly to [[Shedeur Sanders]] falling past round three.
- 2026-05-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-25]]): on reports that Deion Sanders wants to meet with [[Shedeur Sanders]]'s NFL coaching staff 'as a coach, not as a parent,' Waldman said 'fuck that,' calling it something he predicted when Shedeur was drafted -- that Deion would keep angling to stay involved as a parent. Waldman argued no coach who genuinely wants background on a player asks for a meeting with another team's coach through the media; it should happen privately, coach-to-coach. He said there's real narcissism in Deion's behavior that has rubbed off on Shedeur (without calling Shedeur himself a narcissist), pointing to how Shedeur handled a joint photo shoot/media moment against [[Cam Ward]], who stayed focused purely on football. Waldman said Shedeur can become a good quarterback but needs to 'cut the umbilical cord' from Deion and control his own public narrative, and speculated Deion may be positioning for a future head-coaching job (floating the Browns) partly to keep managing his son's career and his own image.
