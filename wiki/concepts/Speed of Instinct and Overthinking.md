## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]): Waldman credits Dan Hatman (Scouting Academy, NOT a tracked expert) with the term 'playing at the speed of instinct' and uses it to explain the standard camp arc for young receivers. Early camp tests isolated skills — vertical routes where the receiver catches the ball are the easiest thing for a beat writer to see — so players whose wheelhouse is straight-line speed look sensational in May through early August. Then layers get added, defenses play more sophisticated coverage, the quarterback starts changing plays at the line, and a player who is not comfortable begins thinking. Once he thinks he slows down, is a beat late on all execution, and is no longer playing at the speed of instinct. Hatman's parallel: roster hopefuls are asked only to do the basics fast, and doing the basics fast is what flashes, which does not translate when the season demands problem solving.
