## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-03-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-31]]): prospect age analysis is a nice idea with a troublesome application — Waldman likens it to the decade-old receiver model where Eagles analytics consultants filtered out anyone under 6-foot-2 and 210 pounds. Filters have value after you have evaluated every player's skills, but they are susceptible to ham-handed application, and building a scoring system around age without generating wild player-to-player variation is rife with problems. The deeper issue is that 'he will get better with experience' is the same rationalization heard annually about first-round quarterbacks who wash out. To be useful the idea requires identifying which skills actually improve with experience, which need positive rather than negative experiences to grow, and which never improve if not mastered before the pros. Age is 'a sloppy coat of white paint without examining the surface where it's supposed to go.' He argues 'raw' and 'inexperienced' are vague terms meaning different things: raw as in unexposed to the big stage (advanced skills, no tournament experience) is not the same as raw as in technically and conceptually unskilled, which is what [[Malik Willis]] was entering the league. Applied to the 2024 class: [[Drake Maye]] and [[J.J. McCarthy]]'s field reading would not simply improve with reps, and experience alone is not why [[Michael Penix Jr.]] and [[Bo Nix]] read the field consistently — self-awareness comes from skills and the desire to build them, not from age.
- 2025-04-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-22]]): an older prospect's production has to be discounted but not dismissed. On [[Andrew Armstrong]], who turns 25 on Halloween of his rookie season after a lost COVID year and a transfer up from Texas A&M Commerce, Harmon raised the age objection himself before critics could — conceding a 25-year-old beating an 18-year-old corner is a real possibility — while still arguing the in-breaking success rates (85% dig, 84% curl, 84% slant) at high volume are meaningful. His resolution is to price the risk into the expectation rather than the grade: not a home run swing at the next [[Nico Collins]], but a reasonable third-round bet on a 6-4 X with an 88th percentile wingspan who can beat press.
