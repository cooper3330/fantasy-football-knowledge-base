# Deliberate Rest and Creative Productivity

## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-10-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-17 (Writing Craft and Content)]]), with co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys; not a tracked expert): time away from the desk is part of the analytical work, not a break from it. Harstad puts roughly 10% of his writing on the screen and 90% away from it — walks, the gym — and cites a decades-old US Army study in which artillery teams forced onto eight-hours-on, eight-hours-off shifts landed many more hits on target than a team firing continuously for three or four days, despite nominally working half as long. Waldman says he burns out three to four times a year and treats it like eating an endless plate of chocolate chip cookies; his rule is a football blackout every Friday from noon, extended into Saturday if needed, and during 16-18 hour RSP stretches he stops when a single paragraph starts taking an hour, because output only degrades from there. He credits Charles Bukowski and Pulitzer winner Jackie Banaszynski for the same framing — if you see me bouncing a ball off a wall, I'm writing — and describes the subconscious needing the conscious mind out of the way. Practical corollary for anyone doing evaluation work at volume: schedule the off-hours deliberately rather than grinding.
