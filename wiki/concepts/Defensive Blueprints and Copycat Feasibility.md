## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): a defensive game plan that works is only a league-wide blueprint if other teams have the materials to run it. Pittsburgh shut down [[Jonathan Taylor]], harassed [[Daniel Jones]] into two strip sacks and three interceptions, and denied [[Tyler Warren]] the free flat releases that had been producing gimme first downs — but Waldman put only about a 35 percent chance on defenses replicating it, because most do not have T.J. Watt, Alex Highsmith, Nick Herbig and a Jalen Ramsey who can play safety. He separated the two ingredients: the personnel edge, which is scarce, and the discipline of simplifying calls and refusing to let a linebacker or safety chase a decoy route that already has coverage, which is copyable. He also held a fantasy verdict in escrow on it — [[Colston Loveland]] is the better player than Warren, but Warren's fantasy demotion depends on whether the blueprint travels.
