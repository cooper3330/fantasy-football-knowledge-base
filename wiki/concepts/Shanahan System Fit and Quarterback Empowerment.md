## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-30]]): not every quarterback needs to be a Shanahan quarterback. He describes the system as having every answer baked into the play call — Shanahan's stated view is that quarterbacks do not need to check because there are answers to a weak-side blitz and a strong-side blitz inside each design — which makes it 'a great floor elevator' but not always the way to maximize a top-tier quarterback. His template is the Rams going from the [[Jared Goff]] incubator offense to spreading the field and activating every blade of grass with [[Matthew Stafford]], and he applies it directly to [[C.J. Stroud]]: get him in the gun, let him be a point guard, move away from under-center play-action. Koh (not a tracked expert) frames the same idea as playbook mastery versus coverage recognition, and worries a new coordinator restarts Stroud's learning curve. Harmon notes the league is drifting from the Shanahan tree toward Sean Payton and Detroit derivatives.
- 2025-02-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-04]]): people wrongly bundle Sean McVay's offense in with the Shanahan system. McVay has run several different offenses keyed to what the quarterback wants and what the surrounding talent is good at — cookie-cutter Shanahan play-action and screens to lift [[Jared Goff]]'s floor, then shotgun/empty 11-personnel drop-back football once [[Matthew Stafford]] arrived in 2021, then back toward the Goff-style play-action menu in 2024. Crucially, the focal point is not a static X receiver: it was [[Puka Nacua]] moving everywhere or [[Cooper Kupp]] as a power slot, neither of which is the number one read in most Shanahan concepts. Koh (not tracked) adds the run-game timeline — exclusively outside zone until 2023, then almost exclusively gap scheme — as evidence of adaptability. The applied point: Bobby Slowik ran the Shanahan greatest hits in Houston and never got past them, so Harmon wants Nick Caley to blend the Patriots and Rams worlds and hand [[C.J. Stroud]] Stafford-style latitude rather than an offense where every answer is pre-built into the play.
- 2025-02-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-26]]): Harmon and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) argued that positional interchangeability is close to a prerequisite for playing receiver in the Shanahan system — [[Brandon Aiyuk]], [[Jauan Jennings]] and [[Ricky Pearsall]] all fit because each can move inside and outside, which is what makes the trio work as a three-receiver set. The corollary is fragility: with [[Brock Purdy]] a good-to-borderline-great quarterback rather than one who can put an offense on his back, Harmon said you have to surround him with players who can get open, and removing Aiyuk leaves nobody who can win deep. On the Rams side, Harmon argued a McVay-tree offense can bend toward its quarterback rather than the reverse, citing Matt LaFleur molding McVay concepts to Aaron Rodgers's preferences en route to two MVPs and the Rams already living in more drop-back, empty-spread looks for [[Matthew Stafford]] than the [[Jared Goff]] version — with the caveat, raised by Koh, that getting Rodgers off a lifetime of West Coast dink-and-dunk into three-step-drop dig routes is the real obstacle, and that the Rams may be the one team able to dictate terms to him.
- 2025-03-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-03]]): Waldman's harshest take of the episode was on coordinator ego overriding quarterback autonomy. Citing Pittsburgh Courier reporting that Arthur Smith told [[Russell Wilson]] not to change the system or adjust to what the defense was doing after a near-500-yard game against Cincinnati, Waldman argued that taking a player who is performing well and telling him to be 'a pixel in my offense' — with worse results following — is disqualifying, because reading the defense and making adjustments is part of the job description. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) offered the defense that Smith's reputation was forged on genuine work ethic rather than nepotism; Waldman accepted that and redirected the blame partly at media who told Smith he was a genius. The general principle he applied elsewhere: [[Derek Carr]] sits at the low end of Kellen Moore's quarterbacks because scheme cannot fix a player's ceiling, while [[Deebo Samuel]] in Cliff Kingsbury's screen-and-RPO offense shows the opposite case — a knowable scheme still works if the weapons win their matchups.
- 2025-05-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-19]]): Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) framed [[Brock Purdy]] as the player you see on the field 'except with a tiny little Kyle Shanahan in his brain pulling the levers,' noting Shanahan told the owner during rookie camp that Purdy would probably end up the starter. Waldman accepts the framing but rejects its dismissive reading — that is the same argument made about Sean McVay and Jared Goff, and it undersells the job. His reframe: this is a player who executes the instructions given to him in the way the coach wants, which is part and parcel of playing quarterback in the NFL and is genuinely hard. Purdy understands the offense and executes it quicker, with the accuracy and confidence most quarterbacks lack.
