# Post-Rookie-Year Receiver Model

## Definition

A quantitative model built by **Adam Harstad** (RSP Cast *Film and Theory*
co-host — not a tracked expert) that grades wide receivers immediately after
their rookie year to forecast future value. It combines two terms: a
touchdown-adjusted yards-per-route-run efficiency score (weighted 2x, with a
20-point bonus per touchdown — a constant borrowed from 1989's *Hidden Game of
Football* adjusted-yards-per-attempt work) and a usage score (routes run per
game, divided by team pass attempts per game). The two are put on a common
scale (100 = perfectly average among all qualifiers: receivers with 250+
routes run as a rookie since 2006) and weight-averaged. Harstad deliberately
avoids tuning the model by maximizing R², calling that overfitting to past
data, and instead validates it by "face validity" — does the ranked list of
names look like the outcomes we already know happened.

Historically bucketed ranges (per Harstad, as of the 2023 rookie class):
- **Below ~91 (even with top-50 draft capital):** "abandon all hope" — Jordy
  Nelson is the standing outlier exception; the realistic recent ceiling for
  this range is a career depth/complementary role (Nelson Agholor, Darius
  Heyward-Bay, Jacoby Meyers).
- **~91–100:** still mostly misses, but a few complicated/qualified hits.
- **~100–110:** roughly average — includes real long-term starters (Robert
  Woods-type outcomes) but no guarantee.
- **~110–117:** "very good rookie season," expectation is a long-term starter.
- **~118–123:** "fantastic rookie season" — historically almost no outright
  busts in this range (Mike Evans, Chris Olave, Terry McLaurin, A.J. Green,
  Keenan Allen, Julio Jones all cleared it).
- **123+:** the historic "big four" tier (Odell Beckham Jr., Ja'Marr Chase,
  Justin Jefferson, A.J. Brown).

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-11 — **Adam Harstad** (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-01-11 (Post-Rookie Model)]]) [Dynasty]: introduces the 2023 rookie class results — a record 18–19 qualifiers (previous high: 15, in 2014), which he attributes partly to the 17-game season but mostly to teams being more willing to throw underprepared rookies into full-time roles than in past years. Flags a known confound: receivers who play more in 3-plus-WR sets get deflated efficiency scores because target competition rises with each extra receiver on the field, independent of quality — a receiver moving from a 3-WR to a 2-WR role should be read as a positive sign even if the raw score doesn't move.
- 2024-01-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]], same episode [Dynasty]: explicitly frames the model as one input, not a verdict — "I often like players more or less than the model suggests... you're not going to shut the whole thing down" when it disagrees with your own tape read. Cites his own 2023 case: the model favored Christian Watson over Garrett Wilson, and Waldman said at the time he still wouldn't trade Watson for Wilson regardless of the score. Uses the model in this episode mainly to sanity-check and re-rank his existing 2023 rookie WR board (see individual player pages: [[Dontayvion Wicks]], [[Jayden Reed]], [[Rashee Rice]], [[Tank Dell]], [[Puka Nacua]], [[Zay Flowers]], [[Jordan Addison]], [[Quentin Johnston]], [[Jalen Hyatt]], [[Marvin Mims Jr.]], [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]], [[Josh Downs]], [[Michael Wilson]], [[Cedric Tillman]], [[Tyler Scott]], [[Jonathan Mingo]], [[Demario Douglas]]).
- 2024-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-02-13]]): a Harstad-adjacent comparison from a different methodology (Harmon's own Reception Perception charting rather than Harstad's efficiency/usage score), floated only as a pointer to Harmon's separate YouTube mailbag rather than argued out on the podcast itself — [[Rashee Rice]]'s rookie-year profile compared to [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]'s, the reference case elsewhere in this wiki for a slot-heavy rookie role expanding into a true year-two alpha.
- 2024-05-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-09]]): Previewing RP's Year 2 receiver profiles, Harmon warned that rookie-year fantasy expectations are frequently 'way too out of whack' -- [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]]'s 'disappointing' 630-yard rookie season actually graded well on tape and by RP metrics given he played behind two superstar-caliber receivers, and [[Rashad Bateman]] and [[Zay Flowers]] both showed real in-season route-running growth that a box score alone doesn't capture. His framework for reading Year 2 breakouts leans as much on situational context -- target competition ahead of the player, scheme fit, injury history entering the year -- as on raw Year 1 box-score outcomes.
- 2024-05-17 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-05-17]]) [Dynasty]: discussing [[Ja'Lynn Polk]], Angelo lays out an archetype of receivers who aren't the sexiest athletes but become long-term producers because coaches can rely on them down-to-down — he names [[Noah Brown]], [[Puka Nacua]], Jacoby Meyers, [[Chris Godwin]] and [[Khalil Shakir]] as examples, and compares Polk's under-the-radar profile to [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]'s as a rookie, whose competence wasn't obvious on tape pre-draft but showed up quickly in games through route/timing chemistry and catch-point aggression rather than pure talent.
- 2024-05-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-05-21]]): he explicitly compares [[Dontayvion Wicks]]'s rookie trajectory -- charted success rates rising from the in-season rookie report through season's end -- to [[Nico Collins]]'s path, where a strong underlying reception-perception profile went unnoticed as a rookie before breaking out once target competition thinned in year two. Harmon says the offensive environment (unlike Collins's situation) is already in place for Wicks, making him a plausible year-two breakout even though nobody on Green Bay's crowded WR room projects to a clear 120-target role.
- 2024-06-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-18]]), comparing [[Chris Olave]]'s RP profile across his first two NFL seasons to [[Garrett Wilson]]'s: while Olave's numbers are excellent and stable (75.8% to 76.3% vs. man, 81.2% to 81.5% vs. zone, press locked around 77%), there's no year-two jump the way Wilson showed a leap to what Harmon calls a '93rd-percentile-type' man/press score. Harmon treats the presence or absence of that sophomore-to-junior leap as a meaningful signal of diverging developmental trajectories within the 2022 receiver class ([[Garrett Wilson]], [[Drake London]], Olave), even when poor QB/scheme context depresses all three's box-score outcomes similarly.
- 2024-07-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-07-25]]): the year-three jump is a real and identifiable inflection, and Reception Perception separation data can flag it before the box score does. His template is [[Stefon Diggs]], whose first two seasons were merely very good before a 78.7% success rate vs man in 2017 led the NFL and sat above the 90th percentile — the indicator that another level existed, well before the big statistical seasons in Buffalo. He applies the framework to [[Chris Olave]], whose year-one and year-two marks are almost identical across man, zone and press and who therefore faces a fork: plateau as a very high tier-two receiver like [[Terry McLaurin]] or [[Tyler Lockett]], or make a [[Nico Collins]]-style leap. He asks the same of third-year [[Rashid Shaheed]], where the unlock would be the zone-coverage score rather than man.
- 2024-08-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-06]]): Harmon argues year-two projection has to account for how much offseason time a rookie lost, because receivers are not Madden players who return to normal on activation — a new coordinator does not know how to deploy them or which routes they are best on, and reps have to be banked in practice since in-season work is game-plan install, not skill development. He applies this to [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]], whose missed offseason he blames for a directionless first four weeks. The exception is stars: he says [[Justin Jefferson]] would just go back to being Justin Jefferson, but the logic holds for roughly 95% of the league. He also notes the inverse problem with [[Puka Nacua]] — a huge rookie year invites the question of whether it was the apex rather than a baseline.
- 2024-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-08]]): Harmon argues the market has become far too quick to bury receivers after one year, or even after a few August practices. Rookies — especially day-two and day-three picks — must adjust to NFL speed, NFL route trees and playing varied alignments they rarely saw in college, so a veteran running ahead of a rookie in camp is normal and not a signal ([[Adonai Mitchell]] behind [[Alec Pierce]]). Equally, a bad rookie season is not terminal: he cites [[Josh Palmer]] as the template for a poor rookie year becoming a functional pro, offers it as the realistic path for [[Jonathan Mingo]], and applies the same patience to [[Jalen Tolbert]], whose 39 career targets he calls an incomplete grade rather than a verdict. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) applies the model less charitably to [[Xavier Legette]]'s 2024 production.
- 2024-08-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-19]]): a subset of receivers start slow for the first two to four years of a career and then become genuinely good, so a late-blooming camp riser deserves openness rather than dismissal. His named examples are [[Adam Thielen]], Ed McCaffrey and Derrick Mason, and he applies the model to [[Olamide Zaccheaus]] — a player WR coach Drew Lieberman says should have started in Atlanta and who forced his way from late-round/UDFA status into a deep-threat role that was not even the strength of his game. Waldman is careful about the claim's strength: he is not predicting it, only saying we should be open to Zaccheaus possibly being that guy.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-19]]): Harmon offers a journey comparable rather than a style comparable for [[George Pickens]] — [[D.J. Moore]], whose rookie-year Reception Perception profile was a mess as a route runner at 52.7 percent success vs man, worse than Pickens has been, before jumping from 47 to 68 percent vs press in year two and settling above 70 percent vs man from 2020 to 2022 with strong zone and press marks. Harmon is explicit that the two players are not similar as athletes or archetypes; the point is the shape of the development curve, and that Pickens could be a year-three RP breakout.
- 2024-10-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-03]]) [Dynasty]: the core error behind the 2022-class disappointment is assuming great rookie year plus good second season equals [[Justin Jefferson]]. 'Most receivers don't do that' — most plateau somewhere and never make the jump to the elite tier. Harmon's supporting evidence is that his elite tier entering the prior season — Jefferson, [[Davante Adams]], [[Stefon Diggs]], [[Tyreek Hill]], [[A.J. Brown]], [[Ja'Marr Chase]], [[Cooper Kupp]] — came from draft classes scattered across 2014, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2019 and 2021, not one or two per year on schedule. He also separates two failure modes: being good immediately and then never improving ([[Chris Olave]], [[Drake London]], with near-identical year-one and year-two RP profiles) versus actively regressing ([[Jahan Dotson]]). Koh's counter is that better quarterback play tends to unlock a step forward; Harmon's caveat is that it is four games in and points to [[CeeDee Lamb]]'s slow 2023 start as proof the story is not written.
- 2024-11-22 — According to Adam Harstad, untracked co-host ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]) [Dynasty]: Harstad described his rookie production model as weighing the factors of a rookie season that historically translate to later production rather than the raw counting stats. Two outputs he cited: [[Drake London]] as the 14th-best rookie season in the sample, sitting next to [[A.J. Green]], [[Keenan Allen]] and Julio Jones, which he used to argue against the market selling London; and [[Chris Olave]] as the 7th-best rookie season since 2008, behind [[Odell Beckham Jr.]], [[Ja'Marr Chase]], [[Justin Jefferson]], [[A.J. Brown]], [[Puka Nacua]] and [[Mike Evans]], and ahead of Marques Colston, [[Terry McLaurin]], A.J. Green, Keenan Allen and Julio Jones. Harstad's framing of the downside case: the worst outcome near the top of that list is McLaurin, a genuinely good fantasy receiver — an acceptable floor for a discounted asset. The model also flagged [[Tyreek Hill]] after year one.
- 2024-12-31 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-31]]) [Dynasty]: 'a bad rookie season is not all that defines you.' Harmon argues the modern six-game-and-it's-over verdict on rookie receivers is wrong, using [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] as the live case and citing [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] (bad statistical rookie year, now one of the most impressive receivers in the league), [[Jalen Tolbert]] (not a star but a useful player) and [[Quentin Johnston]] (serious flaws, but a plausible WR3 rather than out of the league). His counterweight is honest: sometimes the excuses are wrong and the player is [[Skyy Moore]] — 'it is jover' for some of them. Koh reframes it as the old year-three breakout standard, which he says the dynasty space has largely abandoned. Harmon extends the watchlist to other 2023-class receivers: [[Dontayvion Wicks]], [[Michael Wilson]], [[Demario Douglas]], and says probably not [[Jonathan Mingo]] now that he is on a second team.
- 2025-01-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-09]]): the episode's most-improved discussion sharpens the year-two framework around created plays versus plays created for you. Harmon says the tier-one marker is a receiver who creates plays on his own, and that [[Puka Nacua]]'s rookie season skewed heavily toward scheme-created touches under Sean McVay while year two tipped toward iso wins in tight coverage — the shift, not the raw production, is the signal. He explicitly says manufactured touches are not an insult, citing [[Malik Nabers]] as a rookie who maximizes them. [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] is the other case: a fine but unremarkable rookie profile followed by a second-half leap to bottom-of-tier-two play. Harmon also applies a forward-looking version to [[Khalil Shakir]], wondering whether he can add layers beyond the short-area slot role the way [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] did across his career.
- 2025-05-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-23]]): rookie-year charting data is 'infinitely more valuable' than prospect data, and analysts should be quick to drop their priors once a player is seen in the NFL — especially when the pro role differs from the college one. He cites [[Rashee Rice]] (poor prospect profile, different NFL role) and [[Jalen McMillan]] (slot at Washington, perimeter flanker in Tampa) as cases where the prospect chart simply does not describe the job being done. The corollary is that a rookie-year collapse in a graded strength is real information, not noise: [[Ja'Lynn Polk]] going from an 80%-plus contested catch rate and near-zero drops as a prospect to 20% and a 10.7% drop rate is the kind of reversal Harmon treats as a genuine miss on his own evaluation rather than something to explain away.
- 2025-10-09 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys; NOT a tracked expert) with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-09]]) [Dynasty]: buy rookie receivers, sell sophomore receivers. The mechanism is that the market talks itself into excuses in the window right after a rookie season, so the price barely moves even on a disaster — he cited N'Keal Harry, whose rookie year was about as catastrophic as possible and whose dynasty ADP fell only six to eight slots. That makes a rookie receiver an unusually stable store of value through a rebuild. The catch is follow-through: you must actually sell after year one if nothing showed, and most managers can't, because they start imagining the Demaryius Thomas outcome (underwhelming rookie year, later All-Pro). He pairs this with selling aging All-Pros like [[Davante Adams]] and [[Travis Kelce]] regardless of production, since the value loss over a rebuild window is guaranteed.
