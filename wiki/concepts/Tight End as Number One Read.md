## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): in most NFL offenses the tight end is never the first read in a concept — the talent gap between the tight end and the receiver room has to be so wide that the tight end effectively ascends an entire playbook, and teams would rather hit big plays to their wide outs than nickel-and-dime with tight ends. He cites [[Sam LaPorta]] falling behind [[Jameson Williams]] in Detroit once Williams was activated, and reserves the exception for a [[Travis Kelce]] or [[George Kittle]] — noting even Kittle is not always first in the progression, and that his usage reflects a particular offense and a particular player. Harmon applies it to [[Dalton Kincaid]]: the error was fantasy drafters (not necessarily Buffalo) assuming Kincaid was good enough to become the No. 1 read.
