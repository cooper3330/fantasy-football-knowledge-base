## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-13 (Paper Champions and All-Timers Across Eras)]]): untracked co-host Adam Harstad argues that whenever a fan first starts paying attention to football gets locked in as the golden era, and every aesthetic preference thereafter is dictated by it. His example is people who grew up in the 1970s demanding a return to defense and running, when that football was in reality unwatchable — which is precisely why the Mel Blount rule and the surrounding rule changes happened, because the league could not grow the sport playing that way. He names his own bias openly: the early 2000s, Clinton Portis, Ricky Williams, LaDainian Tomlinson, Priest Holmes, tight ends like Tony Gonzalez, Antonio Gates and Shannon Sharpe, and the post-realignment AFC West Denver naked-bootleg concepts he calls the platonic ideal of play design. Useful as a self-check on why analysts privilege certain archetypes.
