## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-05-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-07]]): teams periodically announce that a big receiving tight end will play X receiver, and Harmon argues the idea has an almost unbroken failure record because these players are simply not better receivers than wide receivers, so the deployment produces diminishing returns. His examples: [[Kyle Pitts]], a top-10 unicorn athlete for whom outside alignment was a cutesy wrinkle at best and who was then not good enough inline; [[Dalton Kincaid]], sold as a slot weapon and squeezed out by [[Khalil Shakir]] inside and Dawson Knox inline because he is a big slot receiver and a small tight end; and now the report on [[Elijah Arroyo]], where Harmon notes there are no 250-pound X receivers in the league. He allows the technical loophole — a tight end can end a snap as the outermost man on the line when a receiver motions across — but says that is not an X role. Co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) framed the messaging as a tell about team desperation on the perimeter rather than a real plan, adding that college defensive backs let this look work in a way NFL corners will not.
- 2025-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]): the recurring front-office claim that a big rookie tight end can play X receiver should be treated as a warning rather than a selling point. Seattle GM John Schneider said some coaches — reportedly including Clint Kubiak — thought [[Elijah Arroyo]] could do it, and Harmon's reaction was flat rejection. His argument is the [[Kyle Pitts]] case: putting a tight end at X made him the biggest and slowest X receiver in the league, and Arroyo at 6-5 and 254 pounds is bigger and likely slower. Harmon adds the incentive test — if a player could genuinely play receiver he would be a receiver, because he knows what receivers get paid. Both hosts argue the honest description would be 'big slot', a role Seattle actually needs, and that red-zone work and deep seams are not X receiver duties.
- 2026-07-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-07-21]]): argued the popular framing of tight ends as would-be X receivers ('the next Jaxon Smith-Njigba') is usually a category error, since the two positions are used so differently even for the most mobile TEs. He noted only a handful of receivers even drew a target as an inline tight end last year -- [[Puka Nacua]] (4), [[Michael Pittman Jr.]], [[Khalil Shakir]], [[Christian Watson]] and [[Tre Tucker]] among them (3 each) -- underscoring how rare true alignment overlap is, and that the only genuine WR-usage/TE-usage crossover comp he'd endorse is [[Mike Gesicki]]. Harmon used [[Brock Bowers]]' low inline-TE target rate (21.7%) as evidence Bowers is already closer to a movement piece than a traditional TE, but still argued the better real-world comp for that kind of deployment is [[Drake London]]'s off-ball/Z usage rather than a wide receiver breakout model like JSN's.
