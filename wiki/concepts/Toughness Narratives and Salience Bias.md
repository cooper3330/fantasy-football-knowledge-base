# Toughness Narratives and Salience Bias

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Film & Theory)]]): the industry holds a myth that football players are gladiators who will always throw their bodies into the fire, and 'soft' is the worst insult available — worse than 'bad' — so once a receiver crosses that line the perception is permanent. Waldman argues that if you watch enough film on Anquan Boldin, Steve Smith, Larry Fitzgerald or Austin Collie you will find plays where they let up on the ball too; those plays were excused because the reputation was already established or the rest of the game was strong. [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] had two such moments in one game inside a bad game, which is what fixed the narrative. Harstad (Football Guys, not a tracked expert) frames the same effect as salience: if you do not see it happen, it did not happen. [[Mike Evans]] is said to have terrible hands in years he drops two passes in a nationally televised game and fine hands in years his drops come in unwatched Bucs games; he cites [[CeeDee Lamb]] this year as the current instance. Their shared conclusion is that players are simply temperamentally different, and demanding one be another is not evaluation.
