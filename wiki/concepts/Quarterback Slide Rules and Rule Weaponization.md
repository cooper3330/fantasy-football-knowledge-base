## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-12-05 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-05]]): quarterback protection rules are good for the product — quarterback injuries are 'way, way, way down' from 25 years ago and fewer backup snaps is a major reason offense is better — but if you tell a defender he will miss weeks for hitting a sliding quarterback, you cannot let quarterbacks exploit that. Harstad wants a 15-yard penalty for a faked slide, full stop, and objects to [[Patrick Mahomes]] drifting toward the sideline before pulling up late. Both hosts allowed Tom Brady's point that some onus belongs on offensive players, while Harstad rejected Brady's older claim that quarterbacks should protect defenseless receivers themselves — 'some quarterbacks are not that good', and receivers stuck with bad passers still deserve protection. Waldman likened rule-bending to [[DeAndre Hopkins]] as veteran savvy: smart, not cheating, and the fix belongs to the rule-makers.
- 2025-05-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-26]]): on the tush push ban vote (defeated 22-10), Waldman separates sour grapes from safety. He says he would suspect competitive motive if Kliff Kingsbury, Brian Daboll or Dallas were leading the charge, but Sean McDermott doubling down cuts against that read, since Buffalo is among the highest quarterback-sneak teams even though [[Josh Allen]] does not score nearly as much from inside the five. With medical experts saying there is no definitive proof but common-sense mechanisms for added injury, he prefers the NFL getting ahead of an injury question rather than its usual pattern of letting the curve pass it by for years. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) disagrees, saying the only injuries so far are to the pride of teams that lost to it, and notes Jerry Jones openly admitting he flip-flops on whether he is against the play or just against Philadelphia having an edge. Waldman also doubts owners are the right people to ask, preferring coaches with boots-on-the-ground contact with players.
