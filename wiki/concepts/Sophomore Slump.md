## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-12-30 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-30]]): sophomore slumps are real, but the definition matters — more often than not what looks like a player slump is the slump of the team around him. The genuine player-side version is a rookie who starts strong, is relied on more, decides he has 'made it' and eases off the offseason work; Waldman's example is Ryan Mathews with San Diego, an unbelievably talented back Norv Turner was ready to build the whole offense around, who never dedicated himself in year two and salvaged only a decent career. The failure mode he describes is treating reaching the NFL as the goal rather than thriving in it. Bob Harris (co-host, NOT a tracked expert) adds that slumps arrive at any point in a career — second year, third year, anywhere — and usually owe more to circumstances around the player than to the player, so he resists the label. Applied to [[C.J. Stroud]], Waldman lands on infrastructure collapse while Harris keeps the slump label for market-value reasons.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]): quarterback development is an ebb-and-flow process across the first two years, and defenses come back from a full offseason better prepared, posing obstacles the young player has not yet proven he can solve. Waldman applied this to [[Bo Nix]] at Underdog QB7, expecting challenges that lower his ADP and value without opening the trap door — a low-end QB1 floor with top-15 upside. The same reasoning underlies his caution on treating any strong rookie season as a settled baseline.
- 2025-05-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-26]]): Waldman uses the year-two adjustment as the core of his [[Jayden Daniels]] fade — historically, rookie quarterbacks with strong first years still struggle somewhat in year two because NFL defenses have more film and more time on them, and he expects opponents to have spent the summer building tests for Daniels specifically. He is careful that this is not a bust call; it is a price argument, since the payoff for a repeat is already in the QB3 ADP. He contrasts this with [[Jalen Hurts]], who has already been through the rookie hype and the second-year adjustment period, and with the general reluctance to pay a 'historic season premium' — historic seasons are historic because they do not happen often, which is also his stated reason for passing on [[Brock Bowers]].
