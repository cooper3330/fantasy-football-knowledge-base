## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Going Deep Listener Q&A)]]), answering a listener question about which running back metrics are sticky: start with the offensive line, measured as yards before contact per attempt, then read the back's efficiency per rush against it — you want the back at or above the expectation the blocking sets, never below. His two poles are [[DeAndre Swift]] in Chicago (line first or second in yards before contact at about 1.89, back 31st or 32nd in efficiency per attempt — an indictment) and early-career [[Saquon Barkley]] with the Giants (line 28th or 29th in yards before contact, back at 4.6-4.7 yards per carry — lemonade from lemons). His second metric is missed tackles forced, or yards created, because it is what gets coordinators a second contract: it is the thing the other guy cannot do. But he flags the trap — coaches conflate making people miss with playing on schedule. Swift has earned a third contract on it while being poor down to down; [[David Montgomery]] worked in Detroit and not Chicago; [[Miles Sanders]] worked in Philadelphia and nowhere else. [[Matt Waldman]] agrees but insists the metrics only stick if paired with front-end analysis of the player's movement type.
