## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-11-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-11]]): 'resilience is not an accident,' and Waldman argues the analytics crowd tends to divorce emotion from football because it can't be measured by their tools — while football is a physical game where an inability to feel getting punched in the mouth would be pathological. Detroit is his case study: Dan Campbell's derided 'biting kneecaps' introduction described a real philosophy, the team absorbed heavy early losses, and the Campbell/Brad Holmes pairing acquired great players a lot of people didn't think were great — [[David Montgomery]] ('too slow'), [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] (six to eight receivers drafted ahead of him, and he reads that list before every game), [[Jared Goff]] (dismissed as a McVay puppet, run out of town as loose change in a trade, and who took a Troy Aikman-style beating at Cal first). Goff threw five interceptions — one a Hail Mary, two tipped, one genuinely bad — and still won. Waldman extends the frame to Nick Caserio hitting on DeMeco Ryans in Houston, and inverts it for Chicago, where he questions the organization's approach.
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14]]): Detroit is the worked example. Waldman argues the media that mocked Dan Campbell's kneecap-biting introductory press conference as bro-code phraseology misread legitimate emotional intelligence — Campbell was announcing a plan to build a culture of resiliency, and taught momentum explicitly, gathering players after hard practices during Hard Knocks to explain how to seize momentum and curtail an opponent's. Angelo adds that Brad Holmes built the roster on the same axis: culturally fitting players who are good at football, indifference to 40 times and bench press, and knowing where a player will be down to down. They point to the 1-win season where Detroit became the team nobody wanted to play, ownership sticking with Campbell after year two because the locker room never fractured, and 4-0 since Aidan Hutchinson's injury as proof. Angelo calls this the best team in the NFL and the hardest out.
- 2025-01-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-06]]): a Black Monday episode built almost entirely on this idea. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) argued the key variable is a healthy coach–general manager pairing on the same page, citing Detroit's Brad Holmes and Dan Campbell plus Minnesota, Houston and San Francisco, and noting Kyle Shanahan demanded that control in San Francisco — which is why he expects Ben Johnson to be selective and prioritize front-office alignment over the fastest ladder climb. Waldman added the corollary that a coach inheriting a lame-duck GM like Trent Baalke gains bargaining power and a ready-made scapegoat, so the arrangement is really a backdoor dismissal. Both defended continuity as the default — Indianapolis with Shane Steichen, the Giants with Daboll and Schoen, Miami with McDaniel — arguing teams with sustained success find the right combination and stay with it, while fans are notably reactive. Waldman also flagged the Rooney Rule risk that interviews for candidates like Aaron Glenn can become performative rather than merit-based.
- 2025-01-27 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-27]]): his blunt private-conversation answer on evaluating new hires is that 'nobody fucking knows' who is a good GM or a good GM–coach pairing before the fact, because political dynamics dominate outcomes and most commentators have never managed anything and cannot see those dynamics — the whole thing is a crapshoot. He cites Dan Hatman of the Scouting Academy (not a tracked expert) as favouring hiring GM and coach at the same time, so neither accumulates political clout over the other and neither can backbite and blame; they walk planks of the same length. Both hosts point to Campbell–Brad Holmes, Kwesi Adofo-Mensah–Kevin O'Connell and Nick Caserio–DeMeco Ryans as duos nobody knew would be dynamic, and note the copycat league will keep replicating the young-coach/young-GM template — except Dallas, who will do what Jerry Jones does. Applied cases: the Trent Baalke perception problem in Jacksonville ([[Trevor Lawrence]]), defending Sean McDermott via the Marty Schottenheimer precedent, and Brian Schottenheimer in Dallas, where Waldman feels the criticism but notes Dan Campbell was mocked too.
