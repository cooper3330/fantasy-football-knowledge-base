# NFL Combine and Pro Day Skepticism

## Definition

Matt Waldman's recurring position that combine workouts and pro days are
overrated as team-evaluation tools relative to film. In his framework, these
events are genuinely useful mainly for filling information gaps on
small-school or lesser-scouted prospects — not for moving the needle on
players teams have already scouted thoroughly on tape. He separates this from
the entertainment value of the events, which he doesn't dispute.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-02-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-02-26]]): the combine's real value to NFL teams is filling gaps on players they haven't seen enough of — smaller-school prospects, or players whose measurable athletic traits the tape doesn't clearly show — plus in-person interviews. He does not consider it a legitimate tool for re-grading players teams have already scouted extensively, and calls any team claiming a workout changed its mind on an established prospect "nonsense." Cites [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] skipping all combine workouts (and going without an agent) as the correct call for a prospect who's "done all that he needs to do" on film — losing weight to look faster in a drill unrepresentative of his actual game would only cost him development time elsewhere. Relays (from a prior *Going Deep* episode) [[Brandon Angelo]]'s first-hand critique of combine testing reliability — as a former Purdue track athlete and performance trainer familiar with the actual equipment used, Angelo has found the machines inconsistently calibrated and the resulting numbers "very frequently not nearly as accurate as they're made out to be." Recounts a historical Alex Smith pro-day anecdote (via the late Gil Brandt/*Sports Illustrated* reporting, corroborated independently to Waldman by a scout who was in the room): a St. Louis Rams front-office executive brought scouts an edited highlight tape of Smith's pro day and told them to disregard their own completed scouting reports, nearly causing a fistfight among the scouting staff — offered as concrete evidence of how scripted and gamed pro days can be, the same dynamic Waldman says overrated Zach Wilson and Mitchell Trubisky (neither tracked) as prospects. Endorses Kent Lee Platt's Relative Athletic Score (RAS) — Platt is not a tracked expert, an external analytics account — as a worthwhile secondary reference, with the explicit caveat that film should "trump" the measurement. Applies the same logic to combine invitations for lower-profile 2024 running back prospects: see [[Deshaun Fenwick]] (invited, unresolved measurables) vs. [[Blake Watson]] (not invited, read as a limited-team-interest signal rather than a talent verdict).
