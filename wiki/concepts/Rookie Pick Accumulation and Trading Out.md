# Rookie Pick Accumulation and Trading Out

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]) [Dynasty]: trading out of the final three rounds of rookie drafts is a good idea this year, and Waldman is starting to think it is a good idea most years — or at least worth gauging where in a given class the talent gets dicey and setting the trade-out point there. He cites Alex Hanowitz's line that picks don't get hurt, don't get suspended, don't lose interest in the game and don't retire, framing pick accumulation as investing: convert picks into more and higher picks each year and in two or three years you are holding multiple firsts, seconds and thirds with the option to draft or roll forward. His stated caveat is honesty about the hit rate — anything from round three on 'feels like a total crap shoot,' and probably round two as well; Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) countered that round one can be a crapshoot too, citing his roster's [[Javonte Williams]]. Waldman admitted he took no offers this year and drafted players instead ([[Elijah Arroyo]] rather than a 2027 third), calling it 'the optimism of the unknown.'
- 2025-10-09 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys; NOT a tracked expert) with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-09]]) [Dynasty]: rookie picks are the only genuinely safe store of value in dynasty — they never tear an ACL, never get suspended, and when one prospect busts another shoots up the board to offset, so a pick holds its price where a player like [[Joe Burrow]] can lose value to a single injury. That makes picks the backbone of a rebuild. His caveat is a market shift: rookie picks are far harder to acquire now than five to ten years ago because the market has tightened around them, which is why he has pivoted toward buying rookie receivers, selling sophomore receivers, buying proven veteran quarterbacks and hunting discounted dented cans instead.
- 2026-03-19 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-19]]): With a late-first/early-second rookie pick, Angelo would look to trade for proven veterans if his roster is one or two players from contending, and separately floated trading two years out -- dealing 2027 draft capital for extra 2028 first- and second-round picks -- for rebuilding teams that can't get the specific 2027 pick they want. He argued this year's late-first picks play more like mid-seconds, with value flattening through the second and third rounds, and that patient rebuilders can still trade back into the 2027 class using accumulated 2028 draft capital roughly a year from now.
- 2026-04-01 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-01]]) [Dynasty]: For the 2026 RB class -- weak on draft capital and opportunity but underrated on talent, in Waldman's view -- he recommended fantasy teams not one or two runners from contention should trade current rookie picks for more, earlier picks in the 2027 and 2028 drafts rather than reach for this class's backs, prioritizing receivers and quarterbacks early and trading for established RBs once a talent stockpile is built. He named [[Jeremiah Love]] as the sole exception worth an early pick for contenders.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-05-07 (Going Deep with Brandon Angelo)]]): with the 2026 rookie class seen as wonky/weak and everyone hoarding 2027 first-round capital for a loaded class (led by receivers Jeremiah Smith and Cam Coleman, a deep running back group, and quarterbacks Dante Moore and Drew Mastermaker as his preferred names over Arch Manning), Angelo's advice for managers without extra future picks: the 2026 class has a clean top seven (Jeremiah Love, [[Jordyn Tyson]], [[Carnell Tate]], [[Makai Lemon]], Fernando Mendoza, [[KC Concepcion]], [[Jadarian Price]]) followed by a wide-open, largely consensus-free middle. His preferred lever is trading a 2026 second-round pick plus a 2027 second for one premium 2026 pick, or packaging a 2026 third for an extra 2027 second, since the 2027 second can both jump a talent tier this year (e.g. the gap he sees between [[Jadarian Price]] and [[Carnell Tate]]/[[Jordyn Tyson]]/[[Makai Lemon]]) and buy an extra late-second/early-third dart throw like [[De'Zhaun Stribling]] or DeMond Claiborne. He specifically avoids picks 8-10 -- [[Eli Stowers]], a Jets rookie tight end, and [[Omar Cooper Jr.]] -- calling that range the most likely to bust.
