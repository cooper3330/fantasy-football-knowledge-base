## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-12-19 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys co-host on RSP Film and Theory, NOT a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-19 (NFL QB Lunacy)]]): the belief that a team can only commit resources to one quarterback at a time is, in his words, 'mind-bogglingly wrong,' and it is the one take that reliably makes his mentions explode. His arithmetic: if quarterbacks hit at roughly 50%, taking one gives a 50% chance of a franchise guy and taking two gives 75%, with a 25% chance you have two and can trade one to recoup the cost. He notes teams already spend at this level, just concentrated — Carolina spent 1.01 and 1.09 on [[Bryce Young]], Denver spent top-of-market money plus multiple picks on Russell Wilson — and nobody calls that idiotic. Historical proof: Dallas in 1989 carried Troy Aikman and Steve Walsh, watched both 18 months, then traded the loser for a first, second and third and won three Super Bowls in four years. He also argues no non-QB rookie moves the needle — Vegas shifts the line about half a point for a peak Davante Adams absence. [[Matt Waldman]] fully endorses the framework and adds that of seven draft picks, injuries, busts and depth players leave maybe one worth a second contract, so quarterback is the right place to buy extra lottery tickets.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): it is good to have two quarterbacks on a good team, and he would franchise [[Sam Darnold]] even though Minnesota's public posture is that [[J.J. McCarthy]] is ready. His reframing is the substance: you do not tag the veteran as a backup plan, you tag him to buy the time to find out whether the young quarterback can actually become the starter. The supporting logic is scarcity and division strength — there are not enough quarterbacks to go around, and in an NFC North with an improved Chicago, a good Detroit and a capable Green Bay, losing one quarterback ends the season. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) counters that paying big starter money on top of a rookie contract may signal the team does not truly believe McCarthy is ready.
