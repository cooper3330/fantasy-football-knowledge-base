# Number One Receiver Slot Snap Allocation

## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-13]]): if he were building an offense he would almost always want his number one receiver at about 30% of snaps in the slot, purely to hunt mismatches. His example is [[Mike Evans]], a receiver carved from stone as an X 'through and through', who was still moved to 25-30% slot in the Brady years and under Liam Coen, to Harmon's approval. Applied to the NFC East: [[Malik Nabers]] should move around all three alignments but at roughly 70-30 rather than the 50-50 he played in his last LSU year, since Robinson occupies the slot; [[CeeDee Lamb]], by contrast, is a true 50-50 player and a genuine weapon inside, which is part of why Dallas hired Brian Schottenheimer to preserve the existing offensive ecosystem — even though Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) have said for years Lamb could just play X without hurting his production.
- 2025-02-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-21]]): the episode's organising principle is that a team's receiver need is a pre-snap role, not a position — 'we're alignment bros over here,' asking whether a team needs a slot, an X, a vertical option or a dig-route runner. Harmon's evidence is that the best 2024 offenses spread slot work across everyone rather than assigning it: Atlanta had nobody above 50% slot rate but everyone above 30% ([[Drake London]] 37.2%, [[Darnell Mooney]] 42.1%, Ray-Ray McCloud 46.5%), and Tampa Bay kept both [[Mike Evans]] and [[Jalen McMillan]] under 70% of snaps out wide. He argues the motion-heavy McVay-tree designs run by Zac Robinson and Liam Coen are dynamic precisely because alignment is fluid, and cites [[DeVonta Smith]] going from a 24.3% to a 47.5% slot rate under Kellen Moore with career-best YAC as the same effect elsewhere.
- 2025-02-26 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-26]]): Harmon put a hard charting boundary on what a pure X receiver actually is — among players charted in 2023, only four were at or above 80% of snaps both outside and on the line of scrimmage: [[Nico Collins]], [[Rashod Bateman]], [[Davante Adams]] and [[Brandon Aiyuk]]. He used that list to argue that [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], outside on 79.2% of snaps per Pro Football Focus and frequently on the line, was being deployed as a near-exclusive X, and that doing so is a misallocation even for a receiver capable of playing there — the [[CeeDee Lamb]] template being that a star is maximized when freed to the slot, flanker and in-breaking routes rather than pinned wide. He applied the same logic in reverse to [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]], whose 77% slot rate he expects to fall toward 50/50, and to [[Ricky Pearsall]], whose slot label he thinks undersells flanker ability.
- 2025-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-12]]): Harmon argues the Rams' free agent target had to be a multi-positional receiver rather than a pure X, because you want [[Puka Nacua]] taking reps both outside and in the slot, in motion, and in stacks and bunches to get him going after the catch — which is only possible if the other receiver can hold the X. [[Davante Adams]] fits: 42.4% of his sampled snaps came in the slot and 52% on the line of scrimmage, so he is a primary X who still moves inside, and Harmon guesses Nacua ends up in the slot more often than Adams. Nacua's own 2024 Next Gen Stats split was 51% outside, 46% inside, remainder backfield/motion. Koh (not tracked) makes the symmetrical point that the argument works with the names reversed — you also want Adams in the slot — and Harmon concedes it, adding that what Sean McVay does with alignment is put big-bodied receivers on slot corners.
- 2025-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-19]]): Harmon uses off-the-line snap rate as a role-constriction alarm. Only eight player seasons in the Reception Perception database from 2014 on sit at 85%+ of snaps off the line of scrimmage — Jordan Matthews, Kiki Coutee, [[Wan'Dale Robinson]], Jarvis Landry, Golden Tate (twice), Jamison Crowder and now [[Cooper Kupp]] at 86.9% — and he characterizes that list as slot-only, hyper-specific role players. On the other end, he argues the rare and valuable thing is two receivers who can both run as '80-80' players (80% outside, 80% on the line): [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Tee Higgins]] can each be a full-time X, which is precisely what frees Cincinnati to move Chase inside for mismatches and let Higgins post 2.57 yards per route run from the slot without either being pigeonholed.
- 2025-05-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-28]]): Harmon states the working benchmark explicitly — even dominant X receivers will play 20-25% of their snaps in the slot, so a figure in single digits is an alignment failure rather than a position label. He scrolled the full list of receivers who ran a route in 2024: [[Rashad Bateman]] sat at 7.7% on 444 routes (43rd in routes run), and the next single-digit name was [[Javon Baker]] at 177th with 40 routes. Reference points he cites: [[Terry McLaurin]] at 18% under Cliff Kingsbury, which he already considers too low; [[Zay Flowers]] at 41.7%; Amon-Ra St. Brown at 44%; [[Jameson Williams]] at 30%; [[Ja'Marr Chase]] at 32%; [[Diontae Johnson]] at 7.9% in 2021 versus a career-best-efficiency 21.3% in 2023; and [[Malik Nabers]] just under 30%, which Harmon would raise to 35% for more posts and digs.
- 2025-06-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-04]]): Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked), calling themselves the alignment bros, apply the idea to Arizona — [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] took roughly 80 percent of his snaps outside, much of it on the line of scrimmage, and only 20.8 percent in the slot while [[Michael Wilson]] ran 32 percent slot, a split Harmon wants flipped. He points out the irony that Wilson's own Reception Perception profile ended by arguing that Wilson's positional flexibility should let Arizona move Harrison around rather than tethering him to the X spot. The same logic drives their read of Las Vegas, where Chip Kelly moving [[Jakobi Meyers]], [[Jack Bech]] and [[Brock Bowers]] between inside and outside alignments is treated as a real signal of how targets will be distributed.
- 2025-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-13]]): citing Rich Rebar of Sharp Football (not a tracked expert), [[Jayden Reed]] ran a total of seven routes from one- and two-receiver sets over the past two seasons — 2.6% of Green Bay's dropbacks in those sets — and [[Josh Downs]] just 16 over the same span. Harmon treats this as the single biggest cap on both players' fantasy ceilings: the charting says each is a full-field separator who wins at all three levels, but neither is trusted on the field in condensed personnel, so they cede high-leverage downs to bigger, worse-separating teammates. He notes Green Bay's rigid weight thresholds for receivers — Reed an outlier who was listed at 187 then quickly reported at 195 because the Packers essentially never draft under 195 — as a possible top-down organizational reason rather than a coaching preference.
- 2025-07-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-11]]): Harmon states the modern rule plainly — you do not take your best receiver and leave him at X, you move him around to weaponize him, and he points to [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[CeeDee Lamb]], [[Justin Jefferson]] and [[Ja'Marr Chase]] (career-high slot snaps in 2024) as evidence. [[Drake London]] is the case study: after 85% and 82.4% on-line snaps under Arthur Smith, Zac Robinson moved him to 52.7% off the line and 38.9% in the slot, and he finished fifth in the NFL in slot yards (563) and first among 100-plus-route receivers in slot target rate at 34%. Harmon also credits Brian Daboll with [[Malik Nabers]] (second) and Doug Pederson with [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] (fifth) for refusing to tether young receivers to X.
- 2025-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]): the share of true number ones who live on the line of scrimmage has collapsed, and the charted evidence is that only four receivers in his 2024 sample cleared 80% of snaps on the line — [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] at 80.4%, plus [[Quentin Johnston]], [[Nico Collins]] and [[George Pickens]], with [[Courtland Sutton]] likely joining once charted. [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Justin Jefferson]] are conspicuously absent because elite offenses now move their best receiver everywhere. Harmon's exception is Collins, whose ability to run X essentially every snap at elite efficiency is itself the mark of rarity, and a testament to [[C.J. Stroud]]. James Koh (not a tracked expert) puts it as a settled matter — in 2025 the best receivers in the game play 25-30% in the slot, and it is not up for debate for a player you believe is generational.
- 2025-07-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-07-30]]): [[Drake London]]'s 2024 is Harmon's cleanest proof of the principle — after two years at over 80% outside-and-on-the-line under Arthur Smith, he took a career-high 52.7% of snaps off the line and 38.9% in the slot in Zac Robinson's scheme and produced a near-1,300-yard season, unlocking zone-beating ability and a 27% dig route rate. Harmon stresses the point is not that outside X snaps are wasted — sometimes it is the right matchup — but that almost none of the league's best receivers live at 80/80. He applies the same argument to [[Tetairoa McMillan]] and to [[Nico Collins]], who has been an 80/80 player his whole career, and cites [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] being deployed like [[Courtland Sutton]] as the cautionary case.
- 2025-08-01 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-01]]): playing your number one receiver at a very high line-of-scrimmage rate is generally not the role you want for him, which is why fans complain about [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]'s deployment. Harmon's charting of players at 79%-plus on-line snaps returns [[Quentin Johnston]], [[Nico Collins]], [[Jaylen Waddle]], [[George Pickens]], Harrison, [[DK Metcalf]] and [[Courtland Sutton]]; the 2023 list of 80%-plus was [[George Pickens]], [[Drake London]], [[Mike Evans]], [[Brandon Aiyuk]], [[Jameson Williams]], [[Davante Adams]], [[Rashod Bateman]] and Collins — only Collins and Adams qualifying as genuinely elite. Collins has been 89.1% and 83.4% on the line the last two years and has never taken more than 17% of sampled snaps in the slot, which Harmon calls a throwback. His stated expectation is that moving a number one off the line unlocks easier production, citing [[Justin Jefferson]] taking fewer X snaps in 2024 and 'getting more layups', and he would be stunned if Collins's alignment numbers repeat.
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-13]]): the AFC North preview was structured almost entirely around where a receiver lines up rather than raw projection. [[Ja'Marr Chase]] is the success case — slot rate rising 14.3% to 20.6% to 24.8% to a career-high 32.3%, with pre-snap motion routes going 9, 20, 50, 100 by season, and Jacob Gibbs data showing a 36% target-per-route-run rate in motion versus 23% static. Harmon recalled Chase telling him he had to earn the coaching staff's trust to play inside, because pre-snap responsibility is a real burden — the same reason Ben Johnson demands pre-snap perfection from his receivers. The counter-cases: [[Jerry Jeudy]] was miscast in the slot in Denver when he profiles as a flanker; [[Diontae Johnson]] is perceived as a slot receiver purely because Matt Canada fed him line-of-scrimmage targets; and [[Rashad Bateman]] took only 7.7% of snaps in the slot, dead last among 40-plus-reception receivers, capping his layup volume.
- 2025-09-09 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-09]]): Harmon called it coaching malpractice at this point to take your number one receiver and use him as a static boundary X, pointing listeners to the free [[Ja'Marr Chase]] 2024 Reception Perception profile he posted before Week 1 as the detailed case study in how Cincinnati altered Chase's role. He applied the same lens to [[Garrett Wilson]], whose outside rate rose every season in New York (64.4% to 72% to 75.8%) while his slot, backfield and off-line snaps fell — and whose favorite routes, deep overs, posts and digs, declined with them. Wilson told Harmon after his rookie year that he wanted more slot work precisely to run those routes. Week 1 under Tanner Engstrand reversed it: 36.4% slot rate, dig usage trending back up.
- 2025-09-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-23]]): Week 3 gave two illustrations of alignment defining a receiver's ceiling. With [[CeeDee Lamb]] out, [[George Pickens]] took 2.6% of his snaps in the slot, which Harmon says exposes the offseason claim that Dallas would use him like Lamb as an obvious lie and caps what he can do as a number one; Harmon and Koh agreed the workable target is roughly a 70/30 outside/inside split, the kind of split top receivers actually get. Separately, [[Malik Nabers]] being left on the boundary at 20.9 air yards per target drew Harmon's sharpest criticism — a double-team excuse is legitimate for a boundary-only X like [[Nico Collins]], not for a move-around player who should be condensed to the line as a slot, stacked, and given switch releases to stress communication.
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-13]]): the episode is a case study in alignment as the hidden driver of receiver production. [[A.J. Brown]]'s wide alignment climbed 67% to 70% to 75% to 83% across 2022-2025, which Koh calls criminal mismanagement and Harmon calls malpractice for a number one; the loss of Kellen Moore removed the tweaks that had Smith motioning inside and Brown occasionally off the line. In Denver, [[Pat Bryant]] taking 79% inside snaps in Week 10 pushed [[Troy Franklin]] to 76% outside, changing what routes Franklin runs; Harmon notes Franklin struggles with press and over the middle so perimeter volume is a poor fit. [[Matthew Golden]] is the rookie version — both hosts want him parked at flanker rather than rotated through slot, X and flanker before he is ready.
