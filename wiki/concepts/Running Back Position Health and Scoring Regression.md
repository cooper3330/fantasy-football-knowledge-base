## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest JJ Zachariason (Late Round Draft Guide, not a tracked expert) argued the top-24 running backs in 2024 were the healthiest in roughly 15 years, and that 90% or more of the position's scoring bump came from that health rather than talent. The gains were concentrated in low-end RB1 and RB2 range, not the top five or six. His roll call: [[J.K. Dobbins]] was the least healthy at RB24 with 13 games; [[Rico Dowdle]] 16, [[Tony Pollard]] 16, [[Najee Harris]] 17, [[Rachaad White]] 16, [[D'Andre Swift]] 17, [[David Montgomery]] and [[Joe Mixon]] 14, [[Breece Hall]] 16, [[Chuba Hubbard]] 15, [[Bucky Irving]] 17, [[Jonathan Taylor]] 14, with [[Alvin Kamara]] the only top-12 back under 16 games. Because backups did not have to step in, the usual Zero RB payoff never materialized and ADP closely matched finish. He expects a market overcorrection into inflated 2025 running back costs, and will again run a hero/anchor RB build — lock in an [[Bijan Robinson]]-type RB1, play volatility at RB2.
- 2026-08-18 — According to Dave Kluge (Football Guys, not a tracked expert) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-18]]): Kluge's #3 surprise is that draft boards 'look like it's 2006 again,' with running backs going heavy in rounds 1-2 (he's seen 6-8 drafters in a room walk out with two RBs in the first two rounds). He attributes this to last year being a 'wonk year' -- naming Harold Fannin Jr. as its symbol -- where running backs stayed unusually healthy while several high-value receivers got hurt, and argues the market has thrown out '20 years of data' showing running backs get injured more than any other position; he'd rather take early receivers like [[Drake London]], [[AJ Brown]], [[Nico Collins]], [[Rashee Rice]] and [[Malik Nabers]] than round-2 running backs this year. Harris pushes back hard with a scarcity argument he's held for years: injuries can happen to anybody, but the talent gap between a WR7 and a WR40 is far smaller than the equivalent gap at running back, because a pass-heavy, 3-WR-base league makes receiver touches more replaceable than true bell-cow carries, which he says 'run out fast.' Kluge separately names a cluster of question-mark backs he'd fade relative to receivers this range -- [[De'Von Achane]], [[Saquon Barkley]], [[Omarion Hampton]] and [[Kenneth Walker III]] -- versus the handful of backs he does trust early: [[Jahmyr Gibbs]], [[Bijan Robinson]], [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[James Cook]], [[Jonathan Taylor]] and [[Chase Brown]].
