## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-10-17 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and co-host Josh (not tracked) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-17]]): Six weeks into the season, the hosts ran their annual segment ranking offenses by trade appeal rather than pre-season projection. Josh's tiers: 'driverless wagons' -- offenses productive no matter who's plugged in (Cowboys, Buccaneers, Chiefs, Lions); 'does this new-model wagon really work' -- surprising performers still lacking a proven third skill piece or trusted quarterback (Colts, Falcons); 'wagon premium, if only it came family size' -- strong offenses with value concentrated on too few reliable fantasy pieces (Bills, Packers, Rams, with asterisked Bears and Chargers for being crowded or banged up); 'came with a sports package' -- bad-team offenses unexpectedly supporting useful fantasy pieces (Saints, Panthers); 'does this wagon come with a warranty' -- offenses that were supposed to be great but broke down (Cardinals, Raiders, Eagles); and 'please discontinue this wagon model' -- offenses to sell out of entirely (Titans, with Cleveland added by Harris). The stated purpose is translating offensive-environment quality directly into buy/hold/sell trade decisions on individual players rather than treating team context as background noise.
- 2025-12-01 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-12-01]]): Harris used Philadelphia's four-game offensive collapse -- following a Bears blowout loss where the Eagles 'looked helpless as a kitten' and 'badly coached' -- to make a broader point about how volatile preseason offensive-environment projections are. He noted the same Eagles offense had scored 33 on the Rams and topped 300 yards against Denver earlier this season, and pointed to other August misses: the Chargers passing on every down, Drake May's Patriots offense turning out good, Seattle being explosive, and the Raiders being a 'dumpster fire.' His conclusion: offensive environments change over a season, and fantasy managers get surprised by a dozen teams' actual tier relative to their preseason expectation every year.
- 2026-08-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-06]]): projected offensive-environment risers include Tampa Bay ([[Chris Godwin]] a year healthier in the slot next to [[Emeka Egbuka]] outside, [[Baker Mayfield]] healthier, a better line, [[Kenneth Gainwell]] added -- Waldman expects more mistake-free football even with [[Mike Evans]] gone), the Chargers under the new Mike McDaniel-influenced staff (short-area game built around [[Ladd McConkey]] and [[Quentin Johnston]], multiple-tight-end sets helping the run game for [[Keaton Mitchell]] and [[Omarion Hampton]], and a scheme suited to [[Justin Herbert]]'s strength off-script versus his weaker full-field drop-back reads, which Waldman likens to 'Carson Wentz-itis'), and New Orleans (Angelo: [[Tyler Shough]] progressing, strong infrastructure and line, two capable backs even with [[Alvin Kamara]] aging, and playmakers in [[Jordan Tyson]], [[Chris Olave]] and [[Juwan Johnson]] -- Angelo praises OC Kellen Moore). Projected decliners: Miami (built around a limited, manufactured [[Malik Willis]] passing game that Waldman expects defenses to solve after a handful of games, cutting into [[De'Von Achane]]'s receiving work) and Indianapolis (Angelo: [[Daniel Jones]] returning from Achilles loses scrambling equity, dropping the offense from a tier-two environment last year toward tier three or four, with [[Alec Pierce]]'s post-surgery health also uncertain).
- 2026-10-23 — According to [[Chris Harris]] with guest Cousin Josh (DetectiveFish on Instagram, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-10-23]]): the two ran their recurring six-weeks-in exercise sorting every NFL offense into five trust tiers instead of relying on preseason predictions -- true wagons (3+ weekly must-starts), partly-trustable wagons capped at two reliable names, good surprises, surprisingly bad (not yet disasters), and 'wheels fell off.' Harris's true wagons were just Detroit (anchored by an elite RB, Amon-Ra St. Brown, and a tight end angling for a new contract) and San Francisco (resilient around Purdy/Kittle/McCaffrey regardless of who else is hurt); Josh initially listed six, also including Cincinnati, Jacksonville, the Rams, and Buffalo, before the two negotiated down to agreeing Jacksonville (Trevor Lawrence, Parker Washington, Brenton Strange) belongs as a third. In the 'partly trustable, caps at two' tier they debated the Chiefs, Ravens, Saints, Falcons and Texans. Good surprises: Carolina (centered on Tetairoa McMillan, Jalen Coker, Chuba Hubbard) and Green Bay (Jordan Love, Matthew Golden, pending Josh Jacobs's return) -- both called among the biggest surprises of the year -- plus a softer case for Pittsburgh (Tyler Warren, Aaron Rodgers, DK Metcalf) as more conservative-but-playable than a true wagon. Surprisingly bad but not disastrous: the Cowboys, Seahawks, Washington, and Buccaneers, each previously hyped as above-average offenses that have underdelivered. 'Wheels fell off': the Chargers, Broncos, and Eagles, which Harris and Josh both drafted into heavily (Josh had real money on Justin Herbert as MVP and an over on Jaylen Waddle's yardage) and now agree are disasters, with the Broncos singled out as having the least excuse since the offense looks brutal regardless of play-caller or health. Josh estimated preseason situational reads land around 55-60% accurate this year rather than his usual ~70%.
