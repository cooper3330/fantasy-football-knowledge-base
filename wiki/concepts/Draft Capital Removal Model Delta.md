<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-04-21 — According to guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21 (Film & Data with Dwain McFarland)]]): he runs his supermodel twice, with and without draft capital, and treats the gap between the two ranks as an overvaluation flag. Three or four spots is noise; roughly 40 spots is where the flag has historically been real — his examples are [[Jahan Dotson]], Jalen Reagor and [[Kadarius Toney]], all busts. The middle band around -27 is survivable but not stardom: [[Zay Flowers]], [[D.J. Moore]] and [[Rashod Bateman]]. Applied in this episode: [[Tyler Warren]] -24, [[Jack Bech]] -36, [[Tetairoa McMillan]] -16 (not a worry), [[Isaiah Bond]] plus seven without draft capital, and [[Kyle Williams]] once flagged as 42 spots underrated before his capital rose. McFarland also noted the non-draft-capital model performs best at tight end, where the other inputs correlate 0.70 with three-year success on their own versus 0.76 with capital added — less so for running backs, and less again for receivers. [[Matt Waldman]] treated the flag as a useful cross-check on his own film grades rather than a verdict.
