# Bench Spots as Information Options

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-09-05 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys co-host, not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Film and Theory)]] [Dynasty]: the end of your bench should be filled with players who will tell you within a week or two whether you have something, never a player who might break out in week nine — because that is not one roster spot, it is seventeen weekly spots, and holding a guy eight weeks burns eight of them when you could have cycled four two-week looks and bought four times the information. A player on the street is there because your league's consensus says he has no value, so the only way he matters is if the consensus is wrong; adding him is buying a right of first refusal on him next week rather than a commitment. Harstad also frames the waiver wire as your practice squad — a 20-man roster is really 30 once you count the ten you are tracking — and notes NFL practice-squad poaching is rare, as is a league adding a cut player within a week (he estimated around 2% of the time), because if you were the highest manager on him nobody else is bidding higher. He cited cycling Richie James on and off one roster roughly ten times over the years. [[Matt Waldman]] added the same discipline in the other direction: be emotionally invested in winning and in valuing talent over need, not in being right about a specific player.
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-19]]) [Dynasty]: the hosts revisited the principle that it is often better to take a real swing and leave a visible hole on your roster than to fill the hole with a mediocre player who does not actually solve anything. Waldman framed [[Skylar Thompson]] as exactly that swing — a high-ceiling, low-floor dart he would rather hold at the end of a dynasty bench or in redraft than a Rams receiver like [[Jordan Whittington]] or [[DeMarcus Robinson]], or a Chiefs back other than [[Samaje Perine]]. Co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) described treating the waiver wire as an extension of his roster, cycling Thompson on and off over the years — protecting him when new information might be coming, sending him back to the street when it was not — and summarized the trade-off as 'I'd rather have the guy who I think sucks over the guy I know sucks,' since QB26 production is easy to find later while QB12 production is not. He added the underrated benefit that a failed swing frees the bench spot quickly.
- 2025-08-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he articulated the value of pre-loading a name rather than a roster spot — the point of hearing [[Roman Wilson]] named in August is that if he goes six-for-80 with no touchdown in Week 1, most managers say they need to see it multiple times, while a listener who already has the name thinks I have a roster spot, I will take the cheese on one game. He also separated must-handcuff pairs, where he will find a way to draft the backup ([[Bijan Robinson]] with [[Tyler Allgeier]], [[Christian McCaffrey]] with [[Isaac Guerendo]]), from speculative depth-chart-hole stashes that do not rise to that level. On [[Najee Harris]] he added that a stash is not mandatory even for the [[Omarion Hampton]] drafter, but is cheap enough that owning both can produce a league winner.
- 2025-08-22 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-22]]) [Dynasty]: Harris restates the purpose of his annual Super Deep Sleeper list, and it is explicitly not a draft list — these are names outside the top 170 that he says you should not draft in a normal-sized league, that lack clear and obvious roles and are not even certain to make a roster. The value is twofold: they are worth thinking about in dynasty, and having said a nice thing about them on the show means you are quicker to pounce with FAAB or a waiver claim if and when the situation clears. He is direct that the list is not supposed to hit on most names — one or two discoveries justify it. His worked example is [[Jordan Mason]] from the 2024 list, 'a pretty pure and clean hope shot' nobody was drafting in August, balanced against [[Malachi Corley]] and [[Luke McCaffrey]] from the same list as reminders that these are not all supposed to hit.
