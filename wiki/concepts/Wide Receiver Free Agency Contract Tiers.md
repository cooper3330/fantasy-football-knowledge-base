## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-12-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-05]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon sketched a working tier structure for receiver contracts. The 30-million-plus tier he currently limits to [[Tyreek Hill]], [[Brandon Aiyuk]], [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[A.J. Brown]], [[CeeDee Lamb]] and [[Justin Jefferson]], with Jefferson and Lamb around 35 million; he does not place [[Tee Higgins]] in that group even though he expects the market to pay him there. Below that, [[Calvin Ridley]]'s 23 million — widely mocked when signed — now looks like nothing given cap inflation, and that is roughly the number he would pay a very good non-elite receiver. The tier he actually wants to shop in is the post-rookie-contract bargain at 11 to 13 million: [[Jakobi Meyers]], [[Darnell Mooney]], and prospectively [[Elijah Moore]]. His stated rule of thumb is to avoid third contracts for receivers who are aging or coming off injury and uneven seasons — [[Amari Cooper]], [[Chris Godwin]], [[Stefon Diggs]], [[DeAndre Hopkins]] — and he called 2025 a weak receiver free agent market overall.
- 2024-12-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-16]]): taking the money now can cost a receiver the quality label attached to his work long-term. A WR2 whose production is aided by an elite teammate and quarterback — his example is [[Tee Higgins]] with [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Joe Burrow]] — will be asked to do more elsewhere than he can deliver, so after the one big payday his stock and his next contract fall. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) offered the historical parallel of Alvin Harper leaving Dallas for Tampa Bay and flopping, noting greatness is talent plus usage plus supporting cast, none of which travels, while conceding [[Derrick Henry]]'s 2024 shows it can work out. Harris also floated the cheap-owner scenario of trading a record-contract receiver instead of paying him; Waldman rejected it outright.
- 2025-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-16]]): the 2025 receiver free-agent class is what he called a past-time list — [[DeAndre Hopkins]], [[Amari Cooper]], [[Davante Adams]] (expected release), [[Marquise Brown]], [[Keenan Allen]], [[Elijah Moore]], [[Chris Godwin]], [[Brandon Cooks]], [[Mike Williams]], [[Stefon Diggs]], plus [[Tee Higgins]] if he somehow escapes the tag — mostly players who were great three years ago and are coming off injuries. His structural point: number one wideouts never reach free agency, so a team wanting to transform its receiver room has to find a trade candidate, and with [[DK Metcalf]] looking likely to stay in Seattle and the incoming draft class lacking a top-line prospect (no [[Malik Nabers]] lying around, in Harmon's phrasing), the market is thin. He added that teams are thirsty for outside receivers, which is why he thought [[Diontae Johnson]] could have commanded roughly three years and 40 million.
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-13]]): the 2025 receiver market is unusually weak at the top, which lifts the few players coming off good seasons. Harmon's ladder runs [[Ja'Marr Chase]] / [[Justin Jefferson]] / [[CeeDee Lamb]] at the top, [[A.J. Brown]] at $32 million and [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] at $30 million next, with [[Terry McLaurin]] worth roughly $30-31 million — older, but coming off a career year with fewer NFL miles. Everyone else available ([[DeAndre Hopkins]], [[Amari Cooper]], [[Davante Adams]], [[Keenan Allen]], [[Chris Godwin]] and [[Cooper Kupp]] off injuries, [[Stefon Diggs]]) is not coming off a peak season, and Harmon flip-flops on whether [[Tee Higgins]] hits the market at all or is re-signed or tagged in Cincinnati. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) counters that at 29 it is hard to justify top dollar for McLaurin and floats a shorter three-year deal instead.
- 2025-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-12]]): Harmon identifies a roughly $10M per year tier he describes as the 'we can play you and it is not an embarrassment to the franchise' threshold for wide receivers — [[Tutu Atwell]] effectively set the market, with [[Dyami Brown]] at about $10M, [[Josh Palmer]] on a multi-year deal in that range, [[Marquise Brown]] and [[Darius Slayton]] around the same money, and [[Gabe Davis]] as the odd outlier who got more the year before. Above that, both hosts note the funny-money problem in headline APY figures: [[DK Metcalf]]'s five-year, $150M Steelers deal has no reported guarantee structure that convinces Harmon he plays it out, drawing an explicit comparison to [[Davante Adams]]' Raiders contract, which carried a massive APY he was never going to see and which the Jets cut rather than pay. Harmon also relays Patrick Claybon's reframing that fans should care less about overpays because it is not their money. Koh's counter is that the years matter even if the dollars do not — locking in three to four years at $45M for [[Geno Smith]] constrains a rebuild in a way a one-year deal would not.
- 2025-03-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-13]]): the March 2025 market produced a compressed and distorted middle tier. [[Darius Slayton]] and [[Josh Palmer]] both signed identical three-year, $36M deals, [[Gabe Davis]] is already on $13M a year after a terrible season, and Jakobi Meyers at $11M is, per Harmon, better than all of them — which he expects will eventually make Meyers unhappy. Koh uses the same numbers to make a positional-market point: [[Saquon Barkley]] signed three years, $37.8M last offseason and [[Derrick Henry]] is on 'Darius Slayton money', while [[David Montgomery]] and [[James Conner]] make $9.5M and $9.1M as the heart of their run games — less than a third receiver on the Giants. Against that backdrop Harmon reads [[Chris Godwin]]'s three years, $55M as a real hometown discount, with reporting that he left $20M on the table.
- 2025-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-03-19]]): the post-TV-money cap environment has broken the old receiver pay ladder — [[Ja'Marr Chase]] at $40.25M AAV clears [[Justin Jefferson]] and [[CeeDee Lamb]] at ~$35M and [[A.J. Brown]] at $32M, and with New England at $89M, Arizona at $63M and the Chargers at $54M in space, Harmon believes any second-tier free agent gets bid over the top. That is why he reads [[Tee Higgins]]'s $29M AAV as a real discount. He also notes the leverage mechanic: Higgins fired his agent and moved to Chase's, letting the pair refuse to sign the Chase deal until the Higgins deal came too, with quarterback and coaching support turning it into everybody-versus-ownership. The counter-theme both hosts endorse is that chasing the last dollar often lands receivers in 'garbo situations' — [[Chris Godwin]] returning to Tampa Bay and Higgins staying are cited as players choosing the better productive environment.
- 2025-04-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-08]]): $10 million a year is now simply the going rate for a playable WR3/WR4 — [[Tutu Atwell]], [[Dyami Brown]] and [[Josh Palmer]] all at $10M, [[Curtis Samuel]] around $8M last offseason. Set against rookie contracts, that inverts the usual complaint about 'number two' prospects: [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]] costs $8.8M as the fourth pick, [[Rome Odunze]] $5.6M as the ninth, and [[Zay Flowers]], [[Jordan Addison]] and [[Xavier Worthy]] all sit under roughly $3.5-4M — Greg Dortch and Noah Brown money for genuinely impactful first-round players. Harmon's conclusion: teams should keep drafting good receivers even without a true number one profile, and he expects many to do so in round one; it is also why receivers increasingly balk at their rookie deals entering years four and five. Koh (not tracked) adds the Rams pay this way at receiver while refusing to pay running backs at all, preferring system-fit replacements.
