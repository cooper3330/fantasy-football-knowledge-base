# Late-Round Quarterback and Positional Flattening

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-08-13 — According to Dave Kluge of Footballguys (guest, not a tracked expert) on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-13]]: 2025 saw a big flattening of quarterback, so even though [[Josh Allen]], [[Lamar Jackson]], [[Jayden Daniels]] and [[Jalen Hurts]] are the difference makers, the opportunity cost of a third-round quarterback is too high — he would rather wait to rounds 12-14, accept a few fewer points, and build out the running back and receiver rooms, then draft two late quarterbacks to double his chances of hitting. [[Chris Harris]] frames the same idea as his own surprise: QB7 going in the sixth round is 'disgusting', because there is no measurable difference between QB7 and QB15 or even QB20. Harris disagrees on method — he will not roster two quarterbacks in a one-quarterback league, preferring to keep firing at other positions — and names [[Joe Burrow]] in the fourth as the one quarterback price he thinks is right.
- 2025-08-15 — According to Cousin Josh (untracked co-host of the [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast]]) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-15]]): after the super-tier of [[Josh Allen]], [[Lamar Jackson]], [[Jayden Daniels]], [[Joe Burrow]] and [[Jalen Hurts]], he is simply not getting involved at quarterback — the same way he will not take tight end four or five. He would rather wait 50 to 60 picks and take [[Jared Goff]], [[Brock Purdy]], [[Dak Prescott]] or [[Justin Herbert]]. [[Chris Harris]] made the argument concrete with [[Baker Mayfield]] at ADP 69 versus Herbert at 109: if the two prices were swapped, the analysis would flip, which shows the objection is to the draft slot rather than the player. He cited Dave Kluge's ADP work that drafters take QB7 and beyond early simply because the rest of their starting lineup is filled and they feel they need a quarterback — an impulse he calls silly and asks listeners not to draft that way.
- 2025-08-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-25]]): there is no difference between QB7 and QB20 this year, so his message is to take [[Josh Allen]] or [[Lamar Jackson]] if that is your thing — he himself will take [[Joe Burrow]] in the fifth — but if you miss the top six, do not spend a sixth-round pick on [[Baker Mayfield]] or a seventh on [[Bo Nix]]; wait and wait and wait. Guest JJ Zachariason (not tracked) adds two market findings from his ADP work: later-round quarterbacks who had bad touchdown rates the previous season are better buys than those with good ones, because touchdown rate regresses while ADP chases it; and second-year quarterbacks drafted QB7 through QB18 outperform expectation at a high rate because ambiguity discounts them. He also argues game theory against reaching — if you can take [[Drake Maye]] in round eight, others went earlier, and the value is in waiting even longer for the [[Jordan Love]] and [[C.J. Stroud]] types. Worst case, he and Denny have tracked ten years of streaming and can still assemble a Frankenstein low-end QB1 off waivers, though mobile quarterbacks have made that harder than in 2015.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): Harris laid out his 2025 quarterback structure as a hard six-deep tier and then nothing — 'there's the top six and there's just no way in the world you should ever take seven, eight, nine, ten; there's no reason, just wait forever, take QB18, it doesn't matter, they're all the same.' He is usually waiting, will take [[Joe Burrow]] in the fifth when available, and took [[Patrick Mahomes]] around the sixth in a recent mock. He would take [[Lamar Jackson]] or [[Josh Allen]] in the third where they go in sharp drafts, but not in the second round where they go in home leagues. Raybon (Action Network, not tracked) described the same tier structure differently — the top four, then Mahomes and Burrow who are great but not scrambling quarterbacks, then it gets dicey before the rushing quarterbacks return.
- 2025-08-28 — Asked whether quarterback strategy changes in 14-plus-team leagues, according to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he tries not to get cute at quarterback in those formats, because you cannot wait until the fourteenth round and somebody else will take three quarterbacks, leaving nothing on the waiver wire for byes or injuries — so you must also draft a backup. He stresses it is purely about scarcity, not a philosophical hatred of quarterbacks, and cites his 16-team league (the League of American Recreational Gridiron Enthusiasts) as the case in point. DENNY CARTER of Rotoworld (guest, NOT a tracked expert) agreed, describing a 14-team superflex draft where he did not mess around at all: [[Lamar Jackson]] with the second pick, [[Justin Fields]] in the third and [[Cam Ward]] later, despite his instinct to mix it up. Carter adds that stashing a late riser like [[Jackson Dart]] is how you cover the position in those formats.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-29]]): guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) says he has been very happy this draft season being the last person in his league to take a quarterback, preferring to roster a handful of [[Caleb Williams]], [[Justin Fields]] and [[Jordan Love]] and see what shakes out, which is why he scored [[Lamar Jackson]], [[Josh Allen]] and [[Jayden Daniels]] as 6-7 risks against [[Josh Jacobs]] and other second-round talent on the board. Harris scored the same quarterbacks as 3s and 4s, with no question about them as players and only about where he would spend the pick. Harris notes one exception in his own drafting: [[Joe Burrow]] is the only quarterback he tends to take high, and he took him in the fifth round of a best ball draft [Best Ball].
- 2025-10-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-03]]): Harris's single highest-conviction in-season take is to trade away quarterbacks — anyone except [[Josh Allen]] and [[Lamar Jackson]] (the latter only because an injured player won't fetch full value). His number: the gap between QB3 and QB13 is about four fantasy points per game, which matters but is recoverable if the upgrade elsewhere makes it up. Passing is down and quarterbacks are 'all kind of the same,' so the play is to find a manager who still thinks a quarterback is a huge advantage and weasel scarce-position players out of them. He points to FantasyPros having [[Jalen Hurts]] 23rd and [[Jayden Daniels]] 40th overall as the pricing error to exploit, and to his own rosters — [[Joe Burrow]] and [[Brock Purdy]] both KO'd — as proof you can survive on [[Sam Darnold]] for 19 points. Guest Josh Van Blank (NOT tracked) agrees, noting there are 18 startable quarterbacks and teams sitting on spares you can piece together weekly.
- 2026-05-07 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-05-07]]): in single-QB leagues, Harris argues the position is now so flat and replaceable that reaching for one is almost never correct -- he cites [[Jordan Love]] potentially going undrafted despite a plausible 4,000-yard, 30-touchdown season sitting out there, and notes [[Daniel Jones]]'s 2025 waiver-wire run as proof a streamed QB can carry a roster for six weeks. Both hosts (Harris and untracked co-host Josh) say they got by last season entirely on cheap, late/waiver quarterbacks ([[Brock Purdy]]/[[Sam Darnold]] for Harris) and reiterate they will not draft a quarterback before the middle rounds in single-QB formats even for a clear QB1 like [[Josh Allen]]. This is explicitly contrasted with Superflex, where quarterback scarcity forces early investment instead.
- 2026-08-18 — According to Dave Kluge (Football Guys, not a tracked expert) and [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-18]]): Kluge's #5 ADP surprise is that elite quarterbacks ([[Josh Allen]], [[Lamar Jackson]], [[Drake Maye]]) are being pushed down draft boards further than expected, while clearly startable QBs -- [[Bo Nix]] and [[Jared Goff]] outside the top 15, [[Kyler Murray]] at QB17, [[Tyler Shough]] and [[Malik Willis]] outside the top 20, [[Daniel Jones]] at QB24 -- go very late. He points to last year's [[Matthew Stafford]] (drafted outside the top 20, finished top-3) as the template repeating itself, and frames this not as a take on one player but on the position's overall depth: don't pay up for a quarterback. Harris fully agrees but pushes back that this isn't unique to 2026 -- 'quarterback is very flat and very inflated' most years -- and wonders if the market is finally starting to internalize the wait-on-QB approach he's preached for years.
