## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-09-05 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys co-host, not a tracked expert, who has projected returns for Footballguys since 2013) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-05 (Film and Theory)]]: he traced the whole arc — kickoffs from the 30 in the mid-2000s with return rates of 88-94% and David Akers leading the league at about a 20% touchback rate; the 2011 CBA moving kickoffs to the 35 and touchbacks to the 25, dropping returns to about 60%; kickers then intentionally placing the ball at the goal line to bait suboptimal returns, taking rates to the low 40s by 2022; and the 2023 fair-catch rule collapsing it to about 22%, the lowest in history. The 2024 XFL-derived alignment has both teams lined up close with nobody moving until the ball is fielded, so injury rates should resemble any other scrimmage play. The competition committee could not pass touchbacks at the 35 (old-guard resistance) and settled at the 30 for a one-year trial, passing roughly 31-32; Harstad expects the league to push for the 35 next year rather than revert. League statisticians and polled special teams coordinators both model a 50-60% return rate, versus about 90% with touchbacks at the 35. He rejected Mike Florio's prediction of a very low rate, arguing the play is no riskier for the kicking team than the old kickoff and outcomes land at nearly the same field position — it only looks different. Fantasy implication: return scoring becomes live again, making players like Keisean Nixon and KaVontae Turpin startable in some formats, the way Devin Hester was his WR3 and [[Tyler Lockett]] supplemented into WR1 seasons early in his career. [[Matt Waldman]] backed the safety case with a first-hand story from ex-NFL edge Ryan Riddle about a coverage collision that hospitalized two players, noting a depth special-teamer's six-week injury can end a career where a star's cannot.
