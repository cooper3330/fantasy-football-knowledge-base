# Goodhart's Law and Metrics as Targets

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-10-09 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys; NOT a tracked expert) with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-09]]): Goodhart's Law says 'when a metric becomes a target, it ceases to be a good metric.' Harstad's examples: page views and time-on-page are decent proxies for writing quality until you optimize for them; NBA shooting percentage is a decent quality proxy until players stop taking free end-of-quarter half-court heaves to protect it. Applied to evaluation, drops are a real negative — a cloned [[Ja'Marr Chase]] with twice the drops is worse — but making them a target cost people [[Mike Evans]], [[Amari Cooper]], Brandon Marshall and Terrell Owens, and drops are unstable sample to sample anyway. Same for size/speed thresholds (Al Davis and Darrius Heyward-Bey) and SPARQ, which stopped being an edge the moment teams adopted it. Harstad's slogan: 'we value what we measure, so make sure you measure what you value.' Waldman's version: use a threshold late in the process as a tiebreaker with compensatory factors, never as an up-front filter.
