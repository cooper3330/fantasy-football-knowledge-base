## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): he describes the 'GM special' — a player like Kyle Williams whom area scouts grade as a fourth- or fifth-round pick, until a decision-maker shows the room highlight clips and asks why the group is not more focused on him. Asked to quantify it, Waldman estimates (explicitly guessing, from stories he hears) that GMs overriding scouting work off hit clips happens 20-30% of the time, and that owners hearing something and overruling a completed scouting and GM process happens 30-35% of the time — which combined, he says, is why scouts he knows are so frustrated. He adds the structural cause: owners understand the NFL is an entertainment industry, which works at cross purposes with due diligence.
- 2026-08-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Football Podcast - 2026-08-17]]): Waldman describes this rookie class's big, tall, fast receivers ([[Jacoby Lane]], [[Ted Hurst]], [[Denzel Boston]], [[Caleb Douglas]]) as 'catnip for minicamp' -- acrobatic one-handed catches and fade-route wins that get 'oohing and aahing' through camp before the same players get benched by late August because they haven't learned to read coverage, release cleanly, or run good routes against man. Waldman contrasts this fetishization of highlight-reel plays with the boring-but-valuable skills -- discerning zone, running flat breaks, protecting the quarterback from picks -- that actually determine playing time.
