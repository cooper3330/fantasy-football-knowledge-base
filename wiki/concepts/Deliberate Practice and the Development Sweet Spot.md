## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-11-07 — According to co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-07]]): Harstad, drawing again on David Epstein, said deliberate practice is only efficient inside a narrow band — you want to be succeeding roughly 80-90% of the time and failing 10-20%. Above that band the task is too easy and you gain nothing; below it, it is beyond your current skill and you cannot extract the lesson. He used piano Hannon exercises as the model: run the easy ones at a speed that keeps you at 80-90%, crank the tempo, move on, and by the end of all 60 you technically have the skills to play anything. Waldman described the same shape in his own bass and saxophone routines — 20 minutes of identical daily work played very slowly with one new conscious focus each cycle (finger placement, then speed and intonation, then note names across the fretboard), plus two or three max-speed attempts a week, and learning new phrases in fragments until they are second nature. Both stressed that the binding constraint is patience, not talent; Waldman used his dog's reaction as a comic barometer for how long a learner tolerates sounding bad. The football application: whether to start or bench a young QB is really a question of where his success rate sits inside that band.
