# Run-Pass Mix as a Lazy Fade Argument

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): when the argument against a player is run-pass mix, depth chart or coaching, it can be real, but Harris says it is usually just a lazy argument made by people who do not want to like the player. His Week 1 evidence is the Chargers: the constant offseason chatter about Greg Roman and Jim Harbaugh being run-focused felt real silly once [[Justin Herbert]] threw, and Harris generalizes it as there being no such thing in the current NFL as a run-pass mix that cannot sustain a fantasy passing game, unless the components of that passing game stink. He is careful to caveat that it is one week against a Chiefs front that is stout against the run, and that the real test comes in Week 2.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-02]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harris and guest Jeff Bell of Football Guys (not tracked) argued the blanket 'it's a passing league' claim no longer holds league-wide. Bell noted passing-yard props that used to sit at 265 now sit at 185 and are missed — [[Bryce Young]] throwing 126 yards in a trailing script, and Bill Simmons and Cousin Sal guessing 220 for [[Spencer Rattler]] against Buffalo when the real number was about 150. Harris's explanation: defenses have adjusted to three- and four-wide as a base formation, sitting back and daring offenses to sustain error-free drives, and a lot of quarterbacks are not capable of that. Bell added the kickoff rule and long-range kicking — a coach who needs only 20 yards from the 35 to try a 62-yarder — as a further drag on passing volume. Harris's qualifier: it is still a passing league for some quarterbacks, just not all.
