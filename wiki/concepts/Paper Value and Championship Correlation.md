## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-13 (Paper Champions and All-Timers Across Eras)]]) [Dynasty]: untracked co-host Adam Harstad argues the industry mocks 'winning the offseason' too glibly. If we had platonic-ideal player values, the roster with the most paper value would by definition win the most championships, because value would perfectly measure contribution to winning. Real-world values — Dan Hendry's, FantasyCalc, KeepTradeCut — are flawed proxies, but as long as they correlate with the ideal they carry the same tendencies at weaker strength. He illustrates calibration with a poison-detector sorting M&Ms into two bowls: perfect correlation gives one clean bowl, zero correlation is a coin flip where both bowls poison you at the same rate (and being wrong 100% of the time is as useful as being right, per George Costanza). Real value tools sit between: you would still rather eat from the high-value bowl. The caveat both hosts stress is that this is barely actionable — 'consistently hold the most valuable roster' reduces to 'only draft good players,' a fifty-step plan with 'and never make a mistake' as step one. Trent Richardson was briefly the most valuable dynasty asset; paper value that does not convert falls fast. Waldman's addition is that value still beats picking the fourth-most-valuable team.
