# Quarterback Development Honeymoon Period

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2026-04-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-13]]): he frames public and team expectations for a young quarterback as tracking a marriage. Year one, if the QB performs, is a honeymoon -- everything idealized, nobody wants to see the flaws. Then defenses adjust ('real life hitting') and fans, media and the front office start pointing fingers at the quarterback rather than helping him adjust; former-QB media members flip from praise to criticism for engagement ('clickety clickety clackety click'). Teams that work through that stage together -- learning strengths, weaknesses and how pieces fit -- can reach a championship-caliber comfort level; teams stuck in the blame stage head toward a 'divorce' (the QB gets run out). Cites [[Jayden Daniels]] as possibly entering that adjustment stage now, and [[Baker Mayfield]], [[Desmond Ridder]], [[Jake Locker]] and [[Drew Lock]] as past examples of the cycle playing out badly. Singles out [[Sam Darnold]] as one of the rare survivors -- he 'got kicked out of the house' in New York (an environment Waldman jokes was dysfunctional to the point of needing an exorcism) but worked on his craft and found a much better fit recently, illustrating that the marriage can still end in a late 'remarriage' success story rather than a permanent bust label.
