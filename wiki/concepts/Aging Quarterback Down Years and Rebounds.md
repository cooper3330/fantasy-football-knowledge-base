## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Film & Theory Sequences, Signal, Steelers)]]), relaying co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert): the apparent quarterback age cliff is partly a selection artifact. Old quarterbacks are almost universally terrible in their final season — that is why it is the final season — but if you ignore that last year, it is very common for an old quarterback to have a bad year, come back, and rebound. Harstad's examples were Philip Rivers, who looked toast in San Diego around 2012-13 and then had four to six more good seasons, and Kurt Warner, who looked like he had taken too many hits in the Mike Martz protection scheme in New York before turning it around in Arizona. The mechanism he gives is that a 26-year-old with a down year simply plays again and regresses to the mean, while a 36-year-old with a down year may just retire, so observers see a cliff where there is variance plus attrition. Applied directly to [[Aaron Rodgers]]: Harstad holds both possibilities open and does not rule out significant improvement.
- 2026-09-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-09-03]]): the hosts framed [[Matthew Stafford]]'s market undervaluation (outside top-12 QB ADP) the same way they'd describe [[Tom Brady]] in 2020 or Aaron Rodgers in 2021 -- an accomplished, aging veteran the market wrongly wrote off, who then delivered an MVP or championship-caliber season. Expected touchdown regression off Stafford's roughly 48-TD 2025 (down to something like 35) doesn't change the read, since he still projects as a top-5 fantasy QB in a top-tier scheme with elite weapons.
