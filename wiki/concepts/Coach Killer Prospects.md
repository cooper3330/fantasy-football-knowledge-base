# Coach Killer Prospects

## Definition

[[Matt Waldman]]'s definition of a "coach killer" — a term he distinguishes
explicitly from its common usage. It is **not** a kid who undermines his coach
through bad character or locker-room behavior. It is a structural outcome: a
front-office executive forces an unready rookie quarterback into the starting
job before he's ready, driven by draft-slot sunk cost rather than the coaching
staff's own evaluation. The coach is then caught in a no-win bind — start the
QB and get blamed for his struggles, or sit him and get fired for not playing
the team's investment — "unless [the coach] can prove without a shadow of a
doubt... this was well beyond my fault... and that rarely happens." The term is
almost always applied to quarterbacks specifically, because no other position
creates that same exec-vs-coach proxy fight.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] on *Going
  Deep* ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-04-11 (Going Deep - Fave Evals,
  Coach-Killer Candidates, and High-End Career Outcomes)]]): triggered by a
  media label (Kirk Bankert or similar, uncredited on air) calling [[Drake
  Maye]] a coach killer. Waldman's historical framing example: Josh Rosen
  (not tracked) as the first modern instance that comes to mind. Angelo's
  historical example: [[Justin Fields]] paired with then-Bears HC Matt
  Nagy's Andy Reid-style passing scheme "made no sense" for a quarterback who
  needed a more run-driven development plan — contrasted with Mitch Trubisky
  (not tracked), who struggled under Nagy but caught fire once OC Bill Lazor
  shifted the offense to run-heavy 12-personnel football.
- 2024-04-11 — same episode: three named 2024 candidates. **[[Drake Maye]]**
  — Waldman's lead pick, needing "a year or two of seasoning" because North
  Carolina's system (Mitch Trubisky, Sam Howell, Maye) never demanded
  NFL-timeline processing. **[[J.J. McCarthy]]** — Waldman's second pick,
  despite personally liking him and comping him to Brock Purdy; the risk is a
  repeat of the "Mitch Trubisky syndrome," where a well-liked, camera-friendly
  prospect simply doesn't pan out. **[[Jayden Daniels]]** — Waldman's third
  pick, rejecting an easy [[Lamar Jackson]] parallel (Waldman graded Daniels
  the better pure pocket passer of the two on tape) and instead worrying the
  league either rushes him before his half-beat-late processing catches up,
  or over-corrects and stair-steps him so long he never sheds the label. See
  [[Quarterback Processing and Confidence]] for the underlying processing
  critique, and [[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]] for the North
  Carolina program-archetype argument this overlaps with.
