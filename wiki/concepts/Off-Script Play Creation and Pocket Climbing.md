# Off-Script Play Creation and Pocket Climbing

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-06-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-23]]): coaches and fans overvalue off-schedule creation because the highlights are memorable, but the hidden cost is systemic. When a quarterback breaks containment instead of finishing his drop and climbing one or two steps, he ruins the timing of the concept, generates pressure that did not exist, wastes blockers who had their man controlled by leaving the pocket and surrendering the angle they had won, and forces receivers to abandon routes and re-scramble. Waldman applies this to [[Kyler Murray]] against OC Drew Petzing's plan to lean into it further, and uses [[Baker Mayfield]] as the counter-example — the Rose Bowl against Georgia showed him thriving against zone and collapsing once man coverage forced on-time throws from inside the pocket, and only when he learned to climb in Tampa did he approach a Drew Brees-type profile. The fantasy consequence: quarterbacks who cannot climb suppress their pass catchers, which he cites for [[Michael Wilson]] and for Murray having supported more than one fantasy receiving option just once in seven years.
