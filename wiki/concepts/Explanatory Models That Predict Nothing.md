# Explanatory Models That Predict Nothing

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Film & Theory)]]) relaying Adam Harstad (Football Guys, not a tracked expert): any model that explains everything after the fact but predicts nothing before it explains nothing — it is storytelling. Harstad's worked example is [[Tyler Warren]]: if Warren collapses, the ready explanation is that defenses had no film on him; if he stays great, the explanation is that he was always a superior player. Because an explanation is available for every outcome, none of them is informative. His remedy is to draw a line in the sand in advance, say plainly whether you think the player will be good or bad, then actually check whether your calls beat chance — and if they do not, that is either a model problem or a run of bad luck, both worth knowing. Waldman applies the same test to a theory he floated from [[Brandon Angelo]] that Bears GM Ryan Poles scouts height-weight-speed because he was an offensive lineman, graded that way himself; Harstad grants that Poles's formative experience shapes him but counters with Billy Beane, who was the height-weight-speed prospect, flamed out, and then deliberately drafted the opposite type — so the theory does not predict a direction and is not useful.
