## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-11-06 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06 (Players We Think We Know)]]): admittedly unscientific and all vibes, but he distinguishes quarterbacks who take ownership of an offense from those administering someone else's system. [[Jared Goff]] is playing extraordinarily well, but that is Ben Johnson's and Sean McVay's offense; [[Bo Nix]] is a Jay Cutler type where as Bo goes the offense goes. Harstad prefers the owner type and finds those offenses more fun to follow, invoking the Johnny Unitas line that you haven't arrived until you can tell your coach to go to hell, and Norm Van Brocklin asking out of Los Angeles because the coach wouldn't let him run the offense. He argues Drew Brees is misread as a system quarterback — the Saints were the Brees offense more than the Patriots were the Tom Brady offense, which is a statement about authorship, not quality. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed and pointed to Nix standing up to Sean Payton on the sideline early, which Payton loved.
- 2026-01-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-27]]): discussing Josh Allen sitting in on Buffalo's head-coaching interviews, Harmon said he 'doesn't like it at all.' A quarterback should have real input on what kind of coach and scheme he wants, but sitting in the interview room turns it into the quarterback interviewing his own future boss, flipping the power structure and pressuring candidates to play up to the quarterback rather than to ownership. He contrasted this with Baltimore, where owner Steve Bisciotti said he'd get Lamar Jackson's input but that he alone makes the hire, and warned against pushing Allen toward an 'Aaron Rodgers'-style career arc of outsized organizational say, preferring instead a LaFleur-Rodgers dynamic where the coach pushes the quarterback out of his comfort zone rather than catering to him. He also cited Allen's role in Buffalo drafting [[Keon Coleman]] as a data point against giving him more evaluative power.
