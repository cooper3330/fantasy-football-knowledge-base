# Twelve Personnel Rise and Slot Receiver Playing Time

## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-02]]): 12 personnel rate has climbed to 22.4% across 2024-2025, against 18.9% from 2016-2023 and 13.8% from 2011-2015, and Harmon treats this as a leading cause of falling wide receiver reception totals. His reasoning: teams want a second tight end to wad it up in protection and to avoid being predictable — he cites Atlanta, who play some of the most 12 personnel in the league with a second tight end not even running a route on 30% of dropbacks, and blames the Falcons' first three weeks on being the ultimate predictable offense that only passed from shotgun out of 11 personnel. Because you cannot line up a slot receiver when two tight ends are on the field, the third receiver's snaps are structurally capped, which is why the [[Alec Pierce]] and [[Romeo Doubs]] archetypes stay on the field while small man-beating slots sit. Harmon also frames 12 personnel as the way to beat cover two: block it up front and attack the intermediate middle with dig routes rather than 'mickey mouse' short throws.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-03]]): the heavy-personnel trend showed up in two games in one week. The Rams ran 'well over 30 plays with three tight ends on the field at the same time' against New Orleans — a deliberate application of the idea Harris and Andy Behrens discussed on the Yacht Club show that 'they want to be big when the defense is small' — and it worked so completely that Los Angeles 'could pretty much have named their number', repeatedly reaching the one-yard line and sneaking it in. Harris says the Rams 'took a page out of the Bills playbook from the Carolina game'; Buffalo used less three-tight-end but 'tons and tons and tons' of two-tight-end sets with 29 running back carries. The fantasy consequence he draws is direct: it suppresses receiver value ([[Khalil Shakir]] needing seven catches for 40 yards) and concentrates scoring in quarterback sneaks and short touchdowns rather than in the backfield or on the perimeter.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-04]]): Seattle entering Week 9 ranked 31st in 11-personnel rate, which Harmon uses to explain why [[Tory Horton]] could not show himself to be a full-time player despite being clearly the third receiver — Horton has run a route on 59% of dropbacks and been on the field for 46% of snaps, and tight end [[A.J. Barner]] actually led the team in routes on Sunday night. Harmon makes the same point about New England, where multiple-tight-end usage with Austin Hooper, [[Hunter Henry]] and [[Jack Westover]] is part of why [[Demario Douglas]] fell under a 26% snap share. The lesson he draws is that a receiver's route participation can be capped by personnel philosophy rather than by evaluation.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-06]]: 11-personnel rate is the first filter on whether a traded receiver actually has a role. Rates cited against a league average of about 59% — Las Vegas at 29% last week, fewest in the league; Seattle at 35.8%, second-lowest, and 29th in passing plays out of 11; Pittsburgh lowest of all; New Orleans third-highest at 71.5%, with Tennessee first and Cincinnati second. Koh's argument is that a winning Seattle team will not rebuild its offense around [[Rashid Shaheed]] and that Las Vegas, having traded [[Jakobi Meyers]], is even less motivated to go 11 personnel. Harmon's counter is time-limited: Seattle's heavy sets work because defenses answer in base with three linebackers, an outrageous advantage that funnels targets to [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]], but a good playoff defensive coordinator will counter with pass personnel, and Seattle's efficiency in pure dropback situations is worse than when playing heavy with a lead — so they need receiver depth to throw their way out of it.
- 2026-01-29 — According to JJ Zachariason of Late Round Fantasy Football (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-29]], discussing with [[Matt Harmon]]: WR scoring cratered in 2025 - lowest total WR fantasy points since 2017, and WR share of combined RB/WR/TE fantasy points was the lowest since at least 2011. The NFL's pass rate in 2025 was its lowest since 2011, and average 12-personnel usage hit 22.4% (highest since tracking began in 2020, vs 21.8% in 2024 and under 20% every year before that). Zachariason ties this to defenses playing more two-high shells, which lowered average quarterback aDOT and pushed offenses into heavier personnel to unlock play-action; that puts more tight ends on the field competing for the intermediate/perimeter targets mid-tier WRs used to get. WR target share hit its lowest mark since 2017 while TE target share (and TE first-read target share, an all-time high) kept climbing. He notes the elite tier (25%+ target-share players) held steady - it's specifically the WR2-through-WR4 tier, the 10-15% target-share players, that vanished; only 13 WRs hit 14+ PPR points per game in 2025 vs 26 in 2024. Harmon agrees the WR2 spot has felt 'flat' for years and calls the arbitrary WR1/WR2 tiering convention itself outdated given how the scoring data actually clusters.
- 2026-02-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-02-27]]): league-wide 11-personnel usage fell from 61% in 2024 to 56% in 2025, driven heavily by the Rams' drop from ~82% to 59%. New Orleans bucked the trend hardest, jumping 34 percentage points in 3-plus-receiver plays (33.8% to 67.8%) under [[Kellen Moore]] -- by far the league's largest increase, with Kansas City a distant second at under 8 points -- while Atlanta swung the opposite direction, falling 41 points (86.2% to 45.1%) as its receiver corps beyond [[Drake London]] collapsed. Harmon expects Atlanta to lean into more 12 personnel under the new Kevin Stefanski/Tommy Rees staff, making tight end usage (Kyle Pitts, a possible David Njoku addition) a key storyline there, while expecting Kellen Moore's Saints to keep spreading the field.
- 2026-03-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-28]]): Waldman connects the NFL's multi-year swing back to gap/power run schemes (power, counter, toss, trap) -- a response to lighter, faster defensive personnel built to stop spread passing games -- to a coming decline in pure slot-receiver demand. As offenses lean on two- and three-tight-end sets (hardest for defenses to defend) built around big, blocking-capable tight ends rather than route-running 'big slot' tight ends, situational slot receivers on those teams could see volume reduced to afterthought status. He doesn't expect true flanker-caliber slot weapons like [[CeeDee Lamb]], [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[Drake London]] and [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] to vanish -- they'll just get more of their slot snaps disguised as tight-end alignments -- but he thinks the broader pure-slot population shrinks from a majority to a minority role over the next several draft classes, contingent on the NFL finding enough quality big tight ends to fuel the shift.
- 2026-04-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-02]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman argued that as more NFL offenses shift to two- and three-tight-end personnel to counter lighter, faster defensive fronts built for spread attacks, teams need fewer true slot receivers -- a trend he used to explain why he prefers [[Carnell Tate]] (an outside/split-end profile) over [[Makai Lemon]] (a slot-leaning profile) even though he rates both very highly, since Tate's skill set projects to be more valuable given where offensive scheme is heading.
- 2026-05-05 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-05]]) [Dynasty]: Harmon expects [[Drake London]]'s slot snaps in Atlanta to decrease this season as the team trends toward more 12-personnel, heavier packages, pushing London to play more from an outside alignment while receivers like [[Zachariah Branch]] and Jimmy Horn Jr.-style motion pieces create leverage underneath.
- 2026-05-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and untracked co-host James Koh ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-14]]): the hosts compared Jacksonville's crowded four-receiver room ([[Brian Thomas Jr.]], [[Travis Hunter]], [[Jacoby Meyers]], [[Parker Washington]]) to the Green Bay Packers' rotation-heavy 12-personnel approach, citing route-participation data ([[Jayden Reed]] leading Green Bay at 74% in 2024, [[Romeo Doubs]] at 80% in 2023 and 74.3% in 2025, [[Matthew Golden]] at 48% in 2025) as the likely shape of the Jaguars passing game if the team leans into heavier personnel as its draft signaled. Both agreed that outcome makes individual Jaguars WR fantasy value hard to predict week to week, mirroring how Packers receivers have historically been difficult to roster with confidence.
- 2026-06-16 — According to Matt Bove ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-06-16]]): the Bills will mix in 12- and even 13-personnel occasionally under new HC Joe Brady, but 11 personnel remains their base. He floated that Jackson Hawes' standout run-blocking could push the staff toward Hawes-and-Dalton Kincaid two-tight-end sets instead of the traditional Dawson Knox-and-Kincaid pairing, with Khalil Shakir and DJ Moore as the receivers on the field in those looks -- illustrating how a blocking-tight-end's development can reshuffle even a receiver-heavy passing offense's personnel groupings.
