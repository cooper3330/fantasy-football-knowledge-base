# Touch Volume and In-Game Adaptation

## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-13 (Paper Champions and All-Timers Across Eras)]]): Waldman proposes that part of why the early-2000s backs looked so good was workload itself, not just talent. A back with heavy volume plays the game within the game — he sees enough repetitions of the same fronts to know how linebackers and safeties align on a concept run three times already, and can adjust in the fourth quarter. A back given three touches in the first quarter and one before halftime never accumulates that read. He cites [[Rico Dowdle]]'s heavy-usage stretch as a modern example, and an Eagles defensive tackle who diagnosed an inside-zone look he had seen earlier in the game and blew it up for a fumble, precisely because he was not rotating out. Waldman does not believe backs are physically softer now; he thinks they would simply look better if teams ran more. Harstad partly agrees — he grants Dowdle would be better regarded with more volume, and makes the parallel case that 1970s receivers like Harold Jackson are underrated because their teams barely threw — but insists the early-2000s backs were genuinely, separately great.
