# Rushing Quarterbacks and Receiver Support

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-10-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: from a Football Guys study of top-15 quarterbacks over five years, Waldman uses 20% of total yardage gained on the ground as the line between a mobile quarterback and a running quarterback — past that, supporting-cast fantasy value diminishes, usually leaving one WR1-caliber option and otherwise WR3/WR4 outcomes. He deliberately says 'running quarterback' rather than the euphemism 'mobile', noting [[Josh Allen]] and Steve Young qualified while [[Brock Purdy]] does not. Practical use: don't draft on it, but check it four to six weeks into a season to decide whether a hoped-for receiver breakout is still viable. Co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) agrees conceptually but says use rush ATTEMPTS, not yards: efficiency is irrelevant to receivers, since every carry consumes a snap that could have become a broken-coverage deep shot, and yards make the measure circular (see [[Jalen Hurts]], and [[Lamar Jackson]] at 27% rushing yardage in 2020 while supporting only two WR4s). Harstad reserves yards-share for career-level classification of runners like Randall Cunningham and Michael Vick.
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): using [[Kyler Murray]]'s stated intent to run more in 2025, Waldman rejects the assumption that more quarterback rushing improves a team. His argument is that scrambling more stresses the offensive line and the receivers, who must sustain blocks and extend routes longer, and that it usually means the quarterback is passing up chances to create quickly and efficiently from structure — the plays that actually generate explosives. He points to the absence of winning seasons and of multiple supported receivers across Murray's rushing-heavy years as the evidence, while conceding the fantasy value of a 500-yard rushing quarterback is real and separate from team quality.
- 2025-06-05 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-05]]): heavy-rushing quarterbacks distort the skill positions around them in two directions. Down: fantasy wants touchdowns and receptions, and Angelo notes running quarterbacks — [[Lamar Jackson]], [[Jalen Hurts]], [[Jayden Daniels]] — take low-red-zone carries and are not check-down passers, so backs do not get the hook routes and flats a [[Jared Goff]] offense feeds [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and [[David Montgomery]]; Angelo's stated exception is that Daniels's RPO and screen volume rescues Washington's backs. Up: Angelo argues [[Justin Fields]] hyper-targets one receiver, citing [[D.J. Moore]]'s ~1,500-yard season, which is the basis for liking [[Garrett Wilson]] and [[Mason Taylor]] while fading [[Breece Hall]]. Waldman adds the compounding problem — an inefficient rushing-quarterback offense creates predictable down-and-distance, which telegraphs personnel too.
