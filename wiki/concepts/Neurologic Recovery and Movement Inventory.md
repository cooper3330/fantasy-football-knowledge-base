## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-02-27 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-27]]): the limiting factor after a ligament or tendon repair is not the structure but the neurology. Surgeons in 2025 reliably reconstruct an ACL or Achilles, but what cannot be replicated is the pathway of millions of neurons carrying information to those tendons, ligaments and supporting muscles — a player severs 20-plus years of accumulated movement inventory. Angelo has seen explosive athletes unable to walk upstairs without a hitch for eight months post-Achilles rupture despite a physiologically sound repair, and notes the deficit shows up at speed: not walking down a hallway at two miles an hour but escaping 'human bears' at fifteen and delivering an accurate boundary throw. He extends it positively as well — mastery is the same mechanism, the pathway widening from a one-lane road to a five-lane highway, which is why running backs come into tune around 26-27 ([[Saquon Barkley]]'s age) and why the 10,000-hours idea is really about neurologic inventory rather than hours logged. [[Matt Waldman]] analogizes it to moving a motherboard. Applied to [[Kurtis Rourke]] and cited against [[Jonathan Brooks]]'s re-tear.
