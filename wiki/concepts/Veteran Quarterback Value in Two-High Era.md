## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): Harmon argues defensive coordinators currently have the jump on offenses, and the offenses still working are well-designed ones fronted by veteran quarterbacks who have seen every coverage — Detroit, Seattle with [[Geno Smith]], Tampa Bay with [[Baker Mayfield]], and now Carolina with [[Andy Dalton]] — because a veteran can identify the blitz at the line, slide protection and still push the ball over the middle. Young quarterbacks like [[Caleb Williams]] and [[Anthony Richardson]] have too much on their plate against these play callers. Both hosts insist cover two and two-high shells are over-discussed and not a boogeyman: Harmon says offenses have solved two-high before and will again, that the real variable is what teams do behind the shell with pressure, safety rotation and gloved-up underneath coverage, and that the Shanahan-style answer is play-action shots over the middle off a good run game. Koh marvels that cover two went from dominant to ridiculed to fashionable again inside Dalton's career, and both reject Mel Kiper's suggestion of legislating two-high out of the game.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-10]]): Harmon restated his position that veteran quarterbacks are underrated because their accumulated catalog lets them solve pressure and coverage pre-snap — [[Kirk Cousins]] against Todd Bowles is his example, diagnosing the blitz and going straight to [[Drake London]] on in-breakers or [[Darnell Mooney]] on the out — but added the boundary condition this episode: the edge only holds if the quarterback can still move or if he is right pre-snap every time. [[Aaron Rodgers]] can't move, has to be perfect pre-snap, and was fooled all game by the two best blitz-and-coverage disguisers in Minnesota and Denver. Harmon extends it to scheme fit: older passers who aren't flexible enough to run modern concepts push offenses back into static ISO ball, and he half-jokes about whether Matt Canada was unfairly blamed for what Ben Roethlisberger wanted to run.
