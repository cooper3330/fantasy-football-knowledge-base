# Fantasy Quarterback Value vs NFL Quarterback Quality

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-10-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-21 (Feel It or Fk It 10.21.24)]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: an explicit soapbox that the two evaluations are separate questions. 'Two things can be true — you can be the best at something and not good at another thing and vice versa.' His examples: [[Patrick Mahomes]], clearly the best quarterback in the NFL but not a good fantasy quarterback, with his last top-ten regular-season week in Week 12 of the prior year while [[Daniel Jones]] already had three; [[Drake Maye]], a better fantasy quarterback than real quarterback because bad pass placement is masked by volume and a bad defense forcing throws; and [[Anthony Richardson]], where 'I don't care if he's a good NFL quarterback' as long as the rushing upside and job security hold. The positive rule he states: hunt fantasy quarterbacks on lousy teams that play from behind and must throw, or quarterbacks without protection who are forced out of the pocket and run. He also argues fantasy managers should not adopt the NFL's disdain for hero ball, because scramble-drill chaos is fantasy points.
- 2024-11-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-04]]): two things can be true at once — [[Patrick Mahomes]] can be the best NFL quarterback alive and not a great fantasy quarterback, and [[Anthony Richardson]] can be a poor NFL quarterback while learning on the job and still be a phenomenal fantasy quarterback. Waldman states the position bluntly: as a fantasy investor he does not care whether the player is good, he wants points, and his evidence is Richardson's first four games of 2023 and the 2024 opener. He also frames how he evaluates differently from a film grinder here — he says he is looking at opportunity, system and surrounding talent rather than grinding tape, and notes that Chris Ballard independently says the same words about Richardson that he does.
- 2024-11-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-25]]): he stated the general principle twice in one episode — great NFL players do not have to be great fantasy players, and great fantasy players are not always great NFL players; what fantasy needs is that they be good enough to hold a job and be productive. [[Nick Chubb]] is the first direction: a still-excellent NFL back who is not a good fantasy play because Cleveland's line and pass volume deny him the touches. [[Anthony Richardson]] is the reverse: a quarterback many call horrible who Waldman thinks can be one of the greatest fantasy quarterbacks of all time, with job security as the only real gate. He applied the same split to receivers, saying [[DeVonta Smith]] is the better fantasy play while [[Jordan Addison]] is the better wide receiver.
- 2024-12-02 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-02]]): a good NFL quarterback is not automatically a startable fantasy quarterback, and the gap is usually about offensive design rather than talent. Bob Harris (not tracked) made the case via [[Patrick Mahomes]] — once a must-start, now not, because Kansas City's offense no longer asks him to throw 50 touchdowns, which is not his fault — and applied the same reading to [[C.J. Stroud]]: a solid NFL starter who cannot be trusted as a weekly fantasy QB1. Waldman agreed and added [[Kyler Murray]] from the other direction: fairly rated in fantasy at QB8-15 because he creates yardage with his legs, yet overrated as an actual quarterback because he is not a field general.
- 2025-01-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-13]]): Waldman restates his recurring position that [[Kyler Murray]] is a good fantasy quarterback but not a good NFL quarterback, and that the distinction has direct consequences for the receivers attached to him. His evidence is distributional: Murray spreads the ball around and has supported two top-24 fantasy receivers only once, in the DeAndre Hopkins seasons, and Hopkins ultimately did not want to keep playing with him — which Waldman reads as a veteran receiver's verdict on the quarterback. [[Matt Harmon]] agrees on the practical fantasy consequence, saying Murray will not elevate a second-tier receiver to elite production, but pushes back on Hopkins as a witness, noting Hopkins put up productive seasons with Brian Hoyer and Ryan Mallett.
- 2025-01-27 — According to Bob Harris (co-host, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-01-27]]: defending a position his SiriusXM colleague Jeff Mans dislikes, Harris insists two things can be true at once — [[Patrick Mahomes]] is not a great fantasy quarterback and is simultaneously the best quarterback in the NFL, because on any given Sunday he can be better than your quarterback or simply good enough when paired with a defense playing on point. [[Matt Waldman]] supports the mechanism: the league's two-high response to Mahomes stripped the deep game out of the sport, and Mahomes adapted by playing low-mistake, game-managing football — Tom Brady-ing it — which wins games without generating fantasy points. Waldman also frames the 2024 lesson on upside quarterbacks: the high-risk pick like [[Anthony Richardson]] is only tolerable when the second tier of steady options is strong enough to bail you out, and this season it was not.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: the case for shopping outside the top 15 at quarterback is that a lot of those passers will produce very close to the QB8-through-QB12 range, and the margin of difference between quarterbacks after QB15 is small enough that you can take chances on several and stream weekly matchups profitably. He uses [[Geno Smith]] at QB22 as the worked example — 68%-plus completions and 4,000 yards projected, with play action off a strong run game and a real pass-catching group — arguing the position's fantasy value is set by situation and margin, not by where the league ranks the passer.
- 2025-08-20 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)] [Dynasty]: the [[Anthony Richardson]] benching for [[Daniel Jones]] is the object lesson for both cardinal errors. Assuming a good runner must be a competent enough passer without having seen it is one; assuming a great passer cannot be an elite fantasy quarterback without the rushing cheat code is just as bad. Harris quantifies it: 400 rushing yards and four touchdowns is barely more than three fantasy points a week, which is worth having only when attached to an actual good throwing quarterback, not one producing 3,200 yards, 23 touchdowns and 14 interceptions. He applies the same logic positively to [[Joe Burrow]], the only top-six quarterback he drafts, and says analysts who made Richardson the dynasty QB5 a year ago should have to take a year off from doing shows.
