## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-04-18 — According to guest Cory Kinnan (not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Harmon]]'s show ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-18]]): plenty of NFL scouting orthodoxy is archaic dinosaur thinking, but quarterback height is not one of those things — he calls himself a height purist, and his argument is base-rate: only one starting quarterback in the league is under 6'1". He reads [[Kyler Murray]]'s stagnation as a consequence of having to create his own windows, get outside structure along the boundary and reset his feet because in-structure work only comes easily when it is fully in rhythm, and suspects slow eyes contribute too. He rejects the Drew Brees appeal as survivorship bias — one generational outlier held up against ten failures. Harmon adds that [[Russell Wilson]] is the other sub-6'1" example, and that his offenses collapse into boundary throwing with nothing over the middle, while Chicago's poor interior line made [[Caleb Williams]]' 6'1" frame felt last year.
- 2025-05-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-14]]): Harmon says people dance around the point, but short quarterbacks structurally cannot see the deep-intermediate middle of the field, and that this caps the usable route tree of their best receivers. [[Kyler Murray]] threw a dig route on just 3% of attempts in 2023 and 2.9% in 2022, with 0% comebacks and 4.9% posts — which he calls a direct production concern for [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], whose best route by success rate is the dig. [[Russell Wilson]] is his historical case: never worked the middle of the field, and to this day lives outside the numbers on go balls and curls. The stated exceptions prove the mechanism rather than break it — [[Bryce Young]] is currently doing it and Drew Brees did it in New Orleans, both behind big interior offensive lines that opened sight lines. Harmon's corollary is that a short quarterback's receiver needs either offensive line investment or scheme that moves him to where the throws actually go.
- 2025-06-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-25]]): Harmon offers what he calls an educated opinion that of all the short quarterbacks in the league, [[Bryce Young]] works the middle of the field the best — not in terms of driving the football on digs, which he concedes Young cannot do, but in processing and literally seeing the middle of the field. Guest Cory Kinnan (not tracked) agrees, saying it is very rare to find, and contrasts it with the running joke about Russell Wilson and the middle of the field being 'the plague.' The framing separates two things usually bundled under height: sight lines and willingness to attack the middle, versus the arm strength required to complete the deeper in-breaking throws.
- 2025-07-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception - 2025-07-23]]): height limits which throws a quarterback will even attempt, and the practical cost is the middle of the field. He applies it to [[Kyler Murray]] — not as an insult, but Murray is short, does not see over-the-middle throws and has never been a drop-back-and-rip-it passer — and the consequence is that [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]'s best charted route, the dig at 87.5% success on 24.1% of routes, largely goes unfed. James Koh (not a tracked expert) supplies the supporting split from Murray's 2023 profile: 3% dig rate at a 50% success rate, second only to his near-zero comeback rate, while noting quarterbacks are extremely difficult to isolate and better personnel could raise it. The conflict is scheme-level: Drew Petzing's Stefanski-derived offense is built on the big in-breakers Murray does not throw.
