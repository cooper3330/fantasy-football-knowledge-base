# Reception Perception Methodology

## Definition

[[Matt Harmon]]'s receiver-charting project (published at ReceptionPerception.com)
and his standing analytical framework on this show. Every route is charted from
all-22 tape and receivers are graded on **success rate versus coverage type**
(press / man / zone) rather than on raw box-score production, with further
splits by **route type** (e.g. curl-route success rate as a share of routes
run) and **alignment usage** (X / flanker / slot, plus motion and pre-snap
usage). Prospects are typically charted on a 3-game sample first — enough,
per Harmon, "to tell if a guy can play and if he cannot" — before expanding to
a full ~8-game sample for the site's published profile. Cross-class prospect
grades accumulate on Harmon's "stacked board," a running ranked list across
draft classes; a **tier-one** grade means a player is worth picking in the
top 10 as a "ready-made star." The site's charting approach extends to other
positions via other contributors — e.g. Derrick Klassen's quarterback
success-rate-against-pressure work — though that is not Harmon's own
methodology.

## Expert Takes

- 2024-01-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-16]]): cites the site's in-season man-coverage separation charting directly to support a fantasy-relevant claim — [[Puka Nacua]] now separates better against man coverage than [[Cooper Kupp]] does, underpinning the read that "the baton has been passed" in the Rams' receiver room. Also cites colleague Derrick Klassen's related pressure-charting work (published on the same site, not Harmon's own charting) to support a [[Tua Tagovailoa]] critique: Tua's 2022 success-rate-against-pressure ranked 5th-lowest among starters, alongside Kyler Murray, [[Jared Goff]], Aaron Rodgers, [[Lamar Jackson]] and Jalen Hurts.
- 2024-01-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-01-18]]): the fullest demonstration of the methodology yet, applied to three early 2024 draft prospects ([[Marvin Harrison Jr.]], [[Malik Nabers]], [[Rome Odunze]]) off explicitly early 3-game samples. Concrete metrics named on air: **success rate vs. press coverage** (Harrison Jr.'s on pace for the best of the last 4 classes), **success rate on a specific route type as a share of total routes charted** (Nabers: 92% success on curl routes, 22.7% of his sample), and **alignment share** (Nabers over 40% of snaps in the slot; Harrison Jr. profiles as more X-only for now). Confirms the process: 3-game samples are enough "to tell if a guy can play," full site profiles come later off ~8 games. Introduces the **"stacked board"** — Harmon's running cross-class prospect ranking, where a **tier-one** grade means "worth picking in the top 10... a ready-made star." Only five receivers had earned that grade across the 2021-2023 classes as of this episode: Ja'Marr Chase (#1 on the board), Chris Olave, DeVonta Smith, [[Drake London]], and Garrett Wilson (Olave/Smith/Wilson not tracked in this wiki). See [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Drake London]] for the player-page application.

- 2024-03-07 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-07]]): applies the full stat suite (success rate vs. man/zone/press, route-type share, alignment share) to explain a deployment-driven down year for two veterans rather than a talent decline — [[Calvin Ridley]]'s press-coverage success rate fell ~10 points on a route tree that was 48.6% comeback/curl/9-route and under 10% slants while lined up outside 81.8% of snaps; [[Marquise Brown]]'s slant success rate and after-contact tackle-breaking numbers both declined while Arizona lined him up on the line 77.4% of snaps despite him never topping 65% success vs. press in his career. Both used as examples of alignment/route-tree usage materially distorting a receiver's charted output relative to his true talent level. See [[Scheme vs Talent]].

- 2024-03-12 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-12]]: applies three straight seasons of charted data to [[Michael Pittman Jr.]] — success rate vs. man coverage of 74.3%/74.2%/74.8% across 2021-2023, over 80% vs. zone and press each year, and a 2023 route tree that was 61.4% slants/curls/digs (12.8% digs, 20% curls, 28.6% slants) — as evidence his production ceiling is capped by quarterback play rather than talent, comparing his usage profile to a "Bud Light version" of peak Michael Thomas (not tracked) with the New Orleans Saints.

- 2024-03-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-03-19]]): puts an explicit percentile calibration on the man-coverage success-rate scores for the first time on air — any charted success rate vs. man coverage above roughly 75% pushes a receiver into "the 80th percentile," a threshold he says separates merely good charted receivers from extremely good ones. Applied live to [[Keenan Allen]] (dig/curl/slant/flat scores in the 83-93% range) and [[Curtis Samuel]] (a career-long ~75%+ success rate vs. man).

- 2024-04-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-04]]): a historical validation study of the methodology itself, sparked by a Discord member (J.P. Bennett). Looking back across charted prospects from the 2016-2018 and last four draft classes (~100 total), Harmon found 32 players who finished below the 35th percentile in two of the three main coverage metrics (success rate vs. man/zone/press) in college. Of those 32, only 9 (28%) had "moments" in the league, and only 5 (15%) are "inarguable hits": [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[Cooper Kupp]], [[Rashee Rice]], Tyler Boyd and Juju Smith-Schuster (neither Boyd nor Smith-Schuster tracked in this wiki). Harmon's read: RP is "pretty damn accurate" at the NFL level once charted, but its college-level signal is weaker because of the gap in competition/coaching level, and college non-separators specifically need a "big slot" role transition to hit — every one of the five hits made that move. Applied directly to [[Keon Coleman]]'s 2024 prospect profile. See [[Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes]].

- 2024-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] on *Reception Perception: The Show* ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-11]]): a charting-rules clarification prompted by [[Adonai Mitchell]]'s and [[Xavier Worthy]]'s Texas tape — declines to chart a receiver "sauntering" through a route with no real intent to be thrown the ball as an actual route attempt for success-rate purposes, since it isn't a legitimate route the quarterback could have targeted. Also applies the man/press/zone success-rate framework and route-type-share splits in full to Mitchell's finished profile: 84th percentile vs. man, 83rd vs. press, only 31st vs. zone; 82.8% of snaps on the line (highest of any prospect charted this cycle); 35% of routes run downfield (corner/post/nine) and 52% short/intermediate in-breakers, with 82.4%/89.5% success rates on digs/outs specifically.
- 2024-04-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-04-25]]): explained his contested-target charting rule on air -- a target only counts as "contested" if it was a genuinely winnable ball; short/underthrown passes a receiver has to reach through a defender for aren't counted, because the goal is isolating receiver skill from surrounding variables like quarterback accuracy. He noted this can make his contested-catch and contested-target rates diverge from other outlets' numbers, and can make specific players (e.g. [[Javon Baker]] at UCF) look worse by raw rate than the tape suggests when their own quarterback play was poor.
- 2024-06-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-06-04]]): when a full-season sample is skewed by a clear in-season inflection point, Harmon splits it to isolate signal -- for [[Jameson Williams]] he charted weeks 11-13 separately from a week-15-on sample and found the route-tree strengths/weaknesses looked similar between the two but the overall success-rate numbers diverged enormously (season-long ~57%/72%/53% vs. man/zone/press vs. a week-15-on 67%/85%/65%), because the early sample was 'non-functional NFL player' level bad. Harmon's stated benchmarks for a good player remain ~70% success rate vs. man and ~80% vs. zone. He also cautioned against overweighting how a sample ends just because it's most recent, comparing it to overreacting to a team's late-season hot streak, even while conceding the week-15-on data for Williams is more representative of his current baseline than the full-season figure.
