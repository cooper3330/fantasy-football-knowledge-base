## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]): guest Dan Hatman (former Eagles and Giants scout, NOT a tracked expert) relayed a Louis Riddick line taken from the Belichick and Saban Cleveland staff — a roster is always balancing positional versatility against positional redundancy. You can name the best 53 on paper today, but by week two or three six of them are dinged or you are facing a wrinkle that needs a specific answer, and you cannot have only one player on the roster who can supply it. Hatman describes receiver rooms as basketball teams needing a big, a point guard and swing players, with deliberate Venn-diagram overlap so the swing guys can partly do the big man's job. The same logic applies to a backfield: you cannot carry only one back who can pass protect or only one who can get across the goal line. Ultimately it reduces to trust, which is the burden on a player through his first two years.
