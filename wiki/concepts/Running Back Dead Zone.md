## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]): the 'running back dead zone' (roughly rounds 3-6 in a 12-team redraft) is misunderstood as a talent cliff -- his research shows 4th-round RBs aren't meaningfully different in output from 8th-round RBs in that range, because of a supply-and-demand dynamic: only about 10 true bell cows get drafted before the dead zone starts, and teams reaching for the 'next' RB are often betting on projected-but-unproven volume (e.g. Mike Davis in Atlanta before Cordarrelle Patterson stole the role) rather than talent, making those picks fragile. He reframed it as more of a 'minefield' than a true dead zone because rookie RBs specifically outperform there: rookies taken in the RB2/high-RB3 range hit at a rate comparable to low-end RB1s because the market underprices players it hasn't seen play, even when the prospect-score talent profile is strong. Other positions (elite/mobile QBs, top WRs, some TEs) have historically been good value in that same draft range too, creating an opportunity cost for reaching at RB there.
- 2024-10-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-28]]): running back by committee 'was told to go fuck itself' in 2024, and his favourite thing about the season is that he could recommend old-school drafting — backs in the first two rounds, early — and be right, because a shit-ton of them got genuine bell-cow work. He named [[Derrick Henry]], [[Saquon Barkley]], [[Joe Mixon]], [[Josh Jacobs]], [[James Cook]] when healthy and [[Aaron Jones]] (about 100% of Minnesota's back touches on a Thursday night) and put the count at a good dozen-plus, close to half the league in non-committee situations. Waldman, who came up in 1986 when committees did not exist, framed this as a reversal for proponents of Zero RB / 'upside down drafting' — the approach Paul Charchian called 'do the opposite' — and named Tampa Bay as a team that still has not got the message.
- 2026-04-30 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-04-30]]): Harris explicitly labeled his RB23-30 tier ([[David Montgomery]], [[Chuba Hubbard]], [[Tony Pollard]], [[J.K. Dobbins]] and others) the 'running back dead zone,' the range where fantasy managers typically pivot to receivers, and argued this is exactly the range where the market both over- and under-corrects: backs dinged mainly because the market has decided their situation is bad tend to be undervalued relative to talent, while backs the market gets excited about because their situation looks 'infallible' tend to be overvalued.
