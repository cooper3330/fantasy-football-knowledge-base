## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]): the 'running back dead zone' (roughly rounds 3-6 in a 12-team redraft) is misunderstood as a talent cliff -- his research shows 4th-round RBs aren't meaningfully different in output from 8th-round RBs in that range, because of a supply-and-demand dynamic: only about 10 true bell cows get drafted before the dead zone starts, and teams reaching for the 'next' RB are often betting on projected-but-unproven volume (e.g. Mike Davis in Atlanta before Cordarrelle Patterson stole the role) rather than talent, making those picks fragile. He reframed it as more of a 'minefield' than a true dead zone because rookie RBs specifically outperform there: rookies taken in the RB2/high-RB3 range hit at a rate comparable to low-end RB1s because the market underprices players it hasn't seen play, even when the prospect-score talent profile is strong. Other positions (elite/mobile QBs, top WRs, some TEs) have historically been good value in that same draft range too, creating an opportunity cost for reaching at RB there.
