# Contract Holdout Slow Starts

## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-27]]): receivers who miss camp in a contract dispute have historically started slowly even when the deal gets done, and Harmon offers two charted cases. [[CeeDee Lamb]] in a fully healthy, non-holdout 2023 was targeted on 29% of his routes for 102.9 yards per game; in Weeks 1-5 after his holdout that fell to 22.6% and 75.6 yards. [[Brandon Aiyuk]] in 2023 was targeted on 24% of routes for 83.9 yards per game; in Weeks 1-5 after his holdout, 22.3% and 62.8 yards — and Harmon says that figure is generous because it includes one blow-up game against Arizona. His key point is that the usual rebuttal, continuity of quarterback and scheme, does not save you: both Lamb and Aiyuk had years of continuity with their quarterback and head coach and still lagged, with Dak Prescott and Lamb both saying it took time to get going. He applies the frame to [[Terry McLaurin]], stressing it is a risk to be aware of rather than a prediction.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-04]]): the pattern now has three data points he names together — [[Brandon Aiyuk]] in 2024, and [[Jauan Jennings]] and [[Terry McLaurin]] in 2025. The mechanism is not the contract dispute itself but the missed practice time: Aiyuk was active in September and the 49ers simply didn't use him. Harris's practical takeaway is to rank the returning holdout, but not so high that you feel obligated to start him, and to consider waiting a week to see what the usage looks like. He also separates blame from effect — he is never mad at a player for using the only leverage he has, and points at the team for risking its own offense to squeeze out a few more nickels — while noting the harder problem is diagnostic: with a returning hold-in you may not be able to tell whether something is actually different.
- 2025-09-08 — According to Bob Harris (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-08]]): the missed-August-reps effect is broader than holdouts and should be a live Week 1-to-October filter. He cites Fantasy Points charting showing [[A.J. Brown]] won zero routes against Dallas after barely practicing in August, and names [[Terry McLaurin]], [[Jauan Jennings]], [[Justin Jefferson]], [[De'Von Achane]] and [[Tyreek Hill]] as players in the same bucket, cautioning nothing is guaranteed in any individual case. He then supplies the counter-evidence himself: Cincinnati's offense had everyone on the field in August and still started slowly, so 'maybe my whole missing August thing just doesn't matter at all' and the real lesson is that nothing is trustworthy until October. [[Matt Waldman]] adds that being right or wrong in football is a temporary condition.
- 2025-09-08 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-08]]): the [[Terry McLaurin]] opener is a reason to invoke precedent rather than panic or dismiss. McLaurin trailed [[Deebo Samuel]] 10 targets to 4 after his hold-in, and Harris says we saw the [[Brandon Aiyuk]] experience last year after a holdout, so we are not shocked and this could last a while. He and guest **Sam Gold** (not tracked) disagree on the diagnosis of the one big missed deep shot: Harris does not think it was about missing training camp, and Gold, after going back and forth on whether it was a chemistry problem from lost preseason reps, leans toward it simply being a bad throw by [[Jayden Daniels]] on a route where McLaurin created plenty of window.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Andy Behrens (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-03-05]]): Discussing [[George Pickens]]' tag by Dallas and [[Breece Hall]]'s tag by the Jets, Harris argues the franchise/transition tag mechanism is 'incredibly outmoded, anti-competitive' and predicts real holdout risk for both players in 2026 unless their teams get long-term deals done before the season -- warning that fans shouldn't assume a tagged star simply reports and produces normally, citing the pattern set by Sauce Gardner before he got paid by the Jets.
- 2026-07-14 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-07-14]]): discussing a hypothetical George Pickens hold-in, Harris cites a years-old interview quote from Mike Tomlin (on Le'Veon Bell's holdout) distinguishing 'training shape' from 'in-game shape' - missed camp reps tend to cost a player roughly the first couple of regular-season games of effectiveness even if he's otherwise fit, which is why Harris would drop a hold-in player a tier rather than off his board entirely.
- 2026-08-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Dave Richard (CBS Fantasy, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-03]]): discussing [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and [[Bijan Robinson]]'s simultaneous August holdouts, both hosts reiterate the pattern that holdout players routinely 'get it right before the season starts,' then stink for the first few weeks or suffer an injury that costs them more time on top of the missed camp reps - the most recent example cited is Terry McLaurin's 2025 holdout. They set a concrete deadline of roughly two weeks before Week 1 (around August 29th) as the point where an unsigned star must actually be moved down draft boards rather than assumed to work itself out.
- 2026-08-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-04]]): With [[Bijan Robinson]] signing a $51 million guaranteed deal and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] expected to follow shortly, Harris says this settles what could have become a protracted camp holdout story -- he never seriously believed Robinson would let it drag deep into August, and both backs can now be drafted 'in relative comfort' near the top of non-Superflex boards without holdout risk overhanging their draft value.
