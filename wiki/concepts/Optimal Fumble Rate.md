## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Film & Theory Sequences, Signal, Steelers)]]), relaying co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert): the optimal fumble rate is never zero. Harstad cited a Doug Drinen article from roughly twenty years ago — if you never fumble, you are not fumbling enough — and argued it is trivially easy not to fumble: secure the handoff, wrap it with two hands and go down, which he said is what Ty'Son Williams did in the second half of his starting debut and it ruined his career. Push harder and your fumble chance might go from 0.1% to 0.5%, but you gain two or three yards per carry; there is an equilibrium point where the extra yards offset the extra risk, and only past that point should a back dial it back. Waldman's addition is the diagnostic side: one fumble means nothing, and the worry threshold is a cluster — camp fumbles, a college ball-security history, coaches talking about it, or a coach known to be a stickler. Applied on air to [[Ashton Jeanty]] (don't worry) versus [[Bhayshul Tuten]] in a three-man committee (worry more).
