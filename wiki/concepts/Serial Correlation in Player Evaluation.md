## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-11-22 — According to Adam Harstad, untracked *Film and Theory* co-host ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]): serial correlation is when several apparently independent opinions are actually all responding to the same input, so five draft analysts agreeing tells you much less than it appears — if a player does something on film that all film evaluators love but that does not translate, every one of them is wrong for the same reason. [[Terry McLaurin]] is his inverse example: something in McLaurin's technique made film-first evaluators want nothing to do with him, and they were all wrong together. The payoff is the opposite case: when genuinely uncorrelated processes — none with an outstanding individual hit rate — converge on the same player, the odds of them all being wrong plummet. He applied it to [[Chris Olave]], where [[Matt Waldman]]'s process-based film work, [[Matt Harmon]]'s binary did-he-get-open [[Reception Perception Methodology]], Harstad's own yards-per-route-run sheet and prospect model, and air-yards market share all love him for different reasons. Harstad's caveat: the processes are not perfectly uncorrelated since all are responding to the same facts on the ground; they simply evaluate them in different ways.
