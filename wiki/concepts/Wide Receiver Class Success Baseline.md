## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): the reasonable expectation for any receiver class is roughly 4.5 players who deliver at least three years of starter production, beginning within their first four seasons in the league, out of the 25-30 receivers drafted each year. A successful NFL receiver career with starter production spans about three and a half to six years, and most receivers with multiple starter seasons rarely reach top-24 or top-12 production, averaging less than one year of that caliber in a career. Waldman's management conclusion, for real and fantasy rosters alike: because few receivers deliver multiple consecutive years of starter production, you should be adding receivers every single year to keep the influx coming. He also cautions that talent is only one of many drivers of opportunity, and that fans wrongly credit or blame talent alone for hits and misses. Applied to 2025, this is why he rejects the 'bad class' label — it may lack star power, but he expects four to five players to hit that starter-production bar.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]) [Dynasty]: whether 2025 is a bad receiver class depends entirely on the criterion. Waldman says if the test is having four or five primary receivers — guys who beat man coverage on a timing route on third-and-15 when everyone in the stadium knows the ball is going to them — then yes, it is bad, because few if any of those exist here. But if the test is producing a batch of WR2/WR3/WR4 types with long careers, it is a pretty good class. He suggests these prospects are not [[Brian Thomas Jr.]] or [[Malik Nabers]], but could yield mid-range [[Tee Higgins]], [[Mike Williams]] or early-career [[Chris Godwin]] outcomes. His strategic conclusions for dynasty: after the first five receivers there is value in round one to one-and-a-half; alternatively trade back for 2026-27 picks; or stockpile receiver depth and trade it for the running back you actually want, since you keep building value at one position either way.
- 2025-04-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-03]]) [Dynasty]: he expects the 2025 class to be a disappointing fantasy draft relative to its talent, and framed the receiver group as thinner than 2022 or 2023 beyond the first five or six — a large group of prospects who have developed many tools in the toolbox but are not masters of any of them, where past classes had stars with three tools used better than these players use eight. [[Brandon Angelo]] agreed the landscape will push real third-round talents into the sixth. Waldman's strategic conclusion, against his own usual preference for investing in receivers and quarterbacks: if you need only one or two running backs, pick your spots early at tight end and receiver and then load up on backs from round two or three onward, or trade out of 2026-2027 picks for higher current picks and stockpile.
