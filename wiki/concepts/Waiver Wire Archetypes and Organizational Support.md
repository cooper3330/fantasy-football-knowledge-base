## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-11-22 — According to Adam Harstad, untracked *Film and Theory* co-host ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]) [Dynasty]: Harstad's thesis is that waivers barely matter, arrived at by asking what the world would look like if they did — top dynasty and redraft rosters would be full of waiver gems, and they are not. He tracks acquisition source on a spreadsheet and about 5% of his player starts come from waiver adds despite being historically strong there, and he puts 80-90% of a team's fate as settled by the end of the draft. Screening the top 100 by FantasyCalc dynasty consensus value, only about 11-12 were plausibly on waivers at some point; some ([[Tyreek Hill]], [[Puka Nacua]]) were unpredictable, but two archetypes repeat: **highly drafted backup quarterbacks** and **later-round backup running backs the organization visibly supports**. Running backs spike harder and do not need to be good to produce; quarterbacks carry a multi-year holding cost, which he mitigates by clearing defenses and adding backup QBs once his season is over. His governing line: players are on waivers because we already have plenty of reasons to think they suck, so what you need is a reason to think you are wrong — and organizational behavior is that reason. [[Matt Waldman]] largely agreed while pushing back on the lottery-ticket enthusiasm, and added IDP archetypes Harstad's model misses (aging pass rushers who can still take everyone's lunch money behind bad offensive line play, tackle-heavy safeties on bad defenses, rotating cornerbacks) plus his own skill-position tells: praise about route-running detail or a back getting the yards the play asked for, rather than highlights. Both cited snap counts, roster-bubble reporting and unprompted mentions by a coach or quarterback (Dan Campbell on [[David Montgomery]]) as the strongest signals, and Harstad noted contrasting successful extremes — Jordan McNamara rostering 13-14 running backs versus Dan Hindery rostering 13 receivers — as evidence there is no one-size-fits-all rule, only edges moving you from 50% to 55%.
- 2025-08-21 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-21]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: he defined the super-deep sleeper archetype he has run for nearly two decades — players you should explicitly NOT draft in a 12-team league, nobody inside the top 200 (really not even the top 160), meant as dynasty stashes and as names to recognize before your leaguemates when they start getting bandied about in-season. Hits are not the point: picking outside the top 200, either you miss most of your calls or you are cheating. He also flagged the archetype's practical limit — [[Tahj Brooks]] would be the number one waiver add everywhere the moment [[Chase Brown]] got hurt, so the edge is not really in the obvious injury case but in the intermediate ground where a starter is dealing with something the team is not describing. Guest Jake Ciely (NOT a tracked expert) noted last year's version produced [[Bo Nix]] and [[Jordan Mason]], which he graded as one and a half hits out of five.
