## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-08-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-08-22]]): the safest tight ends come from offenses built on condensed looks — receivers bunched tight to the formation, two and three tight end sets, a fullback on the field. Those alignments force tight ends into matchups with defensive ends, linebackers and safeties, everything looks the same in the play-action game, and the resulting middle-of-field option routes drive an immense amount of tight end value. Waldman names Shanahan's offense, McVay's offense, Detroit under Ben Johnson, and now Buffalo under Joe Brady with [[Dalton Kincaid]] and Dawson Knox; the tight ends this reliably supports are [[George Kittle]], [[Sam LaPorta]], [[Travis Kelce]] and Mark Andrews, then [[Kyle Pitts]] as a riskier McVay-system bet, with [[Evan Engram]] as the sixth-or-seventh-off-the-board signal that the pool has run dry. He applies the same lens to New Orleans' Kubiak-style scheme in arguing for [[Taysom Hill]].
- 2025-08-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-13]]): three AFC North offenses are squeezing their third receiver out of existence with tight-end-heavy personnel, and the fantasy consequence is that the WR3 job may be worth less than the TE2 job. In Cleveland he expects rookie [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] to out-snap whoever wins the third receiver role, which caps [[Diontae Johnson]]. In Pittsburgh, Arthur Smith added [[Jonnu Smith]] alongside [[Pat Freiermuth]] and [[Darnell Washington]], with reported four-tight-end packages in camp using Connor Heyward as a tight end/fullback hybrid; Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) joked about 13 and 14 personnel and noted the only reason the conversation exists is Smith's willingness to do weird things. In Baltimore, Todd Monken said [[DeAndre Hopkins]] makes him want more 11 personnel but then called third tight end Charlie Kolar a starter. Koh called continued heavy 12 personnel around a declining [[Mark Andrews]] a mistake; Harmon was more forgiving of Andrews but agreed it starves [[Zay Flowers]] and [[Rashad Bateman]] of slot reps.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]]: every team this offseason is hunting a second tight end who can block, because defenses keep going more two-high with lighter bodies and offenses want to counter with heavier bodies. He expects Las Vegas to lean on 12 personnel — rarely shown in preseason but used in practice — pairing [[Michael Mayer]] as the true in-line blocker in the box with [[Brock Bowers]] flanked or winged outside him, which both preserves Bowers' matchup value and makes the Raiders more dangerous running to the edge. The requirement is real blocking, not availability: Nguyen said Mayer must become a genuinely good edge blocker in year three for the design to unlock.
- 2026-03-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-05]]): relaying a theory from analyst Max Toscano (via Sigmund Blum) that NFL defenses' umbrella/spread-countering coverages are forcing offenses back into heavier 12/13-personnel groupings, Waldman argued the highest-value tight ends are becoming zone-reading, shallow-and-red-zone pass catchers who can block, like [[A.J. Barner]], rather than traditional man-beating 'move' tight ends. Angelo agreed the theory 'has teeth,' framing it as an echo of the earlier shift from 11-personnel, Tyreek-Hill-driven offenses toward valuing tight ends who can stay on the field as blockers; he named [[Tucker Kraft]] and [[Tyler Warren]] as high-end versions of the blocking archetype and 'unicorns' like [[Brock Bowers]], [[Colston Loveland]] and George Kittle as rare players talented enough to both block and separate. Both flagged pure 'move' tight end prospects -- [[Elijah Arroyo]], Isaiah Likely, and to some extent [[Trey McBride]] -- as harder to project in this environment, and floated [[Jackson Hawes]] as a sleeper beneficiary on Buffalo's depth chart behind [[Dalton Kincaid]] if he develops as a receiver, since he's already the best blocker on that roster.
- 2026-03-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-03-11]]): citing Reception Perception writer Max Toscano (untracked), NFL offenses may be shifting from 11-personnel spread looks toward condensed 12/13/21/22-personnel sets as a counter to the 'umbrella' nickel/dime coverages that neutralized spread passing and mobile QBs after 2023. If that holds, demand shifts from 'move' tight ends who win man coverage like oversized receivers toward big in-line blockers who can also catch and rumble like old-school fullbacks -- Waldman cites [[Tucker Kraft]], [[Tyler Warren]] and [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] (with [[A.J. Barner]] a lesser archetype fit but saved by his after-catch ability) as early examples, all finishing as top-12-or-better rookie-year fantasy tight ends in 12-personnel-heavy offenses. Toscano's framework leaves two survivor archetypes: 'five-tool unicorns' who can beat press, run routes, catch, carry and block ([[George Kittle]] pre-injury, arguably [[Colston Loveland]] next), and move tight ends good enough to outproduce their own team's WR2/WR3 ([[Brock Bowers]], [[Trey McBride]], [[Kyle Pitts]] at his best). Waldman is responding by introducing a second 2026 RSP grading system alongside his existing 'depth of talent' score: an 'inline depth of talent' ('high depth') score that raises blocking from 15% to 30% of the composite -- equal to pass catching -- and halves the separation/route-running weighting, while keeping the old spread-oriented score as the RSP's default. Under the new scoring, Bowers still grades as an elite outlier despite mediocre blocking because his receiving talent is so rare, but most other top blockers-who-catch rise relative to pure route runners. Applying this lens to the 2026 class: in a continued-spread world Waldman sees 9 potential weekly-contributor-caliber prospects, 7 who could become high-end fantasy options within 2-3 years and 5 future weekly starters; in the 'dystopian'/inline world that drops to only 3-4 high-end move-TE prospects, with 6 different in-line/pass-catching tight ends (4 of whom likely wouldn't have mattered in a spread scheme) gaining fantasy relevance instead. [Dynasty]
- 2026-04-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-04-20]]): Waldman called the 2026 tight end class unusually compressed and flagged real risk in drafting any tight end before the fourth round (or very late third) this cycle -- even having just done so himself in a superflex startup. His logic: to land a true move tight end you generally have to draft early, and if the league doesn't shift further toward 12/13 personnel, backs of this range (comping [[Oscar Delp]] to [[Tucker Kraft]], alongside [[Tyler Warren]]/[[A.J. Barner]]-type profiles) risk being passed over once a stronger class arrives. He said it may end up being 'much ado about nothing,' but sees this year specifically as one where reaching for a tight end could backfire.
