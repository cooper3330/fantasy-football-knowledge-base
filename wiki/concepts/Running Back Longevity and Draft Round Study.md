## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06]]): Waldman ran a roughly 20-year count (careers ending no earlier than about 2015-16) of players with at least seven NFL seasons and at least five seasons of RB2-or-better or WR2-or-better fantasy production. He found 23 running backs and 23 receivers — essentially even — with more than half the backs still active. Of the 23 backs, nine were first-round picks, 16 came in the first two rounds, and only two were drafted after round three; among receivers roughly three times as many, about six, came after the third round. He also notes he gave [[DK Metcalf]] a courtesy season to qualify while no back needed one, and that many popular counterexamples such as [[Austin Ekeler]] never reached five qualifying seasons. His conclusion is that the draft-media line — never take a back in round one, but a receiver is fine — looks weaker than assumed, and that it partially defends Pete Carroll taking [[Ashton Jeanty]] sixth overall. The counterweight he keeps: from a lineup standpoint receivers still win, because you start three to five of them versus two or three backs, so you get more outs.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06 (Players We Think We Know)]]) [Dynasty]: Waldman hand-built a study of players starting careers around 2005-2006, asking how many had at least seven-year careers (playing to roughly 29 or 30) with at least five of those years as a fantasy starter — RB1/RB2 or WR1/WR2, i.e. top-24 production. He found 23 qualifying running backs and, to his surprise, exactly 23 qualifying receivers. Of the 23 backs, 16 were first- or second-round NFL picks and only two came after the third round; the receiver group had fewer early picks and about three times as many late-round qualifiers (roughly six after round three). His conclusion cuts against draft media orthodoxy that you never spend early on a back and can always find one later: you can find one, but the odds that he gives you two full three-year dynasty windows are lower. He also noted RB1 and WR1 top-five scoring both range roughly 320 to 400 points, so the positional points argument is thinner than assumed. Adam Harstad (NOT tracked) was horrified at the manual method and offered to run it in Stathead in 30 seconds.
