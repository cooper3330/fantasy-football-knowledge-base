## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-13]]): the Eagles' Super Bowl win kills the excuse that you cannot win while paying a quarterback big money. Previous winners were rookie-deal [[Patrick Mahomes]], Tom Brady taking below market, or other rookie-contract quarterbacks; [[Jalen Hurts]] makes $51 million a year and, in Harmon's view, is not in the Mahomes / [[Josh Allen]] / [[Lamar Jackson]] tier — Harmon and co-host James Koh (not tracked) have openly debated where Hurts ranks. Harmon's lesson for Jacksonville, Miami with [[Tua Tagovailoa]] and the Chargers with [[Justin Herbert]] (whom he puts one or more tiers above the other two) is simply to build the roster; he mocks GMs like Ryan Grigson blaming Andrew Luck's contract. He extends it to Cincinnati: you can pay a quarterback top dollar and two receivers, as Philadelphia did with [[A.J. Brown]] and [[DeVonta Smith]]. Koh adds a counterpoint from the other side — this may be a strange age where middling veterans on big deals like [[Kyler Murray]] and Tagovailoa are quietly available.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]): the case for paying a merely good quarterback is an argument about the alternative, not about the player. Applied to [[Brock Purdy]], Waldman notes only five members of the current $50M-a-year club outproduced Purdy in 2024 and only four in 2023, and that if you decline to pay, roughly 62% of the time a first-round quarterback pick fails — costing another three years even for a fast-moving staff like the Shanahans'. He also expects salary inflation to continue as long as attendance and TV deals hold, predicting the $50M club becomes a $75M club before long. A related bias he names: many evaluators cannot differentiate the quarterback from the strong team around him.
