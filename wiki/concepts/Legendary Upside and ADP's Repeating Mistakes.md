# Legendary Upside and ADP's Repeating Mistakes

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-08-28 — Asked by a listener why ADP makes the same mistakes every year — overdrafting rookies, the first tight end and the first quarterback — DENNY CARTER of Rotoworld (guest, NOT a tracked expert) said on [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]] that we like to tell ourselves stories about what Pat Daugherty calls legendary upside: the year's first tight end, once [[Travis Kelce]]-era names like Jimmy Graham or Gronkowski and now [[Brock Bowers]] or [[Trey McBride]], gets a narrative where he scores 15 touchdowns on top of the receptions and becomes a legitimate first-round pick; rookies get the same treatment, with [[Travis Hunter]] as this year's best-case-scenario story. He compares it to bettors who take the over on everything — receptions, yardage, touchdowns — because people love, love, love the over. According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]]): he agrees and says it was said well, noting the market does sometimes correct (Antonio Brown as WR1 was a reaction to the prior year) but that the same categories of error recur, [[Sam LaPorta]] being a recent example of the tight-end version.
