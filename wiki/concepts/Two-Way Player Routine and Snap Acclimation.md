## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-12-12 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-12]]): the usual assumption is that stripping a two-way player down to one position frees him to concentrate — Angelo argues the opposite for a player like [[Travis Hunter]]. Physiologically and from a routine standpoint the body is conditioned to prepare for and play X snaps; take that away and you throw off everything, losing 'a rhythm to what you do as an athlete.' Because playing both ways has been natural to Hunter since high school and he has never failed at it, Angelo says an NFL decision-maker should give him significant snaps on both sides and let the player himself judge the load. [[Matt Waldman]] calls the habit-and-acclimation framing new to him — he had always thought of it as freeing up concentration — and doesn't push back, noting nobody else is discussing it from that angle.
- 2025-04-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-02]]): [[Travis Hunter]] is already an outlier by definition — 100+ total snaps in 11 of 13 games on both sides of the ball, while barely attending offensive meetings, is not something that happens at the college level. Harmon argued the consensus that Hunter must be a full-time corner with an offensive package is driven partly by scarcity (great corners are far harder to find than great receivers) and partly by what is easy for a coach or fan to conceptualize, not by evidence. He noted Daniel Jeremiah (not tracked) was the first to argue the reverse — offense-first with defensive moonlighting. Harmon also observed that Colorado's stripped-down route tree may itself have been a consequence of Hunter not being in offensive meetings, and that NFL life removes the student-athlete time restrictions that capped his preparation. Koh (not tracked) pressed the Shohei Ohtani analogy — precedent said it was impossible until it happened — while conceding the durability question at 188 pounds is real.
- 2025-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-25]]) [Dynasty]: the [[Travis Hunter]] case has shifted from a physical question to a mental and technical one. Liam Coen's stated plan is to have Hunter master exactly one offensive position — the F/slot — plus one cornerback spot, with expansion deferred to future seasons, and Harmon takes the phrase 'build more in years to come' as the key tell. Harmon's evaluation point is that a stripped-down college route tree and never practising with the receivers means an elite separator can still be a non-elite route runner, and that a motion-heavy NFL scheme drawing illegal shift flags exposes the operational load. Koh (not tracked) argues the industry, himself included, spent all its time on whether a body could handle two-way snaps and almost none on whether one player could carry two playbooks, and separately expects Jacksonville to force a one-side choice; Harmon disagrees and calls it a long-term project. Both flag that coaches talk about expanded usage in ways that do not translate — Koh cites Kevin O'Connell promising [[Justin Jefferson]] more slot reps and the count going down while the reps got more impactful.
