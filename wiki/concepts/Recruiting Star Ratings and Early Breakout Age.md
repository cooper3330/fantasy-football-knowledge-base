# Recruiting Star Ratings and Early Breakout Age

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-10-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and co-host Felix Sharpe of Campus2Canton (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-23]]) [Dynasty]: legacy recruiting star ratings are a weak prior and early college production is the better signal. Their examples: [[Ashton Jeanty]] (three-star, roughly RB80-something by the services, C2C's RB16, now a Heisman-ceremony and first-round-RB candidate), [[Quinshon Judkins]] (three-star), [[Aidan Chiles]] (lower-rated) and [[Isaac Brown]] (three-star). Waldman explains why the data crowd's early-breakout-age preference has real logic behind it: at positions with heavy competition for touches, breaking out early means you beat upperclassmen for the role, which is itself an indicator of talent — 'there's some correlations to that that makes sense.' Practical implication for Campus to Canton formats: scoop up true-freshman producers like [[Isaac Brown]] and [[Nick Marsh]] immediately, because you never know where the trajectory takes them and you don't want them sitting in next year's freshman pool.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Adam Harstad (NOT tracked) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-19 (NFL QB Lunacy)]]): prompted by [[Michael Penix Jr.]] entering the NFL a year older than the average rookie quarterback, Harstad relays a take that breakout age should be shelved as a factor until the COVID eligibility years wash through, then offers a stronger version — maybe breakout age was always largely accounted for and just looked like a signal because of a cluster of hits. Waldman allows it may hold some value at wide receiver specifically, because starting at 19 at Alabama or LSU with a rich receiver room ahead of you is genuinely impressive, but the same age at a low-level program is not, so the quality of competition for the job has to be priced in before the number means anything.
