## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-10-24 — According to co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, Dynasty in Theory — NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-24]]) [Dynasty]: the 'many leagues, one team' idea, credited to Brian Malone. Conventional wisdom says dynasty market value is theoretical because you cannot trade across leagues — Mahomes costs more in a league full of his fans and less in a league of Bengals and Bills fans. Harstad's counter is that you effectively can: if the [[Lamar Jackson]] owner in your league will not take [[Jayden Daniels]], trade Daniels for [[Brock Bowers]] in one league and Bowers for Jackson in another. Across the whole portfolio you gained a Bowers and lost a Bowers; the net is more Jackson exposure and less Daniels exposure. Because you now have as many potential trade partners as leagues, you buy in the league where a player is cheapest and sell where he is most expensive — and you can harvest free draft capital, e.g. Daniels for Jackson plus a second in one league and Jackson for Daniels plus a second in another, leaving the portfolio identical plus two rookie picks. Caveats: rosters must stay balanced across leagues, and it works as a mental exercise even with one team by imagining the counter-trade. [[Matt Waldman]] disagrees on philosophy — he prefers concentration, saying almost every dynasty team he has had shared a base of six to eight guys ([[Travis Kelce]], [[Nick Chubb]], Lamar Jackson, [[Patrick Mahomes]], [[Amari Cooper]], [[Stefon Diggs]], [[Adam Thielen]]) because he valued them differently than the market, and he loves going all in rather than diversifying. Harstad's counter is that he wants a piece of every player in the NFL not to mitigate risk but because he wants to be invested in every player's journey.
