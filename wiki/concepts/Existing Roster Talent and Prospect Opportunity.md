## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21]]): the existing talent on a team's roster is the most overlooked input into how a scouting grade converts to playing time, and Waldman 'absolutely feels it'. Media and fans see a contributor or rotational-starter grade plus draft capital and assume the rookie takes the job, forgetting that one to four players already on the depth chart typically carry similar grades, know the system, and understand NFL defenses better. He adds a second-order risk: if an overzealous coach or GM forces the rookie in early, the mistakes get read as proof that rookies should not play, and the veteran goes back in. The exception is a franchise-caliber grade paired with real capital — 'that's when teams quit lying, with the checkbook and the draft capital'. Waldman said he plans to add this to the RSP grading key next year, stating how many similar-grade players a typical depth chart holds, and argued the same logic should be applied to a prospect's college situation, not just his landing spot. His worked examples: [[Ashton Jeanty]], [[Luther Burden III]] and [[Travis Hunter]] as grades high enough to start immediately, versus [[Jack Bech]] or [[Luke McCaffrey]], well-liked players whose buzz cools when they land in the third or fourth round.
