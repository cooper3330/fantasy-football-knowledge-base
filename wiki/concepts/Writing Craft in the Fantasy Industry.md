## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-10-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-17 (Writing Craft and Content)]]), in conversation with co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys; not a tracked expert): fantasy football writing is two crafts, and Harstad argues the writing half is 'not subordinate' to the fantasy half. Most entrants arrive from the fantasy side, confident as players and scouts but underdeveloped as writers, and Harstad says you 'can't bullshit the writing part' — he names plenty of insightful people who are not good fantasy writers because they only have half the job. The two things that wash people out, in his view: realizing writing itself demands dedicated craft, and deadlines — 'it's very hard to write when you've got nothing to say, but it's due in three hours.' Waldman adds that the RSP, the work he is best known for, is simultaneously what he is most and least proud of, because roughly 1,100 pages must be edited in about a month, versus the 9-12 weeks a 72-page university magazine got with a dozen people fact-checking. His conclusion, quoting Doug Drennan: don't let the perfect be the enemy of the good. Waldman also separates hobby from craft — a craft is what you are intentional about and try to improve, including the podcast itself.
- 2024-12-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-12-23]]), in an extended segment with untracked co-host Bob Harris (in the industry since 1993; Waldman started the RSP in summer 2006): entering fantasy content is harder than ever — more outlets and healthy competition mean a higher barrier and a fiercer battle for attention, and both hosts say they benefited from being 'grandfathered in.' On the premise that you no longer need to be a good writer, both reject it: Bob Harris calls written communication vital to success in almost anything, and Waldman says you need it unless you are exceptional on unscripted video — noting JJ Zachariason (untracked) writes to stay concise, and that Waldman reads every article aloud like a speech before publishing so it sounds like him. Bob Harris's counter-suggestion: the best way to improve as a writer is to start doing video and write your scripts. Both describe permanent impostor feeling — 31 years in, Bob Harris is 'sure that's the one that exposes me as a fraud' on every submit.
