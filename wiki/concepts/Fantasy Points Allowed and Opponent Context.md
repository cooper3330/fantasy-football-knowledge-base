## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-09]]): he does not believe fantasy points allowed by defenses to tight ends is predictive at all -- if a team wants to take away the tight end, anyone can probably do it, so it comes down to that week's defensive plan. [[Matt Waldman]] agreed and revealed he opens with the stat deliberately as a setup: 'most of these teams can be paper champions in terms of who they faced.' He demonstrated it on Denver, ninth-stingiest against tight ends but having faced no murderer's row -- [[Tyler Warren]] got 79 yards, then largely [[Dallas Goedert]] and little else. He used the same context-first move on the Rams run defense, where the stingy ranking is partly a function of facing teams that had leads and did not need to run. Harris said he was much more persuaded by the underlying usage argument (that [[Justin Fields]] force-feeds tight ends) than by the allowed-points ranking.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-04]]): Harmon uses Cincinnati to argue that a bad defense is not uniformly bad, and that knowing where it leaks predicts who eats. The top yardage producers against the Bengals this season are tight ends, running backs and slot receivers — [[Colston Loveland]] (118, the most of anyone), [[Pat Freiermuth]], [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]], [[Sam LaPorta]], [[Matthew Golden]] (64% of his snaps in the slot that game), [[Josh Jacobs]], [[Olamide Zaccheaus]], [[Harold Fannin Jr.]], [[Dylan Sampson]], [[Marvin Mims Jr.]], [[Parker Washington]] — with [[Courtland Sutton]] the first true outside receiver on the list. Harmon says their linebackers and safeties are horrific but cornerback DJ Turner has quietly been excellent, allowing a 47% completion rate and a 78 passer rating in coverage, and is one of only two Bengals with a PFF grade above 63 alongside Trey Hendrickson. That combination, he argues, explains [[Rome Odunze]]'s zero-catch game inside a 600-yard offense.
