## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-13]]): the most valuable complementary addition to a target-hogging alpha is a 'lid lifter' — a receiver whose job is to clear space rather than produce. Harmon is blunt that for the Giants he is 'mostly looking for someone that I don't really care about', someone who lifts the top off coverage so [[Malik Nabers]] can get the ball underneath and go to work as the best after-catch player Harmon has charted since 2021. The Giants were second-lowest in deep receiver yards, so the need is concrete; [[Marquise Brown]] is about the only true lid lifter Harmon sees in free agency. A second benefit is workload relief — Harmon worried Nabers took too many hits on underneath routes — and a third is forcing the defense to account for someone else. Washington's version was [[Dyami Brown]], a relatively one-dimensional deep threat whose value came largely from drawing coverage opposite [[Terry McLaurin]] on a team that still finished eighth in deep yards.
- 2025-08-22 — According to guest Ted Nguyen of The Athletic (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-22]]: Las Vegas is a clean case study in drafting and starting receivers for the vertical function rather than for overall quality. Because the offense is stacked with interior pass catchers — [[Jakobi Meyers]] in the slot and intermediate middle, [[Brock Bowers]] as effectively a slot receiver, [[Michael Mayer]] in 12 personnel and [[Ashton Jeanty]] taking slot snaps — the Raiders need outside receivers who can take the lid off and open space inside. That is why the raw 6-foot-5, 4.3 [[Dont'e Thornton Jr.]] is being pushed into the starting lineup so early and why [[Tre Tucker]] starts outside despite Koh (not tracked) saying he isn't a starting-level NFL receiver, while the more polished but slower [[Jack Bech]] cannot win that job.
