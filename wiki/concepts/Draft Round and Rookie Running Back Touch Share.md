## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: charting data since 2019 shows rookie touches per game falling sharply by draft round — first rounders about 18 a game, second rounders 12, third rounders 10, fourth rounders 7, declining from there. The lowest-volume first rounder in that window was [[Travis Etienne Jr.]] at 15; [[Bijan Robinson]] was 16, [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] just under 16, [[Josh Jacobs]] 20 and [[Najee Harris]] 22. Angelo's inference is that seven touches a game is not enough to score fantasy points or build a career trajectory, so late-round backs must be targeted specifically where competition is weak — his example is [[Tyrone Tracy Jr.]] with the Giants facing only [[Devin Singletary]]. [[Bucky Irving]] at just under 15 touches as a fourth rounder is the outlier that proves the rule, roughly double the round-four average. [[Matt Waldman]] added a caveat: the sample spans a pass-happy cover-two era, and if the run-game resurgence of the past two-plus seasons holds, fourth-round touch numbers may start to rise — two or three round four-to-six backs getting big carries would be the tell.
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24 (Feel It or Not with Bob Harris)]]) [Dynasty]: asked whether 2025 is a great fantasy running back class, Waldman says the potential is there but overall 'fuck it', because the comparison being made is to 2017 — a class with roughly 10-11 backs who produced multiple years of starter-level play, including [[Christian McCaffrey]], [[Leonard Fournette]], [[Alvin Kamara]] and [[Aaron Jones]], plus undrafted [[Austin Ekeler]]. He calls four to five a good class and thinks 2025 could yield four to six, maybe seven. The bigger constraint is draft capital: NFL demand for backs is low, most of last year's class went after round four, and he would not be surprised if the vast majority of 2025 backs go in rounds five through seven, partly because backs make good special teamers. He raises the Packers' 2017 template — Jamaal Williams in the fifth and Aaron Jones in the sixth — as a plausible pairing model, e.g. [[Damien Martinez]] then [[Dylan Sampson]]. Day-three backs like [[Dylan Laube]] and [[Kimani Vidal]] are opportunity-dependent until proven otherwise.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): his wildest 2025 prediction is that no more than two or three backs go in the first three rounds and that at least three teams will draft two backs between rounds four and seven, Green Bay's Jamaal Williams/[[Aaron Jones]] pattern repeated across the league — which he expects to make fantasy managers 'pissed the f*** off', because the day-one or day-two back they crowned ([[Quinshon Judkins]]) gets undercut three rounds later by another back ([[Dylan Sampson]]). He says he has 12 starters graded in this class, the most he has ever had by a long shot, with fringe starters pushing it to 15 and contributors like [[Marcus Yarns]] projecting as sixth-rounders or UDFAs who would be top-five-to-seven backs in a normal class. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees, adding that day three of this draft is a fantasy manager's nightmare. Waldman also warns the later pick often gets the hype while the earlier one lasts.
