## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-16]]): winning a route and getting the throw are different things for small boundary receivers. Harmon names this 'the Elijah Moore rule' — the receiver is open, but how many quarterbacks actually want to take that throw to a sub-6-foot player on the boundary? His stated answer for [[Elijah Moore]]'s career so far is zero quarterbacks other than Joe Flacco. He raises it as the reason [[Kyle Williams]], at 5'10 190 with an X-receiver route tree, probably gets kicked into the slot or operates as a flanker in the NFL rather than living outside — a deployment risk that is about quarterback willingness, not the receiver's separation ability.
- 2025-06-13 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-06-13]]): Harmon applied this directly to [[Jayden Reed]], writing in the profile that the Packers are a smart organization who know more than he does. His point is that even when the charting says a smaller receiver can beat man coverage and win outside, countless organizations and multiple coaching staffs can simply decide the player is a gadget piece and never give him the chance — he cites [[Elijah Moore]] and [[Curtis Samuel]] as the precedents. Anyone invested in Reed should hold that downside in the range of outcomes. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) pushed back hard, arguing smart organizations do boneheaded things all the time for political and locker-room reasons, and that nobody in Green Bay has studied the receiver position more than Harmon has.
- 2025-10-02 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-02]]): Harmon extends the rule explicitly to [[Josh Downs]], and names it alongside the [[Jahan Dotson]] rule, [[Jayden Reed]] and [[Khalil Shakir]] as the same problem — excellent man-coverage separators who are small, and who therefore run into playing-time limits in an NFL where 12 personnel usage is rising. He traces the archetype back to his prospect-profile discussion of [[Kyle Williams]], allowing Williams may turn out to be more than that. His stated posture is deliberate restraint: he is trying not to get over his skis excited about these players from a fantasy perspective, because they should be the high-reception guys and structurally they no longer are. Harmon adds that he disagrees with the NFL coaches who refuse to put a small slot at flanker in 12 personnel — but that his disagreement does not change the snap counts.
