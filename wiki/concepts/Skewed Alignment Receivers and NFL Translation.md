## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2026-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-16]]): across 535 charted NFL player seasons in the Reception Perception database (as of April 2026), only four have taken more than 70% of snaps from a single outside alignment (one side of the field) -- three Cliff Kingsbury-coached DeAndre Hopkins/Terry McLaurin seasons and one Chip Kelly-coached Nelson Agholor rookie year, both college-style coaches. Lowering the threshold to 60%+ yields just 19 seasons (3.6% of the sample), split among those same coaches' players, rookie receivers, and one more Kingsbury-era Hopkins year. Harmon treats heavy one-side alignment in college charting as a red flag correlating with poor NFL translation -- career and first-two-season yards-per-game for prospects fitting the profile are 'really ugly' -- with only [[Drake London]], [[D.J. Moore]], [[Rashee Rice]] and [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]] breaking the mold. He applied the framework live to 2026 prospects [[Bryce Lance]] (balanced alignment, no flag) and [[Brennan Thompson]] (82% right-outside snaps, flagged).
- 2026-04-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-04-23]]): this year's class has roughly ten receivers who line up almost exclusively on one side of the formation ('skewed alignment' players), including [[Reggie Virgil]] (90.4% of snaps at left WR) and passing mentions of CJ Daniels and Deon Burks (both 'barely made the cutoff'). Harmon notes the trend is spreading beyond the Big 12 (Baylor, Texas Tech, TCU) into SEC programs (Tennessee, Mississippi State) as more staffs run the stripped-down, half-field-read 'Art Briles coaching tree' offense, including at USC under Lincoln Riley. He cites that skewed-alignment receivers as a group have historically averaged only 18.9 yards per game in the NFL, calling that 'scary,' though he stresses correlation-versus-causation caution and says he isn't outright red-flagging these prospects -- with the exception of a truly overwhelming talent like Drake London (also a USC skewed-alignment product), Harmon says he's unlikely to rank a one-side-of-the-field guy as a true top prospect going forward. He extends the point to college quarterbacking broadly, arguing shrunken pre-snap reads and hash-mark/field-width differences are widening the college-to-NFL translation gap for both receivers and passers.
- 2026-05-14 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-05-14]]): Harmon revisited [[Jack Bech]] as a case study -- a college X-only, skewed-alignment prospect at TCU whose rookie-year NFL profile confirms he lacks true X-receiver vertical speed (36% nine-route, 33% corner-route success) despite plus short/intermediate separation skills. Harmon's argument: good coaching staffs find ways to redeploy such players into movement Z/F roles that fit their traits (as the Rams did with Robert Woods and Cooper Kupp) rather than sticking them at a positional label the traits don't support.
- 2026-06-02 — According to Matt Harmon ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-06-02]]): Harmon flags [[Jalen Royals]] as a textbook skewed-alignment prospect out of Utah State (85% of sample snaps from the left-outside spot, stripped-down route tree), the kind of profile that leaves NFL translation uncertain, but that he still liked for open-field/YAC ability -- a 48% broken-tackle rate on in-space targets -- and matching measurables (4.42 forty, 93rd-percentile 10-yard split) to his Rashee Rice prospect comp.
