## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-01-28 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]): the story of the 2024 season is backs weaponizing great rushing ecosystems — [[Saquon Barkley]] above all, plus [[Derrick Henry]] and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] — but the two halves must be held apart. Philadelphia's environment is elite on its own (Harmon points to [[Will Shipley]]'s 57-yard C-gap run behind a Mekhi Becton pull), and Detroit's line would make David Montgomery productive regardless; the special back is what converts a good ecosystem into an unsolvable one, especially when the quarterback is a designed-run threat. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) extends it prescriptively — the right back elevates a passing offense too, which is his argument for Washington drafting one early — while Harmon treats that as a later-phase luxury behind line, receiver and defensive needs.
- 2025-02-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-04]]: the Chip Kelly plus Pete Carroll pairing in Las Vegas is a rushing-ecosystem signal strong enough that Harmon says outright 'we're going to want to draft some running back from this team' in 2025, even though the roster currently has no back either host likes ([[Zamir White]] a doubtful scheme fit, [[Alexander Mattison]] likely gone). Koh's evidence is Kelly's ground history: 2013 Eagles led the league with 2,566 rush yards, over 200 more than second-place Buffalo, and over 2,000 total yards from LeSean McCoy; ninth in 2014; 14th in 2015 with DeMarco Murray as a bad system fit; fourth in 2016 in San Francisco with Colin Kaepernick and Carlos Hyde. The mechanism both stress is conflict — an explosive run game plus underneath route threat is what buys one-on-one boundary looks and shot plays.
- 2025-02-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-10]]): after Philadelphia's Super Bowl win, both hosts argue the headline is the running back but the body of the article is the trenches — when the offensive line and defensive front are that good, everything else is icing. Waldman expects copycat behaviour: teams will look to mimic paying a back to run behind a great line, but the band of lines capable of launching that success is narrow (he names Philadelphia and Detroit, with the Giants' trench work underrated). This is why his running back rankings are conditional on landing spot — [[Breece Hall]] on a good team or [[Nick Chubb]] behind an excellent line would jump the board, while a great plan (Steve Spagnuolo holding [[Saquon Barkley]] to 57 yards) still loses if the quarterback can run.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): Sean Payton's offense thrives specifically when it has a 'joker' back who runs angle and option routes, stands up in pass protection and generates explosives — the [[Alvin Kamara]] role that produced Payton's best years in New Orleans — and that role matters for both reality and fantasy, since it stresses a defense in a dimension the offense otherwise lacks. Angelo distinguishes it sharply from the secondary Mark Ingram-style role he thinks [[Audric Estime]] already fills in Denver. [[Matt Waldman]] applies the same ecosystem logic to Baltimore, arguing a top-three rushing attack with a 31-year-old [[Derrick Henry]] should draft both an A back and a B back in this class's later rounds rather than lose the whole run game to one injury.
- 2025-08-07 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-07]]): the 'age of the weapon back' is real but is mostly a spacing story, not a target story. Backs at the top of his 'weapon back pyramid' — [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and [[De'Von Achane]] — can align in the slot or out wide and force a defense to account for them on the third level, which neuters blitz packages: against Cover 1 or Cover 0 the back-on-linebacker or back-on-safety matchup becomes the biggest mismatch on the field, opening wheels, angles and option routes and widening the intermediate windows for the outside receivers. The entry requirement is a narrow skill set — the ability to create explosives from multiple alignments against backers and safeties, which requires high-end play speed and acceleration. [[Matt Waldman]] pushed back from a fantasy angle: if these backs are effectively decoys, their own touch and target volume may not rise at all, which he flagged as the hard part to pinpoint. Angelo agreed that camp noise about a back 'working at receiver' does not forecast fantasy production.
- 2025-09-03 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-03]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Harmon said he is high on the Seattle rushing ecosystem overall, not just [[Kenneth Walker III]] — Klint Kubiak's outside zone scheme plus Walker's tackle-breaking and big-play ability is 'a seamless fit', with [[Zach Charbonnet]] as a genuinely good fallback the team nonetheless refuses to mix in. He made a parallel ecosystem argument in Jacksonville, where [[Bhayshul Tuten]]'s one strong trait is specifically an outside zone fit under Liam Coen. On the pass-game side he framed [[Bijan Robinson]]'s projected receiving spike as a function of [[Michael Penix Jr.]] being a non-scrambler who checks down quickly, the same way Chase Brown became a volume receiver for [[Joe Burrow]].
