## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-19]]): co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) laid out the strongest indicator he knows for late-round and undrafted players who eventually hit — not rookie-year play, but surviving roster competition year after year and climbing the depth chart. The normal path for a seventh-rounder or UDFA is to make the roster as a rookie, be replaced the next year and be out of the league; he put it at roughly 90% of such players. So [[Tyson Bagent]] playing well as a rookie moves the needle for him 'not at all,' while Bagent being the year-two backup means a lot, because the Bears could have brought someone in. Cited precedents: Tony Romo (three years on the Cowboys), Kurt Warner, Marc Bulger, Jake Delhomme. He paired this with a fantasy blind spot he calls having no object permanence — Gronkowski fell in drafts as the injury-prone one because Jimmy Graham's and Julius Thomas' injuries happened before anyone was watching. Applied to [[Skylar Thompson]]: the last thing anyone saw was a struggling seventh-round rookie, so the two years of sticking are invisible. Waldman independently made the same point about Thompson surviving the Mike White competition.
- 2024-09-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-20]]), relaying a point Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) made on that morning's *Film and Theory* episode: when a late-round quarterback sticks on an NFL roster for three seasons, that survival is itself a signal worth monitoring — those players tend to develop into at least journeyman starters and often deliver a notable season or run of seasons, with Tony Romo the best-known example. Waldman applied it to [[Skylar Thompson]], now in year three in Miami with good weapons. He paired it with [[Geno Smith]] as the archetype of the late bloomer who eventually proves it, calling Smith exceptional but 'the exception that kind of meets the rule,' and with [[Daniel Jones]] as the franchise-tag case where a team retains a QB simply because nobody better is available.
- 2024-11-14 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14 (Age Cliffs and Dented Cans)]] [Dynasty]: the most reliable waiver-wire archetype in dynasty is the 'unsexy' late-round back with institutional buy-in — no hype coming in, but the team keeps him. Any late-drafted player making the roster as a rookie is unlikely; one who keeps making it in years two and three is evidence 'this organization wants to be in the business of that player.' His examples are [[Kyren Williams]], [[Chase Brown]], [[Chuba Hubbard]], [[Tyler Allgeier]], Jerome Ford, [[Jaylen Warren]] and — at quarterback in superflex — [[Tyson Bagent]]. He is explicit that even this archetype rarely amounts to anything; it is only 'slightly less unlikely than others.' [[Matt Waldman]] added the inverse read on [[Khalil Herbert]]: a dysfunctional organization discarding a player is weak evidence about the player, because the regime that drafted him is long gone.
