## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Film & Theory Sequences, Signal, Steelers)]]), relaying co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert): much of what fantasy managers observe is random, and the *order* events arrive in carries far less meaning than the storytelling brain insists. Harstad's framing — 'I'm not an analytics guy, I'm a storyteller at heart; I love numbers because they flesh out the story' — is that a player who finishes with three strong weeks was always going to have three good weeks somewhere, and they happened to land at the end, which we then read as 'it's finally clicking' and extrapolate into next season. The same trap works early: a role player's week-one explosion reads as a breakout when the odds of any one of his three annual big games landing in week one were roughly three in seventeen. His example was Devery Henderson, a very good situational deep threat for the Saints whose occasional 80-yard touchdown was matchup or luck, not emergence. Harstad's rule: all new data is good data, but do not get fooled by the sequencing of the data; ADP stays more predictive than results for roughly the first four weeks. Waldman's amendment is that reaction *is* merited when the change is structural rather than statistical — a clear injury with a clear successor.
