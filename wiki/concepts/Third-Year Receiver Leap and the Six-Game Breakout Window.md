## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-18]]): the industry has compressed the old third-year-breakout heuristic into a six-game window — 'you've got six games to break out, otherwise you're a bust' — and he thinks that is wrong, listing [[Luther Burden III]], Jack Bech and the Houston rookies as players already being written off after two weeks. He offers [[Zay Flowers]] and [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] as live third-year leaps happening in real time, and argues the tell is charting improvement across the first two seasons rather than early raw production. He also notes the reverse error: [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]]'s big rookie finish was dismissed as fraudulent because DeAndre Swift and T.J. Hockenson were out, a judgement that aged badly. Koh adds that industry consensus lagged the charting on Flowers by roughly seven ranking spots.
- 2025-09-25 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-25]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: discussing [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]'s stalled second season, Harris invoked the old third-year wide receiver breakout as the remaining reason not to be out completely — remarkable, he noted, for a top-five overall pick already priced as a WR28-30 coin flip against [[Trey Benson]]. Guest Ben Gretch (Stealing Signals, not tracked) framed the year-two expectation that failed: you hope the game slows down and the player starts imposing the skills he showed in college, and with Harrison that has not happened at basically any point, only flashes. Both stressed the long runway a high first-round pick gets — years to prove it — as the reason patience is still rational even when the current-season usage says otherwise.
- 2025-11-04 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-11-04]]): [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]] is the cleanest illustration of the arc Harmon says he now prefers to call the year-three 'breakthrough' rather than breakout — year one he looks like he belongs but gets outproduced by veterans and the market overreacts to that; year two the staff is slow to route the offense through him (it took a [[DK Metcalf]] injury and, Harmon jabs, the since-fired Ryan Grubb) yet he finishes as the most productive receiver per route against man coverage; year three he graduates outright into the elite tier. Harmon adds that graduation is not purely an individual event: quarterback, scheme and alignment all have to converge, which is why he frames JSN, [[Sam Darnold]] and Klint Kubiak as elevating each other simultaneously.
- 2026-08-03 — According to Ian Hartitz (untracked co-host) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-08-03 (Feel It or Fk It with Ian Hartitz)]]): building his case for [[Parker Washington]], he cited a study of second-half-versus-first-half fantasy scoring surges -- 20 receivers over the last decade averaged 8-plus more fantasy points per game in the second half of the season -- and found the breakouts that carried into the following year came almost exclusively from receivers on good, playoff-contending offenses; the one notable sustained exception on a bad team was Amon-Ra St. Brown. He used this to argue Washington's second-half Jaguars breakout (a playoff team) is a stronger forward-looking signal than a typical garbage-time-driven surge.
