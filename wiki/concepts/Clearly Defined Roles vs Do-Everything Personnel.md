## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Film & Theory Sequences, Signal, Steelers)]]): co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) contrasted the long Belichick-copying vogue for do-everything players — a Troy Brown who is also a sixth defensive back — with Sean Payton's offense, where roles are narrowly defined and each player excels inside his lane: Alvin Kamara catching the short stuff, Michael Thomas the intermediate, Kenny Stills or Devery Henderson the deep stuff. Harstad's point is the beauty of simplicity, and that it is not the only way to build an offense but has produced sustained success. Waldman's fantasy extension is that clearly defined roles make players easier to draft, because you are just letting them play to their strengths at a specific point on the spectrum, and that the do-everything player may be the bigger long-term headache to retain and manage. He raised [[Troy Franklin]], [[Marvin Mims Jr.]] and [[Pat Bryant]] as the current Payton-offense versions of the question without evaluating them.
