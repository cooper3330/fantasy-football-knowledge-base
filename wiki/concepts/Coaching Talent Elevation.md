## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-01-28 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]: the test for a coach is whether he elevates talent above its level, and he argues Andy Reid has done it on offense going back to Philadelphia — McNabb, a Michael Vick people thought was finished, Kevin Kolb drawing a second-round pick — so pairing a talent elevator with a generational quarterback in Patrick Mahomes explains Kansas City. He then asks where Sean McDermott, a defensive head coach, has ever elevated a Buffalo defense: they were great when stacked with Pro Bowlers, still failed in the last two minutes, and rate 12th in EPA per play, 16th in success rate and around 24th in dropback success rate this year — so he would consider moving on, possibly elevating Joe Brady with a sharp young defensive coordinator. [[Matt Harmon]] is more cautious — no NFL team makes that call when the bar is Kansas City — but agrees McDermott's teams come out tight in big moments. Both credit Dan Quinn with genuinely elevating Washington's defense from arguably the league's worst to respectable.
- 2025-01-30 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-30]]): two hiring patterns are worth tracking for fantasy. First, the tight-end-coach-into-coordinator pipeline — Rams passing game coordinator and tight ends coach Nick Caley being the example — because as [[George Kittle]] once put it to him a tight end has to 'block like a tackle and run routes like a wide receiver,' so that coach is fluent in both the run scheme and the passing game; Harmon likes coaches with many different roles in their background, citing Caley's Patriots stints as offensive assistant, fullback coach and tight ends coach, and Kevin Stefanski. Second, offensive-line and protection competence is his fired-coordinator tell: Klint Kubiak's Saints line played far above its talent while Ryan Grubb in Seattle made a bad line worse, and Houston's disaster made the Slowik firing necessary. Koh (not a tracked expert) adds that former head coaches make organized coordinators, and that the college recruiting grind is pushing head coaches like Chip Kelly and Jeff Hafley back into NFL coordinator jobs.
- 2025-02-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-03]]): a pushback on coordinator brain-drain panic. Asked about Detroit losing Ben Johnson and passing game coordinator Tanner Engstrand, Waldman said 'oh fuck no' — the guts of a team are in the trenches, Detroit's are more than fine, the skill talent is good, and Dan Campbell remains. Campbell may not be the schematic genius, but with GM Brad Holmes he is a reason the roster looks the way it does, and that is the 'brain' worth worrying about. He applied a similar logic to Tampa Bay, where promoting internal passing game coordinator Josh Grizzard preserves continuity rather than forcing [[Mike Evans]] and [[Baker Mayfield]] to learn a new system, and to Chip Kelly in Las Vegas, whom he credits with third- and fourth-ranked offenses in 2013-14 using Michael Vick, Nick Foles and Mark Sanchez — but who still needs a line and a quarterback that can execute.
- 2025-02-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-17]]) [Best Ball]: 'pay attention to these coaching moves' — Waldman's clearest actionable edge in this episode was scheme and staff change rather than player re-rating. Klint Kubiak's arrival is why he calls [[Kenneth Walker III]] criminally underpriced, citing what that offense did for [[Alvin Kamara]]; Ben Johnson in Chicago is why he is on [[Caleb Williams]] and gut-feeling [[Rome Odunze]], with the added expectation that Johnson forces an offensive line upgrade. Untracked co-host Bob Harris supplied the in-season version — the immediate jump in Williams' production when Thomas Brown took over play-calling, QB3 to QB5 for multiple weeks.
- 2025-02-21 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-02-21]]): the 'brain drain' problem for offenses under defensive-minded head coaches. Tampa Bay lost Dave Canales, then Liam Coen, in back-to-back years, and Harmon puts the odds that new OC Josh Grizzard is an upgrade over Coen at roughly half a percent — Coen was an upgrade on Canales even though Canales was good. His Philadelphia case study cuts both ways: promoting passing game coordinator Brian Johnson to replace Shane Steichen kept the same structure but lost the sequencing and button-pushing, while Kellen Moore later ran the same offense with the fat trimmed and won a Super Bowl. Harmon contrasts Detroit, where Dan Campbell's own offensive background and prior play-calling insulate them post-Ben Johnson, with Tampa where Todd Bowles offers none. Koh argues Tampa should be very concerned and expects a step back; Harmon is less pessimistic but says the risk cannot be hand-waved, with retaining [[Chris Godwin]] as the main mitigation.
- 2025-06-16 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-16]]): he pushes back on judging a returning coordinator by the failure of his head-coaching stint — people say Chip Kelly's approach did not work in the NFL, but it did work; what failed in Philadelphia was the non-scheme, organizational innovation, not the play-calling. Waldman also credits Kelly with evolving from the all-guns-blazing Oregon tempo toward a more traditional NFL approach. The broader principle he draws is that hiring veteran coaches who have sat in the head chair understand its pitfalls, and putting one back in the role that earned him the promotion is a strong fit — reinforced here by Kelly and Pete Carroll being of the same generation. Bob Harris (not tracked) supplies the Kelly-era evidence: three Philadelphia seasons ranked third in points per game, third in total offense, fourth in rushing yards per game, second in running plays of 10-plus yards and fourth in passing plays of 25-plus yards, plus LeSean McCoy's rushing title and Carlos Hyde's near-1,000-yard 2016 in 13 games.
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-04]]): a case study in resisting a coaching-narrative bump, using Brian Schottenheimer in Dallas. Harris calls the belief that Schottenheimer unlocks the Cowboys' passing game 'real revisionist history' — as an offensive coordinator from 2006 to 2020, across the Jets, Rams and Seahawks, Schottenheimer never once put his team in the top half of the league in passing yards, and the book on him was that he was too run-heavy and death to passing games. He was already the Cowboys' coordinator last year with Mike McCarthy calling plays, so Harris questions how much can philosophically change. He adds the point announcers won't make: there's no way 31 other teams would have hired Schottenheimer as head coach, and the immediate reaction to the hire was embarrassment — a Jason Garrett-type situation where Jerry Jones cheaped out. Harris's read is that people talked themselves into it because they wanted a reason to love [[George Pickens]]; he concedes he could just be wrong, 'that's why they play the games'.
- 2025-11-03 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-03]]): asked whether Ben Johnson's play calling is more than 50 percent responsible for [[Caleb Williams]] looking better, Waldman split the answer — long term, feel it; the way people are currently framing it, 'fuck that'. His argument is that crediting the coach inverts the causation: with any half-decent coordinator as a rookie, Williams would already be further along, and he is now learning in year two what he should have had down in year one. Saying the coach is making him better ignores that the previous structure was one Waldman thinks no other quarterback would have survived as well. He extended it to how media and reactive fans treat a talented person put into a bad structure and then placed on career suicide watch the moment real structure arrives.
