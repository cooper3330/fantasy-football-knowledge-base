# Start Your Best Players

## Definition

Lineup philosophy: start the players you rated highest when you acquired them,
rather than deviating week to week on matchup. The counter-position to matchup
streaming.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-14 — **Adam Harstad** (Footballguys, guest — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-14]] [Redraft (Standard)]: argues managers overrate how much opposing defense moves a good player's outcome — "Justin Jefferson, if he's healthy, it doesn't matter who he's going up against." His rule is to rank his own roster and start top-down: "I brought Jacobs here to start those games for me." Offered a concrete cost of breaking it — he auto-filled rankings one week, unknowingly benched Breece Hall for Zach Moss, and lost both the matchup by six points and the season points tiebreaker by ten, missing the playoffs. Noted the asymmetry in how we remember these: "I'm sure there are times when I've deviated from my rule and it's helped me, but I don't remember those."

- 2023-12-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-14]]): partially dissents — describes himself as "more of a matchup tinkerer," believing he can go deeper on certain situations, while acknowledging it has also burned him. Frames matchup deviation as most defensible when forced by circumstance (an injury to a starter) rather than chosen.

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: corroborates his matchup-tinkerer stance in practice — recommends [[Jake Browning]] as a low-end QB1 explicitly to "play the matchups," and rules on [[Tre Tucker]] and [[Treylon Burks]] as matchup-and-volume-dependent WR4s rather than as roster-rank starts. **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) frames the playoff-week version of the same posture: "this time of year you're taking the gifts that are given to you and feeling them to the degree you need to, and not overplaying the hand."
- 2024-09-23 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-23]]): stay the course for the first five to six weeks even if you are 0-4 or 0-5 — Waldman explicitly said he does not worry about the 0-4 number the way most managers do, because if you keep accumulating quality players the record catches up. Most managers who think they need to make mass changes do not. Even when multiple marquee players go down — his example was losing [[Cooper Kupp]] and [[Puka Nacua]] and then an [[A.J. Brown]] or [[DeVonta Smith]] on top — you can still find receiver help later on a first-come-first-serve basis without also dumping [[Mark Andrews]] or [[Travis Kelce]]. He rejected Mike Dempsey's 'ceremonial benching' ritual, while conceding there is a point at which frustration with a star justifies trying something else. Two disciplines he offered: look at the bigger picture — snaps on the field and routes run — before reacting to a box score; and cut the dart throws first. If you are debating dropping Andrews while [[Christopher Brooks]] sits on your roster getting three carries, you already know the answer. Tight end is the one position where he would come off early, because replacement production is findable — and also the position where he expects to get it wrong by cutting Andrews too soon.
