## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-09-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-04 (Film & Theory Sequences, Signal, Steelers)]]): co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) called hiring proven head coaches once a little of the shine is off their name the biggest hack in the NFL — everyone chases the next wunderkind, the next Kyle Shanahan, when the single most important driver of team success is also the hardest to measure, which is culture. He named Pete Carroll's return, Mike Vrabel, Jim Harbaugh and Sean Payton, and admitted Payton is probably no longer two steps ahead of the league the way he was in 2006-07 but has repeatedly proven he can build and maintain one. Waldman agreed and added two constraints on innovation: owners hire a coach for the thing he is famous for and then demand that thing, and pushing the envelope too far can fail dramatically in public. Harstad's alternative explanation is that brilliant groundbreaking ideas are just rare — most coaches get zero, some get one, and Belichick retooling three times is sui generis. Waldman contrasted the Rooneys, who behave like independent-film producers facilitating the director's vision, with Dan Snyder-style owners who want to be the first name on the marquee — the head coach as their Jared Goff. Both argued the league also needs more retread GMs, since GMs rarely get a second chance.
- 2025-11-06 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06 (Players We Think We Know)]]): in the 'if you owned a team' segment, Harstad said culture is the whole job — the owner is the only long-term stakeholder, since players' careers are short and most coaches assume a four-year horizon, so long-term plans should be made explicit. He would tell a coach up front he has five years, judged not on win totals but on professionalism and whether he is undermining culture (his negative example was Josh McDaniels in Denver, destroying the room without a losing mandate), then hold to it against public pressure: a bad plan is not worse than constantly discarding plans. He cited Jeremy Foley's line that anything that must be done eventually should be done immediately. He would treat players as adults and whole people — family, schooling, outside passions — rather than as spreadsheet simulacra a team cuts to send a message. Waldman agreed and said he would start further upstream, building the interview, background and psychological evaluation infrastructure that decides calls like Detroit taking [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] over [[DeAndre Swift]], or Baltimore clearing Jimmy Smith when Chicago had him off the board. Waldman's own end goal: circumvent the NFL bylaws to leave the franchise to the city on a Packers-style trust inside 15 years, and hire a PR person to sell fans on sucking for five to seven years first.
- 2026-01-27 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2026-01-27]]): reacting to Pittsburgh hiring Mike McCarthy to replace Mike Tomlin, Harmon called it a 'retread of retreads' hire that signals the Steelers are scared of a real rebuild despite having good young bones on both lines. He noted McCarthy's last two stops (Green Bay, Dallas) both saw their offenses become instantly more dynamic the moment he left -- Matt LaFleur in Green Bay, Brian Schottenheimer/Klayton Adams in Dallas -- and pushed back on the idea that McCarthy is a proven 'quarterback developer,' arguing Aaron Rodgers and Dak Prescott were already elite before and after their time with him. Because ownership is expected to give McCarthy his full five years regardless of results, Harmon predicted the Steelers are 'stuck in the mud' and won't reach an AFC Championship game for as long as he's the coach, especially if the hire is really meant as a vehicle to lure Aaron Rodgers back to Pittsburgh.
