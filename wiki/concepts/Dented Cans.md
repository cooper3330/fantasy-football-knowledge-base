# Dented Cans

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-11-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-14 (Age Cliffs and Dented Cans)]]) [Dynasty]: a 'dented can' is a player whose market price has collapsed for reasons that do not reflect a change in ability — a bad situation, a down statistical year, or unmet expectations. The segment is an annual RSP tradition; 2023's cans included [[Najee Harris]], [[Derrick Henry]] and [[Alvin Kamara]], all of whom rebounded in 2024. Harstad stresses the emotional mechanism behind the discount: dynasty managers will forgive a player they expected nothing from, but a player they invested in and who disappointed becomes 'dead to me' — so the deepest discounts sit on former high-expectation names like [[Najee Harris]], [[DeAndre Swift]] and [[Javonte Williams]]. He also argues the age-cliff framing makes these buys rational: a high-variance 70% chance of full prime production plus 30% bust is worth more in a win-first-of-12 format than a certain 70% of prime.
- 2024-11-22 — According to Adam Harstad, untracked co-host, with [[Matt Waldman]] agreeing ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-22]]) [Dynasty]: the pair named [[Chris Olave]] 'the' dented can for 2025 — a case they said they had embarrassingly forgotten in the prior week's dented-can episode. The structure of the argument is the template for the concept: the discount is entirely situational (injuries, a poor fit with [[Derek Carr]], New Orleans in turmoil) while every evaluative process still loves the player, leaving him outside the top 20 dynasty receivers with, in Harstad's words, the most bulletproof case in the market to ascend into the top 10. Harstad called Olave his number one dynasty buy outright.
- 2025-08-27 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-27]]): Harris articulated the injury-discount version of the dented-can idea while taking [[Christian McCaffrey]] at 11-1 for the rushing title — a McCaffrey-style player in the Shanahan system without the injury risk would be priced near [[Saquon Barkley]] at around plus 375, so the entire gap is the health discount and that gap is where the value sits. He applied the same reasoning to [[Puka Nacua]]'s low 5.5 touchdown prop, judging the number to be about injury risk rather than ability. The stated cost of the approach: he fully expects some of these players not to finish the season, which is why McCaffrey is no longer his 1.01 even as he stays a first-rounder.
- 2025-09-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-11]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: Faced with the 49ers injury cluster — [[Brock Purdy]] two to five weeks with turf toe, [[George Kittle]] on IR a month, [[Brandon Aiyuk]] out until roughly Week 6 — Harris articulates his rule: he is not in the business of trading away players the moment the market decides to pay less for them, because that is usually not how you win. Since nobody is talking about shutting any of these players down, everyone should in theory be back and healthy by the second half, so if you like the players as players you buy rather than sell, provided you can withstand the fallow weeks. The price discipline matters: for Kittle it has to be really cheap, and not tight end for tight end — you send a surplus player to whoever just wants out. He generally dislikes trading after one week because it is the least anyone will ever know, but does not mind finding out how panicky the injured players' managers are.
- 2025-10-09 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys; NOT a tracked expert) with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-09]]) [Dynasty]: hunting undervalued assets is the one way a rebuilding roster does not merely hold value but gains it. His template is the highly drafted back who showed enough but disappointed in his first stint and is now heavily discounted — [[Javonte Williams]] and [[J.K. Dobbins]] as current examples, [[Leonard Fournette]] as the one that paid off. He stressed these buys need not fit the rebuild's timeline at all; Fournette was arguably too old to hold through one, but nobody would give anything for him, so holding cost nothing and the upside was a second act.
