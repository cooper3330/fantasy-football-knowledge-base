# Weak Quarterback Play and Receiver Value

## Definition

How much a bad or backup quarterback should discount the receivers around him.
The claim tracked here is that managers systematically over-discount: modern
receiving talent is good enough at route running and catching that it raises
the level of quarterback play, rather than the quarterback capping the
receivers.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-18 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]: states it as a rule he set for himself before the 2023 season — "I was going to not let less-than-ideal quarterback situations scare me off of wide receivers." Credits the framing to Jeff Mans on SiriusXM's fantasy pre-game show: receiving talent "has come to a point where it can raise the level of quarterback play," making players who would formerly have been journeymen serviceable not just for NFL purposes but for fantasy purposes. Cites his own 2022 lessons with DK Metcalf and Tyler Lockett, and says the industry likely repeated the mistake in 2023 with Michael Pittman Jr. — "we really overplay that in our mind."

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: supplies the mechanism from film. With Nick Mullens starting in Minnesota, he is comfortable starting all of [[Justin Jefferson]], [[T.J. Hockenson]] and [[Jordan Addison]] because "when you see that these players are reliable as route runners, it inspires confidence from even just NFL-caliber, not starting-caliber, quarterbacks to fit the ball in the tight spots — because the receivers are where they need to be."

- 2023-12-18 — **Matt Waldman**, same episode, on the size of the discount: it is real but small — bump each affected pass catcher **one tier** below his absolute ceiling and keep starting him, rather than benching him. The exception is the specific throw the backup can't make: [[Tee Higgins]] becomes boom/bust with [[Jake Browning]] because Browning doesn't throw the moving 25–40-yard ball that Joe Burrow does, while [[Ja'Marr Chase]] "is still going to be Ja'Marr Chase."

**Practical read:** discount for the *throws the quarterback cannot make*, not
for his name. Alpha receivers and reliable route runners survive a downgrade;
receivers whose value is concentrated in one throw type do not.
