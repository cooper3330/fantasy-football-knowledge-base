## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]): crediting former NFL player Ryan Riddle, Waldman describes a draft-capital bias in practice reps driven by sunk cost. A UDFA might get three reps, and one mistake in three reads as 'see, he can't play', while a second- or third-round pick on his third team gets triple the reps, may be failing at a higher rate percentage-wise, and is read as a starter working through things. His illustration is Carlos Hyde versus Isaiah Crowell in Cleveland: it took injuries to Hyde, Jamal Lewis and Dion Lewis across three preseason games before Crowell got fourth-quarter game reps, played well, and became a multi-year starter. Waldman ties it to Kyle Shanahan's line that teams do not know what they have with running backs until they see them in game situations, since camp cannot test pass protection, two-minute operation and diagnosis under real conditions. Hatman agreed the middle-of-the-bell-curve players are the hard ones for teams too.
- 2025-08-29 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-29]]): Harmon publicly corrected his own priors on Houston's rookies, saying the whole-offseason assumption that [[Jayden Higgins]] would play ahead of [[Jaylin Noel]] rested on bowing to draft capital — the earlier pick, the open outside job — rather than evidence, and that neither camp report supports it. He now puts them at closer to even odds to be the more productive rookie and prefers Noel as a prospect. The inverse case ran through the same episode: [[Jalen Coker]] as an undrafted free agent surviving the offseason wars against an eighth overall pick and multiple veteran additions, which Harmon said usually does not happen, and his repeated warning that draft position had made the market misprice day-three charting favorites like [[Tory Horton]] and [[Malik Washington]].
