# Post-Bye Rookie Bump

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-10-22 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-22]]): Harmon says he subscribes to the post-bye rookie bump — he credits Hayden Winks of Underdog (not tracked) as the first person he heard articulate it — but insists it is not a blanket rule and not magic. The mechanism, in his telling, is coaching: a staff watches the first-half film, sees a rookie winning on his routes, and comes out of the bye designing him as the number one read in more concepts. That is what he expects for players like [[Rome Odunze]] and [[Ladd McConkey]] who are winning on film. It does not apply to a rookie who isn't beating man coverage — with [[Xavier Worthy]], Harmon argues Andy Reid's film review leads to designed touches rather than first-read status, because 'they don't go on their bye week and suddenly evolve like Pokemon into a new player.'
- 2024-11-12 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-11-12]], relayed with [[Matt Harmon]]'s agreement: the usual expectation is that the bye is when good rookies take at least a baby step forward, which is why [[Caleb Williams]]'s post-bye collapse surprised him. Williams had shown tangible progression from weeks 3 through 6 — a couple of 300-yard games, nine passing touchdowns to three interceptions — and drew Washington and Arizona out of the bye, decent matchups to build on. Instead the three post-bye games were atrocious, with Koh reading hesitancy and fear of mistakes on tape. Harmon's addition is that the bye clearly involved very little self-scouting by the Chicago staff, so the bump is contingent on the coaching using the week, not automatic.
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-09]]): the pattern he leans on is that promising rookies who have not been playing much 'start to get more work off the bye week,' because the bye gives a staff the practice runway to expand a young player's package. He applied it directly to [[Luther Burden III]] coming out of Chicago's bye into a Washington matchup, pairing it with corroborating signals from outside the offense -- a special teams coach saying Burden is too good a receiver for them to keep using there much longer. [[Chris Harris]] treated the pattern as an argument for a speculative bench add rather than a lineup start, since roster percentage (10% in Yahoo) means you can act before it happens.
- 2025-10-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-10-16]]): pushing back on the 'post-bye rookie bump' idea being 'magic,' Harmon noted that after Chicago's Week 5 bye, coach Ben Johnson said his priority was fixing the run game rather than scheming up more touches for [[Luther Burden III]] or Colston Loveland -- and the offensive line's improved Week 6 performance bore that out, even though Burden's route share did tick up to 39.4%.
