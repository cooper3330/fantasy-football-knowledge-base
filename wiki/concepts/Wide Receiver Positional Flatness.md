## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-09]]): a point he says so often that listeners want to stuff cotton in their ears -- 'receiver is unbelievably flat, we don't know what's going to happen from wide receiver 5 to wide receiver 50.' The practical consequence he draws is that weekly start/sit choices deep in the receiver range are close to coin flips, so speculative adds like [[Luther Burden III]] (10% rostered) are cheap and defensible, while a ranking gap of ten or fifteen spots should not by itself decide a lineup. [[Matt Waldman]] used the same flatness to justify starting a 7%-rostered [[DeAndre Hopkins]] over rostered top-50 receivers; Harris accepted the logic in principle while calling that specific application a wild card, and used it again on [[A.J. Brown]] -- 'who cares if you're ranking him 10 or 23? I have to use him' -- before deciding the flatness cut the other way and he could sit him.
- 2025-10-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-10]]): his number five hold is deliberately a non-answer — [[Jaxon Smith-Njigba]], [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] and [[Puka Nacua]], with [[Justin Jefferson]] arguably alongside — because 'receiver from number four to number 50 is an absolute cluster.' The consequence he draws is a trade rule rather than a ranking: if you own one of the three genuine 'it' guys, don't trade them; everyone else is tradable in the right deal for the right running back, or for a package pairing a receiver with a back. Guest Cousin Josh calls this a chicken answer and argues [[Courtland Sutton]] belongs in the small group of receivers you can actually count on. Harris also concedes an argument he'd respect on Nacua — that he is the new Matt Bryant-style perpetual injury risk who is hurt in every game and one of these times won't get up, so trading him for [[Jonathan Taylor]] would not be terrible.
