## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-07-29 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-29]]): guest Dwain McFarland of Fantasy Life (NOT a tracked expert) lays out the play-design levers that convert directly into fantasy points. Per route run for a wide receiver, a play-action route is worth 26% more PPR, motion at the snap 55% more, and two-receiver personnel 29% more than three-receiver sets (fewer mouths to feed). By those measures Arthur Smith in Atlanta ranked second in the NFL in play action, fourth in motion at the snap at 57%, and first in two-receiver personnel — grouping him with Kyle Shanahan and Mike McDaniel — which McFarland argues means the bad Atlanta results were quarterback play, not scheme, since 70% of NFL plays are first-read plays and [[Desmond Ridder]] couldn't make the first read. His foil is Zach Taylor, who does none of these things and has been able to rest on [[Joe Burrow]], [[Ja'Marr Chase]] and [[Tee Higgins]]. Waldman adds Smith's real sin was player utilization — [[Drake London]] in the low 80s of pass-play snaps instead of 92-94%, [[Kyle Pitts]] the same, [[Bijan Robinson]] under 50% of rushing attempts.
- 2024-08-27 — According to guest Derek Klassen (not a tracked expert) and host James Koh (not tracked) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-08-27]]): an extended discussion of which play callers actually add value. Klassen is of two minds on Kliff Kingsbury — a poor NFL passing-game caller running simple RPOs, four-wide quick game, slot fades and go balls, but with one of the better gun and quarterback run games in football, which happens to match [[Jayden Daniels]]'s strengths; the problem is Washington's personnel. On college-to-NFL translation, Koh warns against assuming Seattle inherits Washington's offense with Ryan Grubb, who has worked almost entirely under Kalen DeBoer and never spent a day in the NFL — the same pattern as Pete Carmichael away from Sean Payton, and Joe Brady, who Klassen believes was not really calling LSU's offense and struggled in Carolina before improving at Buffalo. Klassen rates Grubb's scheme as NFL concepts retrofitted to college, well above Kingsbury's high-school air raid, but notes Washington's elite line let them win with five-man protection. Both also question Kellen Moore's run game and refusal to design running-back targets.
- 2024-09-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-10]]): Week 1 gave a clean A/B on deploying motion and quick game. Ben Johnson used full-speed pre-snap motion to get [[Jameson Williams]] off the line and away from press man, producing a 42.9% first-read target share and 68 of his 121 yards after the catch, while simultaneously pushing [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] onto the line as a power slot — Harmon calls the two evolving in conjunction 'really cool to see,' though he expects a learning curve for Johnson in redistributing touches. Against that, Cliff Kingsbury put [[Terry McLaurin]] in pre-snap motion zero times (46 snaps left, three right, per Grant Paulson's charting). Harmon's principle: motion and rhythm throws are good tools, but 'do that shit with your best players' — Kingsbury has instead motioned the Mickey Mouse players since Arizona, where [[DeAndre Hopkins]] did the same static iso work McLaurin is doing now.
