## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-03-31 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-31]]): Waldman details the RSP quarterback charting system and why box score stats are not contextually sound data. Completion percentage cannot show how precise placement is relative to the route break and coverage position, whether a bad outcome was the receiver's fault despite a good decision, or whether the quarterback executed with the appropriate speed of confidence. He charts every throw by situation, platform, target distance, target zone, accuracy type and required velocity — scripted vs unscripted, on/off platform, from a stance or on the move, same-side vs opposite-hash, with and without pressure, versus man and zone. Distances are measured from the quarterback's throwing point to the receiver, not the line of scrimmage: short 0-14, intermediate 15-28, vertical 29-42, deep 42-plus, each with sideline, flat and middle-of-field zones. Accuracy thresholds are drawn from Next Gen Stats averages for starting quarterbacks in each situation, and he grades pinpoint accuracy (exactly where the target should arrive given route, receiver position and both coverage layers) against general accuracy (a reasonable chance to catch it). He concedes all charting data has a small sample — regardless of what anyone in public claims — so contextualizing matters more, and he will override the data by a tier when warranted. He cites [[Baker Mayfield]] as overvalued and [[Lamar Jackson]] as undervalued by box score accuracy, with [[Drew Lock]], [[Zach Wilson]] and [[Brock Purdy]] as calls the method got right.
