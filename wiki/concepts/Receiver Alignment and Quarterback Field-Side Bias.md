# Receiver Alignment and Quarterback Field-Side Bias

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-09-25]]): where a receiver lines up matters when the quarterback has a strong field-side bias. Their case study is Washington — [[Jayden Daniels]] threw 18 of 21 completions, 81 percent, to the right side, which Koh argues is natural for a right-handed mobile quarterback who wants to move right to keep throwing with his right arm, yet Cliff Kingsbury kept [[Terry McLaurin]] static on the left for 79 percent of Monday night's snaps and for all but nine snaps across Weeks 1 and 2. McLaurin asked on the sideline to move right and the game-clinching touchdown followed. Koh's rule is that if a coach will not move receivers around, design the static alignment on the side the quarterback actually throws to; he pushes back at the who-cares-where-he-lines-up crowd, saying it absolutely matters. Harmon refines it: the receiver need not switch sides wholesale, he can align as the farthest-left player but tighter to the line so his routes work back across to the quarterback's preferred side.
- 2024-10-10 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-10-10]]): co-host James Koh (not tracked) raised that 81% of [[Marvin Harrison Jr.]]'s yards and 77% of his targets came lined up on the left, and that six of [[Kyler Murray]]'s seven touchdowns went left — noting that most mobile QBs throw to their run side and dominant-hand side, so Murray is an outlier. Harmon's mechanical explanation is that alignment, not quarterback preference, drives it: as the left boundary X, Harrison is usually the only receiver to that side, so in 11 personnel Murray reads a pre-snap one-on-one and takes the shot almost regardless — vertical, back-shoulder, or a layup curl/comeback — because he trusts the matchup. Koh's counterpoint is that Murray otherwise spreads the ball fine both ways, so it may still be a first-read issue.
- 2024-12-19 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2024-12-19]]): revisiting the alignment lever in the [[Terry McLaurin]] versus Quinyon Mitchell rematch. Harmon — calling himself and James Koh 'two alignment bros' — argued Washington has to move McLaurin around after he was shut down on the left side in the first meeting, and pointed to his production coming from the right side the previous week as evidence the staff is willing to vary it. He ties the alignment question to coverage identification rather than to the corner alone: Philadelphia plays the most cover six in the league, cover one on 23.5% of snaps and heavy cover three, and McLaurin's damage comes against single high (13-150 vs cover one, 483 yards at 17.3 yards per target vs cover three). The execution burden falls on [[Jayden Daniels]] to read it pre- and post-snap out of no-huddle and get the ball to McLaurin's side.
- 2025-01-23 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-23]]): the Commanders-Eagles preview turned on alignment lock. Co-host James Koh (not tracked) argued that in a Cliff Kingsbury offense the X receiver is essentially tied to one side of the line, which is how Quinyon Mitchell was able to erase [[Terry McLaurin]] in Week 11 (two targets, one catch, 10 yards) — 76% of McLaurin's snaps came on the left wide side that game, falling to 61% in the rematch with only 12% right wide and more slot. Harmon set a threshold on this: 60% on one side is 'totally fine' and not worth ragging on a coordinator for; the warning zone is much higher than that. He also credited the ability to move a primary receiver around to having a trustworthy secondary option emerge, in this case Dyami Brown late in the season. Harmon applied the same lens negatively to [[Keon Coleman]], whom he called a pure outside receiver 'much to my dismay'.
- 2025-01-28 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) on [[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-01-28]]: static alignment is a coaching failure you can measure. In the NFC Championship [[Terry McLaurin]] took 90 percent of his snaps outside and 82 percent on the left against Quinyon Mitchell — a corner who had not travelled all year — producing one catch on five targets for seven yards there, against two catches on two targets for 44 yards and a long touchdown on the nine snaps he aligned right. His point is that an offensive coordinator's job is to notice the corner is not travelling and simply flip the receiver. [[Matt Harmon]] partly defends Cliff Kingsbury — Washington had beaten Philadelphia in the regular season leaning into that same matchup, and Kingsbury's offense has always been static, though he moved [[DeAndre Hopkins]] and [[Marquise Brown]] inside in Arizona — but agrees McLaurin should see 25-30 percent slot usage for easier targets, and that the fix is a smaller gap between WR1 and WR2.
- 2025-04-11 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-11]]): lining up on one outside position for more than 70% of charted snaps is a Reception Perception red flag, and he read out the full list — [[Javon Baker]], [[Devontez Walker]], [[Brendan Rice]], Dorial Green-Beckham, Kevin White, Corey Coleman, Josh Doctson, Kenny Lawler, Chris Garrett, Laquon Treadwell, Southern Miss Michael Thomas, Roger Lewis, [[JuJu Smith-Schuster]], Dede Westbrook, Chad Hansen, ArDarius Stewart, Trent Taylor, Ishmael Zamora, [[Josh Reynolds]], [[DJ Moore]], James Washington, [[Dyami Brown]], Tylan Wallace, [[Drake London]], David Bell, Danny Gray, Cedric Gilman, [[Rashee Rice]], [[Charlie Jones]], [[Quentin Johnston]]. Harmon counts only four real dudes on it — Smith-Schuster, Moore, London and Rice — and notes all four went on to move around the formation rather than stay tethered outside. He applies the flag to [[Jack Bech]] (72.4% outside right) while arguing Bech's projected slot/flanker versatility is the escape route, and clears [[Tre Harris]], who moved between sides.
- 2025-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-16]]): the one-side-of-the-field red flag was applied in both directions in the same episode. [[Jalen Royals]] took 84.6% of his sample snaps at left outside receiver, 14.9% in the slot, one snap at outside right and one in the backfield — 'squarely red flag zone' for Harmon, and worse because he has no multi-position history to fall back on, unlike [[Jack Bech]]. [[Kyle Williams]] passes the same test: he leaned left but never approached the roughly 70%-one-side threshold, mixing right-side and slot work into a 76.9%-outside deployment. Harmon also notes Royals' corner-route grade (60.7%) is depressed by being asked to run them as the far boundary receiver from condensed stacks, so alignment context can suppress a route score as well as flag it.
- 2025-08-08 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-08-08]]): Harmon quantifies static alignment as a measurable cost. Only four seasons in Reception Perception history have a receiver aligned over 70% of sampled snaps at one outside position: [[DeAndre Hopkins]] in 2020 (81.2% left) and 2021 (82%) under Kliff Kingsbury, Nelson Agholor's 2015 rookie year, and [[Terry McLaurin]] in 2024 at 72.3% left. The practical consequence, he argues, is that defenses always know where you are and can bracket or travel their best corner — he points to McLaurin being blanked in the Week 11 Eagles game, which alone dragged his zone success rate from 80.2% to 75.9%. His fix is moving the receiver into the slot and to the right side more often; he notes McLaurin appeared to call his own number to flip sides in Week 3 and caught the game-winner.
