## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-11-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-13 (Paper Champions and All-Timers Across Eras)]]): Waldman rejects the common X-and-O argument that Jerry Rice and Lawrence Taylor were merely products of their era and would not dominate today. Untracked co-host Adam Harstad sharpens the rebuttal: that claim assumes a player who adapted himself to one environment would be incapable of adapting to another, which is silly — in this era they would simply use this era's techniques. Both allow real exceptions where the physical paradigm, not the skill, is the binding constraint: Harstad thinks Dwight Stephenson, a 279-pound center he calls the best ever, would not translate to a league of 330-pound linemen, and that Joe Greene was uniquely valuable because he peaked in the only window when running was more efficient than passing, whereas Alan Page's pass-rush skill set would be worth far more today. Waldman's own picks (Dan Marino, Bert Jones, Steve McNair in modern rules) run the other direction — greatness plus better protection and training equals bigger numbers.
