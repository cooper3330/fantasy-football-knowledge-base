# League Trend Cycles and Market Inefficiency

## Definition

The idea that NFL roster-building trends are cyclical rather than progressive:
there is no permanently correct answer to "big back or small back", "3-4 or
4-3", "scheme or talent" — the right answer depends on what the rest of the
league is doing, and the advantage sits with whoever zags while the trend is
crowded. Relevant to fantasy because trend extrapolation ("the league is
moving toward X, so draft more X") is usually late by the time it is
articulated.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-21 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host on *RSP Film and Theory* — not a tracked expert in this wiki) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]: frames the league as "a series of trade-offs... from a game theory perspective." His worked example is Pittsburgh switching to the 3-4 in the 1990s: the front was underused at the time, so the Steelers could buy tweener pass rushers — too small to be 4-3 ends — in the fifth and sixth rounds and turn them into All-Pros. Other teams noticed the inefficiency and copied it; once roughly half the league ran the 3-4, those same players were being drafted early and the edge was gone, at which point the advantage flips back to the 4-3. His equilibrium estimate: "what's the ideal number of teams running the 3-4? It's probably like eight or ten" — the point at which incoming talent costs the same either way and a new expansion team should be indifferent.

- 2023-12-21 — **Harstad**, same episode: applies the identical logic to running back size. "If everybody in the NFL has a big running back, then you can get a small running back and take advantage of all these defenses that are all built to stop these big powerhouse bruising running games. If everybody in the NFL has a small running back, then you could get a big back like Derrick Henry and take advantage of all of these nickel defenses and tweener linebackers." His read as of December 2023: the league has been in the undersized-back zone "for much of the past decade" and is not obviously swinging either way — "but at some point the pendulum will swing, because that's what the pendulum does." See [[Running Back Size and Movement Skills]].

- 2023-12-21 — **Harstad**, same episode, on the failure mode: when [[Derrick Henry]] broke out, the popular reading was that the league had over-corrected toward small backs and that Henry proved big backs were coming back — "ignoring, of course, that Derrick Henry is a one of one. Oh, the solution is just draft the next Derrick Henry." Waldman's reply: "get a Stradivarius, they're only $19.95 at Walmart." A trend inference drawn from a single outlier is not an inefficiency you can buy.

- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]): adds two mechanisms for how cycles start and tip. First, **the extremes get rewarded, not the middle** — as defenses get smaller and quicker you can win with a very fast small back *or* a back who runs through tackles, and the constraint is relative: the small backs of the Tony Dorsett / Walter Payton / Gale Sayers / Warrick Dunn era were facing smaller defenses too. Second, **divisions seed trends before the league does** — "teams draft towards their division," so a style spreads locally first, and the tipping point comes when a smart team looks at eight games' worth of division opponents doing the same thing and deliberately counters it.

- 2023-12-21 — **Waldman**, same episode: Cordarrelle Patterson as the cycle made visible — the comparison between his 2013 and 2023 usage says more about the league than about the player. Once nickel defenses became standard, teams re-discovered gap and toss concepts against them, and only then did a player with his build and skill set have an obvious job.
