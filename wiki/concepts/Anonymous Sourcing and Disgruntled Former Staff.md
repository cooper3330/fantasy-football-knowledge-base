## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Going Deep Listener Q&A)]]): a framework for discounting anonymously sourced player-character reporting, applied to the Tyler Dunne piece on [[Caleb Williams]]. Waldman's argument is that the article may be well written but violates how sourcing is taught — it does not establish what each source's function was or who is saying what, which makes the whole unbelievable even where parts may be true. He demonstrates the symmetry by sketching an equally shoddy article arguing the opposite: that the Bears' scouting staff behaved like church-lady teetotalers, over-weighted the wrong character markers, missed on player after player, and are gone for that reason. His structural point is that when a new GM arrives the scouts are the first fired and the likeliest to talk, and they remain socially connected to online former-scout accounts — so a wave of coordinated negative sourcing about a quarterback is predictable. [[Brandon Angelo]], not a Bears fan, adds that roughly 95% of the sources were people no longer in the building, who are not going to have the kindest words.
