## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-03-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-24]]): a 'whiteboard player' has the skills to execute every step of a play to perfection when it unfolds exactly as drawn up, but his game is prone to short-circuiting the moment something develops unexpectedly. Waldman's stated rule is that whiteboard players are rarely successful long-term starters unless they possess some compensatory athletic factor that earns them matchup advantages; without it, the ceiling is a situational reserve contributor. He applied the label in this episode to a 2025 receiver he did not think had that compensatory trait.
- 2025-08-04 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-04]]): teams keep mistaking whiteboard processing for on-field processing when evaluating quarterbacks. His example is [[Desmond Ritter]] — corrected to [[Desmond Ridder]] — who was praised for acing the whiteboard but who, on film, misjudged defender trajectory and closing angles toward receivers so badly that Waldman jokes he would have needed help crossing the street. He applies the same skepticism to Cleveland's praise of [[Dillon Gabriel]]'s processing, noting that when the defense disguised looks and switched coverages pre-snap in a reported Sunday-night practice, Gabriel had an awful session. The distinction matters because whiteboard fluency is what gets reported out of camp, while the on-field version is what determines whether a young quarterback can start.
