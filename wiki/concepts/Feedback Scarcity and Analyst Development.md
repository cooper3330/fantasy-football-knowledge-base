## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-10-17 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-17 (Writing Craft and Content)]]), with co-host Adam Harstad (Footballguys; not a tracked expert): writers publish into a void. Harstad estimates that about 99.9% of the time a piece gets 'absolute silence' — no encouragement and no criticism — and warns against the instinct to read no feedback as bad feedback, since 'when it's bad the universe will very quickly let you know.' He cites a three-sentence 2014 email from Gene Bramall saying he liked Harstad's recent pieces as the single most valuable thing anyone sent him early at Footballguys, and now makes a habit of sending short unsolicited praise as the highest return-on-effort act of his day. Harstad also says negative feedback bounces off him — 'he's not my people' — but constructive criticism is what actually stings; he was irate for weeks over Waldman's praise-sandwich email suggesting shorter paragraphs, before noticing that writers he admired, like Mike Tannier, wrote exactly that way. Waldman's counterpart lesson from poetry submissions: form rejections mean nothing, a handwritten note on one means keep going. Both caution against letting negative feedback outweigh positive, and Waldman reframes vitriolic reader email as evidence of investment. Harstad now rarely gives unsolicited constructive criticism and asks first whether someone wants impressions or an actual critique.
