## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-09-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-19]]): co-host Adam Harstad (not a tracked expert) presented the finished version of a toy model he had long reasoned about in his Dynasty in Theory column — how much a single fantasy week is worth. He had previously estimated that one guaranteed regular-season win raises title odds by 1-2%; the completed model, built on historical playoff rates by record, put it at the high end, moving a 12-team league entrant from roughly 8% to about 10%, mostly because thin margins make that win decisive for a first-round bye, and a bye is close to an automatic playoff win. The practical consequences: a player missing four games at the start of the year loses far less than the 25% of games he misses, while a player who plays 12 games then misses the last four loses far more than 25% of his value — if you post the low score in any playoff week, no route to a title exists. He also stressed the model changes with team strength: a team with a 90% weekly win expectation loses almost nothing to a regular-season absence, while a 30% team can be eliminated by one. He was extending it to identify the optimal moment for a dynasty win-now trade. Waldman asked for the practical applications; both applied it to [[Isiah Pacheco]] and the Chiefs backfield.
- 2024-09-26 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-09-26 (Win-Now Trades and Jayden Daniels)]]) [Dynasty]: co-host Adam Harstad (Football Guys, NOT a tracked expert) replaced his old intuitions here with an actual model of championship odds, and reported several results that surprised him. A guaranteed high score in the wildcard round is almost as valuable as an extra regular-season win even for a team with a 70% expected win rate, because playoff weeks are single elimination. Marginal regular-season wins do NOT decline in value at first — two guaranteed wins beat one by more than one beat zero, three beat two, and four is about level with three — because each win pulls an average 7-7 team closer to the roughly 10-4 record needed for a bye (9-5 might sneak it). Only after the bye is effectively in hand do additional wins decay. Likewise, improving weekly win probability shows accelerating returns again from 80% to 85%, because you are simultaneously buying bye odds and playoff-round odds, two distinct prizes. His summary conclusion: missing the playoffs one year to secure a bye the next is probably a net positive to lifetime championship expectation.
- 2024-11-25 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-11-25]]): the [[Isiah Pacheco]] return is an example of NFL playoff incentives cutting directly against fantasy playoff hopes. Waldman argued Kansas City's rational move with a wildly successful season underway is to keep Pacheco's leg healing, hand him only sparing touches in a Carson Steele-type role, and leave [[Kareem Hunt]] as the lead — which is precisely the weeks fantasy managers need production. Bob Harris (not tracked) framed the same logic as getting healthier for the games that matter more than the ones that matter now. Waldman flagged the honest caveat that this is the call he most expects to be wrong about, since teams do not always behave rationally with a returning starter.
- 2025-11-11 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-11-11]]): as of Week 11 he refuses to make waiver decisions on playoff schedules - 'I don't want to be looking at 15, 16, 17 week schedules that are a month and six weeks away, it's just don't play that game'. He puts securing handcuffs, a startable second quarterback for the post-bye stretch and next-man-up insurance ahead of matchup projection that far out.
