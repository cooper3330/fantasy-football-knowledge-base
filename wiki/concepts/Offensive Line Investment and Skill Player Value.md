## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-03-10 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-10]]): a team's willingness to spend real draft capital on the offensive line is a gate on every skill player's fantasy outcome, and Seattle is his case study. He said John Schneider 'likes to go to goodwill and big lots' for linemen, doubled down publicly again this offseason on not prioritizing the position, and that the skill players Seattle has discarded over the past two to three years would all have been much better with a line. The consequences he drew: [[Sam Darnold]] is a great Klint Kubiak scheme fit in theory but exposed in practice, and [[Kenneth Walker III]] presses and swings for home runs to compensate for the blocking. Co-host Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) cited former Broncos GM Ted Sundquist that a team needs at least one and ideally two good tackles and that you cannot pluck one mid-season. Waldman applied the same gate to [[Christian Kirk]] in Houston and to Carolina's rebuild around [[Bryce Young]].
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): the reason a premium running back landing spot can be a bad one is the trenches — he would not want [[Ashton Jeanty]] in Jacksonville because the line is putrid, and Dallas would be worse, since a team with a talented back must rebuild the trenches first or the back simply posts a low yards per carry on a low-scoring offense. Waldman extends the argument to draft strategy generally: trench players must fit a tight box of size, arm length, height and speed, so they cannot be found late, whereas backs can — pointing to [[De'Von Achane]] in the third, [[Isiah Pacheco]] in the seventh and [[Bucky Irving]] on day three — which is why he expects teams to take developmental defensive ends early and backs late. [[Brandon Angelo]] agrees on the trenches, while noting Liam Coen could still maximize Jeanty as a receiver.
- 2025-05-19 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-19]]): the Dallas offensive line is the hinge of his whole Cowboys valuation. It is one of the worst in the league and mid-rebuild, which is why he prefers the cheap upside of [[Jaydon Blue]] to paying for [[Javonte Williams]] as a lead back — line rebuilds rarely take hold in one offseason, the Falcons' pre-season rebuild before Devonta Freeman's big year being the rare counterexample. The same line caps [[Dak Prescott]] at roughly 4,500 yards where he would project 4,900-5,000 behind a good one, and lowers his [[CeeDee Lamb]] yardage projection — though with a paradoxical upside, since less time to throw raises the target volume. Bob Harris (Footballguys, not tracked) offered the bull case: Ryan Schottenheimer's stated balance and new coordinator Klayton Adams working with the former Cardinals line coach, Arizona having ranked seventh in rushing and tied first in 40-plus-yard carries.
- 2025-06-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-06-05]]): the line determines whether a scheme's fantasy math works at all. Angelo's version: a two-back committee is great when the line is strong, as in Detroit, where it yields a top-three back and a mid-teens back; behind a weak line the identical structure yields two backs in mediocre no-man's land — his read on [[Breece Hall]] and [[Braelon Allen]]. Waldman's version is on the pass side: interior pressure, not blitzes, is what degrades quarterback play, because completion percentage, turnover rate and yards per attempt all bottom out against pressure generated without extra rushers. Hence his conditional on [[Dak Prescott]] — if Tyron Smith holds up at guard against more powerful interior rushers and the rest of the line improves marginally, a rebound follows; if not, the whole Dallas passing projection moves.
- 2025-10-06 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-06]]): Week 5 produced the same question twice — Dallas was down four of five offensive line starters and the Chargers lost another backup tackle (Trey Pipkins) mid-game — and his Yacht Club chat kept asking whether to bench skill players over it. His stated rule: 'I don't think we get away from regular starters in fantasy for the reason of offensive linemen missing,' while openly conceding 'that can go badly' and that you can't see the future. It didn't for Dallas — [[Javonte Williams]] scored twice and broke a 66-yard run. It very much did for the Chargers, where guest **Sam Gold** (NOT a tracked expert) explained the mechanism: Washington's answer to its own poor pass rush was to blitz relentlessly, blitzing is feast-or-famine and beatable with good pick-up coordination, but a backup-to-the-backup tackle can't communicate, so it kept working and shut down the passing game. Harris admits he genuinely doesn't know whether [[Ladd McConkey]], [[Keenan Allen]] and [[Quentin Johnston]] stay startable, with his instinct being that a week of practice usually restores usability.
- 2025-11-06 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] and [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06]]): Angelo quantifies the line's share of running back efficiency — top-five lines (Broncos, Bills, Colts, Chiefs, Eagles) average 5.14 yards per carry from their lead back, bottom-five (Browns, Dolphins, Saints, Raiders, Bengals) 4.16, roughly a full yard, so yards per carry is largely a line statistic. He applies it to [[Ashton Jeanty]], [[J.K. Dobbins]], [[James Cook]], [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] and [[David Montgomery]], and notes bad lines also cost you drives and neutral game script, which is how big statistical outputs happen at all. Waldman adds the scouting counterpart: the most important habit he stumbled into evaluating backs was divorcing the line from the runner's skills, techniques and traits — done well, a [[Isaiah Pacheco]] or [[Kyle Monangai]] shines through a bad line, while a [[Tevin Coleman]] can look like a world beater statistically on runs that were fully blocked.
- 2026-01-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-01-05]]): points to [[Caleb Williams]]'s 2025 leap once the Bears' offensive line improved as the clearest recent proof that a bad line can mask a skill player's true talent -- and applies the same logic directly to [[Ashton Jeanty]], whose rookie-year offensive line and quarterback situation in Las Vegas were, in Waldman's view, as bad or worse than Chicago's had been, making Jeanty's true talent level essentially untested as a rookie.
- 2026-08-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Dave Richard (CBS Fantasy, not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-03]]): flagged Houston's offensive line, now in year three under this group and still getting overrun in both controlled scrimmages and preseason games, as a direct drag risk on [[Nico Collins]] and [[David Montgomery]]. Harris says he weighs beat reports from joint practices (specifically an upcoming one against the Raiders) more heavily than preseason-game film, since reporters watching controlled scrimmages can actually see who's winning reps; he contrasts Houston's struggle with Chicago's offensive line 'gelling' successfully last year after a similar shaky start.
- 2026-08-04 — According to Ross Tucker (guest, not a tracked expert; [[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2026-08-04]]): Tucker's framework for grading O-lines is that a unit is only as good as its weakest link -- he'd rather have five average linemen than three all-pros and two below-average starters, since defenses hunt the weak links and all-pros can't fully compensate. He applies this across six 2026 units with real fantasy stakes: the [[Saquon Barkley]]-led Eagles (better health plus a new play-action-friendly scheme could restore 2024-level line play), the Chargers (new scheme but five legitimate starting question marks), Carolina (tackle depth crisis behind [[Chuba Hubbard]] and [[Jonathan Brooks]]), Baltimore (three new interior starters around [[Derrick Henry]] and Lamar Jackson), Tennessee (an up-tempo bet around [[Cam Ward]], [[Tony Pollard]] and [[Carnell Tate]] despite unproven interior linemen), and Cleveland (all five projected starters are new -- a 'grand experiment' Tucker calls unprecedented in his experience).
