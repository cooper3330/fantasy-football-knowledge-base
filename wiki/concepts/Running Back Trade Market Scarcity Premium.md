## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-09-26 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Cousin Josh (not a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-09-26]]): the defining feature of the in-season trade market is that running backs are automatically valued far higher than receivers in any deal — and Harris argues they should be. If you didn't take one in the first two or three rounds, obtaining one now means overpaying, full stop; there are times you have no choice but to trade for a running back, and you are going to have to pay. Corollaries the hosts draw: whoever is dealing a genuine star ([[George Kittle]]) is usually doing it because their backfield is a mess, so the pressure point is offering 15 running back touches a week, not a better receiver; a mid-round running back who popped ([[Javonte Williams]], [[Cam Skattebo]], [[Bhayshul Tuten]]) is the currency other managers actually want; and giving up a usable back for a receiver is where you get annihilated, so you must be very sure about the receiver. Josh notes that if this dynamic doesn't hold in your league, take advantage.
- 2025-10-03 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-03]]): the entire five-players-to-trade-away segment resolves to one thesis — 'there's just not that much difference between anything except running backs right now in the league.' There are 15-20 backs you need and you should get as many as you can, and Harris says he doesn't know how you win in fantasy now without them. Guest Josh Van Blank (NOT a tracked expert) supplies the shorthand from his league: a 'running back tax' — if a back and a receiver are equal in value, add another 10 places because you hold the back — and cites home-league trades like [[Jakobi Meyers]] for [[Kendre Miller]] as evidence people are chasing any back who might get 15-20 touches. Harris's caveat is that the tax cuts both ways: it's very hard for receiver-rich, back-poor managers to be choosy, but it also means most sellers won't hand you a back straight up for a receiver.
- 2025-10-10 — According to [[Chris Harris]] and guest Cousin Josh (Detective Fish, NOT a tracked expert) ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-10-10]]): the whole trade discussion collapses into running back scarcity. Josh repeatedly declines to sell receivers he'd otherwise move — [[Courtland Sutton]], [[George Pickens]] — not because he loves them but because he cannot get the running back he wants back; an offer of Pickens for [[Breece Hall]] gets refused, and he won't take [[Tyler Warren]] for Pickens. Harris states the reciprocal: because most of his teams need running backs and not receivers, he'd trade essentially any receiver outside his top three for the right back, and would take [[J.K. Dobbins]] over Pickens 100 times out of 100. Both note that the reason a fair-looking swap gets refused is that everyone playing fantasy already knows how hard backs are to acquire, so the scarcity premium is priced in on both sides and the trades simply do not clear.
