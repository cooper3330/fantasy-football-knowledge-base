## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-08-28 — According to [[Chris Harris]] ([[Harris Fantasy Football Podcast - 2025-08-28]]): asked where the phrase comes from, he defines Category 1 as everything we actually know — [[Bucky Irving]] plays for Tampa Bay, he had a really good last few games and looked terrific — and Category 2 as everything else: the stuff a lot of people pretend they know but genuinely cannot, which we have to guess at and then simply watch unfold. His canonical example is the Tampa Bay rushing work share, which nobody outside the building will be told in advance. He ties it to a broader complaint about certainty in the industry: analysts recite memorized bullet points of yards per route run or yards over expected as though they were known quantities, when in fact we are horrendous at predicting which situations will be great and which will be horrible — he cites nobody having the Commanders' offense good and the Dolphins' offense bad a year in advance. His practical conclusion is to make situation matter as little as possible this time of year and to talk about whether players are good.
