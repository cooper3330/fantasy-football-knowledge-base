## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-10-10 — According to Adam Harstad (NOT a tracked expert), co-host with [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-10-10 (Regression Candidates and Exuberance-Pessimism)]]) [Dynasty]: if each manager in a 12-team league estimates every team's title odds, each set sums to 100%, but the twelve self-estimates sum to well over 100% — because a manager is by definition among the highest in the league on their own players, or they would have traded them. Harstad wrote ten years ago that the fix was a blanket haircut of about 10% pessimism; he now disagrees with his past self, citing a 2017 leaguemate who started 0-4, kept competing, went 6-7, made the playoffs on tiebreakers and won the title. His revised position: aim for accuracy, and better still build a process where the estimate does not matter — his own dynasty goal is simply to make the team better tomorrow than today at 1-4 or 4-1 alike, removing surfaces error can cling to. He concedes it still matters for win-now trades, since production only has value inside a competitive window. Actionable implication: future first-round picks are systematically undervalued. [[Matt Waldman]] amends it with 'know thyself' — deliberately stretch in the direction opposite your temperament as a calibration exercise.
