## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2024-07-12 — According to JJ Zachariason (not a tracked expert) ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2024-07-12]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: his 'pocket passer trap' theory holds that immobile, high-end QB1s hit their ceiling (20-22+ fantasy points/game) at a worse rate than mobile QB1s/high-end QB2s. Since 2014, only 5 of 23 immobile QBs drafted in the top 5 rounds (under 3 rushing points/game) beat their ADP expectation, while mobile QBs (Lamar Jackson, Jalen Hurts, Josh Allen in their breakout years) have consistently delivered outlier QB1 seasons off cheaper QB2-range ADP. The mechanism: passing production is far more volatile than rushing production, and mobility is knowable in advance, so mobile QBs offer more predictable ceiling. His actionable advice: avoid paying a premium for a hyped immobile QB1 (e.g. CJ Stroud) and instead pair two cheap 'pocket passer' QB2s (Purdy, Tua, Stafford, Goff, Cousins types), betting at least one matches or beats the expensive option.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21]]): the archetype trap is a big-bodied quarterback with a big arm whose bullet points — size, arm strength, tight-window throws, a long college résumé — make analysts project pocket feel that the film does not support. Waldman's specific diagnostic is edge pressure, the most common pressure a pocket sees: a quarterback with 'pocket deafness' either holds the ball too long or short-circuits, handles it acceptably only when he knows it is coming, and unlike top quarterbacks fails to get away with the unseen version some percentage of the time. He applied the frame to [[Tyler Shough]] this week, with Ryan Lindley as the cautionary comp and [[Daniel Jones]] and [[Kenny Pickett]] as the failure modes: the physical tools present, 'the soul of a good pocket quarterback' missing.
- 2025-05-20 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-20]]) [Redraft (Standard)]: guest JJ Zachariason (Late Round Draft Guide, not a tracked expert) restated and quantified the trap he named — the top six quarterbacks by ADP who are not mobile have historically performed worse than mobile high-end QB2s in the QB13-QB18 range, last year's example being [[Jayden Daniels]]. His framing of the era: in 2015 there was essentially no correlation between where a quarterback went in the top 18 and his fantasy finish, but since [[Lamar Jackson]]'s 2019 MVP season the unicorns — Jackson, [[Josh Allen]], [[Jalen Hurts]], now Daniels — deliver both points per game and predictability, which is why the late-round quarterback strategy is more obsolete than it was when he wrote the 2012 ebook. The counter-play is two mid-range QB2 pocket passers late, whose combined hit probability beats one expensive high-end QB1 pocket passer. He cited [[C.J. Stroud]] in 2024 as the cautionary case: a quarterback can genuinely improve as a passer and still not move his fantasy numbers, because volatile touchdowns drive the scoring. [[Joe Burrow]] is the acknowledged exception year.
