# Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes

## Definition

The tendency to assign players narrative archetypes ("gritty technician",
"raw athlete") that track appearance and pedigree rather than actual measured
traits — and the evaluation errors that follow.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-14]]): argues these archetype labels are frequently applied backwards, and that they are visibly correlated with player appearance rather than play. His thought experiment: obscure every receiver head-to-toe at the Senior Bowl and have people identify them from movement alone — he expects the standard characterizations would invert. Cites Jordy Nelson (4.51 speed, routinely described as a possession/technique receiver) as a case where the label didn't match the athletic profile.

- 2023-12-14 — **Adam Harstad** (guest — not a tracked expert), same episode: gives the cleanest example — the reputations of Stefon Diggs and [[Adam Thielen]] in Minnesota were effectively swapped. "The player everybody thought Thielen was, that's who Diggs was, and the player everybody thought Diggs was, that's who Thielen was." Diggs was cast as the raw athletic YAC machine and Thielen as the gritty precise route-runner; both were closer to the reverse.

- 2023-12-18 — **Bob Harris** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]: a first-person account of the box-score version of the same error. He had "dialed back" on [[Jordan Addison]] through a long stretch of poor fantasy output, and in doing so missed a real role change — Addison had moved up out of the No. 3 spot in the rotation. "I was just dialed back looking at the outcomes and not looking at what was going on on the field... he's very good, eyeball test."

- 2023-12-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-18]]): extends the "label doesn't match the trait" point to speed specifically — "you don't always have to have blazing speed to be a top wide receiver." Names [[Keenan Allen]] and [[Amon-Ra St. Brown]] as top-5 receivers whose value comes from winning against both man and zone and from inside/outside versatility, not athletic testing. See [[Zone vs Man Route Running]].

- 2023-12-21 — **Adam Harstad** (co-host — not a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]: the archetype penalty is era-dependent, not fixed. In a talent-centric league — which he argues is where the NFL sat in December 2023 — teams build around what a player can do, which produces "a diversity of builds that's greater, less of a homogenization at certain positions... a good time to be an atypical running back, or an atypical anything." In a scheme-centric league the same players get told "we're doing what we're doing, I don't care what you can do." His casualty list from the previous scheme-centric stretch: Percy Harvin, LaMichael James, Colin Kaepernick, Pat White, Vernon Davis. Practical version: when the label "he doesn't have a position" is priced into a prospect, check which way the league is currently swinging. See [[Scheme vs Talent]] and [[League Trend Cycles and Market Inefficiency]].

- 2023-12-21 — **Harstad**, same episode, the reverse error: after [[Derrick Henry]] broke out, the archetype ("big powerful back") was treated as the transferable thing, when the player was "a one of one." Extrapolating a body type from one outlier is the same category mistake as extrapolating a scouting label from an appearance.

- 2023-12-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-21]]), on his own process: describes rebuilding his wide receiver evaluation system after the Hakeem Butler miss (he had Butler graded near Julio Jones), spending a couple of years studying wide receiver coach Drew Lieberman's instructional work to get more granular about which traits to prioritize. His stated position on misses: "don't worry about your misses, worry about just continuing to get better... they may not turn out to be misses after just one year or two years." Notable because his critics cite one or two named misses while he says he could name more himself.

**Why this matters for drafting:** archetype labels leak into ADP. Where a
player's reputation and measured profile diverge, the reputation is often what
is priced — which is where value appears.
