# Scouting Bias and Player Archetypes

## Definition

The tendency to assign players narrative archetypes ("gritty technician",
"raw athlete") that track appearance and pedigree rather than actual measured
traits — and the evaluation errors that follow.

## Expert Takes

- 2023-12-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2023-12-14]]): argues these archetype labels are frequently applied backwards, and that they are visibly correlated with player appearance rather than play. His thought experiment: obscure every receiver head-to-toe at the Senior Bowl and have people identify them from movement alone — he expects the standard characterizations would invert. Cites Jordy Nelson (4.51 speed, routinely described as a possession/technique receiver) as a case where the label didn't match the athletic profile.

- 2023-12-14 — **Adam Harstad** (guest — not a tracked expert), same episode: gives the cleanest example — the reputations of Stefon Diggs and [[Adam Thielen]] in Minnesota were effectively swapped. "The player everybody thought Thielen was, that's who Diggs was, and the player everybody thought Diggs was, that's who Thielen was." Diggs was cast as the raw athletic YAC machine and Thielen as the gritty precise route-runner; both were closer to the reverse.

**Why this matters for drafting:** archetype labels leak into ADP. Where a
player's reputation and measured profile diverge, the reputation is often what
is priced — which is where value appears.
