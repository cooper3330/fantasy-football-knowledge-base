## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-09-23 — According to co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) with [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-23]]): Koh charts two run-game context metrics — unblocked defender rate (a defender arriving completely unblocked on a given run) and loaded box rate (one more defender in the box than there are blockers, e.g. seven rushers against six blockers when a tight end is in). His league averages last year were roughly 19% unblocked and about 23% loaded boxes. [[Ashton Jeanty]] in Week 2 posted 54.5% unblocked and roughly 55% loaded boxes, more than double both baselines. Koh argued numbers that extreme point beyond bad blocking toward tipped plays, and Harmon agreed personnel tells can cause it, citing the Raiders using [[Michael Mayer]] as the inline tight end in 11 personnel over [[Brock Bowers]] as a run tell. The framework is a way to separate a back's ability from his environment.
- 2025-09-25 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-09-25]]): [[Ashton Jeanty]]'s Raiders charting produced the extreme case for this metric — nearly 40% of his runs against a loaded box versus a 24% league average among last year's charted group, and a 32% unblocked defender rate that is the highest Harmon has ever charted in Reception Perception. Unblocked means either a defender shedding a block in a millisecond or arriving completely unmarked, and Harmon reads the pattern as an offensive line that does not know who to block, which he calls a coaching failure rather than a talent one. The counterweight in the same chart is that Jeanty's success rate against unblocked defenders, 56.7%, is also the highest Harmon has ever recorded, so the metric separates the back's individual quality from the environment's failure. Koh (not tracked) adds the defensive-structure read: Las Vegas is seeing single-high looks and running into loaded fronts anyway.
- 2026-02-13 — According to [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2026-02-13]]): identifies pre-snap box-count reading as a key non-physical running back trait he looks for signs of on tape without being able to fully confirm it without interviewing the player -- counting defenders within 5 yards of the box left and right of center, tracking how that count redistributes on shifts, and diagnosing numbers advantages (e.g., a receiver motioning across who won't block, or a mobile quarterback drawing an extra defender) to know pre-snap where a cutback should go. Used [[DeAndre Swift]] in Philadelphia (where [[Jalen Hurts]]'s rushing threat created a numbers edge) and [[Jahmyr Gibbs]] behind Detroit's since-diminished interior line as examples of how personnel and structural numbers, not just raw vision, drive this skill's payoff.
