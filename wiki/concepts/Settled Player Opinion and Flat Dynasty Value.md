# Settled Player Opinion and Flat Dynasty Value

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-10-31 — According to Adam Harstad (untracked Film and Theory co-host, [[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-31]]) [Dynasty]: once the market is fully confident in who a player is -- an MVP-caliber star like [[Patrick Mahomes]], or a known-quantity 'very good but not elite' veteran like [[Dak Prescott]] -- big performances stop teaching the market anything new, so instead of declining the way platonic-ideal value theory says it should, real-world lowercase-v value holds flat. That flatness is the inefficiency: you can acquire the player at his going dynasty price, start him for years of production, and sell him later at roughly the same price, making the intervening production effectively free. Harstad said he identified this with Mahomes back in 2020 (going in the third round of startups despite being an obvious top-tier QB) and cited Dak Prescott (steady 8th-9th-10th round startup price for years) as the clearest non-superstar case, with Matt Waldman adding Justin Jefferson and Brock Bowers, and Harstad floating Matthew Stafford, Justin Herbert and C.J. Stroud as further examples or candidates.
- 2025-11-06 — According to Adam Harstad (Footballguys, NOT a tracked expert) on [[Matt Waldman]]'s show ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-11-06 (Players We Think We Know)]]) [Dynasty]: the useful question isn't just who your cornerstones are, it's which players the market's opinion will never change about. For a [[Patrick Mahomes]], everyone already agrees who he is, so performing to expectation leaves dynasty value flat and all that production effectively free. The money is in the gap — a player you are confident about whom consensus doubts, like [[Ashton Jeanty]] struggling with the Raiders, because their opinion can only rise. Harstad adds that quarterbacks are the easiest position to place in the 'known' bucket, both because careers are long and stable and because, as a numbers analyst, he sees the tightest match between statistics and underlying play at quarterback. He warns of one systematic distortion: dynasty is age-phobic, so a 27-year-old running back gets downgraded on longevity fears regardless of play — 'tell that to [[Derrick Henry]]', since good players simply last longer. [[Matt Waldman]] used the same frame from the scouting side.
