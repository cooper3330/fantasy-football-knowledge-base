# Linear Athletes and Non-Linear Skill Demands

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-03-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-03-18]]): Waldman repeatedly flags 'linear movers' as a specific evaluation trap, on the grounds that most of football is not played in straight lines. A linear athlete produces on well-blocked screens, in open space where he can rumble to top speed, and on slow-developing play-action seams — but is disadvantaged on the non-linear core skills of the position: winning releases against tight coverage at the line, setting up man coverage during the stem, winning breaks against man, making defenders miss in the open field, and redirecting against defenders as a run blocker. He applies the frame to [[Terrance Ferguson]], whose speed and vertical are drawing praise but whose short-area quickness and bend do not match, and to [[Caden Prieskorn]], who does well in a straight line but struggles to move outside his frame, change direction sharply and bend deeply. Waldman's rule is that such a player must be great at his linear specialty and at minimum competent at the rest.
- 2025-05-05 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-05]]): most linear players — those who do their best work in a straight line — are not very good, but a high-end linear player is genuinely valuable, and the difference is whether the offense builds a route menu around the limitation. His example is [[David Njoku]], whom Cleveland feeds crossers, over routes, seams and corners: angular routes that do not require breaking back to the quarterback or an advanced route toolbox, letting him win with size, speed and length. The contrast is [[Harold Fannin Jr.]], whose profile includes timing routes and routes breaking back to the quarterback, which win against man coverage rather than only zone — the skill set Waldman says survives a scheme change.
- 2025-05-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-05-15]]) [Dynasty]: he applies the linear-athlete tier explicitly to tight ends — [[David Njoku]] is a 'high-end linear athlete' who can hurdle defenders, run past people and win contested balls but does not run seams, zone sits and corner routes the way [[Travis Kelce]] does, which is why he peaked with [[Joe Flacco]] feeding him overs and crossers. [[Terrance Ferguson]] is the mid-to-low-end version, and Waldman ties the Rams' repeated appetite for high-cut linear tight ends who do not produce to 'Higbee syndrome'. [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] is the contrast case: a smaller player whose timing-route craft, after-catch shake and blocking make him the more valuable fantasy asset despite the athletic profile. [[Brandon Angelo]] extends the idea to receiver, arguing [[Nyck Harbor]] is a 10.11 track athlete whose linear perspective makes everything look difficult on the outside and who belongs at tight end.
- 2025-07-07 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-07]]): the dividing line for elite fantasy tight ends is whether they can run man-to-man and timing routes that break back to the quarterback, which requires the ability to bend and decelerate. He named [[Sam LaPorta]], [[Travis Kelce]], [[Trey McBride]] and [[Harold Fannin Jr.]] as tight ends who can, and [[David Njoku]] as a 'high-end linear player' who cannot — very good on crossers, seams and corners where the break is away from the passer and he can simply outrun people. His stated ceiling for the linear profile is about TE5, never a regular top-three finisher, no matter how heavily the quarterback targets him.
- 2025-07-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-07-21]]): two tight ends in one episode illustrate the pattern — elite straight-line athleticism that never converts into football play. [[Noah Fant]] is the 'spark athlete' John Schneider covets, dangerous when schemed behind a linebacker up the seam, but he drops his share, does not gain position and is not a good timing route runner, which is exactly the gap between a reliable top-12 tight end and a borderline one; Waldman says his moment passed in Denver and that not every great athlete is a great football player. [[Terrance Ferguson]] he calls linear in the mold of Fant and [[David Njoku]] but less talented than either as a pass catcher, with a [[Mike Gesicki]] ceiling. In both cases the fantasy value that exists is scheme-manufactured — big plays that excite highlight watchers — rather than earned by route craft.
- 2025-08-11 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-11]]): Waldman uses [[David Njoku]] as the reference case — a great athlete but a linear one, meaning he doesn't break down well at the top of stems and so can't run the receiver-style timing routes that [[Travis Kelce]] ran at his peak or [[George Kittle]] runs now. That confines him to deep overs, corners, seams, crossers and screens, which Waldman calls a pretty nice selection but not the full route tree. The fantasy consequence is deployment: linear tight ends get used in-line and on motion touches, while non-linear ones like [[Harold Fannin Jr.]], [[Sam LaPorta]] and to an extent [[Trey McBride]] can be aligned in the slot and run the whole tree, which is where the target volume lives.
