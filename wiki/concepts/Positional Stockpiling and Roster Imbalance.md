## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-10-09 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-10-09]]) [Dynasty]: one rebuild tactic is to pour picks into a single position rather than spreading them thin — he took roughly half a 35-man devy roster in running backs (17 at one point, 12 now), reasoning it is harder to go 0-for-7 on seven running backs than on a balanced spread, and the survivors ([[Jacory Croskey-Merritt]], [[Quinshon Judkins]], [[Cam Skattebo]]) become tradeable surplus. Adam Harstad (Footballguys; NOT a tracked expert) agreed the math holds directionally — by reductio ad absurdum, owning every running back guarantees ten top-ten backs — but pushed back on the size of the effect: concentrating cuts variance in both directions, capping upside at ten hits where a balanced roster could theoretically land twenty, and exposes you to class-quality variance (stockpiling backs in 2017 versus 2015). He called it possibly more of a psychological comfort play, which he does not dismiss, and added the real precondition: unbalanced rosters are great in liquid leagues where you can trade to rebalance, and dangerous where trading is rare.
