# Contact Mitigation and Dictating Contact

## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-02-13 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] and [[Brandon Angelo]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-02-13]]) [Dynasty]: the trait separating durable elite backs is not brute force but mitigating contact so damage does not accumulate over 300-plus touches. Waldman argues the best backs dictate the collision — striking first with a stiff arm or pad into an open chest, timing acceleration into contact so they are already spinning or moving on their second move while the defender reacts. Angelo adds the physiological version: a straining, contracted runner absorbs force, while a relaxed one repurposes it and can chain movements, which is why crash guidance tells you to relax. Named exemplars: Jamaal Charles, [[Alvin Kamara]], LeShawn McCoy, Marshawn Lynch, Walter Payton, prime [[Ezekiel Elliott]], [[Nick Chubb]], [[Derrick Henry]] (bigger, but all blows glancing) and [[Bijan Robinson]], who Waldman says he has never seen get blown up. The counterexample is [[David Montgomery]], who both hits and gets hit hard and who Angelo doubts can stay on the field for 17 games at this stage. [[Ashton Jeanty]] is the class prospect they credit with the trait.
- 2025-05-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] and co-host James Koh (not a tracked expert) ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-05-16]]): the one real caution on [[Ladd McConkey]] is how much punishment he absorbs — Harmon wishes he did not have to scrape himself off the turf after every hit and says he may need to attend the '[[Tyler Lockett]] school of avoiding contact.' The tension is that the contact-seeking is also productive: McConkey was in space on 64.4% of his catches and went down on first contact only 48% of the time, breaking one and often two tackles, after a prospect profile with multiple broken tackles on 31.3% of 16 in-space attempts. Koh draws the line by route type — let him finish in-breakers and digs, but on nines and anything along the boundary, get out of bounds rather than take the sideline hit.
