## Definition


## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-04-14 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-14]]): ten days before the draft he says roughly 1% of everything said publicly will come true, and that he lacks the bandwidth or energy to ingest the nonsense and put it to any use, so he deliberately avoids most pre-draft coverage. Bob Harris (not a tracked expert) agrees, saying he watches the insiders only to stay familiar with the storylines, that nobody is telling the truth right now, and that the tell is hearing ten players linked to forty teams when only thirty-two exist. Both allow that interesting information can still be gleaned from the coverage — the objection is to treating volume as signal.
- 2025-04-16 — According to [[Matt Harmon]] ([[Reception Perception The Show - 2025-04-16]]): when the media says a prospect is 'rising up draft boards', he is not rising — the media is catching up to where teams have had him all along, because people in the league start feeding that information to outsiders. He uses [[Kyle Williams]] as the live example: nobody was discussing him at the combine, he was not even on Harmon's initial charting list, and then within two weeks big-name media voices simultaneously started naming him as a riser. Harmon treats that pattern as an alarm bell that teams like the player, and it is why he expects Williams to go higher than the public consensus.
- 2025-04-21 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-21]]): on draft week, 'everyone is lying until they start spending the draft capital' — the checkbook and the pick are the only honest signals. Waldman applied it twice in the same episode: to [[Cam Skattebo]], where NFL buzz ran hot or cold depending entirely on which insider you asked, and to a Tom Pelissero report that five or six coaches called [[Jalen Milroe]] the best running quarterback ever, better than [[Lamar Jackson]] — Waldman's response was that a lot of it is smoke, but it only takes one person to believe it for a quarterback to go in round one. He said he would be surprised, though not shocked, if [[Shedeur Sanders]], [[Jackson Dart]], [[Tyler Shough]] and Milroe all went in the first round, and that such an outcome would say more about the league's unwillingness to develop quarterbacks than about the players.
- 2025-04-24 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-24]]): the week before the draft is 'silly season', where anonymous scout quotes exist so that a player falls to the team leaking them. He demonstrated it by reading historical anonymous evaluations without naming the subject first — 'he plays crazy... I don't even think gunslinger is the right word, he's reckless' (May 2017, [[Patrick Mahomes]]); 'entitled attitude', 'lacking leadership qualities', will 'struggle to elevate beyond a game manager' ([[Jalen Hurts]]); and on [[Lamar Jackson]], 'has no shot at playing quarterback in the NFL, none', 'he's not that good', and a culture-shock claim about him being insulated, with only one of four quotes even halfway positive. [[Brandon Angelo]] adds that the character claims typically have no observable basis. Waldman applies the same lens to the 2025 leak that Alabama's skill players would have left had [[Jalen Milroe]] returned, and to the media reframing a rise as a fall.
- 2025-04-28 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-04-28 (Feel It or F-It Post-Draft)]]): two mechanisms to discount. First, late pre-draft buzz — when big personalities start dropping a name days before the draft (he cites Todd McShay and Daniel Jeremiah on [[Jordan James]]), it is not that they dislike the player, it is that they want the audience to associate that name with them if it hits. Second, anonymous scout quotes: he points to Tom Pelissero's pre-draft anonymous quotes on [[Shedeur Sanders]] jibing with Josina Anderson's combine reporting, then lists what anonymous scouts historically said — [[Jalen Hurts]] would be a locker-room problem with no leadership qualities, [[Lamar Jackson]] definitely was not a quarterback, [[Patrick Mahomes]] would throw twenty Favre-style interceptions. Meanwhile people who do not talk to media told him Sanders interviewed well and was poised. Bob Harris (not tracked) adds that the big-name analysts were clearly told a range and then spent the broadcast covering themselves when he did not go there.
