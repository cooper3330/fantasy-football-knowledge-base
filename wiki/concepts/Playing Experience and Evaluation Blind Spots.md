## Expert Takes
<!-- Attributed, dated bullets on how specific experts apply/discuss this concept -->
- 2025-08-15 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-08-15]]): having played a position does not guarantee you can evaluate or teach it, and playing one system can narrow the lens further. Waldman's analogy is the post office versus Federal Express — both deliver, by different processes — and his example is Kurt Warner, whom he explicitly calls a net asset to the space rather than a detriment, being definitive about the [[Anthony Richardson]] blindside sack against Baltimore when nobody outside the building knows the play call or who set the protection. He contrasts Jason Brown of Last Chance U, who said he respects Warner but noted Warner never coached or developed quarterbacks. Waldman also cites a former safety dismissing [[Nick Chubb]]'s rookie debut, and Jim Sorgi as a backup who never got reps behind Peyton Manning. Hatman adds the teaching version: what works for one learner does not work for the next, which is why not every Hall of Famer becomes a good coach. Both note producers reward definitive 45-second soundbites over nuance.
- 2025-09-18 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-18 (Film & Theory)]]): former players analyzing film are mostly a net positive but must be read against their frame of reference. He credits Ryan Fitzpatrick and Kurt Warner for correctly explaining the [[D.J. Moore]] route adjustment that people were blaming on [[Caleb Williams]], and Jeff Schwartz on pass protection, while noting the amusement of watching Twitter try to teach football to actual NFL quarterbacks. But he draws a line between having played a position and having coached it: on the [[Anthony Richardson]] blindside sack, a quarterbacks coach argued on air that no coach would ever design a blindside hot read requiring the quarterback to turn and throw into free pressure, showing it on the field, and that the ex-backup quarterbacks claiming otherwise would never have been permitted that latitude themselves. Waldman's counter-example of frame-of-reference failure is Will Blackmon, a defensive back, declaring after half a preseason game that [[Nick Chubb]] would never start in the league.
- 2025-09-22 — According to [[Matt Waldman]] ([[Matt Waldman's RSP Cast - 2025-09-22]]): responding to Ryan Clark (not a tracked analyst) telling Peter Schrager to stay quiet on a subject because he was not a former player, Waldman argued that 'just because you played defensive back in the NFL doesn't mean that you know a damn thing about running back play.' His evidence that Clark's [[Ashton Jeanty]] monologue lacked positional knowledge was what it omitted — no footwork, no decision making, no vision, no pad level, no running back skill set of any kind, only an 'it factor' claim tied to skipping the combine. Waldman contrasted this with NFL players who have told him they learned more about skill positions from his charting work than from playing, and allowed the reverse case too, saying he has full respect for Kyle Brandt discussing running backs because Brandt played the position.
